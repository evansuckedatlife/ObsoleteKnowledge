## summary

**Ding Liren** is a Chinese chess grandmaster who became the seventeenth undisputed *World Chess Champion* after defeating *Ian Nepomniachtchi* in the 2023 title match in *Astana*. Renowned for his formidable positional understanding, tenacious defensive calculation, and classical opening preparation, he became the first player representing *China* to capture the world crown in classical chess. His historic ascent crowned decades of development in Chinese chess, which had earlier produced dominant teams at the Chess Olympiad and numerous women's world champions.

## you gotta know

- Won the 2023 *World Chess Championship* in *Astana* by defeating Russian grandmaster *Ian Nepomniachtchi* in rapid tiebreaks after their fourteen-game classical match ended in a 7–7 deadlock.
- Decided the decisive fourth rapid tiebreak game with the daring move 46...Rg6, known as the "self-pin," deliberately refusing a threefold repetition to maintain winning chances rather than heading toward blitz playoffs.
- Qualified for the 2022 *Candidates Tournament* in *Madrid* only after *Sergey Karjakin* was disqualified by FIDE, requiring Ding to play twenty-eight classical games in five weeks in China to meet the activity requirements.
- Earned the World Championship berth because reigning five-time classical champion *Magnus Carlsen* declined to defend his title, elevating Ding as the runner-up of the Candidates to the title clash.
- Assembled a legendary 100-game unbeaten streak in top-level classical chess between August 2017 and November 2018, which stood as the longest undefeated streak in elite chess history until surpassed by Carlsen.
- Won the prestigious *Sinquefield Cup* and the *Grand Chess Tour* finals in 2019, cementing his place in the upper echelon of world chess alongside the elite competitors of the modern era.
- Led the Chinese national team to gold medals at the 41st and 43rd Chess Olympiads in 2014 and 2018, claiming individual gold on board one at the Batumi Olympiad.
- Defended his title in late 2024 in *Singapore* against Indian teenage prodigy *Gukesh Dommaraju*, the youngest challenger in chess history.
