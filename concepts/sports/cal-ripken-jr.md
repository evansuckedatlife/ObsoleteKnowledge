## summary

**Cal Ripken Jr.** is an American former professional baseball shortstop and third baseman who played twenty-one seasons in Major League Baseball for the *Baltimore Orioles* from 1981 to 2001. Revered across the sport as **The Iron Man**, he broke *Lou Gehrig*'s seemingly insurmountable record of 2,130 consecutive games played, eventually extending the streak to 2,632 consecutive games over more than sixteen seasons. A nineteen-time All-Star, two-time American League Most Valuable Player, and member of the 3,000-hit club, he revolutionized the shortstop position by proving that tall, powerful athletes could provide elite fielding and run production up the middle.

## you gotta know

- Shattered *Lou Gehrig*'s historic record on September 6, 1995, by playing in his 2,131st consecutive game at *Camden Yards*, an emotional celebration that captivated the nation and helped restore public affection for baseball following the 1994 players' strike.
- Ended his voluntary consecutive games streak at 2,632 on September 20, 1998, against the *New York Yankees*, quietly taking himself out of the lineup before the game without fanfare.
- Transformed defensive expectations for major league shortstops at six feet four inches tall, defying conventional baseball wisdom that reserved the middle infield exclusively for smaller, agile utility players.
- Won the American League Rookie of the Year Award in 1982 and followed immediately by claiming the 1983 American League MVP Award while leading the *Baltimore Orioles* to a World Series championship over the *Philadelphia Phillies*.
- Captured a second American League MVP Award in 1991 during one of the greatest individual seasons ever recorded by a shortstop, batting .323 with thirty-four home runs, 114 runs batted in, and a Gold Glove Award.
- Collected 3,184 career hits and 431 home runs, joining the elite 3,000-hit club with a single off *Minnesota Twins* pitcher *Héctor Carrasco* in April 2000.
- Deeply tied to Baltimore baseball through his father, longtime coach and manager *Cal Ripken Sr.*, and his brother, second baseman *Billy Ripken*, with whom he formed a famous double-play combination for several seasons.
- Elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame in 2007 on his first ballot, receiving 98.53 percent of the vote, which stood among the highest induction percentages in baseball history.
