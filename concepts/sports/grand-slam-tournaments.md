## summary

The **Grand Slam tournaments** are the four most prestigious tennis competitions in the world, held annually across different continents and surfaces. Winning all four in a single calendar year is the sport's ultimate achievement. These tournaments attract the world's best players and generate enormous commercial and cultural interest, shaping careers and legacies.

## you gotta know

- The four Grand Slams are *Wimbledon* (grass, England), the *US Open* (hard court, United States), the *French Open* (clay, France), and the *Australian Open* (hard court, Australia).
- Winning all four Grand Slams in a single calendar year is called a "calendar-year Grand Slam," a feat achieved only five times in modern tennis history.
- The *Australian Open* is held in January, making it the first Grand Slam of the year; *Wimbledon* in summer is considered the sport's most prestigious event.
- Grand Slam tournaments award the most ranking points and prize money in professional tennis, making them central to player careers and the world rankings.
- *Rod Laver* won the calendar Grand Slam twice (1962, 1969); *Serena Williams* holds the most Grand Slam singles titles with 23.
- Each Grand Slam has distinct characteristics: Wimbledon's grass favors fast players, the *French Open* clay rewards patience and spin, hard courts are more neutral.

## connections

- [[wimbledon]] — the oldest Grand Slam and most culturally prestigious tennis tournament.
- [[us-open]] — the American Grand Slam, known for its fast hard courts and energetic crowds.
- [[french-open]] — played on clay, a surface that historically favored European players and demanded different tactics.
- [[australian-open]] — the first Grand Slam of the calendar year, held during the Australian summer.
- [[roger-federer]] — won a record 20 Grand Slam titles during his career, defining modern tennis excellence.
- [[pete-sampras]] — dominated the Grand Slams in the 1990s with 64 Grand Slam tournament appearances.
- [[novak-djokovic]] — holds the record for most Grand Slam men's singles titles with 24.
