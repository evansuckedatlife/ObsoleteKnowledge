## summary

The **Hammer Throw** is one of the four traditional throwing events in track and field athletics, along with the shot put, discus, and javelin. Competitors contest the discipline by swinging and rotating a heavy spherical metal head attached by a flexible steel wire to a grip handle, releasing it into a marked turf sector from within an enclosed throwing circle. Steeped in centuries of folk heritage and contested at the modern [[olympic-games]] since 1900, it demands extreme rotational velocity, centrifugal control, and technical precision.

## you gotta know

- Traces its historical roots to ancient Celtic martial contests and medieval sledgehammer-casting trials at the Scottish Highland Games before standardizing around a circular enclosure in late nineteenth-century Britain.
- Modern men's competition utilizes a 16-pound (7.26-kilogram) implement, while women compete with an 8.82-pound (4.0-kilogram) implement, matching the corresponding weights used in the shot put.
- Competitors initiate momentum with stationary overhead winds before executing three to four rapid heel-toe rotational turns across a concrete circle measuring seven feet (2.135 meters) in diameter.
- Conducted inside a heavy, high-walled U-shaped protective mesh cage designed to arrest mishandled throws traveling at speeds exceeding one hundred kilometers per hour.
- Included as an official men's track and field event at the [[olympic-games]] since the 1900 Games in Paris, while women's competition was officially integrated at the 2000 Sydney Games.
- Soviet athlete *Yuriy Sedykh* established a historic men's world record of 86.74 meters in 1986, which stood as one of the longest-lasting throwing marks in track and field history.
- Polish champion *Anita Włodarczyk* revolutionized the modern women's event, winning three consecutive Olympic gold medals and becoming the first woman to throw beyond eighty meters.

## connections

- [[olympic-games]] — premier international multisport competition where the men's event has been staged continuously since 1900 and the women's event since 2000.
- [[ancient-greece]] — birthplace of classical Olympic throwing traditions such as the discus and javelin that inspired modern Olympic field events.
- [[soviet-union]] — dominant athletics superpower whose state sports institutes pioneered rotational biomechanics and produced record-setting throwers throughout the late twentieth century.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical era when track and field field events served as prominent athletic battlegrounds between Eastern Bloc and Western athletes.
- [[germany]] — regular continental powerhouse in Olympic throwing disciplines that produced celebrated champions such as *Karl-Hans Riehm* and *Betty Heidler*.
