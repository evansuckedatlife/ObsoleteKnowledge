## summary

**Surfing** is a surface water board sport in which a rider rides on the forward face of a moving wave, typically carrying the surfer toward the shore. Originating in ancient Polynesia and deeply rooted in indigenous Hawaiian culture as *heʻe nalu*, the practice transitioned into a global counterculture and competitive athletic discipline during the twentieth century. It gained full international recognition as a medal sport at the 2020 Summer *[[olympic-games]]* in *[[japan]]*, standing alongside *[[swimming]]* as one of the premier aquatic sports.

## you gotta know

- *Duke Kahanamoku*, a legendary five-time Olympic medalist in *[[swimming]]* from Hawaii, is widely celebrated as the father of modern surfing for popularizing the sport worldwide across the *[[united-states]]* and Australia in the early 1900s.
- The sport debuted at the Summer *[[olympic-games]]* at the 2020 Tokyo Games held at Tsurigasaki Beach in *[[japan]]*, where *Italo Ferreira* of Brazil and *Carissa Moore* of the *[[united-states]]* claimed the inaugural gold medals.
- Pre-colonial Hawaiian society viewed wave riding not merely as recreation but as a spiritual, social, and royal discipline known as *heʻe nalu*, practiced by both commoners and nobility using carved wooden boards such as *olo* and *alaia*.
- The modern professional circuit is overseen by the World Surf League (WSL), featuring prestigious events at legendary breaks like the Banzai Pipeline on Oahu's North Shore, Jeffreys Bay in South Africa, and Teahupo'o in Tahiti.
- American surfer *Kelly Slater* is widely regarded as the sport's greatest competitor, having captured a record eleven WSL World Championships and becoming both the youngest and oldest world champion in history.
- The mechanics of surfing rely on distinct equipment categories, including longboards, agile shortboards developed during the 1960s shortboard revolution, and specialized big-wave guns designed to handle swells exceeding twenty feet.
- Extreme big-wave surfing centers on locations such as Nazaré in Portugal and Peʻahi (Jaws) in Maui, where surfers often rely on tow-in jet skis to catch colossal Atlantic and Pacific waves that reach heights over eighty feet.

## connections

- [[swimming]] — sister aquatic discipline shared through pioneers like *Duke Kahanamoku*, who excelled in both sports.
- [[olympic-games]] — the international multi-sport festival where surfing made its official medal debut in 2021.
- [[united-states]] — the nation encompassing key modern hubs in Hawaii and California that fueled international growth.
- [[japan]] — the host country of the Tokyo Games where the sport held its inaugural Olympic competition.
- [[colonialism]] — the historical force whose arrival in Polynesia suppressed traditional surfing customs before twentieth-century revival.
- [[australian-open]] — a premier Australian sports tournament representing the nation that pioneered professional board design and competitive surfing.
