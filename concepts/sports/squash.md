## summary

**Squash** is a high-speed racket sport played by two or four players in a four-walled court using a small, hollow rubber ball. Descended from earlier court pastimes in nineteenth-century *[[england]]*, the sport demands exceptional aerobic conditioning, agility, tactical deception, and spatial awareness. Governed globally by the World Squash Federation, it is celebrated for intense rallies and has earned selection for inclusion in the *[[olympic-games]]*.

## you gotta know

- Invented around 1830 at the prestigious *Harrow School* in *[[england]]*, where students discovered that a punctured, soft rubber ball produced an erratic, squashable bounce.
- Contested within an enclosed rectangular court where players alternate striking the ball above the "tin"—a strip along the bottom of the front wall functioning like a tennis net.
- Players hit the ball against any of the four walls, provided it strikes the front wall before touching the floor and bounces no more than once.
- Dominated during the late twentieth century by legendary Pakistani masters *Jahangir Khan* and *Jansher Khan*, with Jahangir achieving a fabled 555-match unbeaten streak.
- Modern international professional competition is governed by the Professional Squash Association (PSA), which stages major world championships and the prestigious British Open.
- Egypt emerged as the preeminent global squash powerhouse in the twenty-first century, producing dominant men's and women's world champions such as *Amr Shabana*, *Ramy Ashour*, and *Nour El Sherbini*.
- After decades of campaigning by international sporting bodies, squash was officially ratified for inclusion as an Olympic medal sport for the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

## connections

- [[wimbledon]] — premier tennis championship sharing British nineteenth-century racket-sport heritage and grass-court pedigree with squash.
- [[england]] — country where Harrow School pupils codified the original equipment and court regulations.
- [[british-empire]] — global conduit through which British military officers and colonial civil servants dispersed squash to Pakistan, Egypt, and Australia.
- [[olympic-games]] — global athletic festival that approved the sport for its official program at the 2028 Summer Games.
- [[roger-federer]] — tennis champion whose footwork, wrist control, and racket precision reflect technical affinities with elite squash play.
- [[united-states]] — nation with longstanding hardball and softball squash traditions centered in universities and urban athletic clubs.
