## summary

The **steeplechase** is a middle-distance obstacle race in track and field and equestrian sports named after historic cross-country races between church steeples. The premier modern athletic variation, the *3000 metres steeplechase*, requires runners to clear large solid hurdles and a challenging water jump on every circuit. Contested at the international level and the *[[olympic-games]]*, the event combines cardiovascular endurance with technical barrier clearance and tactical racing.

## you gotta know

- Derives its historic name from eighteenth-century horse races in Ireland and *[[england]]* where competitors galloped across countryside using distant church steeples as landmarks.
- The standard modern track distance is 3,000 metres, which encompasses 28 dry barrier clearances and 7 water jumps over seven and a half track laps.
- Unlike standard sprint hurdles that fall when struck, steeplechase barriers are heavily weighted, immovable wooden beams that athletes frequently step directly upon.
- The water jump consists of a barrier followed by a water pit that slopes upward from its deepest point of approximately 70 centimetres directly under the hurdle.
- Men's steeplechase debuted at the 1900 Paris *[[olympic-games]]*, whereas the women's event was officially added over a century later at the 2008 Beijing Games.
- Kenyan male athletes dominated the event with historic supremacy, winning every men's Olympic gold medal contested between 1984 and 2016.
- In equestrian competition, the race refers to demanding jumping events over ditches and brush fences, epitomized by the Grand National held annually near Liverpool.

## connections

- [[olympic-games]] — premier international multisport competition where the 3000 metres steeplechase stands as a marquee track and field contest.
- [[england]] — birthplace of the historical equestrian and cross-country steeple-to-steeple running races.
- [[france]] — host nation of the 1900 Paris Games where Olympic steeplechase running events were first formally introduced.
- [[british-empire]] — global network through which British amateur athletics associations codified barrier dimensions and standard race rules.
- [[ancient-greece]] — origin of ancient running traditions that modern track organizers fused with British cross-country obstacle running.
- [[united-states]] — country with collegiate and national programs that have produced Olympic medalists in both men's and women's steeplechase.
