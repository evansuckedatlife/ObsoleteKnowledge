## summary

**Richard Petty**, hailed throughout American motorsport as **The King**, is an American former stock car racing driver who competed in the *NASCAR Cup Series* from 1958 to 1992. Piloting his trademark Petty blue and red number forty-three car, he established all-time records with two hundred career race victories and seven series championships. His unprecedented track dominance, instantly recognizable feathered cowboy hat and dark sunglasses, and tireless devotion to racing fans established him as the defining figure in the growth of modern auto racing in the *United States*.

## you gotta know

- Established an unassailable record of two hundred career victories in NASCAR's premier Cup Series, finishing ninety-five wins ahead of his nearest challenger, *David Pearson*.
- Won seven NASCAR Cup Series national championships in 1964, 1967, 1971, 1972, 1974, 1975, and 1979, a benchmark matched only by *Dale Earnhardt* and *Jimmie Johnson*.
- Captured the prestigious *Daytona 500*—known as The Great American Race—a record seven times, scoring victories in 1964, 1966, 1971, 1973, 1974, 1979, and 1981.
- Executed the most dominant single season in motorsport history in 1967, capturing twenty-seven race victories, including an astonishing streak of ten consecutive race wins.
- Drove primarily for his family-operated organization *Petty Enterprises*, founded by his father *Lee Petty*, campaigning the iconic number forty-three *Plymouth Superbird* and *Dodge Magnum*.
- Survived numerous severe crashes during his career, including a violent tumble along the frontstretch wall at Darlington Raceway in 1970 that led NASCAR to mandate protective nylon window nets.
- Secured his two-hundredth and final Cup Series triumph at the 1984 Firecracker 400 at Daytona International Speedway with President *Ronald Reagan* watching in person as the first sitting president to attend a NASCAR race.
- Received the Presidential Medal of Freedom from President *George H. W. Bush* in 1992, becoming the first automotive racing driver in history to earn the nation's highest civilian honor.
