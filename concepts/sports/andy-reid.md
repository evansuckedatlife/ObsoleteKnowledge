## summary

**Andy Reid**, affectionately nicknamed **Big Red**, is an American football coach who established himself as one of the most prolific offensive minds in the history of the [[national-football-league]]. Rising through the coaching ranks under *Mike Holmgren* with the *Green Bay Packers*, he served as the head coach of the *Philadelphia Eagles* for fourteen seasons before taking the helm of the *Kansas City Chiefs* in 2013. Across his storied career in the [[united-states]], he revolutionized West Coast offensive systems, mentored franchise quarterbacks including *Patrick Mahomes*, and captured multiple [[super-bowl]] championships to cement his status as a legendary offensive tactician.

## you gotta know

- Led the *Kansas City Chiefs* to three [[super-bowl]] titles (*LIV*, *LVII*, and *LVIII*) alongside quarterback *Patrick Mahomes*, engineering an era of sustained dominance across the [[national-football-league]].
- Served as head coach of the *Philadelphia Eagles* from 1999 to 2012, guiding the team to five *NFC Championship Games* and an appearance in *Super Bowl XXXIX*, where they fell to quarterback [[tom-brady]] and the *New England Patriots*.
- Renowned for building an expansive coaching tree across professional football, having mentored successful head coaches such as *John Harbaugh*, *Doug Pederson*, *Ron Rivera*, *Sean McDermott*, and *Matt Nagy*.
- Began his professional coaching ascent as an offensive assistant and quarterbacks coach under *Mike Holmgren* in *Green Bay*, helping tutor *Brett Favre* during a victory in *Super Bowl XXXI*.
- Developed a high-scoring, motion-heavy offensive scheme that emphasizes pass-catching running backs, run-pass options, and elite physical [[athleticism]] in space to exploit defensive coverage schemes.
- Played offensive tackle collegiately at *Brigham Young University* (*BYU*) under legendary coach *LaVell Edwards* after transferring from *Glendale Community College*.
- Appeared as a youth competitor in the *1971 Punt, Pass, and Kick* competition broadcast on national television, creating an iconic clip where his imposing frame dwarfed fellow youth participants.
