## summary

**American culture** encompasses the shared values, practices, and artistic traditions that define identity in the *United States*, from mass entertainment and sports spectacle to individualism and democratic ideals. It evolved through immigration, industrialization, and global influence, creating a distinctive blend of pragmatism, innovation, and self-reinvention. Major institutions like professional sports, television, and advertising have become central to how Americans express and consume culture.

## you gotta know

- American culture emphasizes individual achievement, upward mobility, and meritocracy as core values.
- Sports like football and baseball function as cultural rituals that unite Americans across regions and social divides.
- Television and mass media transformed American culture in the mid-20th century, creating shared national moments and celebrities.
- Advertising and consumer capitalism are deeply woven into American cultural identity, defining aspirations and lifestyle.
- The *Super Bowl* represents a quintessential American phenomenon: a sporting event that also functions as a cultural and commercial spectacle.
- American performance arts, from jazz to modern dance, emerged from blending immigrant traditions with local innovation.
- Diversity and pluralism are paradoxically both central to American self-image and sites of ongoing cultural tension.
- The concept of the "American Dream"—the belief that hard work and determination can overcome any obstacle—has shaped cultural narratives and immigration patterns for centuries.
- Hollywood and the film industry became primary exporters of American cultural values globally, creating an outsized influence on international perceptions of *United States* society.
- Fast food chains and consumer brands became symbols of American culture, representing both innovation and homogenization critiques.
