## summary

The **San Diego Chargers** were an American football franchise based in San Diego, California, competing in the NFL from 1961 to 2016. Born from the defunct American Football League, they became one of the league's most dynamic offensive teams, particularly in the early 2000s when led by quarterback [[philip-rivers]]. Despite their explosive play and passionate fan base, the franchise left San Diego for Los Angeles in 2017, ending a 56-year chapter in the city's sports history.

## you gotta know

- Established in 1960 as a founding member of the American Football League (AFL); joined the NFL after the [[afl-nfl-merger|AFL-NFL merger]] in 1970, representing a pivotal moment in professional football consolidation.
- Won their lone Super Bowl appearance in 1994 (Super Bowl XXIX), though they lost to the [[san-francisco-49ers]], marking the franchise's greatest disappointment despite a roster of Hall-of-Fame talent.
- Built a dynasty in the early 2000s around quarterback [[philip-rivers]], winning multiple AFC West titles and reaching multiple playoffs despite rarely reaching the Super Bowl and frustrating a dedicated fan base.
- Known for explosive offenses featuring [[drew-brees|Drew Brees]] in the late 1990s and early 2000s, setting franchise passing records before Brees left for [[new-orleans-saints|New Orleans]] and pursued his own championship dreams.
- Quarterback [[philip-rivers|Philip Rivers]] defined the franchise from 2004–2019, holding numerous team records and leading multiple division-winning seasons while becoming a symbol of individual excellence without ultimate team success.
- Played in two home stadiums: the historic Qualcomm Stadium (shared with the San Diego Padres) and later the newer Dignity Health Sports Park, reflecting the team's evolution.
- Relocated to [[los-angeles-chargers|Los Angeles]] in 2017 after decades of contentious negotiations with the city, becoming a shared tenant with the Los Angeles Rams and leaving a loyal fanbase behind.
