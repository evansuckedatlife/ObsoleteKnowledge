## summary

**CONCACAF**, officially the **Confederation of North, Central America and Caribbean Association Football**, is the continental governing body for association football across North America, Central America, and the Caribbean basin, along with the South American nations of Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana. Founded in September 1961 through the merger of the *NAFC* and the *CCCF* in Mexico City, the organization operates as one of *FIFA*'s six continental confederations and oversees premier regional tournaments including the biennial *Gold Cup* and the *CONCACAF Champions Cup*. The confederation is historically dominated by the fierce rivalry between Mexico and the [[united-states]], while also serving as a major focal point during international anti-corruption investigations that reshaped modern global sports administration.

## you gotta know

- Formed in 1961 in Mexico City through the unification of the *North American Football Confederation* (*NAFC*) and the *Confederation of Central American and Caribbean Football* (*CCCF*), creating a unified administrative body under *FIFA*.
- The confederation's flagship tournament for senior men's national teams is the *CONCACAF Gold Cup*, inaugurated under that name in 1991 to replace the older *CONCACAF Championship*; Mexico has claimed the most titles, closely followed by the [[united-states]], with Canada being the only other nation to win the modern tournament.
- Club competitions are headlined by the *CONCACAF Champions Cup* (formerly the *CONCACAF Champions League*), historically dominated by Mexican clubs from *Liga MX*, such as *Club América* and *Cruz Azul*, before the Seattle Sounders broke their continental streak in 2022.
- The rivalry between the United States and Mexico anchors regional competition, famously dramatized by the recurring two-nil scoreline known as *Dos a Cero*, which occurred in multiple World Cup qualifying matches in Columbus, Ohio, as well as the round of sixteen at the 2002 World Cup.
- The federation includes several non-independent territories affiliated due to geographical and political ties, such as Martinique, Guadeloupe, and French Guiana, which maintain connections to [[france]] and compete regionally without full independent membership in *FIFA*.
- Three South American nations—Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana—compete in this confederation rather than *CONMEBOL* because of cultural, linguistic, and historical links to the Caribbean stemming from European [[colonialism]].
- The governing body was thrust into international prominence during the 2015 *FIFA corruption case*, which saw former confederation presidents *Jack Warner*, *Jeffrey Webb*, and *Alfredo Hawit* arrested or indicted by the Department of Justice in the [[united-states]].
- Hosted the global spectacle when the [[united-states]] staged the 1994 tournament, and the confederation jointly hosts the expanded 2026 tournament alongside Canada and Mexico across North American metropolitan venues.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — home of the confederation's administrative headquarters in Miami and one of its two dominant national teams.
- [[france]] — sovereign nation associated with non-sovereign regional participants Martinique, Guadeloupe, and French Guiana.
- [[spain]] — primary linguistic and cultural heritage of most Central American and Mexican member nations.
- [[england]] — birthplace of modern association football codes adopted across all continental confederations.
- [[olympic-games]] — global multi-sport competition for which the confederation organizes regional under-twenty-three qualifying tournaments.
- [[colonialism]] — historical phenomenon explaining why Anglophone Guyana and Dutch-speaking Suriname joined this Caribbean-aligned confederation.
