## summary

The **Lou Marsh Award**, officially renamed the *Northern Star Award* in 2022, is an annual trophy awarded to Canada's top athlete of the year, chosen by a panel of sports journalists. Established in 1936 by the *Toronto Star* to honour its former sports editor, the prize recognises both professional and amateur competitors across all athletic disciplines. It stands as one of the most prestigious individual honours in Canadian sporting culture.

## you gotta know

- Established in 1936 in memory of *Lou Marsh*, a pioneering athlete, referee, and longtime sports editor of the *Toronto Star* newspaper.
- Renamed the Northern Star Award in November 2022 following public scrutiny regarding historical racial commentary and derogatory language found in Marsh's published writings.
- Voted upon each December by a dedicated committee of Canadian sports journalists and broadcasters representing nationwide media outlets.
- First presented in 1936 to track and field star *Phil Edwards*, a bronze medalist in middle-distance running across three consecutive Olympic competitions.
- Won a record four times by hockey superstar *Wayne Gretzky*, who took home the honour in 1982, 1983, 1985, and 1989.
- Awarded across a diverse spectrum of sports, honouring figures such as golfer *Mike Weir*, basketball icon *Steve Nash*, sprinter *Donovan Bailey*, and swimmer *Penny Oleksiak*.
- Historically presented as a distinctive black marble trophy with a gold-plated medallion celebrating Canadian athletic preeminence.
