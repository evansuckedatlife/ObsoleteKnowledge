## summary

**Ultimate Frisbee**, officially recognized simply as **Ultimate**, is a non-contact team field sport played with a flying disc between two squads of seven players. Invented in 1968 by high school students in Maplewood, New Jersey, the game combines the continuous movement and athletic endurance of soccer with the passing and scoring dynamics of American football. It is uniquely celebrated for its foundational governing ethos, the *Spirit of the Game*, which mandates highly competitive play governed entirely by self-officiation without referees.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1968 at Columbia High School in Maplewood, New Jersey, within the *United States*, by a group of students led by future film producer *Joel Silver*.
- Governed universally by the concept of the *Spirit of the Game*, placing the responsibility for fair play, foul calls, and rule enforcement directly on the players rather than on impartial referees.
- Points are scored when a team completes a pass into the opponent's designated end zone, similar to a touchdown in gridiron football.
- Players holding the disc are forbidden from running; they must establish a pivot foot and advance the disc strictly through throwing to teammates within a ten-second stall count.
- A turnover occurs immediately whenever a pass touches the ground, is caught out-of-bounds, or is intercepted or knocked down by a defender, creating rapid transitions between offense and defense.
- Modern international governance is overseen by the *World Flying Disc Federation* (WFDF), which has achieved formal recognition from the International Olympic Committee.
- Elite competition features distinct specialized throws such as the backhand, forehand flick, hammer, and scoober, paired with intricate offensive structures like vertical and horizontal stacks.
