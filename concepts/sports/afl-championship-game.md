## summary

The **AFL Championship Game**, officially titled the **American Football League Championship Game**, was the annual postseason title contest that decided the champion of the American Football League from 1960 through 1969. Contested between the winners of the league's Eastern and Western Divisions, the game showcased competitive excellence throughout the division-dominated era before the circuit blended into the broader professional football landscape. Following the historic agreement that prompted the [[afl-nfl-merger]], the final four winners advanced directly to face the champion of the [[national-football-league]] in what became celebrated as the [[super-bowl]].

## you gotta know

- Inaugural title clash occurred on New Year's Day in 1961, where the *Houston Oilers* edged the *Los Angeles Chargers* 24-16 behind veteran quarterback *George Blanda*, establishing the league's high-scoring aerial identity.
- The 1962 title contest between the *Dallas Texans* and *Houston Oilers* became the longest professional football game played up to that time, requiring two full overtime periods before *Tommy Brooker* kicked a game-winning field goal for *Dallas*.
- The 1962 contest is famously remembered for the coin toss blunder by *Texans* captain *Abner Haynes*, who erroneously declared "We'll kick to the clock," inadvertently giving *Houston* both the wind advantage and the football.
- Running back *Keith Lincoln* of the *San Diego Chargers* recorded an unprecedented 329 yards from scrimmage during the 1963 championship victory against the *Boston Patriots*, an offensive explosion highlighting the tactical innovations of head coach *Sid Gillman*.
- The *Buffalo Bills*, anchored by quarterback *Jack Kemp* and a stout defense, captured consecutive league crowns in 1964 and 1965 by repeatedly stifling the high-powered offense of *San Diego*.
- The final four AFL champions represented the league in the first four installments of the [[super-bowl]], highlighted by the 1968 *New York Jets* who defeated the *Oakland Raiders* for the AFL crown before upsetting *Baltimore* in [[new-york-city]] sports lore.
- The *Kansas City Chiefs* defeated the *Oakland Raiders* 17-7 in the final edition played in January 1970, concluding ten championship clashes before both franchises officially entered the unified [[national-football-league]].
