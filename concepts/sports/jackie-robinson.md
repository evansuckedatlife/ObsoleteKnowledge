## summary

**Jackie Robinson**, born **Jack Roosevelt Robinson**, was an American baseball infielder and societal pioneer who dismantled Major League Baseball's twentieth-century racial barrier when he debuted for the *Brooklyn Dodgers* on April 15, 1947. A dynamic base runner, six-time All-Star, and 1949 National League Most Valuable Player, his exceptional athletic exploits were matched by his immense psychological courage in the face of violent racial animus. His historic integration of the national pastime stood as a foundational milestone that heralded and invigorated the broader [[civil-rights-movement]].

## you gotta know

- Shattered the six-decade-old baseball color line on April 15, 1947, taking the field at first base for the *Brooklyn Dodgers* at *Ebbets Field* and ending the era of racial exclusion.
- Recruited by visionary Dodgers president *Branch Rickey*, who sought an individual with the athletic mastery and moral fortitude to resist retaliating against virulent racism from opposing players, umpires, and fans.
- Captured the inaugural Major League Baseball Rookie of the Year award in 1947, an accolade subsequently rechristened the Jackie Robinson Award in his honor fifty years later.
- Earned the 1949 National League Most Valuable Player award after leading the league with a .342 batting average and stealing 37 bases, propelling Brooklyn to the National League pennant.
- Propelled the *Brooklyn Dodgers* to six National League pennants and the franchise's solitary Brooklyn championship triumph in 1955, finally dethroning their perennial crosstown rivals, [[the-new-york-yankees]].
- Lettered in four distinct varsity sports at UCLA—track and field, basketball, football, and baseball—becoming the first student-athlete in the institution's history to achieve that distinction.
- Stood court-martialed as an officer in the [[united-states]] Army during [[world-war-ii]] for refusing an order to move to the back of an unsegregated military bus in Texas, winning total acquittal before receiving an honorable discharge.
- Honored posthumously in 1997 when Major League Baseball orchestrated the universal retirement of his uniform number 42 across all major league clubs, an unprecedented honor in professional team sports commemorated annually on Jackie Robinson Day.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — historic campaign for racial equity in which Robinson served as both an early sports trailblazer and an active, outspoken political organizer alongside Martin Luther King Jr.
- [[new-york-city]] — metropolitan hub where Robinson attained sporting immortality at Brooklyn's *Ebbets Field* and later founded the minority-owned *Freedom National Bank* in Harlem.
- [[the-new-york-yankees]] — crosstown subway series adversaries against whom Robinson's Dodgers fought in six tense World Series clashes during the golden age of New York baseball.
- [[ichiro-suzuki]] — international baseball trailblazer whose later transition to the major leagues expanded the international and cultural boundaries pioneered by Robinson's breakthrough.
- [[babe-ruth]] — iconic baseball titan whose towering athletic legacy was rivaled in cultural and social importance by Robinson's transformation of the American racial landscape.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict during which Robinson served as a second lieutenant in the United States military, establishing his early public resistance to racial segregation.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — legal cornerstone whose guarantees of equal protection formed the intellectual and constitutional bedrock of Robinson's advocacy for civil rights.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose cultural consciousness and segregated civic institutions Robinson profoundly reshaped through nonviolent athletic determination.
