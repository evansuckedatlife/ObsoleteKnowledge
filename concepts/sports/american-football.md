## summary

**American football** is a collision sport played primarily in [[united-states|North America]], featuring two teams of eleven players competing to advance an oval ball down a 100-yard field toward the opponent's end zone. Born in the late 19th century from a fusion of [[rugby]] and soccer, it has evolved into one of the world's most watched and commercially powerful sports, with the [[national-football-league|NFL]] standing as its professional apex. The sport's combination of strategic complexity, athleticism, and cultural significance has made it central to American identity and entertainment.

## you gotta know

- Played with an oval ball on a 100-yard field marked by goal lines and end zones; each team must either score (six points for touchdown, three for field goal) or prevent the opponent from advancing 10 yards to retain the ball.
- Employs a "down" system where a team gets four attempts to advance the ball 10 yards; failure results in a turnover on downs, while success gives four fresh attempts.
- Positions divide into offensive (quarterback, running backs, receivers, offensive line) and defensive units (defensive line, linebackers, defensive backs), with specialization driving modern strategy.
- [[super-bowl|The Super Bowl]] championship game is the most-watched annual sporting event in [[united-states|the United States]], commanding enormous television audiences and advertising rates.
- College football predates the professional game and remains deeply embedded in American university culture, with some rivalries (Alabama–Auburn, Ohio State–Michigan) and bowl games (Rose, Orange, Sugar) rivaling the NFL in cultural importance.
- Evolved from 19th-century rugby and association football (soccer), with American innovators codifying scrimmage-based play, the forward pass, and the down system that distinguish it from rugby.
