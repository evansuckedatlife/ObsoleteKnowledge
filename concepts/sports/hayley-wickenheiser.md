## summary

**Hayley Wickenheiser** is a Canadian physician, sports executive, and former professional athlete widely regarded as the greatest player in the history of women's [[ice-hockey]]. Over a monumental international career spanning more than two decades, she led the Canadian national team to four consecutive gold medals and one silver medal at the [[olympic-games]]. A pioneering trailblazer, she became the first woman to play professional men's hockey as an outfield skater in Europe and later transitioned into prominent executive roles in the *National Hockey League*.

## you gotta know

- Debuted for the Canadian national women's team at age fifteen in 1994, remaining the cornerstone of Canada's international dominance for twenty-three seasons until her retirement in 2017.
- Captured four consecutive gold medals at the [[olympic-games]] (Salt Lake City 2002, Turin 2006, Vancouver 2010, and Sochi 2014), alongside an initial silver medal at Nagano 1998.
- Named tournament Most Valuable Player at both the 2002 and 2006 Winter Olympics, finishing her illustrious career as the all-time leading scorer in Olympic women's hockey history with 51 points.
- Broke international gender barriers in 2003 by signing with *HC Salamat* in Finland's men's division, becoming the first woman to score a goal in a men's professional league in a non-goaltender role.
- Competed in softball at the 2000 Summer Olympics in Sydney, Australia, joining a select group of dual-season Olympians from Canada.
- Inducted into the *Hockey Hall of Fame* in 2019 in her very first year of eligibility and received appointment as an Officer of the *Order of Canada*.
- Earned a medical doctorate from the *University of Calgary* and assumed high-ranking front-office responsibilities as an Assistant General Manager for the *Toronto Maple Leafs*.
