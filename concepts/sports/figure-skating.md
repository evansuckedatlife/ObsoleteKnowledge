## summary

**Figure skating** is an artistic winter sport in which individuals, pairs, or groups perform choreographed routines on ice wearing bladed skates, judged on technical execution and presentation. Developed from recreational gliding into a formal competitive discipline in the nineteenth century, it became the first winter sport contested at the *[[olympic-games]]*. It combines high-level athleticism, such as multi-revolution rotational jumps and intricate footwork, with musical interpretation and theatrical aesthetics, serving as one of the premier spectator events at international multi-sport competitions.

## you gotta know

- Contested initially at the 1908 Summer Olympics in *London* and the 1920 Summer Olympics in *Antwerp* before becoming a permanent, marquee cornerstone of the inaugural Winter *[[olympic-games]]* in *Chamonix*, *[[france]]* in 1924.
- Recognizes six major jumping maneuvers divided into edge jumps (*Axel*, *Salchow*, *loop*) and toe-assisted jumps (*toe loop*, *flip*, *Lutz*), with the *Axel*—invented by *Axel Paulsen*—being the only jump entered from a forward outside edge.
- Compulsory figures, geometric patterns traced meticulously on ice that gave the sport its name, constituted a major portion of competitive scoring for decades until their elimination from elite international competition in 1990.
- Dominated historically by several distinct eras, notably the three consecutive Olympic ladies' titles of Norwegian superstar *Sonja Henie* (1928–1936), who revolutionized the discipline by integrating balletic choreography, short skirts, and showmanship.
- Polarized by intense international and geopolitical rivalries, particularly during the *[[cold-war]]* when the *[[soviet-union]]* and later *Russia* maintained an unprecedented forty-four-year streak of winning Olympic gold in the pairs event from 1964 through 2006.
- Shaken by major scandals, including the infamous 1994 assault on *Nancy Kerrigan* involving associates of rival *Tonya Harding*, and the 2002 Salt Lake City judging scandal in pairs skating that prompted the replacement of the traditional 6.0 scoring system with the cumulative ISU Judging System.
- Modern competition features men's singles, women's singles, pair skating, ice dance (which emphasizes rhythm, edges, and lift technique over acrobatic jumps), and the team event introduced at the 2014 Winter Olympics in *Sochi*.
- Pushed to historic technical boundaries in the twenty-first century through the proliferation of quadruple jumps by skaters such as *Yuzuru Hanyu*, *Nathan Chen*, and *Ilia Malinin*, who landed the first fully rotated quadruple Axel in international competition in 2022.

## connections

- [[olympic-games]] — the premier global multi-sport competition where the discipline originated as an Olympic event and commands massive international viewership.
- [[ice-hockey]] — fellow ice rink discipline sharing the winter arena infrastructure while contrasting collective physical contact with individual artistic precision.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical ideological contest that turned international judging panels and athletic medal counts into symbolic state battles.
- [[soviet-union]] — dominant superpower whose state-sponsored sports academies produced legendary champions, especially in pairs and ice dance.
- [[united-states]] — nation with a storied lineage of Olympic singles gold medalists including *Dick Button*, *Peggy Fleming*, *Dorothy Hamill*, and *Kristi Yamaguchi*.
- [[france]] — host of the inaugural 1924 Chamonix Winter Games and home to pioneering competitive clubs and champions.
- [[entertainment]] — commercial spectacle industry that absorbed retired Olympic champions through lucrative touring ice shows such as *Ice Follies* and *Stars on Ice*.
