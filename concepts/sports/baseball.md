## summary

**Baseball** is a bat-and-ball sport played between two teams of nine players, typically across nine innings on a diamond-shaped field with four bases. Emerging in 19th-century [[united-states|America]] as a refinement of earlier bat-and-ball games, it became enshrined as the nation's pastime and claims a central place in American culture, history, and identity. The sport's blend of individual achievement, strategic team play, and statistical depth has generated the world's most voluminous sports literature and analysis.

## you gotta know

- Played on a diamond with four bases (home, first, second, third); the batting team scores a run by hitting the ball and circling all bases, while the fielding team records outs through strikeouts, force plays, or tag plays.
- Each batter faces a pitcher throwing from a mound; hitters accumulate three strikes to be struck out or draw walks from four balls, making plate discipline and pitch selection central to the game.
- The [[world-series|World Series]] championship, first played in 1903, is the oldest championship series in North American professional sports and remains baseball's supreme competition.
- Professional baseball in [[united-states|the United States]] has been organized under the [[major-league-baseball|Major League Baseball]] (MLB) structure since the late 19th century, with the American League and National League competing for prestige.
- Iconic franchises like the [[the-new-york-yankees|New York Yankees]], Boston Red Sox, and Los Angeles Dodgers have accumulated deep histories spanning generations, creating powerful traditions and passionate regional identities.
- The sport's statistical sophistication—earned run average, batting average, wins above replacement, sabermetrics—has made it an intellectual pursuit, with baseball's numbers enabling deeper analysis than nearly any other sport.
- Evolution from earlier bat-and-ball games (rounders, cricket) into its modern form occurred across the 19th century, with the Knickerbocker Club codifying standardized rules in 1845 that became foundational to modern play.
