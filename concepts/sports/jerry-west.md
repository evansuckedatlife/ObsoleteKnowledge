## summary

**Jerry West**, fully named **Jerome Alan West**, was an iconic American basketball player, coach, and front-office executive whose silhouette was immortalized as the official silhouette of the National Basketball Association. Known as **Mr. Clutch** for his late-game heroics and nicknamed **The Logo**, he starred for the Los Angeles Lakers across fourteen seasons after winning an Olympic gold medal for the *[[united-states]]* in 1960. Later transforming into one of the greatest general managers in modern sports history, he architected the dominant "Showtime" Lakers and built dynasties that defined decades of professional basketball.

## you gotta know

- Inspired the globally recognized red, white, and blue silhouette of the NBA logo designed by *Alan Siegel* in 1969, cementing his everlasting identity as **The Logo**.
- Earned the enduring moniker **Mr. Clutch** due to his ice-blooded scoring in high-pressure moments, most famously sinking a sixty-foot desperation buzzer-beater in Game Three of the 1970 NBA Finals.
- Remains the only player in basketball history to be named NBA Finals Most Valuable Player while playing on the losing team, earning the inaugural award in 1969.
- Led the Los Angeles Lakers to the historic 1971–72 NBA championship, a magical season that included an all-time professional sports record of thirty-three consecutive victories.
- Won a gold medal as co-captain with *Oscar Robertson* on the undefeated American men's basketball squad at the 1960 Summer *[[olympic-games]]* in Rome.
- Reached the NBA Finals nine times during his playing career, enduring agonizing championship losses primarily to the Boston Celtics before finally capturing his elusive title ring in 1972.
- Served as general manager of the Los Angeles Lakers, assembling the famed "Showtime" era with *Magic Johnson* and *Kareem Abdul-Jabbar* before orchestrating the 1996 acquisitions of *Shaquille O'Neal* and *Kobe Bryant*.
- Enshrined into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame three times: as an individual player, as a member of the 1960 Olympic team, and as a contributor.
