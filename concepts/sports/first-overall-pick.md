## summary

The **first overall pick** (commonly called the **number one pick** or **No. 1 overall pick**) is the inaugural selection made by a sports franchise during an entry draft, traditionally awarded to the team with the poorest regular-season record to promote competitive balance. Established in the [[united-states]] by the [[national-football-league]] in 1936 before spreading across North American professional leagues, this selection represents an enormous franchise-altering asset burdened by intense public scrutiny. The status of being chosen first carries significant commercial prestige, record rookie contracts, and enduring historical debate regarding generational greatness versus notorious draft busts.

## you gotta know

- Conceived by league executive *Bert Bell* for the 1936 [[national-football-league]] draft, where *Jay Berwanger*, the inaugural *Heisman Trophy* winner from the *University of Chicago*, was selected first overall by the *Philadelphia Eagles* but never played professional football.
- Operates as the foundational mechanism of reverse-order competitive parity in American team sports, contrasting sharply with the meritocratic open qualifiers and individual rankings characteristic of [[golf-as-sport]] and [[grass-court-tennis]].
- Frequently produces franchise cornerstones and championship architects, exemplified by Hall of Fame quarterback *Peyton Manning* ([[peyton-manning]]), who was selected first overall by the *Indianapolis Colts* in 1998 and led them to victory in the [[super-bowl]].
- Generated one of the most consequential draft dilemmas in sports history in 1998 when Indianapolis weighed *Peyton Manning* against *Ryan Leaf*, the latter chosen second overall by the [[san-diego-chargers]] before becoming widely regarded as one of sports history's biggest disappointments.
- Prompted historic draft-day standoffs by elite prospects refusing to sign with the drafting franchise, notably *John Elway* in 1983 and *Eli Manning* in 2004, both of whom forced blockbuster trades to alternate destinations.
- Expanded into [[ice-hockey]] via the *National Hockey League*, where legendary superstars such as *Mario Lemieux* and *Sidney Crosby* were selected first overall to rescue struggling franchises and capture the [[stanley-cup]].
- Prompted the creation of weighted draft lotteries in modern sports leagues to discourage deliberate losing, or "tanking," by organizations attempting to guarantee themselves the premier collegiate prospect.

## connections

- [[national-football-league]] — the pioneering professional organization that instituted the annual amateur draft system in 1936.
- [[peyton-manning]] — legendary quarterback whose selection at the top of the 1998 draft redefined his franchise and validated the pick's potential.
- [[san-diego-chargers]] — franchise that held the top draft position in 2004 and engineered a blockbuster trade involving *Eli Manning*.
- [[super-bowl]] — the ultimate championship contest that struggling franchises hope to reach by drafting a generational top selection.
- [[ice-hockey]] — professional team sport that adopted draft lotteries to award premier amateur talent pursuing the [[stanley-cup]].
- [[golf-as-sport]] — individual sport governed by qualification exemptions rather than centralized amateur entry drafts.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose closed-league sports architecture popularized collegiate drafts and competitive parity regulations.
