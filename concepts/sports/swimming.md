## summary

**Swimming** is a propulsion-based water sport and life skill in which athletes propel themselves through water using coordinated physical movements of the arms, legs, and torso. Documented across antiquity from prehistoric cave art to classical civilizations, **competitive swimming** emerged as an organized modern sport in nineteenth-century England before becoming a cornerstone of the modern *[[olympic-games]]*. Alongside *[[surfing]]*, it anchors global aquatic culture, testing cardiovascular endurance, stroke biomechanics, and raw sprint speed across four regulated competitive strokes.

## you gotta know

- Contested at every modern Summer *[[olympic-games]]* since the revival in Athens in 1896, standing alongside track and field athletics as the most watched and prestigious Olympic disciplines.
- Standard international pool competitions feature four recognized strokes: freestyle (typically swum using the front crawl), backstroke, breaststroke, and butterfly, as well as individual medley and relay formats.
- Dominated historically by American swimmer *Michael Phelps*, the most decorated Olympian of all time, who captured twenty-eight total Olympic medals, including twenty-three gold medals, between 2004 and 2016.
- Prominent American pioneer *Duke Kahanamoku* won three Olympic gold medals in freestyle sprint events before traveling the world to popularize the sister aquatic sport of *[[surfing]]*.
- The sport is governed internationally by World Aquatics (formerly FINA), which oversees regulations on technical equipment, including the high-profile ban on hydrodynamic polyurethane *tech suits* following the 2008–2009 record blitz.
- Open-water swimming has developed into a grueling Olympic discipline centered on the ten-kilometer marathon swim, contested in open oceans, bays, and rowing basins subject to tides, weather, and currents.
- American standout *Mark Spitz* set a long-standing benchmark at the 1972 Munich Games by winning seven gold medals, each in world record time, a record that stood until Phelps's eight golds at Beijing in 2008.

## connections

- [[surfing]] — sister aquatic sport sharing cultural icons like *Duke Kahanamoku* and foundational water safety and wave-riding skills.
- [[olympic-games]] — the premier international sporting spectacle where swimming has been contested continuously since 1896.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the ancient culture where swimming was prized as a fundamental mark of education, as reflected in the proverb about being unable to read or swim.
- [[united-states]] — the dominant international powerhouse in competitive swimming, producing record-breaking champions like *Michael Phelps* and *Mark Spitz*.
- [[japan]] — a traditional aquatic power whose swimmers have claimed Olympic glory and who hosted historic aquatic showdowns at the Tokyo Games.
- [[france]] — European swimming center producing Olympic champions such as *Léon Marchand* and *Laure Manaudou*, and host of the Paris Games.
- [[australian-open]] — iconic Melbourne sports championship representing Australia, an international swimming powerhouse known for rivalries with American swimmers.
