## summary

**Mike Bossy** was a Canadian professional *ice hockey* right winger who starred for the *New York Islanders* during their historic dynasty in the early 1980s. Widely celebrated as one of the purest, most lethal goal scorers in the history of the *National Hockey League*, he helped drive his team to four consecutive *Stanley Cup* championships. He holds the record for the most consecutive 50-goal seasons in league history and finished his career with the highest goals-per-game average of any player with at least 200 appearances. Despite a career prematurely truncated by severe back injuries, his clinical marksmanship, sportsmanship, and postseason clutch scoring remain legendary in North American sports.

## you gotta know

- Led the *New York Islanders* dynasty to four consecutive [[stanley-cup]] championships from 1980 through 1983, anchoring the most dominant era in franchise history.
- Scored 50 or more goals in nine consecutive campaigns, establishing an unbroken *NHL* record that commenced with his sensational rookie season in 1977–78.
- Became only the second skater in league annals, after *Maurice Richard*, to achieve the storied feat of scoring 50 goals in 50 games, clinching the milestone on January 24, 1981, against the *Quebec Nordiques*.
- Won the *Calder Memorial Trophy* as the rookie of the year after netting 53 goals, breaking the previous first-year goal-scoring record.
- Claimed the *Conn Smythe Trophy* as playoff most valuable player in 1982 and scored the [[stanley-cup]]-winning goal in both the 1982 and 1983 finals.
- Formed the famous "Trio Grande" forward line alongside Hall of Fame center *Bryan Trottier* and power forward *Clark Gillies*, dominating opposing defenses across [[new-york-city]].
- Captured the *Lady Byng Memorial Trophy* three times for gentlemanly conduct and high sportsmanship, consistently refusing to engage in fighting during a notoriously physical era.
- Retired at age thirty in 1987 due to chronic back pain, finishing with 573 goals in just 752 regular season contests for an all-time record average of 0.762 goals per game.
