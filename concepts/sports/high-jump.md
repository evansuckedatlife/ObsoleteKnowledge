## summary

The **high jump** is a core track and field athletic event in which competitors sprint toward an elevated horizontal bar and leap unassisted over it onto a cushioned landing mat. Contested at the modern Olympic Games since their inception in 1896, the event underwent a biomechanical revolution in the late 1960s with the invention of the backward-arching **Fosbury flop**. Today, high jumping is governed worldwide by World Athletics and stands as one of the definitive measurements of explosive human verticality.

## you gotta know

- Revolutionized at the 1968 Mexico City Olympic Games by American athlete Dick Fosbury, who unveiled the **Fosbury flop**, jumping headfirst over the bar with his back facing the ground.
- Allowed by the development of thick foam landing mats in the 1960s, which made landing safely on the back and shoulders possible after decades of hazardous sand or sawdust pits.
- Dominated before 1968 by upright or belly-down methods, including the nineteenth-century **scissors jump**, the Eastern cut-off, the Western roll, and the **straddle technique**.
- Held in men's competition at the inaugural 1896 Athens Olympic Games and added to women's competition at the 1928 Amsterdam Olympic Games.
- Governed by rules that require competitors to take off from a single foot; taking off with both feet together results in an immediate foul.
- Features world records set by Cuban jumper Javier Sotomayor (2.45 meters / 8 ft 0.5 in, set in 1993) and Ukrainian jumper Yaroslava Mahuchikh (2.10 meters / 6 ft 10.75 in, set in 2024).
- Provides each athlete three successive attempts to clear a given height; three consecutive failures at any height eliminate the jumper from the competition.
- Famously concluded at the 2020 Tokyo Olympics when Mutaz Essa Barshim of Qatar and Gianmarco Tamberi of Italy elected to share the Olympic gold medal rather than contest a jump-off.
