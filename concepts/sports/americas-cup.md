## summary

The **America's Cup**, affectionately known as the **Auld Mug**, is the oldest continuously contested international trophy in modern sport, predating the modern *[[olympic-games]]* by forty-five years. First contested in 1851 off the southern coast of *[[england]]*, the competition takes the form of a head-to-head match race between the defending yacht club and an officially recognized international challenger. Combining elite seamanship with cutting-edge naval architecture, commercial prestige, and fierce legal maneuvering, the regatta represents the pinnacle of competitive sailing.

## you gotta know

- Inaugurated on 22 August 1851 when the black-hulled schooner *America*, representing the New York Yacht Club, defeated a fleet of fifteen British yachts in a race around the Isle of Wight to claim the Royal Yacht Squadron's Hundred Guinea Cup.
- Generated the longest recorded winning streak in international sports history, as the New York Yacht Club successfully defended the trophy for 132 consecutive years from 1851 until 1983 across twenty-five defenses.
- Surrendered in historic fashion in September 1983 when the Australian challenger *Australia II*, funded by Alan Bond and skippered by John Bertrand featuring Ben Lexcen's revolutionary winged keel, defeated Dennis Conner's *Liberty* in Newport, Rhode Island.
- Governed by the *Deed of Gift*, a formal nineteenth-century trust document that establishes the parameters of challenge matches, yacht specifications, and yacht club qualifications, frequently resulting in contentious litigation in the Supreme Court of New York.
- Revolutionized modern yacht design through progressive eras, evolving from nineteenth-century schooners and massive J-Class sloops of the 1930s to Twelve-Metre class yachts, composite catamarans, and modern AC75 foiling monohulls that travel above the water at speeds exceeding fifty knots.
- Dominated in the twenty-first century by southern hemisphere syndicate Team New Zealand, Swiss syndicate Alinghi, and American syndicate Oracle Team USA, the latter pulling off an improbable 9-8 comeback in San Francisco Bay in 2013.
- Produced the famous, possibly apocryphal remark delivered to Queen Victoria when she inquired who had finished in second place behind the victorious schooner *America*: "Ah, Your Majesty, there is no second."

## connections

- [[united-states]] — country whose syndicates dominated the competition from its inception and defended the trophy continuously for over a century.
- [[england]] — host of the inaugural 1851 regatta at the Isle of Wight and home to the Royal Yacht Squadron, which originally commissioned the trophy.
- [[british-empire]] — global maritime power whose aristocracy repeatedly attempted and failed to reclaim the trophy through legendary challengers such as Sir Thomas Lipton.
- [[olympic-games]] — premier international athletic festival founded decades after the yachting challenge was already well established.
- [[new-york-city]] — metropolitan home of the New York Yacht Club, which served as the longstanding trustee and custodian of the trophy.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of rapid technological advancement and engineering metallurgy that transformed naval construction and materials for competing syndicates.
- [[france]] — European sailing nation that repeatedly fielded challengers through the *Baron Marcel Bich* campaigns in the twentieth century.
