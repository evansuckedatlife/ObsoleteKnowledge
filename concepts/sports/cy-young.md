## summary

**Cy Young**, born **Denton True Young**, was an American Major League Baseball pitcher whose extraordinary durability and dominance across twenty-two seasons established monumental pitching records. Pitching at the turn of the twentieth century in the [[united-states]], he secured 511 career victories, a total far exceeding that of any other pitcher in the history of the sport. His lasting influence on the game led Major League Baseball to create the *Cy Young Award* in 1956 to annually recognize the most outstanding pitcher in each league.

## you gotta know

- Holds the Major League Baseball all-time record for career pitching victories with 511 wins, an iconic milestone universally deemed untouchable by modern pitching standards.
- Serves as the namesake of baseball's *Cy Young Award*, introduced by Commissioner *Ford Frick* in 1956 to honor each league's preeminent pitcher.
- Hurled the first perfect game of baseball's modern era on May 5, 1904, while pitching for the *Boston Americans* against the *Philadelphia Athletics*.
- Received his famous moniker *Cy*, short for *Cyclone*, from teammates who likened his overwhelming, high-velocity fastball to a destructive storm.
- Set unbreakable Major League records for career games started (815), complete games (749), and total innings pitched (7,356) through supreme physical stamina.
- Pitched Boston to victory in the inaugural modern World Series in 1903, throwing nearly thirty innings across four appearances in the historic best-of-nine triumph over Pittsburgh.
- Inducted into the *National Baseball Hall of Fame* in Cooperstown in 1937 as a celebrated member of its second class of honorees.
- Developed his arm strength working on an Ohio farm before entering professional baseball with the *Cleveland Spiders* in 1890.
