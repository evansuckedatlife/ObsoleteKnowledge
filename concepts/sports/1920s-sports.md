## summary

The 1920s marked a transformative era in sport known as the *Golden Age of Sports*, when athletics ascended from a regional pastime to a dominant cultural force in American society. Prosperity, mass media coverage, and the emergence of transcendent athletic celebrities transformed sports into mass entertainment. This decade produced some of sport's most iconic figures and established many traditions that persist today.

## you gotta know

- American prosperity and leisure time surged in the 1920s, creating unprecedented appetite for spectator sports and athletic competition.
- Tennis became a major entertainment draw, with players like *Bill Tilden* and *Helen Wills Moody* achieving celebrity status rivaling movie stars.
- Radio broadcasting brought live sports coverage into millions of homes, making athletes into national figures beyond local fame.
- Babe Ruth's emergence as a home-run hitter in baseball transformed the sport from a tactical, inside game to an entertainment spectacle.
- Heavyweight boxing matches drew massive crowds; *Gene Tunney* and *Jack Dempsey* commanded audiences in the millions.
- Golf, tennis, and professional athletics professionalized dramatically, with sponsorships and appearance fees reshaping athlete economics.
- The era established the archetype of the sporting celebrity—charismatic, media-savvy, and commanding enormous cultural influence.
- College football drew enormous crowds; the "Four Horsemen" of Notre Dame became household names, with games attracting 50,000+ spectators.
- *Gertrude Ederle*, an Olympic swimmer, captured the public imagination by becoming the first woman to swim the English Channel in 1926.
- Spectator sports attendance and gate revenues reached unprecedented levels; stadiums grew larger to accommodate demand from passionate fans.
- Newspapers devoted entire sections to sports coverage, with sportswriters achieving literary prominence and shaping how fans understood athletic achievement.
- The professionalization of sports created a new economic ecosystem with managers, agents, and promotional figures managing athlete careers and earnings.
