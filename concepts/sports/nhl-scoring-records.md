## summary

**NHL scoring records** document the achievements of hockey's greatest offensive players, measuring goals, assists, and points across single seasons, careers, and playoff runs. These records serve as historical markers of excellence and have been shattered repeatedly as the sport evolved, the league expanded, and athletes' training became more sophisticated. Record-holders became legends whose accomplishments defined entire eras of professional hockey.

## you gotta know

- *Wayne Gretzky* holds the NHL record for all-time points with 1,963 total (894 goals, 1,963 assists), a record many consider untouchable.
- *Maurice Richard* was the first player to score 50 goals in a season (1944–45), a milestone that seemed impossible before his breakthrough.
- *Bobby Orr* holds the defenseman scoring record with 139 assists in a single season, demonstrating that scoring excellence was not confined to forwards.
- Goal-scoring records have been broken repeatedly: *Phil Esposito* held the single-season record at 76 goals in 1970–71 until *Alexander Ovechkin* surpassed it.
- *Bobby Hull* accumulated 610 career goals, a mark that stood as the record before being eclipsed by later generations of goal-scorers.
- The term "point per game" rate has become crucial to comparing players from different eras and under different league conditions.
- Modern players accumulate points more efficiently due to changes in the game: expanded rosters, higher scoring rates, and longer careers.
- *Alexander Ovechkin* became the fastest player to reach 600 career goals and continues to pursue Gretzky's all-time scoring record, keeping historical records in active conversation.
- Assist records reveal the evolution of team play; *Gordie Howe* and later *Mark Messier* excelled at creating opportunities for teammates alongside their personal scoring prowess.
- Single-season records often fluctuate based on league expansion, schedule length changes (teams historically played fewer games), and modifications to scoring systems and rules.

## connections
