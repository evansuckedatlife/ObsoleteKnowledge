## summary

**Kareem Abdul-Jabbar** is an American former professional basketball player widely considered one of the greatest athletes in the history of team sports. Over a monumental twenty-season career with the Milwaukee Bucks and Los Angeles Lakers, he won six NBA championships, a record six Most Valuable Player Awards, and retired as the league's all-time leading scorer with 38,387 points. Beyond his dominance on the court, Abdul-Jabbar is renowned for his prolific career as an author, cultural commentator, and outspoken advocate during the [[civil-rights-movement]].

## you gotta know

- Perfected the unstoppable, virtually unblockable skyhook, utilizing his towering frame, wingspan, and high release point to score consistently against any defender throughout his two-decade career.
- Won three consecutive NCAA national championships at UCLA under legendary coach *John Wooden* between 1967 and 1969, prompting the NCAA to temporarily ban the slam dunk in a futile effort to curb his dominance.
- Boycotted the 1968 Summer [[olympic-games]] in Mexico City as a principled protest against racial inequality and systemic injustice faced by African Americans in the [[united-states]].
- Converted to Sunni Islam and changed his name from *Lew Alcindor* to **Kareem Abdul-Jabbar** in 1971, shortly after leading the Milwaukee Bucks to their first NBA championship alongside *Oscar Robertson*.
- Formed the centerpiece of the glamorous 1980s *Showtime* Los Angeles Lakers alongside point guard *Magic Johnson*, winning five additional championships and the 1985 Finals MVP award at age 38.
- Co-starred alongside martial arts master *Bruce Lee* in the film *Game of Death*, engaging in an iconic fight scene showcasing his flexibility and martial arts training.
- Held the NBA's all-time regular-season scoring record for nearly four decades until *LeBron James* surpassed his mark of 38,387 points in February 2023.
