## summary

The **Centennial Olympic Park bombing** was a domestic terrorist pipe-bomb attack that took place on July 27, 1996, in Atlanta, Georgia, during the Summer Olympic Games. Detonated during a late-night concert in the public heart of the Olympic village, the blast killed one woman directly, contributed to a fatal heart attack of a cameraman, and injured over one hundred people. It is a critical event in modern history, famous both for the heroic actions and wrongful persecution of security guard *Richard Jewell* and the subsequent capture of antigovernment extremist *Eric Rudolph*.

## you gotta know

- Occurred shortly after 1:00 a.m. on July 27, 1996, at Centennial Olympic Park during a free late-night concert performed by Jack Mack and the Heart Attack.
- Security guard *Richard Jewell* discovered the suspicious green military backpack containing three large pipe bombs packed with nails and directed evacuation efforts that saved scores of lives.
- Alice Hawthorne of Albany, Georgia, was killed by flying shrapnel, while Turkish cameraman Melih Uzunyol suffered a fatal heart attack rushing to film the scene; 111 others were injured.
- Within days of being hailed as a national hero, *Richard Jewell* was wrongly leaked as the FBI's primary suspect, resulting in intense media scrutiny and libel suits before full exoneration.
- The Games continued following a brief pause and memorial ceremony, with International Olympic Committee officials and athletes resolving not to surrender to terror.
- The actual perpetrator was identified as far-right extremist *Eric Rudolph*, who also bombed abortion clinics and an Atlanta gay nightclub before spending five years hiding in the Appalachian wilderness.
- Rudolph was captured in 2003 by a rookie police officer in Murphy, North Carolina, and subsequently sentenced to multiple consecutive life terms without parole.
