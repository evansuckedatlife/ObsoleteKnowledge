## summary

**Donovan Bailey** is a Jamaican-born Canadian track and field sprinter who achieved international stardom by winning two gold medals at the 1996 Centennial Olympic Games in *Atlanta*. By capturing the men's 100-metre title in a world record time of 9.84 seconds, he claimed the unofficial mantle of "World's Fastest Man" and restored national pride to Canadian athletics in the post-Seoul era. Renowned for his explosive top-end speed and clutch championship performances, he subsequently anchored Canada's 4×100-metre relay squad to Olympic gold and secured multiple world championship crowns.

## you gotta know

- Won the 100-metre gold medal at the 1996 Olympic Games in *Atlanta*, breaking *Leroy Burrell*'s world record with a blazing time of 9.84 seconds despite a mediocre reaction time out of the starting blocks.
- Anchored the Canadian men's 4×100-metre relay team—alongside *Robert Esmie*, *Glenroy Gilbert*, and *Bruny Surin*—to Olympic gold in Atlanta, decisively defeating the favored squad from the *United States*.
- Won the 150-metre match race against American 200-metre Olympic champion *Michael Johnson* at the *SkyDome* in Toronto in June 1997, billed as the race to crown the true "World's Fastest Man," after Johnson pulled up with a quadriceps injury.
- Captured the 100-metre individual world title at the 1995 World Athletics Championships in *Gothenburg*, Sweden, establishing his emergence as the dominant global sprinter.
- Set a world indoor record in the 50-metre sprint in 1996 with a mark of 5.56 seconds in Reno, Nevada, an explosive short-distance record that stood unbroken for decades.
- Competed under the tutelage of coach *Dan Pfaff* in Austin, Texas, joining a celebrated training group that emphasized biomechanical efficiency and maximum velocity maintenance.
- Inducted twice into Canada's Sports Hall of Fame, both as an individual athlete and as a member of the triumphant 1996 Olympic 4×100-metre relay team.
