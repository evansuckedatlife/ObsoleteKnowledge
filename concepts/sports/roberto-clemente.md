## summary

**Roberto Clemente**, born *Roberto Clemente Walker*, was a Puerto Rican professional baseball right fielder who played eighteen seasons in Major League Baseball for the *Pittsburgh Pirates*. Renowned for his formidable throwing arm, exceptional hitting prowess, and passionate humanitarian advocacy, he became the first Latin American superstar elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame. His tragic death in an aviation accident while delivering earthquake relief in 1972 cemented his immortal standing as an athletic luminary and an enduring champion of human dignity.

## you gotta know

- Reached the historic milestone of exactly three thousand regular-season hits on September 30, 1972, stroking a fourth-inning double off New York Mets pitcher Jon Matlack at Three Rivers Stadium.
- Perished on December 31, 1972, at age thirty-eight when an overloaded cargo aircraft crashed into the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of Puerto Rico while carrying emergency supplies to earthquake victims in Managua, Nicaragua.
- Inducted into the National Baseball Hall of Fame in Cooperstown through a special election held in March 1973, in which the mandatory five-year post-retirement waiting period was waived.
- Guided the *Pittsburgh Pirates* to two World Series titles in 1960 and 1971, earning World Series Most Valuable Player honors in 1971 by batting .414 and hitting two home runs against the Baltimore Orioles.
- Collected twelve consecutive Gold Glove Awards from 1961 to 1972 for defensive excellence in right field, tying Willie Mays for the most outfield Gold Gloves in major league history.
- Won four National League batting championships in 1961, 1964, 1965, and 1967, and earned the 1966 National League Most Valuable Player award while wearing uniform number twenty-one.
- Confronted rampant racial and cultural prejudice throughout his career in the *United States*, openly challenging segregation in spring training facilities and rebuking journalists who Anglicized his given name.
- Memorialized by Major League Baseball through the *Roberto Clemente Award*, an annual honor presented to the player who best combines extraordinary baseball skills with outstanding philanthropic work.
