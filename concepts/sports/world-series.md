## summary

The **World Series** is the championship series of Major League Baseball, contested annually between the American League and National League champions. First played in 1903, it is a best-of-seven competition held in autumn and stands as the defining trophy of professional baseball in North America. It remains one of the most storied sporting events in the world, watched by millions and steeped in nearly a century and a half of athletic tradition.

## you gotta know

- Established in 1903 as an annual best-of-seven playoff series between league champions; the format solidified the modern baseball championship and remains the oldest professional sports championship in North America.
- The [[the-new-york-yankees|New York Yankees]] have won more World Series titles than any other franchise, dominating the mid-twentieth century with 27 championships across a century of dominance.
- [[babe-ruth|Babe Ruth]] and the Yankees defined the series in the 1920s–30s, setting the template for dynasty-building in baseball and establishing the Yankees' cultural identity as a powerhouse franchise.
- Nicknamed the "October Classic" for its traditional autumn timing, though recent expansions have occasionally pushed games into November, representing baseball's commitment to completion despite weather challenges.
- Suspended during [[world-war-i|World War I]] (1918–1919) and [[world-war-ii|World War II]] (1943–1944) due to wartime priorities, highlighting baseball's role in American civic consciousness during national crises.
- Games shifted from early afternoon slots to evening broadcasts starting in the 1970s, expanding viewership and making it a primetime spectacle that now reaches global audiences.
- The series has produced iconic moments: walk-off home runs, no-hitters, and individual heroics that define careers and haunt franchises for generations, creating a narrative tradition unmatched in American sports.
