## summary

The **javelin throw** is a premier track and field athletics event in which competitors throw a light metal spear, known as a **javelin**, as far as possible down an outfield sector. Directly descending from ancient warfare and hunting practices, the discipline was a cornerstone of the ancient Olympic pentathlon in *[[ancient-greece]]* before entering the modern *[[olympic-games]]* for men in 1908 and women in 1932. The sport tests pure kinetic transfer, explosive arm acceleration, and aerodynamic mastery, famously leading officials to redesign the implement when modern athletes began endangering stadium spectators.

## you gotta know

- Modified dramatically in 1986 after East German athlete *Uwe Hohn* threw an extraordinary world record of 104.80 meters in 1984, threatening to overshoot the athletic infield and strike spectators.
- Redesigned by the international governing body by shifting the implement's center of gravity four centimeters forward, causing the nose to pitch downward earlier in flight, reducing total distances by roughly ten percent, and ensuring a visible downward mark on the turf upon landing.
- Originated in *[[ancient-greece]]* as both a combat discipline and an athletic competition, where ancient athletes propelled the wooden shaft using an *ankyle*, a leather thong looped around the center to impart a stabilizing spin.
- Dominated across the modern era by Czech champion *Jan Železný*, who holds the current men's world record of 98.48 meters set in 1996 and captured three consecutive Olympic gold medals between 1992 and 2000.
- Executed unlike other throwing events such as the shot put or discus because athletes do not spin within a circle; throwers build velocity down a straight runway, transitioning into rhythmically complex cross-over strides before delivering the spear over the shoulder.
- Contested prominently at the 1936 Berlin *[[olympic-games]]* in *[[germany]]*, where home favourite *Gerhard Stöck* earned gold alongside track victories by American sprinter *[[jesse-owens]]*.
- Enforces strict rules requiring the athlete to hold the shaft only by its cord grip at the balance point, prohibiting turning one's back to the field during the throw, and invalidating any attempt where the metal tip does not hit the ground before any other part of the implement.
- Underwent a corresponding women's redesign in 1999, after which Czech thrower *Barbora Špotáková* established the modern women's world record of 72.28 meters in 2008.

## connections

- [[olympic-games]] — core athletics contest featured in the ancient games and the modern Olympic programme since 1908.
- [[ancient-greece]] — ancient civilisation that integrated spear throwing into the classical pentathlon as an essential martial skill.
- [[jesse-owens]] — legendary track and field peer who shared stadium grounds at the 1936 Berlin Games where throwing events featured prominently.
- [[germany]] — nation with a storied throwing heritage whose athletes prompted both the 1936 Olympic triumph and the 1986 implement redesign.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical power that adapted Greek spear traditions into martial throwing weapons such as the heavy *pilum*.
- [[united-states]] — athletic power that has regularly contested global championships, claiming early Olympic gold through *Cy Young* in 1952.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflict where physical training in grenade and spear mechanics informed twentieth-century athletic conditioning.
