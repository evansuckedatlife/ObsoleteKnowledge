## summary

The **Montreal Expos**, affectionately known in French as **Nos Amours**, were Canada's first Major League Baseball franchise, competing in the National League from 1969 through 2004. Established during the league's 1969 expansion era and named in tribute to the landmark *Expo 67* World's Fair, the club introduced professional major league baseball to an enthusiastic bilingual market. Despite cultivating generations of transcendent talent, stadium issues and a devastating strike led to their 2005 relocation to the [[united-states]] capital as the Washington Nationals.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1969 as the first Major League Baseball franchise established outside the [[united-states]], beginning play at outdoor *Jarry Park* (*Stade Parc Jarry*).
- Moved in 1977 into the cavernous *Olympic Stadium* (*Stade Olympique*), an architectural icon constructed for the 1976 Summer Olympics that suffered from structural and financial difficulties.
- Suffered their most agonizing postseason defeat on "Blue Monday" in Game 5 of the 1981 NLCS, when *Rick Monday* of the Los Angeles Dodgers hit a decisive ninth-inning home run off pitcher *Steve Rogers*.
- Built Major League Baseball's best record in 1994 (74–40) under manager *Felipe Alou*, only to have their World Series aspirations erased when the 1994–95 players' strike prematurely cancelled the season and postseason.
- Developed an extraordinary pipeline of future Hall of Fame stars, including catcher *Gary Carter*, dynamic outfielder *Andre Dawson*, stolen base king *Tim Raines*, and slugger *Vladimir Guerrero*.
- Traded away superstar talent due to severe financial constraints, famously dealing future Cy Young winners *Pedro Martinez* and *Randy Johnson* after developing them into premier arms.
- Employed renowned photojournalist *Denis Brodeur*, the father of legendary hockey goaltender [[martin-brodeur]], as their official team photographer for decades.
- Contested the *Pearson Cup* against the [[toronto-blue-jays]] in an annual exhibition and later regular season rivalry representing Canadian baseball supremacy.

## connections

- [[toronto-blue-jays]] — Ontario counterpart and fellow Canadian franchise that shared the national baseball spotlight and contested the *Pearson Cup*.
- [[martin-brodeur]] — son of longtime Expos team photographer Denis Brodeur and native Montreal sports hero.
- [[the-new-york-yankees]] — powerhouse American League club that acquired numerous former Montreal stars, including Tim Raines and John Wetteland.
- [[babe-ruth]] — iconic baseball home run king whose historical standard defined excellence across the major leagues.
- [[derek-jeter]] — prominent major league rival who faced former Expos stars throughout his championship tenure.
- [[united-states]] — nation where the franchise played the vast majority of its road schedule and to which it relocated in 2005.
- [[france]] — historical cultural source of the Francophone heritage celebrated throughout Montreal and the team's identity.
