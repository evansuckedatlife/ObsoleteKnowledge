## summary

**Soldier Field**, originally commissioned as **Municipal Grant Park Stadium**, is a historic open-air stadium situated along Lake Michigan in Chicago, Illinois, within the [[united-states]]. Widely recognized as the venerable longtime home of the Chicago Bears in the [[national-football-league]], it is one of the oldest operating stadiums in professional sports. Beyond American football, the venue has hosted cultural and sporting milestones of international significance, including matches during the 1994 FIFA World Cup in [[soccer]] and the inaugural 1968 [[special-olympics]].

## you gotta know

- Opened in 1924 under the name Municipal Grant Park Stadium before being officially rededicated as Soldier Field in 1925 in memory of American military personnel lost in [[world-war-i]].
- Serves as the oldest stadium in the [[national-football-league]], acting as the primary home field for the Chicago Bears franchise since the team relocated from Wrigley Field in 1971.
- Staged the historic inaugural International Special Olympics Games in July 1968, where *Eunice Kennedy Shriver* launched the global [[special-olympics]] movement before a crowd of supportive spectators.
- Hosted five matches during the 1994 FIFA World Cup, including the lavish opening ceremony and the tournament's opening fixture between Germany and Bolivia, cementing its place in modern [[soccer]] history.
- Served as the setting for the celebrated 1927 "Long Count Fight" between heavyweight boxing legends *Gene Tunney* and *Jack Dempsey*, drawing over one hundred thousand attendees.
- Underwent a controversial major reconstruction between 2002 and 2003 that inserted a modern asymmetric glass-and-steel bowl directly inside the original neoclassical colonnades.
- Suffered the rare revocation of its National Historic Landmark designation in 2006 after federal authorities determined the modern renovations had compromised its historic architectural character.
- Provided a home venue for Major League Soccer club Chicago Fire FC across multiple eras and served as a stage for monumental public gatherings led by figures such as *Franklin D. Roosevelt* and *Martin Luther King Jr.*.
