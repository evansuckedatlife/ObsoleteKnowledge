## summary

The **New Orleans Saints** are a professional [[american-football|football]] franchise competing in the [[national-football-league|NFL]]'s NFC South, founded in 1967 and based in New Orleans, Louisiana. The team transformed from decades of mediocrity into a championship contender following the arrival of quarterback [[drew-brees|Drew Brees]] in 2006, coinciding with the city's recovery from Hurricane Katrina. That partnership produced the franchise's sole Super Bowl victory (Super Bowl XLIV in February 2010) and created a powerful symbol of resilience and community redemption in American sports.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1967, the franchise endured 39 years of losing records and futility before [[drew-brees|Drew Brees]] arrived, establishing themselves as an NFL perennial embarrassment.
- [[drew-brees|Drew Brees]] arrived in 2006 during the Saints' Post-Katrina rebuilding phase, signing a contract to lead the franchise's resurrection just as New Orleans itself sought recovery.
- Won [[super-bowl|Super Bowl XLIV]] in February 2010 (the 2009 season), defeating the Indianapolis Colts and giving New Orleans its first championship, creating a transformative moment for the city's morale and identity.
- Head coach Sean Payton orchestrated the championship alongside Brees, employing innovative defensive schemes (the "Blitzkrieg" defense) and Brees's passing excellence to dominate opponents.
- [[drew-brees|Drew Brees]] set numerous NFL passing records during his tenure (2006–2019), becoming the franchise's most successful and celebrated player and revitalizing a dormant organization.
- Remained competitive through the 2010s, winning multiple playoff appearances and division titles, though the post-Brees era (2020-present) has seen a return to inconsistency.
- The 2009 championship became symbolic of New Orleans' resilience after [[hurricane-katrina|Hurricane Katrina]], with the Saints' victory serving as an emotional and cultural counterpoint to the devastation the city had endured just four years prior.
