## summary

The **Los Angeles Chargers** are a professional [[american-football|football]] franchise that relocated to Los Angeles in 2017 after 56 years in [[san-diego-chargers|San Diego]]. Born from the divisive relocation of the storied [[san-diego-chargers|San Diego Chargers]], they share a stadium with the Los Angeles Rams and compete in the AFC West. Despite early promise and star quarterback [[philip-rivers|Philip Rivers]], the franchise has struggled to establish identity or success in Los Angeles, never reaching the Super Bowl and facing persistent questions about fan loyalty in their new market.

## you gotta know

- Relocated from [[san-diego-chargers|San Diego]] to Los Angeles in 2017 following contentious negotiations with the city, taking many fans and decades of franchise history with them.
- Played one season (2017) at the StubHub Center (later Dignity Health Sports Park) before moving to SoFi Stadium in 2020, sharing the facility with the Los Angeles Rams.
- [[philip-rivers|Philip Rivers]] continued as quarterback through 2019 after the relocation, maintaining individual excellence but failing to restore the playoff success the franchise had sought.
- Never reached a Super Bowl as the Los Angeles franchise, despite high preseason expectations, becoming a symbol of relocation's disruptive effect on organizational momentum.
- Drafted star pass rusher Joey Bosa in 2016 and developed promising young talent, but inconsistent management decisions and coaching changes hampered sustained success.
- Struggled with fan support in Los Angeles, where sports culture remains divided among established franchises (Lakers, Dodgers, Rams) and NFL fan populations remain less densely concentrated than in San Diego.
- The relocation decision generated significant controversy, with team owner Dean Spanos overruling an NFL owners' vote and a San Diego ballot measure that opposed the move, representing a turning point in franchise-city relations.
