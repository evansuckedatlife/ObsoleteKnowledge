## summary

**lacrosse** is a team sport of Indigenous North American origin played with a small rubber ball and a long-handled stick equipped with a netted pocket called a crosse. Players use the head of the stick to catch, carry, pass, and shoot the ball into the opposing team's goal. Recognized historically as **stickball**, **baggataway**, or **tewaarathon**, the game holds deep spiritual and cultural significance among First Nations and Native American peoples, particularly the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), and has evolved into one of the fastest-growing field sports in North America.

## you gotta know

- Originated as a ceremonial practice among Indigenous peoples across eastern North America, known to the Haudenosaunee as **tewaarathon** (the Creator's Game), where it served healing, spiritual, and conflict-resolution purposes.
- Given its modern name **lacrosse** by seventeenth-century French Jesuit missionary *Jean de Brébeuf*, who noted the crosse stick resembled a bishop's pastoral staff or crosier.
- Modern rules were first codified in Montreal in 1867 by Canadian dentist *William George Beers*, who founded the Montreal Lacrosse Club, replaced the traditional deerskin ball with hard rubber, and authored the sport's first standard rulebook.
- Designated as Canada's official national summer sport by an act of Parliament in 1994, balancing the status of *[[ice-hockey]]* as the official winter sport.
- The Haudenosaunee Nationals (formerly the Iroquois Nationals) compete in international competition as an independent sovereign indigenous sporting delegation, traveling under their own Haudenosaunee passports.
- Contested as an official medal sport at the *[[olympic-games]]* in 1904 (St. Louis) and 1908 (London), and scheduled to return to the Olympic program for the 2028 Los Angeles Games in the fast-paced Sixes format.
- Played primarily in two distinct modern formats: field lacrosse, contested outdoors on a turf field with ten players per side, and box lacrosse, played inside an enclosed ice arena or turf rink with six players per side.
- Dominated at the collegiate level by the NCAA Division I Men's Lacrosse Championship, historically controlled by storied programs such as Syracuse, Johns Hopkins, Princeton, and Virginia.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — co-host of traditional tribal ancestral homelands and home to the sport's massive collegiate and professional expansion.
- [[olympic-games]] — featured as an Olympic medal sport in 1904 and 1908 and revived for the 2028 Summer Games in Los Angeles.
- [[ice-hockey]] — directly influenced Canadian ice hockey tactics and shares indoor arena infrastructure in box lacrosse.
- [[colonialism]] — centuries-long European encounter, missionary reporting, and eventual institutional appropriation of Indigenous ceremonial stickball.
- [[british-empire]] — spread across nineteenth-century English-speaking dominions, public schools, and athletic clubs following codification in Montreal.
- [[france]] — French missionary *Jean de Brébeuf* coined the sport's modern European name during early colonial explorations in New France.
- [[larry-bird]] — fellow fixture of collegiate and scholastic athletic traditions across the American eastern seaboard and Midwest.
