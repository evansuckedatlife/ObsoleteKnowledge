## summary

The *Detroit Red Wings* are one of the oldest and most successful franchises in professional ice hockey, founded in 1926 and competing continuously in the *National Hockey League*. Based in Detroit, Michigan, they established a dynasty in the 1930s and 1940s, then dominated the sport with legendary players like *Gordie Howe* and *Terry Sawchuk* from 1946 through the 1970s. The franchise has won 11 *Stanley Cups*, more than nearly any other NHL team, and shaped hockey's competitive and cultural landscape.

## you gotta know

- Founded as the Detroit Cougars in 1926, renamed the Falcons briefly, then becoming the Red Wings in 1932—one of the NHL's six Original Six franchises.
- Won three consecutive Stanley Cups (1936, 1937, 1943) and then a four-year dynasty (1950–1955), establishing early franchise dominance.
- *Gordie Howe*, nicknamed *Mr. Hockey*, spent nearly his entire career with the Red Wings, helping define the franchise's identity and excellence.
- *Terry Sawchuk* was a legendary goaltender with the Red Wings in the 1950s, combining exceptional skill with toughness characteristic of the era.
- The franchise played in the original *Detroit Olympia* from 1927 to 1979, then moved to Joe Louis Arena, creating continuity in the team's home base.
- Known for a development system and organization that attracted talent and maintained competitiveness across multiple generations.
- The red wing logo is one of hockey's most iconic symbols, representing enduring tradition and excellence in the sport.
- The "Production Line" of the late 1940s and 1950s—*Gordie Howe*, *Ted Lindsay*, and Sid Abel—was one of hockey's greatest forward lines, revolutionizing the game's offensive possibilities.
- Detroit's manufacturing heritage connected the city to hockey culture; the team's identity was woven into working-class Detroit pride and civic identity.
- The franchise's 11 Stanley Cups place it among hockey's most storied organizations, with championships spanning from the 1930s through the early 2000s.
- Coached by legends like *Jack Adams*, the Red Wings established organizational excellence and a commitment to development that sustained success across decades.
- The rivalry between Detroit and Montreal during the 1950s created some of hockey's greatest playoff matchups and memorable moments.
