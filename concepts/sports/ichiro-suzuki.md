## summary

**Ichiro Suzuki**, widely referred to mononymously as **Ichiro**, is a Japanese former professional baseball outfielder whose mastery of contact hitting, plate discipline, and defensive precision made him one of the most prolific players in the history of international sport. After an acclaimed career in Nippon Professional Baseball with the *Orix BlueWave*, he moved to the [[united-states]] in 2001 to join the *Seattle Mariners*, shattering prejudices against Asian position players in Major League Baseball. Over three decades across two hemispheres, he collected 4,367 top-tier professional hits and established the modern single-season major league hit record.

## you gotta know

- Broke George Sisler's 84-year-old Major League Baseball single-season hit record in 2004 by racking up 262 hits, an offensive milestone that stands as one of the most resilient in modern baseball history.
- Swept the American League Rookie of the Year and American League Most Valuable Player awards in 2001, guiding the *Seattle Mariners* to an American League record-tying 116 regular-season wins in his debut major league campaign.
- Recorded ten consecutive 200-hit seasons and ten consecutive Gold Glove Awards from 2001 through 2010, establishing unprecedented benchmarks for outfield durability and elite contact consistency.
- Amassed 3,089 hits in Major League Baseball and 1,278 hits in Nippon Professional Baseball for an aggregate professional tally of 4,367, surpassing Pete Rose's all-time professional hit total.
- Hit the first inside-the-park home run in MLB All-Star Game history in 2007 at AT&T Park in San Francisco, an electric blast into the right-field corner that earned him the game's Most Valuable Player honors.
- Cultivated an iconic, deeply disciplined batting stance and routine, extending his bat toward the mound with his right arm while tugging his right jersey sleeve with his left hand before unleashing his kinetic pendulum swing.
- Traded in July 2012 to [[the-new-york-yankees]], where he contributed to deep postseason runs alongside veteran stalwarts [[derek-jeter]] and [[mariano-rivera]] and recorded his milestone 4,000th combined hit.
- Led the national team of *Japan* to consecutive World Baseball Classic championships in 2006 and 2009, driving in the game-winning runs in the tenth inning against South Korea in the 2009 final before finishing his playing career at the Tokyo Dome in 2019.
