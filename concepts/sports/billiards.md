## summary

**Billiards** refers to a comprehensive family of cue sports played on a cloth-covered rectangular table bounded by elastic rubber cushions, using a cue stick to strike billiard balls. Originating in Western Europe as an indoor adaptation of outdoor lawn games during the late Middle Ages, it evolved through the centuries into major competitive disciplines including carom billiards, pool, and snooker. The game combines precise geometry, rotational mechanics, and tactical maneuvering, standing as an internationally played pastime and professional discipline.

## you gotta know

- Evolved from outdoor lawn bowling games such as croquet during the fifteenth century in France and England before moving onto indoor tables.
- Encompasses three traditional primary branches: carom billiards played on pocketless tables, pocket billiards or pool, and snooker.
- Relies upon hitting an object ball using a cue ball propelled by a leather-tipped wooden stick, an invention credited to *François Mingaud* in 1807.
- Employs the application of rotational spin known as *English*, which alters the cue ball's path, angle of deflection, and behavior off the cushions.
- Established early professional tournament prominence in the nineteenth century, driven by vulcanized rubber cushions and composite phenolic resin balls.
- Features snooker as a dominant global television sport, centered around a 22-ball layout played on twelve-foot tables with stringent scoring rules.
- Regulated globally by the World Confederation of Billiards Sports, which seeks inclusion within major multisport international competitions.

## connections

- [[england]] — home country where modern snooker, English billiards, and standardized pub games were developed and institutionalized.
- [[france]] — historical birthplace of royal indoor billiard play during the reigns of *Louis XI* and *Louis XIV*.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — manufacturing era that revolutionized billiard equipment through vulcanized rubber cushions, slate beds, and synthetic materials.
- [[british-empire]] — global imperial network through which British military officers invented snooker in colonial India and spread cue sports worldwide.
- [[united-states]] — nation where distinct forms of pocket billiards, notably eight-ball, nine-ball, and straight pool, achieved massive cultural prominence.
- [[renaissance]] — transitional historical epoch during which lawn pastimes moved indoors onto cloth tables across European aristocratic courts.
