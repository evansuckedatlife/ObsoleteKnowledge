## summary

The **Special Olympics**, occasionally referenced as the **Special Olympic Games**, is an international sports organization and global athletic movement dedicated to empowering children and adults with intellectual and cognitive disabilities. Founded in the [[united-states]] in 1968 by *Eunice Kennedy Shriver*, the initiative grew out of backyard summer day camps and held its inaugural international gathering at Chicago's [[soldier-field]]. Through ongoing year-round athletic programs and international competitions, it provides millions of athletes worldwide with opportunities to develop physical fitness, demonstrate courage, and experience community inclusion.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1968 by *Eunice Kennedy Shriver*, inspired by the institutionalization, social exclusion, and athletic passion of her elder sister *Rosemary Kennedy*.
- Staged its inaugural International Special Olympics Games in July 1968 at [[soldier-field]] in Chicago, welcoming roughly one thousand competitors from across the [[united-states]] and Canada.
- Guided by the athlete oath recited at every opening ceremony: "Let me win. But if I cannot win, let me be brave in the attempt."
- Distinguished fundamentally from the Paralympic Games, which emphasize elite competition for athletes with physical impairments, whereas the Special Olympics focuses on athletes of all skill levels with intellectual disabilities.
- Utilizes an equitable "divisioning" system in which athletes are grouped into heats based on age, gender, and previous scores, ensuring that participants compete against peers of comparable athletic ability.
- Received official recognition from the International Olympic Committee in 1988, standing as the only non-governmental entity permitted to use the protected "Olympics" name.
- Developed the Unified Sports program, which places athletes with and without intellectual disabilities on the same competitive roster in team sports such as [[soccer]] and basketball to break down social barriers.
- Features alternating World Summer Games and World Winter Games held every two years, supporting competitions that encompass sports ranging from track and field to floor hockey and speed skating.

## connections

- [[soldier-field]] — the historic Chicago stadium that served as the venue for the first International Special Olympics in 1968.
- [[olympic-games]] — the international multi-sport institution that provided the framework, ceremonial torches, and trademark authorization for the movement.
- [[united-states]] — the country where *Eunice Kennedy Shriver* initiated the movement and hosted its initial national competitions.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the twentieth-century American reform climate that helped catalyze broader advocacy for disability rights and deinstitutionalization.
- [[soccer]] — one of the most widely played and popular team sports featured in the global Special Olympics summer competitive catalog.
- [[ice-hockey]] — the cold-weather team discipline that helped shape floor hockey and winter sporting events within the World Winter Games.
