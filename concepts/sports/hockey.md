## summary

**Hockey** designates an umbrella family of traditional stick-and-ball team sports where opposing squads maneuver a ball or disk into the opponent's goal using curved sticks. While contemporary North Americans usually equate the term with fast-paced play on ice, globally the unqualified term refers predominantly to outdoor **field hockey**, played on turf across Europe, Asia, and Australasia. Emerging from archaic ball-and-stick pastimes into codified nineteenth-century disciplines, hockey games encompass major variants like roller hockey, street hockey, and the frozen-pitch discipline of **bandy**.

## you gotta know

- Refers internationally to *field hockey*, which has been a staple men's event at the Summer *Olympic Games* since 1908 and a women's event since 1980.
- Codified modern rules for field hockey during the mid-nineteenth century in public schools and clubs across *England*, forming the Hockey Association in 1886.
- Spawned *ice hockey* on frozen ponds and rinks in Canada during the nineteenth century, establishing one of the premier winter sports in the world.
- Governed on grass and synthetic turf by the International Hockey Federation (FIH), with international powerhouses historically centered in India, Pakistan, Australia, Germany, and the Netherlands.
- Employs a J-shaped stick with a flat playing side and a curved back; players are strictly prohibited from utilizing the rounded reverse side of the stick to strike the ball.
- Includes **bandy**, an ancestral cousin played with curved sticks and a small orange ball on expansive outdoor sheets of ice, especially popular in Russia and Scandinavia.
- Transitioned almost entirely from natural grass to watered artificial turf pitches in international competition during the 1970s, which radically increased game speed and altered tactical dribbling.

## connections

- [[henri-richard]] — legendary French-Canadian center who dominated the ice-based variant of the sport by winning eleven Stanley Cups.
- [[ice-hockey]] — the winter variant played on ice with skates and a vulcanized rubber puck.
- [[olympic-games]] — the premier international athletic gathering hosting both summer field hockey and winter ice hockey tournaments.
- [[england]] — the birthplace of the codified modern field game and its original nineteenth-century governing bodies.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial network that exported and popularized field hockey throughout South Asia, Australasia, and Africa.
- [[stanley-cup]] — the celebrated trophy emblematic of championship achievement in North American professional ice hockey.
- [[wayne-gretzky]] — the widely recognized greatest offensive player in the history of ice hockey.
