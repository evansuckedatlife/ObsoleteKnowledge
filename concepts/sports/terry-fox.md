## summary

**Terry Fox** (born **Terrance Stanley Fox**) was an iconic Canadian athlete, humanitarian, and cancer research activist who embarked on the historic *Marathon of Hope* in 1980. After having his right leg amputated above the knee due to osteosarcoma at age eighteen, he resolved to run across Canada to raise money and awareness for cancer research. His heroic transcontinental effort captured international admiration, transforming him into an enduring symbol of resilience, selflessness, and national unity across the Commonwealth and the wider world.

## you gotta know

- Embarked on the *Marathon of Hope* on April 12, 1980, dipping his artificial right leg into the Atlantic Ocean near St. John's, Newfoundland, with the goal of running west to the Pacific Ocean in Vancouver.
- Maintained an astounding pace of approximately twenty-six miles—the full distance of a marathon—every single day on an uneven, custom-built prosthetic limb that caused blistering and bone bruises.
- Originally aimed to raise one dollar for every one of Canada's twenty-four million citizens, a fundraising target that was eventually surpassed as his effort galvanized the entire country.
- Forced to end his cross-country run after 143 days and 3,339 miles (5,373 kilometers) outside Thunder Bay, Ontario, on September 1, 1980, after cancer spread to his lungs.
- Named a Companion of the Order of Canada—the nation's youngest recipient at age twenty-two—and received the Lou Marsh Trophy as Canada's top athlete of 1980 before his death in June 1981.
- Inspired the annual *Terry Fox Run*, established in 1981 by British-Canadian businessman *Isadore Sharp*, which has since evolved into the world's largest single-day cancer research fundraiser, raising over eight hundred million dollars globally.
- Competed as an accomplished wheelchair basketball athlete before his run, winning three national championships alongside fellow activist and Paralympic pioneer *Rick Hansen*.
