# Tennis

## summary

**Tennis** is a racket sport played on a rectangular court between two players (singles) or four players (doubles), where players hit a felt-covered ball over a net to score points—the server must land serves in the service box, and rallies continue until a player fails to return the ball in bounds or makes an unforced error. Originating in 12th-century France as a hand-striking game (jeu de paume) and evolving into the modern lawn-tennis game in 19th-century England, tennis became an Olympic sport (1896) and the basis for the four *Grand Slam* tournaments: Wimbledon, the U.S. Open, the French Open, and the Australian Open. Tennis combines speed, strategy, endurance, and technical precision, and professional tennis has produced iconic athletes who dominated the sport and transcended it culturally.

## you gotta know

- Tennis scoring is unique: within a game, points are counted as 0 (love), 15, 30, 40, and game (a player must win by 2 if tied 40–40, called deuce); a set is won by the first player to win 6 games with a 2-game lead, or via tiebreak (first to 7 points by 2) at 6–6; a match is typically best-of-three or best-of-five sets.
- The four *Grand Slam* tournaments—*Wimbledon* (grass courts in England), the *French Open* (clay courts in Paris), the *U.S. Open* (hard courts in New York), and the *Australian Open* (hard courts in Melbourne)—are the sport's most prestigious events; winning all four in a single calendar year is the "Calendar Grand Slam," achieved only by *Don Budge* (1938) and *Margaret Court* (1970).
- Modern tennis evolved from *jeu de paume*, a French hand-striking game played indoors; when played outdoors on grass in the 19th century, players adopted rackets; *Major Walter Clopton Wingfield* codified the modern game in 1873, and it spread rapidly through the British Empire and America.
- The *serve* is critical: a player serves two attempts (faults if both miss the service box), and a powerful, accurate serve provides a huge advantage; legendary servers like *Pete Sampras* and *Goran Ivanisevic* often dominated opponents through serve alone.
- Tennis surfaces dramatically affect play: *grass* (Wimbledon) is fast and favors big servers and net-rushers; *clay* (French Open) is slow and bouncy, favoring baseline rallies and footwork; *hard courts* (U.S. Open, Australian Open) are intermediate in speed and provide consistent bounce.
- Professional tennis was long segregated by sex and amateur-only: women could not play most tournaments; the *Open Era* (beginning 1968) allowed professionals to compete. *Billie Jean King*'s advocacy was crucial; in 1973, she defeated *Bobby Riggs* in the "Battle of the Sexes," proving women's tennis was compelling and commercially viable.
- The sport has produced several transcendent champions: *Roger Federer* won 20 Grand Slams and set the standard for elegant, all-around play; *Serena Williams* won 23 Grand Slams and revolutionized women's tennis with power and athleticism; *Novak Djokovic* has won 24 Grand Slams using exceptional defensive consistency.
- Modern professional tennis is grueling: top players compete in 70+ matches annually across continents; the mental and physical toll of the sport has driven innovations in training, nutrition, psychology, and equipment (racket technology, string materials, footwear).
