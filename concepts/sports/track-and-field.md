## summary

**Track and field** is a diverse collection of sports comprising competitive running, jumping, throwing, and combined events contested on an oval running track and adjacent enclosed field. Rooted deeply in the ancient Panhellenic festivals of *Ancient Greece*, modern athletics emerged in nineteenth-century *England* and forms the centerpiece of the modern *Olympic Games*. Governed internationally by *World Athletics*, it remains a foundational discipline in competitive sports culture worldwide, showcasing fundamental human athletic capacities of speed, endurance, power, and technical coordination.

## you gotta know

- Traces its formal competitive lineage to the ancient *Olympic Games* staged in *Ancient Greece*, where the *stadion* footrace served as the premiere contest.
- The centerpiece discipline of the modern summer *Olympic Games* since the inaugural 1896 Athens games, featuring signature sprinting stars such as *Usain Bolt*, *Jesse Owens*, and *Carl Lewis*.
- Contests are divided into track running events (sprints, middle-distance, long-distance, hurdles, and relays) and field events (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault, shot put, discus throw, hammer throw, and javelin).
- Elite multi-event competitions measure all-around athletic mastery through the men's decathlon and women's heptathlon, traditionally crowning the victor as the world's greatest athlete.
- Major international governing body *World Athletics* (formerly the *IAAF*) oversees the biennial *World Athletics Championships* and ratifies official world records.
- Pioneering historical benchmarks in the sport include *Roger Bannister* breaking the four-minute mile in 1954 in *England* and *Bob Beamon* shattering the long jump world record at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics.
- Decisive innovations transformed the sport across eras, notably the introduction of synthetic all-weather tartan surfaces, fiberglass pole vaulting poles, and *Dick Fosbury*'s revolutionary backwards high-jump technique, the Fosbury Flop.
