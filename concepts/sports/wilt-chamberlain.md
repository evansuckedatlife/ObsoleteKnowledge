## summary

**Wilt Chamberlain** was an American professional basketball player whose unprecedented physical dominance and statistical achievements redefined the sport during the 1960s and early 1970s. Standing over seven feet tall, he starred for the *Philadelphia/San Francisco Warriors*, the *Philadelphia 76ers*, and the *Los Angeles Lakers*, setting individual records widely regarded as unbreakable. His storied rivalry with *Bill Russell* of the *Boston Celtics* served as the premier centerpiece of professional basketball in the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Scored an astonishing 100 points in a single game on March 2, 1962, playing for the *Philadelphia Warriors* against the *New York Knicks* in Hershey, Pennsylvania.
- Averaged an unprecedented 50.4 points per game across the entire 1961–1962 season, while also averaging an extraordinary 48.5 minutes per game due to playing virtually every minute of regular time and overtimes.
- Claimed the all-time career rebounding record in professional basketball history with 23,924 total rebounds, surpassing his fiercest on-court rival, *Bill Russell*.
- Led the league in total assists during the 1967–1968 season, deliberately setting out to demonstrate that his playmaking could match his historic scoring prowess.
- Prompted several major rule changes in basketball to mitigate his overpowering advantages, including widening the lane, instituting offensive goaltending, and revising free-throw shooting regulations.
- Played college basketball for the *Kansas Jayhawks*, leading them to the 1957 national title game, and toured internationally with the *Harlem Globetrotters* before joining the professional ranks.
- Won two championships, first capturing the title with the dominant 1966–1967 *Philadelphia 76ers* and later with the 1971–1972 *Los Angeles Lakers*, who achieved a record 33-game winning streak.
