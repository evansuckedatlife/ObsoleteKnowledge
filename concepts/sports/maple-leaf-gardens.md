## summary

**Maple Leaf Gardens** is a historic indoor arena located at the corner of Carlton Street and Church Street in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Commissioned by *Conn Smythe* during the depths of the *Great Depression*, the venue served as the iconic home of the *Toronto Maple Leafs* of the *National Hockey League* from 1931 until 1999. Revered as one of the venerable cathedrals of North American athletic architecture, it hosted eleven [[stanley-cup]] championships, landmark political rallies, cultural concerts, and seminal international sporting encounters.

## you gotta know

- Constructed in only five months in 1931 under the leadership of Leafs owner *Conn Smythe*, opening its doors on November 12, 1931, amidst the financial hardships of the *Great Depression*.
- Served as the long-running backdrop for the legendary radio and television broadcast *Hockey Night in Canada*, narrated by voice icons *Foster Hewitt* and his son *Bill Hewitt*.
- Witnessed eleven [[stanley-cup]] title celebrations by the *Toronto Maple Leafs*, including four championships during the 1960s before their prolonged championship drought.
- Staged the opening game of the *1972 Summit Series* between Canada and the [[soviet-union]], in which the visiting Soviet squad stunned the Canadian hosts with a 7–3 victory.
- Hosted Game 2 of the historic 1946 inaugural season of the *Basketball Association of America*, a direct precursor to modern professional [[basketball]], between the *Toronto Huskies* and the *New York Knickerbockers*.
- Served as an essential venue for musical history, staging performances by *The Beatles*, *Elvis Presley*, and *Frank Sinatra*, as well as major political conventions and religious assemblies.
- Underwent extensive adaptive reuse in the twenty-first century, converting its lower levels into a retail market while preserving its upper bowl as the home athletic arena for *Toronto Metropolitan University*.
