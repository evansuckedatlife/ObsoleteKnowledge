## summary

**Aaron Hernandez** was an American football tight end whose extraordinary athletic career in the *[[national-football-league]]* collapsed into infamy following his arrest and conviction for first-degree murder. Teaming with Rob Gronkowski on the New England Patriots, he formed one of the most prolific two-tight-end tandems in professional football history catching passes from *[[tom-brady]]*. His sudden downfall, incarceration, and eventual suicide at age twenty-seven exposed dark intersections of violence, institutional failure, and advanced chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) in contact sports.

## you gotta know

- Selected by the New England Patriots in the fourth round of the 2010 NFL Draft after a decorated collegiate career with the Florida Gators, where he won a national championship and received the John Mackey Award as the nation's premier tight end under Urban Meyer.
- Formed the revolutionary "Boston TE Party" tandem alongside Rob Gronkowski under head coach Bill Belichick, combining for 169 receptions, 2,237 receiving yards, and twenty-four touchdowns during the 2011 regular season.
- Scored a touchdown in *[[super-bowl]]* XLVI in February 2012 on a pass from *[[tom-brady]]*, although the Patriots were ultimately defeated by the New York Giants.
- Signed a lucrative five-year, forty-million-dollar contract extension with the Patriots in August 2012, donating fifty thousand dollars to the Myra Kraft Giving Back Fund upon signing.
- Arrested at his North Attleborough home in June 2013 and immediately released by the Patriots following the shooting death of semi-pro football player Odin Lloyd in a nearby industrial park.
- Convicted of first-degree murder, unlawful possession of a firearm, and ammunition offenses in April 2015, receiving a mandatory sentence of life imprisonment without the possibility of parole.
- Acquitted in April 2017 of the 2012 Boston double homicide of Daniel de Abreu and Safiro Furtado, only to be found dead by suicide in his cell at the Souza-Baranowski Correctional Center five days later.
- Diagnosed posthumously by Boston University researchers with severe Stage 3 chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE), an unusually advanced degree of brain damage rarely documented in an individual of his young age.
