## summary

**Larry Bird**, fully named **Larry Joe Bird** and famously nicknamed **The Hick from French Lick**, was an American professional basketball player, coach, and front-office executive for the Boston Celtics and Indiana Pacers. Renowned for his lethal perimeter shooting, pinpoint court vision, fierce competitive edge, and clutch heroics, Bird led the Celtics to three National Basketball Association championships during the 1980s. His fierce coast-to-coast rivalry with *Magic Johnson* revitalized professional basketball in the *[[united-states]]* and captivated global sports audiences.

## you gotta know

- Famously led the undefeated Indiana State Sycamores to the 1979 NCAA Championship Game, where he faced *Magic Johnson*'s Michigan State Spartans in the highest-rated television broadcast in American college basketball history.
- Selected sixth overall in the 1978 NBA Draft by the Boston Celtics' general manager *Red Auerbach*, who shrewdly utilized an eligibility loophole to retain Bird's draft rights for a full year while Bird finished college.
- Won three NBA championships with the Boston Celtics (1981, 1984, and 1986), capturing NBA Finals Most Valuable Player honors in 1984 and 1986.
- Won three consecutive regular season NBA Most Valuable Player awards from 1984 to 1986, becoming the third player in league history alongside *Bill Russell* and *Wilt Chamberlain* to accomplish the three-peat feat.
- Formed the legendary Boston frontline alongside Hall of Fame frontcourt teammates *Kevin McHale* and *Robert Parish*, considered one of the greatest front lines ever assembled.
- Won the inaugural three NBA Three-Point Contests consecutively from 1986 through 1988, famously asking fellow competitors in the locker room before the 1988 shootout who was coming in second place.
- Won an Olympic gold medal as a co-captain of the 1992 *[[united-states]]* Men's Olympic Basketball team, the celebrated *Dream Team*, at the Barcelona *[[olympic-games]]*.
- Remains the only person in NBA history to be voted Most Valuable Player, Coach of the Year (with the Indiana Pacers in 1998), and Executive of the Year (in 2012).
