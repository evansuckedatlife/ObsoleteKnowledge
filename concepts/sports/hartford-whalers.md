## summary

The **Hartford Whalers** were a beloved professional franchise in North American [[ice-hockey]] that competed in the *World Hockey Association* (*WHA*) and the *National Hockey League* (*NHL*) from 1972 through 1997. Based in Hartford, Connecticut, the club garnered a fervent regional following recognized for its iconic green, blue, and white harpoon-tail logo and celebratory fight song. The team relocated to North Carolina in 1997 to become the *Carolina Hurricanes*, leaving behind an enduring legacy in New England sporting folklore.

## you gotta know

- Originally founded in 1972 in Boston as the *New England Whalers*, the franchise was a charter member of the rebel *World Hockey Association*, winning the inaugural *Avco World Trophy* championship in 1973.
- Relocated permanently to Connecticut in 1974, playing inside the newly constructed *Hartford Civic Center Coliseum* before moving briefly to Springfield when the Hartford arena's heavy roof collapsed under snow in 1978.
- Hockey icon *Gordie Howe* joined the organization in 1977 to skate alongside his sons *Mark Howe* and *Marty Howe*, creating an immortal family lineup before retiring after the 1979–80 season at age fifty-two.
- Merged into the *NHL* in 1979 alongside three other former *WHA* survivors: the *Edmonton Oilers*, *Quebec Nordiques*, and *Winnipeg Jets*.
- Famed for their infectiously upbeat brass arena anthem, *"Brass Bonanza"*, composed by *Jacques Ysaye* under the pseudonym *Jack Say*, which played over public loudspeakers following every home goal.
- Hall of Fame forward *Ron Francis* served as the franchise's cornerstone talent and captain throughout the 1980s, setting enduring club records in goals, assists, and total games played.
- Owner *Peter Karmanos Jr.* relocated the organization southward in 1997 after arena negotiations stalled, rebranding the roster as the *Carolina Hurricanes*, who subsequently captured the [[stanley-cup]] in 2006.
