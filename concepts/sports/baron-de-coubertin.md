## summary

**Baron de Coubertin**, born *Pierre de Frédy*, was a French pedagogue, historian, and sporting visionary revered as the father and architect of the modern [[olympic-games]]. Inspired by the classical ideals of [[ancient-greece]] and the physical education programs of nineteenth-century English public schools, he founded the *International Olympic Committee* (*IOC*) in 1894. Through tireless international advocacy across [[france]] and Europe, **Baron Pierre de Coubertin** revived the Olympiad at the 1896 *Athens Games*, championing universal amateur sport as an instrument for fostering character, cross-cultural diplomacy, and world peace.

## you gotta know

- Convened the historic *Sorbonne Congress* in Paris in June 1894, where delegates unanimously approved his motion to resurrect the quadrennial athletic festivals of antiquity.
- Served as the second president of the *International Olympic Committee* (*IOC*) from 1896 to 1925, following the inaugural tenure of Greek representative *Demetrios Vikelas*.
- Designed the iconic Olympic symbol featuring five interlocking rings of blue, yellow, black, green, and red on a white field, representing the unity of the inhabited continents.
- Authored the famous Olympic motto *Citius, Altius, Fortius* (*Faster, Higher, Stronger*), adopting the Latin phrase coined by his close friend, the Dominican educator *Henri Didon*.
- Promoted the philosophy of *Olympism*, positing that balanced [[athleticism]], moral discipline, and intellectual culture should be blended in harmonious education.
- Won the gold medal in literature at the 1912 *Stockholm Olympics* for his work *Ode to Sport*, submitted pseudonymously under the names *Georges Hohrod* and *Martin Eschbach*.
- Advocated strict amateurism during his lifetime, reflecting class-conscious aristocratic views on fair play that sparked major later debates over athlete compensation.
- Relocated the headquarters of the IOC to *Lausanne*, Switzerland, during [[world-war-i]] to safeguard the movement's neutrality, where he was ultimately buried with his heart entombed in *Olympia*.

- [[athleticism]] — the core principle of physical excellence, self-discipline, and moral development that his Olympic revival celebrated.
- [[world-war-i]] — the catastrophic conflict that disrupted the Olympic cycle and prompted him to move the *IOC* headquarters to neutral Switzerland.
- [[modernism]] — the late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century intellectual movement running parallel to his restructuring of global sporting festivals.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the socioeconomic transformation that created the modern leisure class, organized schooling, and transport networks enabling global athletic competition.
