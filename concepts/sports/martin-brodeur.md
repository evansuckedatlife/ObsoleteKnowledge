## summary

**Martin Brodeur** is a legendary Canadian-American ice hockey goaltender who played nearly his entire twenty-two-year National Hockey League career with the New Jersey Devils. Celebrated for his extraordinary durability, hybrid stand-up style, and revolutionary puck-handling ability, he stands as the winningest netminder in professional hockey history. His stellar backstopping anchored three *Stanley Cup* championships and propelled Team Canada to two Olympic gold medals.

## you gotta know

- Holds the all-time National Hockey League records for career goaltending wins (691) and regular season shutouts (125) over 1,266 career games.
- Captured three [[stanley-cup]] championships with the New Jersey Devils in 1995, 2000, and 2003, serving as the defensive cornerstone of their famed neutral zone trap system.
- Awarded the *Vezina Trophy* as the league's top goaltender four times (2003, 2004, 2007, and 2008) and claimed the *William M. Jennings Trophy* five times.
- Revolutionized the goaltender position with his exceptional stickhandling, which allowed him to clear pucks and break up opposing dump-ins with the effectiveness of a third defenseman.
- Prompted the NHL to institute the trapezoidal restricted zone behind the net following the 2004–05 lockout, an initiative widely dubbed the "Brodeur Rule" designed to curb goaltender puck manipulation.
- Scored three career goals—two in the regular season and one in the 1997 playoffs against the Montreal Canadiens—setting the all-time record for goals scored by a goaltender.
- Born in Montreal, Quebec, as the son of *Denis Brodeur*, an Olympic bronze-medalist goalie in 1956 who spent decades as the official team photographer for the [[montreal-expos]].
- Won two Olympic gold medals representing Team Canada, backstopping the men's national team to victory at Salt Lake City in 2002 and contributing to the gold medal triumph at Vancouver in 2010.
