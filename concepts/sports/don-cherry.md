## summary

**Don Cherry**, widely known by his nickname **Grapes**, is a Canadian ice hockey commentator, former coach, and minor-league defenseman who became one of the most recognizable and polarizing figures in North American sports broadcasting. For nearly four decades, he co-hosted the iconic segment *Coach's Corner* during CBC's *Hockey Night in Canada*, celebrated for his flamboyant, custom-tailored suits, unyielding nationalism, and advocacy of hard-hitting, physical hockey. Before his media career, he guided the *Boston Bruins* to four consecutive first-place division finishes and two appearances in the *Stanley Cup* Finals during the late 1970s.

## you gotta know

- Co-hosted the first-intermission segment *Coach's Corner* on *Hockey Night in Canada* from 1986 to 2019 alongside straight-man broadcaster *Ron MacLean*, attracting millions of viewers each Saturday.
- Served as head coach of the *Boston Bruins* from 1974 to 1979, cultivating the aggressive "Lunch Pail A.C." identity and winning the *Jack Adams Award* as NHL Coach of the Year in 1976.
- Guided Boston to the *Stanley Cup* Finals in 1977 and 1978, falling both times to the dominant *Montreal Canadiens* dynasty coached by *Scotty Bowman*.
- Suffered one of hockey's most infamous coaching mishaps during Game 7 of the 1979 Semifinals against Montreal, when a late "too many men on the ice" bench penalty allowed *Guy Lafleur* to tie the game, leading to an overtime defeat.
- Played professional hockey for nearly two decades, primarily in the American Hockey League where he won four *Calder Cup* championships, appearing in exactly one NHL playoff game with Boston in 1955.
- Championed old-school, physical hockey, outspokenly defending fighting and aggressive body-checking while frequently criticizing European players, visor mandates, and safety-oriented rule alterations.
- Voted the seventh-greatest Canadian of all time in the 2004 CBC television special *The Greatest Canadian*, finishing ahead of *Alexander Graham Bell* and *Wayne Gretzky*.
- Dismissed from Sportsnet and *Hockey Night in Canada* in November 2019 following controversial on-air remarks regarding Canadian immigrants and the wearing of Remembrance Day poppies.
