## summary

The *Boston Bruins* are one of the *National Hockey League's* oldest and most storied franchises, founded in 1924 as the league's first American team. Based in Boston, Massachusetts, they emerged as a powerhouse in the 1960s and 1970s, winning multiple *Stanley Cup* championships and developing a reputation for skilled, physical hockey. The Bruins' success established Boston as a major market for professional ice hockey.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1924 as part of the NHL's American expansion, making them the first U.S.-based franchise in professional hockey.
- Won their first Stanley Cup in 1929, establishing early prominence in the league's competitive hierarchy.
- The 1960s and 1970s defined their greatest era, winning the Stanley Cup in 1970 and 1972 with one of the most dominant teams ever assembled.
- Bobby Orr revolutionized the defenseman position while wearing the Bruins' black and gold, fundamentally changing how the position was played.
- Eddie Shore, an earlier Bruins star from the 1920s and 1930s, was a fierce competitor known for brutal checking and physical dominance on defense.
- Played their home games at the Boston Garden, an iconic arena that became synonymous with Bruins hockey through much of the 20th century.
- The franchise embodies a physical, gritty approach to hockey that emphasizes checking, intensity, and winning through superior conditioning and toughness.
- The 1970 Stanley Cup run featured the legendary overtime goal by *Bobby Orr* against the St. Louis Blues, with the image of him diving after scoring immortalized in hockey lore.
- *Phil Esposito* anchored the Bruins offense during their dynastic era, setting NHL scoring records and anchoring the team's potent forward line.
- The Bruins' style of play influenced how American hockey developed, proving that an American franchise could compete at the highest professional level.
- Won consecutive Stanley Cups in 1970 and 1972, then returned to prominence in the late 1980s and 1990s with notable playoff runs.
- The franchise is part of Boston sports tradition alongside the Red Sox and Celtics, contributing to the city's identity as a major sports center.
