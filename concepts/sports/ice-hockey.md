## summary

**Ice hockey** is a professional sport played on ice between two teams of six players, where competitors use sticks to control a puck and score by shooting it into the opposing team's goal. Originating in Canada in the 19th century, ice hockey evolved into one of the world's most physically demanding and strategically complex sports. The *National Hockey League* is the premier professional league, with the *Stanley Cup* as its championship trophy; the sport is deeply woven into Canadian national identity and has achieved global prominence.

## you gotta know

- A standard ice hockey game has three 20-minute periods with stoppages, using a frozen regulation ice rink divided into three zones separated by blue lines.
- Each team fields a goaltender plus five skaters, with positions including centermen, wingers, and defensemen, creating complex positional and tactical strategies.
- Ice hockey is one of the few major sports where physical contact (checking) is legal and expected, making it exceptionally demanding physically.
- The sport originated in Canada and remains culturally central to Canadian identity; Canada has historically dominated international competition.
- Professional ice hockey in North America is dominated by the *National Hockey League*, founded in 1917, which is the oldest continuously operating professional sports league in North America.
- The *Stanley Cup*, originally donated as the championship trophy for hockey in 1892, is the most iconic trophy in professional sports, with complex traditions around its custody.
- Ice hockey combines speed, skill, strategy, and violence in ways that distinguish it from American football, basketball, and other mainstream sports.
- The sport emphasizes continuous play with rolling substitutions, creating a fast-paced game where players typically play only 40 seconds to 2 minutes per shift before rotating to the bench.
- Ice hockey's rules include offside (entering the offensive zone before the puck), icing (shooting the puck beyond the opposite goal line), and high-sticking violations.
- The sport is played at every level from recreational leagues to professional competition, with youth hockey systems in Canada and the northern United States developing players from childhood.
- International ice hockey competitions like the Olympics and World Championships represent national pride, with games between traditional rivals (Canada, Russia, Sweden, Finland) drawing massive audiences.
- Ice hockey has produced a culture of toughness and grit, where players often continue playing through injuries and pain, embodying the sport's demanding physical nature.
