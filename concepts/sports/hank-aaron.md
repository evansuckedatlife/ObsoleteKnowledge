## summary

**Hank Aaron** was an American professional baseball right fielder who played twenty-three seasons in *Major League Baseball*, primarily for the *Milwaukee* and *Atlanta Braves*. Revered as one of the most consistent and formidable hitters in athletic history, he famously eclipsed *Babe Ruth*'s seemingly insurmountable career home run mark in 1974. His athletic excellence while persevering through virulent racial hostility cemented his standing as a towering American sports figure and enduring champion of racial justice.

## you gotta know

- Nicknamed **Hammerin' Hank**, he began his professional career in the *Negro American League* with the *Indianapolis Clowns* in 1952 before signing with the *Boston Braves* organization.
- Surpassed *Babe Ruth*'s record of 714 career home runs on April 8, 1974, launching his 715th home run off Los Angeles Dodgers pitcher *Al Downing* at Atlanta Stadium.
- Endured voluminous hate mail, vile racial epithets, and credible death threats from segregationists while chasing the home run crown during the peak aftermath of the [[civil-rights-movement]].
- Concluded his illustrious major league playing career with 755 total home runs, a regular-season record that stood unbroken for over three decades until 2007.
- Remains the all-time *Major League Baseball* career record holder for runs batted in (2,297) and total bases (6,856), underscoring his peerless offensive productivity.
- Selected to twenty-five All-Star Games, won the 1957 National League Most Valuable Player award after leading the *Milwaukee Braves* to a World Series championship, and secured three Gold Glove awards.
- Inducted into the *National Baseball Hall of Fame* in Cooperstown in 1982 on his first ballot, receiving over ninety-seven percent of the votes, and awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2002.
