## summary

**Cricket** is a traditional bat-and-ball team sport contested between two squads of eleven players on an oval grass field centered around a twenty-two-yard pitch with wickets at each end. Emerging in south-east [[england]] during the late medieval and early modern eras, the sport was codified by the *Marylebone Cricket Club* (*MCC*) at *Lord's* and spread across the globe along trade and military routes established by the [[british-empire]]. Its pinnacle five-day format, **Test cricket**, represents one of the longest enduring international sporting traditions, while shorter limited-overs iterations such as *One Day Internationals* (*ODIs*) and *Twenty20* (*T20*) leagues have turned it into the second most popular spectator sport in the modern world.

## you gotta know

- Governed globally by the *International Cricket Council* (*ICC*) based in Dubai, while the historical custodians of the official *Laws of Cricket* remain the *Marylebone Cricket Club* (*MCC*) founded in London in 1787.
- **Test cricket** is the longest official format, played over five days with two innings per side, restricted to a select group of full-member nations that includes nations across the former territories of [[colonialism]].
- The most celebrated bilateral rivalry is *The Ashes*, a Test series contested biannually between [[england]] and Australia, named after a satirical obituary published in 1882 proclaiming the death of English sport following an Australian victory at *The Oval*.
- Australian batsman *Sir Donald Bradman* is widely considered the sport's greatest statistical anomaly, retiring in 1948 with an unmatched career Test batting average of 99.94 after being bowled for a duck in his final innings.
- The sport's equipment revolves around three vertical wooden stumps topped by two horizontal wooden bails, defended by a batsman using a flat willow blade against a bowler who delivers a hard leather-seamed sphere with an unbent elbow.
- The 1932–1933 English tour of Australia sparked an international diplomatic crisis known as the *Bodyline* controversy, in which England captain *Douglas Jardine* instructed fast bowler *Harold Larwood* to bowl short-pitched deliveries at the bodies of Australian batsmen to nullify Bradman.
- India represents the sport's economic and cultural epicenter in the contemporary era, driven by the commercial explosion of the franchise-based *Indian Premier League* (*IPL*) and the mythic national stature of iconic batsmen such as *Sachin Tendulkar* and *Virat Kohli*.
- Only featured once at the summer [[olympic-games]] in Paris in 1900, where Great Britain defeated [[france]] in a single twelve-a-side match, before being voted back into the program for the 2028 games in Los Angeles.

## connections

- [[england]] — origin nation where the sport developed in the Weald and where the *MCC* codified the official rules.
- [[british-empire]] — primary political vehicle responsible for transplanting the game into South Asia, the Caribbean, Australasia, and Southern Africa.
- [[colonialism]] — geopolitical process through which the sport became deeply entangled with racial hierarchies, national independence movements, and post-colonial identity.
- [[olympic-games]] — multi-sport competition that hosted a solitary gold medal match in 1900 and scheduled the game's return in 2028.
- [[wimbledon]] — prestigious lawn tennis championship hosted in London that shares deep Victorian social and amateur heritage with the English summer cricket season.
- [[australian-open]] — major tennis tournament situated in Melbourne, sharing sports infrastructure alongside the historic *Melbourne Cricket Ground*.
