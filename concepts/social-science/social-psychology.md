## summary

**Social psychology** is the scientific study of how individuals think about, influence, and relate to one another. It examines the gap between how people predict their own behavior and how they actually behave in social contexts—a gap usually far wider. Social psychologists have conducted landmark experiments revealing how ordinary people commit extraordinary harms under authority, conform to obviously false group judgments, and abandon strangers in distress. The discipline emerged in mid-20th century and fundamentally reshaped understanding of human nature, morality, and social influence.

## you gotta know

- The field bridges psychology (individual mind) and sociology (group structures), asking how social context powerfully shapes individual behavior, cognition, and morality in ways people often fail to predict.
- Solomon Asch's conformity experiments and Stanley Milgram's obedience experiments (1950s–60s) are foundational; both revealed disturbing compliance with group or authority pressure—ordinary people showing unexpected capacity for conformity and harm.
- *Stanford Prison Experiment* (Philip Zimbardo, 1971) raised ethics concerns while demonstrating how roles and institutional contexts override individual ethics and turn good people into abusive authority figures.
- Social psychology explains phenomena like the *bystander effect* (diffusion of responsibility), *groupthink* (suppression of dissent), *cognitive dissonance* (belief adjustment), and *attribution bias* (blaming disposition not situation) that contradict folk psychology.
- The discipline emerged partly as response to WWII and the Holocaust, driven by urgent need to understand how ordinary people participate in atrocities and how propaganda manipulates cognition.
- Modern social psychology emphasizes both the sobering power of social contexts to shape behavior and the human capacity for individual resilience, moral courage, moral disengagement, and dissent.
- Contemporary social psychology has diversified beyond Western college students, incorporating cross-cultural research, digital interaction, and attention to how power and marginalization shape psychological processes.
