## summary

The **Toronto Stock Exchange** (abbreviated as the **TSX**, and formerly designated the **TSE**) is the preeminent securities marketplace in Canada and one of the largest equity exchanges in the world by market capitalization. Located in the financial district of downtown Toronto, the exchange is owned and operated by the multinational financial services company *TMX Group*. The market serves as an international powerhouse for capital formation in natural resources, mining, oil, gas, and banking, defining the financial architecture of the Canadian corporate economy.

## you gotta know

- Officially created in 1861 by an association of twenty-four Toronto financiers meeting at the Masonic Hall, initially transacting a handful of bank and railroad securities for half an hour daily.
- Historically associated with its art deco headquarters building on Bay Street before shuttering its physical trading floor in 1997 to become one of the first major global exchanges to switch entirely to computer-automated electronic trading.
- Benchmarked by the flagship *S&P/TSX Composite Index*, which covers approximately 70 percent of total market capitalization and features heavy sectoral weightings in commercial banking and natural resource extraction.
- Recognized globally as the foremost equity venue for mining, mineral exploration, and energy ventures, listing more natural resource companies than any competing global exchange.
- Accompanied by the *TSX Venture Exchange* (TSXV), a specialized public venture capital marketplace headquartered in Calgary designed for emerging junior exploration and startup enterprises.
- Absorbed its major domestic competitor, the *Standard Stock and Mining Exchange*, in 1934 during the Great Depression, solidifying Toronto's dominance over the rival Montreal Stock Exchange.
- Endured severe institutional fallout during the 1997 *Bre-X* minerals scandal, wherein fraudulent gold claims in Busang, Indonesia, caused billions in market losses and spurred strict national standards under National Instrument 43-101.

- [[toronto-metropolitan-university]] — nearby institution whose Ted Rogers School of Management prepares business students for equities trading and commercial banking careers around the exchange.
- [[supply-and-demand]] — core economic principle governing continuous auction mechanisms, order matching books, and price discovery across listed equities.
- [[adam-smith]] — classical political economist whose analysis of commercial division of labor and financial capital markets outlines the market function of joint-stock exchanges.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical expansion of railway networks, manufacturing syndicates, and resource extraction that drove early Canadian stock market expansion.
- [[john-maynard-keynes]] — monetary theorist whose writings on speculative liquidity, investment psychology, and equity conventions mirror modern market operations.
- [[karl-marx]] — political philosopher who analyzed stock exchanges as central instruments of bourgeois property accumulation and finance capital consolidation.
