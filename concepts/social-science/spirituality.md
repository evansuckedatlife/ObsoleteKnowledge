## summary

**Spirituality** denotes the subjective, experiential dimension of human engagement with the sacred, the transcendent, or ultimate reality, functioning either within established religious traditions or as an autonomous quest for personal meaning. In the social sciences and humanities, the concept bridges the anthropological investigation of [[culture]], [[symbolism]], and [[ritual]] with psychological inquiries into altered states of consciousness, mental health, and [[self-actualization]]. Encompassing historical mysticism, contemplative asceticism, and contemporary secular practices, it reflects how individuals interpret existence, transcend the boundaries of the ego, and cultivate enduring purpose across diverse historical and civilizational contexts.

## you gotta know

- *William James* inaugurated the empirical psychological study of personal faith in *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902), arguing that unmediated mystical feelings and individual religious states form the primary phenomena from which ecclesiastical dogmas, creeds, and institutions secondarily crystallize.
- In [[humanistic-psychology]], [[abraham-maslow]] defined [[peak-experiences]] as spontaneous, ecstatic moments of profound unity, awe, and clarity, conceptualizing them as naturalistic mystical episodes essential for psychological maturation and [[self-actualization]].
- Anthropologists such as *Clifford Geertz* analyze spirituality through the lens of [[culture]] and [[symbolism]], interpreting sacred rites as cultural mechanisms that establish pervasive, long-lasting moods and motivations by formulating conceptions of a general order of existence.
- Sociologists observe a pronounced contemporary demographic shift toward individualized belief systems categorized as spiritual but not religious, reflecting widespread disillusionment with orthodox authority in [[christianity]] and [[judaism]] in favor of syncretic, personal exploration.
- In literary [[modernism]], authors turned to personal spiritual mysticism to grapple with post-war disillusionment and mechanical urbanization, as reflected in the ecstatic, visionary poetics of *Hart Crane* and the supernatural folkloric fiction of *Isaac Bashevis Singer*.
- Medieval Christian mysticism provided an institutional and creative channel for profound personal devotion, famously demonstrated by the Benedictine abbess *Hildegard of Bingen*, whose illuminated manuscripts, visionary theology, and liturgical chants expanded medieval religious expression.
- In comparative religion and Eastern philosophies, spiritual disciplines such as Buddhist mindfulness and Hindu yogic meditation emphasize transcending the illusory individual self to realize direct experiential identity with ultimate reality.
- While classical [[psychoanalysis]] under [[sigmund-freud]] skeptically dismissed spiritual sentiment as an "oceanic feeling" rooted in infantile helplessness, contemporary [[cognitive-science]] utilizes neuroimaging to study the neural substrates and therapeutic benefits of meditation and contemplative prayer.

## connections

- [[abraham-maslow]] — Conceptualized spiritual ecstasy as peak experiences that serve as the psychological summit of personal self-actualization.
- [[self-actualization]] — The highest developmental tier in humanist psychology, characterized by autonomous meaning-making and transcendent peak experiences.
- [[humanistic-psychology]] — The movement that legitimized the academic investigation of personal subjective experiences, holistic well-being, and mystical consciousness.
- [[existentialism]] — Philosophical tradition examining how individuals construct authentic personal meaning and spiritual purpose in the absence of external absolutes.
- [[ritual]] — The formal symbolic practices and patterned communal actions through which interior spiritual commitments are dramatized and sustained.
- [[culture]] — The overarching matrix of shared symbols, values, and customs that contextualizes and shapes subjective spiritual interpretations.
- [[symbolism]] — The communicative system through which ineffable mystical insights, sacred myths, and transcendent intuitions are encoded and expressed.
- [[sigmund-freud]] — Formulated foundational psychoanalytic critiques of spiritual belief, famously interpreting the oceanic feeling as a regression to infantile narcissism.
