## summary

**Communitas** is an anthropological concept denoting an intense experience of unstructured, egalitarian solidarity, shared vulnerability, and profound fellowship among individuals undergoing a transition together. Theoretical interest in the phenomenon was established by Scottish-born anthropologist *Victor Turner*, who expanded upon *Arnold van Gennep*'s pioneering work on rites of passage. The concept explains how societies periodically dissolve institutional hierarchies, social ranks, and rigid role assignments during liminal thresholds, revitalizing cultural bonds before structural order is reimposed.

## you gotta know

- Articulated by *Victor Turner* in his influential 1969 text *The Ritual Process: Structure and Anti-Structure*, drawing heavily from his ethnographic fieldwork among the *Ndembu* of Zambia.
- Arises primarily within the liminal phase of rites of passage, when initiates are stripped of status, possessions, and secular distinctions to exist betwixt and between social states.
- Differentiated by Turner into three distinct varieties: spontaneous or existential communitas, normative communitas preserved through organizational forms, and ideological communitas articulated as utopian philosophy.
- Stands in dialectical opposition to social structure, which Turner characterized as differentiated, hierarchical, rule-bound, and pragmatic.
- Applied beyond tribal rituals to explain countercultural movements, monastic orders, pilgrimage traditions, musical festivals, and spontaneous revolutionary uprisings.
- Emphasizes absolute ontological equality among participants, who frequently share uniform garb, shave their heads, or endure common physical trials.
- Typically proves transient and volatile, requiring institutionalization into formalized structures and codes or risking complete social dissolution over time.

## connections

- [[ritual]] — serves as the sacred, formal apparatus within which the liminal conditions generating communitas are deliberately staged.
- [[culture]] — provides the mythic symbols, cosmological values, and collective representations mobilized during liminal encounters.
- [[symbolism]] — supplies the sensory metaphors, bodily decorations, and sacred icons that focus collective affect during initiation.
- [[communication]] — facilitates the profound empathic attunement, choral singing, and shared bodily expressions that unite initiates.
- [[compliance]] — contrasts with the organic, unforced cohesion of communitas by relying upon institutional power, sanctions, and hierarchical obedience.
- [[franz-boas]] — laid the empirical foundation for modern cultural anthropology and relativistic understandings of diverse communal rituals.
- [[erik-erikson]] — explored psychosocial moratoriums in adolescent identity formation that parallel the liminal conditions giving rise to communitas.
