## summary

**Sociolinguistics** is the study of language in its social context—how language varies across groups, regions, and social circumstances, and how language both reflects and reinforces social structures. Pioneered by *William Labov*, sociolinguistics revealed that language is not a fixed, homogeneous system but rather constantly changing and deeply intertwined with social identity, class, ethnicity, and power. This field transformed linguistics from an abstract study of language systems to a discipline attentive to the lived reality of how people actually speak.

## you gotta know

- *William Labov* established sociolinguistics through groundbreaking studies of pronunciation variation in New York City, showing that language variation is not random but systematically tied to social class, age, and ethnicity.
- Linguistic variables such as the "r" sound drop in "car" ("cah") correlate with social class in predictable ways—lower-class speakers drop it more often, creating markers of social identity.
- Language change is driven by social mechanisms: younger speakers adopt new pronunciations or vocabulary, which spreads through social networks and across generations.
- Dialects are social and regional varieties of language; no inherent hierarchy exists between "standard" and non-standard dialects, though social power relationships assign prestige to some over others.
- Code-switching—alternating between languages or dialects—reveals bilingual and multilingual speakers' sophisticated linguistic resources and their navigation of social contexts.
- Sociolinguistics demonstrates that language cannot be divorced from society; individual speech choices index social identity, express group membership, and reproduce or resist social hierarchies.
