## summary

**Universal grammar** is *Noam Chomsky's* hypothesis that all human beings are born with an innate, species-specific faculty for language—a set of principles and constraints that make language acquisition possible and explain why all human languages share deep structural properties despite surface diversity. Rather than learning language entirely through imitation and conditioning, children rapidly acquire their native language because their brains are pre-programmed with a universal template, the same in all humans.

## you gotta know

- Proposes that humans possess an innate "language organ" or "universal grammar"—a mental system that constrains what counts as a possible human language.
- All human languages, despite apparent differences in vocabulary and sound patterns, share deep structural principles (recursion, hierarchy, discrete units) that universal grammar explains as innate constraints.
- Accounts for *poverty of the stimulus*: children acquire language so rapidly and accurately from fragmentary input that they must already know something about language structure before exposure—this prior knowledge is universal grammar.
- Explains why children make characteristic errors (like "goed" instead of "went") that reveal they are applying grammatical rules creatively, not passively imitating heard speech.
- The hypothesis shifted linguistics from asking "what do languages have in common descriptively?" to "what innate principles make language acquisition possible?"—a fundamental reorientation.
- Universal grammar is universal across all 7,000+ human languages; there is no evidence of languages lacking recursion or discrete units, suggesting the theory may capture something real about human cognition.
- Critics argue Chomsky overstated the evidence and that language learning involves more learned cultural knowledge than universal grammar alone can explain; the debate continues to shape cognitive science and linguistics.
- The theory remains controversial: some neuroscience suggests innate language circuits, while others emphasize the brain's general learning capacity and cultural transmission of linguistic knowledge.

## connections
