## summary

*Thomas Robert Malthus* was an English clergyman and economist whose "Essay on the Principle of Population" (1798) argued that human population grows geometrically while food production grows arithmetically, inevitably leading to famine, war, and misery unless population is constrained. Though many of his specific predictions proved wrong, Malthus's framework profoundly influenced economic theory, evolutionary biology, and demographic thought. His ideas remain controversial, invoked both by pessimists fearing resource depletion and criticized as justifications for ignoring poverty and inequality.

## you gotta know

- Published "An Essay on the Principle of Population" in 1798, arguing that unchecked population growth would outpace agricultural capacity, creating inevitable scarcity.
- His pessimistic theory contradicted Enlightenment optimism about progress and improvement, suggesting that population checks (famine, disease, war, or "moral restraint") were inevitable.
- *David Ricardo* was profoundly influenced by Malthus, adopting his pessimism about wages and growth into classical economics—the "iron law of wages" reflects Malthusian logic.
- *John Maynard Keynes* later admired Malthus for skepticism about automatic market equilibrium, arguing that Malthus understood savings and demand failures that later economists ignored.
- His theories influenced *Charles Darwin's* thinking on natural selection, as Darwin recognized that Malthusian population pressure created struggle for survival and adaptive advantage.
- Neo-Malthusians in the 20th century invoked population theory to argue for birth control and family planning, though critics charge Malthusianism with victim-blaming poverty and justifying inequality.
