## summary

A **conditioned reflex** is an automatic physiological or behavioral response that is acquired through the systematic pairing of a biologically potent unconditioned stimulus with a previously neutral stimulus. Discovered by Russian physiologist *Ivan Pavlov* during research on canine digestive secretions, the phenomenon demonstrated that physiological mechanisms could be systematically modified by environmental experience. The concept served as the experimental bedrock for twentieth-century behaviorism, providing a strictly mechanistic account of learning that bypassed introspective mentalism.

## you gotta know

- Discovered experimentally by *Ivan Pavlov* in the 1890s when laboratory dogs began salivating in response to predictive environmental cues such as footsteps or metronomes.
- Relies upon associative pairing where a neutral conditioned stimulus precedes an unconditioned stimulus, converting the reflex from an innate unconditioned response into a conditioned response.
- Extensively popularized in Western psychology by *John B. Watson*, who applied classical Pavlovian principles to human emotional development in the controversial 1920 Little Albert experiment.
- Exhibits predictable temporal dynamics including stimulus acquisition, spontaneous recovery following rest intervals, stimulus generalization across similar sensory inputs, and stimulus discrimination.
- Undergoes extinction when the conditioned stimulus is repeatedly presented over prolonged trials in the total absence of the unconditioned reinforcer.
- Differs fundamentally from the operant conditioning championed by *B. F. Skinner*, which focuses on voluntary behaviors shaped by subsequent reinforcements rather than involuntary autonomic responses.
- Extended by later neurobiologists such as *Eric Kandel*, who demonstrated that conditioned reflexes alter synaptic plasticity, neurotransmitter release, and neural connectivity at the cellular level.

## connections

- [[behaviorism]] — adopted the conditioned reflex as its foundational paradigm for defining learning purely as observable stimulus-response contingencies.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — developed operant conditioning to address behavioral modifications beyond the involuntary reflexes studied by Pavlov.
- [[cognitive-science]] — re-evaluated classical associative mechanisms by demonstrating that predictive contingency and cognitive expectancies mediate conditioning.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — explored how deeply ingrained social conditioning and habitual obedience compel individuals to follow malevolent institutional authority.
- [[compliance]] — incorporates conditioned habits of deference and unthinking assent triggered by recognized social cues.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — investigates how acquired environmental reactions interact with genetic canalization across early childhood stages.
- [[soviet-union]] — institutionalized Pavlovian reflexology as the officially favored ideological paradigm for materialist psychological science.
