## summary

**Positive psychology** is a modern movement in psychological science that studies human strengths, well-being, flourishing, and optimal functioning rather than pathology and mental illness. Emerging in the late 20th century as a deliberate rebalancing of psychology's focus, positive psychology inherits the humanistic emphasis on growth and potential while applying rigorous scientific methods to understanding what makes life meaningful and fulfilling.

## you gotta know

- The field formally crystallized in the 1990s under Martin Seligman's leadership; he argued psychology had become overly focused on treating dysfunction and neglected studying what helps people thrive.
- Positive psychology studies empirically investigated virtues like courage, justice, wisdom, and temperance; specific practices like gratitude, mindfulness, and meaning-making; and life conditions that support flourishing.
- Key concepts include well-being (subjective and psychological), resilience (recovery from adversity), flow state (complete absorption in challenging activity), and meaning-making (connecting life experiences to larger narratives).
- The field integrates humanistic psychology's emphasis on human potential with empirical methods; positive psychologists conduct experiments, longitudinal studies, and randomized trials—not merely introspection.
- Positive psychology interventions have shown modest but real effects: gratitude practices increase well-being, strength-based approaches improve academic and work performance, and meaning-focused therapy reduces depression.
- Criticisms note that positive psychology sometimes lacks cultural sensitivity (well-being defined in Western individualist terms), oversimplifies complex phenomena, and risks becoming reductive motivational rhetoric.
- The movement has influenced education, organizational development, clinical psychology, and coaching; it represents a paradigm shift toward prevention and flourishing rather than only fixing problems.
- Seligman's PERMA model identifies five pillars of flourishing: Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, and Accomplishment; interventions target these domains to build overall well-being.
- Positive psychology distinguishes between hedonic well-being (pleasure, satisfaction) and eudaimonic well-being (meaningful living, growth, purpose); both matter but fulfill different human needs.
- Character strengths—identified through the VIA Character Strengths inventory—form the foundation of positive psychology; identifying and developing personal strengths is more effective than focusing solely on fixing weaknesses.
