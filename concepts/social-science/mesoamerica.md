## summary

**Mesoamerica** encompasses the cultural region spanning central Mexico through Central America where some of history's most sophisticated pre-Columbian civilizations developed independently of Old World influence. From the *Olmec* around 1500 BCE through the *Aztec* empire at European contact, Mesoamerican societies developed monumental architecture, complex mathematics including the concept of zero, sophisticated astronomical knowledge, and intricate writing systems. These civilizations demonstrate that complex civilization emerged through multiple independent pathways in human history.

## you gotta know

- The *Olmec* civilization (1500–400 BCE) laid the cultural foundation for later Mesoamerican societies, establishing patterns of urban organization, ritual practices, and artistic traditions that persisted for millennia.
- *Maize cultivation*—the domestication of wild teosinte into productive maize—occurred in Mesoamerica and became the economic foundation supporting population concentration and urban development.
- The *Maya* civilization achieved extraordinary intellectual accomplishments: a sophisticated writing system based on logograms and phonetic signs, mathematical notation including the concept of zero centuries before Eurasian mathematicians, and astronomical calculations predicting celestial events.
- *Chichén Itzá* represents the cultural and astronomical sophistication of late Mesoamerican civilization; the site's architecture and astronomical alignments demonstrate advanced engineering and mathematical knowledge.
- The *Aztec* empire (1428–1521) built the largest city in the pre-Columbian Americas, Tenochtitlán, with intricate city planning, trade networks, and administrative systems rivaling Old World civilizations.
- Mesoamerican civilizations independently invented *writing systems* and maintained sophisticated *astronomical knowledge*—achievements that rank alongside similar developments in Egypt, Mesopotamia, and China.
- The *Mesoamerican calendar*, incorporating a 365-day solar year and a 260-day ritual cycle, reflects astronomical precision rivaling Old World traditions; the Maya predicted Venus cycles with remarkable accuracy centuries before their validation by European telescopes.
