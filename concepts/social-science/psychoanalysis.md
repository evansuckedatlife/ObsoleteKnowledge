## summary

**Psychoanalysis** is a psychological theory and therapeutic method pioneered by *Sigmund Freud*, emphasizing the role of the unconscious mind, repressed conflicts, and early childhood experiences in shaping personality and behavior. As both a theory of mind and a clinical practice, psychoanalysis has profoundly influenced Western culture, literature, art, and psychology, even as its scientific basis remains contested. Its core insight—that much of human motivation lies outside conscious awareness—remains influential in contemporary psychology and psychiatry.

## you gotta know

- Freud posited that the unconscious mind harbors repressed desires, traumatic memories, and instinctual drives (libido) that influence behavior, dreams, and personality development outside conscious awareness.
- The psychoanalytic method involves free association (speaking without censorship) and dream analysis (analyzing symbolic content) to access unconscious material and resolve internal conflicts through catharsis and insight.
- Psychosexual development theory proposes that personality develops through childhood stages (oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital), with fixations at early stages causing later neurosis and character types.
- The Oedipal complex, wherein children harbor unconscious desires for the opposite-sex parent and rivalry with the same-sex parent, became Freud's most famous—and criticized—concept, invoked to explain neurosis and gender roles.
- Defense mechanisms are unconscious psychological strategies (repression, projection, denial, sublimation, rationalization) that protect the ego from anxiety and unacceptable impulses by distorting reality.
- Later psychoanalysts like *Carl Jung*, *Alfred Adler*, and *Karen Horney* modified Freud's framework while preserving the emphasis on unconscious processes and talk therapy; Jung emphasized the collective unconscious and archetypes.
- Modern neuroscience has challenged psychoanalytic theory's empirical basis; contemporary psychoanalysis has repositioned itself as a philosophical perspective on meaning-making rather than a scientific account of mental mechanisms.
- Despite criticisms, psychoanalytic concepts permeate Western culture—Freudian slips, defense mechanisms, and the talking cure remain influential in popular psychology, literature, and art.
