## summary

**Empathy** is the capacity to recognize, comprehend, and vicariously experience the emotional states, perspectives, and mental dispositions of another person. Originating as an aesthetic concept in late nineteenth-century *[[germany]]* before becoming an anchor of twentieth-century clinical psychology in the *[[united-states]]*, it distinguishes itself from sympathy through its focus on accurate internal perspective-taking. It remains an essential cornerstone of clinical psychotherapy, developmental ethics, prosocial research, and social cognitive neuroscience.

## you gotta know

- Originated as the German aesthetic term *Einfühlung* ("feeling into"), coined by *Robert Vischer* and *Theodor Lipps* to explain how observers mentally project their own physical and emotional sensations into works of art.
- *Edward B. Titchener* translated *Einfühlung* into English as **empathy** in 1909, deliberately adapting the ancient Greek *empatheia* to give psychology a rigorous scientific counterpart to sympathy.
- *Carl Rogers* established empathy as one of the three non-negotiable "core conditions" of person-centered *[[humanistic-psychology]]*, asserting that sensing the client's private world "as if" it were one's own, without losing the "as if" quality, is essential for constructive personality growth.
- Psychologists divide the construct into *affective empathy* (the visceral emotional resonance felt in response to another's state) and *cognitive empathy* (the deliberate mental capacity to model another's perspective, closely aligned with Theory of Mind).
- Cognitive neuroscientists identified *mirror neurons* in primate premotor cortex, providing a neurobiological mechanism for immediate sensory and emotional resonance when observing others' actions and facial expressions.
- *Simon Baron-Cohen* proposed the empathizing-systemizing theory and developed the Empathy Quotient (EQ), framing variations in empathic attunement alongside neurodevelopmental conditions.
- *Daniel Batson* formulated the empathy-altruism hypothesis, demonstrating empirically that genuine empathic concern produces truly altruistic motivation to alleviate another's suffering, rather than mere egoistic distress relief.

## connections

- [[humanistic-psychology]] — clinical school founded on therapist empathy, unconditional positive regard, and congruence.
- [[abraham-maslow]] — theorist whose hierarchy of needs highlights self-transcendence, interpersonal attunement, and psychological health.
- [[self-actualization]] — the realization of personal potential, cultivated within empathic, growth-facilitating relationships.
- [[emotion-theory]] — frameworks analyzing the physiological and cognitive components that constitute emotional resonance between individuals.
- [[cognitive-science]] — interdisciplinary field exploring perspective-taking, neural mirroring, and Theory of Mind computations.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — discipline tracing the developmental stages of perspective-taking in infants and children studied by *[[jean-piaget]]*.
- [[conformity]] — social psychological phenomenon where group pressure and interpersonal sensitivity override independent judgment.
