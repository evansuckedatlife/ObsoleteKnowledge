## summary

**Defense mechanisms** are unconscious psychological strategies the mind deploys to protect the ego from distressing thoughts, feelings, or impulses. Theorized by *Sigmund Freud*, they operate automatically to reduce anxiety and maintain psychological equilibrium when the ego faces internal or external threats. Understanding defense mechanisms remains central to psychoanalytic theory and modern psychology. While Freud's theory has been challenged and refined, the core insight that people unconsciously manage unwanted emotions has proven remarkably durable and continues to inform contemporary clinical practice.

## you gotta know

- Operate *unconsciously* — a person typically does not recognize they are employing them.
- Deployed by the *ego* to mediate conflict between the id's impulses and the superego's restrictions.
- *Repression* is the most fundamental mechanism: burying unacceptable thoughts or memories in the unconscious.
- *Projection* attributes one's own unacceptable impulses to others ("I don't dislike him; he dislikes me").
- *Sublimation* channels unacceptable impulses into socially valued activities (aggression into sports).
- *Rationalization* constructs logical-sounding but false justifications for behavior ("I don't care about the party; I had work anyway").
- *Denial* simply refuses to acknowledge distressing reality.
- Range from more *primitive* (younger children, severe stress) to more *mature* (humor, altruism).
- *Reaction formation* converts unacceptable impulses into their opposites (unconscious hatred expressed as exaggerated affection).
- *Intellectualization* uses abstract reasoning to avoid emotional pain ("I find this breakup an interesting case study in attachment theory").
- *Compartmentalization* keeps contradictory thoughts or feelings in separate mental spaces, preventing cognitive dissonance.
- *Displacement* redirects emotional impulses toward a safer target (kicking the dog after being scolded by one's boss).
- Hierarchically organized: mature defenses (humor, sublimation) are more adaptive than primitive ones (denial, projection).
