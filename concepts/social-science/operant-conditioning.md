## summary

**Operant Conditioning** is the learning process through which behavior is shaped by its consequences—rewards that increase behavior and punishments that decrease it. Developed by *B. F. Skinner* in the mid-20th century, this framework explains how organisms learn to modify their actions based on environmental feedback. Unlike classical conditioning, which pairs stimuli involuntarily, operant conditioning is about voluntary behavior followed by outcomes that reinforce or discourage repetition.

## you gotta know

- The core mechanism: a behavior followed by a positive consequence becomes more likely to recur; followed by a negative consequence, it becomes less likely. This simple principle explains vast behavioral repertoires.
- *B. F. Skinner* discovered this through experiments with rats and pigeons in controlled environments, later called "Skinner boxes," establishing the empirical foundations of behaviorism.
- Positive reinforcement (reward) and negative reinforcement (removal of something unpleasant) both *increase* behavior; punishment *decreases* it. These four combinations (positive/negative × reinforce/punish) exhaustively map behavioral consequences.
- Schedules of reinforcement (continuous, fixed-ratio, variable-ratio, fixed-interval, variable-interval) produce different learning curves; variable schedules create stronger, more persistent, extinction-resistant behavior because unpredictability sustains hope.
- The approach has practical applications in education (token economies), management, animal training, behavioral therapy, and behavioral economics. It is central to applied behavior analysis (ABA).
- Classical and operant conditioning differ fundamentally: classical conditions *automatic* responses (salivation, fear) through stimulus pairing; operant conditions *voluntary* behavior through consequences. Both create learning but through different mechanisms.
- Critics argue operant conditioning underestimates internal mental processes, cognition, expectations, and the limits of purely behavioral explanations; cognitive science emerged partly as a response.
- Technological integration: video games, social media, and reward systems deliberately exploit operant conditioning principles to create compulsive use patterns.
