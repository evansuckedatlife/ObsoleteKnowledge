## summary

**Authority** is the recognized right of an individual or institution to make decisions, issue commands, and expect compliance from others. It arises from social recognition, legal structures, or traditions that grant certain actors legitimacy in governing behaviour. Authority is central to understanding how societies organize power and maintain order.

## you gotta know

- Distinguished from raw *power* — authority implies *consent* or acceptance by those being governed, even if that consent is coerced or implicit.
- *Legitimate authority* derives its force from shared norms, law, tradition, or expertise; *illegitimate authority* governs through fear or deception alone.
- Psychological experiments like the [[milgram-obedience|Milgram studies]] reveal how ordinary people will comply with authority figures even when asked to inflict harm.
- The [[stanford-prison|Stanford Prison Experiment]] demonstrated how situational authority (guards vs. prisoners) can rapidly reshape behaviour and social dynamics.
- Authority structures exist in families, schools, workplaces, militaries, and governments — embedded in *hierarchy* and formal roles.
- Anthropologist [[bronislaw-malinowski]] observed that all functional societies vest authority in chieftains, elders, or councils to coordinate collective action.
- Challenge to authority often precedes social change — revolutions, civil rights movements, and reformations all hinge on questioning established legitimacy.
- Psychologist [[stanley-milgram]] documented how perceived authority figures (experimenter in a lab coat) could command obedience to harmful acts; **authority** requires no violence, just submission to someone perceived as having the right to command.
- Authority differs from *influence* — you may be influenced by a peer's suggestion, but authority is obeyed because disobeying feels transgressive or dangerous.
- In organizations, authority can be *de jure* (formal, codified in law or rules) or *de facto* (exercised informally by those with practical power).

## connections

- [[obedience]] — the behavioural response to authority; often automatic rather than consciously reasoned.
- [[hierarchy]] — the social structure through which authority flows from top to bottom.
- [[legitimacy]] — the source of authority's power; loses force when questioned.
- [[ritual]] — ceremonies and practices that reinforce and transmit authority relationships.
- [[milgram-obedience]] — landmark study on how far people will obey authority figures.
- [[stanford-prison]] — experimental demonstration of authority's situational force.
- [[power]] — the underlying capacity to enforce will; distinct from the social recognition that is authority.
