## summary

**Jericho** is one of the world's oldest known permanent human settlements, located in the modern-day West Bank in the Levant. Founded roughly 9,600 years ago, it exemplifies the transition from nomadic hunting-gathering to settled agricultural life during the Neolithic Revolution. Archaeological evidence reveals a fortified settlement with defensive walls, suggesting early complex social organization, trade networks, and concentrated population—making it a window into humanity's first urban experiments.

## you gotta know

- Jericho was occupied continuously from approximately 9600 BCE; at its early peak, it supported around 2,000 inhabitants—extraordinary for its time and suggesting centralized coordination.
- The famous *walls of Jericho*, discovered by archaeologists, were likely defensive structures protecting the community; their presence indicates both significant construction capability and external threat—early evidence of organized conflict.
- The site shows evidence of *pre-pottery Neolithic* occupation, predating ceramic technology; inhabitants used stone tools and relied on agriculture and gathering rather than hunting.
- *Domestication* appears underway: while full grain agriculture did not yet dominate, residents cultivated crops and possibly herded animals, marking the transitional economy between foraging and farming.
- Archaeological layers reveal *cultural development* over centuries: earlier phases show simpler structures and tools; later phases show more sophisticated architecture, suggesting technological and organizational advancement.
- Jericho's location on a freshwater spring made it attractive; access to water was crucial for early agricultural settlements dependent on irrigation, making spring-fed sites prime locations for civilization emergence.
- The site's continuous occupation from 9600 BCE onward makes it an invaluable *time capsule* for understanding how societies evolved; archaeological layers preserve evidence of technological, artistic, and social development over millennia.
- *Tower structures* at Jericho suggest not merely defense but also ceremonial or symbolic functions; their construction required coordinated community labor, revealing emerging capacity for collective organization.
