## summary

**Albert Bandura** was an influential Canadian-American psychologist at *Stanford University* who transformed modern psychology by introducing social learning theory and bridging the divide between strict *[[behaviorism]]* and *[[cognitive-science]]*. He is most celebrated for his landmark 1961 *Bobo doll experiment*, which demonstrated that children acquire behaviors through observational learning and imitation rather than direct conditioning alone. His subsequent formulations of self-efficacy and reciprocal determinism established agency and cognitive mediation as central pillars of *[[developmental-psychology]]*.

## you gotta know

- Conducted the famous 1961 *Bobo doll experiment* at *Stanford University*, demonstrating that nursery school children exposed to an aggressive adult model spontaneously beat, kicked, and verbally assaulted an inflatable clown doll when later placed in a room with toys.
- Formulated *social learning theory*, proposing that individuals learn novel responses by observing the actions of others and the resulting consequences, a process known as *vicarious reinforcement*.
- Expanded his model into *social cognitive theory*, introducing *reciprocal determinism* to explain how personal cognitive factors, environmental stimuli, and overt behaviors continuously interact and influence one another bidirectionally.
- Established the concept of *self-efficacy*, defining it as an individual's belief in their capability to execute courses of action required to attain designated goals, which profoundly influences motivation, perseverance, and emotional resilience.
- Identified four necessary cognitive steps for effective observational learning: *attention* to perceive the model, *retention* to mentally encode the behavior, *motor reproduction* to execute the action physically, and *motivation* to enact it based on anticipated rewards.
- Examined *moral disengagement*, detailing how individuals bypass internal moral self-regulation to justify harmful actions through rationalizations like moral justification, diffusion of responsibility, and dehumanization.
- Served as president of the *American Psychological Association* in 1974 and received the *National Medal of Science* from the *[[united-states]]* in 2016 for his lifelong contributions to the behavioral sciences.

## connections

- [[behaviorism]] — theoretical paradigm whose operant conditioning models he challenged by demonstrating that learning occurs without direct external reinforcement.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — radical behaviorist whose strict rejection of internal mental constructs stood in direct contrast to Bandura's observational and mediational frameworks.
- [[cognitive-science]] — emerging discipline whose focus on mental representation helped shape his evolution from social learning to social cognitive theory.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — discipline profoundly shaped by his findings on childhood socialization, imitation, media violence, and aggressive behavioral acquisition.
- [[jean-piaget]] — developmental psychologist whose stage-based constructivism offered an alternative cognitive framework for understanding child development.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — social psychologist whose obedience studies paralleled Bandura's subsequent work on moral disengagement and situational harm.
- [[conformity]] — social dynamic closely linked to his findings on peer modeling, imitation, and social transmission of group norms.
- [[united-states]] — country where he spent his distinguished academic career at *Stanford University* and received national honors.
