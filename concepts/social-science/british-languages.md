## summary

The **British languages**, traditionally termed the Brythonic or Brittonic languages, constitute one of the two primary branches of the Insular Celtic family alongside the Goidelic languages. Derived from Common Brittonic, an ancient language spoken throughout southern and central Great Britain prior to the Roman conquest, the family today includes Welsh, Cornish, and mainland Breton, as well as extinct idioms like Cumbric. These tongues are characterized typologically by P-Celtic phonological shifts, initial consonant mutations, and complex verb-subject-object syntactic structures.

## you gotta know

- Evolved from Common Brittonic, which was spoken across lowland and upland Britain prior to Roman subjugation and the subsequent arrival of Germanic-speaking Anglo-Saxons.
- Exemplify the *P-Celtic* phonological division within Celtic linguistics, having systematically shifted Proto-Indo-European labiovelar *kw* to the voiceless bilabial plosive *p*, contrasting with the Goidelic *Q-Celtic* reflex.
- Diverged by the sixth century into Western Brittonic (which yielded Welsh and the extinct northern Cumbric) and Southwestern Brittonic (which gave rise to Cornish and continental [[breton]]).
- Feature robust initial consonant mutations, where word-initial consonants alter phonetically according to the preceding lexical trigger, gender, or case relation.
- Preserved early poetic traditions such as the *Y Gododdin*, an Old Welsh elegy attributed to the bard Aneirin memorializing the Battle of Catraeth in northern Britain.
- Subsumed an extensive substrate and superstrate interaction with Latin during centuries of integration within the *Roman Empire*, absorbing significant civic and ecclesiastical vocabulary.
- Modern revitalization has witnessed the revival of Cornish from dormancy alongside the institutional protection of Welsh via legal language acts.

## connections

- [[breton]] — the Southwestern Brythonic tongue transplanted to the European mainland during the early medieval migration to Brittany.
- [[linguistics]] — the discipline governing comparative reconstruction of Proto-Celtic, sound laws, and P-Celtic innovations.
- [[sociolinguistics]] — the study of regional language revitalization, language planning, and diglossia across Wales and Cornwall.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial power whose occupation of Britannia heavily influenced Common Brittonic vocabulary and orthography.
- [[england]] — the territory where Brittonic speech was steadily supplanted by Old English following early medieval migrations.
- [[culture]] — the shared folklore, bardic poetry, and musical traditions unifying the historical Brittonic peoples.
