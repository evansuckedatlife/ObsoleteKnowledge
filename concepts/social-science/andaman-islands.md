## summary

The **Andaman Islands** are an archipelago situated in the Bay of Bengal between India and Myanmar, renowned in social science as a foundational field site for structural-functionalist anthropology. Inhabited for thousands of years by distinct indigenous Negrito groups who maintained hunter-gatherer lifeways in relative isolation, the islands later became an infamous British penal colony. The ethnography of its traditional societies reshaped anthropological understandings of social cohesion, ritual solidarity, and cultural vulnerability under colonial expansion.

## you gotta know

- British social anthropologist *A. R. Radcliffe-Brown* conducted intensive fieldwork in the archipelago between 1906 and 1908, culminating in his seminal monograph *The Andaman Islanders* (1922).
- Radcliffe-Brown's theoretical analysis of Andamanese ceremonial weeping and dancing laid the groundwork for *structural functionalism*, arguing that collective rites maintain societal harmony and reinforce vital social values.
- The indigenous peoples belong to distinct linguistic and cultural divisions, historically including the *Great Andamanese*, *Onge*, *Jarawa*, and *Sentinelese*.
- The *Sentinelese* of North Sentinel Island remain one of the world's most isolated uncontacted peoples, actively resisting outside contact and protected by strict Indian government non-interference laws.
- The British East India Company surveyed the archipelago in the late eighteenth century, later establishing a permanent penal colony at *Port Blair* following the Indian Rebellion of 1857.
- The *Cellular Jail*, constructed by the British between 1896 and 1906, was designed as a panopticon to isolate political prisoners and Indian independence activists in solitary confinement.
- Contact with British colonial administrators and penal outposts led to catastrophic demographic collapse among the *Great Andamanese* due to imported infectious diseases like measles and syphilis.

## connections

- [[culture]] — primary anthropological domain illuminated by the study of Andamanese expressive traditions, kinship customs, and foraging adaptations.
- [[ritual]] — core sociological phenomenon analyzed by Radcliffe-Brown to illustrate how communal practices express emotional dependence on the social group.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial government that transformed the islands into an offshore carceral outpost and administered the catastrophic pacification of indigenous populations.
- [[colonialism]] — global historical dynamic that brought epidemic devastation, territorial expropriation, and administrative disruption to native island societies.
- [[ancient-trade]] — expansive maritime exchange networks that skirted the Bay of Bengal for millennia while leaving the interior islanders remarkably genetically and culturally secluded.
- [[symbolism]] — system of cultural meanings and mythological narratives through which Andamanese groups interpreted natural forces, spirits, and ancestors.
- [[franz-boas]] — contemporary pioneer of modern ethnographic rigor whose relativistic methodology contrasted with Radcliffe-Brown's universalizing functional laws.
