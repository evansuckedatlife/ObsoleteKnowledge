## summary

**Group dynamics** is the study of how individuals behave differently in groups than they do in isolation, and how groups develop norms, hierarchies, and identities. Central to understanding group dynamics are phenomena like conformity, leadership, cooperation, and conflict. The field bridges psychology and sociology, revealing that groups are not merely collections of individuals but entities with their own emergent properties. A group's behavior cannot be predicted simply by summing its members' individual preferences or personalities.

## you gotta know

- Groups develop *norms* — shared expectations about appropriate behavior that members internalize and enforce.
- *Conformity pressure* arises when individuals modify their behavior to align with group expectations, even against their own judgment.
- *Solomon Asch's* conformity experiments revealed that people will publicly agree with obviously incorrect statements to match group consensus.
- Groups exhibit *social facilitation* (better performance on easy tasks) and *social inhibition* (worse performance on difficult tasks).
- *Social loafing* occurs when individuals exert less effort in groups than alone; responsibility diffuses.
- *Groupthink* is a pathological form of group consensus where dissent is suppressed and poor decisions result.
- Leadership roles emerge naturally; formal and informal leaders shape group direction and cohesion.
- Groups can display *deindividuation* — loss of personal identity and self-consciousness that reduces moral restraint, often amplified by anonymity.
- *Group polarization* occurs when discussion amplifies initial tendencies; a moderately liberal group becomes more liberal after deliberation.
- *In-group bias* leads members to favor their own group's outcomes and judge in-group behavior more charitably than out-group behavior.
- *Task interdependence* affects cohesion: groups with tightly linked tasks develop stronger bonds and more intensive communication patterns.
