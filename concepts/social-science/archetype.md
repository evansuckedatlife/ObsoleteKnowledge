## summary

An **archetype** is a recurring cross-cultural symbol, motif, character template, or narrative framework that reappears persistently throughout global myth, folklore, religion, and literature. Synthesized extensively by comparative mythologists such as *James George Frazer* in *The Golden Bough* and structural critics like *Northrop Frye*, it delineates universal patterns of human storytelling and ceremonial life. In quiz bowl and literary theory, the concept serves as the foundational lens for identifying recurring narrative paradigms—such as the dying-and-rising god, the heroic quest, the deluge, and the trickster—across geographically isolated societies.

## you gotta know

- Comparative mythologist *James George Frazer* cataloged global manifestations of the dying-and-rising god motif in *The Golden Bough*, linking agrarian fertility rites to seasonal cycles.
- Literary critic *Northrop Frye* systemized archetypal criticism in *Anatomy of Criticism* (1957), categorizing literary modes and genres into cyclical seasons corresponding to human mythic drama.
- *Joseph Campbell* popularized the monomyth or "Hero's Journey" in *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949), tracing a seventeen-stage cyclical trial shared by world legends.
- Anthropological foundations trace through *Edward Burnett Tylor* and structural anthropology, noting recurrent symbols like the flood narrative found in Mesopotamia, India, and Mesoamerica.
- The trickster figure represents an archetype that transcends continents, embodied variously by *Loki* in Norse myth, *Anansi* in West Africa, *Coyote* among *[[native-americans]]*, and *Hermes* in *[[ancient-greece]]*.
- The world tree or axis mundi, such as *Yggdrasil* or the Mesoamerican *Ceiba*, recurs universally as a cosmic bridge connecting subterranean realms, earth, and the heavens.
- Critical debates distinguish structural and cultural transmission theories (diffusionism) from innate psychic models, questioning whether recurrent motifs arise from shared human experience or cultural borrowing.

## connections

- [[archetypes]] — the psychological substrate in the collective unconscious underlying these mythic figures.
- [[symbolism]] — the broader practice of representing abstract ideals and psychic realities through concrete signs.
- [[ritual]] — traditional patterned ceremonial behaviors that enact and reinforce mythic narratives.
- [[culture]] — the overarching human behavioral and belief systems within which mythic motifs circulate.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — the psychological tradition that interrogated narrative symbolism to decode the human mind.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical culture whose mythological pantheon supplied defining archetypal narratives for Western literature.
- [[native-americans]] — indigenous traditions celebrated for trickster narratives and earth-diver cosmologies.
