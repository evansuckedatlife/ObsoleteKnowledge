## summary

A **price signal** is the information that market prices convey to producers and consumers about relative scarcity, demand, and production costs. When prices rise, they signal that a good is becoming scarce or demand is increasing, prompting producers to expand production and consumers to reduce consumption; when prices fall, they signal surplus, prompting opposite adjustments. Price signals are the market's decentralized communication mechanism, allowing millions of independent decisions to coordinate without central authority.

## you gotta know

- Price signals operate through the *price mechanism*: prices adjust automatically in response to supply and demand imbalances, conveying information without requiring bureaucratic coordination or explicit communication.
- Rising prices incentivize producers to increase supply (expanding production is more profitable) and incentivize consumers to seek substitutes or reduce consumption—two adjustments that restore balance without compulsion.
- Falling prices incentivize producers to reduce supply or shift to more profitable goods, and incentivize consumers to increase consumption—again, automatic rebalancing without central command.
- *Information aggregation*: prices reflect dispersed information about preferences, production costs, and availability; a single price number condenses vast amounts of information that decision-makers need.
- *Shortage and surplus* create price pressure: scarcity drives prices up until quantity demanded equals quantity supplied; excess supply drives prices down until markets clear.
- Price signals fail when markets are *distorted*: monopolies restrict output to raise prices artificially, information asymmetries prevent informed decisions, or externalities cause market prices to misrepresent social costs.
- *Price controls* imposed by governments (price ceilings or floors) override price signals, often creating unintended consequences: rent controls reduce housing supply, minimum wages can increase unemployment—signals suppressed reappear as quantity adjustments.
