## summary

**Homo erectus** (traditionally translated as "**Upright Man**") is an extinct species of archaic human that flourished throughout the Pleistocene epoch from approximately 1.9 million years ago until roughly 110,000 years ago. Originating in East Africa, it was the first hominin to disperse across the continents of Asia and Europe, establishing a widespread geographical presence from the Republic of Georgia to the Indonesian archipelago. The species represents an indispensable evolutionary threshold, bridging primitive, small-brained australopithecines with modern humans and introducing vital technological, anatomical, and behavioral innovations.

## you gotta know

- First hominin to undertake extensive intercontinental migration outside Africa, establishing long-lived regional populations across Eurasia, including Georgia, China, and Indonesia.
- Mastered the production of the Acheulean stone tool industry, pioneering standardized, symmetrical, bifacial handaxes and cleavers that superseded earlier rudimentary Oldowan pebble choppers during the Lower [[paleolithic]].
- Achieved the controlled mastery of fire for cooking food, nighttime illumination, and warmth, a transformative behavioural breakthrough that softened meat, unlocked rich nutritional calories, and fostered early camp-based [[culture]].
- Famous worldwide for historically pivotal fossil specimens such as *Java Man*, discovered on the Solo River by Dutch anatomist *Eugène Dubois* in 1891, and *Peking Man*, excavated from the limestone fissures of *Zhoukoudian* before vanishing during the chaos of [[world-war-ii]].
- Displayed fully committed obligate bipedalism, narrow pelvic dimensions, and modern body proportions, shedding arboreal adaptations in favor of efficient long-distance walking and endurance running.
- Exhibited marked encephalization, with brain volumes ranging between 800 and 1,100 cubic centimeters, housed within a low, elongated cranial vault crowned with a thick supraorbital torus, prominent brow ridge, and sagittal keel.
- Exemplified by the celebrated *Turkana Boy* (also catalogued as *Nariokotome Boy*), an extraordinarily complete 1.6-million-year-old juvenile skeleton discovered near Lake Turkana in Kenya by *Kamoya Kimeu* and *Richard Leakey*.
- Delineated culturally by the *Movius Line*, an influential taxonomic boundary proposed by archaeologist *Hallam Movius* to distinguish Western Acheulean handaxe traditions from persistent Eastern Asian chopper and flake tool traditions.

## connections

- [[culture]] — transmitted social tradition manifested in the standardized manufacturing templates of bifacial tools, group hearth usage, and coordinated collective hunting expeditions.
- [[dna]] — genetic medium whose evolutionary divergence drove critical anatomical transitions, including the expansion of cranial capacity and modern limb elongation.
- [[australopithecus]] — earlier, small-brained African hominin grade with pronounced facial prognathism and residual arboreal adaptations that preceded the emergence of the genus *Homo*.
- [[homo-sapiens]] — anatomically modern human lineage that evolved in Africa hundreds of thousands of years later, inheriting Acheulean fire traditions while evolving high vaulted skulls and complex syntactic speech.
- [[cognitive-science]] — interdisciplinary field studying hominin mental evolution, working memory expansion, causal reasoning, and the cognitive mapping required for bifacial lithic flaking.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict during which the irreplaceable type fossils of *Peking Man* mysteriously vanished en route to the United States Marines in 1941.
- [[ritual]] — structured social practice whose archaeological origins are traced to the communal solidarity, evening gatherings, and hearthside interactions catalyzed by the domestic control of fire.
