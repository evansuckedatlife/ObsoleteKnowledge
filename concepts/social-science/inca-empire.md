## summary

The **Inca Empire** (*Tawantinsuyu*) was the largest pre-Columbian state in the Americas, spanning much of western South America from around 1400 to 1533. Centered in the [[andes|Andean]] highlands, it unified diverse ethnic groups through sophisticated administration, road systems, and engineering, leaving a legacy in architecture, agriculture, and language.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a regional power in the [[andes|Andes]] in the early 15th century and rapidly expanded through military conquest and diplomacy under leaders like Pachacuti.
- **Tawantinsuyu** means "land of the four quarters" — the empire was divided into four regions radiating from the capital at [[Cusco]], in what is now Peru.
- Built an extraordinary network of roads spanning over 40,000 kilometers through mountain terrain, deserts, and jungles — engineering feat that still amazes.
- Developed an intricate *administrative system* without written language (used [[quipu]], knotted strings, for record-keeping); organized labor through the *mit'a* system where citizens owed labour service to the state.
- Architecture and stonework were legendary for precision — fitted stone blocks at [[machu-picchu]] and other sites without mortar, designed to withstand earthquakes; Inca stonework at Cusco still stands after centuries.
- *Agricultural innovation*: Incas developed terracing and irrigation systems suited to the harsh Andean environment, growing potatoes, maize, and quinoa; they kept seed stores (*qollqas*) for famine relief.
- Collapsed rapidly after Spanish conquest in 1533; disease, superior weaponry, and internal divisions allowed Spanish conquistadors to conquer a vast empire with small forces; the Inca had recently emerged from a civil war.
- The Inca road system facilitated Spanish conquest by enabling rapid troop movement; ironically, the empire's administrative genius could not resist European pathogens and steel.
- Quechua language and Inca cultural practices persist in Andean communities today; [[machu-picchu]] and other sites draw millions of visitors and remain symbols of pre-Columbian sophistication.

## connections

- [[machu-picchu]] — iconic Inca fortress city high in the mountains; symbol of Inca engineering and architecture.
- [[quechua]] — the language of the Incas; still spoken by millions in the Andes today.
- [[peru]] — the modern nation that inherits the Inca legacy; Cusco remains a major city.
- [[andes]] — the mountain range where the Inca Empire was centered and which shaped its distinctive culture.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the Spanish conquest of the Inca in 1533 marks a collision between European and Inca worlds.
- [[incas]] — the ethnic group at the empire's core; now used to refer to the pre-Columbian civilization.
- [[pre-columbian-americas]] — the Inca were one of the greatest civilizations before European arrival.
