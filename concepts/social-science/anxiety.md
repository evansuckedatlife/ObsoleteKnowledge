## summary

**Anxiety** is a future-oriented emotional state characterized by subjective feelings of apprehension, worried thoughts, and physiological signs of autonomic nervous system arousal. First rigorously theorized within Western psychology by *Sigmund Freud*, the construct became central to [[psychoanalysis]] as an alarm mechanism alerting the conscious ego to impending internal conflict or repressed unconscious impulses. In modern psychopathology and [[cognitive-science]], anxiety is distinguished from acute fear by its diffuse, anticipatory nature, serving as both a primary diagnostic category and a foundational subject in clinical quiz-bowl literature.

## you gotta know

- Differentiated from acute fear by *Sigmund Freud* and later clinicians; fear represents an immediate reaction to an identifiable external threat, whereas anxiety is diffuse, persistent, and anticipatory.
- Analyzed in *Sigmund Freud*'s 1926 monograph *Inhibitions, Symptoms and Anxiety*, which revised his earlier toxicological libido theory into the concept of signal anxiety deployed by the ego.
- Categorized in classical psychoanalytic theory into three distinct forms: realistic anxiety regarding external dangers, neurotic anxiety concerning instinctual id drives, and moral anxiety generated by the superego.
- Formulated as an existential condition, often termed **Angst**, by *Søren Kierkegaard* in *The Concept of Anxiety*, where he famously described it as the *dizziness of freedom* when contemplating choice.
- Measured empirically by *Charles Spielberger* via the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory, which separates transient emotional states from enduring personality dispositions.
- Conceptualized within [[behaviorism]] as a classically conditioned avoidance response, illustrated historically by the controversial *Little Albert* experiment conducted by *John B. Watson*.
- Mediated neurobiologically through hyperactivation of the amygdala and dysregulation of gamma-aminobutyric acid pathways, prompting treatment through cognitive behavioral therapy and anxiolytic medications.

## connections

- [[sigmund-freud]] — formulated the structural model identifying signal anxiety as an ego alarm.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — utilizes defensive suppression to prevent intolerable psychic tension.
- [[existentialism]] — treats profound existential dread as an inescapable feature of human freedom.
- [[apperception]] — directs conscious attention toward selective appraisal of perceived internal threats.
- [[behaviorism]] — interprets phobic responses as conditioned reflexes sustained by avoidant reinforcement.
- [[cognitive-science]] — studies attentional bias and catastrophic misinterpretations in panic disorders.
- [[humanistic-psychology]] — links emotional incongruence to thwarted movement toward self-actualization.
