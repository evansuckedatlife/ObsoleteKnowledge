## summary

**Martin Seligman** is an eminent American psychologist and educator in the [[united-states]] who revolutionized modern clinical psychology and [[cognitive-science|cognitive theory]] through his discovery of *learned helplessness*. Conducting landmark conditioning experiments alongside *Steven F. Maier* at the *University of Pennsylvania* during the late 1960s, he demonstrated that organisms exposed to inescapable negative stimuli become passively resigned to adversity. He subsequently transformed the discipline by founding the *positive psychology* movement during his 1998 presidency of the *American Psychological Association*, redirecting scientific inquiry from mental pathology toward human flourishing, resilience, and optimism.

## you gotta know

- Discovered the phenomenon of *learned helplessness* in 1967 at the *University of Pennsylvania*, demonstrating that dogs subjected to inescapable electric shocks in a harness later failed to jump a low hurdle to escape shocks in a two-compartment shuttle box.
- Extended the findings to human psychopathology, demonstrating that perceived lack of control produces profound motivational, cognitive, and emotional deficits, providing a foundational behavioral model for understanding clinical depression.
- Challenged prevailing models of [[behaviorism]], demonstrating that learning involves forming cognitive expectations about the contingency between actions and outcomes rather than passive stimulus-response conditioning alone.
- Founded the *positive psychology* movement during his 1998 presidency of the *American Psychological Association*, advocating that psychological science should study human virtues, strengths, and optimal functioning rather than solely curing mental illness.
- Formulated the *PERMA model* of human flourishing, which identifies five measurable elements essential to psychological well-being: *Positive Emotion*, *Engagement*, *Relationships*, *Meaning*, and *Accomplishment*.
- Reformulated his helplessness theory alongside *Lyn Abramson* and *John Teasdale* into the *attributional style* model, identifying that individuals with a pessimistic explanatory style attribute negative events to internal, stable, and global causes.
- Authored influential academic works and bestsellers that popularized cognitive resilience, including *Helplessness: On Depression, Development, and Death* (1975), *Learned Optimism* (1991), *Authentic Happiness* (2002), and *Flourish* (2011).
- Designed the *Comprehensive Soldier Fitness* resilience training program for the [[united-states]] Army, adapting positive psychology assessments and interventions to prevent post-traumatic stress disorder and promote psychological growth in soldiers.

## connections

- [[behaviorism]] — his animal shock experiments directly challenged orthodox conditioning by proving that animals construct cognitive expectancies about helplessness that override automatic avoidance learning.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — the dominant figure of operant conditioning whose purely external reinforcement theories could not readily explain why animals ceased attempting to escape when escape was easily possible.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the emergent paradigm that adopted Seligman's work as empirical proof that internal beliefs about agency, control, and causality critically determine overt behavior.
- [[abraham-maslow]] — early humanist thinker whose focus on self-actualization and human potential laid the theoretical groundwork that Seligman later formalized through empirical research.
- [[humanistic-psychology]] — the therapeutic movement Seligman sought to revitalize and ground in quantitative experimental methodology through the formal establishment of positive psychology.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — contemporary researcher whose obedience experiments at Yale similarly illuminated how situational determinants and feelings of powerlessness constrain human agency.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — field influenced by Seligman's findings on how childhood experiences of mastery or helplessness crystallize into enduring explanatory styles and affect lifelong academic and personal achievement.
