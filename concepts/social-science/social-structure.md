## summary

**Social structure** refers to the enduring patterns of roles, relationships, and institutions that organize a society and give it coherence and stability. Rather than viewing societies as collections of individuals making independent choices, *A.R. Radcliffe-Brown* and subsequent anthropologists emphasized that societies are systems held together by interlocking statuses, kinship rules, rituals, and power hierarchies. Understanding social structure means grasping why people behave as they do not through psychology alone, but through the slots they occupy in a larger web of social organization.

## you gotta know

- Social structure encompasses kinship systems (who can marry whom, inheritance rules), economic roles (producer, merchant, elder), political hierarchies (chief, council, commoner), and ritual specialists (priest, healer).
- *A.R. Radcliffe-Brown* pioneered the analysis of social structure as a logical whole, arguing that institutions and practices maintain social integration and must be studied in relation to one another, not in isolation.
- Rites of passage both reflect and reinforce social structure by marking transitions between statuses—a boy becomes a warrior, a woman becomes a mother, a living person becomes an ancestor.
- Roles within social structure are not freely chosen; they carry prescribed behaviors, rights, and obligations tied to one's position. A "chief" is constrained to act chiefly, a "guest" to act as a guest.
- Status can be ascribed (given at birth, like caste, gender, or family position) or achieved (earned through accomplishment, like becoming a skilled warrior or wealthy merchant).
- Social structures vary dramatically across societies—some are egalitarian with minimal hierarchy, others are rigidly stratified; some emphasize kinship, others property or age.
- Modern societies also have social structure (occupational hierarchies, class, racial stratification) though it is often less visible because it is less sacralized by ritual and is contested.
- Understanding social structure is foundational to anthropology: what looks like individual choice is often constrained by invisible webs of expectation, obligation, and role.

## connections

- [[a-r-radcliffe-brown]] — the anthropologist who made social structure central to anthropological theory.
- [[rite-of-passage]] — ceremonial markers that reinforce and reproduce social structure by codifying status transitions.
- [[kinship-systems]] — the web of marriage, descent, and obligation rules that organize social position in most societies.
- [[ritual]] — the formal practices through which social structure is maintained and enlivened.
- [[anthropology]] — the discipline dedicated to understanding societies as integrated wholes, not lists of traits.
