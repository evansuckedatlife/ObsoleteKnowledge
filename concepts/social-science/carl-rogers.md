## summary

**Carl Rogers** (1902–1987) was an American psychologist who revolutionized psychotherapy through his development of person-centered therapy, grounded in radical empathy and unconditional positive regard. Rogers moved therapy away from the analyst's expert interpretation toward genuine encounter between therapist and client. His humanistic psychology emphasized human potential for growth, authenticity, and self-direction. Rogers was both a rigorous scientist and a humanistic visionary, conducting empirical studies of his own therapeutic method while arguing for freedom and trust as foundations of psychological health.

## you gotta know

- Developed *person-centered* (or *client-centered*) therapy, which places the client's own capacity for growth at the center rather than the therapist's diagnosis or interpretation—a revolutionary shift in therapeutic philosophy.
- Core conditions of effective therapy: *unconditional positive regard* (non-judgmental acceptance), *empathy* (genuine understanding of the client's experience), and *congruence* (therapist authenticity and transparency).
- Rejected psychoanalytic frameworks emphasizing pathology, expert authority, and diagnostic classification; instead trusted clients' own innate tendency toward growth (*actualization*) and self-direction.
- Contemporary and intellectual peer of Abraham Maslow; both shaped humanistic psychology as a "third force" alternative to behaviorism (mechanical conditioning) and psychoanalysis (unconscious determination).
- Expanded therapy beyond clinical settings into education, conflict resolution, group facilitation, and organizational development; his ideas influenced encounter groups and human potential movements of the 1960s–70s.
- Late career advocacy for world peace and cross-cultural dialogue; believed genuine psychological understanding and empathy could reduce violence and enable authentic connection across difference.
- Rogers's influence persists: his emphasis on empathic listening, client autonomy, and collaborative relationship pervades modern counseling, coaching, and restorative justice practices worldwide.
- Criticisms of Rogers: his optimism about human nature seems naive to those emphasizing human capacity for cruelty; his approach can under-recognize structural trauma and limits of individual healing without social change.
