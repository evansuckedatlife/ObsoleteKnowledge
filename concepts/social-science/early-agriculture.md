## summary

**Early agriculture** refers to the initial period when humans first cultivated crops and domesticated animals, roughly 10,000 to 5,000 years ago. This transition from hunting-gathering to farming constituted one of history's most transformative developments, enabling permanent settlements, population growth, and the emergence of complex societies. Sites like *Çatalhöyük* and *Jericho* provide archaeological evidence of early farming communities. Paradoxically, some scholars argue that farming reduced individual nutrition and leisure time compared to foraging, yet its comparative efficiency supported larger populations.

## you gotta know

- Emerged independently in multiple regions: the Fertile Crescent, China, Mesoamerica, and the Andes.
- Featured *primary domesticates*: wheat and barley in the Fertile Crescent; millet and rice in Asia; maize and beans in Mesoamerica.
- Required that humans develop *new technologies*: grinding stones for grain, storage pits, irrigation channels.
- Enabled *permanent settlements* — farmers could not follow herds and thus built villages and towns.
- Led to *population growth* due to more reliable food supply, though paradoxically with poorer nutrition and health.
- Facilitated *social stratification* — surplus production allowed some individuals to avoid farming and specialize (priests, warriors, artisans).
- Created *property concepts* — land became owned and heritable in ways it was not for nomadic peoples.
- Proceeded slowly and unevenly; many communities remained foragers or practiced *mixed subsistence* for centuries.
- *Domestication* itself was gradual: early farmers managed wild plants and animals for centuries before achieving full dependence on cultivars.
- Trade networks emerged around surplus goods (obsidian, pottery, shells), linking early agricultural settlements across distances.
- Demographic pressure increased with agriculture: denser populations created new challenges (disease, sanitation, resource management) that shaped emerging social structures.
