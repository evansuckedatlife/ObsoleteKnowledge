## summary

The **Canadian Radio Broadcasting Commission** was Canada's first public radio broadcasting agency, established in 1932 to counter American commercial airwave dominance and cultivate a distinct national cultural identity. Created by Conservative Prime Minister *R. B. Bennett* following the recommendations of the 1929 *Aird Commission*, it operated as both a national network broadcaster and an industry regulator. Hampered by political interference and inadequate funding during the Great Depression, it operated for only four years before being replaced in 1936 by the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC).

## you gotta know

- Created under the *Canadian Radio Broadcasting Act* of 1932 by the government of Prime Minister *R. B. Bennett*.
- Established in response to the 1929 *Royal Commission on Radio Broadcasting*, chaired by *Sir John Aird*, which famously warned that Canada risked becoming culturally subordinate to the [[united-states]].
- Strongly championed by the *Canadian Radio League*, led by civic organizers *Graham Spry* and *Alan Plaunt*, who rallied public opinion with the slogan "the State or the United States."
- Led by its first chief commissioner, *Hector Charlesworth*, a prominent Toronto literary and drama critic.
- Suffered from a structural conflict of interest by simultaneously operating as a national network programmer and as the regulatory licensing body for private competitor stations.
- Severely underfunded throughout the Great Depression, forcing reliance on private commercial affiliates to broadcast its English and French programming across time zones.
- Dissolved in 1936 by the returning Liberal government of *William Lyon Mackenzie King*, which reorganized public broadcasting into the autonomous Crown corporation known as the *Canadian Broadcasting Corporation* (CBC).
