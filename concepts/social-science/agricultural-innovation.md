## summary

**Agricultural innovation** in pre-Columbian societies encompasses the transformation of wild plants and landscape engineering to sustain dense human populations across ecological zones. Centered notably in Mesoamerica and the Andes, indigenous farmers developed maize domestication, hydraulic management, and wetland cultivation that supported complex urban civilizations. Understanding these technological milestones clarifies how food surpluses drove specialized labor, state formation, and social stratification across the ancient Americas.

## you gotta know

- Centered on the selective breeding of *teosinte* into domesticated maize (*Zea mays*), an achievement of genetic modification over millennia in the Balsas River basin of modern *Mexico*.
- Pioneered the *milpa* polyculture system, commonly called the "Three Sisters" (*maize*, *beans*, and *squash*), which provided complementary dietary proteins and naturally replenished soil nitrogen.
- Developed *chinampas* ("floating gardens") in the Valley of Mexico, an intensive raised-field wetland agricultural system using lake muck, canals, and willow stakes to yield multiple harvests per year.
- Constructed extensive hillside terracing (*andenes* in the Andes and stepped contour terraces in the Maya lowlands) to mitigate soil erosion, trap rainfall, and stabilize fragile soils on mountain slopes.
- Implemented complex hydraulic engineering works, including the monumental dyke of *Nezahualcoyotl* to separate fresh and saline waters in Lake Texcoco and intricate subterranean canal networks across arid regions.
- Facilitated high population densities and the creation of durable food reserves through nixtamalization, an alkaline chemical processing of maize that unlocked essential niacin and amino acids.
- Enabled long-distance trade, occupational specialization, and the rise of administrative hierarchies that managed collective land distribution and resource allocation.

- [[culture]] — the shared knowledge, ritual practices, and seasonal cycles deeply intertwined with crop cycles and agronomic techniques.
