## summary

**Social norms** are the unwritten rules and shared expectations that govern appropriate behavior in a group or society. They prescribe how people should act, think, and feel in various situations, enforced through social approval and disapproval rather than formal law. Social norms are learned through socialization and are central to how groups maintain cohesion and coordinate action.

## you gotta know

- Norms are descriptive (what people actually do) or injunctive (what people think should be done), and these can diverge significantly.
- Norms vary by context: table manners in a formal dinner differ from those at a casual picnic; the same behavior can be appropriate in one setting and insulting in another.
- Violation of norms triggers sanctions—ranging from mild (raised eyebrows, subtle mockery) to severe (ostracism, formal punishment)—depending on norm importance.
- [[culture]] transmits norms across generations; children learn norms through observation, explicit instruction, and reinforcement.
- Norms provide efficiency: without shared norms, every social interaction would require explicit negotiation, making coordination impossible.
- Power differentials shape norms: historically, norms have often reinforced hierarchies and excluded groups (e.g., gender norms limiting women's participation).
- Norms are not static; they shift as groups change composition or face new circumstances, though change is often resisted because norms feel natural and inevitable.
- Deviance from norms can be interpreted as either admirable (moral courage, innovation) or shameful (moral failing) depending on the group's values and the norm's importance.
- [[ritual]] represents the most formalized and valued norms, often marking rites of passage or affirming group identity through synchronized behavior.
- Norm violation triggers social sanctions that serve regulatory functions; mild sanctions preserve group cohesion, while severe sanctions protect core values when they are threatened.

## connections

- [[culture]] — norms are a core component of culture, encoding collective values and expectations.
- [[identity]] — norms shape how identity is performed and expressed; identity partly consists of norm adherence or strategic deviation.
- [[ritual]] — formalized, highly valued norms that mark important social transitions or affirm group membership.
- [[values]] — the underlying principles that norms embody and enforce.
- [[conformity]] — the psychological process by which individuals adopt norms through group pressure.
- [[deviance]] — behavior that violates norms and prompts social reaction.
- [[anthropology]] — the discipline that systematically studies how norms function across cultures.
