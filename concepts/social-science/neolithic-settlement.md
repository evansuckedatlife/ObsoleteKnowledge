## summary

**Neolithic settlement** refers to the transition from temporary, mobile camps to permanent villages and towns during the Neolithic period roughly 10,000–5,000 years ago. This shift was intimately linked to agriculture: once humans committed to cultivating specific plots of land, they needed year-round residence. Neolithic settlements reveal through archaeology the social innovations—property, cooperation, hierarchy—that permanent habitation required, making them windows into civilization's emergence.

## you gotta know

- *Permanent settlement* required abandoning the seasonal mobility of hunter-gatherers; farmers could not follow herds and had to remain near cultivated fields through growing and harvest seasons.
- *Residential architecture* changed fundamentally: structures became more substantial, featuring stored goods, storage facilities, and defended boundaries; remains reveal evolution from simple huts to organized housing compounds.
- *Population concentration* at settlements supported specialization: not all residents farmed; some made tools, processed food, or performed administrative or religious functions—the beginning of occupational diversity.
- *Property concepts* emerged: farmers invested labor in cleared fields and irrigation systems, creating incentive to defend and inherit land; settlement archaeology reveals storage structures protecting accumulated grain and tools.
- *Defensive architecture*—walls and fortifications appearing at sites like Jericho—suggests conflict emerged as populations concentrated around resources and stored wealth; defense became necessary.
- *Social organization* became more complex: permanent settlements required mechanisms to coordinate group labor, manage shared resources, settle disputes, and organize defense—the foundations of political authority.
- *Gender roles* may have shifted with settlement: in mobile forager societies, division of labor was flexible; sedentary farming societies show archaeological evidence of more specialized and rigid gender-based task allocation.
- Settlement size varied dramatically: some Neolithic villages housed only dozens of people, while others like Çatalhöyük accommodated over 1,000 residents in densely packed adobe houses, creating diverse patterns of community organization.
