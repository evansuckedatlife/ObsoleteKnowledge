## summary

**Hierarchy** is the ordering of individuals or groups in a ranked structure where some occupy higher positions with greater authority, prestige, or control than others. Found in human societies from small bands to complex organizations, hierarchy enables coordination and decision-making by creating clear channels of communication and command. It is both a feature of social organization and a psychological reality—humans readily form hierarchical relationships.

## you gotta know

- Hierarchies exist across scales: within families (parents over children), organizations (managers over employees), military structures, academic institutions, and entire societies divided by class or caste.
- *Social hierarchy* in early human societies likely arose with permanent settlements and agriculture, as surplus resources enabled some individuals to accumulate power and command others; hunter-gatherer bands are comparatively egalitarian.
- Hierarchies are not purely coercive; they often rest on *legitimacy*—the belief that authority holders have the right to command—which makes compliance voluntary rather than forced.
- *Status* and *rank* are the perceptual dimensions of hierarchy; people recognize their position relative to others and adjust behavior accordingly.
- Hierarchy can be *fluid* (achieved through merit or temporary advantage) or *rigid* (inherited through birth, as in caste systems); most societies blend both mechanisms.
- Experimental psychology, including *Stanley Milgram*'s obedience studies, revealed how strongly people defer to authority figures perceived as legitimate occupants of hierarchical positions.
- Hierarchies facilitate *coordination* in large groups: with clear command structures, decisions can cascade downward and information can flow upward, solving problems that egalitarian groups find difficult without formal organization.
- *Dominance hierarchies*, observed in primates and other species, suggest humans may have evolutionary predispositions to establish ranked relationships, though culture dramatically shapes how hierarchies form and persist.
