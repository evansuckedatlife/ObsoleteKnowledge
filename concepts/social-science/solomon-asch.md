## summary

*Solomon Asch* was a Polish-American social psychologist famous for the Asch Conformity Experiments, a series of studies conducted in the 1950s that revealed the extraordinary power of group pressure to shape individual judgment and behavior. Through elegant but simple line-matching tasks, Asch demonstrated that ordinary people will contradict their own perception and give obviously wrong answers if a group consensus demands it. His work became foundational to understanding conformity, groupthink, and the limits of individual agency in social contexts.

## you gotta know

- Conducted the Asch Conformity Experiments in 1951-1955, showing that about 75% of participants conformed to obviously incorrect group answers at least once.
- The experimental setup was deceptively simple: participants judged the length of lines alongside confederates (actors pretending to be fellow participants) who gave incorrect answers.
- Participants knew their answers were wrong but conformed to avoid social rejection—demonstrating that social pressure overrides perceptual reality for most people.
- Asch found that conformity increased with group size (up to about 3-4 people) and decreased when even one other person broke the group consensus.
- The experiments revealed conformity as a normal, widespread phenomenon rather than a personality defect—many intelligent, confident people conformed to avoid social disapproval.
- His work became a touchstone for later research on obedience (*Stanley Milgram*) and is frequently cited in discussions of groupthink, jury dynamics, and collective delusion.
