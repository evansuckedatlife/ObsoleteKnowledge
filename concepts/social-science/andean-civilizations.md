## summary

**Andean civilizations** were a complex succession of indigenous pre-Columbian societies that flourished along the Pacific coast, arid valleys, and highlands of western South America for over four millennia. Overcoming harsh geography through innovative terrace farming, monumental irrigation canals, and ecological niche exploitation, these cultures developed sophisticated states without alphabetic writing, wheeled vehicles, or iron metallurgy. The civilizational trajectory culminated in the vast *Inca Empire*, which united millions across the Andes before falling to Spanish conquest.

## you gotta know

- *Caral-Supe* (also called *Norte Chico*), flourishing around 3000 BCE in coastal Peru, represents the oldest known urban civilization in the Americas, constructing monumental platform mounds and sunken circular plazas.
- The *Chavín* culture operated an influential religious and artistic horizon from its highland ceremonial center *Chavín de Huántar*, famous for its subterranean galleries, the *Lanzón* stela, and feline iconography.
- The *Moche* civilization populated the northern coastal valleys, renowned for masterfully naturalistic portrait head ceramics, large mudbrick monuments like the *Huaca del Sol*, and elite tombs such as the *Lord of Sipán*.
- The *Nasca* culture of the arid southern coast produced intricate polychrome pottery and carved colossal geoglyphs, known as the *Nazca Lines*, into the desert pampa for astronomical and water rituals.
- The middle horizon witnessed the rise of dual highland powers: the expansive *Wari Empire*, known for terraced agriculture and administrative centers, and *Tiwanaku*, centered on Lake Titicaca with monumental stone architecture like the *Gate of the Sun*.
- The *Chimú* kingdom, centered at the massive adobe metropolis of *Chan Chan*, dominated northern Peru until conquered by the expansionist Inca state.
- Social organization depended on the *ayllu* kinship collective, reciprocal labor mechanisms like *ayni* and *mita*, and data recording via knotted textile strings called *quipus*.
- Anthropologist *John Murra* formulated the concept of the *vertical archipelago*, showing how Andean communities established satellite colonies across distinct ecological altitudinal zones to access maize, tubers, coca, and marine resources.

## connections

- [[native-americans]] — broader family of indigenous peoples of the Western Hemisphere who independently developed state-level urban civilizations.
- [[ancient-trade]] — contrastive non-market economic paradigm where redistribution, kinship reciprocity, and ecological tier management replaced currency-based commodity markets.
- [[culture]] — rich fabric of textile traditions, ceramic styles, and community obligations that maintained social coherence across disparate highland valleys.
- [[ritual]] — elaborate ceremonial systems, including ancestor veneration, pilgrimage networks, and sacrifice, that legitimized elite political hegemony.
- [[spain]] — European imperial power whose conquistadors, led by *Francisco Pizarro*, toppled the Inca state in the 1530s and exploited indigenous administrative structures.
- [[colonialism]] — historical process of Spanish subjugation, demographic collapse, religious conversion, and economic extraction that reshaped the Andean landscape.
- [[authority]] — institutionalized state power executed through administrative labor tax systems, state warehouses, and royal roadway networks.
