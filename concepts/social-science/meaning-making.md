## summary

**Meaning-making** is the fundamental human process of constructing significance from experience, language, symbols, and cultural contexts. It is both individual—how we interpret our own lives—and social—shaped by the interpretive frameworks our cultures provide. Meaning-making integrates sensory input, emotion, memory, and cultural knowledge into coherent narratives that explain why things matter and how the world works.

## you gotta know

- Meaning is not inherent in objects or events; it emerges from the interaction between a person and their cultural environment—the same symbol or experience can carry radically different meanings across contexts.
- Language is a primary vehicle for meaning-making; words, metaphors, and narrative structures shape how we perceive reality and what becomes thinkable or unspeakable.
- Cultural systems (myth, ritual, religion, art, literature) provide ready-made frameworks for meaning-making; people inherit interpretive tools and then modify them.
- Peak experiences (moments of transcendence, insight, or beauty) are often characterized by sudden shifts in meaning-making—events acquire new significance in retrospect.
- Symbols condense multiple meanings into compact forms (a flag, a color, a gesture); their power lies in their ability to trigger collective meaning-making across a group.
- Literary and hermeneutic traditions have long emphasized that texts, historical events, and human actions require interpretation—they do not yield meaning passively but demand active meaning-making by readers or historians.
- Neuroscience and cognitive psychology suggest that meaning-making is not a late-stage luxury but fundamental to how brains organize experience—the brain continuously generates predictions and narratives.
- Crises of meaning (loss of faith, displacement, trauma) reveal meaning-making's centrality; when usual frameworks break, the search for new meaning becomes urgent and visible.

## connections

- [[interpretation]] — the cognitive act of assigning meaning to signs, texts, or events.
- [[semiotics]] — the formal study of how meaning is produced through signs and sign systems.
- [[symbolism]] — the use of symbols to condense and transmit meaning culturally.
- [[sign-systems]] — structured codes (language, traffic signs, ritual) that organize meaning collectively.
- [[peak-experiences]] — moments when meaning-making becomes intense and transformative.
- [[culture]] — the repository of shared meaning-making frameworks.
- [[narrative]] — the primary structure through which humans organize meaning over time.
