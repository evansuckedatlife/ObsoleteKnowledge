## summary

**Mythology** encompasses the body of stories, narratives, and symbolic systems through which cultures explain natural phenomena, social structures, and moral values. Myths are narratives—often featuring gods, heroes, and supernatural events—that serve functions far beyond entertainment: they encode cosmology, ethics, and identity. Understanding mythology is essential to anthropology, psychology, and literary analysis, as myths reveal how societies construct meaning.

## you gotta know

- Myths are distinguished from folktales and legends by their sacred or explanatory function in a culture's worldview; they address existential questions rather than mere entertainment.
- Common mythological figures appear across cultures (the trickster, the flood narrative, the hero's quest), suggesting either independent invention or shared human psychological patterns—a phenomenon comparative mythologists call *cultural universals*.
- Carl Jung proposed that myths are expressions of universal archetypes buried in the collective unconscious, accessible across all humans; archetypes like the Hero, the Wise Old Man, and the Shadow repeat globally.
- Comparative mythology—the study of myths across cultures—reveals patterns in how societies grapple with creation, death, morality, and power; scholars like James Frazer systematized thousands of myths to reveal underlying themes.
- Myths serve multiple social functions: they justify rituals, encode legal systems, establish kinship rules, and transmit values to new generations—functioning as a culture's operating manual for meaning-making.
- Modernism and Symbolism movements drew heavily on mythological imagery to express psychological and aesthetic complexity; T.S. Eliot, Yeats, and Joyce embedded mythic frameworks into modernist literature to deepen meaning.
- Demythologization—the process of stripping myths of supernatural elements to reveal their historical or psychological truths—emerged in 20th-century biblical scholarship and continues to influence how societies reinterpret their foundational stories.

## connections

- [[ritual]] — myths and rituals are inseparable; myths provide the narrative rationale for ritual practice.
- [[carl-jung]] — pioneer of psychological interpretation of myths as expressions of the unconscious.
- [[james-frazer]] — collected and compared mythologies across cultures in *The Golden Bough*.
- [[religion]] — myths are the narrative foundation of religious belief and practice.
- [[symbolism]] — myths employ symbol systems to convey meaning beyond literal truth.
- [[archetype]] — recurring symbolic figures and narrative patterns Jung identified in myths.
