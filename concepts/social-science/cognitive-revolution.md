## summary

The **cognitive revolution** denotes the profound prehistoric emergence of behavioral modernity in *[[human-evolution]]*, during which archaic *Homo sapiens* developed fully articulate symbolic language, abstract reasoning, and cumulative *[[culture]]*. Occurring roughly seventy thousand to fifty thousand years ago during the Middle to Upper Paleolithic transition, this shift equipped human populations with the cognitive capacity to create art, execute coordinated long-range planning, and forge shared fictional realities. The term also designates the mid-twentieth-century paradigm shift across the social sciences that toppled *[[behaviorism]]* in favor of information processing models, establishing modern *[[cognitive-science]]*.

## you gotta know

- Popularized in evolutionary anthropology as the pivotal threshold where anatomically modern humans developed behavioral modernity, enabling rapid territorial expansion out of Africa.
- Linked archaeologically to the sudden flourishing of representational cave art (such as Chauvet and Lascaux), personal body ornamentation, bone instruments, and figurative sculptures like the Lion-man of Hohlenstein-Stadel.
- Driven by the emergence of syntactically complex language, which permitted the transmission of counterfactual beliefs, mythologies, and abstract kinship rules beyond immediate physical reality.
- Enabled *Homo sapiens* to organize collaborative groups far surpassing natural primate grooming thresholds (frequently described through *Robin Dunbar*'s social brain hypothesis).
- Coincided with the rapid geographic displacement and eventual extinction of other hominin species across Eurasia, including the Neanderthals and Denisovans.
- Refers in twentieth-century academic history to the intellectual transformation led by figures such as *[[noam-chomsky]]*, *George Miller*, and *Jerome Bruner*, who replaced operant conditioning with computational mental representations.
- Accelerated cultural evolution over genetic adaptation, allowing human societies to alter customs, tools, and social arrangements without physical anatomical speciation.

## connections

- [[human-evolution]] — the anatomical and biological lineage within which modern neural plasticity and symbolic intelligence emerged.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the interdisciplinary study of mind and intelligence that arose from the twentieth-century version of the revolution.
- [[behaviorism]] — the stimulus-response orthodoxy that was overthrown when cognitive models restored the validity of studying internal mental states.
- [[noam-chomsky]] — linguist whose 1959 scathing review of *[[b-f-skinner]]*'s *Verbal Behavior* served as the pivotal manifesto for cognitive approaches to language.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — the foremost champion of radical behaviorism whose mechanistic accounts of language acquisition were directly dismantled.
- [[culture]] — the repository of shared symbols, traditions, and collective narratives made possible once symbolic reasoning arose.
- [[ritual]] — early institutionalized practices, such as deliberate grave burials and ceremonial ochre use, that physically signaled abstract metaphysical beliefs.
