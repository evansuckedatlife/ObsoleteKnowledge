## summary

**Spanish**, also termed Castilian, is a global Romance language originating in the northern regions of the Iberian Peninsula after the disintegration of the [[roman-empire]]. Spread across the Americas and the Caribbean through early modern transatlantic [[colonialism]], it has grown to become the second-most spoken native language in the world. In the disciplines of [[linguistics]] and social history, Spanish is renowned for its five-vowel phonemic system, its substantial Arabic lexical superstrate, and its widespread dialectal diversification across the Americas.

## you gotta know

- Evolved from the vernacular of the Kingdom of Castile and was formally codified by *Antonio de Nebrija* in 1492 in his *Gramática de la lengua castellana*, the first printed grammar of a modern European vernacular.
- Contains thousands of Arabic lexical borrowings assimilated during the eight centuries of *Al-Andalus*, frequently recognizable by the fossilized Arabic article prefix *al-*, including *alcalde*, *algodón*, and *álgebra*.
- Forms nominal plurals by suffixing *-s* and historically voiced intervocalic Latin voiceless stops, aligning it with Western Romance tongues in contrast to languages like [[italian]].
- Standard usage is overseen by the *Real Academia Española* (RAE), established in Madrid in 1713, which works in conjunction with the *Asociación de Academias de la Lengua Española* across the Americas to preserve transnational orthographic unity.
- Displays prominent dialectal phonological variations including *yeísmo* (the merger of the palatal lateral with the palatal fricative) and the contrast between peninsular *distinción* and American *seseo*.
- Extensively integrated vocabulary from [[native-americans]] during imperial contact, adopting Nahuatl terms such as *chocolate* and *tomate*, as well as Taíno words like *huracán* and *hamaca*.
- Features widespread grammatical dialect markers such as *voseo* (the substitution of *vos* for *tú* as the second-person singular pronoun), especially prevalent in the Río de la Plata region and across parts of Central America.

## connections

- [[italian]] — closely related Romance language sharing common Latin syntactic structures, frequently compared regarding historical sound shifts and vowel inventories.
- [[spain]] — European nation-state where the Castilian tongue originated, consolidated, and was promulgated as the imperial standard.
- [[colonialism]] — historical process of territorial conquest and religious missionization that established Spanish as the dominant language of Central and South America.
- [[native-americans]] — indigenous civilizations whose distinct languages contributed significant lexical substrata and loanwords to Latin American Spanish.
- [[linguistics]] — field examining Spanish morphosyntax, dialectology, phonological mergers, and its historical descent from Latin.
- [[sociolinguistics]] — discipline exploring language contact, code-switching, and bilingual communities in Latin America and the [[united-states]].
