## summary

**Cognitive behavioral therapy** (*CBT*) is a structured, empirical form of psychotherapy that focuses on identifying, challenging, and altering dysfunctional automatic thoughts and maladaptive behavioral patterns. Developed in the 1960s by psychiatrist *Aaron Beck* alongside the rational emotive behavior therapy of *Albert Ellis*, it emerged from the cognitive revolution as a time-limited alternative to classical psychoanalysis. Today, it represents the gold standard of evidence-based psychological treatment for major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety, panic disorders, and obsessive-compulsive conditions.

## you gotta know

- Developed in the early 1960s by American psychiatrist *Aaron Beck*, who observed that depressed patients consistently suffered from streams of automatic, distorted internal monologues that perpetuated their suffering.
- Paralleled by *Albert Ellis*, who pioneered rational emotive behavior therapy (*REBT*) around the *ABC model*, positing that an Activating event leads to subjective Beliefs, which in turn produce emotional and behavioral Consequences.
- Targets the *cognitive triad* in clinical depression, wherein afflicted individuals maintain pervasive, rigid negative appraisals regarding the self, the ongoing world, and the future.
- Merges cognitive restructuring techniques with empirical behavior modification strategies derived from the operant conditioning theories of *B. F. Skinner*.
- Utilizes collaborative empiricism and *Socratic questioning*, guiding patients to treat their catastrophic assumptions and cognitive distortions as testable hypotheses rather than objective realities.
- Employs behavioral activation and graded exposure therapy, systematically presenting feared stimuli to reduce avoidance and facilitate emotional habituation.
- Spawned "third wave" therapeutic traditions that integrate mindfulness and metacognitive awareness, such as *Marsha Linehan*'s dialectical behavior therapy (*DBT*) and acceptance and commitment therapy (*ACT*).

## connections

- [[cognition]] — the central domain of mental representations, automatic thoughts, and schemas targeted for therapeutic restructuring.
- [[behaviorism]] — supplies the behavioral conditioning principles, reinforcement schedules, and exposure protocols utilized throughout treatment.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — the long-term clinical model developed by *Sigmund Freud* that *Beck* rejected due to its lack of empirical verifiability.
- [[cognitive-science]] — interdisciplinary study of information processing that provided the theoretical backing for *CBT*'s cognitive revolution.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — behaviorist researcher whose discoveries in operant conditioning laid the groundwork for behavioral activation.
- [[sigmund-freud]] — founder of psychoanalysis whose concepts of unconscious conflict and repression *CBT* replaced with conscious schemas.
- [[humanistic-psychology]] — therapeutic movement championed by *Carl Rogers* that emphasized unconditional positive regard in contrast to *CBT*'s prescriptive protocols.
