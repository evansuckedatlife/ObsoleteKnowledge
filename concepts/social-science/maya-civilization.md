## summary

The **Maya civilization** (or the **Maya**) was a major Mesoamerican civilization developed by the Maya peoples, flourishing across modern-day southeastern *Mexico*, *Guatemala*, *Belize*, and western *Honduras* and *El Salvador*. Renowned for developing the only fully literate written script in the pre-Columbian Americas—the logosyllabic *Maya script*—as well as sophisticated mathematics utilizing zero, the civilization produced monumental stepped pyramids, advanced calendar systems, and powerful autonomous city-states like *Tikal*, *Palenque*, and *Calakmul*. Its Classic-period apex, architectural achievements, and resilient survival through environmental disruption and European colonization make it a foundational subject in the study of human [[culture]] and ancient urbanism.

## you gotta know

- Developed sophisticated interlocking calendar systems, notably the 260-day sacred *Tzolkʼin*, the 365-day solar *Haabʼ*, and the vigesimal *Long Count* calendar used to record linear chronological time from a 3114 BCE epoch.
- Invented an advanced vigesimal (base-20) numeral system featuring place-value notation and a stylized shell glyph representing zero, enabling precise astronomical calculations.
- Constructed monumental stone architecture, including towering stepped temple-pyramids, astronomical observatories such as *El Caracol*, expansive acropolises, and ceremonial courts for the Mesoamerican ballgame.
- Organized as decentralized, competitive city-states governed by divine kings (*kʼuhul ajaw*), marked by shifting alliances, trade networks, and intense dynastic rivalries like that between *Tikal* and *Calakmul*.
- Maintained the *Maya hieroglyphic script*, a logosyllabic writing system preserved on carved stone stelae, lintels, painted ceramics, and screenfold bark-paper codices like the astronomical *Dresden Codex*.
- Adapted farming to rainforest terrain through terracing, raised wetland fields, and sophisticated water management systems, including plastered reservoirs, underground cisterns (*chultunes*), and natural sinkholes (*cenotes*).
- Underwent the Classic Maya collapse during the eighth and ninth centuries, when southern lowland centers were abandoned amid prolonged megadroughts, ecological degradation, and escalating warfare, shifting power toward northern centers like *Chichén Itzá*.
- Preserved oral literature and cosmology in colonial-era alphabetic texts such as the K'iche' *Popol Vuh*, which recounts the creation of humans from maize and the myth of the *Hero Twins*.

## connections

- [[culture]] — provides the anthropological framework for analyzing the shared religious ideology, art, and customs uniting politically independent Maya city-states.
- [[ritual]] — structured Maya religious life and political statecraft through royal bloodletting, ancestor veneration, and sacred ballgame ceremonies.
- [[spain]] — the imperial power whose conquistadors, led by *Pedro de Alvarado*, campaigned across Maya lands until the final lowland stronghold of *Nojpetén* fell in 1697.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the historical era of transatlantic contact that brought European colonization, devastating epidemic diseases, and Christian evangelization to Mesoamerica.
- [[native-americans]] — the continental demographic category to which the Maya belong, whose millions of modern descendants still preserve indigenous Maya languages and customs.
- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline transformed by the twentieth-century decipherment of Maya hieroglyphs, initiated by *Yuri Knorozov*, demonstrating the script's phonetic and syllabic nature.
- [[authority]] — embodied by the hereditary *ajaw*, who reinforced hierarchical rule and civic obedience through public ritual displays and claims of divine lineage.
