# Native Americans

## summary

**Native Americans** (or American Indians) are the indigenous peoples of North America, inhabiting the continent for thousands of years before European contact. With hundreds of distinct nations, languages, and cultures, they developed sophisticated societies—from the *Mississippian culture* to the *Haudenosaunee Confederacy*—before European colonization, disease, warfare, and dispossession decimated populations and confined survivors to reservations.

## you gotta know

- Native Americans were not a unified group but hundreds of sovereign nations with distinct languages, governance structures, and cultures; major groupings included eastern woodlands peoples, plains nomads, southwestern pueblos, and Pacific northwest coastal societies.
- European contact (starting 1492) introduced diseases—smallpox, measles, influenza—to which Native populations had no immunity, causing catastrophic mortality (estimates suggest 90% population loss in some regions).
- After American independence, the U.S. government initially treated tribes as sovereign nations and negotiated treaties; however, westward expansion by settlers provoked conflict and forced removals.
- The *Indian Removal Act* (1830) authorized forced relocation of southeastern tribes (Cherokee, Creek, Seminole, Choctaw) westward to *Oklahoma*; the *Trail of Tears* (1838–39) during Cherokee removal killed thousands.
- Throughout the 19th century, the U.S. confined Native Americans to *reservations* (often poor, resource-scarce lands), broke up communal lands through the *Dawes Act* (1887), and suppressed cultural practices (banning ceremonies, forcing children into boarding schools).
- The *American Revolution* initially appeared to offer Native nations a choice between British and American allies; however, Britain's support often proved less substantial, and American independence brought accelerated westward expansion and dispossession.
- The 20th century saw indigenous activism, legal victories (e.g., *Indian Self-Determination Act* of 1975), and cultural revitalization, though Native Americans remain significantly disadvantaged economically and in health outcomes.
