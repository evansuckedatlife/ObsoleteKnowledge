## summary

The **Indus Valley Civilization** (circa 3300–1300 BCE) was one of the world's three great ancient urban societies, alongside Egypt and Mesopotamia. Flourishing along the [[indus-river|Indus River]] and its tributaries in what is now [[pakistan|Pakistan]] and [[india|India]], it featured planned cities, sophisticated drainage systems, and a still-undeciphered script.

## you gotta know

- Emerged around 3300 BCE and thrived during the *Mature Harappan* period (2600–1900 BCE), then declined, possibly due to climate change or river shifting.
- *Planned urban design*: Cities like [[mohenjo-daro]] and Harappa were laid out on a grid with standardized brick sizes, suggesting centralized planning and authority; streets were uniform widths, revealing sophisticated urban planning.
- Developed a sophisticated *drainage and sanitation system* — underground pipes, flush toilets, and public baths that rivaled or exceeded later civilizations' hygiene for centuries; waste water flowed away from settlements through buried drains.
- Created a *pictographic script* (about 400 symbols) that remains undeciphered; appears on seals and pottery but offers no window into their language or beliefs; decipherment has frustrated scholars for over a century.
- *Economic system*: Evidence of extensive trade networks reaching Mesopotamia, the Arabian Peninsula, and Central Asia; weights and measures were standardized, indicating mercantile sophistication; seals found in Mesopotamia testify to long-distance contact.
- Society appears to have been egalitarian or meritocratic — few signs of huge palaces, temples, or elite tombs; wealth was more evenly distributed than in Egypt or Mesopotamia.
- Disappeared around 1300 BCE; reasons remain debated — climate change, flooding, or conquest may have led to collapse and dispersal.
- Unlike Egypt or Mesopotamia, the Indus civilization left no monumental palaces, royal tombs, or temple complexes; some archaeologists infer a more egalitarian society or theocratic system without hereditary rulers.
- The decentralized nature of Indus sites — no evidence of a single capital or dominant centre — suggests distributed political authority, possibly city-states or a confederation.

## connections

- [[mohenjo-daro]] — the largest and most developed city of the Indus civilization; excavated extensively in the early 20th century.
- [[indus-river]] — the river whose flooding and fertility supported this civilization; shaped seasonal cycles.
- [[pakistan]] — modern nation where most Indus Valley sites are located; Mohenjo-daro is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- [[harappa]] — another major city of the civilization; gave the civilization its alternate name "Harappan."
- [[india]] — shares the Indus legacy; some sites lie in modern India, and cultural elements may have influenced later Indian civilization.
- [[bronze-age]] — the Indus civilization was contemporaneous with Bronze Age societies in Mesopotamia and Egypt.
- [[archaeology]] — Indus sites are prime examples of archaeological discovery revealing ancient urban complexity.
