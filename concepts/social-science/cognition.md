## summary

**Cognition** refers to the comprehensive collection of internal mental actions and faculties through which information is acquired, perceived, stored, transformed, retrieved, and applied. Encompassing attention, memory, executive functioning, problem-solving, and language comprehension, it forms the core subject of modern cognitive psychology and neuropsychology. The discipline underwent an intellectual paradigm shift during the cognitive revolution of the 1950s, overturning radical behaviorism by establishing that internal mental architecture can be studied rigorously through computational models and empirical observation.

## you gotta know

- The cognitive revolution of the 1950s systematically displaced strict behaviorism, returning scientific legitimacy to the study of unobservable internal mental states.
- *Noam Chomsky*'s landmark 1959 critique of *B. F. Skinner*'s book *Verbal Behavior* proved that operant conditioning alone cannot explain language acquisition or creative grammar generation.
- Often modeled through the computer metaphor of mind, which treats the brain as biological hardware running cognitive software through symbolic information processing.
- Investigated through developmental stages by *Jean Piaget*, who charted how children construct cognitive schemas through assimilation and accommodation across distinct developmental milestones.
- Explored in the domain of linguistic relativity, also known as the *Sapir-Whorf hypothesis*, which examines how lexical categorization and linguistic structure influence mental perception.
- Analyzed in behavioral economics through the heuristics and cognitive biases documented by *Daniel Kahneman* and *Amos Tversky*, demonstrating systematic departures from rational choice.
- Provides the operational foundation for clinical psychology, particularly in [[cognitive-behavioral-therapy]], where reframing distorted mental processing alleviates psychiatric distress.

## connections

- [[cognitive-science]] — the overarching interdisciplinary field combining psychology, artificial intelligence, neuroscience, and philosophy to study mental systems.
- [[cognitive-behavioral-therapy]] — clinical application that treats psychological disorders by systematically modifying maladaptive cognitive patterns.
- [[linguistics]] — scientific discipline whose exploration of syntactic rules and semantic structures provides direct insight into human mental architecture.
- [[noam-chomsky]] — foundational linguist whose argument for innate cognitive language faculties catalyzed the demise of behaviorism.
- [[jean-piaget]] — developmental theorist who demonstrated that childhood intelligence evolves through distinct qualitative stages of operational thought.
- [[behaviorism]] — historical school that dominated early 20th-century psychology by rejecting internal cognitive explanations as unscientific.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — radical behaviorist whose stimulus-response model of human action was superseded by the cognitive paradigm.
- [[culture]] — collective social environment that provides linguistic and symbolic tools shaping how cognitive schemas categorize experience.
