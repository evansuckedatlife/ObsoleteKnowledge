## summary

**Drive-reduction theory** is a major neo-behaviorist motivational model positing that physiological deprivations disrupt internal homeostasis, generating psychological tension states termed drives that impel organisms to action. Formulated primarily by American psychologist *Clark Hull* in his 1943 work *Principles of Behavior* and later expanded by *Kenneth Spence*, the theory argues that all learning occurs when a behavioral response reduces this unpleasant drive state. It dominated mid-twentieth-century experimental psychology as an ambitious attempt to formalize motivation into rigorous mathematical laws.

## you gotta know

- Formulated by *Clark Hull* in *Principles of Behavior* (1943) and formalized mathematically as reaction potential being a function of habit strength multiplied by drive and incentive motivation.
- Distinguishes primary drives, innate biological necessities essential for survival such as hunger, thirst, pain avoidance, and thermoregulation, from secondary drives, neutral stimuli that acquire motivational power through conditioning like money or social approval.
- Rooted conceptually in the biological principle of homeostasis, asserting that organisms act as self-regulating systems driven to restore physiological equilibrium whenever internal biological balances are disturbed.
- Defines reinforcement strictly as the diminution or termination of drive-induced internal stimulation, establishing the physiological mechanism behind habit formation.
- Critiqued decisively by exploratory and sensation-seeking behaviors that demonstrate animals actively seek stimulation, arousal, and novelty without any antecedent physiological deprivation.
- Overturned by curiosity experiments such as the sensory deprivation studies and primate puzzle-solving investigations of *Harry Harlow*, alongside cognitive mapping findings demonstrated by *Edward Tolman*.
- Contrasted with humanistic models like the hierarchy of needs by *Abraham Maslow*, which argue that behavior is pulled forward by growth and [[self-actualization]] rather than merely driven to alleviate deficit states.
- Diverges from clinical paradigms like [[dream-analysis]] and classical [[psychoanalysis]], which interpret behavioral motives through repressed symbolic complexes rather than overt drive-stimulus mechanisms.

## connections

- [[behaviorism]] — the overarching empirical school of psychology that sought objective, mechanistic explanations for stimulus and response phenomena.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — the radical behaviorist whose focus on operant conditioning and external reinforcement schedules challenged Hullian internal drive constructs.
- [[abraham-maslow]] — the humanistic theorist who formulated the hierarchy of needs in contrast to purely deficit-based drive theories.
- [[self-actualization]] — the ultimate teleological state in humanistic psychology representing personal realization rather than biological tension reduction.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the field whose computational and mentalistic models superseded stimulus-response and mathematical drive-reduction formulas.
- [[dream-analysis]] — the psychoanalytic practice analyzing symbolic unconscious wishes, providing an alternative framework for psychic tension release.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — the psychodynamic school that attributed human actions to unconscious sexual and aggressive drives rather than homeostatic habit reinforcement.
