## summary

**Kinship systems** are the culturally determined frameworks through which societies organize relationships between family members and define rights, obligations, and social identity through descent, marriage, and affinity. They are foundational to understanding how societies structure themselves and transmit property, status, and belonging.

## you gotta know

- Every society has a kinship system; they vary dramatically in who counts as a "relative," what obligations kinship entails, and how inheritance and property flow through families.
- *Types of descent*: Societies trace kinship through *patrilineal* descent (father's line), *matrilineal* descent (mother's line), or *bilateral/cognatic* descent (both parents).
- *Exogamy* (marriage outside one's group) and *endogamy* (marriage within one's group) are marriage rules embedded in kinship systems; they regulate who can marry whom.
- [[a-r-radcliffe-brown]] revolutionized kinship studies by analyzing kinship systems as *functional structures* that organized labour, inheritance, and alliance across generations; he showed kinship was not just genealogy but social roles.
- [[claude-levi-strauss]] demonstrated that kinship systems embody deep structural patterns; marriage rules and descent groups are not arbitrary but follow underlying logic (e.g., elementary structures of kinship); he found universal patterns beneath surface variation.
- *Terminology variations*: Different societies use distinct kinship terminology — some languages distinguish paternal and maternal uncles with separate words; others group them; these terminological differences map onto descent rules and inheritance.
- Modern kinship anthropology examines how kinship systems adapt to migration, colonization, legal systems, and LGBTQ+ relationships, showing kinship as dynamic and contextual; fictive kinship (adoption, chosen families) expands beyond blood and marriage.
- Cross-cultural study reveals no society treats all relatives identically; every society allocates duties, privileges, and status differentially based on relation type, distance, and gender.

## connections

- [[a-r-radcliffe-brown]] — pioneered structural-functional analysis of kinship systems; revolutionized the field.
- [[claude-levi-strauss]] — identified deep structural patterns in kinship and marriage exchange; showed kinship systems as ordered systems.
- [[family-structure]] — the household and family arrangements within which kinship relations are lived and expressed.
- [[marriage-rules]] — the norms governing who marries whom; encoded in kinship systems and enforce group alliance and exchange.
- [[descent-groups]] — lineages, clans, and other groups formed through traced descent; basic units within kinship systems.
- [[structural-anthropology]] — the theoretical approach pioneered by Lévi-Strauss that sees kinship as structured exchange and alliance.
- [[ritual]] — kinship transitions (birth, marriage, death) are marked by rituals that reinforce kinship meanings and solidarity.
