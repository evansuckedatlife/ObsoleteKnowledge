## summary

The **American Psychological Association** (*APA*) is the foremost scientific and professional organization representing psychology in the [[united-states]]. Founded in 1892 at Clark University under the leadership of *G. Stanley Hall*, the organization institutionalized psychology as an empirical discipline distinct from philosophy and medicine. Beyond publishing authoritative journals and setting ethical clinical guidelines, the association is universally recognized for establishing *APA style*, the standard formatting and citation convention used across the social sciences.

## you gotta know

- Founded in July 1892 by a small group of thirty-one founding members, electing pioneering educator and developmental researcher *G. Stanley Hall* as its first president.
- Elected *Mary Whiton Calkins* as its first woman president in 1905, followed by comparative psychologist *Margaret Floy Washburn* in 1921.
- Established *APA style* through a brief seven-page guideline published in 1929 in the *Psychological Bulletin*, which evolved into the universally utilized *Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association*.
- Served as the institutional arena for major theoretical paradigms throughout the twentieth century, witnessing clashes between structuralism, functionalism, [[behaviorism]], and the rise of [[cognitive-science]].
- Maintained a historically contentious relationship with the *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders* (*DSM*), which is actually published by its counterpart, the American Psychiatric Association.
- De-classified homosexuality as a mental illness in 1975, following landmark protests led by gay rights activists and allied psychologists during the early 1970s.
- Experienced internal dissent in 1988 when experimental researchers split to form the *Association for Psychological Science* (*APS*), feeling the original organization prioritized clinical practice over scientific research.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the nation where the association was chartered and where it coordinates national professional psychology standards.
- [[behaviorism]] — the empirical psychological movement spearheaded by members like *John B. Watson* and *B. F. Skinner* who shaped early association leadership.
- [[b-f-skinner]] — prominent behavioral psychologist who served as a distinguished figure and awardee within the organization.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the interdisciplinary revolution that shifted the association's focus toward mental representations, language, and computing.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — one of the major research divisions inaugurated by early leaders like *G. Stanley Hall*.
- [[humanistic-psychology]] — the movement led by figures like *Carl Rogers* and *Abraham Maslow* that established its own major division within the organization.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — the clinical tradition imported from Europe that provoked vigorous debates over scientific legitimacy within association journals.
