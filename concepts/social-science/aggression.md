## summary

**Aggression** denotes any physical or verbal action intended to cause psychological harm, bodily injury, or material destruction against another entity that is motivated to avoid such treatment. In social science and psychology, **aggressive behaviour** is commonly categorized into instrumental forms, which seek an external goal or resource, and hostile or reactive forms driven by impulse and affective anger. Systematic research has traced its genesis across instinctual drives, conditioning paradigms, neurobiological circuits, and observational modeling.

## you gotta know

- Investigated famously by *Albert Bandura* in the 1961 Bobo doll experiment, which demonstrated that nursery children exposed to an adult model pummeling an inflatable doll displayed direct *imitation* and novel hostile actions, establishing *social learning* theory.
- Framed within *psychoanalysis* by *Sigmund Freud*, who initially viewed it as a reaction to the blocking of the pleasure principle before postulating the death drive, or *Thanatos*, an innate destructive impulse turned outward against others.
- Formulated as the frustration-aggression hypothesis in 1939 by *John Dollard*, *Neal Miller*, and colleagues at Yale University, asserting that frustration inevitably induces aggressive impulses and that all aggressive acts presuppose the prior presence of frustration.
- Modified by *Leonard Berkowitz* through cognitive neoassociation theory, arguing that aversive events generate negative affect, which activates fight-or-flight networks; contextual cues such as visible weaponry can trigger hostile actions via the weapons effect.
- Contrasted with pure *behaviorism* by demonstrating that reinforcement alone is insufficient to explain how complex hostile repertoires are acquired without prior execution, highlighting cognitive mediation.
- Studied in developmental trajectories by *Jean Piaget* and *developmental psychology*, distinguishing relational hostilities, such as social exclusion, from overt physical violence across peer groups.
- Examined from ethological and evolutionary perspectives by *Konrad Lorenz*, who posited that hostile drives serve territorial and resource survival functions, while evolutionary sociologists explore how institutional strains during rapid urban transformations, like the *Industrial Revolution*, alter interpersonal conflict.

## connections

- [[albert-bandura]] — psychologist whose landmark Bobo doll experiments proved observational learning of violent acts.
- [[social-learning]] — theoretical paradigm establishing that hostility is acquired through observing social models.
- [[imitation]] — basic cognitive and behavioral mechanism by which individuals reproduce observed hostility.
- [[behaviorism]] — psychological tradition emphasizing stimulus-response conditioning, which initially struggled to explain observational hostility.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — theoretical framework that derived hostile drives from the death instinct and ego defenses.
- [[sigmund-freud]] — founder of psychoanalysis who conceptualized *Thanatos* as the internal source of destructive behavior.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — epoch of rapid social dislocation that prompted sociological investigations into urban crime and interpersonal strife.
