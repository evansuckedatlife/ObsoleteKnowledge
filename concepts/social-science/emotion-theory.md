## summary

**Emotion theory** encompasses the psychological and physiological frameworks developed to explain the origin, structure, and expression of human affective states. Beginning in the late nineteenth century in the *[[united-states]]* and *[[germany]]*, debates centered on whether physiological arousal precedes conscious feeling or whether cognitive appraisal shapes bodily responses. Understanding these models is fundamental to modern neuroscience, affective computing, clinical therapy, and cognitive science.

## you gotta know

- *William James* and *Carl Lange* independently formulated the *James-Lange theory*, asserting that emotional experience is the brain's perception of visceral bodily responses to stimuli—famously stating that we feel afraid because we run from the bear, not that we run because we feel afraid.
- *Walter Cannon* and *Philip Bard* proposed the alternative *Cannon-Bard theory*, arguing that visceral changes are too slow and non-specific to cause distinct emotions; they posited that the thalamus relays sensory information simultaneously to the cortex for emotional experience and to the autonomic nervous system for physical arousal.
- *Stanley Schachter* and *Jerome Singer* developed the *two-factor theory of emotion* in 1962, demonstrating with their famous suproxin (adrenaline) experiment that physiological arousal is non-specific and requires cognitive appraisal or environmental labeling to produce a distinct emotion.
- *Paul Ekman* revolutionized cross-cultural research by establishing six universal basic emotions—happiness, sadness, fear, disgust, anger, and surprise—demonstrated through cross-cultural facial expression studies among isolated populations.
- *Richard Lazarus* advanced the cognitive-mediational theory, asserting that cognitive appraisal precedes both physiological arousal and emotional response, categorizing evaluation into primary appraisal of threat and secondary appraisal of coping resources.
- *Robert Zajonc* contended against Lazarus that affective reactions can occur unconsciously and prior to cognitive processing, initiating the long-running "cognition and emotion" debate in psychology.
- Constructivist accounts, notably developed by *Lisa Feldman Barrett* in the theory of constructed emotion, argue that emotions are not biologically hardwired modules but cognitive concepts constructed dynamically from core affect and past categorization.

## connections

- [[cognitive-science]] — foundational field investigating the computational and appraisal processes governing emotional states.
- [[empathy]] — the interpersonal perception and sharing of emotional states across individuals.
- [[behaviorism]] — classical school led by figures like *[[b-f-skinner]]* that rejected internal mental feeling in favor of observable stimulus-response conditioning.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — approach founded by *[[sigmund-freud]]* emphasizing unconscious drives, repressed affect, and neurotic conflict.
- [[humanistic-psychology]] — therapeutic perspective emphasizing emotional self-awareness, authentic experiencing, and emotional congruence.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — discipline tracing the emergence of affective regulation and attachment bonds from infancy onwards.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical current examining fundamental emotional conditions such as existential dread, angst, and despair.
