## summary

**Classification** is the fundamental cognitive, linguistic, and social process of grouping entities, experiences, and phenomena into conceptual categories based on shared criteria or perceived relationships. In anthropology and sociology, it is famously analyzed as the cultural framework through which societies establish symbolic boundaries, moral orders, and institutional hierarchies. Seminal works, such as *Émile Durkheim* and *Marcel Mauss*'s *Primitive Classification* and *Mary Douglas*'s *Purity and Danger*, demonstrate that social structures dictate how humans organize their universe.

## you gotta know

- Theorized by *Émile Durkheim* and *Marcel Mauss* as a collective representation reflecting the social structure and spatial geography of a tribe or clan.
- Explored by structural anthropologist *Claude Lévi-Strauss* in *The Savage Mind*, demonstrating that indigenous folk taxonomies operate via sophisticated concrete logic.
- Analyzed by British anthropologist *Mary Douglas* in *Purity and Danger*, where dirt is famously defined as "matter out of place" that violates cultural classification.
- Underpins religious and secular prohibitions, dietary rules, and taboo systems that police boundaries between the clean, unclean, sacred, and profane.
- Connects directly to formal [[ceremony]] and [[ritual]], which serve to enforce, renegotiate, or transition individuals between social categories.
- Studied in [[cognitive-science]] and [[linguistics]] through prototype theory (*Eleanor Rosch*), linguistic relativity, and the architecture of lexical fields.
- Investigated by historians of science like *Michel Foucault* in *The Order of Things* to trace how epistemes and taxonomic grids order modern human knowledge.

## connections

- [[ritual]] — repetitive symbolic actions that re-establish categorical boundaries and cleanse anomalies that defy clean classification.
- [[ceremony]] — public formal events designed to mark status transitions across socially recognized categories.
- [[culture]] — the overarching web of shared categories, conventions, and mental schemas that make social life intelligible.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the interdisciplinary field that models categorization, conceptual prototypes, and mental representations in the human brain.
- [[symbolism]] — the expressive tokens, metaphors, and signs used by societies to concretize their abstract systems of categorization.
- [[linguistics]] — the study of language systems, which segment experience into semantic categories and grammatical classes.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical tradition that questions whether essentialist classifications authentically capture individual human existence.
