## summary

**Harappa** was one of the two major cities of the Indus Valley Civilization, flourishing around 2600–1900 BCE in what is now Pakistan. Comparable in sophistication to its sister city *Mohenjo-daro*, Harappa gave its name to the entire civilization, which is sometimes called the Harappan culture. Archaeological evidence reveals advanced urban planning, standardized weights and measures, and an undeciphered writing system. Despite centuries of excavation, Harappa remains one of history's most enigmatic civilizations, rivaling ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia in organizational complexity yet leaving minimal records of political or religious structures.

## you gotta know

- Located in the Punjab region of modern-day Pakistan, along the Ravi River, a tributary of the Indus.
- One of the *largest settlements* of the Indus Valley Civilization, with an estimated population of 30,000–40,000 at its height.
- Featured a *planned grid layout* with streets oriented to cardinal directions, brick construction, and sophisticated drainage systems.
- The civilization is often called *Harappan culture* because Harappa was excavated first and gave the culture its name.
- Contained *manufacturing areas*, suggesting craft specialization in pottery, tools, beads, and seals.
- *Seals with undeciphered writing* suggest literacy and record-keeping, but the script remains one of archaeology's great unsolved puzzles.
- Showed evidence of *extensive trade networks* linking to Mesopotamia, Persia, and the Arabian Peninsula.
- Declined around 1900 BCE due to unknown causes, possibly environmental change or external pressure; much remains mysterious about its collapse.
- *Standardized weights and measures* across the civilization suggest coordinated trade and a common administrative system.
- Public baths and reservoirs indicate sophisticated urban infrastructure and hydraulic engineering.
- The absence of palaces and temples suggests a more egalitarian society compared to contemporary Mesopotamian city-states, though scholarly debate continues.
