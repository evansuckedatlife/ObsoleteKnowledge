## summary

**Stone tools**, or **lithic technology**, encompass the chipped, flaked, and ground stone implements that served as humanity's primary durable technology from the early *Paleolithic* through the *Neolithic*. First manufactured over three million years ago in eastern Africa by ancestral hominins, these artifacts transformed hominin diets and catalyzed [[human-evolution]] by enabling meat butchery, bone marrow extraction, and woodworking. Their stylistic evolution across archeological industries provides anthropologists and prehistorians with the primary material record for tracking early human cognitive development and the emergence of cumulative [[culture]].

## you gotta know

- Represented the primary technological marker of human prehistory, dividing the archaeological record into the *Paleolithic* (Old Stone Age), *Mesolithic* (Middle Stone Age), and *Neolithic* (New Stone Age) based on flintknapping styles and the shift toward ground-stone farming tools.
- *Oldowan* pebble choppers and simple unifacial flakes, named after discoveries by *Louis* and *Mary Leakey* at *Olduvai Gorge* in Tanzania, represent the earliest widespread tool tradition, manufactured by ancestral hominins such as *Homo habilis* roughly 2.6 million years ago.
- *Acheulean* handaxes, characterized by distinct teardrop-shaped bifacial flaking pioneered by *Homo erectus*, persisted as the dominant global tool form for over a million years across Africa, Europe, and Asia, showing remarkable formal consistency over vast spans of time.
- The *Levallois* prepared-core technique emerged during the Middle Paleolithic *Mousterian* tradition associated with Neanderthals, requiring the knapper to predetermine and shape the core's convexities before striking off a single, razor-sharp flake of intended dimensions.
- Upper Paleolithic toolmakers associated with anatomically modern *Homo sapiens* shifted toward standardized blade industries, prismatic cores, and composite hafted weapons, exploiting cryptocrystalline stones like flint, chert, and volcanic obsidian alongside organic antler and bone.
- Specialized pressure-flaking techniques enabled hunter-gatherers, including early [[native-americans]] across North America, to produce finely channeled bifaces such as *Clovis* and *Folsom* spear points designed to penetrate thick-skinned Pleistocene megafauna.
- Microscopic use-wear analysis and residue studies pioneered by modern archeologists have demonstrated that lithics performed versatile functions far beyond hunting, including scraping animal hides, cutting plant fibers, grooving bone tools, and processing roots.
- The oldest securely dated lithic artifacts predate the emergence of the genus *Homo*, discovered at *Lomekwi 3* in Kenya and dated to roughly 3.3 million years ago, indicating that earlier hominins like *Australopithecus* or *Kenyanthropus* possessed knapping behaviors.

## connections

- [[human-evolution]] — technological innovations in butchery and marrow scavenging fueled the metabolic expansion of hominin brains and selected for precision grip.
- [[culture]] — transmitted knapping techniques across generations provide archeologists with the earliest tangible evidence of learned social traditions.
- [[ritual]] — finely polished celts, ochre-ground grinding stones, and non-utilitarian grave goods reflect the symbolic and spiritual dimensions of later lithic production.
- [[native-americans]] — sophisticated fluted point complexes like *Clovis* and *Folsom* illustrate specialized lithic technologies adapted to hunting across late Pleistocene environments.
- [[karl-marx]] — cited the fabrication of stone instruments as the historical emergence of human labor, distinguishing humans from other animals within historical materialism.
- [[symbolism]] — deliberate geometric symmetry in Acheulean bifaces and decorative engravings on late Paleolithic tools reflect an evolving cognitive capacity for abstract aesthetics.
- [[dna]] — ancient paleogenomic mapping traces demographic replacements and interbreeding events that correlate directly with the diffusion or replacement of stone industries.
