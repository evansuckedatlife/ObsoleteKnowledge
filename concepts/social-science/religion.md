## summary

**Religion** is a social system built on shared beliefs about the sacred, organized rituals, and moral frameworks that give meaning to human existence. Religions vary enormously across cultures, time periods, and expressions—from monotheistic faiths to animistic traditions to philosophical systems—but all share the function of addressing ultimate questions of meaning, morality, and human purpose. Religion shapes daily practice, law, art, and community identity for billions of people.

## you gotta know

- Religions typically include a supernatural component (belief in gods, spirits, or transcendent forces) and a social/institutional structure that binds communities together through shared meaning.
- Ritual practice is usually central to religion; worship, prayer, sacrifice, and initiation ceremonies transmit and reinforce religious identity across generations and mark passages in individual lives.
- Anthropologist Clifford Geertz defined religion as a "system of symbols" that establishes powerful moods and motivations among believers, functioning as both cognitive framework and emotional experience.
- Religion functions to address existential anxiety, enforce social norms, validate authority structures, create in-group cohesion, and provide frameworks for interpreting suffering and injustice.
- Magic and religion are often conflated but analytically distinct: magic assumes manipulation of impersonal forces through technical procedure; religion assumes relationship with personal beings (gods or ancestors) through supplication and trust.
- Secularization theory posits that modernization erodes religious belief, though global data shows religion remains influential even in developed nations and that religious revivals occur cyclically.
- Major world religions (Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism) number over 2 billion adherents, while millions follow indigenous religions, syncretistic traditions, and new religious movements.
- The relationship between religion and other domains (science, politics, law) remains contested: some societies mandate separation of church and state; others interweave religious authority with governmental power.

## connections

- [[ritual]] — religion is enacted and transmitted through ritual practice and ceremony.
- [[mythology]] — myths provide the narrative foundation of religious belief and cosmology.
- [[theology]] — the philosophical discipline that systematizes and defends religious doctrine.
- [[clifford-geertz]] — anthropologist whose symbolic interpretation revolutionized how scholars study religion.
- [[magic]] — sometimes conflated with religion but analytically distinct in method and intention.
- [[spirituality]] — personal religious experience distinct from institutional religion.
