# Shortage and Surplus

## summary

**Shortage** occurs when the quantity demanded of a good exceeds the quantity supplied at the current price, leaving some consumers unable to purchase; **surplus** (or glut) is the opposite—quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded, leaving unsold inventory. These states of disequilibrium in markets create pressures toward [[equilibrium-price]], where the two quantities balance. Understanding shortages and surpluses is essential to grasping how markets coordinate millions of individual decisions through price signals.

## you gotta know

- At any given price, a shortage means buyers want more than sellers are offering; a surplus means sellers have more than buyers want.
- Shortages typically drive prices upward as competition for limited supply intensifies; surpluses typically drive prices downward as sellers cut prices to move inventory.
- The shortage or surplus is not a fixed feature of a good—it depends entirely on the current price. Raising price reduces shortage; lowering price reduces surplus.
- Shortages can cause non-price rationing mechanisms: waiting lists, rationing by seller preference, or allocation by lottery, since price is not clearing the market.
- Surpluses can trigger seller strategies like bulk discounts, planned obsolescence, or destruction of excess inventory to maintain price.
- Persistent shortages can indicate price controls (such as rent ceilings or price floors) that prevent equilibrium from being reached.
- Historical examples include oil embargoes creating shortages, agricultural price supports creating surpluses of grain, and housing shortages in cities with restrictive zoning.
- The lag between supply and demand—the time needed for producers to respond to shortage signals—explains why some markets oscillate cyclically between shortage and surplus.

## connections

- [[supply-and-demand]] — the fundamental market dynamic where shortage and surplus represent disequilibrium states.
- [[equilibrium-price]] — the price at which shortage and surplus both equal zero and quantity supplied meets quantity demanded.
- [[market-mechanism]] — the process by which price adjustments respond to and resolve shortages and surpluses.
- [[price-signal]] — the information conveyed by shortage or surplus that prompts market participants to adjust behavior.
- [[elasticity]] — determines how responsive quantity demanded and supplied are to price changes when shortage or surplus emerges.
- [[allocation]] — shortages and surpluses raise the question of how scarce goods get distributed when price alone cannot do so.
