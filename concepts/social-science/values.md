## summary

**Values**, frequently conceptualized across the social sciences as **cultural values**, are the abstract, enduring ideals and evaluative standards that define what is desirable, moral, and worthwhile within a human society or social group. In sociology and cultural anthropology, they constitute the foundational core of [[culture]], translating broad metaphysical worldviews into the tangible principles that guide individual conduct, motivate collective behavior, and legitimize institutional arrangements. Understanding these foundational principles allows scholars to examine how populations maintain social [[conformity]], transmit sacred traditions through [[ritual]], and navigate profound ethical transformations across different historical epochs.

## you gotta know

- *Milton Rokeach* revolutionized the empirical measurement of human belief systems by introducing the *Rokeach Value Survey* (*RVS*), which famously bisected human ideals into eighteen *terminal values*—representing desirable end-states of existence such as freedom, equality, world peace, and wisdom—and eighteen *instrumental values*, which describe preferred modes of conduct such as honesty, ambition, and obedience.
- *Geert Hofstede* pioneered comparative cross-cultural psychology and organizational sociology by analyzing extensive survey data from multinational subsidiaries, identifying foundational dimensions of societal variation that include individualism versus collectivism, power distance, uncertainty avoidance, masculinity versus femininity, and long-term orientation.
- *Max Weber* delineated the crucial distinction between outcome-oriented instrumental rationality (*Zweckrationalität*) and conviction-driven value rationality (*Wertrationalität*), famously demonstrating in *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism* how ascetic religious convictions spurred economic development in [[england]] while championing value-freedom (*Wertfreiheit*) in academic inquiry.
- *Shalom H. Schwartz* formulated the *Theory of Basic Human Values*, mapping ten motivationally distinct universal human principles onto a circumplex continuum structured around two orthogonal dimensions: *Openness to Change* versus *Conservation*, and *Self-Transcendence* versus *Self-Enhancement*.
- *Ronald Inglehart* analyzed global generational data through the *World Values Survey*, demonstrating that rising economic security in post-industrial societies leads populations to shift from survival-oriented materialist concerns toward post-materialist priorities emphasizing individual autonomy, environmental protection, and personal [[self-actualization]].
- *Talcott Parsons* placed internalized cultural standards at the structural center of functionalist sociology, arguing that institutionalized normative patterns maintain social equilibrium and prevent disruption by guiding social actors through dichotomous choices known as *pattern variables*.
- *Clyde Kluckhohn* defined these constructs as explicit or implicit conceptions of the desirable that influence selection among available modes, means, and ends of action, collaborating with *Florence Kluckhohn* and *Fred Strodtbeck* to formulate the *Value Orientations Theory* regarding five universal human problems.

## connections

- [[culture]] — the overarching symbolic matrix within which shared ideals and worldview assumptions are formulated, internalized, and transmitted across generations.
- [[conformity]] — the behavioral alignment with institutional norms and collective expectations that are directly legitimized and sustained by underlying societal principles.
- [[ritual]] — the patterned communal practice that dramatizes, sanctifies, and periodically renews collective commitments to core societal ideals.
- [[authority]] — the institutionalized exercise of legitimate power whose social acceptance depends upon congruence with prevailing ethical and cultural standards.
- [[abraham-maslow]] — humanistic theorist who linked progressive human development to higher-order psychological motivations and transcendent *Being-values*.
- [[self-actualization]] — the realization of personal potential that prioritizes expressive growth, authenticity, and autonomous creativity over material security.
- [[franz-boas]] — founding anthropologist whose framework of cultural relativism demonstrated that each society must be evaluated according to its own distinctive standards.
