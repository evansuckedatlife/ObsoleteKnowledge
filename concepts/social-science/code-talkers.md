## summary

**Code talkers** were indigenous military service members who utilized their complex, unwritten native languages to transmit encrypted tactical battlefield communications during major 20th-century conflicts. Primarily recruited into the *United States Marine Corps* and the *United States Army* during *World War II*, the most celebrated group were the *Navajo code talkers* who operated across the Pacific Theater. Their voice-transmitted, orally encoded communication system was the only military cryptosystem in modern history that was never deciphered by enemy intelligence.

## you gotta know

- First pioneered during *World War I* when *Cherokee* and *Choctaw* soldiers serving in the American Expeditionary Forces in *France* transmitted vital telephone dispatches to confound Imperial German wiretappers.
- Developed into a sophisticated, formalized military cipher during *World War II* after civilian engineer *Philip Johnston* suggested utilizing the intricate Athabaskan syntax of the *Navajo* tongue to the Marine Corps.
- Created by an initial cohort known as the "First Twenty-Nine" Navajo recruits, who designed a two-tiered dictionary that paired Navajo words with military terms, such as *béésh łichíiʼii* ("red metal") for wire and *chʼééh ditsʼiní* ("tortoise") for tank.
- Integrated a phonetic alphabet for spelling out unlisted words, assigning English letters to Navajo terms for birds and animals, such as *shush* ("bear") for B and *tłʼiish* ("snake") for S.
- Proved indispensable at the *Battle of Iwo Jima*, where major signal officer *Howard Connor* declared that the Marines would not have taken the island without the Navajo operators, who sent over 800 error-free messages in the opening forty-eight hours.
- Deployed across both theaters of war with contributions from several indigenous nations, including *Comanche* soldiers in the European Theater and *Hopi*, *Meskwaki*, and *Lakota* service members in the Pacific.
- Kept strictly classified by the *United States* military until 1968, delaying formal national honors until the *United States Congress* awarded the Congressional Gold Medal to the veterans in 2000.
