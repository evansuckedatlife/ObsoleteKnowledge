## summary

**Cognitive linguistics** is an interdisciplinary branch of *[[linguistics]]* and *[[cognitive-science]]* that emerged in the late 1970s and 1980s, primarily in the *[[united-states]]*. It treats language not as an isolated, autonomous mental organ, but as an integral manifestation of general cognitive mechanisms such as perception, categorization, memory, and motor processing. Championed by theorists like *George Lakoff*, *Ronald Langacker*, and *Mark Johnson*, the discipline asserts that meaning is thoroughly grounded in bodily experience, rejecting formal truth-conditional logic and generative modularity in favor of conceptual metaphor, framing, and prototype theory.

## you gotta know

- Established in open opposition to the generative grammar paradigm of *[[noam-chomsky]]*, which held that syntax exists as an innate, encapsulated computational module distinct from general cognition.
- Founded upon the theory of conceptual metaphor formulated by *George Lakoff* and *Mark Johnson* in their seminal 1980 work *Metaphors We Live By*, which proved that everyday thought is systematically organized through metaphoric mappings from concrete physical domains to abstract target domains (e.g., *argument is war* or *love is a journey*).
- Anchored in the principle of embodied cognition, arguing that mental representations, grammatical constructions, and linguistic abstractions are fundamentally shaped by human sensorimotor interaction with the physical environment.
- Developed Cognitive Grammar under *Ronald Langacker*, who demonstrated that grammatical categories are inherently meaningful rather than arbitrary formal transformations, viewing syntax as the conventional symbolic pairing of semantic structures and phonological forms.
- Replaced classical Aristotelian binary categorization with prototype theory, drawing on psychologist *Eleanor Rosch*'s findings that categories possess fuzzy boundaries organized around graded degrees of typicality.
- Formalized conceptual blending and mental space theory through the work of *Gilles Fauconnier* and *Mark Turner*, explaining how humans integrate distinct conceptual inputs to generate novel insights and imaginative counterfactuals.
- Applied directly to political discourse and social analysis through *George Lakoff*'s moral framing frameworks, analyzing how political ideologies rely on competing family metaphors (*Strict Father* versus *Nurturant Parent*).

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — the overarching scientific study of human speech and language that cognitive linguistics revolutionized through functional and semantic approaches.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the parent interdisciplinary matrix integrating psychology, neuroscience, and artificial intelligence with linguistic inquiry.
- [[noam-chomsky]] — the dominant figure of modern formal linguistics whose doctrine of Universal Grammar and syntactic autonomy provoked the cognitive revolt.
- [[sociolinguistics]] — examines how linguistic variations and cognitive conceptual metaphors circulate across distinct cultural and socioeconomic strata.
- [[cognitive-dissonance]] — psychological tension arising when incoming linguistic frames or reality counter-evidence violently collide with deeply entrenched conceptual metaphors.
- [[cognitive-revolution]] — the intellectual shift that established cognitive modeling, providing the epistemological foundation from which cognitive semantics developed.
