## summary

**Humanistic psychology** emerged in the 1950s-60s as a reaction against both behaviourism and psychoanalysis, emphasizing human potential, creativity, and self-determination. It positions people as active agents capable of growth and meaningful choice, rather than passive subjects governed by drives or environmental conditioning.

## you gotta know

- Arose as the "third force" in psychology, offering an alternative to the pessimism of [[sigmund-freud|Freudian]] psychoanalysis (focused on unconscious conflicts and pathology) and the mechanical nature of [[behaviorism]] (stimulus-response, no agency).
- Maslow founded the movement by proposing the hierarchy of needs and the concept of *self-actualization* — the drive to fulfill one's unique potential; self-actualization sits atop a pyramid where basic needs (food, safety) must be met first.
- Carl Rogers emphasized *unconditional positive regard* and *client-centered therapy* — therapeutic relationships should be supportive and non-judgmental; Rogers believed people naturally tend toward growth when conditions are right.
- [[alfred-adler]]'s theory of individual striving for superiority and his focus on social feeling and belonging were early influences on humanistic thinking; Adler emphasized inferiority feelings driving ambition.
- Humanistic psychology celebrates *peak experiences* — moments of profound joy, creativity, or insight when people feel most alive and connected to meaning; these moments reveal human potential.
- The movement values *authenticity* and *personal responsibility* — individuals must take ownership of their choices and growth rather than blaming external forces.
- Contemporary [[positive-psychology]] inherits humanistic themes by studying human flourishing, strengths, and resilience rather than only pathology.
- The movement emphasizes *existential freedom* — individuals create meaning and identity through choices; this differs from seeing people as determined by drives or environment.
- Humanistic psychology influenced education (student-centered learning), organizational development (empowerment, self-actualization at work), and therapeutic practice; its optimism about human potential endures despite being eclipsed by cognitive-behavioural approaches in clinical settings.

## connections

- [[abraham-maslow]] — founder of humanistic psychology; pioneered the concept of self-actualization.
- [[carl-rogers]] — developed person-centered therapy; emphasized empathy and unconditional positive regard.
- [[alfred-adler]] — early theorist of inferiority, striving, and social connection; anticipated humanistic themes.
- [[self-actualization]] — the central goal of humanistic psychology; fulfilling one's potential.
- [[peak-experiences]] — transcendent moments of joy and meaning; key concept in Maslow's humanistic vision.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical movement emphasizing freedom and responsibility; shares humanistic themes about choice and authenticity.
- [[positive-psychology]] — modern inheritor of humanistic focus on strengths, resilience, and human flourishing.
