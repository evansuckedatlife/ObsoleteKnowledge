## summary

**Breton** is an Insular Celtic language of the Brythonic branch, spoken natively in Brittany in northwestern *France*. Brought to the Armorican peninsula by Brittonic migrants fleeing Anglo-Saxon settlement in Great Britain during the Early Middle Ages, it is the only modern Celtic tongue spoken primarily on the European mainland. Long subjected to aggressive French state linguistic centralization, the language has experienced severe contraction yet survives through revitalization networks such as Diwan immersion schools.

## you gotta know

- Classified as an Insular Celtic tongue belonging directly to the Southwestern Brythonic sub-branch alongside Cornish, rather than descending from the ancient Continental Celtic language of Gaulish.
- Brought to Armorica between the fourth and seventh centuries CE by emigrants from southwestern *England* and South Wales, an era traditionally styled the *Age of the Saints*.
- Historically divided into four traditional episcopal dialects: Cornouaillais (*Kerneveg*), Léonard (*Leoneg*), Trégorrois (*Tregerieg*), and the phonologically divergent Vannetais (*Gwenedeg*).
- Experienced severe decline under linguistic centralization policies of the Third Republic in *France*, which strictly forbade regional languages in public education under the rubric of republican unity.
- Employs extensive initial consonant mutations triggered by grammatical environment, including soft (lenition), spirant, and hard (provection) mutations, as well as a distinct mixed mutation series.
- Preserves a rich oral literary heritage celebrated in the nineteenth-century folk collection *Barzaz Breiz*, compiled by Théodore Hersart de La Villemarqué.
- Modern revival efforts center on the *Diwan* associative school system founded in 1977, which utilizes total immersion pedagogy to produce fluent bilingual youth.
