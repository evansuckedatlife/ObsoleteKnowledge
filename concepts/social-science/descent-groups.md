## summary

**descent groups** are social aggregates whose membership is determined through recognized genealogical connections to a common ancestor, functioning as core structural units in kinship systems worldwide. Prominently theorized by British functionalist anthropologists like *A. R. Radcliffe-Brown* and *E. E. Evans-Pritchard*, these corporate formations manage property, arrange marriages, organize collective rituals, and regulate political authority. Understanding their principles—whether patrilineal, matrilineal, or ambilineal—remains foundational to interpreting cross-cultural family dynamics and non-state political order.

## you gotta know

- Lineages are unilineal descent groupings whose members can state their precise apical genealogical connections step by step to a known historical forebear.
- Clans represent larger, more inclusive corporate groups whose members claim common descent from an apical ancestor, though the exact genealogical links are often mythical, totemic, or untraced.
- Unilineal descent traces ancestry exclusively through a single parent, split between patrilineal (agnatic) descent through the male line and matrilineal (uterine) descent through the female line.
- Phratries and moieties organize clans into higher-level dual divisions, frequently regulating group exogamy and structuring ceremonial or sports competitions.
- Segmentary lineage systems, analyzed famously among the *Nuer* of South Sudan, mobilize allied sub-branches according to structural distance during external conflicts or feuds.
- Corporate descent units often hold collective rights in perpetuity over agricultural land, herds, ceremonial titles, ancestral shrines, and physical relics.
- Alliance theorists such as *Claude Lévi-Strauss* critiqued descent theory by arguing that structural exchange through reciprocal marriage rules matters more in maintaining intergroup cohesion than biological lineages.

## connections

- [[culture]] — shapes the symbolic definitions, ancestral dogmas, and rules that govern kinship recruitment.
- [[ritual]] — reinforces lineage unity through ancestral veneration rites and collective life-cycle ceremonies.
- [[symbolism]] — supplies totemic crests and figurative badges that visually manifest clan identity.
- [[dene]] — exhibits historic matrilineal clans and complex lineage bands across Northern Athabaskan societies.
- [[franz-boas]] — demonstrated that social organization and kinship terminology vary independently of biological race.
- [[native-americans]] — features diverse lineage practices, from Haudenosaunee matrilineal longhouses to Southwestern clan arrangements.
- [[dating-methods]] — provides temporal benchmarks for cemetery complexes reflecting lineage residential permanence.
