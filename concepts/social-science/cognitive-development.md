## summary

**Cognitive development** is the process through which children's thinking, reasoning, memory, and problem-solving capacities emerge and change from infancy through childhood to adulthood. Rather than viewing children as miniature adults, developmental psychologists recognize that cognition changes qualitatively—children do not merely know less but think differently at different ages. Jean Piaget's theory of cognitive development through distinct stages revolutionized psychology and education, showing that childhood reasoning involves predictable cognitive structures and limitations that mature over time.

## you gotta know

- Piaget identified four major stages: *sensorimotor* (0–2 years, learning through senses and motor activity), *preoperational* (2–7 years, symbolic thinking but limited logical reasoning), *concrete operational* (7–11, logical thinking about concrete objects), and *formal operational* (11+ onward, abstract reasoning and hypothetical thinking).
- *Object permanence*—understanding that objects continue to exist when hidden from view—emerges in the sensorimotor stage (around 8 months) and marks a major cognitive breakthrough.
- *Egocentrism*—the inability to mentally take another's perspective—characterizes the preoperational stage; children assume others literally see and know what they themselves see and know.
- *Conservation tasks* (judging that quantity remains the same despite changes in appearance) measure concrete operational thinking; younger children fail conservation tasks, believing taller containers hold more liquid despite seeing equal pouring.
- Cognitive development is not merely accumulation of knowledge but active *reorganization of mental structures* through interaction with the physical and social environment—a constructivist view.
- Modern research qualifies Piaget's stages: some capacities (object permanence, number conservation) emerge earlier than Piaget thought; others emerge later; infants show surprising competencies; but Piaget's insight that cognition undergoes qualitative transformation remains confirmed.
- Later theories (Vygotsky's *zone of proximal development*, information-processing models, sociocultural perspectives) expanded Piaget's framework, showing that cognitive development is more social and contextual than his individual-focused model suggested.

## connections

- [[jean-piaget]] — pioneering psychologist whose stage theory founded the scientific study of cognitive development.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — the discipline studying how cognition, emotion, and behavior change across the lifespan.
- [[stages-of-development]] — the framework Piaget created; distinct cognitive periods with characteristic abilities and limitations.
- [[constructivism]] — philosophical and educational approach holding that learners actively construct understanding through experience.
- [[learning]] — cognitive development is learning writ large; the emergence of capacity to learn.
- [[childhood]] — cognitive development is the central concern of childhood studies; how young minds mature.
