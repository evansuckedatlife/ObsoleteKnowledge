## summary

**Conversational implicature** refers to the semantic and communicative process whereby an utterance conveys a meaning that extends far beyond, and sometimes directly contradicts, its literal truth-conditional content. Formulated by the British philosopher *H. P. Grice* in his foundational 1967 *William James Lectures* at Harvard University, the concept sits at the absolute foundation of contemporary pragmatics and ordinary language philosophy. It demonstrates that verbal communication operates not via mechanical decoding alone, but as a rational, cooperative enterprise governed by shared presumptions of communicative intent.

## you gotta know

- Introduced by philosopher *H. P. Grice* in his seminal lecture and 1975 essay *"Logic and Conversation"*, bridging the gulf between formal logic and natural language discourse.
- Grounded in the overarching *Cooperative Principle*, which instructs speakers to make their conversational contributions such as is required at the stage at which it occurs by the accepted purpose of the exchange.
- Operationalized through four fundamental *Gricean Maxims*: Quantity (be sufficiently informative), Quality (speak truth and avoid falsehood), Relation (be relevant), and Manner (be perspicuous, brief, and orderly).
- Implicatures arise in two distinct ways: either through direct adherence to a maxim or through the ostentatious, deliberate *flouting* of a maxim to signal an indirect meaning.
- Classic ironies, metaphors, hyperboles, and understatements represent paradigmatic examples of flouting the Maxim of Quality to force the listener to calculate an implied interpretation.
- Implicatures possess specific diagnostic properties: they are *cancellable* or *defeasible* (one can retract them without semantic contradiction), *calculable* (deducible via contextual inference), and *nondetachable* (substituting synonyms preserves the inference).
- Distinguished sharply from *entailment* and *presupposition*, which are strictly bound to literal semantic truth values rather than dependent on social context and conversational etiquette.
- Evolved in post-Gricean pragmatics into *Relevance Theory*, spearheaded by *Dan Sperber* and *Deirdre Wilson*, who reduced the maxims to a single cognitive principle of cognitive utility.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — the overarching scientific discipline that houses pragmatics, syntax, semantics, and sociolinguistic discourse.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the field examining intentionality, mental modeling, inference engines, and communicative cognition.
- [[sociolinguistics]] — the study of how status, social norms, and politeness strategies determine when speakers deploy indirect implicatures.
- [[conservation-task]] — developmental paradigm where researchers frequently trigger misleading implicatures by asking children the same question twice.
- [[creoles]] — contact languages whose expressive pragmatics demonstrate universal human principles of contextual inference.
- [[constructed-languages]] — engineered languages such as *Lojban* that deliberately attempt to eliminate semantic ambiguity and conversational implicatures.
