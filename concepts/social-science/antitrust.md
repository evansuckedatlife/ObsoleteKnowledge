## summary

**Antitrust** refers to the body of statutes, administrative regulations, and judicial precedents designed to preserve commercial competition and prevent abusive monopoly power. Developed primarily in the *United States* during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries in response to the rise of massive industrial cartels and corporate trusts, these policies seek to curb predatory price manipulation and artificial barriers to entry. Modern legal frameworks balance consumer welfare, economic efficiency, and the limitation of private economic coercion across evolving digital and global markets.

## you gotta know

- The *Sherman Antitrust Act* of 1890 forms the statutory cornerstone of American competition law, prohibiting contracts, combinations, or conspiracies in restraint of interstate trade under Section 1, and monopolization or attempts to monopolize under Section 2.
- The *Standard Oil Co. of New Jersey v. United States* (1911) Supreme Court decision fractured *John D. Rockefeller*'s oil empire and formalized the legal *rule of reason* standard for assessing whether commercial restraints are undue.
- The *Clayton Antitrust Act* of 1914 strengthened enforcement by explicitly barring specific anticompetitive practices, including price discrimination, tying arrangements, exclusive dealing contracts, and interlocking directorates.
- The *Federal Trade Commission Act* of 1914 established the independent *Federal Trade Commission* (FTC) to investigate unfair methods of competition and deceptive business practices alongside the *Department of Justice* Antitrust Division.
- The *consumer welfare standard*, popularized by legal scholar *Robert Bork* and the Chicago School of economics in the 1970s, shifted judicial focus toward price effects and productive efficiency rather than corporate size or market concentration alone.
- Landmark enforcement breakups transformed key American industries, notably fracturing the *American Tobacco Company* in 1911 and dissolving the telecommunications monopoly of *AT&T* into regional *Baby Bells* in 1982.
- Modern revival movements, often labeled *New Brandeis* antitrust or neo-Brandeisian policy, advocate for broader standards that incorporate worker wages, market power concentration, and algorithmic platform dominance.
