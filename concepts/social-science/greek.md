## summary

**Greek** is an independent branch of the Indo-European language family with a documented linguistic history spanning more than thirty-four centuries. Originating in the southern Balkan peninsula and the Aegean basin, it evolved from the Mycenaean syllabic script known as Linear B into the classical alphabet that codified Western philosophy, literature, and science. In [[sociolinguistics]] and structural [[linguistics]], it represents one of the premier historical case studies for diglossia, dialect evolution, and deep lexical diffusion across world languages.

## you gotta know

- Represents an independent primary sub-branch of Indo-European, possessing the longest unbroken written documentation of any living Indo-European tongue.
- The earliest recorded form is *Mycenaean Greek*, preserved on clay tablets written in the *Linear B* syllabary dating from roughly the 14th to 12th centuries BCE.
- Adopted the Phoenician abjad around the 8th century BCE, famously adding dedicated consonant-vowel parity and creating the true Greek alphabet used in [[ancient-greece]].
- *Koine Greek* arose as the lingua franca of the Mediterranean following the conquests of *Alexander the Great*, serving as the language of the Christian *Septuagint* and the *New Testament*.
- Retained continuity through Medieval or *Byzantine Greek*, functioning as the official administrative, theological, and literary vehicle of the Eastern [[roman-empire]].
- In modern times, the language endured a celebrated state of *diglossia* lasting until 1976: the literary, archaizing standard (*Katharevousa*) competed directly with the popular vernacular (*Demotic*).
- Systemic lexical borrowing provided the fundamental terminology for disciplines across natural philosophy, medicine, geometry, and [[cognitive-science]].

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline analyzing its historical phonology, nominal morphology, and genetic classification.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization whose poetry, drama, politics, and civic identity were codified in Greek.
- [[sociolinguistics]] — the field examining the socio-political divide between *Katharevousa* and *Demotic* forms in modern Greece.
- [[culture]] — the shared customs, myths, rituals, and artistic expressions carried across millennia via Greek texts.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial power whose elite culture and eastern administrative apparatus relied fundamentally on Greek literacy.
- [[symbolism]] — the complex semantic representations developed in classical philosophy and Greek mythic traditions.
