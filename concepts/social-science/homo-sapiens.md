## summary

**Homo sapiens**—anatomically and behaviorally modern humans—emerged in Africa roughly 300,000 years ago and eventually spread to inhabit every continent except Antarctica. Characterized by large brains, complex language, abstract reasoning, and cumulative culture, *Homo sapiens* represents the final stage of human evolution and the only human species surviving to the present. The species emerged through natural selection operating on ancestral populations over millions of years.

## you gotta know

- *Homo sapiens* evolved from earlier hominin species, likely through populations ancestral to both modern humans and *Neanderthals*; genetic evidence suggests these groups interbred when ranges overlapped.
- *Anatomical modernity*—the skeletal form we recognize today—arose before *behavioral modernity*; the full suite of abstract thought, symbolic expression, and complex planning emerged gradually over tens of thousands of years.
- The *cognitive revolution*, roughly 70,000 years ago, saw the emergence of symbolic thought, language sophistication, art, and planning capabilities that define modern human behavior—though the mechanisms remain debated.
- *Homo sapiens* were not the first tool-makers; earlier hominins crafted stone tools, but modern humans developed specialized tools, cooperative hunting strategies, and technological innovations at unprecedented pace.
- The species demonstrates *unusual ecological flexibility*: humans adapted to tropical savannas, arctic tundra, deserts, rainforests, and islands—surviving not through physical specialization but through cultural adaptation and learning.
- Genetic evidence shows all modern humans descended from African populations; subsequent migrations and geographic isolation produced the biological diversity observed across populations today, though human genetic variation is minimal compared to other species.
- *Neanderthals* and *Homo sapiens* overlapped for thousands of years in Europe and the Levant; evidence of interbreeding reveals that modern non-African populations carry 1–4% Neanderthal DNA, suggesting admixture rather than replacement.
