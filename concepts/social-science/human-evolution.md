## summary

**Human evolution** is the process by which anatomically and behaviourally modern humans emerged from non-human primate ancestors over millions of years. This process involved gradual changes in body structure, brain development, tool use, and social complexity, shaped by environmental pressures and natural selection.

## you gotta know

- The human lineage diverged from other African apes around 6-7 million years ago; a defining moment was the emergence of *bipedalism* — walking upright on two legs; freed hands for tool-making and carrying food.
- *Australopithecus* (4-2 million years ago) were early bipedal hominins with small brains (400-500 cc); they may have used simple tools but lacked language and complex culture; their fossils show pronounced sexual dimorphism.
- *Homo erectus* (2 million to 100,000 years ago) showed dramatic increases in brain size (700-1200 cc), controlled fire use, and made more sophisticated stone tools (hand axes); first hominin to leave Africa and colonize Eurasia.
- *Homo sapiens* (our species) emerged in Africa around 300,000 years ago; by 70,000 years ago, behavioural modernity appeared with symbolic thinking, language, art, and ritualized burial; this "great leap" may reflect a genetic mutation affecting language capacity.
- The [[olduvai-gorge]] in Tanzania is a key archaeological site where early stone tools and hominin fossils reveal the transition to tool-making and hunting.
- Tool-making, hunting, language, and social cooperation — hallmarks of human culture — evolved gradually and are linked to brain expansion driven by natural selection.
- The [[paleolithic|Paleolithic]] era represents the vast period when human ancestors lived by hunting and gathering, the lifestyle for over 99% of human history; humans remained in Africa until *Homo sapiens* dispersed globally around 70,000 years ago.
- Language likely emerged gradually over millions of years as brain size increased and social structures grew more complex; it enabled abstract thought, planning, and cultural transmission.
- Comparison with other primates reveals capacities that are uniquely human: abstract symbolic thought, recursive grammar, theory of mind, and capacity for cumulative culture.

## connections

- [[cenozoic-era]] — the geological era during which human evolution occurred; spans 66 million years to the present.
- [[olduvai-gorge]] — iconic archaeological site with early hominin remains and stone tools; key evidence of the human evolutionary trajectory.
- [[paleolithic]] — the cultural period of human hunting-and-gathering; spans most of human existence.
- [[homo-sapiens]] — anatomically and behaviourally modern humans; the endpoint of the evolution discussed here.
- [[australopithecus]] — early bipedal hominins on the path to the human lineage.
- [[homo-erectus]] — intermediate form between australopithecus and modern humans; pioneer tool-maker and fire-user.
- [[natural-selection]] — the evolutionary mechanism driving changes in anatomy, behaviour, and cognition.
