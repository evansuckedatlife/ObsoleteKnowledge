## summary

A **conservation task** is an empirical assessment developed by Swiss psychologist *Jean Piaget* to determine whether a child understands that certain physical properties of an object or substance remain invariant despite superficial transformations in appearance. Formulated in Geneva during the mid-twentieth century as part of his broader theory of genetic epistemology, these experiments serve as the diagnostic benchmark marking the cognitive transition from the preoperational stage to the concrete operational stage. Mastery of conservation illustrates how developing minds acquire logical reversibility, decentration, and compensation rather than relying exclusively on immediate perceptual judgment.

## you gotta know

- Formulated by *Jean Piaget* and his long-time collaborator *Bärbel Inhelder* to map the precise developmental stages of childhood reasoning and logical competence.
- The classic liquid volume experiment presents two identical short, wide beakers containing equal amounts of water, pours one into a tall, narrow cylinder, and asks whether the quantity has changed.
- Children in the preoperational stage (typically ages two to seven) consistently fail the test, asserting that the taller glass holds more liquid because they fixate on height alone.
- Passing the test requires overcoming *centration*—the cognitive tendency to focus on only one salient perceptual dimension while ignoring all other relevant factors.
- Success hinges on three core operational insights: *identity* (nothing was added or removed), *compensation* (the liquid is taller but narrower), and *reversibility* (pouring it back restores the original appearance).
- Conservation emerges in a predictable sequence across childhood known as *horizontal decalage*, wherein number is conserved first around age six, followed by mass and liquid volume around age seven or eight, and volume displacement not until age nine to eleven.
- Methodological critics such as *Margaret Donaldson* later demonstrated that children often fail Piagetian tasks because pragmatic framing and repetitive questioning lead them to infer that the adult experimenter expects a changed answer.
- Cross-cultural replications have established that schooling and ecological demands influence the precise age of acquisition, though the sequential structural trajectory remains remarkably robust worldwide.

## connections

- [[jean-piaget]] — the Swiss developmental psychologist who invented the paradigm to illustrate operational thought.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — the discipline within psychology that studies systematic changes across human lifespan reasoning.
- [[cognitive-science]] — the interdisciplinary field investigating mental representations, operations, and rule acquisition.
- [[behaviorism]] — the competing psychological paradigm that dismissed unobservable operational cognitive structures in favor of conditioned stimulus-response patterns.
- [[erik-erikson]] — contemporary developmental theorist who organized childhood growth around psychosocial crises rather than operational cognitive stages.
