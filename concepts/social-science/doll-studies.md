## summary

The **doll studies** were a pioneering series of psychological experiments conducted in the late 1930s and 1940s by African American psychologists *Kenneth Clark* and *Mamie Phipps Clark* to assess the effects of racial segregation on Black children's self-esteem and racial perception. By presenting young children with identical dolls differing only in skin tone and asking them to ascribe positive or negative attributes, the researchers demonstrated that systemic racism caused internalized feelings of inferiority from an early age. The findings provided foundational social science evidence cited by the Supreme Court in the landmark 1954 desegregation decision *Brown v. Board of Education*.

## you gotta know

- Designed and administered by husband-and-wife psychologists *Kenneth Clark* and *Mamie Phipps Clark*, who began the project as Mamie's master's thesis at Howard University before expanding it in [[new-york-city]] and segregated southern schools.
- Presented African American children aged three to seven with four identical plastic dolls, identical in every way except for skin color and hair color, two with brown skin and black hair and two with white skin and yellow hair.
- Asked structured questions prompting subjects to assign affective and moral traits, such as identifying which doll was the "nice doll," which looked "bad," which was the "pretty doll," and which doll had the "nice color."
- Revealed that a substantial majority of Black children preferred the white doll and attributed positive characteristics to it, while rejecting the brown doll as bad or ugly.
- Culminated in the psychologically distressing final prompt instructing the child to "give me the doll that looks like you," which often caused subjects to cry, break down, or storm out of the room in emotional conflict.
- Cited prominently by Chief Justice *Earl Warren* in footnote 11 of *Brown v. Board of Education*, marking the first time modern empirical psychological research directly influenced a monumental [[united-states-constitution]] ruling.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the historic struggle against institutionalized white supremacy that utilized the experiments to strike down school segregation.
- [[developmental-psychology]] — the scientific study of child development, cognition, and identity formation exemplified by the Clarks' protocol.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the legal framework whose Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause was reinterpreted using the study's evidence.
- [[conformity]] — the social phenomenon wherein marginalized children internalize dominant cultural prejudices and social hierarchy.
- [[new-york-city]] — the location of the Northside Center for Child Development founded by the Clarks, where they continued their community psychology work.
- [[authority]] — the institutional and societal power whose enforced segregation generated severe psychological harm in vulnerable youth.
- [[deviance]] — the sociological construct whereby segregated minority groups were pathologized and cast outside normative societal value.
