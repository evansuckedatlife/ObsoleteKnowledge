## summary

**Monogamy** is a form of social, reproductive, or legal pairing in which an individual maintains an exclusive relationship with a single partner at any given time. Although rare among non-avian mammals, persistent pair-bonding developed as a critical behavioral strategy during [[human-evolution]] to facilitate paternal care and sustain altricial offspring with prolonged developmental periods. In anthropology, sociology, and evolutionary psychology, researchers investigate monogamy across its biological, institutional, and normative manifestations.

## you gotta know

- Anthropologists and behavioral ecologists distinguish among *social monogamy* (co-habitating and joint parental care), *sexual monogamy* (exclusive mating), and *serial monogamy* (a succession of exclusive unions).
- Evolutionary biology suggests human pair-bonding evolved in response to intense selection pressures, particularly the nutritional demands of large, energy-dense infant brains requiring cooperative biparental provisioning.
- George Peter Murdock's comparative cross-cultural *Ethnographic Atlas* demonstrated that while polygyny was socially permitted in over eighty percent of historic societies, the majority of individual unions within them remained economically monogamous.
- Western socially imposed monogamy originated primarily in ancient Greco-Roman civil law and civic citizenship requirements, which were subsequently sacralized into strict canon law by [[christianity]] across medieval Europe.
- Neurobiological research in animal models, particularly comparative studies of prairie voles (*Microtus ochrogaster*), demonstrates that pair-bonding and social attachment are regulated by the neuropeptide hormones oxytocin and arginine vasopressin.
- In 19th-century sociological theory, *Friedrich Engels* argued in *The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State* that monogamous marriage arose with private agrarian property to ensure verifiable paternity for inherited wealth.
- Contemporary demographic shifts in modern industrial societies have normalized divorce, non-marital cohabitation, and voluntary non-monogamy, cementing serial monogamy as the predominant marital pattern.

## connections

- [[human-evolution]] — evolutionary lineage during which extended juvenile dependency and high encephalization favored cooperative pair-bonding and paternal investment.
- [[culture]] — complex matrix of legal rules, religious sanctions, and societal expectations that prescribe and enforce exclusive partnerships.
- [[ritual]] — public ceremonial actions, such as nuptial rites and sworn pledges, that mark the formal transition into socially recognized monogamous unions.
- [[christianity]] — religious tradition that elevated lifelong, indissoluble monogamous marriage into a holy sacrament across European civilization.
- [[symbolism]] — material and linguistic emblems, such as wedding bands and bridal garb, that visually communicate marital exclusivity and social commitment.
