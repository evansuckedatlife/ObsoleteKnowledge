## summary

The **Incas** were the ethnic group and people who built the largest pre-Columbian empire in South America, dominating the Andes from roughly the 13th to 16th centuries. Initially an ethnic group centered in the Cusco region, the Incas expanded through military conquest and sophisticated administration to rule millions across territory stretching from modern Colombia to Argentina. The term now encompasses both the imperial period and the cultural legacy that survives today among descendants living in Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, and elsewhere in the region.

## you gotta know

- Originally a small ethnic group in the Cusco basin who came to dominate through military expansion under successive emperors.
- Built the *Tawantinsuyu* (the Four Parts Together), an empire divided into four quarters radiating from Cusco.
- Developed a *sophisticated bureaucracy* organized by decimal hierarchies (units of 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000) for administration and taxation.
- Constructed *Machu Picchu*, mountain sanctuaries, fortresses, and thousands of miles of roads without the wheel or written language.
- Used *quipus* (knotted cords) as a decimal counting and recording system, likely with additional mnemonic functions.
- Spoke *Quechua*, a language that became the lingua franca of the empire and survives in the Andes today.
- Organized society into *ayllus* (community units) and maintained labor tribute systems (*mit'a*) that spread obligations equally.
- Fell to Spanish conquistadors under *Francisco Pizarro* (1532–1572), but Incan resistance and cultural heritage endured.
- Developed *terrace farming* on steep mountain slopes, allowing food production at extreme altitudes and preventing soil erosion.
- The *Sapa Inca* (emperor) was viewed as a divine son of *Inti* (the sun god) and held absolute religious and political authority.
- Despite rapid conquest and collapse, Incan administrative and architectural innovations influenced Spanish colonial governance and remain visible today.
