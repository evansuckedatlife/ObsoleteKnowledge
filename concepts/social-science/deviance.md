## summary

**Deviance** is any behavior, belief, or physical characteristic that violates established social norms, cultural expectations, or formal codified laws within a given society and provokes sanctions or disapproval. Far from being an absolute or inherently pathological condition, it is culturally and temporally contextual, socially constructed through the reactions and definitions imposed by collective institutions and social observers. The sociological examination of norm violation is central to understanding social control, boundary maintenance, group cohesion, and the structural drivers of historical change.

## you gotta know

- Defined sociologically as a relative rather than absolute concept, meaning no act is inherently nonconforming until a specific [[culture]] identifies it as transgressing collective norms and boundaries.
- Theorized by foundational French sociologist *Émile Durkheim* as an inevitable, normal, and even functional component of all healthy societies that serves to clarify moral boundaries and reinforce collective solidarity.
- Explained by *Robert K. Merton* through strain theory, which posits that a structural gap between culturally prescribed goals and institutionalized means forces individuals into adaptation strategies like innovation, ritualism, retreatism, or rebellion.
- Conceptualized through labeling theory by *Howard Becker* and *Edwin Lemert*, who distinguished between initial primary norm-breaking and entrenched secondary nonconformity resulting from society applying stigmatizing tags.
- Explored by conflict theorists influenced by *[[karl-marx]]*, who argue that legal codes and cultural prohibitions are designed by ruling elites to criminalize the behaviors of subordinate classes and protect private property.
- Counterbalanced continuously by social control mechanisms ranging from informal socialization and family rituals to formal coercive enforcement by institutional [[authority]].

## connections

- [[conformity]] — the inverse behavioral pattern of yielding to collective norms and expectations against which nonconforming actions are measured.
- [[culture]] — the shared matrix of values, symbols, and customs that establishes the normative rules whose transgression constitutes norm violation.
- [[authority]] — the institutionalized power of the state and ruling bodies that formalizes and enforces legal penalties against transgressors.
- [[ritual]] — the standardized collective practices through which societies periodically affirm shared morals and stigmatize transgressive acts.
- [[karl-marx]] — the social philosopher whose critique of class stratification informed critical and conflict theories of criminalization.
- [[asch-conformity]] — the landmark social psychological experiments demonstrating how peer pressure forces individuals to suppress private judgment.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — the researcher whose experiments on obedience investigated how ordinary people balance moral convictions against institutional demands.
