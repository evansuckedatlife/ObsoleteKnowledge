## summary

**Learning** is the process by which individuals acquire knowledge, skills, and behavioral patterns through experience, instruction, and interaction with their environment. It is fundamental to cognitive development, allowing humans to adapt to new circumstances, build competence, and transmit culture across generations. Psychologists study learning through multiple frameworks—behavioral conditioning, cognitive development, and social learning—each revealing different mechanisms underlying how experience reshapes the mind.

## you gotta know

- *Conditioning* is the most straightforward learning mechanism: *classical conditioning* (pairing stimuli) and *operant conditioning* (linking actions to consequences) explain how organisms associate events and modify behavior accordingly.
- *B.F. Skinner* pioneered operant conditioning, demonstrating that behavior followed by positive consequences increases in frequency; applied to education, this gave rise to programmed instruction and behavioral modification techniques.
- *Cognitive learning* emphasizes internal mental processes: learners form mental models, recognize patterns, and solve problems through reasoning—not merely responding to environmental stimuli.
- *Social learning*, articulated by *Albert Bandura*, reveals that people learn by observing and imitating others; watching a model perform a behavior and experience consequences affects the observer's own behavior.
- Learning involves *transfer*: skills and knowledge acquired in one context apply to new situations; transfer can be *positive* (one skill aids another) or *negative* (one skill interferes with learning another).
- *Motivation* profoundly shapes learning; intrinsically motivated learners (pursuing knowledge for its own sake) often outperform extrinsically motivated learners (pursuing external rewards), though both mechanisms operate throughout life.
- *Metacognition*—thinking about one's own thinking and learning—enhances learning effectiveness; learners who monitor comprehension, adjust strategies when confused, and reflect on performance learn more durably than those who don't.
- Learning is *context-dependent*; knowledge and skills acquired in one setting may not transfer to different contexts without deliberate effort, a phenomenon called *transfer-appropriate processing*.
