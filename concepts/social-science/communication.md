## summary

**Communication** is the iterative social process through which individuals exchange information, construct shared meaning, and coordinate collective behavior through shared systems of signs, symbols, and semiotic codes. Grounded in both evolutionary biology and cultural transmission, it spans verbal utterances, textual inscriptions, and non-verbal kinesics. Across linguistics, anthropology, and cognitive sociology, it is studied as the foundational matrix enabling human cooperation, ideological reproduction, and the establishment of institutions.

## you gotta know

- Analyzed formally by *Claude Shannon* and *Warren Weaver* in their 1949 mathematical model, which conceptualized message transmission via a sender, encoder, channel, decoder, receiver, and interfering noise.
- Formulated as speech act theory by *J. L. Austin* and refined by *John Searle*, distinguishing locutionary acts, illocutionary forces, and perlocutionary consequences.
- Expanded in semiotics by *Roman Jakobson*, who identified six structural constituent factors paired with communicative functions: expressive, conative, referential, phatic, metalingual, and poetic.
- Elaborated as an interactional imperative by the *Palo Alto School* and *Paul Watzlawick*, who established the axiom that one cannot not communicate in social settings.
- Theorized as communicative action by *Jürgen Habermas*, who argued that rational discourse aims toward unforced mutual consensus distinct from instrumental manipulation.
- Investigated across cultures by *Edward T. Hall*, who differentiated between high-context communication, where contextual cues carry meaning, and low-context communication relying on explicit verbal statements.
- Operates on vocal channels structured by phonological syntax and non-verbal channels governed by proxemics, paralinguistics, facial affect displays, and bodily gestures.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — provides formal structural frameworks for analyzing the syntax, semantics, and morphology of verbal exchanges.
- [[sociolinguistics]] — examines how communicative styles correlate with social stratifications, dialects, and institutional environments.
- [[symbolism]] — supplies the cognitive and material signs through which abstract conceptual meanings are mediated.
- [[culture]] — shapes the shared interpretive codes, values, and contextual presuppositions necessary for comprehension.
- [[communitas]] — represents the profound relational solidarity achieved through shared ritual symbols and liminal encounter.
- [[compliance]] — reflects social interactions where verbal requests or structural demands successfully prompt behavioral assent.
- [[cognitive-science]] — studies internal mental representations, memory retrieval, and predictive inference during message decipherment.
