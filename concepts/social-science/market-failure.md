## summary

**Market failure** occurs when a free market—without government intervention—fails to allocate resources efficiently or fails to produce an outcome society deems just. Markets fail for multiple structural reasons: monopoly power, information asymmetries, externalities, and public goods that cannot be privatized. Market failure is central to economics because it justifies regulation and reveals the limits of the *invisible hand* metaphor. Understanding when and why markets fail is essential to policy design.

## you gotta know

- *Deadweight loss*—the economic waste created when a market does not reach equilibrium—is the hallmark of market failure; it represents real value that could exist but doesn't due to market dysfunction.
- *Monopoly* represents market failure through concentrated power; a single seller can restrict output and raise prices above the competitive level, harming consumers and reducing overall surplus.
- *Externalities* (pollution, education benefits) impose costs or benefits on parties outside the transaction, causing prices and quantities to diverge from socially optimal levels.
- *Information asymmetry*—one party knowing significantly more than the other—can prevent honest trade; sellers of used cars or insurance have vastly more information than buyers, creating incentive for fraud.
- *Public goods* (national defense, clean air, basic research) are non-excludable (can't prevent non-payers from using) and non-rivalrous (one person's use doesn't diminish another's), making markets unable to price them properly.
- The *invisible hand* assumes perfect competition and rational actors; market failures arise when these assumptions break down: monopolistic competition, irrational behavior, transaction costs, and information gaps.
- Market failures justify government intervention through regulation, taxation, subsidies, and public provision of goods; the debate over which failures warrant intervention shapes all economic policy.

## connections

- [[deadweight-loss]] — the quantifiable welfare loss resulting from market failure.
- [[invisible-hand]] — Adam Smith's metaphor for efficient market allocation; market failures are places it breaks down.
- [[market-mechanism]] — the pricing system through which markets coordinate supply and demand; failure reveals its limits.
- [[monopoly]] — a market structure that prevents competition and represents a classic source of market failure.
- [[externalities]] — unpriced costs and benefits that cause markets to misallocate resources.
- [[information-asymmetry]] — imperfect information that prevents markets from functioning efficiently.
