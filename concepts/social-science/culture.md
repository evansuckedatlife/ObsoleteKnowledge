## summary

**Culture** encompasses the shared beliefs, values, practices, artifacts, and meanings that a group of people collectively create and transmit across generations. It is the lens through which societies make sense of the world, organize themselves, and pass down knowledge and identity.

## you gotta know

- Culture includes both *material* elements (tools, buildings, food) and *non-material* elements (beliefs, language, customs, laws).
- Language and culture are deeply intertwined — the structure of a language shapes how speakers categorize and perceive reality, as argued in the hypothesis of [[linguistic-relativity]]; speakers of languages with many colour terms perceive colour gradations differently.
- Culture is *learned* through socialization, not innate — humans are born with capacity for culture but must acquire its contents from their community; children raised in isolation show deficits in language and cultural knowledge.
- [[ritual]] is a key mechanism through which cultures encode and transmit meaning, reinforcing group identity and social bonds; rites of passage, ceremonies, and daily rituals mark cultural membership.
- Every culture contains *subcultures* — smaller groups within the larger society with distinct norms, values, and practices (youth cultures, professional communities, ethnic enclaves); conflicts between subcultures often generate cultural change.
- Anthropologist [[franz-boas]] pioneered *cultural relativism* — the principle that cultures must be understood on their own terms rather than judged by the standards of the observer's culture; this revolutionized anthropology by rejecting evolutionary hierarchies of culture.
- Culture is *adaptive* — it evolves to solve practical problems of survival and coordination, but also carries historical contingencies and may persist even when no longer functional; cultural lag occurs when material conditions change faster than beliefs adapt.

## connections

- [[linguistic-relativity]] — the hypothesis that language structure shapes thought and perception within a culture.
- [[ritual]] — ceremonial practices that anchor cultural meaning and reproduce social order.
- [[anthropology]] — the discipline that studies culture systematically across human societies.
- [[franz-boas]] — founder of American anthropology; established cultural relativism as a core principle.
- [[identity]] — individual and group identity are forged through cultural membership and transmission.
- [[values]] — the ethical and moral priorities embedded in a culture's worldview.
- [[social-norms]] — the shared expectations and rules that culture enforces.
