## summary

**Economics** is the social science that investigates the production, distribution, and consumption of goods, services, and material wealth, examining how individuals, institutions, and sovereign nations allocate scarce resources under competing desires. Formalized during the Scottish [[enlightenment]] and profoundly transformed by the [[industrial-revolution]], the discipline evolved from the broader moral philosophy of political economy into an analytical science modeling market behavior, capital accumulation, and price formation. Understanding its development illuminates central historical debates over state authority, trade protectionism, class conflict, and macroeconomic stabilization that continue to govern modern societies.

## you gotta know

- Founded in its classical systematic form by *[[adam-smith]]*, whose 1776 treatise *The Wealth of Nations* argued that market coordination through individual self-interest, the division of labor, and the *invisible hand* generates widespread social wealth without requiring direct mercantilist regulation by the state.
- Analyzes the allocation of resources through models of [[supply-and-demand]], marginal utility, and price equilibrium, formalizing how competitive markets communicate relative scarcity and coordinate decentralized consumer preferences with firm production.
- Split into distinct microeconomic and macroeconomic branches following *[[john-maynard-keynes]]*, whose 1936 work *The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money* proved that market economies can remain trapped in prolonged depressions due to insufficient aggregate demand, necessitating state fiscal intervention.
- Subjected to foundational critique by *[[karl-marx]]* in *Das Kapital*, which analyzed how industrial capitalism extracts surplus value from wage labor, predicting that inherent contradictions would drive falling rates of profit, worker alienation, and systemic crisis.
- Established the classical doctrine of free trade through *[[david-ricardo]]*, whose 1817 *Principles of Political Economy and Taxation* demonstrated the principle of comparative advantage, proving that international trade benefits both trading partners even if one holds an absolute productive superiority.
- Provided the primary theoretical foundation for the [[anti-corn-law-league]] in nineteenth-century Britain, where classical economic arguments mobilized urban industrialists and workers against agricultural landlords to repeal protective grain tariffs in 1846.
- Traced its earliest systematic model of macroeconomic circulation to the French Physiocrats led by *François Quesnay*, whose 1758 *Tableau Économique* portrayed national production as an interconnected circular flow of wealth between agricultural producers, landowners, and artisans.

## connections

- [[adam-smith]] — Scottish moral philosopher whose 1776 work inaugurated classical political economy by detailing how the division of labor and market incentives generate national wealth.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical transformation from agrarian handicraft to factory mechanized production that created the modern wage-labor system, urban factory economy, and capital markets.
- [[karl-marx]] — philosopher and political theorist who reinterpreted classical economics to demonstrate how capital accumulation depends upon the exploitation of surplus labor value.
- [[david-ricardo]] — classical theorist whose rigorous mathematical deductions established the foundational concepts of comparative advantage, economic rent, and the distribution of national income.
- [[john-maynard-keynes]] — twentieth-century British economist whose revolutionary macroeconomic theories justified active counter-cyclical government spending and monetary management to combat severe recessions.
- [[supply-and-demand]] — core analytical apparatus formalizing how the price and quantity of commodities are determined through the interaction of consumer utility and production costs.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual epoch that catalyzed the secular analysis of human society, replacing religious and traditional economic strictures with rationalist models of commerce and liberty.
