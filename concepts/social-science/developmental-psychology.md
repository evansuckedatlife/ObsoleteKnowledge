## summary

**Developmental psychology** is the study of how psychological processes and behaviour change across the lifespan, from infancy through old age. It examines how individuals grow in cognition, emotion, morality, and social skills, shaped by biology, experience, and culture.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a distinct field in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, building on [[sigmund-freud]]'s theories of psychosexual stages.
- [[jean-piaget]] revolutionized the field by proposing stages of *cognitive development* — sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational — each with distinct ways of thinking.
- [[erik-erikson]] expanded the scope beyond childhood, proposing eight *psychosocial stages* spanning the entire lifespan, from infancy to old age.
- Developmental psychologists study *critical periods* — windows of time when exposure to certain experiences is especially formative for long-term development; missing input during critical periods (e.g., language exposure, visual stimulation) can cause irreversible deficits.
- The field asks core questions: Is development continuous or stage-based? How much is nature versus nurture? What role do relationships, trauma, and culture play?
- *Attachment theory* (especially John Bowlby) emphasizes the deep bond between infant and caregiver as foundational to healthy emotional and social development; secure attachment in infancy predicts better emotional regulation and social competence later.
- Modern developmental psychology integrates neuroscience, showing how brain development underlies psychological milestones; synaptic pruning, myelination, and prefrontal cortex development explain why adolescents are impulsive.
- The field encompasses prenatal development, infancy, childhood, adolescence, and adult development; some theories emphasize universals while others stress cultural variation in developmental pathways.
- Debate continues over plasticity — the degree to which early experiences create permanent limitations or the brain retains capacity for change across the lifespan.

## connections

- [[jean-piaget]] — pioneer of stages of cognitive development; fundamentally shaped the field.
- [[erik-erikson]] — extended developmental theory across the full lifespan with psychosocial stages.
- [[sigmund-freud]] — early theorist of psychosexual stages; influenced all later developmental frameworks.
- [[cognitive-development]] — the study of how reasoning, memory, and problem-solving capacities emerge and change.
- [[psychosocial-development]] — the emotional and social aspects of growing up; central focus of modern developmental work.
- [[learning]] — how individuals acquire new knowledge and skills across development.
- [[childhood]] — the formative period where most development research has concentrated.
