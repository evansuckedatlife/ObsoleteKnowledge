## summary

**Endangered languages** are languages with so few remaining speakers that they face extinction within one to three generations. As globalization, urbanization, and dominant-language education policies accelerate language shift, linguists estimate that roughly half of the world's approximately 7,000 languages will disappear by 2100. The loss of an endangered language erases not only a unique communication system but also irreplaceable knowledge systems, cultural practices, and worldviews embedded in that language.

## you gotta know

- Language extinction accelerates during periods of rapid cultural change; colonial conquest, industrialization, and now digital globalization each triggered mass language death.
- The number of speakers does not correlate exactly with extinction risk; factors include intergenerational transmission (whether children still learn the language at home), community prestige (whether speakers value their language), and access to education.
- Endangered languages are not uniformly distributed; indigenous and minority languages in the Americas, Africa, Asia-Pacific, and Eurasia bear the highest extinction burden—the Americas alone account for roughly 40% of global linguistic diversity.
- Documentation of endangered languages—audio recordings, grammatical descriptions, and digital archives—has become a critical intervention; linguists race to preserve linguistic data before speakers pass away.
- The extinction of a language often coincides with loss of ecological knowledge, oral history, ritual practice, and identity; the [[Basque]] and [[Maltese]] languages survived political suppression partly through deliberate cultural revival efforts.
- Language-death processes vary; some languages fade gradually as bilingual speakers shift to the dominant language; others vanish abruptly after traumatic events (colonization, genocide, forced assimilation).
- Endangered-language communities often experience structural inequalities: marginalized speakers lack economic incentive to transmit the language, while dominant-language schooling actively discourages it.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — the study of language structure and change, including language death mechanisms.
- [[franz-boas]] — pioneered documentation of indigenous American languages before they disappeared.
- [[noam-chomsky]] — universal grammar theory emerged partly through study of diverse language structures now at risk.
- [[language-isolates]] — many language isolates (like [[Basque]]) are endangered; their loss would eliminate unique structures from human linguistic diversity.
- [[linguistic-relativity]] — languages encode distinctive worldviews; extinction erases cognitive and cultural knowledge.
- [[silbo-gomero]] — a whistled language that narrowly escaped extinction through deliberate preservation.
- [[oral-tradition]] — endangered languages are the primary carriers of oral narratives and cultural memory in many communities.
