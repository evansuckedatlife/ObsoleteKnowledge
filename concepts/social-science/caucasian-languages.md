## summary

**Caucasian languages** is an areal designation comprising three indigenous, mutually unrelated language families native to the Caucasus mountain isthmus between the Black and Caspian Seas: *Kartvelian* (South Caucasian), *Northeast Caucasian* (Nakh-Dagestanian), and *Northwest Caucasian* (Abkhazo-Adyghe). Renowned in descriptive linguistics for their formidable phonetic inventories and complex morphosyntax, these idioms feature extraordinary consonant clusters, polypersonal verb agreement, and ergative-absolutive case systems. The region has been described since classical antiquity as a mountain of languages exhibiting some of the highest linguistic diversity on Earth.

## you gotta know

- Classifies structurally into three separate indigenous families with no demonstrated genealogical relationship to one another or to external language families.
- Features *Georgian*, the most widely spoken South Caucasian language, possessing a continuous literary tradition dating back to the fifth century accompanied by its own distinctive script, *Mkhedruli*.
- Known for exceptionally large consonant inventories, exemplified by *Ubykh* in the Northwest Caucasian family, which possessed dozens of distinct consonants alongside only two phonemic vowels before its extinction.
- Displays prevalent ergative-absolutive alignment, where the subject of an intransitive verb receives the same grammatical case marking as the direct object of a transitive verb.
- Exhibits polypersonalism and complex agglutination, in which verbs routinely incorporate prefixes and suffixes that simultaneously cross-reference subjects, direct objects, and indirect objects.
- Contains the *Nakh-Dagestanian* group, spoken in Dagestan, Chechnya, and Ingushetia, celebrated for intricate grammatical noun class systems and extensive locative case paradigms.
- Noted by the ancient geographer *Strabo* and Roman chronicler *Pliny the Elder*, who marveled at the scores of interpreters required to conduct trade in the market city of Dioscurias.
