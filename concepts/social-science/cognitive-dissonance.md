## summary

**Cognitive dissonance** is a foundational theory in social psychology describing the state of mental distress experienced by an individual who simultaneously holds contradictory beliefs, values, or ideas, or whose actions conflict with their convictions. First formulated by *Leon Festinger* in 1957 in the *[[united-states]]*, the framework posits that psychological inconsistency functions as an internal drive state analogous to hunger, compelling people to alter their attitudes, rationalize behaviors, or engage in selective exposure to restore internal equilibrium. It transformed the understanding of human motivation by demonstrating that actions frequently reshape thoughts rather than merely reflecting them, providing a vital explanatory model for self-justification, political polarization, and moral disengagement.

## you gotta know

- Formulated by *Leon Festinger* in his landmark 1957 monograph *A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance*, challenging the prevailing dogmas of *[[behaviorism]]*.
- Documented in the classic infiltration study *When Prophecy Fails* (1956), which observed a UFO doomsday cult led by *Dorothy Martin* (pseudonym *Marian Keech*) that rationalized a failed apocalypse by claiming their devotion had saved the earth.
- Demonstrated experimentally in the 1959 study by *Leon Festinger* and *J. Merrill Carlsmith*, where participants paid a meager one dollar to lie about a dull peg-turning task reported genuinely enjoying it, whereas those paid twenty dollars felt no need to change their attitudes due to sufficient external justification.
- Explains the effort justification effect demonstrated by *Elliot Aronson* and *Judson Mills* (1959), wherein severe hazing or grueling initiation rituals paradoxically increase an individual's subsequent valuation of group membership.
- Produces post-decision dissonance—frequently termed the spreading of alternatives—where individuals artificially elevate their chosen option and denigrate rejected alternatives following a difficult choice to eliminate regret.
- Functions as a powerful psychological catalyst for cruelty, allowing perpetrators to dehumanize victims or adopt victim-blaming rationalizations to reconcile violent deeds with a benevolent self-concept.
- Resolved psychologically through selective exposure, cognitive reframing, denial of responsibility, trivializing the conflict, or actively acquiring new consonant beliefs.

## connections

- [[conformity]] — social pressure often forces compliant actions that clash with private beliefs, generating tension that shifts personal convictions.
- [[asch-conformity]] — demonstrates how group consensus induces individuals to doubt sensory judgment, setting the stage for subsequent dissonance reduction.
- [[behaviorism]] — the mechanistic stimulus-response paradigm that dissonance theory successfully dismantled by showing internal cognitions drive attitude change.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — the famous obedience studies illustrated how subjects gradually rationalized their escalating infliction of electric shocks through step-by-step commitment.
- [[cognitive-science]] — adopted dissonance paradigms to explore neuroimaging correlates of error-detection and conflicting affective processing.
- [[cognitive-linguistics]] — studies how conceptual framing and linguistic metaphors are deployed to resolve or mask deep ideological contradictions.
