## summary

**Social influence** is the process by which individuals' behaviors, attitudes, or beliefs are shaped by the presence or actions of others. It encompasses conformity (adopting group norms), obedience (complying with authority), persuasion, and peer pressure. Social influence operates across all human societies and is fundamental to how groups coordinate behavior, maintain norms, and exercise power.

## you gotta know

- Social influence operates on a spectrum from subtle (seeing others laugh makes you more likely to laugh) to coercive (threats or force).
- Conformity and obedience are distinct: conformity aims to match peers, while obedience responds to authority figures—though both result in changed behavior.
- [[stanley-milgram]]'s experiments revealed that ordinary people will inflict apparent harm on others when instructed by an authority figure wearing a lab coat.
- [[solomon-asch]]'s line-matching experiments demonstrated that people will deny their own perceptions and match incorrect group answers, suggesting conformity pressure overrides individual judgment.
- Social influence can operate through internalization (genuinely believing the influenced view), identification (adopting beliefs to match a respected group), or mere compliance (obeying without agreeing).
- The presence of others alone modifies behavior—social facilitation theory shows that performing well-learned tasks improves around observers but learning new tasks worsens, a phenomenon observed across species.
- Social influence is stronger when the influencing group is cohesive, unanimous, and the individual fears rejection or values membership.
- Peer pressure intensifies during adolescence as social identity shifts from family to peer groups, making teenagers particularly susceptible to group influence.
- Informational influence (influence based on belief that others are correct) differs from normative influence (influence based on desire for social approval).
- Cultural contexts shape how influential social pressure is; individualist societies show somewhat less conformity than collectivist societies, though both demonstrate substantial social influence.

## connections

- [[conformity]] — matching group norms and behaviors, a primary form of social influence.
- [[obedience]] — responding to authority directives, distinguished from conformity.
- [[peer-pressure]] — social influence exerted by age-matched peers, particularly potent in adolescence.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — psychologist whose experiments revealed the power of obedience to authority.
- [[solomon-asch]] — psychologist whose conformity experiments demonstrated the power of group pressure.
- [[authority]] — the source of power in obedience; wielders of authority exert strong social influence.
- [[group-dynamics]] — the broader study of how groups function and influence their members.
