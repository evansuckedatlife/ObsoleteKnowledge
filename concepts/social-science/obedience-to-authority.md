# Obedience to Authority

## summary

**Obedience to authority** is the tendency of individuals to comply with orders and directives from those perceived as legitimate authority figures, even when those orders conflict with personal conscience or moral judgment. This phenomenon shapes nearly all human societies—enabling institutional function and coordination—but becomes dangerous when authority demands harm. Modern psychology has revealed how situational factors (distance from consequences, institutional legitimacy, incremental escalation) can override individual moral reasoning.

## you gotta know

- Stanley Milgram's famous 1960s experiments demonstrated that approximately 65% of ordinary American participants would administer what they believed were fatal electric shocks to innocent subjects, simply because an experimenter in a lab coat told them to continue.
- Obedience to authority differs fundamentally from conformity to peer groups; authority implies hierarchy and the right to command, whereas conformity is alignment with equals' behavior.
- Psychological distance powerfully mediates obedience; when the person giving orders is physically distant or when the harm to victims is hidden, obedience increases dramatically.
- Institutional legitimacy amplifies obedience; people obey more readily in formal settings (laboratories, military barracks, government offices) than in informal contexts, suggesting that *context itself* signals authority.
- Cultural variation exists: societies with high "power distance" (acceptance of unequal hierarchies) show higher baseline obedience than egalitarian cultures, though individual variation within cultures is enormous.
- Graduated commitment (starting with small requests and escalating gradually) can trap people into destructive obedience; once someone has obeyed the first order, subsequent larger requests feel like continuations rather than new choices.
- The willingness to harm under authority has roots in both universal aspects of human social organization and in historical atrocities (Nazi Germany, colonial genocides, modern war crimes) that reveal obedience's darkest potential.
- Resistance to unjust authority increases when people feel solidarity with others resisting, when authority's legitimacy is questioned, or when the moral costs of compliance become unavoidable and visible.

## connections

- [[authority]] — the structural concept that obedience presupposes; the recognized right to command.
- [[conformity]] — the related but distinct tendency to align with peer group norms rather than obey hierarchy.
- [[stanley-milgram]] — the psychologist whose experiments made obedience to authority a central concern of social psychology.
- [[social-psychology]] — the discipline studying obedience as a fundamental social phenomenon.
- [[hierarchy]] — the structural arrangement that authority relationships create.
- [[legitimacy]] — the source of authority's power to command obedience; authority loses force when questioned.
- [[compliance]] — the behavioral outcome of obedience; the act of following orders.
