## summary

**Elisha** was an ancient prophet of the northern Kingdom of Israel during the ninth century BCE, serving as the designated attendant and successor to the prophet *Elijah*. Active during the reigns of monarchs including *Jehoram*, *Jehu*, and *Jehoash*, he performed numerous miraculous acts demonstrating the power of Yahweh throughout the ancient [[middle-east]]. Within the narrative of the [[hebrew-bible]], his prophetic ministry reached its political climax when he orchestrated the overthrow of the royal house of *Ahab*, commissioning the anointing of *Jehu* to extirpate the royal family and dismantle state-supported *Baal* worship.

## you gotta know

- Succeeded the prophet *Elijah*, requesting and receiving a "double portion" of his master's spirit before *Elijah* ascended into heaven on a chariot of fire.
- Cleansed the Syrian military general *Naaman* of leprosy by directing him to immerse himself seven times in the *Jordan River*, later afflicting his own servant *Gehazi* with the disease for illicitly seeking material reward.
- Dispatched a disciple to anoint the army general *Jehu* as king of Israel, commissioning the bloody purge of the Omride dynasty and the violent demise of *Jezebel*.
- Cursed an unruly mob of youths from Bethel who jeered at his baldness, prompting two female bears to emerge from the forest and maul forty-two of them.
- Multiplied a destitute widow's small jar of olive oil into enough volume to pay off her creditors and prevent her sons from being sold into debt slavery.
- Raised the deceased son of a prominent woman from *Shunem* from the dead by stretching himself across the child's body until the boy sneezed seven times and revived.
- Caused an iron ax head to float to the water's surface after an impoverished prophet lost the borrowed tool in the *Jordan River*.
- Performed a posthumous miracle when a corpse thrown hastily into his tomb suddenly revived upon coming into physical contact with his bones.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — the scriptural canon whose Books of Kings record his prophetic deeds, political interventions, and miracles.
- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition that reveres his role as a miracle-working prophet defending monotheism against idolatry.
- [[temple-in-jerusalem]] — the central sanctuary of the southern Kingdom of Judah, operating concurrently with his northern ministry.
- [[middle-east]] — the historical and geographical landscape of ancient Syria and the Levant shaped by his political prophecies.
- [[david]] — the royal founder whose dynasty ruled the southern kingdom during his prophetic career in Israel.
- [[esther]] — a fellow scriptural figure celebrated for courageously preserving the covenant people in times of acute existential crisis.
- [[jesus]] — the New Testament figure who explicitly cited his cleansing of *Naaman* to illustrate prophetic rejection in one's hometown.
