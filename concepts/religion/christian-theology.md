# Christian Theology

## summary

**Christian Theology** is the systematic study and explication of *Christian* faith, doctrine, and practice, encompassing the nature of God, *Jesus* as *Messiah* and Son of God, salvation, the *Trinity*, and ethical living. Developed across centuries by Church fathers, medieval scholastics, and modern theologians drawing on Scripture, tradition, reason, and experience, Christian theology has produced diverse schools of thought—*Catholic*, *Orthodox*, *Protestant*, *Liberation* theology—while maintaining core doctrines of incarnation and redemption.

## you gotta know

- The *Trinity* (Father, Son, *Holy Spirit*) is a foundational doctrine asserting that God exists in three persons yet remains one substance; it emerged from early Christian reflection on God the Father, Jesus the Son, and the Holy Spirit's role.
- The *Incarnation* teaches that God became human in *Jesus* of *Nazareth*, uniting divine and human natures; it affirms both Jesus's full humanity and full divinity, a paradox debated across Christian history.
- *Salvation* is understood variously: as vicarious atonement through Christ's death (substitution), as moral exemplar (Abelard), as victory over sin and death (Christus Victor), or as deification/theosis (Eastern Orthodox); different traditions emphasize different mechanisms.
- *Aquinas* (13th century) synthesized *Aristotelian* philosophy with Christian doctrine, arguing for God's existence through rational proofs and developing natural law ethics; his *Summa Theologiae* remains authoritative in Catholicism.
- *Augustine* (4th–5th century) shaped theology profoundly: original sin, predestination, divine grace, the distinction between the *City of God* and the earthly city; his Neoplatonic framework influenced centuries of theology.
- The *Reformation* (16th century) challenged Medieval theology: *sola fide* (faith alone), *sola scriptura* (Scripture alone), and questioning the mediation of the institutional church reshaped Protestant theology radically.
- Modern theology grapples with historical criticism of Scripture, theodicy (the problem of evil), religious pluralism, and social justice; *Liberation Theology* emphasizes God's preferential option for the poor.
