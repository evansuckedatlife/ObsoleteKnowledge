## summary

**Anna**, universally celebrated in Christian biblical tradition as **Anna the Prophetess**, is a devout Jewish widow featured in the infancy narrative of the *Gospel of Luke*. Residing continually within the courts of the *Temple in Jerusalem* through decades of prayer, fasting, and ascetic fidelity, she recognized the infant *Jesus* as the promised Messiah during his ritual presentation. Along with the venerable *Simeon*, she functions as an inspired prophetic witness who publicly proclaimed the redemption of Israel to all those awaiting divine deliverance in Jerusalem.

## you gotta know

- Featured exclusively in the second chapter of the *Gospel of Luke* (Luke 2:36–38) during the presentation of the infant *Jesus* and the ritual purification of the *Virgin Mary* forty days after childbirth.
- Distinguished as one of the very few women in the New Testament designated with the title of prophetess (*prophetis*), identified specifically as the daughter of *Phanuel* of the northern tribe of *Asher*.
- Described as a woman of extreme old age who had lived with her husband for seven years from her virginity before enduring as a widow until the age of eighty-four (or alternatively, having been a widow for eighty-four years).
- Practiced an unremitting regime of temple piety, never leaving the sacred precincts of the *Temple in Jerusalem* and worshipping day and night through perpetual supplication and religious fasting.
- Approached the holy family at the exact moment *Simeon* delivered his inspired canticle (*Nunc Dimittis*), offering spontaneous thanksgiving to God and declaring the child's identity to the gathered assembly.
- Acted as an initial herald of the incarnation by speaking about *Jesus* to all worshippers in the holy city who were eagerly expecting the messianic redemption of *Jerusalem*.
- Balances the elderly *Simeon* within Luke's carefully balanced theological architecture, satisfying the Deuteronomic standard of two legal witnesses and demonstrating the evangelist's characteristic pattern of pairing male and female recipients of divine revelation.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the long-expected Messiah whom Anna recognized as an infant and proclaimed as the salvation of Israel.
- [[temple-in-jerusalem]] — the second temple complex that served as her spiritual home, monastic refuge, and the site of her messianic proclamation.
- [[gospels]] — the canonical New Testament books, specifically the Gospel of Luke, that preserve the memory of her prophetic witness during the nativity cycle.
- [[simeon]] — the righteous elder in Jerusalem whose song of praise coincided with Anna's thanksgiving during the Presentation in the Temple.
- [[virgin-mary]] — the mother who brought the newborn Christ child to the temple to fulfill the purification offerings prescribed by the Law of Moses.
- [[judaism]] — the scriptural faith whose messianic promises, tribal genealogies, and temple rituals framed Anna's lifelong ascetic devotion.
- [[david]] — the royal king whose ancestral house and promised throne were seen as realized in the child Anna glorified before the assembly.
