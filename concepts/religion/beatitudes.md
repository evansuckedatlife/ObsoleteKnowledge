## summary

The **Beatitudes** are a celebrated series of blessings delivered by *Jesus* of Nazareth, constituting the opening proclamation of the *Sermon on the Mount* in the Gospel of *Matthew*. Ranging over eight or nine aphorisms beginning with "Blessed are...", they articulate an inverted spiritual economy that exalts the humble, the merciful, and the persecuted over worldly triumph. Along with a fourfold parallel set in the *Sermon on the Plain* in the Gospel of *Luke*, they define Christian discipleship and express the interior values of the *Kingdom of God*.

## you gotta know

- Comprise the opening declarations of *Matthew* 5:3–12, commencing with the famous blessing: "Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven."
- Positioned as the theological gate to the three-chapter *Sermon on the Mount* (*Matthew* 5–7), preceding the similes of the "Salt of the Earth" and the "Light of the World."
- Paralleled in *Luke* 6:20–26 in the *Sermon on the Plain*, which presents four concise blessings immediately paired with four symmetrical "woes" directed against the wealthy, satisfied, and praised.
- The title derives from the Latin noun *beatitudo*, meaning supreme happiness or bliss, which renders the Greek term *makarios*.
- Prominent individual verses praise "the meek" (who "shall inherit the earth"), "they that mourn", "they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness", "the pure in heart", and "the peacemakers".
- Deeply indebted to prophetic and wisdom poetry in the *Hebrew Bible*, especially the messianic restoration announced in *Isaiah* 61 and the promise of land to the humble in *Psalm* 37.
- Historically served as the primary moral charter for Christian monastic asceticism, voluntary poverty, and modern philosophies of religious nonviolence.
- Conclude by blessing those who are reviled and persecuted for righteousness' sake, comparing their trials to the suffering endured by ancient prophets.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the central teacher whose opening moral manifesto in Galilee begins with these blessings.
- [[gospels]] — the scriptural texts of Matthew and Luke that preserve the differing traditions of the sermon.
- [[christianity]] — the global religion that regards the blessings as the charter of interior holiness and gospel discipleship.
- [[benedict-of-nursia]] — the monastic founder whose Rule incorporated the Beatitudes' praise of meekness, poverty, and peace into monastic life.
- [[hebrew-bible]] — the ancient scriptural source whose psalms and prophetic texts are quoted and reimagined in the blessings.
- [[catholic-church]] — the tradition that views the Beatitudes as an invitation to the evangelical counsels and the perfection of charity.
- [[peter]] — the foremost disciple who followed Jesus throughout Galilee and witnessed the delivery of these ethical teachings.
- [[paul]] — the early Christian apostle whose theological epistles repeatedly mirror the Beatitudes' focus on joy in suffering and spiritual meekness.
