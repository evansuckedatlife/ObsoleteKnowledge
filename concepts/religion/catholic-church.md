# Catholic Church

## summary

The **Catholic Church** (or *Roman Catholic Church*) is the largest *Christian* denomination, claiming over one billion members worldwide and tracing its authority to *Jesus* and the *Apostle Peter*. Centered in *Vatican City* under the leadership of the *Pope*, it has profoundly shaped *European* history, culture, and politics, surviving schisms (notably the *Protestant Reformation*), reformations, and modernization while maintaining continuity of doctrine and hierarchy across nearly 2,000 years.

## you gotta know

- The Church traces its authority back to *Jesus*'s designation of *Peter* as the "rock" upon which the Church would be built (*Matthew* 16:18); this became the basis for papal primacy and apostolic succession.
- The *Pope* is the head of the Church and the bishop of *Rome*; papal authority developed gradually over centuries, becoming nearly absolute by the *Middle Ages*, though Vatican II (1962–65) introduced more collegial governance.
- The Church developed a hierarchical structure: *Pope* → *Cardinals* → *Bishops* → *Priests* → laypeople; its sacramental theology (seven sacraments—baptism, confirmation, eucharist, penance, extreme unction, holy orders, matrimony) distinguishes it from *Protestant* denominations.
- *Medieval Catholicism* wielded enormous political and economic power, controlling vast lands, taxing populations, and influencing kingship; the *Inquisition* (medieval and Spanish) enforced doctrinal orthodoxy through violence.
- The *Protestant Reformation* (16th century) challenged papal authority and Catholic doctrine, leading to the *Counter-Reformation* (16th–17th centuries), which reformed abuses while reasserting Catholic theology and hierarchical structure.
- The Church produced vast theological literature (*Thomas Aquinas*, *Augustine*, *Dante*), artistic treasures (*Sistine Chapel*, *Gothic cathedrals*), and philosophical traditions; it sponsored science and scholarship alongside periods of obscurantism.
- Modern Catholicism has grappled with secularization, women's roles, clerical celibacy, birth control, LGBTQ+ issues, and scandals (abuse crises); *Vatican II* modernized the Church's approach to the secular world and other religions.
