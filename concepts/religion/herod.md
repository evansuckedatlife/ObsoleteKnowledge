## summary

**Herod the Great** (73–4 BCE) was the Roman-backed king of Judea who ruled during the time of Jesus's birth and early ministry. Though effective at maintaining peace and order, Herod is remembered in Christian tradition primarily through the Gospel account of the *Massacre of the Innocents*—his alleged order to kill all male infants in Bethlehem to prevent the birth of a rival king prophesied by magi. Herod was also a prolific builder, most famously reconstructing the Jewish Temple in Jerusalem as one of antiquity's greatest architectural monuments.

## you gotta know

- Herod was not ethnically Jewish but an Idumean (Edomite) whose family converted to Judaism; he gained power through Roman military support during the civil wars of the late republic.
- He ruled for 34 years (37–4 BCE) as a client king under Rome, maintaining relative peace and prosperity through ruthless suppression of rebellion and internal political intrigue.
- Herod undertook massive building projects including the reconstruction of the *Temple in Jerusalem*, making it one of the ancient world's architectural marvels.
- In the Gospel of Matthew, Herod orders the massacre of male infants in Bethlehem after magi inform him of the birth of a "king of the Jews," triggering Jesus's family's flight to Egypt.
- He was known for paranoia and cruelty, executing his own sons and wife on suspicion of disloyalty, contributing to the negative portrait in Christian tradition.
- After Herod's death, his kingdom was divided among his sons; Judea proper came under direct Roman procuratorial rule, including that of *Pontius Pilate*.
