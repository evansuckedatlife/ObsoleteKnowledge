## summary

**Brahman** is the ultimate, transcendent reality at the heart of Hindu metaphysics—the infinite, formless, eternal principle that underlies and permeates all existence. Unlike *Brahma* (the creator deity), Brahman is the impersonal, non-dual cosmic principle from which all being emerges and to which all consciousness returns. The realization of Brahman and its identity with one's own innermost self (*atman*) is the ultimate spiritual goal in Hinduism.

## you gotta know

- Brahman is described in the *Upanishads* as *sat-chit-ananda* (being, consciousness, bliss)—the unchanging essence underlying the changing phenomenal world.
- The concept of Brahman represents non-duality (*advaita*)—the philosophical position that apparent multiplicity masks an underlying unified reality.
- Brahman cannot be directly described or fully comprehended by language or thought; it can only be realized through direct mystical experience (*moksha*).
- The famous Upanishadic teaching "*tat tvam asi*" ("thou art that") asserts the identity between Brahman and *atman*, the individual soul.
- Brahman is beyond all qualities, attributes, and gender; the personal gods of Hinduism (*Brahma*, *Vishnu*, *Shiva*) are understood as manifestations or aspects of Brahman.
- Liberation (*moksha*) in Hindu philosophy consists of realizing one's fundamental identity with Brahman and escaping the cycle of rebirth.
- Different Hindu philosophical schools (*darshanas*) interpret Brahman's relationship to the phenomenal world differently, with some emphasizing pure transcendence and others emphasizing divine immanence.

## connections

- [[hinduism]] — the religious tradition centered on Brahman as ultimate reality.
- [[vedas]] — the sacred texts that articulate and contemplate Brahman.
- [[upanishads]] — the philosophical portions of the Vedas where Brahman is explored systematically.
- [[atman]] — the individual soul understood to be fundamentally identical with Brahman.
- [[brahma]] — the creator deity, distinct from Brahman the impersonal principle.
- [[karma-and-dharma]] — the cosmic law by which individual beings relate to Brahman.
- [[moksha]] — liberation through realization of one's identity with Brahman.
