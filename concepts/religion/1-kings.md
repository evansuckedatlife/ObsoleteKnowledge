## summary

**1 Kings** is a historical book of the *[[hebrew-bible]]* and the Christian Old Testament that chronicles the transition of the Israelite monarchy from the golden age of the United Monarchy to its disastrous schism. Written in ancient Israel and Judea, the text traces the final days of *[[david]]*, the reign and architectural zenith of *[[solomon]]*, and the subsequent division into the northern kingdom of Israel and southern kingdom of Judah. It is essential reading for understanding Deuteronomistic history, theological critiques of political power, and the emergence of classical prophetic figures confronting royal idolatry.

## you gotta know

- Begins with the succession crisis of King *[[david]]*, where *Bathsheba* and the prophet *Nathan* secure the throne for *[[solomon]]* over his elder brother *Adonijah*.
- Recounts the famous wisdom of *[[solomon]]*, exemplified by the narrative of two women claiming the same living baby, prompting him to suggest cutting the infant in half.
- Details the construction and dedication of the first *[[temple-in-jerusalem]]* on Mount Moriah, built with cedar supplied by King *Hiram* of Tyre.
- Describes the visit of the Queen of *Sheba*, who tests the wisdom and wealth of *[[solomon]]* with hard questions and lavish gifts of gold and spices.
- Details the catastrophic schism under *Rehoboam*, whose harsh tax policies cause the ten northern tribes to rebel under *Jeroboam*, forming the separate northern kingdom.
- Introduces the notorious northern king *[[ahab]]* and his Phoenician queen *Jezebel*, whose state-sponsored worship of *Baal* provokes divine judgment and drought.
- Features the miraculous ministry of the prophet *[[elijah]]*, highlighted by his contest on Mount Carmel against the 450 prophets of *Baal* and hearing God in a gentle whisper.
- Concludes with the judicial murder of *Naboth* engineered by *Jezebel* to seize his vineyard, followed by the death of *[[ahab]]* during battle at Ramoth-Gilead.

## connections

- [[ahab]] — ruler of the northern kingdom whose reign forms the dramatic centerpiece of the second half of the book.
- [[elijah]] — wandering miracle worker who confronts royal apostasy throughout the Omride dynasty.
- [[solomon]] — builder of the royal sanctuary whose rise and moral compromise define the early chapters.
- [[david]] — foundational king whose deathbed instructions initiate the book's political intrigues.
- [[temple-in-jerusalem]] — monumental architectural project dedicated with extensive prayers and sacrifices in chapter eight.
- [[hebrew-bible]] — canonical scripture preserving the Deuteronomistic account of kingship and prophecy.
- [[adultery]] — transgression in ancestral royal narratives that echoes in theological warnings against spiritual infidelity.
