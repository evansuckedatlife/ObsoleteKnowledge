## summary

**Adultery** is the voluntary sexual relationship between a married individual and someone other than their legal spouse, recognized across major religious legal systems as a grave moral and covenantal transgression. Explicitly prohibited in ancient Near Eastern law and codified within the foundational Decalogue of the *[[torah]]*, it carried severe communal penalties in ancient societies. Across Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions, the concept serves both as a concrete legal violation of marriage and as a pervasive theological metaphor for spiritual betrayal, unfaithfulness to God, and idolatry.

## you gotta know

- Explicitly forbidden by the Seventh Commandment of the Decalogue delivered to *[[moses]]* on Mount Sinai in Exodus and Deuteronomy.
- Formally punishable by death by stoning for both offending parties under classical biblical law as laid out in the legal codes of Leviticus.
- Illustrated in the foundational narrative of King *[[david]]*, who engaged in an illicit union with *Bathsheba* and arranged the murder of her husband *Uriah* the Hittite.
- Rebuked by the prophet *Nathan* through the parable of the rich man who stole the poor traveler's solitary ewe lamb, bringing dynastic strife upon the House of David.
- Expanded in the New Testament by *[[jesus]]* during the Sermon on the Mount, who taught that lustful intent in the heart equates to internal commission of the act.
- Featured in the Gospel of John narrative of the woman caught in the act, where *[[jesus]]* silences accusers with the challenge, "Let him who is without sin cast the first stone."
- Employed extensively by prophetic writers such as *Hosea* and *Jeremiah* as an extended metaphor for Israel's spiritual infidelity and worship of foreign deities.
- Governed in the *[[torah]]* by the ordeal of the bitter water (*Sotah*), a temple ritual designed to test a wife suspected by a jealous husband without corroborating witnesses.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — canonical scripture codifying both legal prohibitions and dramatic narrative accounts of royal transgressions.
- [[torah]] — legal foundation outlining the Decalogue, contractual marriage obligations, and judicial penalties.
- [[david]] — monarch whose infamous liaison with *Bathsheba* brought moral condemnation and persistent domestic tragedy upon his house.
- [[solomon]] — royal son of the union between *[[david]]* and *Bathsheba*, who succeeded to the throne after the succession crisis.
- [[1-kings]] — historical book opening with the old age of *[[david]]* and the royal lineage complicated by past domestic violations.
- [[jesus]] — central Christian figure who deepened the moral prohibition to internal thoughts and extended radical mercy to offenders.
- [[catholic-church]] — historical institution upholding the indissolubility of marriage and classifying extra-marital infidelity as mortal sin.
