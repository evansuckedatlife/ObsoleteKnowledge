## summary

**Christian ethics** is the normative theological framework that defines moral duty, virtuous character, and right conduct based on the teachings, life, and revelation of *[[jesus]]*. Centered upon the dual commands of supreme love for God and sacrificial love for one's neighbor, it reinterprets ancient legal codes into internal dispositions of mercy, humility, and justice. Broadly developed across church history by thinkers such as Augustine, Thomas Aquinas, and later reformers, it remains a central pillar of Western moral philosophy and theology.

## you gotta know

- Grounded primarily in the Sermon on the Mount found in the *[[gospels]]*, where *[[jesus]]* delivers the Beatitudes and internalizes moral law beyond mere outward ritual observance.
- Crystallized in the formulation of the Golden Rule, commanding believers to treat others precisely as they would wish to be treated.
- Defined by the Great Commandment, which unites Deuteronomy's call to love God wholeheartedly with Leviticus's injunction to love one's neighbor as oneself.
- Expanded in the epistles of *[[paul]]*, which emphasize faith working through love, spiritual fruits over legalistic works, and the radical unity of believers in Christ.
- Systematized during the Middle Ages into the three theological virtues of faith, hope, and charity alongside the classical cardinal virtues.
- Articulated in social reform movements that leveraged gospel principles to advocate for human dignity, abolitionism, and modern civil rights.
- Debated across historical branches between Catholic natural law traditions, virtue ethics, and Reformation doctrines regarding divine command and grace.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the central figure whose ministry, moral parables, and sacrificial death establish the ethical norm.
- [[christianity]] — the global faith tradition animated by these ethical and theological principles.
- [[gospels]] — the canonical narrative accounts preserving the core ethical sermons and dialogues of Christ.
- [[paul]] — the early apostle whose letters applied Christian moral precepts to communal Greco-Roman life.
- [[peter]] — the leading disciple who exhorted the early church toward holy conduct amidst persecution.
- [[catholic-church]] — the global institution that developed systematic moral theology and natural law traditions.
- [[protestant-reformation]] — the theological movement that re-examined ethical merit, faith, and vocational duty.
