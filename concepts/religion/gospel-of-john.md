## summary

The **Gospel of John** (or *Fourth Gospel*) is the final of the four canonical *Gospels* in the *New Testament*, traditionally attributed to *John the Apostle*. Written likely in the late 1st century, it differs dramatically from the synoptic *Gospels* (*Matthew*, *Mark*, *Luke*) in theology, narrative structure, and emphasis, presenting *Jesus* as the divine Logos (*Word*) through lengthy philosophical discourses, mystical imagery, and symbolic miracles. It alone records *Lazarus's* resurrection and *Mary Magdalene's* intimate encounter with the risen *Jesus*, making it uniquely influential in Christian theology and art.

## you gotta know

- Written in Greek, likely 90–110 CE, decades after *Jesus's* death; scholars debate whether *John the Apostle* authored it or whether it reflects his tradition filtered through later theological reflection.
- The opening declares *Jesus* as the divine *Logos* ("In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God"), establishing his pre-incarnate divinity from the outset.
- Emphasizes mysticism and symbolic theology more than the synoptic Gospels: *I am* statements (*"I am the bread of life," "I am the light of the world," "I am the resurrection and the life"*) reveal *Jesus's* divine identity.
- Contains no nativity narrative, genealogy, or temptation in the wilderness; instead opens with *John the Baptist's* testimony and focuses on extended scenes and discourses rather than rapid-fire parables.
- Records seven symbolic "signs" (miracles): water turned to wine at Cana, healing the royal official's son, the pool of Bethesda healing, feeding the five thousand, walking on water, healing the blind man, and—most dramatically—raising *Lazarus* from the dead after four days.
- The only Gospel that details *Lazarus's* resurrection, the subsequent conspiracy against *Lazarus*, and the anointing of *Jesus's* feet by *Mary* (possibly *Mary Magdalene*) shortly before his crucifixion.
- Records *Jesus's* extended farewell discourse to the *apostles* and the *High Priestly Prayer*, emphasizing eternal life and unity with God; develops the concept of the *Paraclete* (Holy Spirit or Comforter).
- Uniquely depicts *Mary Magdalene* as the first witness to the risen *Jesus*, a profound theologically significant role that elevated women's spiritual authority in early Christianity.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the divine subject of John's distinctive Christology and "I am" theology.
- [[john-the-apostle]] — traditionally attributed as the author, the beloved disciple entrusted with *Mary*.
- [[gospels]] — the canonical four; John stands apart in theology and narrative approach.
- [[lazarus]] — whose miraculous resurrection only John records in dramatic detail.
- [[mary-magdalene]] — who encounters the risen *Jesus* alone in John's account, granted the first resurrection appearance.
- [[peter]] — featured prominently in John, particularly in the post-resurrection scenes and the call to shepherd.
- [[nicodemus]] — a Pharisee whose night conversation with *Jesus* about being "born again" only John records.
- [[new-testament]] — the section of the Bible containing John and the other Gospels.
