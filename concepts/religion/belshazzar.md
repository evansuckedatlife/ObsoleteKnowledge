## summary

**Belshazzar** was the coregent and crown prince of *Babylon* during the sixth century BCE, famously portrayed in biblical tradition as the final sovereign of the Neo-Babylonian Empire. In the Book of *Daniel*, he convenes an extravagant feast for a thousand lords, desecrating the consecrated golden vessels stolen from the *Temple in Jerusalem*. This imperial blasphemy provokes the appearance of a disembodied hand that writes a mysterious judgment upon the palace wall, signaling his sudden death and the overthrow of his realm.

## you gotta know

- Featured prominently in the fifth chapter of the Book of *Daniel*, which describes his lavish royal banquet wherein he and his courtiers drank wine from the sacred vessels of the *Temple in Jerusalem*.
- Mid-feast, the fingers of a mysterious supernatural hand manifested and inscribed a cryptic four-word message upon the white plaster of his royal palace wall.
- Terrified by the apparition, the king summoned the prophet *Daniel* after the court magicians, Chaldeans, and astrologers failed to read or interpret the inscription.
- *Daniel* read the words *Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin*, explaining: *Mene* ("God has numbered your kingdom and finished it"), *Tekel* ("You are weighed in the balances and found wanting"), and *Peres* ("Your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians").
- The biblical narrative concludes with his death that very night, when *Babylon* was captured and dominion passed to *Darius the Mede* and *Cyrus the Great*.
- In historical archaeology, cuneiform inscriptions such as the *Nabonidus Cylinder* confirmed his historical reality as the son and coregent of King *Nabonidus*, who entrusted him with the defense of the capital while dwelling at the oasis of *Tayma*.
- The dramatic feast inspired major artistic masterpieces, notably *Rembrandt*'s canvas depicting the radiant Hebrew script and *William Walton*'s twentieth-century choral oratorio.
- The episode bequeathed famous idioms to the English language, including "the writing on the wall" for impending doom and "weighed in the balance and found wanting."

- [[middle-east]] — the ancient Near Eastern cradle where the transition from Babylonian to Persian hegemony took place.
- [[david]] — the ancestral monarch of the kingdom of Judah, which had fallen under the yoke of Babylon's captivity.
- [[moses]] — the prophet whose divinely revealed law strictly forbade idolatrous desecration and arrogance against God.
