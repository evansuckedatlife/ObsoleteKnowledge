## summary

**Deborah** was a celebrated prophetess, judge (*shofet*), and poet of ancient Israel whose heroic leadership is recorded in the *[[hebrew-bible]]*. Holding court beneath an eponymous palm tree in the tribal territory of Ephraim, she dispensed civil justice and inspired the Israelite tribes to unite against their Canaanite oppressors. Her strategic partnership with the general *Barak* and the decisive victory celebrated in the archaic *Song of Deborah* ended years of subjugation and secured forty years of peace for the nation.

## you gotta know

- Served as the fourth judge of pre-monarchic Israel during the era described in the biblical Book of Judges, holding court beneath the "Palm of Deborah" between Ramah and Bethel.
- Summoned the hesitant commander *Barak* to muster an army of ten thousand men from the tribes of Naphtali and Zebulun at Mount Tabor, rebuking his reluctance by prophesying that the honor of defeating the enemy general would belong to a woman.
- Directed the military campaign against King *Jabin* of Hazor and his commander *Sisera*, whose terrifying vanguard of nine hundred iron chariots became immobilized when torrential rains turned the Kishon River valley into mud.
- Her prophecy of a woman's triumph was dramatically fulfilled when *Jael*, the wife of *Heber the Kenite*, offered hospitality to the fleeing *Sisera*, gave him curdled milk, and drove a wooden tent peg through his temple while he slept.
- Regarded as the composer of the *Song of Deborah* in Judges 5, an ancient victory ode widely considered by biblical scholars to be one of the oldest surviving continuous poetic texts in Hebrew literature, dating to the twelfth or eleventh century BCE.
- Identified in scripture as an *eshet lappidot* (traditionally translated as "wife of Lappidoth" or "woman of torches and fiery spirit") and celebrated with the maternal honorific title "a mother in Israel."
- The poetic victory song explicitly condemns the passive tribes of Reuben, Gilead, Dan, and Asher for failing to join the battle, while heaping praise upon the valiant warriors of Zebulun and Naphtali.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — the primary scriptural text preserving her narrative across the prose history of Judges 4 and the poetic victory hymn of Judges 5.
- [[judaism]] — the ancient religious tradition that venerates her as one of seven canonical female prophets recognized in the Talmud.
- [[torah]] — the foundational Mosaic law according to which Deborah judged legal disputes and called the tribes to righteous obedience.
- [[covenant]] — the sacred treaty between Yahweh and Israel that had been neglected through idolatry, requiring Deborah's prophetic intervention to restore divine favor.
- [[moses]] — the founding prophet and lawgiver whose sister *Miriam* provided the earlier biblical archetype of a musical prophetess leading martial celebration.
- [[david]] — the royal psalmist and warrior-king whose subsequent poetic laments and military campaigns echoed the heroic cadence of Deborah's song.
- [[middle-east]] — the ancient Levant and Canaanite geographical setting in which the Jezreel Valley and Mount Tabor battle took place.
