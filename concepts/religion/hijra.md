## summary

The **Hijra** (also spelled **Hegira**) was Prophet *Muhammad's* migration from *Mecca* to *Medina* in 622 CE, fleeing persecution and establishing the first Islamic community. This event marks the founding moment of Islam as a functioning society and the beginning of the Islamic calendar, making it central to Muslim identity and historical consciousness.

## you gotta know

- Driven by increasing hostility toward *Muhammad* and his followers in *Mecca*, controlled by the polytheistic *Quraysh* tribe, who viewed his monotheistic message as a threat to their religious authority and mercantile interests.
- *Muhammad* and approximately 150 followers (sources vary between 70 and 200) traveled roughly 300 kilometers north to *Medina* over several weeks, with some departing before *Muhammad* to scout the route and ensure safety.
- *Muhammad* was invited to *Medina* as an arbitrator between the feuding Arab tribes of *Aus* and *Khazraj*, both of whom hoped his reputation for justice would resolve their conflicts and bring stability.
- Established the first *umma* (Islamic community), a religiously unified body distinct from tribal affiliations, transforming Islam from a persecuted sect into a functioning state with written laws (the *Constitution of Medina*), defense mechanisms, and governance structures.
- Immediately upon arrival, *Muhammad* established the *Quba* mosque as a refuge and community center, later building the *Masjid al-Nabawi* (Prophet's Mosque), which became the administrative and spiritual heart of the new community.
- The Islamic calendar (Hijri calendar) begins from this date, making Hijra year 1 AH (Anno Hegirae) equivalent to 622 CE; Muslim dates are typically written as AH (After Hijra), fundamentally marking time from this moment of Islamic transformation.
- In *Medina*, *Muhammad* consolidated political and religious authority over roughly a decade, negotiating treaties with non-Muslim tribes, conducting military campaigns against Meccan opposition (including the Battle of Badr in 624 CE), and eventually conquering *Mecca* in 630 CE without significant bloodshed.
- Marked a turning point: the *Meccan period* (roughly 613–622 CE, 13 years of preaching) emphasized spiritual revelation and ethical teaching to a small, persecuted group; the *Medinan period* (622–632 CE, 10 years) focused on state-building, military expansion, legal codification, and establishing Islam as a political and social force.
