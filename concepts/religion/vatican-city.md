## summary

**Vatican City** is the world's smallest independent state and the spiritual and administrative center of the Roman Catholic Church. Located entirely within the Italian city of Rome, this 0.44-square-kilometer city-state is bounded by the Leonine Walls and is the residence of the Pope, head of the Catholic Church. Vatican City's sovereignty, recognized internationally since the Lateran Treaty of 1929, allows the Pope to exercise both spiritual authority over the global Catholic community and temporal rule over a distinct territorial entity.

## you gotta know

- Vatican City became a sovereign city-state in 1929 through the Lateran Treaty signed between the Holy See and Mussolini's fascist Italy, resolving decades of papal-Italian tension since Italian unification in 1870.
- The Pope is the absolute sovereign of Vatican City, exercising executive, legislative, and judicial authority, though he typically delegates administrative functions to a governing body.
- *St. Peter's Basilica*, the largest church in Christendom and the principal church of the Catholic Church, stands in Vatican City and is one of the Renaissance's greatest architectural achievements.
- Vatican City is home to the Roman Curia—the administrative and judicial apparatus of the Catholic Church—and houses numerous offices, departments, and councils that govern Church affairs worldwide.
- The Vatican Archives and Library contain centuries of historical documents, manuscripts, and records, making the Vatican a major repository of Western religious and political history.
- Vatican City maintains a small Swiss Guard, a corps of Swiss mercenary soldiers that has protected the Pope since the 16th century, distinctive for their colorful Renaissance-era uniforms.
- The Vatican issues its own stamps, coins, and maintains a radio station; it is the only state with Latin as an official language, preserving Latin liturgy and scholarly tradition.
- As an independent nation-state at the United Nations, Vatican City has permanent observer status, allowing it to participate in international diplomacy and advocate for Catholic social teaching.
