## summary

A **Caliphate** (Arabic: **Khilafah**) was the foundational political and religious institution established to govern the global Muslim community (*umma*) following the death of the Prophet [[muhammad]] in 632 CE. Exercising authority through a supreme leader styled as a successor (*caliph*), the system fused spiritual guardianship of Islamic law (*sharia*) with executive and military command across vast Afro-Eurasian territories. Spanning pivotal imperial dynasties including the *Rashidun*, the *Umayyad*, and the *Abbasid* eras, the institution fostered administrative continuity with the classical world, sparked the intellectual achievements of the [[islamic-golden-age]], and endured through multiple transformations until its modern abolition under the [[ottoman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Established immediately following the passing of the Prophet [[muhammad]] in 632 CE, beginning with the *Rashidun* ("Rightly Guided") era composed of *Abu Bakr*, *Umar*, *Uthman*, and *Ali*, who oversaw rapid territorial expansion beyond the Arabian Peninsula.
- The foundational schism between [[sunni-and-shia]] denominations crystallized around succession disputes to this office, opposing those who endorsed consensus-based community election against partisans advocating hereditary appointment through the family of *Ali*.
- The Damascus-based *Umayyad* dynasty instituted hereditary imperial rule, established standard Arabic coinage, and directed an expansion that stretched from Central Asia across North Africa into [[spain]].
- The *Abbasid* revolution overthrew the Umayyads in 750 CE, shifting the geographic center of power eastward to *Baghdad* and patronizing the monumental philosophical, scientific, and literary developments of the [[islamic-golden-age]].
- A surviving Umayyad prince, *Abd al-Rahman I*, escaped dynastic slaughter to create an independent emirate in the Iberian Peninsula, which eventually elevated itself into the rival Western *Caliphate of Cordoba* to challenge Eastern prestige.
- The Isma'ili Shi'ite *Fatimid* dynasty founded *Cairo* in 969 CE and proclaimed their own counter-institution, rejecting Abbasid supremacy and sponsoring major cultural and educational institutions such as *Al-Azhar*.
- After the catastrophic Mongol destruction of Baghdad in 1258, the Mamluk Sultanate maintained a ceremonial "shadow caliphate" in Cairo to grant religious legitimacy to military sultans without granting actual executive governance.
- The authority was claimed by the sultans of the [[ottoman-empire]] after their 1517 conquest of Egypt, persisting until the Turkish Grand National Assembly under *Mustafa Kemal Atatürk* legally abolished the office on March 3, 1924, following [[world-war-i]].

## connections

- [[islam]] — the overarching world religion whose theology, ethics, and legal traditions provided the constitutional framework for governance.
- [[muhammad]] — the founder and prophet whose earthly leadership of the early community the institution was specifically created to succeed.
- [[quran]] — the primary sacred scripture that established core imperatives of communal consultation, justice, and moral accountability for rulers.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the Eurasian imperial dynasty that held the mantle for over four centuries before its twentieth-century secular dissolution.
- [[spain]] — the western frontier where an autonomous Umayyad dynasty flourished and maintained an influential rival seat of power at *Cordoba*.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical Mediterranean civilization whose provincial administrative apparatus, tax structures, and civic monuments deeply shaped early Umayyad imperial practice.
- [[world-war-i]] — the catastrophic modern conflict that caused the partition of the Ottoman state and catalyzed the ultimate demise of the office.
