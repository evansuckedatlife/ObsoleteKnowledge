## summary

**Aramaic** is an ancient Semitic language closely related to Hebrew that became the lingua franca of the Near East by the first millennium BCE. While Hebrew remained the sacred language of Jewish scripture and law, Aramaic gained increasing importance in the later biblical period and throughout the Jewish diaspora, particularly after the Babylonian exile. Portions of the Hebrew Bible are written in Aramaic, and the language remains liturgically significant in Jewish prayer (*Kaddish*) and continues to be spoken in small Christian communities.

## you gotta know

- Aramaic belongs to the Northwest Semitic language family alongside Hebrew, with whom it shares grammar and vocabulary roots.
- Following the Babylonian captivity in 586 BCE, Aramaic became the administrative and commercial language of the Persian Empire, eventually influencing Jewish linguistic practice.
- Sections of the books of *Daniel* and *Ezra* in the Hebrew Bible are composed in Aramaic, reflecting the postexilic context.
- The Kaddish, one of the most important prayers in Jewish liturgy, is predominantly composed in Aramaic rather than Hebrew, emphasizing its liturgical permanence.
- Jesus and his disciples spoke Aramaic as their primary language; several Aramaic phrases are preserved in the New Testament, including "*Abba*" (father) and "*Talitha koum*" (rise up).
- Aramaic persisted after Hebrew fell into disuse, becoming the vernacular of Jewish communities in the diaspora while Hebrew remained reserved for prayer and study.
