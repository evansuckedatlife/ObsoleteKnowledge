## summary

**Goliath** is the legendary giant of the Philistines, famous not for his own deeds but for being the unlikely victim of a shepherd boy's victory. Standing between nine and ten feet tall, armored and formidable, *Goliath* represented overwhelming military power—yet *David's* defeat of him with a sling and stone became the foundational myth of underdog triumph in Western literature. His death humiliated the Philistine army and launched *David* to fame and kingship, making *Goliath* indispensable to understanding one of the Hebrew Bible's greatest heroes.

## you gotta know

- *Goliath* was a Philistine warrior from the city of *Gath*, belonging to a race of giants called the Anakim, and stood approximately nine to ten feet tall according to the biblical account.
- He was heavily armed with armor and a shield, bearing a spear with an iron head that weighed six hundred shekels, making him a formidable professional soldier of the ancient world.
- When the Philistine and Israelite armies faced each other with King *Saul* commanding Israel, *Goliath* challenged the Israelites to single combat—winner take all.
- The young shepherd *David*, sent to bring supplies to his older brothers in the Israelite camp, volunteered to fight *Goliath* despite being untested in battle and unarmed except for a sling and stones.
- *David* selected five smooth stones from a stream and approached *Goliath* with confidence in divine favor rather than human weaponry.
- With a single stone fired from his sling, *David* struck *Goliath* in the forehead, felling the giant, who fell face-down on the ground.
- *David* then took *Goliath's* own sword and cut off his head, an act that completely demoralized the Philistine army and sent them fleeing in defeat.
- The victory over *Goliath* became the foundational achievement that made *David* famous throughout Israel, eventually leading to his anointing as king.
