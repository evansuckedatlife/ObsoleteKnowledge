## summary

**Jewish law**, or *halakhah* (Hebrew "the way to walk"), is the comprehensive system of religious, civil, and ethical obligations derived from the *Torah* and developed through centuries of Rabbinical interpretation. It governs every aspect of Jewish life, from dietary practices and Sabbath observance to marriage, commerce, and criminal justice. Far from being a static code, *halakhah* is a living tradition of interpretation and debate, with successive generations of *rabbis* adapting ancient principles to new circumstances while maintaining continuity with their sources.

## you gotta know

- Jewish law originates in the Written *Torah* (Five Books of Moses), which contains 613 commandments (*mitzvot*) covering religious observance, ethical conduct, and ritual practice.
- The *Oral Torah*—later recorded in the *Mishnah* (ca. 200 CE) and expanded in the *Talmud* (ca. 500 CE)—represents Rabbinical interpretation, application, and extension of biblical law.
- Rabbinical authorities use specific hermeneutical methods to derive new applications from Torah: identifying contradictions, comparing similar cases, using verbal parallels, and reasoning from general principles to specific instances.
- Major divisions of Jewish law include *Kodashim* (holy things, especially Temple sacrifice), *Taharah* (purity and impurity), *Mo'ed* (festivals and seasons), *Nashim* (women and family law), *Nezikin* (torts and criminal law), and *Zeraim* (agricultural laws).
- The *Shulchan Aruch* ("Set Table"), compiled by *Joseph Caro* in the 16th century, became the standard code of Jewish law for Sephardic Jewry, while Ashkenazi communities developed their own custom-based variations.
- Jewish law distinguishes between *de-oraita* (obligations from the Torah itself) and *de-rabbanan* (Rabbinical ordinances and safeguards intended to protect biblical law).
- The principle of *pikuach nefesh* ("saving a life") allows violation of almost any Jewish law if necessary to preserve human life, establishing human welfare as a supreme value.
- In modern times, different Jewish movements (Orthodox, Conservative, Reform) approach *halakhah* differently—Orthodox communities view it as binding and immutable, while Reform and Conservative movements see it as evolving.

## connections

- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition for which *halakhah* is the normative system of practice.
- [[torah]] — the scriptural foundation from which Jewish law is derived and interpreted.
- [[talmud]] — the vast compendium of Rabbinical debate and case law that expands biblical law.
- [[rabbi]] — the scholars and authorities empowered to interpret and apply Jewish law.
- [[mitzvah]] — the individual commandments comprising the system of Jewish obligations.
- [[shabbat]] — the Sabbath observance, a central area of Jewish law and practice.
- [[kashrut]] — the dietary laws that regulate what foods are permissible for Jewish consumption.
