## summary

**Atonement** is the theological doctrine that *Jesus's* death on the cross repaired humanity's broken relationship with God by satisfying divine justice and removing the barrier of sin. The concept bridges Old Testament sacrificial practice (where animal offerings made victims "at-one" with God) and New Testament redemption theology. Though universally central to Christian faith, different traditions explain *how* the atonement works through competing metaphors: ransom, satisfaction, moral influence, and mystical union.

## you gotta know

- The term literally means "at-one-ment": the restoration of unity between God and humanity, sundered by sin; historically rooted in the English word-play of *William Tyndale*.
- Rooted in the Jewish temple system, where the High Priest's annual [[sacrificial offering]] on Yom Kippur atoned for the nation's sins, a practice dating to Levitical law.
- In Christian theology, *Jesus* becomes the ultimate and final sacrifice (*Lamb of God*), rendering all further sacrifices unnecessary; this reinterprets Jewish practice in light of redemptive history.
- Medieval scholastic *Anselm* developed satisfaction theory: *Jesus's* death as a debt payment that restores God's honor and satisfies divine justice, a model that dominated medieval theology.
- *Luther* and Reformed theologians emphasized penal substitution: *Jesus* bore the penalty for human sin in the believer's stead, shifting focus from honor to juridical justice.
- Eastern Orthodox Christianity teaches theosis or deification: atonement as mystical union and transformation into Christ's image rather than juridical payment; this reflects non-Western Christological emphasis.
- The *Gospels* repeatedly link *Jesus's* death to "forgiveness of sins," anchoring atonement as the explicit reason for the [[crucifixion]]; this theological framework unifies diverse gospel narratives.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the person whose death accomplishes the atonement.
- [[crucifixion]] — the historical event through which atonement is achieved.
- [[sin]] — the condition the atonement removes or forgives.
- [[repentance]] — the human response through which individuals appropriate the benefits of atonement.
- [[salvation]] — the broader theological framework of which atonement is the central mechanism.
- [[trinity]] — the doctrine explaining how God can simultaneously judge and redeem.
- [[incarnation]] — the prerequisite for atonement: God becoming human to make the sacrifice possible.
