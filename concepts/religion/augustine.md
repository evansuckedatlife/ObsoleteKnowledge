## summary

**Augustine of Hippo** (354–430 CE) was an influential North African theologian and bishop whose ideas profoundly shaped Christian doctrine for over a thousand years. Originally a rhetor drawn to philosophy and sensual living, Augustine converted to Christianity through the influence of his mother and the preaching of *Ambrose*, then became one of Christianity's greatest theological minds. His writings on grace, predestination, original sin, and the nature of God established foundational concepts in Christian theology that remain central to Catholic and Protestant thought alike.

## you gotta know

- Born in Numidia (modern Algeria) to a pagan father and Christian mother, Augustine initially pursued philosophy and lived as a Manichean before his dramatic conversion.
- His *Confessions* (written ~397 CE) are among history's earliest autobiographies, blending personal narrative with theological reflection on grace and redemption.
- Augustine taught that humans are bound by *original sin*—inherited moral corruption from Adam—and depend entirely on God's *grace* for salvation, not human merit.
- He distinguished between the "City of God" (the community of the elect destined for salvation) and the "City of Man" (the earthly kingdom of secular ambition).
- Augustine's theology of *predestination* asserted that God foreknew and predetermined who would be saved, sparking centuries of debate over human free will and divine omniscience.
- As Bishop of Hippo (in North Africa), he defended orthodox Christianity against heresies including *Pelagianism*, which denied original sin and overemphasized human moral capacity.
