## summary

**Divine Nature** denotes the uncreated, infinite, and immutable essence (*ousia*) of God as formulated within classical monotheism and historical Christian dogma. In Christian christology and trinitarian theology, it represents the one eternal godhead shared equally by three distinct persons—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Understanding the relation between this transcendent perfection and the created cosmos is foundational for theological debates over the Incarnation, redemption, and mystical participation.

## you gotta know

- Formulated philosophically through the Greek term *ousia* ("substance" or "essence"), defining what God is in absolute contrast to created, contingent being.
- The First Council of Nicaea (325) affirmed that the Son is *homoousios* ("consubstantial" or "of one essence") with the Father, safeguarding full divine status against *Arianism*.
- The Council of Chalcedon (451) dogmatized the hypostatic union, asserting that *Jesus* possesses two unconfused, unchangeable, indivisible, and inseparable natures: one fully human and one fully divine.
- Scholastic theology, notably synthesized by *Thomas Aquinas*, emphasizes divine simplicity, holding that God's nature is identical with God's existence (*esse*) and devoid of physical or metaphysical parts.
- In [[eastern-orthodox-christianity]], *Gregory Palamas* defended the distinction between God's unknowable transcendent essence and the uncreated divine energies (*energeiai*) through which humanity participates in the divine life (*theosis*).
- Diverging christological models throughout history questioned this balance, such as *Monophysitism* (holding that Christ had only a single, divine nature) and *Nestorianism* (perceived as over-separating the human and divine persons).
- Classical theism attributes properties to this essence including aseity (self-existence), immutability, impassibility, eternity, omniscience, and omnipotence.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the second person of the Trinity in whom the divine essence united with a human nature.
- [[christianity]] — the tradition whose core creeds developed around defining Christ's relation to God.
- [[trinity]] — the doctrine establishing that one singular essence subsists in three distinct divine hypostases.
- [[eastern-orthodox-christianity]] — maintains the distinct Palamite framework separating the divine essence from uncreated energies.
- [[catholic-church]] — codifier of Western scholastic formulations of divine simplicity and the dual-nature dogma of Chalcedon.
- [[eden]] — the created sanctuary where humanity originally walked in intimate communion with the divine presence.
- [[divine-punishment]] — the righteous judgment emanating from the holy character and justice inherent to God.
