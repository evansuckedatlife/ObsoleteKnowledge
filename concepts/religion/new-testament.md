# New Testament

## summary

The **New Testament** is the second and shorter section of the Christian Bible, comprising 27 texts written in Koine Greek during the first century CE. It centers on the life, ministry, death, and resurrection of *Jesus Christ* and the early Christian movement he inspired. The New Testament includes the four *Gospels* (*Matthew*, *Mark*, *Luke*, *John*), which narrate Jesus's life and teachings; the *Acts of the Apostles*, which chronicles the early church's expansion; *Paul*'s letters to various communities; and other epistles and Revelation. Together with the *Hebrew Bible* (the Christian Old Testament), the New Testament constitutes Christian scripture and remains one of the most influential texts in Western civilization.

## you gotta know

- The four *Gospels* present Jesus's life and teachings from different perspectives; *Matthew* and *Luke* share similar material (the "Q source"); *Mark* is the earliest; *John* is philosophically distinctive.
- *Jesus* is the central figure: a Jewish teacher and healer who was crucified by Roman authorities; his followers believed he was resurrected and understood him as the *Messiah* (the Christ).
- *Paul* (Saul of Tarsus) was a Jewish Christian missionary who wrote the earliest Christian texts (his letters, circa 50–64 CE); he argued that Christ's salvation was available to non-Jews, radically expanding Christianity's scope.
- The *Gospels* describe Jesus's teachings through parables, ethical instruction, and miracle accounts; the *Sermon on the Mount* (Matthew 5–7) contains Jesus's most famous moral teachings.
- The *Acts of the Apostles* narrates the early church's formation, the apostles' missionary activities, and Christianity's expansion from Jerusalem into the Mediterranean world.
- The *epistles* (letters) address theological issues, ethical guidance, and pastoral concerns in early Christian communities; they are the earliest Christian theological writings and grapple with questions of faith, grace, and practice.
