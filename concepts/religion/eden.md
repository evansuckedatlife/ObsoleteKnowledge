## summary

**Eden** is the primordial earthly paradise established by God in the opening chapters of the Book of *Genesis*. Depicted as a fertile, well-watered garden containing the Tree of Life and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, it served as the original dwelling place of the first human beings before their transgression. In Western literature and religious thought, it endures as the quintessential archetype of pristine innocence, primordial harmony, and tragic loss.

## you gotta know

- Described in the [[hebrew-bible]] as a garden planted eastward, watered by a single river that divides into four heads: the Pishon, Gihon, Tigris (*Hiddekel*), and Euphrates (*Perat*).
- Created as the idyllic setting where *Adam* was placed to "till and keep" the land, charge over the animals, and live in unhindered fellowship with God.
- Contained two pivotal sacred trees at its center: the Tree of Life, granting immortality, and the forbidden Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil.
- Served as the scene of the temptation, where the cunning *serpent* deceived *Eve*, who ate the fruit and offered it to *Adam*.
- Their eating of the fruit precipitated the catastrophic Fall of Man, stripping them of primal innocence and revealing their nakedness.
- Prompted God to decree [[divine-punishment]] upon humanity and expel the couple from the garden to prevent them from eating from the Tree of Life.
- Guarded following humanity's expulsion by revolving flaming swords and *cherubim* stationed at the east entrance.
- Interpreted allegorically by early Christian theologians and Jewish mystics as representing the spiritual paradise restored in the eschaton or attained through heavenly ascent.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — the ancient scriptural canon in whose opening narrative the garden is detailed.
- [[judaism]] — the tradition that views the garden (*Gan Eden*) both as historical origin and as the heavenly realm awaiting the righteous.
- [[divine-punishment]] — the direct judgment that expelled humanity from the garden into mortal toil and suffering.
- [[divine-nature]] — the holy, transcendent presence that walked in the garden "in the cool of the day."
- [[christianity]] — interprets the expulsion from the garden as the source of original sin redeemed through *Jesus*.
- [[islam]] — honors the garden (*Jannat 'Adn*) as an archetype of the celestial paradise promised to believers.
- [[middle-east]] — the geographical cradle whose major rivers define the biblical coordinates of the garden.
