## summary

The **High Holy Days** (or High Holidays) are the most solemn period in the Jewish calendar, consisting primarily of *Rosh Hashanah* and *Yom Kippur*, observances in the autumn that focus on repentance, reflection, and spiritual renewal. This ten-day period known as the *Days of Awe* is considered the most important season in Judaism, when believers seek forgiveness from God and fellow humans. The High Holy Days center on themes of judgment, atonement, and recommitment to Jewish law and values.

## you gotta know

- Rosh Hashanah ("Head of the Year") marks the Jewish New Year and commemorates the creation of the world in Jewish tradition.
- Yom Kippur ("Day of Atonement") is observed as the holiest day in Judaism, a day of complete fasting and intensive prayer for forgiveness.
- The ten days between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are known as the *Days of Repentance* or *Days of Awe*, for spiritual introspection and amendment.
- Jewish tradition teaches that on Rosh Hashanah, God judges all beings, with final judgment sealed on Yom Kippur; sincere repentance can overturn harsh decree.
- During the High Holy Days, Jews confess sins (*vidui*), recite special prayers (*piyyutim*), and engage in acts of charity to demonstrate sincere repentance.
- These holidays suspend normal work and creative activities; the entire community gathers in synagogue for extended services emphasizing prayer over ritual action.
