## summary

**Salvation** is the central promise of Christianity: deliverance from sin and its consequences through the death and resurrection of *Jesus*. The concept draws from *Abrahamic* religious tradition but achieves its theological weight in Christian doctrine as both the ultimate goal and the work of divine grace. Different Christian denominations debate its mechanism—whether it comes through faith alone, works, sacraments, or divine election—but all hold that *Jesus* made it possible.

## you gotta know

- Rooted in the Hebrew notion of *yeshúa* (deliverance) but transformed by Christian theology into spiritual restoration and eternal life.
- Central to [[crucifixion]] theology: *Jesus's* death is understood as a sacrifice that redeems humanity from the bondage of sin.
- Protestant reform pivoted on the doctrine of *sola fide* (faith alone): salvation is a gift of grace, not earned through works; this doctrine split the Western church in the 16th century.
- Requires both divine initiative (God's grace) and human response (repentance, faith, or acceptance depending on tradition); this tension animates centuries of theological debate.
- Often paired with [[incarnation]]: God became human precisely to accomplish humanity's salvation, making the Incarnation the necessary prerequisite for human redemption.
- The [[atonement]] is the theological mechanism explaining how *Jesus's* death erases sin and restores relationship with God; understanding how this works distinguishes major Christian traditions.
- Eastern Orthodox Christianity emphasizes *theosis* or deification: salvation as union with God and transformation into Christ's likeness, not merely forensic forgiveness.
- Catholic theology adds layers of complexity: salvation involves both God's grace and human cooperation through sacraments, particularly baptism and penance.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the means and agent of salvation in Christian belief.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition where salvation is the ultimate promise.
- [[atonement]] — the theological framework explaining how salvation is achieved.
- [[incarnation]] — God's entry into human form as prerequisite for redemptive work.
- [[grace]] — the divine gift that makes salvation possible despite human unworthiness.
- [[sin]] — the condition from which salvation delivers the believer.
- [[repentance]] — the human response that typically accompanies the receipt of salvation.
