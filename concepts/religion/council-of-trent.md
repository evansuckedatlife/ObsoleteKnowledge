## summary

The **Council of Trent** (1545–1563) was the Roman Catholic Church's major ecumenical council convened to address the theological challenges posed by the Protestant Reformation and to reform Catholic doctrine and practice. Meeting in the northern Italian city of Trent over eighteen years and multiple sessions, the council reaffirmed core Catholic doctrines, standardized liturgical practice through the *Tridentine* Latin Mass, and initiated internal reforms to address corruption and improve clerical education. The council's decisions solidified Catholic orthodoxy and defined the Church's posture for centuries.

## you gotta know

- Convened by Pope Paul III initially in response to the spread of Protestantism, the council aimed to clarify Catholic doctrine and prevent further schism.
- The council reaffirmed papal authority against Protestant claims of *sola scriptura* (scripture alone), asserting the binding authority of both scripture and sacred tradition.
- Trent affirmed the practice of venerating saints and images, the theological reality of purgatory, and the sacramental nature of marriage and other rituals—all contested by Protestants.
- The council mandated the standardization of the Latin Mass (*Tridentine Mass*) and required strict observance of liturgical uniformity throughout the Catholic world.
- Trent established the *Vulgate* (Jerome's Latin translation of the Bible) as the official Catholic text, commissioning a corrected edition to counter Protestant Biblical scholarship.
- The council initiated reforms addressing clerical celibacy, education, discipline, and simony, laying groundwork for the *Counter-Reformation* and renewed Catholic vitality.
