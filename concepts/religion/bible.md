## summary

The **Bible** is the central sacred text of *Christianity*, comprising the *Hebrew Bible* (also called the *Old Testament*) and the *New Testament*. Written across more than a thousand years in Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek, it contains law, prophecy, poetry, wisdom literature, and four *Gospels* narrating the life and teachings of *Jesus*. For Christians worldwide, it is authoritative Scripture; for Jews, the *Hebrew Bible* alone holds that status.

## you gotta know

- The Christian Bible is divided into two parts: the *Old Testament* (39 books in Protestant canon), corresponding to the Jewish *Hebrew Bible*, and the *New Testament* (27 books), focusing on *Jesus* and early Christian communities.
- The *Hebrew Bible* is organized into three sections: the *Torah* (five books of law), the *Prophets*, and the *Writings*, covering creation, Israel's covenant history, poetry, wisdom, and messianic hope.
- The *New Testament* contains four *Gospels* (*Matthew*, *Mark*, *Luke*, *John*), the *Acts of the Apostles*, *Paul's* letters to early churches, and other epistles culminating in *Revelation*.
- The Bible was written by many authors over centuries; early Christians gradually recognized certain texts as divinely inspired (*canon*), while rejecting others as apocryphal or heretical.
- Different Christian traditions recognize different canons: *Protestants* accept 66 books, *Catholics* and *Orthodox Christians* include additional apocryphal texts, creating canons of 73 or 81 books respectively.
- The *King James Version* (1611), *Luther's* German translation, and modern translations (*NIV*, *NRSV*) have shaped how readers encounter Biblical language and meaning across centuries and cultures.
- The Bible's ethical teachings—the *Ten Commandments*, the *Golden Rule*, the *Sermon on the Mount*—have profoundly influenced Western law, morality, and philosophy beyond purely religious contexts.
- Textual criticism and archaeology have revealed the Bible's composition: the *Pentateuch* is a synthesis of sources, the *Gospels* blend oral tradition with theological interpretation written decades after *Jesus*.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — the Christian *Old Testament*, foundational to Jewish and Christian tradition.
- [[torah]] — the first five books, considered the most sacred within Judaism and Christianity alike.
- [[gospels]] — the four canonical accounts of *Jesus's* life, forming the heart of the *New Testament*.
- [[jesus]] — the central figure of the *New Testament* and the foundational authority of Christianity.
- [[paul]] — the apostle whose epistles shape half the *New Testament* and early Christian theology.
- [[quran]] — the sacred text of *Islam*, sharing Old Testament figures and some narrative overlap with the Bible.
- [[apocrypha]] — texts written in the Biblical tradition but not included in the Christian biblical canon.
- [[christianity]] — the religion for which the Bible is central Scripture.
