## summary

A **covenant** (rendered in Hebrew as **berit**) is a solemn, divinely sanctioned compact that establishes an enduring, relational bond between God and humanity or Israel in the *[[hebrew-bible]]* and *[[christianity]]*. Far transcending a secular legal contract, it entails reciprocal moral commitments, ethical stipulations, invocations of blessings and curses, and tangible sacramental signs. Through successive divine treaties established with Noah, Abraham, Moses, and David, the concept forms the central structural framework of biblical theology and redemptive history.

## you gotta know

- The Noahic covenant inaugurated in Genesis 9 represents an unconditional, universal guarantee made to all living creatures that a catastrophic flood will never again destroy the earth, symbolized eternally by the rainbow.
- The Abrahamic covenant in Genesis 15 and 17 promises the patriarch countless progeny, the land of Canaan, and universal blessing, sealed by the perpetual physical sign of circumcision (*brit milah*).
- The Mosaic or Sinai covenant detailed in Exodus 19 through 24 constitutes a bilateral national treaty mediated by *[[moses]]*, codifying the Ten Commandments and the legal precepts of the *[[torah]]*.
- The Davidic covenant recorded in 2 Samuel 7 promises King *[[david]]* an everlasting royal dynasty upon the throne of Jerusalem, establishing the theological bedrock for future messianic expectations.
- Scholars frequently compare biblical covenant formulations to ancient Near Eastern suzerainty treaties, particularly Hittite vassal treaties, which featured historical preambles, stipulations, deposit of texts, witnesses, and blessing-curse sanctions.
- Ancient treaty ratification ceremonies frequently involved "cutting a covenant" (*karat berit*), in which slaughtered sacrificial animals were divided into halves and the contracting parties walked between the pieces to invoke self-imprecatory oaths.
- The prophet Jeremiah looked beyond the broken Sinai pact to proclaim a promised "new covenant" (Jeremiah 31), wherein divine law would be inscribed directly upon human hearts rather than tablets of stone.
- In Christian theology, the institution of the Eucharist at the Last Supper and the *[[crucifixion]]* of *[[jesus]]* are understood as the formal enactment of this new covenant, fulfilling and transforming the Mosaic dispensation.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — the scriptural canon whose overarching narrative arc is structured around the progressive revelation, violation, and renewal of divine covenants.
- [[torah]] — the foundational five books containing the legal requirements, ritual ordinances, and ethical duties demanded by the Sinai covenant.
- [[judaism]] — the faith that conceives of the Jewish people as entering into and perpetually upholding an eternal covenant with the Creator.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition that interprets the gospel of *[[jesus]]* as establishing a new covenant offering universal salvation through divine grace.
- [[abraham]] — the founding patriarch whose faithfulness was rewarded with promises of land, nationhood, and spiritual heritage ratified through covenant.
- [[moses]] — the prophet-mediator who received the covenant code at Mount Sinai and sprinkled sacrificial blood upon the altar and the assembled congregation.
- [[david]] — the royal shepherd-king granted an unshakeable promise that his dynastic line would reign over an everlasting kingdom.
- [[deborah]] — the prophetess and judge who rallied the twelve tribes to honor their covenantal duties when threatened by foreign Canaanite subjugation.
- [[confession]] — the liturgical and personal act of admitting transgressions against divine covenant law in order to seek reconciliation and renewal.
