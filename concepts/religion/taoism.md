# Taoism

## summary

**Taoism** (or *Daoism*) is a philosophical and spiritual tradition originating in *China* that emphasizes harmony with the *Tao* ("the Way")—the underlying principle of the universe—through non-action (*wu wei*), simplicity, and alignment with natural forces. Developing from early philosophical texts like the *Tao Te Ching* attributed to *Laozi*, it became both a philosophical worldview and an organized religion with deities, rituals, and monasteries, profoundly influencing *East Asian* thought and spirituality.

## you gotta know

- The *Tao* is conceptualized as an ineffable, eternal, unchanging force underlying all existence; it cannot be named or fully understood through language, only experienced through living in harmony with it.
- *Wu wei* (non-action or effortless action) is a core concept meaning acting in accordance with the Tao naturally, without force or resistance; it advocates spontaneity, flexibility, and acceptance of the natural course of events.
- The *Tao Te Ching* (attributed to *Laozi*, likely compiled 4th–3rd century BCE) is the foundational text of philosophical Taoism, presenting paradoxical wisdom about the Way through short, poetic passages.
- *Yin and Yang* represent complementary opposites in Taoist cosmology: yin (passive, dark, feminine) and yang (active, light, masculine) together generate and balance all phenomena; neither dominates; harmony requires both.
- Organized religious Taoism developed later (*2nd century CE* onward), incorporating folk deities, ancestor veneration, and ritual practices; it created a priesthood and monastic traditions alongside philosophical Taoism.
- Taoist practice traditionally includes meditation, breath work (*qigong*), and martial arts (*tai chi*) aimed at aligning the self with the Tao and cultivating longevity or spiritual immortality.
- Taoism influenced *Zen Buddhism*, *Korean* Shamanism, and Japanese *Shinto*, spreading throughout *East Asia*; it offers a counterbalance to the hierarchical, duty-focused ethics of *Confucianism* within *Chinese* civilization.
