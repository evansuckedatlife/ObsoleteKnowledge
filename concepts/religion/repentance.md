## summary

**Repentance** is the spiritual act of turning away from wrongdoing, acknowledging sin, and seeking restoration through transformation and restitution. Central to both Jewish and Christian theology, repentance (Hebrew *teshuvah*, meaning "return") represents not mere regret but an active turning back toward God and righteousness. It bridges transgression and divine mercy, embodying the belief that humans can change, that God offers second chances, and that transformation is always possible through sincere contrition and commitment to amendment.

## you gotta know

- In Jewish thought, *teshuvah* (repentance) is one of the fundamental mechanisms through which humans restore their relationship with God and the community after wrongdoing.
- The process of repentance traditionally involves confession of the sin, genuine remorse, restitution to those harmed, and a commitment to refrain from repeating the transgression.
- The High Holy Days (*Rosh Hashanah* and *Yom Kippur*) are centered on repentance, with *Yom Kippur* serving as the Day of Atonement when Jews fast, pray, and seek forgiveness from God and from those they have wronged.
- In Christian theology, repentance is the turning away from sin and toward *Jesus* Christ; it is framed as essential to salvation, often paired with faith (*metanoia* in Greek).
- Biblical narratives exemplify repentance through figures like *David*, who repented of his sin with *Bathsheba*, and the people of *Nineveh* in the book of *Jonah*, who repented at *Jonah's* preaching.
- Repentance differs from mere regret in that it involves action and change—it is not simply feeling sorry but demonstrating through behavior that one has genuinely turned around.
- Both traditions emphasize that sincere repentance opens the door to forgiveness and restoration, even for grave sins, underscoring the concept of God's mercy.
- The Rabbinical tradition holds that the capacity for repentance was one of the things created before the world itself, suggesting its centrality to Jewish understanding of divine justice and mercy.
