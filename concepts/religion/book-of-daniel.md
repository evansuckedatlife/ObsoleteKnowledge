## summary

The **Book of Daniel** is a prominent biblical work comprising court tales of exiled faithful courtiers and vivid apocalyptic visions of world empires. Set during the Babylonian captivity following the destruction of the [[temple-in-jerusalem]], the text depicts the hero *Daniel* interpreting dreams for monarchs and surviving grave perils through uncompromising devotion. As a cornerstone of apocalyptic literature in both [[judaism]] and [[christianity]], the work provides foundational imagery regarding the triumph of God's sovereignty over pagan tyranny, judgment, and the resurrection of the dead.

## you gotta know

- Features the celebrated court tale of *Daniel* being cast into a den of lions for defying King *Darius's* ban on prayer, emerging unharmed by divine preservation.
- Depicts the miraculous deliverance of Daniel's companions *Shadrach*, *Meshach*, and *Abednego* from the fiery furnace of the Babylonian king *Nebuchadnezzar II*.
- Records the famous episode of "the writing on the wall" during a sacrilegious feast held by Prince *Belshazzar*, deciphered by Daniel as *Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin*.
- Interprets *Nebuchadnezzar's* dream of a colossal composite statue of four metals smashed by an uncut stone, representing sequential world empires replaced by God's kingdom.
- Introduces seminal apocalyptic imagery in chapter seven featuring four terrifying beasts ascending from the sea and the visionary figure known as the "Son of Man".
- Notable linguistically as a bilingual composition within the [[hebrew-bible]], shifting between Classical Hebrew and Biblical Aramaic.
- Contains references linked historically by modern critical scholars to the persecutions of the Seleucid ruler *Antiochus IV Epiphanes* and the desecration of the second temple.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — the canonical collection of sacred scriptures containing the text within the Ketuvim.
- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition preserving Daniel as an exemplar of righteousness in exile.
- [[christianity]] — the faith that interprets Daniel's "Son of Man" and messianic chronologies as prophecies of [[jesus]].
- [[temple-in-jerusalem]] — the holy sanctuary whose pillaged golden vessels are desecrated at Belshazzar's banquet.
- [[middle-east]] — the geographic theater of the Neo-Babylonian and Persian empires where the narratives unfold.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the Hellenistic empire of Alexander and the Seleucid dynasty reflected in the book's visions.
