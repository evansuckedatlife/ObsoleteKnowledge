## summary

**Martin Luther** (1483–1546) was a German Augustinian friar, theologian, and priest whose critique of the Roman Catholic Church's practices, particularly the sale of indulgences, sparked the *Protestant Reformation* and fractured Western Christianity. By nailing his *Ninety-five Theses* to the church door in Wittenberg in 1517 and defending his positions against papal authority, Luther initiated a religious revolution that reshaped European theology, politics, and society. *Note: This is the 16th-century German religious reformer, distinct from the American civil-rights leader Martin Luther King Jr., for whom a separate node exists.*

## you gotta know

- Martin Luther was born in Eisleben in Saxony, Germany; he became an Augustinian monk and earned a doctorate in theology at the University of Wittenberg, where he became a professor of biblical studies.
- Luther's theological breakthrough came through studying the biblical book of Romans and the concept of *sola fide* ("faith alone"): he concluded that salvation comes through faith in Christ alone, not through works or sacramental mediation by the Church—a conviction that contradicted Catholic teaching.
- In 1517, Luther composed his *Ninety-five Theses*, a scholarly critique of papal indulgences (the Church's practice of selling remission of sin to raise funds); though composed as an invitation to academic debate, they were translated, printed, and disseminated across Europe within weeks.
- Luther's public conflict with the papacy escalated: he refused to recant his positions at the Diet of Worms (1521) before the Holy Roman Emperor *Charles V*, declaring "Here I stand, I can do no other"—a defiant moment that became legendary in Protestant tradition.
- Luther was excommunicated by *Pope Leo X* in 1521; he subsequently translated the *Bible* into German (completed 1534), making scripture accessible to common people and establishing him as a founder of biblical scholarship and Protestant theology.
- Luther emphasized *sola scriptura* ("scripture alone") and *sola fide*, rejecting papal authority, priestly intermediaries, and many Catholic sacraments and practices; he advocated for a simplified church governed by local princes and scripture rather than Rome.
- Luther married *Katharina von Bora*, a former nun, in 1525—a defiant act against clerical celibacy that symbolized Protestant rejection of Catholic moral hierarchy and established the Protestant clergy as married, ordinary people rather than a separate caste.
- Luther's ideas were translated into political reform by German princes who broke from Rome; his theology profoundly influenced the emergence of Protestantism across Northern Europe (Lutheranism, Calvinism, and other denominations) and permanently split Western Christianity.
