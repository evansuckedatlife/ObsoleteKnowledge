## summary

**Delilah** was a central biblical figure in the *Book of Judges* within the [[hebrew-bible]], famously remembered as the lover and betrayer of the Israelite warrior-judge *Samson*. Residing in the fertile *Valley of Sorek*, she was approached and bribed by the five rulers of the [[philistines]] to uncover the secret source of *Samson*'s superhuman physical strength. Her persistence, emotional manipulation, and eventual extraction of his secret led directly to his blinding, capture, and public humiliation, securing her legacy as an archetypal femme fatale and enduring symbol of treacherous betrayal in Western literature, art, and music.

## you gotta know

- Bribed by each of the lords of the [[philistines]] with eleven hundred pieces of silver, representing an astronomical financial reward to uncover how *Samson* could be subdued and bound.
- Endured three deceitful explanations from *Samson* regarding his strength, including binding him with fresh bowstrings, tying him with unused ropes, and weaving his locks into a loom.
- Accused *Samson* of mocking her and lacking genuine love after each ruse failed, persistently pressing him daily until his soul was vexed to death by her nagging.
- Extracted his confession that he had been consecrated as a *Nazirite* unto God from his mother's womb, and that cutting his uncut hair would cause his strength to abandon him.
- Lulled the exhausted champion to sleep upon her knees before calling upon a conspirator to shave off the seven locks of his head.
- Taunted him with the cry that the [[philistines]] were upon him, realizing his divine power had departed when God withdrew from the desecrated warrior.
- Facilitated his immediate capture by the waiting Philistine soldiers, who gouged out his eyes and dragged him in bronze shackles to *Gaza* to turn a heavy prison grindstone.
- Prominently featured across Western cultural history, including *Peter Paul Rubens*'s dramatic Baroque painting, *John Milton*'s tragedy *Samson Agonistes*, and the grand opera by *Camille Saint-Saëns*.

## connections

- [[hebrew-bible]] — canonical collection whose historical *Book of Judges* details her interaction with the last major judge of early Israel.
- [[philistines]] — sea-peoples and traditional adversaries of Israel whose aristocratic lords orchestrated and funded her betrayal.
- [[divine-judgment]] — theological principle realized when *Samson* invokes God to collapse the temple of *Dagon*, destroying his captors.
- [[divine-mercy]] — compassionate grace granted by God in restoring *Samson*'s miraculous strength for one final sacrificial victory.
- [[middle-east]] — ancient geographical territory where the *Valley of Sorek* lay along the disputed borderlands of Dan and Philistia.
- [[tragedy]] — classical dramatic motif reflected in *Samson*'s fatal blindness to her obvious duplicity and his catastrophic downfall.
- [[judaism]] — ancient faith preserving the prophetic accounts of the judges as moral lessons on obedience, fidelity, and temptation.
