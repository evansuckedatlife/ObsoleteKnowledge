## summary

**Umma** (also *ummah*) is the Arabic term for the Islamic community—the collective of all believers in *Islam*, transcending ethnic, national, and tribal boundaries. Established formally by *Muhammad* during the [[hijra]] to [[medina]], the umma represents both a spiritual brotherhood and a political entity that united formerly disparate Arab tribes under shared religious law and common purpose. The concept remains foundational to Islamic identity and governance.

## you gotta know

- The word derives from the Arabic *umm* (mother) but came to mean a community united by faith and law rather than blood kinship; it is simultaneously spiritual and political.
- *Muhammad* explicitly created the umma upon arrival in [[medina]] through the *Constitution of Medina*, a social contract defining rights and duties of believers and allies; this is the foundational document.
- Membership is based entirely on acceptance of *Islam*—belief in *Allah* and *Muhammad's* prophethood—not race, language, or social status; this was radically egalitarian for its time.
- The umma abolished pre-Islamic tribal rivalries (*jahiliyyah*) by subordinating tribe to religious community, a revolutionary political achievement that transformed Arabian social structures.
- Conceived as the "best community" (*khayr ummah*) in the *Quran*, chosen by God to bear witness to truth and guide humanity; this carries both privilege and moral responsibility.
- Includes both Muslims living together in Islamic territory and those dispersed across non-Muslim lands, bound by shared observance and law; geographical dispersion did not diminish collective identity.
- The early umma under *Muhammad* and his successors (the *Rightly Guided Caliphs*) expanded rapidly through conquest and conversion, spreading from Arabia across three continents within a century.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the religion defining membership in the umma.
- [[muhammad]] — the prophet who established the umma as a deliberate political and spiritual entity.
- [[hijra]] — the migration that marked the formal constitution of the umma in [[medina]].
- [[medina]] — the city where *Muhammad* founded the umma and established its first community.
- [[quran]] — the scripture that defines the umma's beliefs, law, and identity.
- [[sharia]] — the Islamic law that governs the umma's conduct and relationships.
- [[caliphate]] — the political institution claiming to lead and represent the umma after *Muhammad*.
