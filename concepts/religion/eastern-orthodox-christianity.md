## summary

**Eastern Orthodox Christianity** is the second-largest Christian communion in the world, consisting of autocephalous churches governed by episcopal synods and united in sacramental theology, liturgical tradition, and patristic continuity. Emerging historically from the Greek-speaking eastern provinces of the *Roman Empire* and *Byzantium*, it recognizes the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople as *primus inter pares* ("first among equals"). The tradition places supreme doctrinal weight on the seven Ecumenical Councils, mystical theology, and the bodily resurrection of *Jesus*.

## you gotta know

- Traceable in ecclesiastical succession directly to the ancient sees of the East—Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem.
- Severed institutional communion with the Western church in the East-West Schism of 1054, largely over papal jurisdictional claims and the Latin addition of the *Filioque* clause to the Nicene Creed.
- Places the bodily resurrection of *Jesus* at the absolute summit of its theology, celebrated liturgically during *Pascha* through the triumphant chant "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death."
- Rejects the Western scholastic focus on satisfaction atonement and original guilt, emphasizing instead healing from spiritual sickness, cosmic liberation from death, and deification (*theosis*).
- Maintains the theological distinction formulated by *Gregory Palamas* between God's inaccessible essence and accessible, uncreated energies, enabling human participation in the [[divine-nature]].
- Employs veneration of icons as a fundamental dogmatic affirmation of the Incarnation, settled decisively after the Iconoclastic controversies at the Second Council of Nicaea (787).
- Utilizes the Divine Liturgy of *John Chrysostom* or *Basil the Great*, featuring continuous congregational singing, extensive incense, and communion under both kinds via a liturgical spoon.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the broader global faith of which Orthodoxy preserves the historic eastern branch.
- [[divine-nature]] — understood via the Palamite distinction allowing humans to partake of God's uncreated energies.
- [[catholic-church]] — the Western sister church separated since the Great Schism of 1054 over dogma and hierarchy.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial civilization whose Byzantine continuation shaped Orthodox law, architecture, and liturgy.
- [[jesus]] — the incarnate Lord whose life, crucifixion, and triumphant bodily resurrection form its central dogma.
- [[crucifixion]] — viewed not merely as forensic penalty, but as a voluntary descent into death to conquer it from within.
- [[paul]] — the apostle whose epistles on grace, spiritual life, and bodily resurrection inform its liturgical epistles.
