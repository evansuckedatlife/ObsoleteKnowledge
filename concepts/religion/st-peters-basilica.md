## summary

**St. Peter's Basilica** is the largest church in the world and the spiritual and administrative center of the Roman *Catholic Church*, located within *Vatican City* in Rome. Built over 120 years (1506–1626) to replace the ancient Constantinian basilica, it became Christendom's greatest architectural achievement and artistic treasure. Its construction was bankrolled by the controversial sale of indulgences—a practice that sparked *Martin Luther's* critique and the *Protestant Reformation*—making St. Peter's simultaneously the Church's grandest monument and an unwitting catalyst for its greatest schism.

## you gotta know

- Commissioned by Pope *Julius II* in 1506, St. Peter's was constructed to honor *Saint Peter*, the apostle whose ministry in Rome and martyrdom on this site formed the basis of papal authority and apostolic succession.
- The basilica is built over the believed burial site of *Saint Peter* himself; archaeological excavations have uncovered a necropolis beneath the altar, lending historical weight to the site's sanctity.
- Construction was superintended by successive genius architects: *Donato Bramante*, *Raphael*, *Michelangelo*, and *Carlo Maderno*. *Michelangelo*, who redesigned the dome, famously donated his labor out of piety.
- The iconic dome, designed by *Michelangelo* and completed after his death, is 136 meters (446 feet) tall and remains one of the most recognized architectural forms in the world, dominating Rome's skyline.
- Pope *Leo X* funded construction partly through the sale of indulgences (spiritual remission for sins), particularly through the indulgence preacher *Johann Tetzel's* aggressive campaigns in Northern Europe. *Luther's* protest against this abuse sparked the *Protestant Reformation*.
- The baroque master *Gian Lorenzo Bernini* transformed the basilica's interior and exterior: he designed the grand colonnade piazza, the magnificent baldachin (canopy) over the high altar, and numerous sculptures that exemplify *Counter-Reformation* grandeur.
- St. Peter's contains over 100 chapels, abundant sculptural and painted masterpieces (*Raphael*, *Caravaggio*), and the tombs of popes including *Saint Peter* himself. Its treasury of artistic and architectural achievement rivals any cathedral in Christendom.
- The basilica took over 100 years to complete (1506–1626) due to wars, financial crises, and changing architectural visions; its very prolonged construction made visible the tensions between Renaissance idealism and baroque drama.
