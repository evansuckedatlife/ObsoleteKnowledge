## summary

**Bathsheba** was the wife of *Uriah the Hittite* who became the mother of *Solomon* after King *David* seduced her and orchestrated her husband's death. Her story is one of the Bible's most famous illustrations of royal power, sin, and consequences—David's affair with her triggered divine punishment and internal conflict within his kingdom. Though initially wronged, Bathsheba became a powerful court figure and instrumental in securing her son Solomon's succession to the throne.

## you gotta know

- Bathsheba was the daughter of Eliam and wife of the Hittite warrior Uriah, a trusted general in David's army.
- David saw her bathing, desired her, and summoned her to his palace; she became pregnant with his child.
- To cover up the affair, David ordered Uriah's death in battle by positioning him in the fiercest fighting, then took Bathsheba as his wife.
- The prophet Nathan confronted David with a parable, condemning his sin; God struck down the illegitimate child as punishment for the transgression.
- Solomon, their second son, was born after David's repentance and became David's chosen successor, despite being younger than his brothers.
- Bathsheba acted as queen mother and advocated powerfully for Solomon at court, ensuring his rightful ascension to the throne despite rival claims.
- Her story illustrates the complex intersection of royal power, morality, and redemption—David's most grievous sin paradoxically produced the greatest and wisest king of Israel's entire dynasty.
