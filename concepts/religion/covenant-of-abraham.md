## summary

The **Covenant of Abraham** is the foundational agreement between God and *Abraham* in Jewish tradition, establishing Abraham as the father of a chosen people and promising his descendants would multiply, inherit the land of Canaan, and serve God. Sealed by the rite of *circumcision* (*brit milah*), this covenant became the legal and spiritual basis for Jewish identity and practice. The covenant is understood as perpetual and unconditional—passed from Abraham to *Isaac* to *Jacob*—making all Jews descendants of the covenant promise.

## you gotta know

- God promised Abraham that his descendants would be as numerous as the stars and would inherit Canaan as an eternal possession (Genesis 12, 15, 17).
- The covenant was formally sealed through *circumcision*, which became the sign of the covenant inscribed on the male body of every Jewish male child.
- God changed Abram's name to Abraham ("father of multitudes") to reflect the covenant's promise; his wife Sarai became Sarah ("princess").
- The covenant passed to Abraham's son *Isaac* (the child of promise) rather than *Ishmael*, establishing the line of descent through the promised son.
- Circumcision (*brit milah*) on the eighth day of a male child's life ritualizes entry into the covenant community and remains a central Jewish practice.
- The covenant emphasized God's unconditional grace rather than human merit, establishing the principle that Israel's chosenness derived from divine favor, not earned righteousness.
