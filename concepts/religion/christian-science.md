## summary

**Christian Science** (officially organized as the **Church of Christ, Scientist**) is a distinctive religious movement founded in the late nineteenth-century *[[united-states]]* by the religious leader *Mary Baker Eddy*. Centered upon the philosophical conviction that material reality and bodily suffering are spiritual illusions, the faith teaches that disease and sin are overcome by bringing human thought into harmony with the infinite perfection of God as divine Mind. It is renowned for its steadfast reliance on prayer and metaphysical healing in place of conventional medicine, as well as for establishing the internationally recognized daily newspaper *The Christian Science Monitor*.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1879 in Boston, Massachusetts, by *Mary Baker Eddy* following her recovery from an ostensibly fatal spinal injury in 1866, an event she attributed to meditating upon a healing performed by *[[jesus]]* in the New Testament.
- The fundamental doctrinal textbook is *Science and Health with Key to the Scriptures*, authored by Eddy in 1875, which together with the *King James Bible* serves as the ordained, impersonal dual pastor of the church.
- Teaches a radical theological idealism asserting that God is all-encompassing Spirit and Mind, while material substance, illness, sin, and death are erroneous beliefs of the *mortal mind* lacking true spiritual existence.
- Adherents typically avoid conventional pharmaceutical and surgical medicine, instead turning to accredited Christian Science practitioners who provide metaphysical treatments through prayer to dispel false mortal perceptions.
- The movement's international administrative headquarters is *The First Church of Christ, Scientist*, widely known as *The Mother Church*, situated at a landmark architectural plaza in Boston, Massachusetts.
- Sunday worship and Wednesday testimony meetings feature no ordained clergy or extemporaneous preaching, relying instead on elected lay First and Second Readers who recite alternating passages from the Bible and Eddy's writings.
- Established *The Christian Science Monitor* daily newspaper in 1908 in response to sensationalist yellow journalism, fulfilling Eddy's mandate to spread unbiased news and earning numerous Pulitzer Prizes.
- Eddy's ideas drew early inspiration from mental healer *Phineas Parkhurst Quimby*, though Eddy insisted her discovery was an independent divine revelation distinct from mesmeric suggestion.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the broad religious tradition from which Christian Science emerged as a restorationist movement seeking the primitive healing ministry of the early church.
- [[jesus]] — revered as the Way-shower and master metaphysical healer whose works demonstrated scientific divine laws rather than supernatural suspensions of nature.
- [[united-states]] — the country where the movement originated and developed during the late nineteenth-century flowering of new religious movements.
- [[protestant-reformation]] — the theological lineage of individual biblical interpretation and congregational governance that informed Eddy's church polity.
- [[covenant]] — the spiritual bond through which believers express their divine sonship and fulfill moral obligations under the governing laws of God.
- [[confession]] — the traditional practice of acknowledging transgression, reinterpreted in Christian Science as the mental uncovering and destruction of mortal error.
- [[american-literature]] — the nineteenth-century American literary and transcendentalist tradition that shared philosophical parallels with Eddy's metaphysical idealism.
