## summary

The **Albigensian Crusade** was a twenty-year military campaign initiated in 1209 by Pope *Innocent III* to eradicate the *Cathar* heresy in southern *[[france]]*. Centered in the region of Languedoc, this conflict marked the first time the papacy deployed the crusading apparatus and its plenary indulgences against internal European Christian populations. The war devastated Occitan culture, decimated the dualist religious sect, drastically expanded French royal sovereignty over the Mediterranean south, and catalyzed the development of the medieval *[[inquisition]]*.

## you gotta know

- Initiated following the murder of the papal legate *Pierre de Castelnau* in 1208, an assassination blamed on Count *Raymond VI* of Toulouse.
- Targeted the *Cathars* (also known as Albigensians), dualist believers who held that physical matter was created by an evil deity and rejected the sacraments of the *[[catholic-church]]*.
- Opened with the infamous 1209 Sack of Béziers, where papal representative *Arnaud Amalric* allegedly uttered the phrase, "Kill them all; God will know His own."
- Led on the ground by the brutal northern French baron *Simon de Montfort*, who seized regional lands and defeated southern nobles at the Battle of Muret in 1213.
- Saw the intervention of King *Peter II* of Aragon, who crossed the Pyrenees to defend his Occitan vassals but was killed during the fighting at Muret.
- Led directly to the establishment of the Order of Preachers by *[[dominic]]*, who sought to combat heresy through educated theological persuasion and mendicant poverty.
- Concluded with the Treaty of Meaux-Paris in 1229, transferring extensive Languedoc territories to the French Crown under King *Louis IX*.
- Culminated in the fall of the mountain fortress of Montségur in 1244, where over two hundred *Cathar* *perfecti* were burned en masse in a bonfire.

## connections

- [[catholic-church]] — religious authority that authorized holy war, indulgence dispensations, and doctrinal suppression within Western Christendom.
- [[france]] — kingdom whose Capetian monarchy capitalized on northern crusader victories to absorb the independent Occitan south.
- [[rome]] — seat of the papacy that coordinated military bulls, legates, and subsequent inquisitorial courts.
- [[inquisition]] — institutional apparatus formalized in the wake of the crusade to hunt down surviving dualist heretics.
- [[dominic]] — Spanish churchman whose itinerant preaching against southern French heretics spurred the Dominican Order.
- [[christianity]] — broader religious tradition within which deep ideological schisms triggered domestic crusading warfare.
