## summary

**Joseph** is a patriarch of ancient Israelite tradition, the favored son of *Jacob* whose story occupies a central section of the *Book of Genesis* and prefigures the later Exodus of Israel from Egypt. Sold into slavery by his jealous brothers, Joseph rose to prominence in the Egyptian court through divine favor and wisdom, eventually enabling the survival of his family during famine and facilitating their settlement in Egypt. His narrative arc from victimization to vindication and redemption became a theological template in Jewish and Christian tradition, illustrating divine providence and the possibility of moral transformation.

## you gotta know

- Joseph was the eleventh of Jacob's twelve sons and his favorite, a status that aroused his brothers' resentment; Jacob gave Joseph a multicolored coat (or "coat of many colors"), a symbol of favoritism that intensified sibling rivalry.
- Joseph's brothers, envying his favored status and angered by his dreams of their subordination, sold him into slavery to Midianite merchants; they deceived Jacob by showing him Joseph's bloody coat, claiming he had been killed by a wild animal.
- In Egypt, Joseph was purchased by *Potiphar*, an official of *Pharaoh*; he rose to prominence in Potiphar's household through competence and divine favor until Potiphar's wife falsely accused him of seduction, resulting in his imprisonment.
- While imprisoned, Joseph interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh's chief cupbearer and baker, demonstrating prophetic insight; the cupbearer's restoration to favor eventually led to Joseph's release and presentation to Pharaoh.
- Joseph interpreted Pharaoh's dreams of seven fat cattle devoured by seven lean cattle and seven full ears of grain devoured by seven thin ears as prophecies of seven years of abundance followed by seven years of famine; Pharaoh appointed him governor of Egypt to prepare for the crisis.
- During the famine, Joseph's family in Canaan faced starvation and traveled to Egypt to buy grain; Joseph, recognizing them, tested his brothers' character before revealing his identity, ultimately forgiving them and reconciling with his father Jacob.
- Joseph's family settled in the land of *Goshen* in Egypt, where they flourished and multiplied; this settlement became the foundation for the tradition of Israel's bondage in Egypt and the subsequent Exodus.
- Joseph's story emphasizes divine providence and moral redemption: betrayed and enslaved, he maintained integrity, rose to prominence through wisdom and favor, and ultimately used his power to save his family—a pattern that influenced later Jewish and Christian theology.
