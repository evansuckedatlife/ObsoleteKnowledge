## summary

A **giant** is an oversized humanoid being appearing across world mythology, folklore, and sacred literature, typically embodying primordial chaos, brutal physical strength, and elemental opposition to divine order. From the *Gigantes* and *Titans* of Greek tradition to the *Nephilim* and *Rephaim* of Semitic scriptures, these monstrous figures often represent an elder world overcome by heroic or celestial power. In religious and epic traditions, slaying such a colossus demonstrates the supremacy of faith, intellect, or lawful civilization over untamed wilderness and raw violence.

## you gotta know

- Felled famously in biblical narrative when the Philistine champion *Goliath* of Gath was slain by a young shepherd armed only with a sling and smooth stones.
- Described in the scriptures as remnants of archaic races such as the *Nephilim*, the *Anakim*, and *Og*, the Amorite king of Bashan whose iron bedstead was celebrated for its enormous dimensions.
- Warred against the Olympian pantheon in the Greek *Gigantomachy*, having been birthed by the earth goddess *Gaia* to avenge the imprisonment of the Titans.
- Embodied in classical myth by monstrous single-eyed cyclopes such as *Polyphemus*, who was blinded by *Odysseus* in the *Odyssey*.
- Depicted in Norse cosmology as the *Jötnar*, primordial beings inhabiting *Jötunheimr* whose conflicts with the gods culminate in the cataclysmic doom of *Ragnarök*.
- Personified across European medieval romance and Arthurian legend as savage forest brutes, such as the monstrous giant of *Mont-Saint-Michel* vanquished by *King Arthur*.
- Transformed into allegorical archetypes in post-classical literature, notably *Giant Despair* of Doubting Castle in *John Bunyan*'s spiritual allegory *The Pilgrim's Progress*.

## connections

- [[david]] — the Israelite monarch renowned for his youth victory over the Philistine colossus *Goliath*.

- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical culture that codified myths of the rebellious earthborn *Gigantes*.
- [[torah]] — the core Mosaic legal books mentioning archaic giants encountered by Israelite scouts.
- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition that preserves exegeses of biblical primeval colossi and antediluvian figures.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the dramatist who frequently invoked mythical titans and colossi as poetic metaphors for tyrannical overreach.
