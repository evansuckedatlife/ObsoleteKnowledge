## summary

**Atman** is the Hindu concept of the individual self or soul, understood in Vedantic philosophy as ultimately identical with [[brahman]], the universal consciousness. The recognition of this identity is the core insight of Hindu metaphysics and the gateway to spiritual liberation (*moksha*). This doctrine transforms the personal self from an isolated ego into a manifestation of the infinite divine principle.

## you gotta know

- The Sanskrit word means "self" and refers to both the individual soul and the underlying reality of consciousness in all beings; it is simultaneously personal and universal.
- Central to the [[upanishads]], particularly the great teaching *Tat Tvam Asi* ("Thou Art That")—the declaration that Atman is Brahman, expressed in the *Chandogya Upanishad*.
- The apparent separation between individual selves (*jiva*) and universal reality is illusion (*maya*), not metaphysical fact; this forms the foundation of non-dualism (*advaita*).
- Realization of one's true Atman—that one is not the limited ego but infinite consciousness—is the ultimate spiritual goal and constitutes the sole path to liberation.
- Each being's Atman is eternal, unchanging, and immune to decay, unlike the temporary physical body and fluctuating mind; this permanence explains the continuity of consciousness across lifetimes.
- Subject to the cycle of rebirth (samsara) until this recognition liberates the soul; its actions (*karma*) determine the quality of each rebirth, a process spanning potentially countless lives.
- Provides the metaphysical foundation for [[karma-and-dharma]]: the Atman persists across incarnations and accumulates karma accordingly; spiritual practice (*yoga*, meditation, knowledge) can accelerate liberation.

## connections

- [[brahman]] — the universal reality with which Atman is fundamentally identical; their unity is the heart of Vedantic philosophy.
- [[hinduism]] — the religious tradition in which Atman is central to cosmology and soteriology.
- [[upanishads]] — philosophical texts that elaborate the Atman-Brahman identity through dialogue and mystical instruction.
- [[karma-and-dharma]] — the ethical framework governing Atman's journey through rebirth across multiple lives.
- [[vedas]] — the foundational scriptures that introduce Atman as a core concept in the Vedantic portions.
- [[moksha]] — liberation achieved through the realization that one's Atman is Brahman, the ultimate spiritual goal.
