## summary

**Resurrection** refers to the theological belief and historical claim central to Christianity that *Jesus* rose from the dead three days after his crucifixion, restoring his body and establishing his divine power and victory over death. This event is the cornerstone of Christian faith, the vindication of Jesus's teachings, and the foundation of hope in eternal life for believers. Without the Resurrection, Christianity as a religion would not exist; it is recounted in the four Gospels and interpreted by *Paul* and subsequent theology as proof of Jesus's divinity and the inauguration of salvation for humanity.

## you gotta know

- The Resurrection is described in all four Gospels (*Matthew*, *Mark*, *Luke*, *John*) as occurring on the third day after crucifixion; Jesus appeared to his disciples multiple times before ascending to heaven, commissioning them to spread his message.
- *Mary Magdalene* and other women are the first witnesses to the empty tomb in all four Gospel accounts; they encounter the risen Jesus or angels announcing his resurrection—a detail significant given women's limited legal testimony status in ancient Jewish law.
- *Paul*, writing in his *First Letter to the Corinthians* (c. 53 CE), offers the earliest Christian account of the Resurrection and describes post-resurrection appearances to Peter, the twelve apostles, and hundreds of believers; Paul emphasizes that the risen Jesus had a "spiritual body," not merely a restored flesh.
- Early Christians believed the Resurrection demonstrated Jesus's divinity and vindicated his claims; it became the central miracle authenticating Jesus's message and separating Christianity from other Jewish movements.
- The Resurrection is intertwined with Jesus's Ascension (his return to heaven 40 days later) and his promised return (the *Parousia*); these three events form the arc of Christian salvation history.
- Different Christian traditions interpret the Resurrection's nature variously: some emphasize bodily/physical resurrection; others stress spiritual resurrection; Roman Catholicism and Eastern Orthodox Christianity maintain literal bodily resurrection as dogma.
- Skeptics and scholars debate the historical basis of the Resurrection: some view it as a hallucinatory or psychological experience of the apostles; others as a literal miracle; still others as a theological claim rather than historical event.
- The Resurrection grounds Christian hope in personal immortality and God's victory over evil and death; it anchors sacraments like the *Eucharist* (commemorating the Last Supper before crucifixion) and *Baptism* (initiating the believer into Christ's death and resurrection).
