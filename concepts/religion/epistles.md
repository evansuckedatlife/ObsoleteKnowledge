## summary

The **Epistles** (principally the **New Testament Epistles**, including the major corpus of the **Pauline Epistles**) comprise twenty-one letters that form the primary theological and pastoral foundation of the Christian scriptural canon. Authored predominantly by the apostle [[paul]], alongside letters attributed to [[peter]], [[john-the-apostle]], *James*, and *Jude*, these texts were dispatched to early Christian assemblies and leaders across the [[roman-empire]]. By addressing immediate congregational controversies, moral challenges, and doctrinal definitions regarding grace, salvation, and Christology, they established the enduring theological vocabulary of [[christianity]].

## you gotta know

- Encompass twenty-one discrete writings in the New Testament, conventionally divided into thirteen letters attributed to [[paul]], the anonymous *Epistle to the Hebrews*, and seven General or Catholic letters.
- The authentic letters of [[paul]] (such as *Romans*, *1 and 2 Corinthians*, and *Galatians*) represent the earliest surviving Christian texts, written prior to the composition of the canonical [[gospels]].
- Formulated the seminal doctrine of justification by faith through divine grace rather than adherence to Mosaic ritual law, expounded comprehensively in *Romans* and *Galatians*.
- Addressed the cosmic consequences of the [[fall-of-man]], establishing the typological comparison between Adam's transgression and the universal life offered through Christ's resurrection.
- Provided practical institutional frameworks for early congregational governance, pastoral oversight, and qualifications for church officers within the Pastoral letters (*1 and 2 Timothy*, *Titus*).
- Incorporated early lyrical hymns and confessional statements, notably the celebrated *Carmen Christi* in Philippians 2 outlining Christ's humble self-emptying (*kenosis*) and subsequent exaltation.
- The Catholic or General letters, bearing the traditional names of figures like [[peter]] and [[john-the-apostle]], offered encouragement under societal marginalization and warned against theological distortions.
- Catalyzed the theological debates of the [[protestant-reformation]], as *Martin Luther* derived his foundational concept of *sola fide* through intense study of the Epistle to the Romans.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the faith whose dogmatic theology, ecclesiology, and ethical conduct were principally formulated through these letters.
- [[paul]] — the prolific apostle who authored the majority of the collection to instruct, encourage, and correct nascent diaspora congregations.
- [[jesus]] — the central messianic figure whose life, ministry, [[crucifixion]], and exaltation constitute the focal point of apostolic exposition.
- [[peter]] — the leading disciple whose attributed letters provided pastoral exhortation and ethical guidance to persecuted communities in Asia Minor.
- [[john-the-apostle]] — the apostolic leader whose three epistles stressed theological truth, mutual love, and resistance to schismatic teachers.
- [[fall-of-man]] — the scriptural narrative of human alienation analyzed by *Paul* to demonstrate the universal human need for divine redemption.
- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean political and infrastructural world through which these letters were carried and copied among early house churches.
- [[gospels]] — the four biographical narratives of Christ that serve alongside these epistolary treatises as the dual pillars of New Testament theology.
