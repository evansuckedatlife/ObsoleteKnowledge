## summary

The **Fall of Man** (also known as **The Fall** or the **Fall of Humanity**) denotes the transition of the first human beings from primordial innocence and harmony with God into a state of guilt, alienation, and mortality. Chronicled in the third chapter of Genesis in the [[torah]], the narrative relates how the serpent deceived the woman into eating forbidden fruit from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, which she then shared with her husband. While understood in [[judaism]] as an archetype of human moral choice and free will, it became the foundation for doctrines of original sin, inherited guilt, and redemption through [[jesus]] in classical [[christianity]].

## you gotta know

- Originated in the Genesis 3 narrative, in which the serpent persuaded the woman to eat fruit from the forbidden tree, leading both original humans to disobey the divine prohibition.
- Brought immediate psychological and spiritual shame, causing the couple to recognize their nakedness, sew garments from fig leaves, and attempt to hide from God among the trees.
- Prompted divine judgments that imposed severe pain in childbirth, marital hierarchy, arduous agricultural toil among thorns and thistles, and physical mortality expressed as returning to dust.
- Culminated in the permanent expulsion of humanity from the Garden of Eden, with cherubim and a whirling flaming sword stationed eastward to guard the tree of life.
- Systematized as the doctrine of original sin by the Western church father *Augustine of Hippo*, who argued that Adam's guilt and disordered desires passed to all descendants.
- Developed extensively in the Pauline [[epistles]], where Christ is characterized as the "last Adam" whose sacrificial obedience reverses the universal condemnation introduced by the first Adam.
- Provoked the historic *Pelagian* controversy over whether humanity inherited corrupted nature and guilt or preserved unimpaired moral freedom to obey divine law without preparatory grace.
- Reinterpreted during the [[protestant-reformation]] by *Martin Luther* and *John Calvin*, who viewed the event as causing total depravity and the complete bondage of the human will.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the faith that constructed its theological doctrines of human nature, original sin, and redemption upon this narrative.
- [[torah]] — the foundational five books of Moses containing the Genesis account of creation, the garden, and human transgression.
- [[hebrew-bible]] — the broader scriptural canon framing the covenant history between God and humanity following the garden expulsion.
- [[epistles]] — the apostolic letters in which *Paul* formulated the theological contrast between Adam's disobedience and Christ's righteousness.
- [[catholic-church]] — the ecclesial body whose ecumenical councils, including Carthage and Trent, formally defined dogmas regarding inherited sin.
- [[protestant-reformation]] — the sixteenth-century theological movement that emphasized total depravity and justification by faith in response to human fallenness.
- [[jesus]] — the central figure whose incarnation, life, and [[crucifixion]] are understood as the divine remedy delivering humanity from the effects of sin.
