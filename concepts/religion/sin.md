## summary

**Sin** is an act, thought, or condition of transgression against divine law or moral order, fundamentally alienating humanity from God. Central to [[judaism]], [[christianity]], and [[islam]], the concept defines the fracture of the sacred covenant and the spiritual barrier that necessitates moral accountability, *repentance*, and divine *grace*. In Western theology, literature, and legal philosophy, reflections on its origins, severity, and remission have deeply shaped concepts of guilt, justice, and redemption.

## you gotta know

- In Christian theology, the doctrine of *original sin*, formulated notably by *Augustine of Hippo* based on the fall of man in Genesis and the epistles of [[paul]], holds that all humans inherit a fallen nature and moral guilt from the transgression of *Adam* and *Eve*.
- Medieval Catholic tradition systematised moral failings into the *seven deadly sins* (pride, greed, wrath, envy, lust, gluttony, and sloth), codified by *Pope Gregory I* and later famously structured as terraces of purgation in *Dante Alighieri's* *Purgatorio*.
- The [[catholic-church]] distinguishes between *mortal sin*—grave offenses committed with full knowledge and consent that completely sever sanctifying grace—and *venial sin*, which weakens but does not destroy the soul's communion with God.
- In the Greek text of the [[gospels]] and Septuagint, the predominant word for sin is *hamartia*, an archery term literally meaning "missing the mark," conceptualising human failing not merely as malicious defiance but as a tragic deflection from divine purpose.
- The [[hebrew-bible]] established ritual expiation through the sacrificial system of the [[temple-in-jerusalem]]—notably the annual ritual of *Yom Kippur* featuring the scapegoat sent into the wilderness—prefiguring the Christian understanding of [[crucifixion]] as the ultimate redemptive sacrifice of [[jesus]].
- In [[islam]], human beings do not inherit original guilt, as all are born with a pure innate disposition (*fitra*); instead, sin (*dhanb* or *khatiah*) represents moral forgetfulness and disobedience to the commandments revealed in the [[quran]], forgivable through sincere contrition (*tawba*) to *Allah*.
- The [[protestant-reformation]] erupted over contrasting views of sin's remedy, as *Martin Luther* argued that humanity is simultaneously justified and sinful (*simul justus et peccator*), rejecting Catholic penance and indulgences in favor of salvation solely through faith in divine grace.

## connections

- [[jesus]] — the Messiah in Christian doctrine whose death and resurrection provide redemption and liberation from the power of sin.
- [[crucifixion]] — the supreme sacrifice on the cross through which Christians believe the guilt and penalty of human sin were decisively atoned.
- [[paul]] — the apostle whose epistles laid the primary theological foundation for original sin, universal human fallenness, and justification by faith.
- [[hebrew-bible]] — the scriptural foundation depicting humanity's initial rebellion in Eden and establishing laws, covenants, and sacrificial atonement.
- [[catholic-church]] — the institutional tradition that formalised the sacramental system of confession, penance, and the dogmatic distinction between mortal and venial sins.
- [[temple-in-jerusalem]] — the sacred sanctuary where ancient Israel offered daily sacrifices and annual Day of Atonement rituals to cleanse collective sin.
- [[protestant-reformation]] — the sixteenth-century theological revolt that re-evaluated the total corruption of the human will and asserted justification through faith alone.
