# Protestant Reformation

## summary

The **Protestant Reformation** was a 16th-century religious and political upheaval beginning in *Germany* with *Martin Luther*'s challenge to *Catholic Church* authority and doctrine, resulting in the fracture of Western *Christianity* into *Protestant* denominations and the *Catholic* Counter-Reformation. It fundamentally reshaped religious practice, theology, politics, and society across *Europe* and eventually the world.

## you gotta know

- *Martin Luther*, a *German* monk and theologian, initiated the movement in 1517 by publicizing his *Ninety-Five Theses* criticizing *Catholic* indulgences and papal authority; though initially intended as academic debate, his ideas spread through the printing press and ignited popular movements.
- Core Protestant doctrines emerged: *sola scriptura* (the Bible alone as authority, not Church tradition), *sola fide* (salvation by faith alone, not works or sacraments), and the *priesthood of all believers* (challenging clerical hierarchy).
- *John Calvin* in *France* and *Switzerland* developed *Calvinist* theology, emphasizing predestination and the sovereignty of God; his doctrines were influential in *Scotland*, the *Netherlands*, and later *North America*.
- *England* broke from Rome under *King Henry VIII* (1534), creating the *Church of England*, partly for political reasons (papal refusal to annul his marriage) and partly influenced by Protestant ideas.
- The Reformation sparked violent conflict: *Peasants' Wars* in *Germany* (1524–25), *Religious Wars of France* (1562–98), and the *Thirty Years' War* (1618–48), which devastated *Central Europe*.
- The *Peace of Westphalia* (1648) ended major religious warfare in *Europe*, establishing the principle *cuius regio, eius religio* (the ruler determines the region's religion) and creating a fractured religious landscape.
- The movement had profound effects: the democratization of literacy (Protestants emphasized Bible-reading), the rise of nation-states, the questioning of authority, and eventually contributed to the *Enlightenment*.
