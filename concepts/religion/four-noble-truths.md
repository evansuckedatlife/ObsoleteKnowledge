## summary

The **Four Noble Truths** are the foundational doctrinal framework of Buddhist philosophy and spiritual practice, formulating the reality, origin, and cessation of suffering. Delivered during the historical Buddha's first post-enlightenment sermon at Deer Park in Sarnath, they analyze existence using an ancient medical diagnostic model: identifying the illness, isolating its etiology, declaring the cure, and prescribing the remedy. Comprehending and actualizing these truths constitutes the path to liberation from the cycle of rebirth and the attainment of nirvana.

## you gotta know

- Delivered in the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* ("Setting the Wheel of Dhamma in Motion") to the five ascetics who had previously abandoned the Buddha.
- Structured around the diagnosis of *dukkha*, often translated as suffering, unsatisfactoriness, anxiety, or unease inherent to conditioned phenomena.
- Identifies the first truth as *Dukkha Ariyasacca*, asserting that birth, aging, illness, death, and encountering the unpleasing are fundamentally sorrowful.
- Identifies the second truth as *Samudaya Ariyasacca*, teaching that suffering arises directly from *tanha* (craving, thirst, or attachment) and ignorance.
- Identifies the third truth as *Nirodha Ariyasacca*, affirming that the complete cessation, detachment, and eradication of craving leads to *nirvana*.
- Identifies the fourth truth as *Magga Ariyasacca*, providing the practical roadmap to liberation known as the *Noble Eightfold Path*.
- Categorized therapeutically by commentators as analogous to the ancient Indian medical paradigm: disease, cause, recovery, and therapeutic regimen.

## connections

- [[buddhism]] — the spiritual tradition for which these four propositions serve as the bedrock doctrinal foundation.
- [[hinduism]] — the broader Indian religious matrix from which early Buddhist philosophical terminology and soteriology emerged.
- [[upanishads]] — the philosophical texts whose inquiries into worldly transience and liberation parallel Buddhist insights.
- [[vedas]] — the ancient Indian liturgical corpus whose ritual sacrificial efficacy was rejected in favor of psychological liberation.
- [[taoism]] — the classical Chinese philosophical system with which Buddhist teachings syncretized upon entering East Asia.
