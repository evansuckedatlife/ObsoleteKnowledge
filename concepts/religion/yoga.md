## summary

**Yoga** is a comprehensive philosophical and physical discipline rooted in Hindu and Indian traditions, aimed at achieving spiritual liberation (*moksha*) through the systematic integration of body, mind, and spirit. Emerging from Vedic foundations and fully articulated in classical texts like the *Yoga Sutras*, it encompasses physical postures (*asanas*), breathing techniques (*pranayama*), meditation, and ethical practices, uniting individual consciousness with the universal *Brahman*.

## you gotta know

- Derives from Sanskrit *yuj*, meaning "to join," "to yoke," or "to unite," expressing yoga's fundamental goal of union between the individual self (*atman*, the eternal soul) and ultimate reality (*Brahman*, the transcendent ground of all being).
- Foundational concepts appear scattered throughout the *Upanishads* (philosophical texts exploring *Brahman*) and hints in the *Vedas* (oldest Hindu scriptures), but yoga crystallized into a systematic, codified philosophical discipline in *Patanjali's* *Yoga Sutras* (circa 400 CE), which defines yoga as "the stilling of the mind's fluctuations."
- The *Bhagavad Gita*, Hinduism's most popular spiritual text, teaches multiple simultaneous yogic paths suitable to different temperaments: *karma yoga* (yoga of action and duty), *bhakti yoga* (yoga of devotion and emotional surrender), and *jnana yoga* (yoga of knowledge and intellectual realization), emphasizing that each is a valid and complete path to *moksha* (liberation).
- Physical postures (*asanas*) and breathing techniques (*pranayama*) are merely preliminary stages—the "lowest rungs" of yoga—designed to calm the body; higher yoga involves mental discipline, ethical restraint, sensory withdrawal (*pratyahara*), focused concentration (*dharana*), meditation (*dhyana*), and ultimately *samadhi* (enlightenment or absorption into *Brahman*).
- *Chakras* are understood as energetic/spiritual centers aligned along the spine (seven major chakras in most traditions) through which practitioners circulate *prana* (cosmic life force or vital energy) via controlled breathing, visualization, chanting of sacred syllables (*mantras*), and meditation.
- Modern Western yoga typically emphasizes physical postures and fitness, but traditional yoga is inseparable from comprehensive ethical guidelines (*yama* like non-violence and truthfulness; *niyama* like self-discipline and cleanliness), spiritual knowledge (*jnana*), and devotional practice (*bhakti*) toward ultimate spiritual liberation.

## connections

- [[hinduism]] — the religious tradition in which yoga originated and remains central.
- [[vedas]] — the Vedic texts from which yoga philosophy emerges.
- [[brahman]] — the ultimate reality that yoga practitioners seek to experience and unite with.
- [[bhagavad-gita]] — the philosophical text expounding multiple yogic paths.
- [[chakras]] — the energy centers engaged through advanced yogic practice.
- [[buddhism]] — the tradition that adopted and adapted yoga techniques, particularly in *meditation*.
