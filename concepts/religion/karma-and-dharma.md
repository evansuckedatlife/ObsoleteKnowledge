## summary

**Karma and dharma** are interlocking concepts central to Hindu and Buddhist understanding of existence: *karma* is the law of moral causality—actions generate consequences that bind an individual to the cycle of rebirth (*samsara*)—while *dharma* is the ethical and cosmic order, one's duty in accordance with one's role and station in life. Together they form a framework explaining suffering, justifying social hierarchy, and providing a path toward spiritual liberation through righteous action and devotion to duty.

## you gotta know

- *Karma* (from Sanskrit "action") operates as a moral law of cause and effect: every action produces consequences that the agent must experience, either in the present life or in future rebirths.
- *Karma* is understood not as punishment or reward imposed by an external judge, but as an intrinsic causal law built into the fabric of existence—actions naturally produce corresponding effects over time.
- *Dharma* (from Sanskrit "to hold" or "to sustain") refers to duty, righteousness, law, and the cosmic order that maintains the universe; it also designates one's particular duties based on caste, gender, age, and stage of life.
- A person's *svadharma* ("own dharma") specifies the particular obligations suited to their position in society; fulfilling one's *svadharma* is considered the highest virtue, even if it conflicts with universal principles.
- The *Bhagavad Gita* exemplifies this tension when *Arjuna* is urged by *Krishna* to fight in battle as his *dharma* as a warrior, despite the moral objections he raises about killing relatives.
- *Karma* and *dharma* together explain why individuals are born into different social positions: past-life *karma* determines present circumstances, while current *dharma* determines future *karma* and rebirth.
- Liberation (*moksha* in Hinduism, *nirvana* in Buddhism) is achieved not by rejecting action but by performing action without attachment to results, following one's *dharma* while recognizing the illusion of the separate self.
- The cycles of *karma* and rebirth can only be escaped through ultimate spiritual realization—the recognition that the individual *atman* (self) is identical with *Brahman* (ultimate reality), or through Buddhist practices leading to the cessation of desire.

## connections

- [[hinduism]] — the religious tradition where *karma* and *dharma* are foundational concepts.
- [[buddhism]] — the faith that adapted and reinterpreted *karma* and *dharma* in its own cosmology.
- [[brahman]] — the ultimate reality to which liberation according to *karma* and *dharma* leads.
- [[vedas]] — the ancient texts that first articulated concepts of *karma* and *dharma*.
- [[rebirth]] — the cyclical reincarnation driven by accumulated *karma*.
- [[atman]] — the individual self whose *karma* determines its rebirth and *dharma* in successive lives.
- [[liberation]] — the spiritual goal achieved by transcending the cycles of *karma* and *dharma*.
