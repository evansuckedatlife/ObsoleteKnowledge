## summary

**Laozi** (literally *"Old Master"*) is the legendary author traditionally credited with composing the *Tao Te Ching*, the foundational text of *Taoism* and philosophical Daoism. Living during the late *Zhou Dynasty* (exact dates debated, possibly 6th–5th century BCE), he is revered as a sage and spiritual patriarch in Chinese philosophy and religion. Though his historical existence remains contested by scholars, *Laozi* became one of the most influential philosophical voices in human history, shaping Eastern thought, martial arts, medicine, and aesthetics for over 2,500 years.

## you gotta know

- His name means *"Old Master"*; he may be a historical person, a composite figure, or a legendary projection. Traditional biography claims he was the court archivist of the Zhou dynasty and withdrew from society to write the *Tao Te Ching*.
- He is the reputed author of the *Tao Te Ching* (or *Daodejing*, "The Way and its Virtue"), a cryptic, poetic text of 81 aphoristic chapters that became the philosophical cornerstone of *Taoism* and influenced all East Asian thought.
- The *Tao Te Ching* teaches *wu wei* ("non-action" or "effortless action"), the principle of flowing with the natural way of the universe rather than imposing force or will—a radical alternative to *Confucius's* emphasis on ritual, hierarchy, and moral cultivation.
- In religious Taoism, *Laozi* is deified: he is one of the Three Pure Ones (the highest deities in Taoism) and is depicted as an immortal sage who achieved enlightenment through spiritual practice and union with the *Tao*.
- He embodies the Taoist ideal of simplicity, humility, and withdrawal from the material world and political ambition—a philosophical counterweight to Confucian engagement and social responsibility.
- The *Tao* he describes is ineffable: "The Tao that can be spoken of is not the eternal Tao"—meaning reality transcends language, logic, and conceptual thought, requiring direct intuitive experience.
- His influence pervades Chinese literature, painting, calligraphy, and martial arts (kung fu, tai chi); merged with *Buddhist* thought to create *Zen* Buddhism, which spread throughout East Asia.
- The *Zhuangzi* (another foundational Taoist text written centuries after *Laozi*) centers on *Laozi* and the butterfly paradox, deepening his philosophical legacy through dialogical storytelling.

## connections

- [[tao-te-ching]] — the text he is credited with authoring, the philosophical foundation of Taoism.
- [[taoism]] — the spiritual and philosophical tradition centered on his teachings and deification.
- [[confucius]] — his great philosophical rival; they represent opposed visions of proper conduct and society.
- [[wu-wei]] — the central concept in *Laozi's* philosophy, the principle of non-action or effortless action.
- [[yin-yang]] — the symbol of complementary opposites central to Taoist cosmology and *Laozi's* thought.
- [[zen-buddhism]] — the East Asian tradition that merged Buddhist and Taoist thought, especially *Laozi's* ideas on intuition and transcendence.
- [[zhou-dynasty]] — the historical period in which *Laozi* is traditionally placed and from which he withdrew.
- [[daoism]] — the modern pinyin spelling of the tradition *Laozi* founded, increasingly used alongside *Taoism*.
