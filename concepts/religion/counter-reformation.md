## summary

The **Counter-Reformation** (16th–17th centuries) was the Catholic Church's comprehensive response to the *Protestant Reformation*, combining internal Church reform with theological and institutional reassertion. Initiated by the *Council of Trent* (1545–1563), it reformed clergy corruption, clarified Catholic doctrine, and launched the *Jesuit Order* to reclaim souls and territories lost to Protestantism. Culturally, it sparked the *Baroque* artistic movement and sacred music renaissance, transforming religious art into emotionally powerful propaganda for Catholic renewal.

## you gotta know

- The *Council of Trent*, summoned by Pope *Paul III*, convened to address corruption (*clerical celibacy*, indulgence abuses) and define Catholic doctrine against Protestant challenges, becoming the blueprint for modern Catholicism.
- The *Jesuit Order* (*Society of Jesus*), founded by *Ignatius of Loyola* in 1540, became the Church's intellectual spearhead: educators, missionaries, and theologians who recovered *Catholic* territories in Eastern Europe and launched missions to Asia and the Americas.
- The Counter-Reformation reaffirmed sacramental theology, papal supremacy, and the veneration of saints and relics—precisely the practices *Luther*, *Calvin*, and *Zwingli* had rejected—but with greater pastoral care and clearer theological justification.
- *Baroque art* became the visual language of Counter-Reformation piety: dramatic lighting, emotional intensity, sensory richness, and theatrical grandeur designed to move congregants toward devotion in ways *Protestant* austerity did not.
- Sacred music flourished under Counter-Reformation patronage: *Palestrina*, *Orlando di Lasso*, and *Tomás Luis de Victoria* composed polyphonic masses and motets that balanced artistic complexity with intelligibility of the Latin liturgy, establishing enduring classical forms.
- The *Inquisition* intensified, particularly in Spain and Italy, as the Church sought to suppress heretical thought, Jewish and Islamic communities, and scientific inquiry deemed threatening to doctrine.
- The Counter-Reformation successfully *reconquered* significant territories: *Poland*, *Hungary*, and parts of *Germany* remained (or reverted to) Catholicism; *Southern Europe* solidified as Catholic stronghold while *Northern Europe* remained Protestant.
