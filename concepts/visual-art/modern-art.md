## summary

**Modern Art** encompasses the revolutionary movements and artworks of roughly 1870–1960 that broke decisively from representational traditions and classical norms. It includes [[cubism]], [[surrealism]], [[abstract-expressionism]], and countless other movements that prioritized formal innovation, emotional expression, and conceptual daring over mimetic accuracy. The term reflects both a historical period and an institutional judgment—major museums like [[moma]] and the [[guggenheim]] enshrined "modern art" as a canon that defined aesthetic value for the 20th century.

## you gotta know

- Modernism began as a reaction to industrialization and academic art conventions; artists sought to capture inner experience rather than outer appearance.
- Key movements include [[impressionism]], [[fauvism]], [[expressionism]], [[cubism]], [[dada]], [[surrealism]], and [[abstract-expressionism]].
- The concept privileges radical formal experiment: distorted perspective, non-representational abstraction, unconventional materials, and provocative content.
- Driven by successive "isms" and avant-garde manifestos—Futurism, Suprematism, Constructivism—each claiming to represent the future.
- Museums like [[moma]] and the [[guggenheim]] institutionalized "modern art" as a canon, making taste into institutional authority and market value.
- Many modernist artists were political: [[constructivism]] served the Soviet Revolution; [[dada]] mocked war and bourgeois culture.
- The term "modern" gradually ceded to "contemporary" as 1960s postmodernism challenged modernism's teleological confidence.
- Modern art prioritized the artist's subjective vision and revolutionary intent over craft tradition or market demand.
- The rise of photography freed painting from documentary duty, accelerating the move toward abstraction and expressionism throughout the 20th century.
- Modern art institutions—galleries, museums, biennales, art schools—created an infrastructure of legitimacy and market value that made careers and sustained movements.
- By mid-century, modernism had become the canonical aesthetics: the standard against which all artistic ambition was measured, taught, and institutionalized.

## connections

- [[moma]] — the institution that defined and canonized modern art for American audiences.
- [[guggenheim]] — a parallel institution collecting and celebrating modernist innovations.
- [[cubism]] — among the most revolutionary of modernist movements, transforming representation itself.
- [[surrealism]] — modernism's most psychologically ambitious movement, mining the unconscious.
- [[abstract-expressionism]] — the American postwar movement that claimed modernism's mantle.
- [[modernism]] — the broader philosophical and cultural shift of which modern art is the visual expression.
- [[dada]] — the anti-art movement that questioned what art could be.
