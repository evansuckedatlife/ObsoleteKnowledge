## summary

**Abstraction**, often encompassing **non-representational art** and **non-objective art**, is a foundational concept and aesthetic mode in the visual arts that deliberately departs from the faithful depiction of physical reality in favor of independent compositions of color, form, line, and volume. Emerging during the rapid industrial and intellectual upheavals of the early 20th century, it fundamentally revolutionized Western *[[painting]]* and *[[sculpture]]* by freeing artistic expression from the classical demand for photographic mimesis. By asserting the self-sufficiency of the visual medium, abstraction allowed artists to articulate spiritual, emotional, and structural truths that lay entirely beyond the visible world.

## you gotta know

- Rejection of Renaissance spatial illusionism: Abstraction abandoned linear *[[perspective]]* and anatomical mimicry, treating the canvas or sculpted mass as an autonomous physical surface governed by its own internal compositional logic.
- Spiritual and philosophical foundations of early painters: Wassily *[[kandinsky]]* articulated the theoretical basis for non-objective art in his 1911 treatise *Concerning the Spiritual in Art*, creating expressive improvisations that mirrored the non-mimetic emotional power of music.
- Pure geometric reduction in Europe: Kazimir Malevich founded Suprematism in Russia with his iconic 1915 *Black Square*, while Piet Mondrian developed Neoplasticism within the Dutch *[[de-stijl]]* movement, reducing the visual world to primary colors, black gridlines, and white planes.
- Sculptural reduction to essential essences: Romanian sculptor Constantin Brâncuși pioneered modernist three-dimensional abstraction in works like *Bird in Space*, paring away superficial anatomical details to capture the pure kinetic concept of flight rather than a bird's feathers.
- The transitional breakthrough of Cubism: Pioneered by *[[pablo-picasso]]* and Georges Braque, *[[cubism]]* fragmented three-dimensional objects into multi-perspective planes, providing the indispensable visual bridge from realism to complete non-figuration.
- Post-war triumph of Abstract Expressionism: In mid-century *[[new-york-city]]*, artists like Jackson Pollock (through gestural drip painting) and Mark Rothko (through luminous color fields) established American avant-garde dominance with emotionally charged abstract canvases.
- The *[[1960s]]* shift to Minimalism and contemporary architecture: The decade stripped expressive brushwork away in favor of geometric hard-edge painting and primary industrial forms, establishing visual principles that modern architects like *[[adrian-smith]]* translate into aerodynamic supertall towers.

## connections

- [[abstract-art]] — the overarching historical movement defined by the systematic exploration of non-representational aesthetics.
- [[kandinsky]] — pioneer who formulated the intellectual, musical, and spiritual defense of non-objective painting.
- [[cubism]] — revolutionary style that deconstructed traditional perspective and catalyzed the move toward complete abstraction.
- [[sculpture]] — medium transformed by Brâncuși, Moore, and Arp through the reduction of physical mass to organic and geometric essentials.
- [[painting]] — principal medium wherein the autonomy of line, pigment, and flat support was first radically demonstrated.
- [[1960s]] — crucial decade in which abstraction evolved from gestural expression into hard-edge painting, Op Art, and industrial Minimalism.
- [[pablo-picasso]] — co-creator of Cubism whose spatial deconstructions shattered five centuries of academic pictorial convention.
- [[adrian-smith]] — contemporary architect who applies geometric abstraction and aerodynamic formal reduction to supertall skyscrapers.
