## summary

**Egon Schiele** was an Austrian painter and draftsman whose raw figurative style, agonizing linear intensity, and provocative psychological scrutiny made him a foundational figure of Viennese *[[expressionism]]*. Emerging from the ornate decorative orbit of Vienna Secession master Gustav Klimt, he quickly pioneered an unflinching modernist visual language that laid bare physical vulnerability, eroticism, and existential anxiety. Despite his untimely death at twenty-eight during the 1918 influenza pandemic, his prolific body of works on paper and canvas revolutionized twentieth-century figurative art.

## you gotta know

- Mentored by Gustav Klimt, who bought Schiele's early drawings, arranged patrons, and introduced him to the Vienna Workshop (*Wiener Werkstätte*), before Schiele abandoned Klimt's shimmering *Art Nouveau* gold surfaces for jagged, raw contours.
- Celebrated for visceral, confrontational self-portraits such as *Self-Portrait with Chinese Lantern Plant* (1912), in which he rendered his own body emaciated, sallow-skinned, and twisted with splayed fingers.
- Arrested and incarcerated for twenty-four days in Neulengbach in 1912 on charges of seduction and obscenity; while the kidnapping charge was dismissed, one of his sexually explicit drawings was dramatically burned before the judge.
- Painted the poignant allegorical masterpiece *Death and the Maiden* (*Tod und Mädchen*, 1915), which captured his agonizing emotional separation from his longtime muse and model Wally Neuzil as he prepared to marry Edith Harms.
- Executed drawings and gouaches characterized by tense, razor-sharp contour lines created with graphite or crayon, frequently isolating floating, contorted figures against stark, unpainted paper voids.
- Rebelled against conservative academic training by founding the dissident *Neukunstgruppe* ("New Art Group") in 1909 alongside fellow disillusioned students from the Academy of Fine Arts Vienna.
- Achieved major critical and financial triumph at the 49th Vienna Secession exhibition in 1918, only to succumb to the Spanish flu just three days after his pregnant wife Edith died of the disease.

## connections

- [[expressionism]] — the international movement that prioritized intense emotional experience and psychological angst over objective naturalistic representation.
- [[modernism]] — the broad early-twentieth-century aesthetic revolt against academic conventions within which his raw figurative explorations played an incendiary role.
- [[german-expressionism]] — contemporaneous movement with parallel interests in psychological torment and bodily distortion championed by groups like *[[die-bruecke]]*.
- [[painting]] — medium he redefined through gouache, watercolor, and oil, elevating preliminary drawings into finished, museum-scale expressive statements.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflict during which he served in non-combat administrative roles guarding Russian prisoners of war while continuing to document military personnel.
- [[distortion-in-art]] — deliberate aesthetic manipulation of anatomical proportions, skin tones, and bodily poses to project inner psychological trauma onto external flesh.
- [[emotion-over-representation]] — guiding philosophical tenet of his portraiture that subordinated pleasing likenesses to searing existential authenticity.
