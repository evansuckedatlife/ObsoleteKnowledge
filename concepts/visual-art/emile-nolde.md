## summary

**Emile Nolde** (1867–1956) was a German-Danish Expressionist painter renowned for his intensely colored religious works and landscapes that merged spiritual fervor with modernist formal innovation. Born *Emil Hansen* in the village of Nolde in Schleswig, he adopted his hometown's name as his artist identity. Though he joined Die Brücke late (1906–1907), his work epitomized the group's marriage of distorted form and intense color for emotional and spiritual expression. Nolde's paintings feature biblical scenes, flowers, and seascapes rendered in vivid, non-naturalistic hues, prioritizing inner vision and religious passion over representational accuracy. His later work, though politically compromised by Nazi associations, remains a pinnacle of Expressionist intensity and color-driven abstraction.

## you gotta know

- Born in provincial Denmark but adopted the artistic identity of a German Expressionist through choice and affiliation with Die Brücke.
- Joined Die Brücke in 1906 at age 39, relatively late, but became one of its most distinctive and powerful voices.
- Developed a signature style of intense, non-naturalistic color: brilliant reds, yellows, and blues applied with expressive urgency.
- Created numerous religious works—biblical scenes, life of Christ series—that infused Christian subject matter with modernist intensity and distortion.
- Painted landscapes, especially seascapes and rural scenes, using color expressively to convey emotional and spiritual states rather than visual accuracy.
- Employed watercolor and printmaking alongside oil, producing prints and etchings of comparable intensity.
- His work faced Nazi appropriation and later critical reassessment, complicating his legacy despite the formal power of his paintings.
