## summary

**Andy Warhol** (1928–1987) was an American artist and cultural provocateur who fundamentally transformed modern art by treating mass-produced commercial imagery as worthy subjects for fine art. Born *Andrew Warhola* to Slovak-American parents in Pittsburgh, Warhol moved to New York and became the defining figure of [[pop-art]], a movement that collapsed the boundary between high art and consumer culture. His silkscreened repetitions of soup cans, Coca-Cola bottles, and celebrity portraits challenged everything the Modernist establishment valued: originality, authenticity, and the artist's hand. His studio, *The Factory*, became a cultural epicenter where art, celebrity, and commerce merged into a new mode of artistic practice.

## you gotta know

- Trained as a commercial illustrator and graphic designer; this background shaped his entire artistic philosophy.
- Created *Campbell's Soup Cans* (1962), silkscreen prints of mundane consumer products that scandalized the art world and defined Pop Art.
- Employed silkscreen printing—an industrial, reproducible technique—rather than traditional painting, embodying Pop's embrace of mass production over artistic originality.
- Famously declared "I want to be a machine," articulating Pop's fascination with repetition, mechanical reproduction, and the erosion of individual authorship.
- Produced thousands of portraits, often of celebrities: *Marilyn Monroe*, *Liz Taylor*, *Elvis Presley*—treating fame and image as interchangeable commodities.
- Founded *The Factory*, a multimedia studio-nightclub where artists, musicians, drag performers, and superstars mingled, producing silkscreens, films, and happenings.
- Maintained a detached, cool aesthetic persona that provoked intense debate about the artist's role, intention, and complicity in consumer capitalism.
