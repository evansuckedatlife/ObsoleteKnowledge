## summary

**Paul Klee** (1879–1940) was a Swiss-German painter and pedagogue whose whimsical, experimental approach to color, line, and form made him one of the 20th century's most inventive artists. Working across abstraction, surrealism, and figuration, Klee created a visual language of infinite playfulness: childlike symbols, hieroglyphic marks, and prismatic color harmonies. His teaching at the [[bauhaus]] revolutionized art education by systematizing color and form into teachable principles, influencing generations of students and establishing the school as the 20th century's greatest design laboratory.

## you gotta know

- Born in Bern, trained as a musician; music's compositional logic informed his approach to visual form.
- Joined the [[bauhaus]] in 1921 and taught there until its closure by the Nazis in 1933; his courses became legendary.
- Developed what he called "taking a line for a walk"—the idea that a line's path could be playful, intuitive, and liberating.
- Used transparent watercolor and oil transfer techniques to layer color, creating luminous surfaces of ethereal beauty.
- Systematized color theory for students: primary color relationships, warm/cool contrasts, and color as emotional carrier in visual communication.
- Fled Nazi Germany in 1933 (his work was labeled "degenerate"); spent his final years in Bern, increasingly abstract and political.
- Created thousands of works; his prolific output combined high seriousness with a spirit of playful experimentation.
- His work merged musical structure with visual form, treating paintings as compositions where color, line, and space interacted like notes in musical harmony.

## connections

- [[bauhaus]] — the design school where he taught and revolutionized pedagogy through systematic exploration of form and color.
- [[walter-gropius]] — the Bauhaus founder who invited Klee and trusted his vision for design education.
- [[kandinsky]] — a fellow Bauhaus faculty member and fellow pioneer of abstract art.
- [[abstract-art]] — the movement he helped define through patient investigation of pure form and color.
- [[expressionism]] — the broader movement informing his emotional and painterly approach.
- [[germany]] — the Weimar culture that gave the Bauhaus and Klee intellectual freedom before Nazi suppression.
- [[color-theory]] — his systematization of color relationships became foundational pedagogy.
