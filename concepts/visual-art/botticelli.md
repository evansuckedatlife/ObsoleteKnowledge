## summary

**Sandro Botticelli** (1445–1510) was a Florentine painter whose elegant, decorative style defined the early Italian Renaissance. He is immortalised for two paintings—*The Birth of Venus* and *Primavera*—that revolutionised how Renaissance artists depicted beauty, mythology, and divine grace. Though he worked alongside Leonardo and Michelangelo, his influence operated through a distinctly different aesthetic: flowing lines, ethereal figures, and a spiritual intensity that made him a favourite of the ruling *Medici family* and the intellectual circles of Florence.

## you gotta know

- Born in Florence in 1445 as Alessandro di Mariano Filipepi; "Botticelli" was a nickname derived from his goldsmith brother's surname.
- Apprenticed under Filippo Lippi and later worked in the workshop of Verrocchio, the same master who trained Leonardo da Vinci, absorbing the technical foundations of Renaissance art.
- Painted *The Birth of Venus* (circa 1484–1486), an iconic tempera panel showing the goddess emerging from the sea on a shell, her golden hair and serene face epitomising Renaissance idealised beauty.
- Created *Primavera* (circa 1477–1482), an allegorical scene of spring with dancing figures and flowing drapery that captured the optimism and classical learning of the Medici court.
- Developed a distinctive linear style emphasising flowing contours and rhythmic movement rather than the sculptural solidity favoured by Michelangelo or the optical realism pioneered by Leonardo.
- Received major commissions from the Medici family, for whom he executed both secular mythologies and sacred altarpieces, cementing his position as Florence's court painter.
- Shifted his later work toward religious subjects, particularly scenes of the Virgin Mary, reflecting both personal piety and the rise of the Dominican friar Savonarola's moral influence over Florence.
- Executed *The Temptation of Saint Jerome* and other devotional works that showed deeper psychological drama, moving beyond the decorative surface of his mythological paintings.

## connections

- [[italian-renaissance]] — his work epitomises the period's synthesis of classical learning and Christian spirituality.
- [[leonardo-da-vinci]] — his contemporary and fellow student of Verrocchio; they represented contrasting Renaissance ideals (decoration vs. scientific observation).
- [[michelangelo]] — rival in achieving greatness in Florence; Michelangelo's sculptural solidity contrasts with Botticelli's flowing lines.
- [[the-renaissance]] — central figure whose mythological paintings helped establish the Renaissance reclamation of classical subjects.
- [[medici-family]] — his principal patrons, particularly Lorenzo the Magnificent, who commissioned his most famous works.
- [[perspective]] — mastered the Renaissance achievement of spatial recession while prioritising elegant line over mathematical exactitude.
- [[mona-lisa]] — Leonardo's portrait represents a competing vision of how Renaissance art handled psychological depth and naturalism.
