## summary

**The Renaissance** was the transformative cultural and artistic movement spanning roughly the 14th to 17th centuries, beginning in Italy and radiating across Europe. Rooted in the rediscovery of classical Greco-Roman texts and values, the Renaissance reoriented art, literature, and thought toward humanism, naturalism, and the celebration of individual genius. In visual art, it marked the shift from the flat, symbolic registers of medieval work to illusionistic representation, anatomical accuracy, and perspective—a revolution that defined Western art for centuries.

## you gotta know

- The Renaissance began in Italy (especially Florence and Rome) in the 14th century, where wealthy merchants and patrons like the [[medici-family]] sponsored artistic innovation.
- *Classical revival* was central: artists and scholars looked to ancient Rome and Greece as models for beauty, proportion, and human achievement.
- *Humanism* placed emphasis on individual worth, intellectual inquiry, and the study of ancient texts, reshaping both art and philosophy.
- Technological advances—particularly improved oil painting, the printing press, and developments in optical theory—enabled new artistic possibilities.
- The [[italian-renaissance]] produced Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael, whose works became paradigmatic for Western art.
- *Perspective*, [[anatomy]], and observational naturalism replaced the symbolic, flattened forms of medieval art.
- The Northern Renaissance (in Flanders, Germany, and beyond) developed parallel but distinct innovations, including oil painting and portraiture emphasis.
- The Renaissance gradually gave way to Mannerism and the Baroque as artistic priorities shifted in the 16th and 17th centuries.

## connections

- [[italian-renaissance]] — the geographically specific and historically originary form of the broader Renaissance movement.
- [[leonardo-da-vinci]] — the quintessential Renaissance polymath, embodying its ideals of genius and cross-disciplinary mastery.
- [[michelangelo]] — sculptor, painter, and architect whose works defined High Renaissance achievement.
- [[raphael]] — master of harmony and classical composition, epitomizing Renaissance ideals of balance and beauty.
- [[humanism]] — the intellectual foundation that shaped Renaissance thought and artistic priorities.
- [[perspective]] — the mathematical innovation that enabled Renaissance visual realism.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical movement that inherited and extended Renaissance rationalism.
