## summary

**Automatism** is a creative technique in which the artist suspends conscious control and rational intention, allowing the hand to move across the canvas or paper in response to impulse, intuition, or accident rather than deliberate design. Theorized by Surrealist leader *André Breton* as a path to the unconscious mind, automatism was meant to bypass the censoring rational intellect and access the raw material of dreams, desires, and repressed content. The practice embodied Surrealism's debt to psychoanalysis and its belief that authentic truth lay beneath conscious rationality. Automatism produced both visual works and literary text, and remains a foundational technique for any art that privileges spontaneity and chance over deliberation.

## you gotta know

- Theorized by *André Breton* in the *Surrealist Manifesto* (1924) as central to unlocking unconscious creativity.
- Rooted in Freudian psychoanalysis: the belief that the unconscious mind contains truths the rational mind suppresses.
- Opposite of deliberate composition: the artist abandons planning and allows gesture, accident, and impulse to guide the mark-making.
- *Max Ernst* embraced automatism through techniques like *frottage* (rubbing) and *grattage* (scraping), using tools and accident to generate unexpected forms.
- Produces images and marks that are often gestural, abstract, and psychologically suggestive rather than representational.
- Extends beyond visual art to automatic writing and drawing in Surrealist poetry and experimental literature.
- Later influenced *Abstract Expressionism*, whose gesture-based painting (drip, splatter, gestural mark-making) echoes automatism's embrace of spontaneity and the artist's bodily engagement.

## connections

- [[surrealism]] — the movement that theorized and championed automatism as a core practice.
- [[andre-breton]] — the Surrealist leader who articulated automatism as a path to the unconscious.
- [[max-ernst]] — the artist who developed technical elaborations of automatism through frottage and grattage.
- [[dada]] — the precursor movement that experimented with accident and non-intention.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — the theoretical framework underlying automatism's assumptions about the unconscious.
- [[abstract-expressionism]] — the movement influenced by automatism's embrace of gesture and spontaneity.
- [[chance-in-art]] — the related principle that accident and randomness can generate meaning.
