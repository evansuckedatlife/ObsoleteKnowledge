## summary

**Color theory** is the systematic study of how colors interact, combine, and affect perception and emotion. Far more than a practical guide to mixing pigments, it encompasses the physics of light, the psychology of human perception, and the conventions artists use to organize hues into harmonious relationships. Since the 17th century—particularly through scientists like Isaac Newton—color has been understood through frameworks of complementarity, temperature, and emotional association. Modern painters, from the Impressionists to Wassily Kandinsky, elevated color theory into a philosophical language.

## you gotta know

- *Primary, secondary, and tertiary colors* form the foundational color wheel—a system for understanding relationships and mixtures.
- *Complementary colors* (opposite on the wheel: red-green, blue-orange, yellow-purple) create maximum contrast and visual vibration when placed together.
- *Color temperature* divides the spectrum into warm (reds, yellows, oranges) and cool (blues, greens, purples) tones, affecting mood and spatial perception.
- **Saturation** (or chroma) measures the intensity or purity of a hue, while **value** measures its lightness or darkness; both independent of hue itself.
- [[Kandinsky]] developed a systematic theory linking specific colors to emotional and spiritual states—red for passion, blue for spirituality, yellow for joy.
- [[Paul Klee]] taught color theory at the Bauhaus and produced pedagogical works analyzing chromatic harmony and contrast.
- **Atmospheric perspective** uses color desaturation and cooler tones to represent distant objects, exploiting how air affects color perception.
- The [[Impressionists]] revolutionized painting by treating color as independent of local object color and by juxtaposing pure hues rather than mixing them on the palette.

## connections

- [[kandinsky]] — systematized the spiritual and emotional dimensions of color, treating it as an autonomous language.
- [[paul-klee]] — developed rigorous pedagogical approaches to color at the Bauhaus.
- [[painting]] — color is one of painting's primary materials and expressive tools.
- [[impressionism]] — used color juxtaposition and broken brushwork to explore light and perception.
- [[abstract-art]] — relies on color relationships as the primary source of meaning and structure.
- [[symbolism-visual-art]] — uses color associations to convey narrative and psychological content.
- [[bauhaus]] — taught color theory as foundational to design and visual literacy.
