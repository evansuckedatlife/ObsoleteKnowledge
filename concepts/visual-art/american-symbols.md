## summary

**American symbols** comprise the visual emblems, allegorical figures, monuments, and iconographic motifs created to embody the political values, sovereignty, and cultural aspirations of the *[[united-states]]*. Emerging during the *[[american-revolution]]* with classical allegories and heraldic seals, this visual repertoire expanded over two centuries to incorporate civic statuary, folk figures, and industrial emblems. These potent visual representations came to define how domestic citizens and international audiences perceive American ideals of liberty, democracy, and national destiny.

## you gotta know

- *Statue of Liberty* (*Liberty Enlightening the World*), designed by French sculptor *Frédéric-Auguste Bartholdi* and engineered by *Gustave Eiffel*, stands in *[[new-york-city]]* harbor as the world's most recognizable sculpted emblem of freedom and immigration.
- The *Great Seal of the United States*, adopted by the Continental Congress in 1782, established the *bald eagle* holding an olive branch and arrows, alongside the motto *E Pluribus Unum* and the *Eye of Providence*.
- *Uncle Sam*, a personification of the federal government linked folklore-wise to Troy meatpacker *Samuel Wilson*, achieved iconic visual permanence in *James Montgomery Flagg*'s famed *[[world-war-i]]* recruitment poster.
- The *Liberty Bell* in Philadelphia, rung by abolitionists and women's suffragists as a talisman of civil liberties, features a famous inscription from Leviticus: "Proclaim LIBERTY Throughout all the Land unto all the Inhabitants Thereof."
- *Columbia*, a classical female personification wearing neoclassical robes and a Phrygian cap, served as the primary female avatar of the nation prior to the widespread ascendancy of the *Statue of Liberty*.
- Monumental land art and civic works like *[[mount-rushmore]]* fused natural topography with national iconography, creating enduring pilgrimage sites of democratic leadership.
- In the twentieth century, postwar commercial art and *[[pop-art]]* reinterpreted national emblems into ubiquitous consumer icons, mass-media imagery, and critical visual commentaries.

## connections

- [[american-monuments]] — physical memorials and sculptures that enshrine national emblems in the public landscape.
- [[united-states]] — the republic whose constitution, founding ideals, and history these symbols represent.
- [[sculpture]] — the artistic discipline responsible for creating colossal symbolic figures like Bartholdi's *Statue of Liberty*.
- [[american-revolution]] — the foundational conflict that generated the nation's earliest anti-monarchical and republican iconography.
- [[mount-rushmore]] — the quintessential mountain monument transforming presidential likenesses into national icons.
- [[pop-art]] — the mid-century art movement that repurposed patriotic and commercial American symbols into high art.
- [[american-regionalism]] — an artistic movement that anchored American identity in native rural and Midwestern imagery.
