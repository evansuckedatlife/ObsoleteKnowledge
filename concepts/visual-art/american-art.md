## summary

**American art** encompasses the visual traditions and innovations developed in the United States, from colonial portraiture through contemporary practice, but most distinctly refers to the emergence of a recognizable American artistic identity in the 20th century. Often characterized by directness, attention to light and landscape, and engagement with distinctly American themes—urbanity, isolation, individualism, frontier mythology—American art established itself as a major force only after World War II, when [[abstract-expressionism]] and American realism shifted the center of global artistic gravity from Paris to New York. The tradition is defined less by formal coherence than by a recurring preoccupation with American experience and psychology.

## you gotta know

- American artistic identity developed relatively late compared to European traditions; much colonial and 19th-century American art remained derivative of European models.
- The 20th century witnessed a gradual assertion of American artistic independence, particularly through realism and abstract-expressionism.
- *Edward Hopper* became the iconic voice of American isolation and urban alienation, depicting empty diners, gas stations, and solitary figures with psychological intensity.
- [[abstract-expressionism]]—the post-WWII movement of drip painting, gestural abstraction, and scale—finally positioned American artists at the forefront of global modernism.
- American realism, by contrast, emphasized representation of distinctly American scenes: diners, highways, metropolitan life, small-town existence.
- The art often engages with themes of freedom, individualism, isolation, and the tension between urban modernity and pastoral longing.
- American collectors and museums (particularly the Museum of Modern Art in New York) became instrumental in promoting American art globally after WWII.

## connections

- [[edward-hopper]] — the quintessential voice of American urban isolation and psychological realism.
- [[american-realism]] — the representational tradition emphasizing American subjects and scenes.
- [[abstract-expressionism]] — the movement that established American art as globally dominant after WWII.
- [[modernism]] — the broader aesthetic movement within which American art found its voice.
- [[united-states]] — the national context and subject of American artistic preoccupation.
- [[urban-isolation]] — a recurring theme in American art, particularly in Hopper and his successors.
- [[nighthawks]] — the iconic painting that encapsulates American urban alienation.
