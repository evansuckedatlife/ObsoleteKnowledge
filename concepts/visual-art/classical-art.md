## summary

**Classical Art** encompasses the painting, sculpture, and decorative traditions of ancient Greece and Rome, emphasizing idealized human form, mathematical harmony, and rational beauty. Renaissance artists deliberately studied and revived these principles, making the classical aesthetic the foundation of Western visual culture and establishing enduring standards for representing the human body and architectural space.

## you gotta know

- Celebrated the idealized human figure—particularly male nudes—as the highest subject and vehicle for exploring proportion and movement; the *canon of proportions* was rational and teachable.
- Employed principles of *contrapposto* (the offset stance that conveys weight and naturalism) and three-dimensional volumetric form to achieve the appearance of life.
- Valued symmetry, balance, and the golden ratio as expressions of cosmic order and intellectual perfection; mathematical beauty was believed to be objective.
- Drew inspiration from Greek temples, Roman statuary, and recovered classical texts praising ancient artistic achievement; Renaissance scholars treated classical works as recovered wisdom.
- Sculpture (particularly in marble) was privileged over painting as the truest medium, reflecting ancient hierarchies; relief sculpture bridged two and three dimensions.
- Became the explicit model for High Renaissance masters who directly copied and drew from ancient sculpture, treating the classical past as a continuous living tradition.
- Persisted as the authoritative standard against which all subsequent movements—Baroque, Romantic, Modern—defined themselves as departures; to reject classicism was to define modernism itself.
- Museums, academies, and atelier training enshrined classical principles as the foundation of visual literacy and artistic mastery across centuries of Western education.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — originated the human-centered aesthetic and sculptural traditions.
- [[roman-empire]] — preserved and synthesized Greek forms, creating the classical vocabulary available to the Renaissance.
- [[italian-renaissance]] — the period of conscious classical revival and adaptation.
- [[raphael]] — the High Renaissance master who embodied classical ideals of harmony and balance.
- [[michelangelo]] — achieved classical perfection through anatomical study and idealized proportion.
- [[leonardo-da-vinci]] — investigated classical principles of form and perspective scientifically.
- [[neoclassicism]] — the 18th-century movement that returned to classical purity as reaction against Baroque excess.
