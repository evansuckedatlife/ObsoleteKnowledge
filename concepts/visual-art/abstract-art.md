# Abstract Art

## summary

**Abstract art** is non-representational visual art that abandons depiction of recognizable objects, figures, or scenes in favor of exploring pure form, color, line, shape, and composition as ends in themselves. Emerging in the early 20th century through movements like Cubism and Expressionism, abstract art asserts that visual meaning can arise from formal relationships alone—that a painting need not depict anything external to be profound, expressing instead the artist's emotions, spiritual truths, or investigations of perception and geometry.

## you gotta know

- Abstract art rejects the mimetic tradition of Western painting—the assumption that art's primary purpose is to represent visible reality; instead, it treats color, line, shape, and composition as the direct subject matter.
- *Wassily Kandinsky* (1866–1944) was a pioneer and theorist of abstract art; his geometric abstractions and color studies were grounded in spiritual and musical analogies—he believed colors and forms could evoke emotions directly, like musical notes.
- *Abstract expressionism* emerged in mid-20th-century America, exemplified by *Jackson Pollock*'s drip paintings and *Mark Rothko*'s color fields; these artists used spontaneity, gesture, and scale to express emotional or spiritual content.
- Abstract art encompasses diverse approaches: *geometric abstraction* uses rational forms (circles, grids, angles), while *gestural abstraction* emphasizes spontaneous mark-making and emotional expression; *color field* painting reduces art to relationships of color and tone.
- The movement includes [[Cubism]], which fragmented recognizable objects into geometric components viewed from multiple angles simultaneously, dissolving traditional perspective; though Cubism retained some reference to objects, it opened the path to total abstraction.
- *Suprematism* (Kazimir Malevich) reduced painting to the simplest forms (squares, circles, lines) and pure color relationships, seeking an essential visual language independent of representation.
- Abstract art provoked intense criticism: conservative critics called it incomprehensible, accidental, or a hoax; defenders argued it freed art from the constraints of representation and allowed direct expression of spiritual, intellectual, or emotional states.

## connections

- [[abstract-expressionism]] — a major movement within abstract art emphasizing gesture and emotional expression.
- [[cubism]] — the fragmentation of form that led toward abstraction and away from representation.
- [[surrealism]] — another early-20th-century movement that, while retaining recognizable dream imagery, rejected rational representation.
- [[kandinsky]] — pioneer theorist and practitioner of abstract art.
- [[pablo-picasso]] — major innovator of Cubism and early abstraction.
