# Der Blaue Reiter

## summary

**Der Blaue Reiter** (The Blue Rider) was a Munich-based association of artists active from 1911 to 1914, representing the second major movement within German Expressionism. Named after a painting by *Wassily Kandinsky*, the group prioritized spiritual abstraction, colour theory, and emotional expression over representational accuracy. Unlike the visceral angst of *Die Brücke*, Der Blaue Reiter sought transcendence through abstract form and mystical symbolism.

## you gotta know

- Founded in Munich in 1911 by *Wassily Kandinsky* and *Franz Marc*, who organized exhibitions and publications promoting their modernist vision.
- The name derives from *Kandinsky's* painting "The Blue Rider," where blue symbolized spirituality, transcendence, and the soul, while riders invoked apocalyptic renewal and transformation.
- Core members included *Kandinsky*, *Marc*, *August Macke*, *Paul Klee*, and *Alexei Jawlensky*—a diverse group united by spiritual aspirations rather than stylistic uniformity.
- Published the *Blaue Reiter Almanac* (1911–1912), a landmark manifesto blending visual art, music (including *Arnold Schoenberg's* atonal compositions), philosophy, and folk traditions into a unified vision.
- Emphasized colour and form as expressive vehicles independent of subject matter, believing non-objective art could communicate spiritual truths directly to the viewer's soul.
- The group believed art had a moral and spiritual mission; they sought to awaken modern consciousness through abstraction, primitivism, and emotional intensity.
- Dissolved by 1914 due to World War I; *August Macke* was killed in combat, and most members were conscripted or scattered by the conflict.
- Despite their brief existence (1911–1914), Der Blaue Reiter profoundly influenced abstract and expressionist art worldwide, establishing the principle that art could be purely non-representational.
- The group's philosophical ambition extended beyond visual art to music, dance, and theatre, seeking a total aesthetic and spiritual renewal across all arts.
- Der Blaue Reiter represented one of modernism's most utopian moments—the belief that abstract art could spiritually transform consciousness and society.
