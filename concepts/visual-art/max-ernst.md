## summary

**Max Ernst** (1891–1976) was a German-born Surrealist artist whose technical innovations and hallucinogenic imagery shaped the movement's visual language. Initially aligned with Dada's anarchic rejection of reason, he became one of Surrealism's most committed explorers of the unconscious, developing techniques like *frottage* (rubbing) and *grattage* (scraping) to bypass rational control and access the illogical depths of the mind.

## you gotta know

- Born in Cologne; participated in post-war Dada before pivoting to Surrealism in the mid-1920s, where *André Breton* championed his work as exemplary unconscious expression.
- Invented *frottage* (taking rubbings of textured surfaces to generate automatic imagery) and *grattage* (scraping paint across canvas) as methods to bypass conscious intention.
- Created dreamlike, often nightmarish paintings populated by hybrid creatures, biomorphic forms, and apocalyptic landscapes reflecting psychological turmoil and historical anxiety; his palette remained earthy and disturbing.
- More ideologically committed to Surrealist revolution than *Salvador Dalí*, viewing the movement as a philosophical weapon against rationalism and conformity; he rejected art for pleasure.
- Pioneered collage techniques that anticipated postwar assemblage art; his enigmatic, poetic titles (often found or adapted) added another layer of interpretive slipperiness and automatic meaning.
- Survived World War II in America; his late work remained deeply invested in primordial imagery and the eruption of unconscious material; the artist as visionary shaman.
- His insistence that accident and the uncontrolled mark reveal truth presaged postwar abstraction and *Action Painting*, making him a bridge between Surrealism and Abstract Expressionism.

## connections

- [[surrealism]] — the movement he helped define through technical innovation and psychological depth.
- [[dada]] — his anarchic precursor; Ernst maintained Dada's anti-rational spirit within Surrealism.
- [[salvador-dali]] — fellow Surrealist whose flamboyance contrasted with Ernst's more introverted intensity.
- [[andre-breton]] — Surrealism's theorist and Ernst's champion, though they eventually clashed over ideology.
- [[frottage]] — the automatic technique Ernst invented to access unconscious imagery.
- [[automatism]] — Ernst's embrace of accident and the uncontrolled mark as path to truth.
- [[germany]] — his birthplace and source of anxiety about fascism and destruction.
