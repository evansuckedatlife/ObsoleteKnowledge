## summary

**Renaissance art** was the visual and sculptural revolution that accompanied the Renaissance revival of classical learning, spanning roughly 1300–1600 and originating in Italy. Artists rediscovered techniques lost since antiquity—linear perspective, anatomical precision, naturalism—and reintegrated them with Christian theology to create new possibilities for representation. Renaissance sculptors and painters moved beyond medieval stylization toward convincing depictions of the human form, spatial depth, and emotional psychology, establishing visual standards that dominated Western art until modernism.

## you gotta know

- Began in Florence as artists studied Roman ruins and recovered classical proportional systems and sculptural ideals.
- [[linear-perspective]] (invented by Filippo Brunelleschi) enabled convincing spatial depth; artists could now depict three dimensions on flat surfaces.
- Emphasized anatomical accuracy achieved through life drawing and often cadaver dissection; human bodies became primary subject.
- Introduced secular and humanistic subjects alongside religious themes: classical mythology, portraiture, landscapes, still life.
- Sculpture pioneered by [[donatello]] and [[ghiberti]] recovered Roman contrapposto stance and naturalism in bronze and marble.
- Painting advanced through systematic use of oil paint; layered glazes and luminous color replaced tempera's flatness.
- The [[high-renaissance]] (1495–1520) represented the apex: masters like *Michelangelo*, *Raphael*, and *Leonardo da Vinci* perfected the vocabulary.
- Renaissance artists were often polymaths: painters trained in architecture, studied anatomy, conducted scientific observations—embodying the era's integrated intellectual ambition.
- The movement gradually spread north to the Low Countries, France, and Germany, adapting local traditions to Italian principles of naturalism and classical reference.
- Renaissance art established the standards for Western artistic training for centuries: life drawing, anatomy, perspective, and classical ideals became the curriculum.

## connections

- [[renaissance]] — the broader intellectual movement of which Renaissance art was the visual expression.
- [[italian-renaissance]] — the specific flowering centered in Italy, the movement's heartland.
- [[donatello]] — the sculptor who revolutionized bronze work and recovered classical form.
- [[ghiberti]] — pioneer of perspective and naturalism in relief sculpture.
- [[high-renaissance]] — the culminating phase when artistic achievement reached an apex.
- [[florence-cathedral]] — the architectural complex that symbolized Renaissance achievement and hosted its greatest artists.
- [[perspective]] — the mathematical breakthrough that enabled Renaissance spatial realism.
