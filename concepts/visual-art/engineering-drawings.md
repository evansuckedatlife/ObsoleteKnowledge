## summary

**engineering drawings** are standardized graphical representations used to convey technical specifications, physical dimensions, structural assemblies, and mechanical operations. Developed historically from artistic *perspective* and architectural drafting into a disciplined visual language during the *industrial-revolution*, they govern fabrication across architecture, engineering, and product design. Understanding their historical and visual trajectory bridges fine art traditions with applied technology, highlighting figures like *Leonardo da Vinci* who pioneered cross-sectional and exploded-view representations.

## you gotta know

- Defined by orthographic projection, cross-sections, and axonometric views to communicate precise scale and geometry without optical distortion.
- Pioneered in the *Italian Renaissance* by artists and polymaths, most notably *Leonardo da Vinci*, whose notebooks contain intricate sketches of water mills, flying machines, and siege weapons.
- Formalized mathematically by the French mathematician *Gaspard Monge* through descriptive geometry in the late eighteenth century, which was initially kept as a military secret.
- Central to the *industrial-revolution* in *England* and the *United States*, enabling interchangeable parts and standardized machine tooling through blueprint reproduction methods invented by *John Herschel*.
- Adopted by modernist art and design schools, such as the *Bauhaus* under *Walter Gropius*, which integrated technical drafting into curricula to unify fine art with industrial manufacturing.
- Influenced avant-garde art movements, including *Constructivism* and *De Stijl*, which celebrated mechanical draftsmanship, clean geometric drafting, and functional clarity.
- Modern drafting evolved from manual ink-on-vellum drawing boards to computer-aided design (CAD), maintaining the graphic conventions established in nineteenth-century workshops.

## connections

- [[leonardo-da-vinci]] — filled sketchbooks with foundational mechanical drafts, hydraulic diagrams, and machine designs.
- [[perspective]] — provides the geometric and mathematical basis from which parallel and orthographic projections developed.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformed drafting into a mandatory technical trade for mass production and engineering.
- [[bauhaus]] — merged artistic training with technical drawing and industrial design under *Walter Gropius*.
- [[frank-lloyd-wright]] — relied on precise architectural drawings and drafting methods to realize organic structures.
- [[constructivism]] — drew aesthetic inspiration from technical drafts, blueprints, and engineering structures.
- [[de-stijl]] — embraced the austere orthogonal lines and functional economy characteristic of technical drawings.
