## summary

**Kazimir Malevich** (1878–1935) was a Ukrainian-Russian avant-garde artist and theorist who created [[suprematism]], one of modernism's most radical movements. His *Black Square* (1915), a simple black square on white ground, became the icon of pure abstraction and conceptual art. Malevich reduced painting to its philosophical essence—the claims that art need not depict, that gesture could be meaningless, and that color and geometry alone sufficed for profound aesthetic experience.

## you gotta know

- Created the *Black Square* in 1915, a work that shocked and baffled the art world; it remains the symbol of radical abstraction.
- Developed Suprematism: the belief that supremacy lies in pure feeling, expressed through geometric abstraction alone.
- Moved through Cubism and Futurism before reaching abstraction, each style more radical than the last.
- Coined the term "Suprematism" and published *The Non-Objective World* (1927), his theoretical manifesto.
- Like many Soviet artists, faced suppression under Stalin; his work was deemed "formalist" and subversive.
- The *Black Square* was raised above a door in Malevich's studio, positioned like an icon—a spiritual gesture.
- His later geometric paintings explore endless variations of colored rectangles, striving for "pure feeling."
- Malevich saw Suprematism as a complete break from representation—not modernism, but a new artistic system rooted in pure sensation and zero-objectivity.
- Under Soviet rule, his radical abstraction was denounced as formalist and bourgeois; his work was hidden and nearly destroyed.
- The *Black Square* has been endlessly interpreted: some see it as the ultimate negation of art, others as the purest affirmation of painting's essence.

## connections

- [[suprematism]] — the movement he invented and defined through radical geometric abstraction.
- [[black-square]] — his masterwork and the most famous abstract painting of the early 20th century.
- [[constructivism]] — the Soviet avant-garde movement that competed with Suprematism for ideological dominance.
- [[abstract-art]] — the broader revolution he helped pioneer alongside Western artists.
- [[kandinsky]] — a fellow abstract pioneer who also sought spiritual truth through non-representation.
- [[soviet-union]] — the revolutionary state that initially celebrated then suppressed his work.
- [[minimalism]] — the later movement that drew on Suprematist reduction and geometric purity.
