## summary

**American Film Noir** (commonly termed **film noir** or **classic Hollywood noir**) designates a prominent cinematic cycle and visual style that flourished across Hollywood from the early 1940s through the late 1950s. Developing in the *[[united-states]]* amidst the trauma of *[[world-war-ii]]* and the anxieties of the emergent *[[cold-war]]*, it presented a bleak, morally compromised alternative to conventional studio escapism through cynical protagonists, corrupt institutions, and fatalistic plots. Its distinctive visual grammar, marked by heavy chiaroscuro lighting, unbalanced Dutch angles, and rain-drenched urban nights, fundamentally altered twentieth-century cinematic *[[modernism]]*.

## you gotta know

- Baptized retrospectively in 1946 by French film critic *Nino Frank* after viewing a succession of unreleased wartime Hollywood crime melodramas in Paris, a designation expanded by critics *Raymond Borde* and *Étienne Chaumeton*.
- Visually defined by the stark lighting and spatial distortion of *[[german-expressionism]]*, brought to California by European refugee directors including *Fritz Lang*, *Billy Wilder*, *Robert Siodmak*, and *Otto Preminger*.
- Chronologically bookended in classic scholarship by *John Huston*'s hardboiled detective milestone *The Maltese Falcon* in 1941 and *Orson Welles*'s grotesque border thriller *Touch of Evil* in 1958.
- Adapted extensively from hardboiled crime fiction and pulp literature, especially the grim novels of *Raymond Chandler*, *Dashiell Hammett*, *James M. Cain*, and *Cornell Woolrich*.
- Embellished by striking stylistic devices including extreme deep-focus photography, skewed camera orientations, heavy Venetian blind shadow motifs, and ubiquitous subjective voiceover narration.
- Subverted traditional romantic archetypes through the treacherous figure of the *femme fatale*, memorably incarnated by *Barbara Stanwyck* in *Double Indemnity* and *Jane Greer* in *Out of the Past*.
- Interrogated the corrosive psychological toll of postwar disillusionment, masculine vulnerability, domestic entrapment, and the hollow promises of *[[american-consumer-culture]]*.

## connections

- [[german-expressionism]] — provided the essential stylistic roots of dramatic shadow play, oblique geometry, and psychological dread.
- [[united-states]] — served as the industrial and social crucible where major studio systems developed the noir cycle.
- [[world-war-ii]] — triggered deep social disruption, veteran alienation, and psychological cynicism that defined the genre's tone.
- [[cold-war]] — intensified themes of institutional paranoia, suburban conformity, and moral betrayal throughout late-period noir.
- [[existentialism]] — shared a fundamental philosophical view of doomed human agency within an indifferent and corrupt cosmos.
- [[american-consumer-culture]] — exposed the lethal desperation and criminal greed sparked by postwar dreams of effortless affluence.
- [[expressionism]] — adapted non-naturalistic emotional distortion into photographic lighting conventions for commercial cinema.
