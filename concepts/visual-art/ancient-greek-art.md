## summary

**Ancient Greek art** spans the visual and material culture of the Hellenic world from the Geometric period through the Archaic, Classical, and Hellenistic eras, establishing aesthetic foundations that governed Western artistic traditions for millennia. Characterized by an obsessive pursuit of ideal human proportion, anatomical naturalism, and philosophical balance, Greek vase painting, architecture, and freestanding statuary revolutionized visual representation. Its canon deeply informed Roman copying traditions and was later revived during the *[[italian-renaissance]]* by masters such as *[[michelangelo]]*.

## you gotta know

- Divided chronologically into distinct stylistic periods: Geometric (c. 900–700 BCE), Archaic (c. 700–480 BCE), Classical (c. 480–323 BCE), and Hellenistic (c. 323–31 BCE).
- The transition from rigid Archaic *kouroi* and *korai* to the Classical style was marked by the invention of *contrapposto*, a dynamic asymmetrical stance first seen in the *Kritios Boy*.
- *Polykleitos* codified the ideal male anatomy and mathematical harmony in his theoretical treatise and bronze statue, the *Canon* (demonstrated in the *Doryphoros*).
- Classical architectural orders—Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian—dictated temple design, culminating in the *Parthenon* on the Athenian Acropolis, overseen by the sculptor *Phidias*.
- Although most surviving large-scale sculptures are Roman marble copies, the original Greek masterworks were predominantly cast in bronze via the lost-wax technique and brightly painted with polychrome pigments.
- Hellenistic sculpture abandoned classical serenity in favor of theatrical pathos, intense emotion, and virtuosic dynamism, producing masterpieces like the *Winged Victory of Samothrace*, *Laocoön and His Sons*, and the *Venus de Milo*.
- Greek vase painting developed from geometric silhouettes into narrative black-figure pottery pioneered in Corinth and Athens, followed by the more expressive red-figure technique developed around 530 BCE.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the Mediterranean civilization whose mythology, polis democracy, and philosophy shaped artistic production.
- [[sculpture]] — the primary three-dimensional medium through which Greek anatomical naturalism was perfected.
- [[michelangelo]] — the High Renaissance sculptor whose study of Hellenistic statues profoundly influenced his own monumental figures.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial culture that preserved Greek bronze originals through ubiquitous marble copies.
- [[italian-renaissance]] — the cultural revival that looked to classical Greco-Roman ideals of humanism and anatomy.
- [[perspective]] — early optical systems such as Greek *skenographia* laid early groundwork for architectural depth in painted scenery.
- [[renaissance]] — the pan-European rebirth of antiquity centered on classical Greek and Roman models.
