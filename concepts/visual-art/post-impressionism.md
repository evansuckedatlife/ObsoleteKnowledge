## summary

**Post-Impressionism** describes the diverse artistic movements that followed Impressionism in the 1880s–90s, abandoning the Impressionists' focus on light and optical sensation for expressive color, formal structure, and emotional or spiritual content. Though united only by their rejection of Impressionist methods, artists like *Van Gogh*, *Cézanne*, and *Gauguin* forged radically different visions that transformed painting and established the foundation for 20th-century modernism.

## you gotta know

- Post-Impressionist artists retained Impressionism's bright palette and broken brushwork techniques but subordinated optical accuracy to subjective expression, formal structural order, or symbolic meaning.
- *Vincent van Gogh* channeled tortured psychology through agitated, expressive brushwork and acid color, prefiguring *Expressionism*; his *Starry Night* is anxious romanticism.
- *Paul Cézanne* imposed geometric structure—cylinders, spheres, cones—underlying natural forms, pioneering the formal analysis that led directly to Cubism's fragmentation.
- *Paul Gauguin* pursued primitivism and flattened form, seeking spiritual authenticity beyond European civilization in Tahiti; he influenced Symbolism, Fauvism, and Modernism.
- Emphasized the artist's subjective inner vision over faithful representation; the canvas became a vehicle for feeling, idea, or philosophical statement rather than visual fact.
- Collectively demonstrated that Impressionism's success—capturing light and sensation—had been only one possible direction for modern painting; the true path to modernism lay in structure, emotion, and formal experimentation.
- United only by negation: what they *rejected* (Impressionist method). In diversity lay modernism's birth: no single dogma, only radical individual vision.
- Post-Impressionism's insistence on subjective truth over optical accuracy liberated 20th-century art from realist naturalism and opened the floodgates to abstraction, expressionism, and conceptual experiment worldwide.

## connections

- [[impressionism]] — the parent movement Post-Impressionism defined itself against and transcended.
- [[vincent-van-gogh]] — Post-Impressionism's most psychologically intense figure; his work embodies subjective expressionism.
- [[paul-cezanne]] — the structural genius whose formal analysis anticipated Cubism.
- [[paul-gauguin]] — the primitivist whose escape and symbolic imagery influenced Symbolism and Modernism.
- [[symbolism]] — a parallel movement sharing Post-Impressionism's rejection of naturalism.
- [[expressionism]] — the 20th-century heir to Post-Impressionist subjectivity.
- [[cubism]] — Cézanne's formal analysis directly inspired Cubist fragmentation.
