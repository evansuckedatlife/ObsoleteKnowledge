## summary

**Jan van Eyck** (c. 1390–1441) was a Flemish painter and pioneer of oil painting whose meticulous technique and revolutionary use of the medium transformed European painting. His works are characterized by microscopic detail, luminous color, and philosophical depth encoded in symbolic imagery, establishing the Northern European painting tradition as a sophisticated rival to Italian Renaissance achievement.

## you gotta know

- Born in what is now Belgium; served the court of Burgundy and gained international renown as the greatest Northern European painter of his era; his fame surpassed contemporary Italian masters.
- Did not "invent" oil painting but perfected its technical possibilities, building translucent glazes layer by layer to achieve unprecedented luminosity and color saturation; the medium's potential was fully realized through his mastery.
- Famous for his motto "Als Ik Kan" (As I Can), inscribed in his paintings—humble yet confident assertion of artistic achievement and personal signature.
- Excelled in portraiture, religious altarpieces, and small devotional panels; his *Ghent Altarpiece* (with his brother Hubert) is monumental in scope, ambition, and theological sophistication.
- Employed extreme linear perspective and spatial sophistication comparable to contemporary Italian Renaissance painters but with distinctly Northern sensibility and Burgundian courtly elegance.
- Every detail—fabric textures, reflection in polished armor, light through stained glass—rendered with illusionistic precision that served both aesthetic and symbolic purposes; *symbolism* encoded deep spiritual meaning in everyday objects.
- His technical virtuosity and symbolic depth made him a direct competitor to Italian Renaissance masters; Northern European painting was not derivative but autonomous and equal.
