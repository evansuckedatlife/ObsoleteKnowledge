# Henri Matisse

## summary

**Henri Matisse** (1869–1954) was a French painter, sculptor, and printmaker whose radical use of colour, expressive line, and simplified form made him one of the 20th century's most influential artists. A founder of Fauvism and a lifelong innovator, Matisse pursued beauty and joy through paint, pushing abstraction toward pure expression. His late paper cut-outs represent one of art history's most inventive reinventions, proving that an aging master could pioneer new aesthetic territory.

## you gotta know

- Born in Le Cateau-Cambrésis (1869); studied in Paris initially under conservative classical traditions before his radical embrace of pure colour liberated modern art.
- Co-founded Fauvism around 1905, unleashing unnaturalistic, emotionally expressive colour applied boldly and directly, causing scandal and outrage at the 1905 Salon d'Automne.
- His philosophy was revolutionary: colour is not descriptive or representational but inherently emotive—a vivid *red* expresses more truth about reality than a naturalistic flesh tone.
- Created works across diverse media: paintings, sculptures, prints, illustrated books, murals, and—in his final years (1950s)—monumental paper cut-outs combining colour, form, and spiritual transcendence.
- Late masterworks include *Jazz* (1947), a book illustrated with hand-cut and hand-painted coloured papers combining text and image; chapel designs at Vence (1949–1951) synthesizing architecture, stained glass, and decoration.
- Maintained a lifelong productive artistic dialogue with *Picasso*, each representing contrasting yet complementary modernist paths: Matisse colour and decorative harmony vs. Picasso's form and cubist fragmentation.
- Achieved international celebrity and major retrospectives by the 1930s; remained creatively experimental throughout his 80s, proving that aging artists could pioneer entirely new aesthetic territories.
- His philosophy that joy and beauty were valid artistic goals—against modernist severity—profoundly influenced postwar art, abstract expressionism, and contemporary approaches to colour and form.
- His late cut-outs proved that radical innovation and artistic reinvention were possible at any age; they simplified form to its essential elements while amplifying colour's spiritual power.
- Matisse remains an enduring model of artistic freedom: a master who transcended tradition, reinvented himself repeatedly, and demonstrated that pleasure and profundity are not mutually exclusive in art.
