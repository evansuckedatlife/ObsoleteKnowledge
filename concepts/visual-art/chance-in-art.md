## summary

**Chance in art**, often termed **aleatory art** or **aleatoricism in art**, is the deliberate introduction of randomness, accident, or uncontrolled natural processes into the creation, composition, or execution of an artwork. Developed as a radical philosophical and aesthetic challenge to classical Western notions of intentionality, academic composition, and individual mastery, it emerged prominently in the early twentieth century. By relinquishing conscious authorial control, artists opened visual and conceptual practices to the unconscious mind, industrial accident, and the unpredictable flux of reality.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a fundamental avant-garde strategy through the Cabaret Voltaire in Zurich, where Dadaist *Jean Arp* created collages arranged *according to the laws of chance* by dropping torn papers onto paper.
- Pioneered conceptually by *Marcel Duchamp*, who created *3 Standard Stoppages* (1913–1914) by dropping three one-meter threads from a height of one meter and fixing their curved shapes with varnish.
- Adopted by *André Breton* and the Surrealists through psychic automatism, automatic drawing, decalcomania, and collaborative parlor games such as the *cadavre exquis* (*exquisite corpse*).
- Underpinned the gestural painting techniques of *Jackson Pollock* and *Max Ernst*, who used dripping, pouring, and oscillated paint cans to invite centrifugal physics into pictorial design.
- Revolutionized twentieth-century music and performance through *John Cage*, who composed using the Chinese divination manual *I Ching* and coin tosses to strip musical structure of personal taste.
- Extended into post-war art via the *Fluxus* movement, kinetic art, and happenings, where audience participation and ambient conditions determined the final outcome of the work.
- Anticipated generative and computational digital art, where algorithms, noise generators, and stochastic software produce infinitely variable visual forms without direct manual intervention.

## connections

- [[dada]] — the anti-rationalist movement that first canonized random procedures to repudiate bourgeois civilization after the First World War.
- [[surrealism]] — the literary and visual vanguard that deployed chance mechanisms to bypass conscious repression and access the unconscious.
- [[abstract-expressionism]] — the post-war school whose action painters united physical bodily gesture with spontaneous splashing and pouring.
- [[modernism]] — the overarching intellectual movement that interrogated traditional forms of artistic authorship and representation.
- [[abstract-art]] — the non-representational tradition that embraced organic accidents, splatters, and mathematical indeterminacy.
- [[catherine-deneuve]] — the screen icon whose modern cinema directors utilized improvisational aleatory methods on location to destabilize classical performance.
- [[cesar-pelli]] — the master architect whose glass curtain walls embraced shifting ambient weather and atmospheric chance reflections.
