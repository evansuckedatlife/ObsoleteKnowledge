## summary

**Wassily Kandinsky** (1866–1944) was a Russian painter and theorist who pioneered abstract art, the radical notion that painting need not represent the visual world. His spiritual conviction that color and form possessed intrinsic emotional and musical qualities led him to create some of the first entirely non-objective paintings. His influential treatise *Concerning the Spiritual in Art* established abstraction as a philosophical movement, not merely a style.

## you gotta know

- Born in Moscow; trained as a lawyer before studying art in his late twenties, an unusually late start.
- Co-founded [[der-blaue-reiter]] (The Blue Rider) in Munich in 1911, one of Expressionism's most important groups.
- Created what many consider the first abstract paintings around 1910—non-representational compositions of color, line, and form.
- Developed a color theory that assigned emotional associations: yellow is aggressive, blue is spiritual, black is silent.
- Taught at the [[bauhaus]] in Weimar and Dessau during the 1920s, influencing generations of design and painting students.
- Fled Nazi Germany in 1933 and spent his final years in Paris, continuing to paint abstract works.
- Believed abstract art could express universal spiritual truths that representational art could never achieve.
- His treatise *Concerning the Spiritual in Art* (1911) remains a foundational text in art theory, arguing that color and form possess inherent emotional resonance.
- Kandinsky's color symbolism became systematic: red energetic, yellow sunny, blue spiritual and peaceful, black silence and negation.
- He bridged Russian avant-garde and German modernism, influencing both Constructivist ideology and Bauhaus pedagogy across two continents.
- Kandinsky's late works grew increasingly geometric and controlled, incorporating mathematical principles while maintaining their spiritual aspiration.
