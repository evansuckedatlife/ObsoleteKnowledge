## summary

**Neoclassicism** was an 18th-century artistic and architectural revival that sought to revive the ideals and forms of classical antiquity—Greece and Rome—as a reaction against the ornate excesses of Baroque and Rococo aesthetics. Driven by Enlightenment ideals of reason, order, and virtue, it emphasized clarity, symmetry, and moral seriousness. The movement profoundly shaped not only visual art but also architecture, literature, and political ideology, particularly in the context of revolution and nation-building.

## you gotta know

- Emerged in the mid-18th century as a conscious counter-movement to Baroque and Rococo excess and frivolity.
- Grounded in Enlightenment philosophy: the belief that reason, clarity, and virtue were the highest ideals worth pursuing.
- Privileged *linear* clarity, geometric symmetry, and restrained color palettes over the dynamism and ornamentation of preceding styles.
- Featured subjects drawn from classical mythology and history, chosen for their moral and civic virtue—heroes, republics, duty, sacrifice.
- Became the official aesthetic of revolutionary governments: the French Revolution adopted it; neoclassical style dominated Washington, D.C., and American public architecture.
- Key practitioners included *Jacques-Louis David*, whose *The Oath of the Horatii* became a manifesto for the movement's moral seriousness.
- Extended to architecture and design: the orders of classical grammar (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian) were restored to dominance in public buildings.
- Waned in the mid-19th century as Romanticism reasserted emotion and individualism, though neoclassical civic architecture continues to dominate governmental institutions worldwide.
