# Functionalism

## summary

**Functionalism** in visual art and design is the principle that aesthetic form should be derived entirely from practical function—that ornament divorced from use is superfluous and that beauty emerges from the honest expression of an object's or building's purpose and materials. The doctrine animated the [[Bauhaus]] school, modernist architecture, and industrial design from the early 20th century onward, radically rejecting Victorian ornamentation and reshaping how humans design and inhabit spaces through an ethic of clarity, economy, and utility.

## you gotta know

- The maxim *"form follows function"* encapsulates functionalism; it asserts that the shape of a building, chair, or tool should be determined solely by what it does, not by decorative tradition or aesthetic convention.
- The [[Bauhaus]] school (1919–1933), founded by [[Walter Gropius]], made functionalism its core doctrine: craft and art should unite with industrial production; design should be rational, economical, and serve the masses rather than an elite.
- *[[Le Corbusier]]* exemplified architectural functionalism: his buildings feature stark geometric forms, open floor plans, and materials (concrete, steel, glass) exposed rather than hidden; his notion of "machines for living" reduced buildings to their essential functional elements.
- Functionalism rejected *Victorian ornamentation*—applied decoration, fussy details, and forms serving no structural or practical purpose were deemed dishonest and wasteful; clean lines and unadorned surfaces became markers of modernist integrity.
- In *furniture design*, functionalism produced iconic objects: Ludwig Mies van der Rohe's Barcelona chair and Wassily chair use minimal materials arranged for maximum functionality and comfort; ornamentation is absent, yet the objects are widely recognized as beautiful.
- Functionalism extends to *graphic design* and *typography*: sans-serif typefaces (like Helvetica) emerged from functionalist principles—every line serves legibility, decoration is eliminated, information hierarchy is clarified through scale and spacing.
- *Industrial design* embraced functionalism as the path to economic mass production: standardized parts, minimal waste, and designs optimized for manufacturing reduced costs and made well-designed products accessible to ordinary people.

## connections
