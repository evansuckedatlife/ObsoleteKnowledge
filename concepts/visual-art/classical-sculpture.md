## summary

**Classical sculpture** refers to the sculptural traditions of ancient Greece and Rome, characterized by idealized human forms, geometric proportion, and the pursuit of eternal beauty through stone (particularly marble). Developed over centuries, classical sculpture emphasized the human body as the highest subject and standard of beauty, employing techniques like *contrapposto* (weight shift) to create naturalistic yet idealized figures. From the archaic period through the Hellenistic and Roman eras, classical sculpture established formal and aesthetic principles that dominated Western art for over two millennia, influencing everything from Renaissance masters to contemporary figurative practice. The discipline treats marble as a noble material and carving as the supreme sculptural technique.

## you gotta know

- Began in Archaic Greece (c. 700–480 BCE) with rigid, frontal figures and evolved toward increasing naturalism through the Classical period.
- The Classical period (c. 480–323 BCE) achieved the idealized synthesis: proportioned bodies, idealized faces, and spiritual rather than literal representation.
- Developed formal principles including *contrapposto* (S-curve weight distribution for naturalism), *contrapposto*, and the pursuit of the ideal male or female form.
- *Marble* became the preferred material, particularly white marble from Greece (Pentelicon) and Italy (Carrara), prized for its luminosity and workability.
- The male *nude* became the supreme subject: athletes, gods, heroes—treated as universal ideals of beauty and virtue.
- Roman sculpture copied and adapted Greek originals but added portraiture, commemorative monuments, and architectural relief sculpture to the tradition.
- Dominated Western aesthetic ideals through the Renaissance, when *Michelangelo* and others consciously revived classical principles and directly studied antique sculpture.
