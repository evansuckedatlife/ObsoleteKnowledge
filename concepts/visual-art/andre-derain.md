## summary

**André Derain** was a French painter, sculptor, and co-founder of Fauvism alongside *[[henri-matisse]]*. Working in early twentieth-century *[[france]]*, his landscape canvases and river views epitomized the movement's radical deployment of unmodulated, non-naturalistic color and energetic brushwork. Following World War I, he turned decisively toward traditional classicism and museum-inspired realism, making his complex artistic trajectory central to the shifting debates of European *[[modernism]]*.

## you gotta know

- Co-founded Fauvism with *[[henri-matisse]]* during a transformative summer spent painting together in 1905 at the Mediterranean fishing village of Collioure, developing an explosive chromatic style that shocked the Parisian art world.
- Exhibited at the notorious 1905 Salon d'Automne in Room VII, where art critic Louis Vauxcelles famously lamented that Donatello was standing among wild beasts (*fauves*), unintentionally naming their revolution.
- Traveled to London on commission from art dealer Ambroise Vollard between 1906 and 1907 to produce thirty vibrant Thames cityscapes, including *Charing Cross Bridge* and *Houses of Parliament*, responding directly to Claude Monet's earlier Impressionist views.
- Created *The Dance* (1906), a monumental allegorical composition combining luminous, non-naturalistic pigments with motifs inspired by African masks, Romanesque sculpture, and folk art.
- Maintained a close working dialogue with *[[pablo-picasso]]* and Georges Braque in Montmartre, experimenting briefly with geometric structure and early *[[cubism]]* before rejecting pure abstraction.
- Pivoted toward classical order, archaic sculpture, and seventeenth-century French *[[realism]]* after serving in the artillery during *[[world-war-i]]*, embracing a conservative return to order (*retour à l'ordre*).
- Compromised his critical standing and postwar legacy when he participated in a 1941 official cultural propaganda tour of Nazi Germany organized by sculptor Arno Breker during the occupation of *[[france]]* in *[[world-war-ii]]*.

## connections

- [[henri-matisse]] — lifelong colleague and friend with whom he initiated the Fauvist color revolution at Collioure.
- [[pablo-picasso]] — avant-garde peer in Paris whose interest in non-Western tribal artifacts and early *[[cubism]]* paralleled Derain's experiments.
- [[painting]] — central expressive medium through which he pioneered pure color saturation and later classical composition.
- [[france]] — native country where he worked, bridging the Parisian avant-garde with centuries of national artistic tradition.
- [[modernism]] — early twentieth-century movement whose radical emergence and subsequent classical reassessments he reflected.
- [[expressionism]] — European aesthetic movement whose practitioners adapted the emotive, anti-naturalistic colors pioneered by Fauvism.
- [[animal-symbolism]] — thematic device used by contemporary Expressionists who, like Derain, abandoned natural hues to express spiritual vitality.
- [[andrea-del-verrocchio]] — Renaissance master whose disciplined studio draftsmanship and bronze modeling inspired Derain's postwar classical turn.
