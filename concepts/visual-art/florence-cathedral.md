# Florence Cathedral

## summary

**Florence Cathedral** (formally Santa Maria del Fiore, colloquially "the Duomo") is a masterpiece of Italian Gothic and early Renaissance architecture located in Florence, Italy, begun in 1296 and completed in 1469. Its most iconic feature is [[Filippo Brunelleschi]]'s revolutionary dome (completed 1436), an engineering marvel that crowned the vast octagonal lantern; the cathedral was a symbol of Florence's civic pride and mercantile wealth, attracting the Renaissance's greatest sculptors and architects and serving as the setting for major religious and civic ceremonies.

## you gotta know

- The cathedral was designed by *Arnolfo di Cambio* in 1296 as a symbol of Florence's growing power and wealth; its vast size was ambitious—the empty space above the octagonal crossing was so wide that architects had no solution for covering it for over a century.
- *Filippo Brunelleschi* resolved the dome problem (1419) with an ingenious double-shell construction using herringbone-patterned brick, revolutionary at the time; the dome has no supporting internal framework and remains a triumph of engineering and geometry.
- The cathedral's facade is clad in *polychrome marble*—white, green, and pink—creating a striking geometric pattern; the original facade was partially completed by Giotto (1334) and later completed in the 19th century.
- *Giotto's Campanile* (bell tower), adjoining the cathedral, is a freestanding tower of distinctive geometric marble bands; designed by Giotto and completed by Andrea Pisano, it is one of Florence's most recognizable monuments.
- The cathedral's *interior* was a canvas for Renaissance artists: *Dante Alighieri*, author of the Divine Comedy, was honored with a monument inside; frescoes by *Paolo Uccello* and *Andrea del Castagno* adorn the walls.
- The cathedral was the site of *major Renaissance ceremonies*: the coronation of popes, the beatification of saints, and celebrations of military victories; it was the spiritual and symbolic heart of Florence.
- The bronze *doors* (created by Ghiberti and others) are masterpieces of Renaissance sculpture; Ghiberti's north doors (1452) depict biblical scenes with unprecedented realism and perspective, influencing Renaissance art profoundly.
