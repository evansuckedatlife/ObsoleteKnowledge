## summary

**Vincent van Gogh** (1853–1890) was a Dutch Post-Impressionist painter whose tormented psychology, radical use of color, and feverish brushwork transformed painting into a vehicle for subjective emotional expression. Though he worked for only a decade and died in poverty and obscurity, he created thousands of works—paintings, drawings, letters—that prefigured Expressionism and modern art's liberation from naturalism. His legacy demonstrates that art need not transcribe optical reality to communicate profound truth; instead, it can distort, intensify, and express the interior life of the maker.

## you gotta know

- Born in Groot-Zundert, Netherlands, Van Gogh worked multiple jobs (missionary, laborer, clerk) before becoming an artist in his late twenties, burning with intensity and a desperate desire for meaningful work.
- *The Potato Eaters* (1885) was his first masterwork—a dark, empathetic painting of peasant laborers rendered with unflinching realism but powerful psychological presence, establishing his commitment to expressing inner life through paint.
- Moved to Paris (1886) where he encountered Impressionism and Post-Impressionist colleagues, particularly *Paul Gauguin*, whose symbolic use of color and form profoundly influenced his developing style.
- Developed a personal color theory in which hues express emotion directly: yellows for warmth and spiritual truth, blues for sorrow, reds and greens for passionate conflict—color as language, not as optical accuracy.
- His brushwork became increasingly urgent and visible, with agitated, directional strokes that seem to convey psychological turbulence and emotional intensity; paint itself became an expressive medium, not merely a vehicle for representation.
- Painted obsessively during his Arles period (1888–89)—sunflowers, irises, starry nights, self-portraits—producing some of Western art's most iconic works in a state of feverish inspiration and psychological crisis.
- His mutilation of his own ear in 1888 and subsequent psychiatric hospitalization became inseparable from his legend, but his letters (over 800, primarily to his brother *Theo*) reveal his intellectual sophistication and conscious artistic philosophy.
- Died in apparent suicide at age 37, leaving behind nearly 2,000 works that were completely unknown to the public. His influence on 20th-century art—Expressionism, Fauvism, modern psychology—became immeasurable posthumously.
- His work demonstrates that artistic genius need not produce technically polished or naturalistic images; instead, authenticity of feeling and radical formal innovation matter more than academic skill or social success.

## connections

- [[post-impressionism]] — the movement Van Gogh epitomized; his work is Post-Impressionism's most psychologically intense expression.
- [[expressionism]] — the 20th-century movement he essentially invented through his use of distortion, color, and visible gesture to express emotion.
- [[paul-gauguin]] — fellow Post-Impressionist and close friend whose symbolic color use influenced Van Gogh's own theories.
- [[paul-cezanne]] — contemporary Post-Impressionist whose formal analysis diverged from Van Gogh's emotional approach, yet both prefigured modernism.
- [[netherlands]] — Van Gogh's birthplace, whose light, landscape, and Protestant culture shaped his sensibility throughout his life.
- [[france]] — where he produced his masterworks, finding artistic freedom and community (however fraught) in Paris and southern France.
- [[color-theory]] — Van Gogh's deliberate, idiosyncratic use of color to express emotion rather than optical fact established new possibilities for color in art.
