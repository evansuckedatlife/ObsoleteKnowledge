## summary

**Paul Gauguin** was a leading French [[post-impressionism|Post-Impressionist]] artist whose pioneering Synthetist aesthetic and radical abandonment of Western artistic conventions revolutionized late nineteenth-century [[painting]]. Abandoning his bourgeois career as a Parisian stockbroker, he traveled extensively to Martinique, Brittany, and ultimately French Polynesia, seeking an uncorrupted creative freedom away from European industrialization. His dramatic use of non-naturalistic color, flattened pictorial planes, and mythic subject matter broke decisively from [[impressionism]] and laid the vital conceptual foundations for modern [[symbolism]], [[expressionism]], and broader twentieth-century [[modernism]].

## you gotta know

- Painted famous Polynesian masterpieces such as *Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going?* and *Spirit of the Dead Watching*, which depicted Tahitian life, spiritual folklore, and existential questions through intensely saturated, decorative palettes.
- Lived and worked alongside [[vincent-van-gogh]] in the southern French town of Arles in 1888, an explosive creative collaboration that disintegrated after nine weeks and culminated in [[vincent-van-gogh|Van Gogh]] severing part of his own left ear during a mental breakdown.
- Formulated the style known as Synthetism alongside *Émile Bernard* at the Pont-Aven artist colony in Brittany, utilizing Cloisonnism to enclose flat patches of pure, unshaded pigment within dark, heavy outlines as seen in *Vision After the Sermon* and *The Yellow Christ*.
- Relinquished a lucrative career as a stockbroker, abandoning his Danish wife *Mette Gad* and five children in the wake of the 1882 Paris financial collapse to dedicate himself entirely to avant-garde [[painting]].
- Promoted an idealized, romanticized Primitivism that envisioned indigenous cultures as innocent and spiritually pure, though his self-styled exile to Tahiti and the Marquesas was deeply entangled with French colonial power and personal exploitation.
- Pioneered revolutionary work in non-canvas media, creating rustic, hand-carved [[sculpture]] in wood and ceramic alongside experimental, roughly textured woodcut prints for his autobiographical travelogue *Noa Noa*.
- Spent his final years in impoverished, combative isolation on the remote island of Hiva Oa in the Marquesas, defying local colonial administrators and Catholic missionaries while suffering from advanced syphilis before his death in 1903.

## connections

- [[vincent-van-gogh]] — companion and fellow Post-Impressionist whose intense creative partnership at the *Yellow House* ended in tragic psychological collapse.
- [[paul-cezanne]] — contemporary pioneer whose radical restructuring of spatial depth and brushwork complemented his own break from traditional naturalism.
- [[symbolism]] — movement whose poets and theorists celebrated his rejection of optical description in favor of emotional, mystical, and literary themes.
- [[expressionism]] — twentieth-century movement whose founders drew immense inspiration from his subjective, non-mimetic palette and raw visual rhythms.
- [[pablo-picasso]] — avant-garde master whose early Iberian and African periods, leading toward [[cubism]], were catalyzed by his monumental carvings and paintings.
- [[sculpture]] — medium through which he merged Oceanic woodcraft with European traditions to create tactile, unpolished reliefs.
- [[france]] — nation whose metropolitan art market he fled, yet whose imperial network enabled his travels to the South Pacific.
