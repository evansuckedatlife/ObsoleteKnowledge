## summary

**Cultural nationalism** is an ideological movement asserting that a nation possesses an organic, distinct collective identity defined by shared history, language, cultural heritage, and aesthetic traditions rather than by mere political citizenship. Crystallizing during the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, particularly across Romantic-era *Europe*, it fundamentally shaped the creation of national museums, academic salons, and heritage preservation institutions. In art history and modern museum studies, the concept underpins foundational debates regarding the ownership of ancient antiquities, the institutional ethics of imperial plunder, and state-directed visual culture.

## you gotta know

- It provided the theoretical rationale for establishing major public institutions like the *Louvre* in [[france]] and the *British Museum* in the [[british-empire]], positioning them as encyclopedic custodians of global civilization.
- Central to modern international legal, ethical, and diplomatic controversies surrounding cultural repatriation, including ongoing restitution claims for the *Parthenon Marbles* and the *Benin Bronzes*.
- Influenced the political affiliations and social estrangements of prominent nineteenth-century artists, notably driving [[degas]] to embrace staunch anti-Dreyfusard patriotism during the *Dreyfus Affair*.
- Championed by German Romantic philosophers such as *Johann Gottfried von Herder*, whose concept of the *Volksgeist* inspired avant-garde collectives like [[die-brucke]] to reclaim medieval German woodcut traditions.
- Directly opposed in contemporary cultural heritage law by cultural internationalism, a competing doctrine arguing that cultural property belongs to a shared universal human heritage rather than individual sovereign states.
- Sparked widespread architectural and decorative revivals throughout nineteenth-century *Europe*, such as the *Gothic Revival* in [[england]], as nations sought native vernacular idioms to resist classical Greco-Roman hegemony.
- Co-opted by twentieth-century totalitarian regimes in [[germany]] and the [[soviet-union]] to enforce aesthetic uniformity and purge modernism as alien or degenerate.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — established imperial extractive networks that gathered foreign artifacts into Western national museum collections.
- [[degas]] — adopted an aggressive nationalist stance during the *Dreyfus Affair*, defending the honor of the French military over individual civil liberties.
- [[die-brucke]] — sought an authentic German visual vocabulary by looking back to Northern Renaissance woodcuts and Gothic emotionalism.
- [[enlightenment]] — generated cosmopolitan models of universal human knowledge that later Romantics reacted against with localized cultural identities.
- [[british-empire]] — accumulated cultural artifacts across global territories to substantiate imperial narratives of historic supremacy.
- [[france]] — pioneered the post-revolutionary model of the public museum as an instrument of national identity and civic instruction.
- [[germany]] — developed influential Romantic theories of language, folklore, and visual art as the organic expressions of a distinct people.
