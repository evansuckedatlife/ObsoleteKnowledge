## summary

Coalescing as an international movement during the mid-1960s, **conceptual art** (frequently designated as **conceptualism**) contends that the foundational concept, intellectual premise, or generative system of an artwork takes precedence over its physical execution, craftsmanship, or material presence. Descended directly from the anti-aesthetic provocations of [[dada]], the movement launched a systematic critique of formalist connoisseurship and unique luxury commodities. By substituting texts, ephemeral performances, maps, and photographic records for conventional objects, it fundamentally redefined the ontology of modern artistic practice.

## you gotta know

- Sol LeWitt provided the movement's defining theoretical touchstone in his 1967 essay *Paragraphs on Conceptual Art*, asserting that "the idea becomes a machine that makes the art" and positioning manual execution as an incidental aftermath.
- The movement traced its lineage directly to Marcel Duchamp and his early twentieth-century readymades, particularly his 1917 *Fountain*, which established that artistic significance can stem entirely from institutional framing and conceptual selection.
- Joseph Kosuth visualized the linguistic and semiotic concerns of the movement in his landmark 1965 installation *One and Three Chairs*, juxtaposing an ordinary wooden chair, a full-scale photograph of that chair, and a dictionary definition of the word "chair."
- Robert Rauschenberg presaged conceptual strategies in 1953 with his *Erased de Kooning Drawing*, spending weeks erasing an intricate, layered drawing by Willem de Kooning to produce a piece constituted entirely by deliberate physical absence and intellectual provocation.
- Gallerist and publisher Seth Siegelaub pioneered radical presentation models for dematerialized art, creating exhibitions where printed catalogues, documentation portfolios, and checklists served as the primary exhibition site rather than gallery walls.
- John Baldessari combined institutional critique with deadpan humor, carrying out his 1970 *Cremation Project* (in which he incinerated all paintings he created between 1953 and 1966) and producing ironic prints repeating pedagogical maxims.
- Conceptual practitioners frequently authored written instructions or mathematical formulas, enabling works such as LeWitt's *Wall Drawings* to be installed, scrubbed away, and repainted by professional drafters across different international venues.
- Although conceived in part as an ideological attempt to circumvent market [[commercialism]] by producing uncommodifiable ephemera, dealers and major museums rapidly found avenues to commercialize conceptual contracts, photographic proof, and installation certificates.

## connections

- [[dada]] — historical avant-garde movement whose embrace of readymades, chance operations, and institutional subversion laid the bedrock for conceptualism.
- [[modernism]] — prevailing cultural paradigm whose formalist doctrines of medium-specificity conceptualists deliberately challenged and superseded.
- [[commercialism]] — capitalist economic framework that conceptual artists attempted to subvert by dematerializing tangible, collectible art commodities.
- [[pop-art]] — contemporaneous movement that shared conceptualism's emotional detachment and engagement with mass media while focusing on consumer imagery.
- [[abstract-art]] — artistic lineage whose pure opticality and insistence on visual autonomy prompted conceptualism's pivot toward linguistic and systemic inquiry.
- [[color-in-art]] — formal property that conceptualists stripped of emotional resonance, treating it instead as arbitrary linguistic nomenclature or industrial code.
- [[new-york-city]] — central hub of the postwar art world where alternative spaces, experimental galleries, and key conceptual publications flourished.
