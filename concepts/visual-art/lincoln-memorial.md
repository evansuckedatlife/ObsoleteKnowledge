## summary

The **Lincoln Memorial** is a monumental tribute to *Abraham Lincoln* completed in 1922 on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., designed by architect *Henry Bacon* with its iconic sculpture by *Daniel Chester French*. The seated Lincoln figure, carved from Georgia marble and towering 19 feet tall, has become the visual symbol of Lincoln's legacy and American ideals of emancipation and democracy. The memorial's classical Doric temple design evokes timeless authority; its symbolic placement anchoring the west end of the Mall made it the natural stage for momentous gatherings, most famously *Martin Luther King Jr.*'s 1963 "I Have a Dream" speech.

## you gotta know

- The sculpture's seated Lincoln depicts him in contemplation, hands resting on the chair's arms—a pose suggesting both strength and thoughtfulness.
- Designed by *Henry Bacon* in the Beaux-Arts style, modeled after the Parthenon, projecting eternal grandeur and democratic ideals.
- Constructed of Georgia white marble, Tennessee pink marble, and Colorado Yule marble—materials chosen for their durability and symbolic purity.
- The massive bronze statue was sculpted by *Daniel Chester French*, one of America's greatest sculptors; it weighs 175 tons.
- Located at the western terminus of the National Mall, directly across from the Capitol, creating a symbolic dialogue about Lincoln's place in American history.
- Site of Martin Luther King Jr.'s "I Have a Dream" speech (1963), forever linking the memorial to the Civil Rights Movement and visions of equality.
- The interior walls inscribe Lincoln's Gettysburg Address and his Second Inaugural Address, framing the figure with his own words on union and freedom.
