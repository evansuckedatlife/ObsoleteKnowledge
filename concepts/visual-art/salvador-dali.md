## summary

**Salvador Dalí** (1904–1989) was a Spanish painter, filmmaker, and provocateur who became the public face of [[surrealism]], despite ideological tensions with the movement's founder *André Breton*. Dalí's "paranoiac-critical method"—a technique of deliberate irrationality to access the unconscious—produced hallucinatory paintings of melting clocks, mutated landscapes, and doubles. His technical mastery, showmanship, and unapologetic embracing of kitsch and commerce made him modernism's most famous and commercially successful artist, though also the most controversial.

## you gotta know

- Born in Catalonia, Spain; trained in classical technique before discovering Surrealism's dreamlike possibilities.
- Developed the "paranoiac-critical method": induce a paranoid state to access irrational associations and unconscious truths.
- *The Persistence of Memory* (1931)—featuring melting watches in a hallucinatory landscape—became Surrealism's most iconic image.
- Excelled at painting dreams: impossible spatial logic, fused forms, uncanny juxtapositions that seem both beautiful and disturbing.
- Became a celebrity personality: surrealist outfits, theatrical public appearances, trademark mustache; art and self-promotion merged.
- Embraced commercial art, film, advertising, and fashion—anathema to purist modernists who saw mass culture as corrupting.
- Controversial among Surrealists for his monarchist and religious turns; Breton eventually expelled him from the movement.
- Dalí's technical skill—his ability to render dreamscapes with photographic precision—gave his hallucinatory visions an uncanny credibility.
- He remained prolific and commercially successful even after Surrealism's decline, crossing into fashion, jewelry, and film design throughout the mid-20th century.
- Dalí's legacy remains contested: celebrated as a master of technique and imagination, criticized for commercialism and narcissistic self-promotion.

## connections

- [[surrealism]] — the movement he epitomized visually, though his eclecticism and commerce troubled its ideologues.
- [[the-persistence-of-memory]] — his masterwork and the most recognizable Surrealist painting.
- [[andre-breton]] — Surrealism's founder, who championed then rejected Dalí over philosophical disagreements.
- [[max-ernst]] — a fellow Surrealist painter, though more committed to the movement's revolutionary ideology.
- [[spain]] — his birthplace, where he eventually retreated and continued painting in a theatrical mansion.
- [[dada]] — Surrealism's precursor movement; Dalí emerged as Dada was dissolving.
- [[pop-art]] — the movement that would later embrace commercial imagery, vindicating Dalí's controversial choices.
