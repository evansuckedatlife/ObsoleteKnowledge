## summary

**Painting** is the foundational visual art form: the application of pigment—via brush, palette knife, or other tool—to a prepared surface (typically canvas, wood panel, or wall) to create image, pattern, or abstract composition. As one of humanity's oldest practices, painting bridges ritual, craft, and concept; it has been the dominant vehicle for Western visual art from the [[renaissance]] onward. From representational portraiture to abstract color-field works, painting's flexibility—its capacity to capture light, emotion, and invisible ideas—has made it the medium through which countless movements from [[impressionism]] to [[surrealism]] have articulated their aesthetic and philosophical visions.

## you gotta know

- Humans have painted for tens of thousands of years—cave paintings, ancient Egyptian tomb walls, medieval illuminated manuscripts.
- Western tradition distinguishes painting from drawing and sculpture, though boundaries blur in contemporary practice.
- Painting's unique advantage is its capacity to render light, texture, and translucency through layered pigment and glaze.
- [[perspective]] and anatomical precision were painting's grand achievements in the [[renaissance]], enabling illusionistic depth.
- The invention of oil paint (15th century) transformed the medium, allowing rich color, slow drying, and revisability.
- Photography's invention (1839) challenged painting's documentary function, liberating it toward abstraction and emotional expression.
- Painting remains economically and culturally central: original paintings command vast prices; museums organize around them.
- Digital and photographic media challenged painting's hegemony in the late 20th century, yet painting persists as a foundational medium for artistic experimentation and expression.
- Painting's material properties—the texture of brushstrokes, layering of pigment, interaction of color—cannot be fully replicated in reproduction and remain central to the medium's appeal.

## connections

- [[sculpture]] — three-dimensional counterpart; painting shares exhibition space and historical narratives with sculpture.
- [[drawing]] — the linear precursor; most paintings begin with drawn underdrawing.
- [[color-theory]] — the systematic study of hue, saturation, and emotional association that painters exploit.
- [[perspective]] — the mathematical system that enabled Renaissance painting's illusionistic revolution.
- [[renaissance]] — the period when painting became the dominant artistic medium in Western culture.
- [[impressionism]] — the movement that liberated painting from strict representationalism.
- [[e-e-cummings]] — the poet who was also a painter, integrating visual and linguistic dimensions in his work.
