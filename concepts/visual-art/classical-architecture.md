## summary

**Classical Architecture** refers to the design principles and structural systems of ancient Greece and Rome, characterized by mathematical proportion, the architectural orders, and emphasis on symmetry. Revived during the Renaissance and systematized through the *Enlightenment*, it became the dominant language of Western architecture for centuries, codifying rules of proportion and ornament that transcended centuries of building.

## you gotta know

- Rooted in ancient Greek temples and Roman structures like the Pantheon, emphasizing harmonious proportions and structural logic.
- Built upon the three primary *orders* — Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian — each with distinct capital designs and ratios; the *Composite order* fuses Corinthian and Ionic capitals, representing Roman elaboration.
- Features load-bearing columns, entablatures (the horizontal beam assembly above columns), and pediments (triangular roof sections); the *fluting* (vertical grooves) on columns both visually lightens and structurally strengthens them.
- Emphasizes bilateral symmetry and the golden ratio, creating a sense of permanence and intellectual order; *proportion* was understood as divine and mathematical.
- The *trabeated system* (post-and-lintel) of columns and beams differs from the *arch-and-vault* innovations Romans developed, expanding architectural possibilities.
- Revived intensively during the Italian Renaissance as architects like *Brunelleschi* and *Palladio* studied Roman ruins directly and documented proportional systems.
- Became the foundation of Neoclassical architecture in the 18th century and remained the norm for civic and institutional buildings into the 20th century; even modernism borrowed classical axial planning and proportional systems.
- The *vitruvian principles* of firmness (structural soundness), commodity (usefulness), and delight (beauty) unified theory and practice, making classical architecture a comprehensive philosophical system.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the source of the column orders and temple typology.
- [[roman-empire]] — perfected and adapted Greek orders for monumental engineering.
- [[renaissance]] — rediscovered and adapted classical principles for modern use.
- [[andrea-palladio]] — the Renaissance master who systematized classical rules in his influential treatises.
- [[baroque]] — reacted against classical restraint with ornamental excess.
- [[neoclassicism]] — the 18th-century revival that made classical grammar the mandatory language of official architecture.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement that valued rational order and classical reason.
