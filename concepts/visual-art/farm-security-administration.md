## summary

The **Farm Security Administration** was a *United States* New Deal government agency created in 1937 to combat rural poverty during the Great Depression. Its legendary Historical Section, directed by the economist *Roy Stryker*, commissioned a pioneering documentary photography initiative that captured the human cost of economic devastation and the Dust Bowl. The resulting photographic archive redefined *American realism* and established social documentary photography as a vital instrument of visual culture and government advocacy.

## you gotta know

- Directed by *Roy Stryker*, who instructed photographers to document rural life, agricultural labor, displacement, and community culture across the *United States*.
- Launched originally in 1935 as part of the *Resettlement Administration* under *Rexford Tugwell* before being reorganized under the Department of Agriculture as the FSA.
- Commissioned landmark photographers including *Dorothea Lange*, *Walker Evans*, *Gordon Parks*, *Arthur Rothstein*, *Russell Lee*, and *Marion Post Wolcott*.
- Produced iconic visual works of the era, notably *Lange's* portrait *Migrant Mother* taken in Nipomo, California, and *Evans's* portraits of Alabama sharecropper families.
- Utilized negative hole-punching ("killing" negatives) by *Stryker* to reject images he deemed unsuitable or flawed, a controversial editorial practice that defaced thousands of frames.
- Generated the visual source material for *Walker Evans* and writer *James Agee's* landmark 1941 collaborative book *Let Us Now Praise Famous Men*.
- Shifted focus in 1942 toward wartime industrial mobilization and patriotic morale as the *United States* entered *World War II*, before being transferred to the Office of War Information.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the nation whose federal New Deal programs funded and framed the agency's documentary mission.
- [[american-realism]] — the artistic movement fundamentally shaped by the FSA's unflinching visual record of poverty and labor.
- [[realism]] — the broader artistic philosophy prioritizing objective observation of everyday struggle that underpinned FSA imagery.
- [[world-war-ii]] — brought an end to the Great Depression and redirected the agency's photography toward wartime production.
- [[new-york-city]] — served as a central hub where FSA photographers exhibited work, interacted with the Photo League, and influenced modern media.
- [[alfred-stieglitz]] — championed photography as a fine art in America, against which FSA photographers developed a distinct, socially conscious documentary mode.
