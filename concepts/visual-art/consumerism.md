## summary

**Consumerism** is both a socioeconomic phenomenon—the cultural elevation of acquisition and material goods as markers of identity and happiness—and an artistic subject that Modern and contemporary artists have interrogated, celebrated, and critiqued. The 20th-century rise of mass production, advertising, and consumer culture became a central preoccupation of [[pop-art]], which treated brand names, commercial packaging, and mass-manufactured objects as subjects worthy of fine art. Consumerism thus marks the boundary between art and everyday commerce, raising fundamental questions about taste, authenticity, and cultural value.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a dominant economic and cultural force in the early 20th century, especially after industrialization and mass marketing.
- Treated consumer goods and brand identity as sources of meaning, status, and self-definition within modern societies.
- Became a subject of artistic critique and celebration: Pop artists elevated soup cans, Coca-Cola bottles, and celebrity images to the status of art.
- *Andy Warhol* became the principal voice, treating mass-produced commercial imagery (Campbell's Soup, Marilyn Monroe) as worthy of fine art reproduction via silkscreen.
- Challenged the Romantic and Modernist hierarchies that separated "high" art from "low" commercial culture.
- The 1920s Roaring Twenties witnessed consumerism's cultural apotheosis: advertising, mass production, and disposability became ideals rather than concerns.
- Contemporary art continues to wrestle with consumerism: questions of authenticity, reproducibility, and the artist's role in a market-saturated world.

## connections

- [[pop-art]] — the movement most centrally preoccupied with consumerism as both subject and mirror.
- [[andy-warhol]] — the artist who most famously embraced and interrogated consumer culture in fine art.
- [[campbells-soup-cans]] — the iconic artwork that collapsed the boundary between commercial product and fine art.
- [[mass-media]] — the apparatus through which consumer desire is manufactured and broadcast.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the technological condition that enabled mass production and consumerism.
- [[roaring-twenties]] — the decade that witnessed consumerism's emergence as a dominant cultural value.
- [[capitalism]] — the economic system within which consumerism flourishes as ideology and practice.
