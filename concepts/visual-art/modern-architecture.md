## summary

**Modern architecture** refers to the design movement and building practices of the 20th and 21st centuries that abandoned historical ornament in favour of clean lines, rational form, and structural innovation. Rooted in the machine aesthetic and the belief that form should follow function, modern architecture rejected the decorative excesses of earlier styles and embraced steel, glass, and concrete as expressive materials. It represents not merely a style but a philosophical commitment to progress, efficiency, and the democratisation of space through industrial production.

## you gotta know

- Emerged in the early 20th century as a rejection of Beaux-Arts ornamentation and historicism; pioneers like *Walter Gropius* and the *Bauhaus* school formalised its principles into a coherent philosophy.
- Embraced the dictum "form follows function," meaning a building's aesthetic should arise directly from its structural purpose rather than applied decoration.
- Pioneered the use of industrial materials—steel frames, reinforced concrete, and expanses of glass—enabling radical new spatial arrangements and heights impossible in earlier architecture.
- Valorised the machine aesthetic and mass production ideals; architects designed as if buildings could be manufactured and assembled like automobiles or appliances.
- *Le Corbusier* coined the term "machines for living in" to describe residential architecture, treating houses as optimised functional systems rather than personal expressions of craft.
- Produced canonical works including the Barcelona Pavilion (1929), the Guggenheim Museum (1959), and countless corporate and residential blocks emphasising transparency and repetitive gridded façades.
- Shaped the post-World War II urban landscape, with Modernist apartment blocks, office parks, and public housing becoming the visual signature of rebuilt European and American cities.
- Encountered significant resistance from the 1980s onward as Postmodernism and Neo-traditionalism critiqued its perceived coldness and indifference to human scale and historical context.

## connections

- [[bauhaus]] — the foundational school that codified modernist design principles and trained its leading practitioners.
- [[frank-lloyd-wright]] — American pioneer who developed organic modernism, integrating buildings with their natural surroundings rather than imposing abstract geometric order.
- [[le-corbusier]] — the movement's most theoretically ambitious figure, whose urban visions and domestic designs defined modernist utopian thinking.
- [[walter-gropius]] — founder of the Bauhaus and a central theorist of industrial design's applicability to architecture.
- [[constructivism]] — the parallel Soviet avant-garde movement that pursued similarly radical formal abstraction and rejection of ornament.
- [[modernism]] — the broader cultural movement encompassing literature, visual art, and design alongside architecture.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical foundation enabling mass production of building materials and prefabrication techniques.
