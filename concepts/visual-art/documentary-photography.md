## summary

**Documentary photography** is a visual practice that records real-world people, events, social conditions, and historical moments to provide accurate, truthful evidence and stimulate public awareness or reform. Originating in the nineteenth century with conflict documentation and urban social surveys, it matured into a vital artistic and civic tool during the twentieth century, most notably through government-sponsored projects during the Great Depression. By marrying rigorous aesthetic composition with journalistic integrity, practitioners bear witness to human struggles, transforming ephemeral reality into enduring cultural memory.

## you gotta know

- *Dorothea Lange* captured the definitive portrait of rural poverty during the Great Depression with her 1936 photograph *Migrant Mother*, taken in a pea-pickers' camp in Nipomo, California.
- The Farm Security Administration historical section, directed by *Roy Stryker* in the [[united-states]], employed prominent photographers like *Walker Evans*, *Gordon Parks*, and *Arthur Rothstein* to document agricultural displacement.
- *Jacob Riis* pioneered investigative social reform photography in late-nineteenth-century [[new-york-city]], using dangerous magnesium flash powder to expose tenement living conditions in *How the Other Half Lives*.
- *Lewis Hine* photographed harrowing child labor conditions in American textile mills, glass factories, and coal mines for the National Child Labor Committee, directly prompting legislative workplace reforms.
- *Henri Cartier-Bresson* established the photojournalistic concept of the *decisive moment*, using unobtrusive 35mm Leica cameras to capture spontaneous, geometrically harmonious slices of human life.
- *Walker Evans* collaborated with writer *James Agee* on *Let Us Now Praise Famous Men*, combining unsparing, frontal, unsentimental portraits with prose to depict Southern sharecropping families.
- *Robert Frank* published *The Americans* in 1958, using gritty, off-kilter, and candid imagery across thirty states to dismantle postwar American complacency and myths of universal prosperity.

## connections

- [[american-realism]] — broader aesthetic movement across literature and painting that rejected romantic sentimentality in favor of unvarnished depictions of everyday American life.
- [[united-states]] — nation where state sponsorship, industrial growth, and acute economic crises catalyzed the golden age of socially conscious photojournalism.
- [[alfred-stieglitz]] — photographer and gallery pioneer whose championing of straight photography provided an aesthetic framework that legitimized unmanipulated photographic prints.
- [[dramatic-effect]] — expressive employment of natural contrast, composition, and human gesture to evoke viewer empathy and moral conviction without resorting to fictional staging.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — pivotal social justice struggle in which photojournalists documented protests, police brutality, and marches to galvanize global public opinion.
- [[world-war-ii]] — catastrophic global conflict documented by intrepid combat photographers like *Robert Capa* and *Margaret Bourke-White* on the front lines and in liberated concentration camps.
- [[great-depression]] — catastrophic economic collapse that prompted unprecedented federal commissioning of visual documentation of American labor and migration.
