## summary

The **Athena Parthenos** was a colossal chryselephantine cult statue of the virgin goddess Athena created by the Athenian master sculptor Phidias. Dedicated inside the *[[parthenon]]* on the Athenian Acropolis in 438 BCE, it stood as the supreme civic symbol, religious icon, and financial treasury of Classical Athens in *[[ancient-greece]]*. Although the original statue was destroyed in late antiquity, its iconic design remains widely documented through ancient historical texts, coins, and monumental Roman replicas.

## you gotta know

- Designed and executed by Phidias, the preeminent master of Classical Greek *[[sculpture]]*, who also oversaw the decorative program of the Acropolis and sculpted the *[[statue-of-zeus]]* at Olympia.
- Constructed using the luxurious chryselephantine technique, which affixed plates of carved elephant ivory for exposed skin and more than one thousand kilograms of solid, detachable gold sheet for garments and armor onto an internal wooden core.
- Stood approximately twelve meters tall, showing the armed warrior goddess wearing a triple-crested helmet flanked by sphinxes and pegasi, holding a spear, and bearing a shield resting beside a coiled serpent representing Erichthonius.
- Balanced a four-cubit-tall winged Nike (victory) in the palm of her extended right hand, visually commemorating Athenian military triumphs over the Persian Empire.
- Incorporated dense mythological relief carvings across every available surface, including the battle with the Amazons on the shield exterior, the battle with the Giants on the shield interior, the Centauromachy on her sandal edges, and the birth of Pandora across the marble pedestal.
- Provoked political controversy for Phidias when political rivals accused him of embezzling gold and later charged him with impiety for embedding portraits of himself and statesman Pericles among the combatants on Athena's shield.
- Survives through ancient descriptions by Pausanias and Pliny the Elder, as well as surviving Roman marble copies such as the second-century CE *Varvakeion Athena* and the miniature *Lenormant Athena*.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — Mediterranean civilization whose Golden Age of Athenian democracy and artistic achievement gave rise to the statue.
- [[parthenon]] — monumental Doric temple constructed on the Acropolis specifically to provide an architectural sanctuary for the colossus.
- [[statue-of-zeus]] — Phidias's other monumental chryselephantine masterpiece, revered as one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.
- [[sculpture]] — three-dimensional art form that achieved unprecedented technical sophistication and monumental scale through this work.
- [[rome]] — imperial empire whose copyists preserved the visual iconography of the lost original through marble adaptations.
- [[andrea-del-verrocchio]] — Florentine Renaissance sculptor whose mastery of civic monumentality and anatomical casting traced its lineage to classical prototypes like Athena.
