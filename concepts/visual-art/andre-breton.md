## summary

**André Breton** (1896–1966) was a French poet, essayist, and artist who founded Surrealism in 1924, shaping one of the twentieth century's most influential artistic movements. Through manifestos, theoretical writings, and tireless curation of artists, Breton established Surrealism as a philosophy of liberation—using psychoanalytic concepts of the unconscious, dream logic, and creative automatism to challenge rational, bourgeois culture. Though his authoritarian control over Surrealism generated tension and defection, Breton's vision fundamentally altered how artists understood creativity, meaning, and the power of the irrational.

## you gotta know

- Published the *Surrealist Manifesto* (1924), declaring the movement's allegiance to the unconscious, dreams, and the liberation of the imagination from rational constraint.
- Began his career with Dada after World War I, but became disillusioned with Dada's pure negation; Surrealism retained Dada's revolt but sought to build something affirmative.
- Grounded Surrealism in *Sigmund Freud's* theories of the unconscious and dream analysis, treating art-making as a means to access hidden psychological depths.
- Championed *automatism*—the practice of spontaneous, unplanned creation (automatic drawing, automatic writing)—as a path to bypass conscious control and rational judgment.
- Organized surrealist exhibitions, maintained a famous Paris salon, and published the journal *La Révolution Surréaliste*, exerting considerable curatorial and editorial authority.
- Collaborated intensely with artists including [[Max Ernst]], [[Salvador Dalí]], and many others; his endorsements and rejections significantly shaped careers.
- Eventually expelled or rejected several major surrealists, including Dalí, over philosophical differences or perceived betrayals; these rifts fractured the movement's unity.
- Remained committed to Surrealism through World War II and beyond, despite the movement's fragmentation and the rise of competing avant-gardes.

## connections

- [[surrealism]] — founder and primary theorist of the movement, defining its philosophy and aesthetic.
- [[salvador-dali]] — key surrealist artist whom Breton championed, then repudiated over disagreements about kitsch and commercialism.
- [[max-ernst]] — surrealist painter and frequent collaborator; Breton's support was instrumental in Ernst's recognition.
- [[dada]] — Breton's starting point; he evolved from Dada's negation toward Surrealism's constructive irrationalism.
- [[psychoanalysis]] — Breton's theoretical foundation, importing Freud's concepts of the unconscious into artistic practice.
- [[automatism]] — the creative technique Breton advocated and theorized as central to surrealist method.
- [[france]] — the Paris-centered context from which Surrealism radiated internationally.
