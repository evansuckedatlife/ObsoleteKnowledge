## summary

**Sculpture** is the art of creating three-dimensional form, either by subtractive methods (carving away material like stone or wood) or additive processes (modeling clay, assembling materials, or casting). As one of humanity's oldest arts—alongside painting and architecture—sculpture has served religious, commemorative, and aesthetic functions across all cultures. Unlike painting's illusionistic window, sculpture occupies real space and invites the viewer's bodily presence and movement, creating an intimate physical dialogue between artwork and observer.

## you gotta know

- **Carving** (subtractive) removes material to reveal form, with stone and wood as classical media; **modeling** (additive) builds form from pliable material like clay; **casting** reproduces an original in metal or plaster.
- [[Marble]] became the canonical sculptural medium in antiquity, associated with durability, nobility, and idealized form; quarries in Carrara, Italy supplied Renaissance masters.
- Greek sculpture evolved from rigid, frontal forms to contrapposto (balanced weight-shift), naturalistic anatomy, and psychological expressiveness—innovations that became Renaissance touchstones.
- [[Michelangelo]]'s sculptures, carved from single marble blocks, embodied Renaissance ideals of heroic human form and divine creation.
- *Gian Lorenzo Bernini* ([[bernini]]) revolutionized Baroque sculpture with theatrical drama, emotional intensity, and the manipulation of light on carved surfaces.
- Modernism fragmented sculpture: *Rodin* ([[rodin]]) introduced expressionist gesture and surface; *Brancusi* ([[brancusi]]) reduced form to pure abstraction; *Constructivism* ([[constructivism]]) used industrial materials and geometric structure.
- Sculpture shares exhibition space and institutional contexts with painting but operates in fundamentally different spatial and temporal registers—it demands movement around and through it.

## connections

- [[marble]] — the classical sculptural material, inseparable from Western sculpture's history and ideals.
- [[michelangelo]] — master sculptor whose works defined Renaissance and High Renaissance achievement.
- [[bernini]] — transformed sculpture into dramatic, emotionally intense Baroque expression.
- [[donatello]] — early Renaissance innovator who revived classical anatomy and perspective in three-dimensional form.
- [[classical-sculpture]] — Greek and Roman precedents that dominated Western sculptural ideals for centuries.
- [[painting]] — sibling medium sharing exhibition contexts and artistic traditions.
- [[architecture]] — sculpture functions as architectural ornamentation and exists in dialogue with built space.
