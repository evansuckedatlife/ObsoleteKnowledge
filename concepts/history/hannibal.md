# Hannibal

## summary

**Hannibal Barca** (247–183 BCE) was a Carthaginian general and strategist whose invasion of Italy during the *Second Punic War* brought Rome to the brink of destruction. His tactical genius—especially the double envelopment at *Cannae*—made him one of history's greatest military commanders, though political constraints and lack of reinforcements ultimately prevented him from conquering Rome itself.

## you gotta know

- Born into the *Barcid* family (his father, *Hamilcar Barca*, was Carthage's leading general), Hannibal swore an oath to oppose Rome and spent his youth in Spain as his father expanded Carthaginian holdings; he inherited command and broad authority at roughly age 25 in 218 BCE.
- In 218 BCE, Hannibal invaded Italy with roughly 40,000 troops and war elephants after marching across Spain and the Alps; his dramatic crossing cost significant losses but achieved strategic surprise by bringing the war directly to Roman territory.
- At the *Battle of Cannae* (August 216 BCE), Hannibal's tactical masterpiece saw him draw Roman forces into a center and then envelop them with cavalry attacks on both flanks, annihilating a Roman army of roughly 50,000 soldiers—Rome's worst defeat in the war.
- Despite Cannae, Hannibal could not take Rome itself; Italian allied cities defected to him, but Rome's resilience, local fortifications, and lack of siege equipment constrained his options; he became bogged down in a war of attrition he could not win.
- Roman *Scipio Africanus* eventually invaded North Africa to threaten Carthage itself, forcing Hannibal to return home; *Scipio* defeated him at *Zama* (202 BCE), ending the Second Punic War and Carthage's great-power status.
- After the war, Hannibal served as a magistrate in Carthage, then fled to the Seleucid court (as Rome's enemy) and eventually Bithynia, where he died in exile, reportedly by suicide rather than capture.
