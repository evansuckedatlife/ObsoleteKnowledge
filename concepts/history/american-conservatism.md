## summary

**American conservatism**, also known as **conservatism in the United States**, is a major political philosophy and movement characterized by its advocacy for free-market capitalism, limited government intervention, individual liberty, traditional social values, and constitutional originalism. Taking cohesive form in the aftermath of [[world-war-ii]], the movement united economic libertarians, cultural traditionalists, and staunch anticommunists in opposition to the domestic *New Deal* consensus and the global spread of totalitarianism during the [[cold-war]]. It fundamentally transformed governance in the [[united-states]], achieving national dominance through the conservative realignment of the late twentieth century. Today, it remains one of the two defining forces shaping American political discourse, federal policymaking, and electoral competition.

## you gotta know

- *Ronald Reagan*'s 1980 presidential victory marked the full political ascendance of modern conservatism, implementing supply-side economic policies termed *Reaganomics*, sweeping domestic deregulation, and an aggressive foreign policy posture that pressured the [[soviet-union]].
- *Barry Goldwater*'s 1964 presidential campaign, guided by the manifesto *The Conscience of a Conservative*, mobilized grassroots activists across the American Sunbelt and solidified conservative control over the Republican Party despite his landslide general election loss.
- *William F. Buckley Jr.* founded the influential journal *National Review* in 1955, orchestrating a philosophical synthesis known as *fusionism* that joined libertarian free-market economics, traditional Christian morality, and vigorous anti-communist interventionism while purging fringe factions like the *John Birch Society*.
- The movement pioneered constitutional originalism and the doctrine of judicial restraint to counter progressive legal realism, arguing that the [[united-states-constitution]] should be interpreted according to its text and original public understanding, particularly regarding the [[14th-amendment]].
- The *Moral Majority*, founded in 1979 by Baptist televangelist *Jerry Falwell*, galvanized millions of evangelical Christians into a potent voting bloc centered on [[christianity]], traditional family structures, anti-abortion advocacy, and opposition to the *Equal Rights Amendment*.
- *Russell Kirk*'s landmark 1953 intellectual study *The Conservative Mind* established a respectable academic pedigree for American conservatism, tracing its philosophical roots to British statesman *Edmund Burke* and his skepticism of radical social engineering during the [[french-revolution]].
- The *Sharon Statement*, drafted in 1960 by conservative youth meeting at the family estate of *William F. Buckley Jr.*, created the charter for the *Young Americans for Freedom* by proclaiming that individual freedom and economic liberty derive directly from transcendent moral order.

## connections

- [[ronald-reagan]] — the 40th president whose two terms institutionalized conservative economic policies, tax reductions, and an assertive posture against Soviet communism.
- [[cold-war]] — the multi-decade geopolitical confrontation that provided disparate conservative factions with a vital unifying mission against global Marxist totalitarianism.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the nation's supreme legal framework whose original meaning, structural separation of powers, and federalist limits form the core of conservative jurisprudence.
- [[soviet-union]] — the primary socialist adversary whose global expansion sparked conservative demands for increased defense spending, peace through strength, and rollback diplomacy.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the historic struggle for racial equality that catalyzed debates over states' rights, accelerated political shifts in the South, and transformed partisan loyalties.
- [[christianity]] — the dominant religious tradition that energized the religious right, providing grassroots organizers with institutional networks, moral vocabulary, and social policy priorities.
- [[french-revolution]] — the European cataclysm whose destructive radicalism prompted *Edmund Burke*'s classic reflections, providing the philosophical cornerstone for modern conservative thought.
