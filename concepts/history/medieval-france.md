## summary

**Medieval France** was the feudal realm that gradually coalesced from the fragmented territories following the collapse of Charlemagne's empire into a centralized kingdom under the [[Capetian Dynasty]]. Over centuries, French kings expanded their authority from a small domain around Paris to dominion over the prosperous territories of the Rhône valley, Languedoc, and Aquitaine. By the late Middle Ages, France had emerged as one of Europe's great powers, its cultural influence—from Gothic architecture to courtly romance—reshaping the continent.

## you gotta know

- France emerged from the chaos of the 9th and 10th centuries as feudal lords carved out fiefdoms and local strongmen ruled fragmented regions.
- The [[Capetian Dynasty]] took the crown in 987 and slowly strengthened royal authority, consolidating lands through marriage and conquest over three centuries.
- [[Feudalism]] structured society: peasants owed labor to lords, lords owed loyalty to the king, and allegiance flowed up the hierarchy in exchange for protection and land tenure.
- The northern domains (Ile-de-France, Normandy, Champagne) developed thriving trade routes and urban centers, while southern Languedoc had distinct Occitan culture and Cathar heresy that the north sought to suppress.
- [[Louis IX]] (1226–1270), later canonized as a saint, earned renown through his piety and justice, establishing royal courts that undermined feudal justice and expanded central authority.
- [[Philip IV]] (1285–1314) boldly challenged papal authority, suppressed the wealthy [[Templar Order]] to seize their wealth, and extended royal control further, setting the stage for the [[Hundred Years' War]].
- Gothic architecture flourished—Chartres, Notre-Dame, Reims—as cathedrals and monasteries expressed France's growing wealth, faith, and artistic ambition.
- The Hundred Years' War and internal nobles' struggles would test and reshape the kingdom's structure in the late medieval period.

## connections
