## summary

**Siberia** is an immense geographic and historical region of northern Asia extending from the Ural Mountains eastward to the Pacific Ocean. Conquered and colonized by the *Tsardom of Russia* and the [[russian-empire]] beginning in the late sixteenth century, it transformed Russia into a transcontinental empire enriched by furs, timber, minerals, and fossil fuels. Across imperial and Soviet history, the territory functioned as both an indispensable resource frontier and a notorious carceral system of exile, profoundly shaping the geopolitical trajectory of the [[soviet-union]].

## you gotta know

- Served as the notorious destination for imperial Russian penal exile (*katorga*) and the vast forced-labor camp network of the Gulag under the [[soviet-union]], where millions of political prisoners and dissidents were sent to labor in subzero conditions.
- Traversed by the *Trans-Siberian Railway*, the longest continuous rail line in the world built between 1891 and 1916, which connected Moscow to the Pacific port of Vladivostok and accelerated strategic tensions leading to war with [[japan]].
- Witnessed the mysterious 1908 *Tunguska event*, in which a massive meteor or comet airburst flattened roughly eighty million trees across more than two thousand square kilometers near the Podkamennaya Tunguska River without leaving an impact crater.
- Entered Russian control through the campaigns of the Cossack ataman *Yermak Timofeyevich*, who in the 1580s led an expedition financed by the merchant *Stroganov family* that defeated the *Khanate of Sibir*, opening the region to eastward [[colonialism]].
- Formed the principal base of anti-Bolshevik White forces during the Russian Civil War under the self-proclaimed Supreme Ruler Admiral *Alexander Kolchak*, who established a provisional government at Omsk with the assistance of the mutinous *Czechoslovak Legion*.
- Driven economically in the seventeenth century by the lucrative fur trade known as *soft gold*, which motivated Cossack explorers to reach the Pacific while imposing the harsh *yasak* pelt tax on indigenous groups such as the *Buryats*, *Yakuts*, and *Evenks*.
- Marked its eastern boundary through the 1689 *Treaty of Nerchinsk* with the Qing dynasty of [[china]], a pact brokered by Jesuit diplomats that checked early Russian expansion into the Amur River basin in exchange for legal trading rights in Beijing.

## connections

- [[russian-empire]] — imperial state that conquered, settled, and administratively integrated the region over three centuries of eastward expansion.
- [[soviet-union]] — revolutionized the regional economy through forced-labor industrialization, resource extraction, and strategic Arctic urban development.
- [[colonialism]] — historical framework under which Russian frontiersmen subjugated indigenous peoples and established fortified settlements across Northern Asia.
- [[cold-war]] — global rivalry during which the remote Siberian interior housed secret nuclear installations, closed cities, and missile complexes.
- [[world-war-ii]] — existential conflict during which Soviet factories and civilian populations were evacuated eastward beyond the Urals to sustain the war effort.
- [[china]] — neighboring Eurasian empire that checked Russian territorial expansion along the Amur River through seventeenth-century frontier diplomacy.
- [[japan]] — rival Pacific power that competed for influence in Sakhalin, Manchuria, and the Russian Far East during early twentieth-century wars.
