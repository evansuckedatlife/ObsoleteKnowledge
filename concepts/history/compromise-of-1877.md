# Compromise of 1877

## summary

The **Compromise of 1877** was a backroom political deal that resolved the disputed *presidential election of 1876* by awarding the presidency to Republican *Rutherford B. Hayes* in exchange for the withdrawal of federal troops from the South. This marked the end of *Reconstruction*, the period when federal power protected Black freedmen's rights. With troops withdrawn, white Southern Democrats reasserted control, instituting *Jim Crow* segregation and disenfranchising Black voters for the next century. The compromise thus traded minority rights for political peace, a catastrophic price paid by African Americans.

## you gotta know

- The 1876 election between Hayes (Republican) and *Samuel Tilden* (Democrat) produced disputed returns from *Florida*, *Louisiana*, and *South Carolina*; neither candidate had a clear electoral majority, threatening constitutional crisis.
- Republicans and Democrats negotiated privately: Republicans would concede the election to Hayes if Democrats agreed to cease southern resistance and allow his inauguration; Democrats demanded federal troop withdrawal from the South in exchange.
- Hayes won the presidency with 185 electoral votes to Tilden's 184, but only after Republican-controlled Congress accepted the compromise and certified the disputed electors for Hayes.
- Immediately upon taking office, Hayes withdrew the remaining federal troops from the South; without military protection, Black southerners lost their only defense against white Southern Democratic violence and intimidation.
- The return of Democratic control to the South enabled the rapid imposition of *Jim Crow* laws—legal segregation—and the systematic disenfranchisement of Black voters through literacy tests, poll taxes, and grandfather clauses.
- Black political participation in the South collapsed: in 1868, over half a million Black southerners voted; by 1910, fewer than 10,000 could vote in the entire region; this lasted until the *Voting Rights Act of 1965*.
