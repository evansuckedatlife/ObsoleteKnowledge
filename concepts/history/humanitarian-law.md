## summary

**Humanitarian Law**, formally known as **International Humanitarian Law** (*IHL*) or the **Law of Armed Conflict**, is the specialized discipline of international jurisprudence designed to mitigate the devastation of armed conflict by protecting persons not participating in hostilities and by restricting the permissible means and methods of combat. Rooted in customary military traditions and codifications from the nineteenth century onward, it balances urgent military necessity against universal humanitarian imperatives. Its foundational instruments, most notably the *Geneva Conventions*, define the legal obligations of combatants and supply the normative architecture for prosecuting atrocities worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Distinguishes categorically between *jus ad bellum*, the body of law governing whether states may legitimately resort to armed conflict, and *jus in bello*, the mandatory rules regulating conduct during military operations regardless of the conflict's underlying legality.
- Catalyzed in the nineteenth century by Swiss humanist *Henry Dunant*, whose harrowing eyewitness account of the 1859 *Battle of Solferino* led directly to the establishment of the *International Committee of the Red Cross*.
- Codified into systematic field regulations through the 1863 *Lieber Code*, drafted by jurist *Francis Lieber* and promulgated by President *Abraham Lincoln* to govern Union forces during the *American Civil War*.
- Divided historically into two complementary legal traditions: *Hague Law*, which restricts the weaponry and tactical methods utilized by belligerents, and *Geneva Law*, which safeguards non-combatants, captives, and medical personnel.
- Codified comprehensively in the four *Geneva Conventions of 1949* and their subsequent *Additional Protocols*, establishing universal safeguards for wounded soldiers, shipwrecked naval personnel, prisoners of war, and civilians under military occupation.
- Enforces the core customary principles of distinction between combatants and non-combatants, proportionality in assessing unavoidable collateral injury against direct military advantage, and the strict prohibition of weapons that cause unnecessary suffering.
- Established precedent for universal individual accountability at the *Nuremberg Trials*, repudiating claims of sovereign immunity and defense of superior orders for catastrophic battlefield crimes.
- Serves as the governing statutory body interpreted by modern international tribunals, such as the *International Criminal Court* in The Hague, to prosecute the perpetrators of mass atrocities.

## connections

- [[hugh-thompson-jr]] — embodied the ethical and legal mandate of individual soldiers to protect vulnerable civilians and actively prevent violations of these laws.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global war whose horrific atrocities against prisoners and civilians impelled the comprehensive overhaul of the *Geneva Conventions* in 1949.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical epoch whose concepts of natural law, human dignity, and the social contract provided the intellectual groundwork for limiting the savagery of war.
- [[international-law]] — the broader legal system governing sovereign relations of which this specialized discipline forms a cornerstone.
- [[war-crimes]] — the specific grave breaches of treaty and customary rules that generate direct individual criminal liability under this legal regime.
- [[red-cross]] — the neutral international humanitarian organization that serves as the official promoter, custodian, and monitor of these protective treaties.
- [[nuremberg-trials]] — the landmark post-war tribunals that cemented individual legal responsibility for violating the customary laws of war.
