# Harry Truman

## summary

**Harry Truman** was the 33rd President of the United States (1945–1953), ascending to the presidency upon Franklin D. Roosevelt's death during World War II and inheriting the decision to use atomic weapons against Japan. A former senator from Missouri, Truman faced the onset of the Cold War, the Korean War, and the complex transition to a postwar order. His administration was marked by decisive anti-communist policies including the *Truman Doctrine*, the *Marshall Plan*, and the commitment to contain Soviet expansion, establishing precedents for decades of American foreign policy.

## you gotta know

- Truman was *Franklin Roosevelt*'s vice president for less than 100 days before Roosevelt's death on April 12, 1945; he had minimal foreign policy experience and was largely uninformed of the atomic bomb project until becoming president.
- In August 1945, Truman authorized the atomic bombings of *Hiroshima* and *Nagasaki*, arguing the decision would end the war quickly and avoid a costly invasion of the Japanese mainland; this act remains historically contested and morally weighty.
- The *Truman Doctrine* (1947) pledged American support to nations resisting communist subversion; it provided military and economic aid to *Greece* and *Turkey* and established containment as the foundation of Cold War strategy.
- The *Marshall Plan* (1948–1952), formally the European Recovery Program, provided billions in American aid to rebuild Western Europe; it was both humanitarian and strategic, aimed at preventing economic desperation that might enable communist movements.
- Truman initiated American membership in the *United Nations*, giving it legitimacy; he also backed the creation of *Israel* in 1948, recognizing the new state despite Arab opposition and its strategic complications in the Middle East.
- The *Korean War* (1950–1953) erupted during Truman's presidency when North Korea invaded the South; he committed American troops under UN auspices but controversially dismissed General *Douglas MacArthur* in 1951 over disagreements about war aims and operations in China.
- Truman undertook a limited civil rights agenda, including desegregating the military by executive order and running a "whistle-stop" campaign in 1948 that highlighted his pro-labor, anti-segregation stance; he was reelected in a stunning upset over *Thomas Dewey*.
- Truman had access to the *atomic bomb* and faced numerous Cold War crises including the *Berlin Blockade*, the *Soviet test* of an atomic bomb, and the Korean War; his willingness to use military force and nuclear deterrence defined the early Cold War era.
