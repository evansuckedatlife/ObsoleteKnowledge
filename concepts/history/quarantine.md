## summary

**Quarantine** is the compulsory isolation and restriction of movement imposed on persons, animals, or goods suspected of exposure to a contagious pathogen to prevent wider disease transmission. First formalized in maritime law during the fourteenth-century arrival of the [[black-death]] in the Adriatic ports of [[italy]], the measure differs technically from medical isolation by targeting potentially exposed individuals rather than confirmed symptomatic cases. As one of the oldest and most durable interventions in [[public-health-history]], it has evolved from medieval island lazarettos and armed military sanitary cordons into international biosecurity frameworks and modern border detentions during the [[covid-19-pandemic]].

## you gotta know

- Etymology derives from the Venetian dialect phrase *quaranta giorni*, denoting the mandatory forty-day isolation period required of arriving merchant vessels, crews, and cargo during devastating fourteenth-century outbreaks of the [[black-death]].
- Applied globally on an unprecedented scale during the [[covid-19-pandemic]], encompassing strict national border closures, supervised hotel detentions, and the confinement of passengers aboard cruise ships such as the *Diamond Princess* in [[japan]].
- Distinct in modern epidemiological doctrine from *isolation*: quarantine restricts healthy or asymptomatic individuals who may have been exposed, whereas isolation specifically separates and treats individuals already confirmed to be infected.
- The Derbyshire village of *Eyam* famously enacted an autonomous, voluntary perimeter in 1665 during the Great Plague of [[england]], enduring catastrophic mortality among its residents to prevent the pestilence from spreading to surrounding communities.
- Public health authorities in [[new-york-city]] forcibly confined asymptomatic carrier *Mary Mallon* (*Typhoid Mary*) to Riverside Hospital on North Brother Island for nearly three decades after tracing fatal outbreaks of salmonella to her domestic employment.
- During the Apollo program, [[nasa]] subjected returning astronauts aboard *Apollo 11*, *Apollo 12*, and *Apollo 14* to rigorous containment protocols in the Mobile Quarantine Facility to protect Earth against theoretical extraterrestrial lunar pathogens.
- Historical quarantine enforcement often relied on a *cordon sanitaire*, an armed military or police barrier erected around infected cities or territories to halt all ingress and egress, famously deployed by [[france]] in the Pyrenees during the 1821 yellow fever outbreak in [[spain]].
- The earliest recorded legislative quarantine was enacted in 1377 by the Republic of Ragusa (modern Dubrovnik), establishing a thirty-day waiting period (*trentina*) on the island of Mrkan before Venice created the first permanent maritime *lazaretto* on Santa Maria di Nazareth in 1423.

## connections

- [[black-death]] — fourteenth-century pandemic whose devastating mortality spurred the invention of institutional maritime quarantines.
- [[italy]] — birthplace of the formal forty-day *quarantena* system administered by the Venetian Republic.
- [[covid-19-pandemic]] — modern respiratory crisis that prompted sweeping international lockdowns, mandatory hotel detentions, and cruise ship isolations.
- [[spanish-flu]] — 1918 influenza pandemic where municipal authorities implemented maritime inspections, home quarantines, and public gathering bans.
- [[new-york-city]] — American metropolis whose sanitary inspectors repeatedly isolated typhoid carrier *Mary Mallon* on North Brother Island.
- [[nasa]] — space exploration agency that established bio-containment protocols for lunar astronauts to guard against hypothetical alien microbes.
- [[renaissance]] — European era whose expanding Mediterranean commercial networks necessitated state-administered maritime health cordons.
- [[hebrew-bible]] — religious text whose Book of Leviticus sets forth early precedents for separating individuals afflicted with communicable skin diseases.
