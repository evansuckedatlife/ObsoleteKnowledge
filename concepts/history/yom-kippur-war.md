## summary

The **Yom Kippur War** (October 6–25, 1973) was a major conflict between *Israel* and a coalition of Arab states led by *Egypt* and *Syria*. Egyptian and Syrian forces attacked Israel on the holiest day in the Jewish calendar, aiming to retake territories lost in the 1967 *Six-Day War*. Though Israel ultimately prevailed militarily through rapid mobilization and counteroffensive, the war shattered Israeli confidence in military invincibility and demonstrated Arab armies' modernization and capability. The conflict became a diplomatic turning point: Egyptian success in crossing the Suez Canal provided political space for eventual Israeli-Egyptian peace negotiations, fundamentally altering Middle Eastern geopolitics.

## you gotta know

- *Anwar Sadat*, Egypt's president, sought to recover the *Sinai Peninsula* lost to Israel in 1967; *Hafez al-Assad*, Syria's leader, aimed to retake the *Golan Heights*.
- The war began on October 6, 1973, when Egyptian forces surprised Israel by launching a coordinated attack across the Suez Canal on Yom Kippur, the holiest day of the Jewish year when Israeli society effectively shuts down.
- Initial Arab successes shocked Israel, which had believed the "Bar-Lev Line"—fortifications along the Suez Canal—virtually impregnable and harbored assumptions of Arab military inferiority.
- Israeli Commander *Ariel Sharon* executed a daring crossing that encircled Egyptian forces; combined with air superiority and Soviet military supply limitations, Israel reversed the war's momentum.
- The war lasted three weeks; Israel's tactical victory involved retaking most territory and advancing on Arab capitals, but at an unexpectedly high cost (nearly 3,000 Israeli dead).
- Diplomatically, the war proved pivotal: Sadat's military performance, though ultimately unsuccessful, restored Egyptian credibility and allowed him to negotiate from a position of restored honor.
- U.S. Secretary of State *Henry Kissinger* brokered ceasefire agreements and subsequent disengagement accords, launching shuttle diplomacy between Israel and Arab states.
- The war catalyzed Israeli-Egyptian *Camp David Accords* (1978) and the first Israeli-Arab peace treaty (1979), fundamentally altering Cold War Middle Eastern alignments and establishing Israel's status as a confirmed regional power.
