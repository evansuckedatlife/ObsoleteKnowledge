## summary

**Nebuchadnezzar II** (r. 605–562 BCE) was the greatest king of Neo-Babylon who transformed the city into the ancient world's most magnificent metropolis. A military conqueror who extended Babylonian dominion across the Levant and into Egypt, he rebuilt [[Babylon]] with monumental architecture and engineering marvels, most famously the legendary [[Hanging Gardens of Babylon]]. His reign marked the apex of Mesopotamian civilization before Persia's rise.

## you gotta know

- Inherited the throne from his father *Nabopolassar*, the founder of the Neo-Babylonian Empire, and immediately began expanding Babylonian territory through aggressive military campaigns across the Levant.
- Conquered Judah and destroyed the Temple of Jerusalem in 586 BCE, exiling the Jewish population to Babylon in captivity—a pivotal event recorded in biblical texts and Babylonian chronicles.
- Rebuilt Babylon with massive city walls, the celebrated Ishtar Gate decorated with brilliant blue-glazed brick, and reconstructed the legendary Tower of Babel, making it the ancient world's most imposing capital.
- Commissioned the [[Hanging Gardens of Babylon]], one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, allegedly built for his homesick Median wife *Amytis* to remind her of her homeland.
- Extended Babylonian rule from Mesopotamia across Syria-Palestine to the borders of Egypt, creating an empire rivaling the greatest ancient powers in territorial extent and strategic reach.
- Maintained a stable economy, managed sophisticated irrigation networks and agricultural systems, and patronized art, literature, astronomy, and mathematics—earning the title "Nebuchadnezzar the Great."
- His remarkably long reign (over 40 years, 605–562 BCE) gave Babylon unprecedented peace, prosperity, and cultural flourishing; political instability and decline returned after his death.
