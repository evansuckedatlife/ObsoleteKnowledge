## summary

The **Nullification Crisis** was a constitutional confrontation between the federal government and *South Carolina* (1828–1833) over the state's claim to invalidate federal tariff laws. Driven by Southern anger over protectionist tariffs that damaged the region's agricultural economy, *South Carolina* asserted the radical doctrine that states could reject federal laws deemed unconstitutional. The crisis exposed deep divisions over federal versus state authority and foreshadowed the ideological battle that would culminate in the American Civil War.

## you gotta know

- The crisis centered on the *Tariff of 1828* (the "Tariff of Abominations"), which imposed high duties on manufactured imports to protect Northern industry but devastated Southern cotton planters by raising prices for goods they needed.
- *John C. Calhoun*, then Vice President, secretly authored the South Carolina Exposition and Protest (1828), arguing that states possessed the sovereignty to nullify federal laws within their borders.
- *South Carolina's* Nullification Ordinance (November 1832) declared the tariffs null and void within the state, threatening secession if the federal government attempted to enforce them.
- President *Andrew Jackson*, despite being from the South, refused to tolerate nullification; he denounced it as incompatible with the Constitution and Union and was prepared to use force to ensure federal law compliance.
- The crisis was defused through a compromise tariff (1833) negotiated by *Henry Clay*, which gradually reduced rates while preserving the principle of federal authority—thus *South Carolina* could claim victory on the tariff while backing down on nullification.
- *Daniel Webster* delivered his famous "Liberty and Union, now and forever, one and inseparable" speech, articulating the nationalist interpretation of the Constitution against state sovereignty claims.
- The Nullification Crisis established that states could not unilaterally void federal law, but it also demonstrated that sectional grievances and competing constitutional philosophies remained unresolved.
