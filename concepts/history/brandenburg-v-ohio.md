## summary

**Brandenburg v. Ohio** was a landmark 1969 decision of the Supreme Court of the [[united-states]] that fundamentally reshaped First Amendment jurisprudence regarding political speech and advocacy of violence. Arising from a prosecution under state criminal syndicalism statutes, the ruling established the modern **Brandenburg test**, which shields provocative and radical speech unless it is specifically directed to inciting **imminent lawless action** and is likely to produce such action. By invalidating older sedition doctrines, the decision created the most expansive legal protection for dissident and controversial expression in modern democratic history.

## you gotta know

- Originated in 1964 when *Clarence Brandenburg*, a leader in the *Ku Klux Klan* in rural Hamilton County, Ohio, invited a television journalist to film a rally featuring burning crosses, derogatory slurs, and ambiguous threats of vengeance against the federal government.
- Resulted in Brandenburg's conviction under the *Ohio Criminal Syndicalism Act*, which imposed fines and imprisonment for advocating violence, sabotage, or unlawful methods of terrorism as a means of political reform.
- Overturned the conviction in a unanimous, per curiam opinion issued by the *Warren Court* on June 9, 1969, discarding decades of restrictive speech precedents.
- Expressly overruled the 1927 precedent *Whitney v. California*, which had upheld the imprisonment of political activists simply for participating in organizations that advocated the violent overthrow of government.
- Replaced the venerable but permissive *clear and present danger* standard, originally formulated by Justice *Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.* in *Schenck v. United States* (1919), with a rigorous two-pronged constitutional test.
- Established the requirement that government may not forbid advocacy of law violation except where speech is directed to inciting or producing imminent lawless action and is likely to incite or produce such action.
- Accompanied by influential concurring opinions from Justices *Hugo Black* and *William O. Douglas*, who argued that the First Amendment provides an absolute protection for political speech divorced from overt conduct.
- Served as a critical judicial shield during the turbulence of the Vietnam War and the [[civil-rights-movement]], protecting anti-war demonstrators, civil rights activists, and controversial minority speakers from state censorship.
