## summary

The **Alberta Social Credit Party**, whose members were widely known as the **Socreds**, was a provincial political movement in Alberta, Canada, that governed without interruption from 1935 to 1971. Founded during the catastrophic economic distress of the *Great Depression*, the movement initially sought to implement the radical monetary theories of British engineer *C. H. Douglas* to relieve severe poverty among prairie farmers and working families. Under the decades-long premiership of *Ernest Manning*, the party abandoned unorthodox financial experiments and transformed into a deeply influential bastion of social and fiscal conservatism that guided Alberta through its historic post-war petroleum boom.

## you gotta know

- Swept into power in the 1935 Alberta provincial election under the leadership of *William "Bible Bill" Aberhart*, a Calgary high school principal and radio evangelist whose prophetic broadcasts galvanized rural voters against eastern financial institutions.
- Campaigned on the *Social Credit* doctrine of *C. H. Douglas*, which argued that capitalism produced an inherent gap between production costs and consumer purchasing power—termed the "A+B theorem"—and promised every adult citizen a monthly basic dividend of twenty-five dollars.
- Clashed directly with the federal government and the vice-regal office of the [[governor-general-of-canada]] when imperial reservation and federal disallowance powers were invoked to strike down provincial banking and debt legislation as unconstitutional intrusions into federal jurisdiction.
- Suffered a decisive legal defeat in the *Reference Re Alberta Statutes* ruling of 1938, in which the Supreme Court of Canada invalidated core party bills, including the *Bank Employees Civil Rights Act* and the *Accurate News and Information Act*, which attempted to muzzle press criticism.
- Transformed under *Ernest Manning*, who assumed power following Aberhart's death in 1943, systematically purging antisemitic fringe elements, dropping radical credit schemes, and aligning the administration with free-enterprise capitalism during the [[cold-war]].
- Oversaw Alberta's rapid economic modernization after the 1947 *Leduc No. 1* crude oil discovery, using burgeoning oil royalties to eliminate the provincial debt, construct public infrastructure, and maintain low taxation without a provincial sales tax.
- Fell from power in the 1971 election to *Peter Lougheed* and the *Progressive Conservative Association of Alberta*, ending thirty-six consecutive years of rule, though its populist legacy directly inspired *Preston Manning* to establish the *Reform Party of Canada*, an ancestor of the modern [[conservative-party-of-canada]].

## connections

- [[conservative-party-of-canada]] — provided the ideological and regional ancestry for the western Canadian populist movement that eventually merged into the modern national party via the Reform movement.
- [[governor-general-of-canada]] — intersected during the constitutional crisis of the late 1930s when federal authorities used disallowance and vice-regal reservation powers to nullify radical provincial banking statutes.
- [[british-empire]] — derived its foundational monetary doctrines from British engineer *C. H. Douglas* and operated within the British-inherited Westminster parliamentary and vice-regal system.
- [[christianity]] — drew fundamentalist moral conviction, rhetoric, and grassroots organizing power from *William Aberhart*'s evangelical radio sermons and the *Calgary Prophetic Bible Institute*.
- [[world-war-ii]] — marked a pivotal turning point as wartime federal economic centralization and rising resource demand blunted provincial monetary experimentation.
- [[cold-war]] — shaped *Ernest Manning*'s staunchly anti-communist governance, which promoted Christian social conservatism and private enterprise as shields against socialist central planning.
