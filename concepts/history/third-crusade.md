## summary

The **Third Crusade** (1189–1192), also known as the **Kings' Crusade**, was a major Western European military expedition launched to reconquer the Holy Land following the fall of Jerusalem to the Muslim sultan *Saladin*. Spearheaded by three of medieval Europe's most powerful sovereigns—Holy Roman Emperor *Frederick I Barbarossa* of [[germany]], King *Philip II* of [[france]], and King *Richard I* of [[england]]—the campaign sought to reverse the collapse of the Latin Crusader states in the [[middle-east]]. Although the crusaders recaptured vital coastal strongholds like Acre and Jaffa, internal rivalries and logistical exhaustion prevented the reconquest of Jerusalem itself. The war concluded with the Treaty of Jaffa, preserving a viable coastal kingdom and securing safe passage for unarmed Christian pilgrims.

## you gotta know

- Prompted directly by the catastrophic Battle of Hattin in 1187, where *Saladin* shattered the army of Jerusalem and captured the True Cross, driving Pope *Gregory VIII* to issue the papal bull *Audita tremendi*.
- Remembered as the *Kings' Crusade* because it rallied three preeminent Western rulers: Emperor *Frederick I Barbarossa*, King *Philip II Augustus* of [[france]], and King *Richard I* of [[england]].
- *Frederick Barbarossa* led a massive army overland and routed the Seljuks at Iconium, but accidentally drowned in the Saleph River in 1190, causing the German contingent to disintegrate.
- The protracted Siege of Acre (1189–1191) served as the primary military engagement of the campaign, culminating in a Crusader triumph after the arrivals of *Richard I* and *Philip II*.
- *Richard the Lionheart* ordered the massacre of nearly three thousand Muslim prisoners outside Acre at Ayyadieh when hostage negotiations and financial exchanges stalled.
- *Philip II* abandoned the Holy Land shortly after Acre fell, driven by intense personal rancor toward *Richard* and an ambition to seize English continental possessions in [[france]].
- *Richard I* won a brilliant tactical victory at the Battle of Arsuf (1191), holding his infantry in tight defensive formation against horse archers before unleashing a crushing heavy cavalry charge.
- Concluded in 1192 with the Treaty of Jaffa, which left Jerusalem under Ayyubid rule while confirming Crusader control from Tyre to Jaffa and granting [[christianity|Christian]] pilgrims access to holy sites.
- Financed across Angevin lands by the "Saladin tithe", an extraordinary ten percent levy on revenues and movable property instituted under King [[henry-ii]] that transformed administrative taxation in [[england]].

## connections

- [[saladin]] — Ayyubid sultan whose capture of Jerusalem provoked the campaign and who negotiated the Treaty of Jaffa.
- [[england]] — kingdom ruled by *Richard the Lionheart*, which was subjected to heavy taxation to bankroll the overseas crusade.
- [[france]] — kingdom of *Philip II Augustus*, who abandoned the siege after Acre's fall to plot against Angevin territory.
- [[middle-east]] — geographic region where the sieges, coastal marches, and diplomatic negotiations took place.
- [[christianity]] — faith whose European adherents sought to liberate the holy sites of Jerusalem from Muslim rule.
- [[islam]] — religion uniting Egyptian and Syrian armies under Ayyubid leadership during the holy war.
- [[henry-ii]] — English monarch whose administration instituted the Saladin tithe shortly before his death in 1189.
- [[king-john]] — younger brother who plotted to seize control in [[england]] during Richard's crusade and subsequent captivity.
