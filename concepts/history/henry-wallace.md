## summary

**Henry Wallace**, known formally as **Henry Agard Wallace** or **Henry A. Wallace**, was an American statesman, agronomist, and editor who served as the thirty-third Vice President of the *[[united-states]]* under *Franklin D. Roosevelt*. A brilliant agricultural scientist who pioneered the commercial breeding of high-yield hybrid corn, he served as a central architect of the agrarian *[[new-deal]]* before guiding nationwide economic mobilization during *[[world-war-ii]]*. In the postwar era, Wallace emerged as the foremost champion of progressive internationalism, mounting a historic third-party presidential bid in 1948 that challenged the emerging containment policies and nuclear escalation of the early *[[cold-war]]*.

## you gotta know

- Served as the thirty-third Vice President of the *[[united-states]]* from 1941 to 1945, presiding over the Senate and coordinating vital defense production throughout *[[world-war-ii]]*.
- Revolutionized modern global farming in the 1920s by conducting groundbreaking genetic research on hybrid corn and establishing the massively influential *Hi-Bred Corn Company* (subsequently Pioneer Hi-Bred).
- Directed federal agrarian relief as Secretary of Agriculture from 1933 to 1940, formulating the Agricultural Adjustment Act and creating the "ever-normal granary" to insulate family farmers from price collapses.
- Delivered the celebrated 1942 "Century of the Common Man" address, championing global economic democracy, universal decolonization, and international human rights in explicit opposition to conservative visions of imperial hegemony.
- Ousted from the 1944 Democratic vice-presidential ticket at the national convention by conservative urban bosses and Southern delegates who feared his outspoken racial liberalism and prolabor sympathies, replacing him with *Harry S. Truman*.
- Dismissed as Secretary of Commerce in September 1946 by President *Harry S. Truman* after delivering a controversial public speech at Madison Square Garden denouncing aggressive anti-Soviet diplomatic confrontations.
- Mounted a historic third-party campaign as the 1948 presidential nominee of the *Progressive Party*, demanding immediate racial desegregation, universal healthcare, women's equality, and peaceful coexistence with the *[[soviet-union]]*.
- Severed ties with the Progressive Party and repudiated his accommodationist foreign policy views in 1950 after North Korea invaded South Korea, publicly supporting the military intervention authorized by the United Nations.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — the global ideological conflict whose early Truman Doctrine and containment policies he vehemently opposed in favor of international cooperation.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global war during which he oversaw domestic supply allocations and served as the Roosevelt administration's chief progressive spokesperson.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose agricultural science, executive branch policies, and mid-century political trajectory he fundamentally shaped.
- [[soviet-union]] — the rival socialist superpower with whom he urged continuous diplomatic rapprochement, mutual disarmament, and economic partnership.
- [[harry-truman]] — the Democratic rival who supplanted him on the 1944 presidential ticket and subsequently defeated his third-party challenge in the 1948 general election.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the crusade for racial justice that his 1948 campaign advanced by refusing to speak before racially segregated audiences during tours of the American South.
- [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] — the four-term president who elevated him to the Cabinet and the vice presidency as a key visionary of the welfare state.
