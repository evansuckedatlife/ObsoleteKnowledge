## summary

**Friedrich Paulus** was a German military commander who led the Sixth Army during *World War II*, most notably during the catastrophic Battle of Stalingrad. Trapped by a massive Red Army encirclement in late 1942, he surrendered his surviving forces in January 1943 after defying *Adolf Hitler*'s fanatical orders to commit suicide or fight to the last bullet. His surrender shattered the aura of German military invincibility, transformed him into an enduring symbol of Nazi collapse on the Eastern Front, and led to his postwar role as a prominent anti-Nazi voice in Soviet captivity.

## you gotta know

- Surrendered on January 31, 1943, from his headquarters in the basement of the ruined Univermag department store in Stalingrad, yielding over 90,000 surviving German soldiers to the *Soviet Union*.
- Promoted by *Adolf Hitler* to *Generalfeldmarschall* the very day before his capitulation, an explicit calculation reminding him that no German field marshal in military history had ever been captured alive.
- Trapped inside the pocket known as the *Kessel* following Operation Uranus, the massive Soviet pincer movement engineered by *Georgy Zhukov* and *Aleksandr Vasilevsky* that severed the Sixth Army's supply lines.
- Refused to attempt an unauthorized breakout to link up with Field Marshal *Erich von Manstein*'s relief effort, Operation Winter Storm, citing strict fuel shortages and Hitler's categorical ban on retreat.
- Relied on empty assurances from *Hermann Göring* that the Luftwaffe could supply the surrounded pocket with 300 tons of supplies daily by air, an operation that proved an abject logistics failure.
- A career staff officer rather than a frontline commander, he played an instrumental role in drafting the operational plans for Operation Barbarossa before inheriting command of the Sixth Army upon the sudden death of *Walter von Reichenau*.
- While held in Soviet captivity, he joined the anti-Nazi National Committee for a Free Germany (*Nationalkomitee Freies Deutschland*) and made propaganda radio broadcasts urging German soldiers to depose Hitler.
- Testified as a surprise prosecution witness at the 1946 Nuremberg International Military Tribunal, directly incriminating former colleagues *Wilhelm Keitel* and *Alfred Jodl* before spending his final years in East Germany.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — commanded the critical Axis army at the strategic turning point of the global war in Europe.
- [[germany]] — the state whose armed forces he served from imperial times through the Reichswehr and the Wehrmacht.
- [[soviet-union]] — the power whose Red Army trapped his forces at Stalingrad and held him in captivity for a decade.
- [[cold-war]] — spent his final years living in Dresden under the surveillance and patronage of the East German communist regime.
- [[french-resistance]] — news of his army's annihilation at Stalingrad electrified clandestine French resistance groups and demoralized collaborationist authorities.
- [[french-intelligence]] — Allied and Free French intelligence services tracked the strategic fallout of his capture to estimate German reserve redeployments.
