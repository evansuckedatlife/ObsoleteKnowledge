## summary

The **Works Progress Administration** (**WPA**), renamed the **Work Projects Administration** in 1939, was the largest and most ambitious American New Deal agency created by President [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] to provide emergency economic relief during the [[great-depression]] in the [[united-states]]. Established by executive order under the Emergency Relief Appropriation Act of 1935 and directed by *Harry Hopkins*, the agency employed more than eight million jobless citizens on public works infrastructure ranging from rural roads to municipal airports. Beyond heavy construction, its innovative Federal Project Number One extended federal patronage to writers, musicians, actors, and visual artists, permanently transforming American cultural heritage before the agency was liquidated in 1943 during [[world-war-ii]].

## you gotta know

- Administered by Roosevelt's close confidant *Harry Hopkins*, the agency spent approximately eleven billion dollars between 1935 and 1943, employing more than eight and a half million individuals across the [[united-states]].
- Reshaped national civil infrastructure by constructing or renovating over 650,000 miles of highways, 125,000 public buildings, 8,000 parks, and major transportation hubs such as [[new-york-city]]'s LaGuardia Airport.
- Financed Federal Project Number One, an unprecedented cultural initiative encompassing dedicated federal programs for art, music, theater, and writing that sustained thousands of creative professionals during the economic collapse.
- Sponsored the creation of thousands of public murals, posters, and easel paintings through the Federal Art Project, championing American [[realism]] and supporting artists such as *Thomas Hart Benton*, *Jackson Pollock*, and *Diego Rivera*.
- Documented American folklore and regional history through the Federal Writers' Project, which published the American Guide Series, compiled thousands of firsthand interviews for the *Slave Narrative Collection*, and employed authors central to [[american-literature]] including *Zora Neale Hurston*, *John Steinbeck*, and *Richard Wright*.
- Produced groundbreaking and socially conscious theatrical works under *Hallie Flanagan*'s Federal Theatre Project, staging the journalistic *Living Newspaper* series and *Orson Welles*'s celebrated 1936 all-Black production of [[william-shakespeare]]'s *Macbeth*.
- Attracted fierce political controversy and conservative accusations of communist sympathy and electoral patronage—satirized by detractors as "We Piddle Around"—which spurred passage of the 1939 Hatch Act and the shuttering of the theater division.
- Administered the National Youth Administration under *Aubrey Williams*, an auxiliary program providing part-time work and education grants to secondary and collegiate students, featuring prominent civil rights advocate *Mary McLeod Bethune* as Director of Negro Affairs.

## connections

- [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] — authorized the agency via Executive Order 7034 as a cornerstone of the Second New Deal relief strategy.
- [[great-depression]] — catastrophic economic collapse that produced mass unemployment and necessitated direct federal work relief.
- [[world-war-ii]] — industrial mobilization for the global conflict eliminated widespread joblessness and led to the agency's formal liquidation in 1943.
- [[harlem-renaissance]] — creative flowering whose visual artists and writers received vital economic support and national exposure through agency arts programs.
- [[united-states]] — sovereign nation whose physical geography, public architecture, and municipal services were comprehensively modernized by agency labor.
- [[american-literature]] — national canon enriched by agency-funded documentary investigations, state travel guides, and oral history archives.
- [[jazz]] — American musical tradition widely disseminated across communities through agency-subsidized public concerts and music education projects.
