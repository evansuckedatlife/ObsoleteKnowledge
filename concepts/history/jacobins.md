## summary

The **Jacobins** were the most powerful radical faction within the *French Revolution*, named after their meeting place in a former monastery (the Jacobin convent). The Jacobin Club evolved from a salon of moderate revolutionaries into a formidable political organization whose members—including *Robespierre*, *Danton*, and *Marat*—dominated the revolutionary government during the *Reign of Terror*. Their pursuit of republican ideals and revolutionary justice fundamentally shaped the Revolution's trajectory and legacy.

## you gotta know

- Originated as the Society of Friends of the Constitution (1789), meeting in the Jacobin convent in Paris to discuss revolutionary principles and debate how to implement the Revolution; the convent location gave them their enduring name.
- Grew into a nationwide network of clubs promoting revolutionary ideology, with over 2,000 affiliated societies by 1793—a powerful organizing structure that spread radical ideas throughout France and coordinated political action.
- Embodied radical revolutionary principles: republicanism (against any return to monarchy), popular sovereignty (power derived from the people), and economic equality—far more radical than the moderate Girondins, who sought constitutional monarchy or limited republic.
- Dominated the radical phase of the Revolution (1792–1794): controlled the Committee of Public Safety (the executive body) and drove the Reign of Terror, a period of systematic executions targeting perceived enemies of the Revolution.
- Advocated for the execution of King Louis XVI (which happened in 1793), the abolition of feudalism and the church, de-Christianization of France, and aggressive defense of the Revolution against internal counter-revolutionary plots and external invasion by monarchical powers.
- Under Robespierre's leadership, transformed into the instrument of Revolutionary Terror; implemented mass executions (40,000+ died, though many executions were local), virtue-centered ideology, and suppression of perceived counter-revolutionary activity, religious practice, and even rival revolutionary factions.
- Collapsed with Robespierre's execution in July 1794 (the Thermidor reaction), ending the Terror and ushering in the more conservative Directory; Jacobin power was broken, many surviving Jacobins were executed or imprisoned, and the Revolution's radical phase ended.
