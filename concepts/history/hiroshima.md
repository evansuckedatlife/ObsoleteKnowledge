## summary

**Hiroshima** is a Japanese port city on southwestern Honshu that became the first city in history targeted by an atomic weapon. On August 6, 1945, the *United States* bomber *Enola Gay* dropped the uranium bomb *Little Boy* directly over the city center, incinerating tens of thousands of residents instantly and leveling several square miles. Today, *Hiroshima* stands internationally as an enduring symbol of nuclear devastation, post-war reconstruction, and global advocacy for nuclear disarmament and peace.

## you gotta know

- Targeted on August 6, 1945, by the *United States Army Air Forces* B-29 Superfortress *Enola Gay*, piloted by Colonel *Paul Tibbets*, which released the atomic bomb *Little Boy*.
- The bomb detonated approximately 600 meters above the Shima Surgical Clinic with an explosive yield equivalent to roughly fifteen kilotons of TNT, instantly killing an estimated seventy to eighty thousand civilians and military personnel.
- Chosen as the primary target because of its dense urban layout, industrial works, and role as the headquarters of the Japanese Second General Army under Field Marshal *Shunroku Hata*.
- The Hiroshima Peace Memorial, widely known as the *Genbaku Dome* or Atomic Bomb Dome, was designed by Czech architect *Jan Letzel* and preserved in its ruined state as a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- The harrowing story of *Sadako Sasaki*, a young girl who developed radiation-induced leukemia and sought to fold one thousand origami cranes before dying at age twelve, inspired the Children's Peace Monument in the Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park.
- John Hersey published the landmark journalistic work *Hiroshima* in 1946 in *The New Yorker*, detailing the catastrophic aftermath through the firsthand accounts of six surviving *hibakusha*.
- Survivors known as *hibakusha* suffered long-term radiation sickness, severe keloid scarring, elevated leukemia rates, and pervasive social discrimination throughout postwar *Japan*.
