## summary

**Alexandria** was founded by *Alexander the Great* in 332 BCE on Egypt's Mediterranean coast and became the intellectual and commercial capital of the Hellenistic world. Home to the legendary Library of Alexandria and the Pharos lighthouse—one of antiquity's Seven Wonders—it remained a beacon of learning, trade, and cultural exchange under the *Ptolemaic* dynasty and later Roman rule. The city embodied the fusion of Greek and Egyptian civilizations and served as *Cleopatra*'s seat of power during her final reign.

## you gotta know

- Founded by *Alexander the Great* in 332 BCE after his conquest of Egypt, strategically chosen for its defensible natural harbor and position at the mouth of the Nile River.
- Became the capital of Egypt under the *Ptolemaic* dynasty following *Alexander*'s death, replacing the ancient inland capital of Memphis and establishing a new Hellenistic center.
- Named in honor of *Alexander* and intentionally designed as a cosmopolitan Greek city, blending Mediterranean culture with Egyptian traditions and attracting settlers from throughout the known world.
- The Library of Alexandria housed hundreds of thousands of scrolls and was the ancient world's greatest repository of knowledge, attracting scholars, philosophers, and poets from across the Mediterranean.
- The Pharos lighthouse, standing over 300 feet tall on the island of Pharos, guided Mediterranean shipping and ranked as one of antiquity's Seven Wonders and greatest engineering feats.
- Served as *Cleopatra VII*'s royal residence and seat of power during the final century of Ptolemaic rule, hosting her famous encounter with *Julius Caesar* and her alliance with *Mark Antony*.
- Declined after the *Roman Empire*'s conversion to *Christianity*, as pagan learning was suppressed, classical institutions were dismantled, and its role as a cosmopolitan intellectual center diminished.
