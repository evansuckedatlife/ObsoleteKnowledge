## summary

**South Carolina** was one of the original thirteen colonies and played a pivotal role in American history as a center of plantation slavery and a bastion of southern secession. Wealthy planter elites dominated the colony and state, building vast rice and cotton fortunes on enslaved African labor. The state first nullified federal authority in the 1830s and became the first to secede from the Union in 1861, igniting the Civil War.

## you gotta know

- Established as a British colony in 1670 with Charleston becoming a major Atlantic port for the slave trade and colonial commerce.
- Developed an extreme concentration of wealth and enslaved people; by 1800, the majority of South Carolina's population was enslaved Africans and African Americans, creating a slave-majority society.
- Rice plantations in the coastal lowcountry generated enormous wealth through slave labor, making slaveholders a planter aristocracy with outsized political influence over colonial and later state affairs.
- Home to the [[Nullification Crisis]] (1828–1833), when South Carolina asserted that states could reject federal tariffs they deemed unconstitutional, threatening national unity.
- Produced intellectual defenders of slavery, including John C. Calhoun, whose theories of states' rights and minority protection shaped Southern ideology and secession movements.
- First state to secede from the Union in December 1860 after Abraham Lincoln's election, directly triggering the [[American Civil War]].
- [[Fort Sumter]], where Confederate forces fired the first shots of the Civil War in April 1861, stands in Charleston Harbor as a symbol of the conflict's origin.
- Produced [[Robert Smalls]], an enslaved riverboat pilot who boldly seized a Confederate vessel and escaped to the North, later serving in Congress during Reconstruction as a champion of freedom.
