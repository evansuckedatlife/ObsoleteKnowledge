## summary

**William Jennings Bryan** (1860–1925) was a dominant figure in American politics for three decades, a three-time presidential candidate and fiery orator who championed agrarian interests and anti-establishment reform. He rose to national prominence in 1896 with his legendary "Cross of Gold" speech at the Democratic National Convention, which electrified rural voters opposed to monetary policies favoring Eastern industrialists. Though he never won the presidency, Bryan reshaped the Democratic Party and became the moral voice of American populism.

## you gotta know

- Born in Salem, Illinois; became a lawyer and Nebraska Congressman known for his moral oratory and anti-establishment rhetoric.
- Delivered the iconic "Cross of Gold" speech at the 1896 Democratic Convention, declaring "you shall not press down upon the brow of labor this crown of thorns; you shall not crucify mankind upon a cross of gold."
- Ran for president three times (1896, 1900, 1908), each time losing to Republican opponents despite commanding passionate support among farmers and laborers.
- Championed free silver — unlimited coinage of silver at a fixed ratio to gold — as a remedy for agricultural depression and rural debt.
- Criticized industrial monopolies and railroad trusts; was a leading voice of the populist and progressive movements attacking corporate power.
- Served as Secretary of State under Woodrow Wilson (1913–1915), opposing American entry into World War I on pacifist grounds.
- Became a cultural symbol of rural and religious America; his passionate defense of Christian fundamentalism during the 1925 Scopes trial was his final major public moment and legacy.
