## summary

**Claude McKay** was a Jamaican-born American poet, novelist, and journalist whose passionate writing helped ignite the *Harlem Renaissance* of the 1920s. Combining traditional European verse forms like the sonnet with fierce defiance against racial violence and colonial oppression, he became an essential voice of Black cultural identity and political radicalism. His work bridged Caribbean literature, American civil rights advocacy, and global revolutionary socialist movements.

## you gotta know

- Penned the famous Shakespearean sonnet *"If We Must Die"* during the violent racial unrest of the 1919 Red Summer, establishing an enduring anthem of collective Black self-defense and dignity.
- Published the landmark 1928 novel *Home to Harlem*, which became a major commercial success and won the Harmon Gold Award, though it sparked debate among Black intellectuals over its frank portrayal of nightlife.
- Authored the seminal 1922 poetry collection *Harlem Shadows*, widely credited by historians and literary critics with formally launching the *Harlem Renaissance*.
- Traveled to the *Soviet Union* in 1922 to attend the Fourth Congress of the Communist International, writing extensively about race and international class struggles before eventually growing disillusioned with authoritarian communism.
- Born in Sunny Ville, Jamaica, he began his literary career writing dialect poetry capturing local peasant life before emigrating to the *United States* in 1912.
- Converted to Roman *Catholicism* in his final years while living in Chicago, dedicating his time to educational and social work among the poor until his death in 1948.
