## summary

**John Locke** (1632–1704) was an English philosopher whose theories of *natural rights*, *empiricism*, and the *social contract* became foundational to liberal democracy, American constitutionalism, and modern political thought. His insistence that legitimate government rests on the consent of the governed and that individuals possess inalienable rights to life, liberty, and property profoundly shaped democratic revolutions and the Enlightenment.

## you gotta know

- Locke argued in *Two Treatises of Government* (1689) that humans in a *state of nature* possess inherent rights (life, liberty, property) and form governments via social contract to protect these rights—a radical vision that monarchies existed to serve people, not vice versa.
- His *empiricism*—the epistemological stance that knowledge derives from sensory experience, not innate ideas—opposed *Descartes*' *rationalism* and became the philosophical backbone of the scientific method and modern materialism.
- Locke championed religious toleration and the separation of church and state, arguing in *Letter on Toleration* that coerced faith is incoherent and that civil government has no warrant to enforce belief.
- His political theory directly influenced the *American Revolution* and the *U.S. Constitution*: the *Declaration of Independence* echoes his "life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness" (transposing his "property"), and constitutional protections of individual rights flow from his framework.
- Locke opposed absolute monarchy and advocated for limited, representative government with separated powers (executive, legislative, judicial)—a structure later refined by *Montesquieu* and embodied in the American system.
- His theory of *natural rights* became a rallying cry for liberal movements globally: if rights precede government, then tyranny violates a pre-political moral order, justifying resistance.
