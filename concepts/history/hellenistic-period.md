## summary

**Hellenistic Period** designates the three centuries of Mediterranean and Near Eastern history extending from the death of *Alexander the Great* in 323 BCE to the fall of the Ptolemaic Kingdom at the Battle of Actium in 31 BCE. Defined by the fragmentation of Alexander's realm into competing dynastic empires, the era saw the decline of the traditional independent Greek polis and the rise of autocratic, divine-right monarchies. It was an age of remarkable intellectual vitality, witnessing revolutionary breakthroughs in mathematics, astronomy, and engineering, alongside new philosophical schools addressing individual ethics.

## you gotta know

- Traditionally dated from 323 BCE, marking the death of *Alexander the Great* in Babylon, to 30 BCE, when *Octavian* annexed Egypt following the suicides of *Mark Antony* and *Cleopatra VII*.
- Opened with the turbulent *Wars of the Diadochi*, four decades of relentless conflict among Alexander's marshals, including *Perdiccas*, *Antigonus Monophthalmus*, *Ptolemy I Soter*, and *Seleucus I Nicator*, resulting in the permanent division of the imperial territory.
- Witnessed the foundation and flowering of the *Library of Alexandria* and the *Mouseion*, establishing state-sponsored scholarly institutions that revolutionized philology, textual criticism, and empirical cataloging.
- Produced unprecedented scientific and mathematical advancements, led by *Euclid*'s axiomatic geometry, *Archimedes*' principles of hydrostatics and mechanics, and *Eratosthenes*' remarkably accurate calculation of Earth's circumference.
- Gave rise to prominent philosophical systems tailored to an era of imperial upheaval, most notably *Stoicism*, founded by *Zeno of Citium*, and *Epicureanism*, developed by *Epicurus*, both emphasizing tranquility and personal ethics over civic participation.
- Pioneered dramatic, emotionally charged realism and theatrical dynamism in sculpture, producing iconic masterpieces such as the *Winged Victory of Samothrace*, the *Laocoön and His Sons*, and the *Pergamon Altar*.
- Culminated in the steady political absorption of the eastern Mediterranean kingdoms by the expanding [[roman-empire]], sealed by Roman victories at Cynoscephalae, Pydna, and Actium.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the Classical era whose municipal autonomy, artistic ideals, and civic politics were superseded by the monarchies of this period.
- [[hellenism]] — the overarching cultural and linguistic synthesis that flourished throughout these centuries.
- [[hellenistic-world]] — the physical realm of successor kingdoms and cosmopolitan urban centers where the events of this age played out.
- [[roman-empire]] — the rising Mediterranean superpower that systematically conquered and inherited the successor states, bringing the period to an end.
- [[middle-east]] — the vast region encompassing ancient Persia, Syria, and Mesopotamia that served as the primary arena of dynastic competition.
- [[silk-road]] — the burgeoning commercial network connecting Mediterranean merchants to Central Asia, India, and Han dynasty [[china]].
