## summary

**Edward of Woodstock**, famously celebrated by history as **the Black Prince**, was the eldest son and heir apparent of King *Edward III* of *[[england]]* and the most renowned English military commander of the Hundred Years' War. Acclaimed throughout medieval Europe for his tactical brilliance, he led devastating field victories against the forces of *[[france]]* at Crécy and Poitiers. Although he was celebrated as the paragon of fourteenth-century chivalry, he suffered debilitating illness and predeceased his father, never ascending to the throne he had fought so relentlessly to enrich.

## you gotta know

- Commanded the English vanguard at the *Battle of Crécy* (1346) at only sixteen years old, where his father famously refused to dispatch aid so the youth could "win his spurs."
- Won his most famous victory at the *Battle of Poitiers* (1356), outmaneuvering a much larger French army and capturing King *John II* of France for an exorbitant ransom.
- Pioneered and executed large-scale *chevauchées*—deep, mounted cavalry raids across the French countryside that destroyed food supplies, terrorized rural populations, and stripped revenue from the French crown.
- Governed the vast Duchy of Aquitaine as its prince starting in 1362, maintaining an opulent court at Bordeaux that alienated local Gascon nobility through high taxation.
- Intervened militarily in the Castilian Civil War in *[[spain]]*, winning a brilliant tactical victory at the *Battle of Nájera* (1367) to restore the deposed King *Peter of Castile*.
- Contracted a persistent, debilitating chronic illness during his Iberian campaign, likely dysentery or amoebiasis, which severely impaired his physical capabilities for the rest of his life.
- Commanded the notorious *Siege of Limoges* (1370), where contemporary chroniclers recorded the indiscriminate slaughter of hundreds of French inhabitants following the city's capitulation.
- Never referred to by his famous epithet during his lifetime; the nickname first appeared in Tudor-era writings, likely inspired by black armor, his tournament shield, or his ferocious reputation.
- Buried in *Canterbury Cathedral*, where his original heraldic surcoat, shield, gauntlets, and ornate bronze effigy remain preserved.

## connections

- [[england]] — the realm he represented as prince, establishing its military supremacy on continental European battlefields.
- [[france]] — the rival kingdom where he conducted destructive raiding campaigns and captured the reigning sovereign.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian theater where his intervention at Nájera broke his health and ruined his personal finances.
- [[christianity]] — the faith that underpinned medieval chivalric vows, royal legitimacy, and his burial shrine at Canterbury Cathedral.
- [[british-empire]] — the later imperial state whose national mythology romanticized his martial victories as early triumphs of English arms.
- [[renaissance]] — the subsequent cultural and historical era whose chroniclers popularized his enduring nickname.
