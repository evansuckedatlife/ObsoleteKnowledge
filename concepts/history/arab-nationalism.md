## summary

**Arab nationalism** is a political and cultural movement asserting shared identity and collective interests among Arabs across the Middle East and North Africa. Emerging from resistance to Ottoman and European colonial rule, it produced secular nationalist ideologies, pan-Arabist unity projects, and modern nation-states across the region.

## you gotta know

- Began in the late 19th century as an intellectual movement among Arab intellectuals under *Ottoman Empire* rule, influenced by European nationalism and enabled by printing presses and journalism.
- Early figures: *Butrus al-Bustani* and *Ibrahim al-Yaziji* articulated Arab linguistic and cultural identity distinct from Ottomanism; *Abd al-Rahman al-Kawakibi* critiqued Ottoman religious authority.
- Gained momentum after World War I with the collapse of the *Ottoman Empire* and the *Sykes-Picot Agreement's* arbitrary borders, fracturing the former Ottoman heartland into mandates and nation-states.
- *Gamal Abdel Nasser* embodied its peak: the 1952 Egyptian Revolution, nationalization of the *Suez Canal*, the *Suez Crisis* (1956), and the dream of pan-Arab union (merger with Syria, 1958–1961).
- Central ideologies: Nasserism (Arab socialism + anti-imperialism), *Ba'athism* (*Ba'ath Party* in Syria and Iraq), communist variants, and varying degrees of Islamic integration (debated whether Arab nationalism was secular or religiously grounded).
- Contested *Zionism* and the establishment of *Israel*, framing it as a colonial intrusion backed by Western powers; motivated successive Arab-Israeli wars (1948, 1956, 1967, 1973) and Palestinian nationalism.
- Fractured by inter-Arab rivalries (Iraq vs. Syria, Saudi Arabia vs. Egypt, monarchies vs. republicans), Cold War alignments (Soviet vs. American patronage), and sectarian divisions (Sunni vs. Shia).
- Economic disparities and oil wealth concentrated power: wealthy monarchies (Saudi Arabia, UAE) pursued national interests over Arab unity; Egypt's declining influence after 1973 weakened the movement.
- By late 20th century, superseded in many contexts by Islamism (*Muslim Brotherhood*, *Hamas*), national patriotism, and sectarian identities; the *Arab Spring* (2011) briefly revived Arab nationalism before sectarian conflict resurged.
