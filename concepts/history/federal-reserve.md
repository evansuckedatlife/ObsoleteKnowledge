## summary

The **Federal Reserve** is the central banking institution of the [[united-states]], created in 1913 to furnish an elastic currency, afford means of rediscounting commercial paper, and establish more effective supervision of national banking. Formed by the [[federal-government-united-states]] under President *Woodrow Wilson* following recurring financial panics, the system combines twelve regional reserve banks with a governing central board in Washington, D.C. Its monetary policies, reserve requirements, and emergency lending capabilities have played a decisive role in shaping twentieth-century macroeconomic performance, economic stability, and financial crisis management.

## you gotta know

- Established by the *Federal Reserve Act* of 1913, signed into law by *Woodrow Wilson* following the Panic of 1907 and the confidential 1910 *Jekyll Island* meeting organized by Rhode Island Senator *Nelson Aldrich*.
- Organized as a decentralized institutional structure comprising twelve regional Federal Reserve Banks situated in major commercial centers such as [[new-york-city]], coordinated by a public Board of Governors appointed by the president and confirmed by the Senate.
- Regulates the supply of money and credit through core operational mechanisms including the discount rate, reserve ratio requirements, and open market operations overseen by the *Federal Open Market Committee*.
- Subject to a statutory dual mandate enacted by Congress requiring the system to pursue both maximum sustainable employment and long-term price stability.
- Famously critiqued by economists *Milton Friedman* and *Anna Schwartz* in *A Monetary History of the United States*, who argued that the central bank deepened catastrophic economic distress during the late 1920s and 1930s by contracting the money supply and failing to provide emergency liquidity to solvent banks facing depositor runs.
- Substantially restructured by the *Banking Act of 1935*, pushed by Board Chair *Marriner Eccles*, which stripped regional banks of open market autonomy and concentrated supervisory power within the national board.
- Addressed historical demands for currency elasticity and liquidity that had previously driven agrarian revolts, such as the warehouse credit proposals advanced by the [[farmers-alliance]].
- Exercised extensive constitutional authority under the [[federal-commerce-power]] to oversee interstate financial transactions, commercial credit markets, and bank holding companies.
- Guided through severe post-war macroeconomic challenges by chairs such as *Paul Volcker*, who broke double-digit inflation through aggressive interest rate hikes, and *Alan Greenspan*.

## connections

- [[federal-government-united-states]] — sovereign authority that chartered the central bank, oversees its mandates, and appoints its governing leadership.
- [[federal-commerce-power]] — constitutional foundation enabling national supervision over interstate banking networks, commercial credit, and monetary instruments.
- [[farmers-alliance]] — nineteenth-century agrarian movement whose agitation for monetary expansion and elastic federal credit anticipated the central bank's liquidity functions.
- [[new-york-city]] — financial capital hosting the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, the operational arm that executes open market operations.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformative economic era whose recurring liquidity panics demonstrated the necessity for a permanent lender of last resort.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflict immediately following the bank's creation during which the system facilitated war financing and bond sales.
