## summary

**British politics** refers to the system of government and political activity in the United Kingdom, characterized by parliamentary democracy, constitutional monarchy, and party competition. From the 19th century onward, British politics became the model for liberal democracy in the English-speaking world, with expanding suffrage, cabinet government, and competing party ideologies. The political evolution of Britain—from aristocratic oligarchy to mass democracy—shaped not only the nation but influenced democratic movements globally.

## you gotta know

- The Glorious Revolution (1688) and subsequent settlement established parliamentary supremacy over the monarchy, creating the foundation for constitutional democracy.
- The Great Reform Act (1832) expanded voting rights and redistributed electoral representation, establishing a pattern of incremental democratic expansion throughout the 19th century.
- The Liberal and Conservative parties emerged as the dominant competing forces; ideological shifts between free trade liberalism and protective conservatism defined 19th-century politics.
- Women's suffrage became a major political battleground; *Emmeline Pankhurst* and the suffragettes waged a campaign of civil disobedience that ultimately succeeded in 1928 (age 21).
- The rise of the Labour Party in the early 20th century fundamentally altered British politics, representing working-class interests and challenging the Liberal-Conservative duopoly.
- British politics adapted to decolonization, the welfare state, and managed economic decline in the post-World War II era; tensions between social democracy and market liberalism persist.
