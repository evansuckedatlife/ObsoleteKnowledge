## summary

The **battleship era**, also termed the **dreadnought era** or the **age of the battleship**, was the epoch in modern naval history spanning from the late nineteenth century through [[world-war-ii]] when heavily armored, large-caliber gun capital ships represented the ultimate measure of military prestige and global dominance. Emerged from the metallurgical and steam breakthroughs of the [[industrial-revolution]], this strategic doctrine held that national supremacy hinged upon the possession of a battle fleet capable of defeating rival warships in decisive fleet action. The era incited ruinously expensive imperial arms races, shaped high-seas diplomacy during confrontations like the [[spanish-american-war]] and [[world-war-i]], and redefined global geopolitics before carrier-based naval aviation rendered big-gun surface combatants obsolete.

## you gotta know

- *HMS Dreadnought*, commissioned by the [[british-empire]] in 1906, revolutionized naval architecture with its uniform battery of ten twelve-inch guns and steam turbine propulsion, instantly rendering every existing pre-dreadnought battleship obsolete.
- The mysterious explosion of the second-class battleship *USS Maine* in Havana harbor in 1898 precipitated the [[spanish-american-war]], highlighting the rising diplomatic and naval stakes surrounding modern armored warships.
- An intense Anglo-German naval arms race prior to [[world-war-i]] spurred unprecedented shipbuilding programs, culminating in the massive 1916 *Battle of Jutland*, the largest surface fleet clash between dreadnoughts in history.
- The 1922 *Washington Naval Treaty* sought to prevent a destabilizing postwar naval race by establishing strict capital ship tonnage ratios and imposing construction moratoria among the world's leading naval powers.
- The era witnessed its dramatic zenith and demise during [[world-war-ii]], as heavily armored super-battleships like Germany's *Bismarck* and Japan's *Yamato* proved fatally vulnerable to coordinated strikes by carrier-launched aircraft.
- The theoretical doctrine underpinning the epoch was articulated by *Alfred Thayer Mahan* in his seminal 1890 work *The Influence of Sea Power upon History*, which argued that command of the sea via decisive battle was essential for imperial greatness.
- Technological roots of the era traced back to the [[american-civil-war]], where the revolutionary clash between the ironclads *USS Monitor* and *CSS Virginia* demonstrated the absolute necessity of armored hulls and revolving gun turrets.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — supplied the advances in steel production, steam turbines, armor-piercing shells, and heavy machinery required to build gigantic capital ships.
- [[world-war-i]] — marked the operational peak of dreadnought fleets, characterized by distant blockade, strategic caution, and the clash at Jutland.
- [[world-war-ii]] — demonstrated the eclipse of the big-gun warship by the aircraft carrier and the submarine in oceanic warfare.
- [[british-empire]] — sought to maintain global naval supremacy through its Two-Power Standard and by initiating the dreadnought revolution.
- [[germany]] — challenged British maritime dominance through the ambitious fleet acts promoted by *Kaiser Wilhelm II* and *Grand Admiral Alfred von Tirpitz*.
- [[japan]] — rose to major naval power status after defeating the [[russian-empire]] at Tsushima and went on to construct the largest battleships ever floated.
- [[united-states]] — demonstrated its ascending global reach by dispatching the sixteen battleships of the Great White Fleet on a worldwide circumnavigation in 1907.
