## summary

**Indigenous Language Revitalization** is the collective effort by native communities worldwide to restore, preserve, and promote languages suppressed or displaced by colonialism, assimilation policies, and dominant global languages. Many indigenous languages faced extinction as colonial powers imposed their own tongues and restricted traditional education. Modern revitalization movements represent both cultural survival and political reclamation by indigenous peoples asserting their identity and rights.

## you gotta know

- Driven by the recognition that indigenous languages carry unique cultural knowledge, spiritual traditions, ecological understanding, and ways of perceiving the world that are lost when languages die; language loss means cultural erasure.
- Often responds to historical suppression policies: residential schools for Native Americans that forbade native languages, colonial bans on indigenous language use in public settings, and systemic pressure toward assimilation into dominant languages.
- Involves community-based initiatives: immersion schools where children are taught entirely in indigenous languages, language documentation projects recording endangered tongues, online resources and apps for learning, and intergenerational teaching programs connecting elders with youth.
- The [[Ainu]] language of Japan, once suppressed by the state, is undergoing revival efforts as Japan (belatedly) recognizes Ainu as indigenous rights and cultural heritage; Ainu museums and language centers now operate throughout Hokkaido.
- The [[Sámi]] languages of northern Scandinavia face revival initiatives amid growing recognition of the Sámi people's distinct indigenous identity; Norway, Sweden, and Finland have adopted policies supporting Sámi language education in schools.
- Many Native American nations (Navajo, Cherokee, Lakota, Hawaiians, and others) are implementing language programs to teach younger generations; some operate immersion schools and produce media content in indigenous languages.
- Linked to broader movements for indigenous sovereignty and the recognition that cultural survival depends on linguistic continuity; language preservation has become a legal right in some jurisdictions, protected alongside indigenous land and political rights.
