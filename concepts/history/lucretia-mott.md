## summary

**Lucretia Mott** (1793–1880) was an American Quaker activist and pioneer of the women's rights movement, spending seven decades fighting for female equality, abolition, and social justice. Excluded from speaking at an abolitionist convention because of her gender, Mott resolved to organize for women's rights and co-organized the Seneca Falls Convention (1848) with *Elizabeth Cady Stanton*. Her moral clarity, oratorical power, and unwavering commitment to radical equality made her one of the most influential female activists of the 19th century.

## you gotta know

- Born into a Quaker family, which emphasized conscience, social activism, and gender equality; became a Quaker minister herself at age 28, defying religious convention.
- Witnessed and participated in the abolitionist movement during the 1830s-1840s; became radicalized about women's second-class status after being silenced at an 1840 antislavery convention in London.
- Co-organized the Seneca Falls Convention (1848) with *Elizabeth Cady Stanton*, the first formal women's rights convention in American history; drafted its Declaration of Sentiments modeled on the Declaration of Independence.
- Became a national figure in the women's suffrage movement; lectured extensively on women's equality, abolition, and temperance reform across America and Europe.
- Worked to build bridges across reform movements, arguing that women's emancipation was essential to universal human liberty and social progress for all peoples.
- Remained active in reform until her death at 87 in 1880, achieving personal satisfaction in witnessing the early successes of the women's movement though not living to see female suffrage.
