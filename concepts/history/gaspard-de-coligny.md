## summary

**Gaspard de Coligny** was a French nobleman, military commander, and Admiral of France who rose to become the preeminent political and military leader of the Protestant Huguenots during the French Wars of Religion. After converting to Calvinism, he exerted deep personal and political influence over the young King *Charles IX*, advocating for a united French foreign policy against Spanish hegemony. His targeted assassination in August 1572 served as the immediate catalyst for the horrific *St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre* across [[france]].

## you gotta know

- Born into the illustrious Montmorency family, he distinguished himself in combat during the Italian Wars before attaining the high rank of Admiral of [[france]].
- Converted to Reformed Protestantism in the late 1550s while imprisoned in the Low Countries, emerging alongside the *Prince of Condé* as the champion of the Huguenots.
- Promoted early French colonial ventures during the [[age-of-exploration]], sponsoring Huguenot settlements in South America (*France Antarctique* in modern Brazil) and Florida to create Protestant refuges.
- Commanded Protestant armies across numerous pitched battles during the early Wars of Religion, displaying extraordinary tenacity and strategic resilience even after severe tactical defeats.
- Gained the ear of King *Charles IX* following the 1570 Peace of Saint-Germain-en-Laye, urging the monarch to intervene in the Dutch Revolt to unify French Catholics and Protestants against [[spain]].
- Aroused the fierce hostility of the ultra-Catholic House of Guise and the Queen Mother *Catherine de' Medici*, who viewed his anti-Spanish war policy and court ascendancy with acute alarm.
- Survived an initial assassination attempt by an arquebus sniper on August 22, 1572, while walking home from the royal Louvre palace in Paris.
- Murdered in his sickbed on August 24, 1572, by assassins led by the *Duke of Guise*; his defenestrated and mutilated corpse was displayed to the Parisian populace, igniting the *St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre*.

## connections

- [[france]] — the kingdom he defended in foreign campaigns and within which he led the Huguenot faction during devastating civil wars.
- [[christianity]] — the overarching faith whose deep theological rupture between Roman Catholicism and Protestantism defined his life and tragic demise.
- [[spain]] — the rival Catholic superpower whose European dominance he sought to challenge by allying with rebel Dutch Protestants.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the era of maritime expansion during which he financed French voyages of colonization to the Americas.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural and intellectual epoch that formed his early education, humanist court life, and military career.
