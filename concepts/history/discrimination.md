## summary

**Discrimination** is the unfair or prejudicial treatment of individuals or groups based on characteristics such as race, gender, national origin, religion, disability, or socioeconomic class. In legal and historical contexts, it encompasses institutionalized systems of subjugation, disenfranchisement, and segregation enforced by state authority or systemic social exclusion. Overcoming entrenched discrimination has been the defining catalyst for landmark constitutional amendments, global human rights charters, and twentieth-century civil rights legislation.

## you gotta know

- Distinguishes doctrinally between *de jure* discrimination, which is explicitly codified in statutory law or administrative policies, and *de facto* discrimination, which persists through societal customs and economic inequality.
- Institutionalized in the post-Civil War American South through *Jim Crow* statutes, poll taxes, literacy tests, and grandfather clauses designed to systematically disenfranchise Black citizens.
- Challenged fundamentally by the landmark Supreme Court ruling *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954), which unanimously overturned the "separate but equal" doctrine established in *Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896).
- Targeted federally by the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which outlawed discrimination in public accommodations, employment, and federally assisted programs based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.
- Safeguarded at the ballot box by the Voting Rights Act of 1965, which established federal oversight mechanisms to abolish discriminatory voting practices across southern jurisdictions.
- Barred in housing and residential lending by the Fair Housing Act of 1968, addressing discriminatory practices such as racial covenants and systemic redlining.
- Excluded minority groups historically from elite professions, executive leadership, and foreign diplomatic postings, prompting targeted executive orders to integrate public institutions.
- Analyzed in contemporary jurisprudence through standards of judicial review, requiring "strict scrutiny" under the Equal Protection Clause for governmental classifications involving suspect classes.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — mobilized nationwide campaigns of nonviolent resistance to dismantle legalized racial apartheid in the *United States*.
- [[14th-amendment]] — provided the primary constitutional weapon against state discrimination via the Equal Protection and Due Process clauses.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — served as the foundational legal framework tested and reshaped by anti-discrimination jurisprudence.
- [[diplomatic-service]] — barred minority diplomats historically until desegregation initiatives opened bilateral ambassadorial roles to women and people of color.
- [[enlightenment]] — established philosophical theories of universal human equality and inherent rights that directly challenged feudal and racial hierarchies.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — attempted initial federal interventions to protect formerly enslaved populations through landmark civil rights legislation and constitutional amendments.
