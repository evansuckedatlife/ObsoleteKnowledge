## summary

The **Hundred Days**, also known historically as the **War of the Seventh Coalition** or the **Cent-Jours**, was the climactic 1815 period spanning *Napoleon Bonaparte*'s escape from exile on the island of Elba to the second restoration of the Bourbon monarchy under King *Louis XVIII*. Beginning with Napoleon's audacious march on Paris without firing a shot, this unexpected resurgence panicked the European delegates assembled at the *Congress of Vienna*, who proclaimed him an outlaw and combined their military might. The dramatic episode ended on the rolling fields of modern Belgium at the *Battle of Waterloo*, sealing the permanent downfall of imperial France and the pacification of nineteenth-century Europe.

## you gotta know

- Commenced on March 1, 1815, when *Napoleon Bonaparte* landed at Golfe-Juan on the southern coast of [[france]] with approximately one thousand veteran guardsmen after slipping away from his Mediterranean principality on Elba aboard the brig *Inconstant*.
- Secured control of the country through the legendary encounter at Laffrey near Grenoble, where Napoleon walked out alone before royalist infantry of the 5th Regiment of the Line, opened his greatcoat, and proclaimed, *“If there is among you a soldier who would kill his Emperor, here I am.”*
- Prompted King *Louis XVIII* to flee the Tuileries Palace under cover of night, enabling Napoleon to enter the French capital on March 20, 1815, and reclaim imperial power without spilling French blood.
- Provoked the assembled delegates at the *Congress of Vienna* to issue an unprecedented joint declaration labeling Napoleon an outlaw who had placed himself outside the protection of civil society.
- Generated the *Seventh Coalition*, uniting Britain, Prussia, Austria, and Russia in an alliance that pledged to keep hundreds of thousands of troops in the field until Napoleon was permanently neutralized.
- Promulgated the *Acte Additionnel*, an amended constitutional charter drafted alongside the liberal politician *Benjamin Constant*, in a calculated effort to rally French republicans and liberals to the imperial standard.
- Concluded militarily with the *Waterloo Campaign* in the Low Countries, where Napoleon defeated *Gebhard Leberecht von Blücher*'s Prussians at the *Battle of Ligny* but suffered catastrophic defeat days later at the *Battle of Waterloo*.
- Ended officially with Napoleon's second abdication on June 22, 1815, his surrender to the British warship *HMS Bellerophon*, and his final, heavily guarded exile to the South Atlantic island of Saint Helena.

## connections

- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the exiled French emperor whose bold return to power and final military defeat define this historic interval.
- [[france]] — the continental empire that briefly rallied behind the restoration of the imperial regime before submitting to Bourbon rule.
- [[england]] — the maritime empire that spearheaded the coalition diplomacy and commanded the allied army holding the line in Belgium.
- [[french-revolution]] — the monumental political upheaval whose revolutionary institutions and military doctrines Napoleon sought once more to defend.
- [[battle-of-waterloo]] — the decisive engagement near Brussels where coalition forces permanently shattered imperial French military power.
- [[duke-of-wellington]] — the Anglo-Allied field marshal whose disciplined defensive positions on the ridge of Mont-Saint-Jean thwarted French assaults.
- [[coalition-wars]] — the sweeping sequence of European campaigns against revolutionary and imperial France, terminating with this final seventh conflict.
