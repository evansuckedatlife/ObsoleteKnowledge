## summary

**Capital punishment** is the legal imposition of death as punishment for crimes, practiced across cultures and centuries but increasingly restricted in the modern era. The history of capital punishment reflects evolving moral philosophy, changing methods, and deep social disagreement about state authority to execute. Controversial trials and cases became flashpoints in broader debates about justice, mercy, and the deterrent effect of execution.

## you gotta know

- Practiced since ancient times in nearly all societies; methods varied widely—from crucifixion and beheading to hanging and, later, electrocution and lethal injection.
- In medieval Europe, execution was public spectacle; punishment was conspicuously severe to deter crime, with the body displayed as a warning. The shift toward private execution reflected changing philosophical views of punishment.
- The 19th and 20th centuries saw intense moral and practical debate: abolitionists argued execution was immoral or ineffective; retentionists argued it was justified punishment and necessary deterrent.
- The *Sacco and Vanzetti* case (1927) became a symbol of capital punishment's dangers; the controversial execution of the anarchists catalyzed international protest and doubt about justice.
- The *Clutter family murders* case (1959) and subsequent trials illustrated capital punishment's role in American criminal justice; Truman Capote's *In Cold Blood* (1966) sparked renewed moral questioning.
- The United States remains the only Western democracy that practices capital punishment; abolition movements have achieved success in Europe and many other nations, while the U.S. continues to execute.
