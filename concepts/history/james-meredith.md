## summary

**James Meredith** was an African American civil rights pioneer whose 1962 enrollment at the University of Mississippi became one of the defining battles of the civil rights movement. His courageous determination to attend a segregated institution, supported by federal intervention and defended by figures like [[medgar-evers]], forced the nation to confront the reality of institutional racism. His victory opened higher education to Black students across the South and vindicated the long struggle for equal access.

## you gotta know

- Refused to accept Mississippi's segregation and applied to the University of Mississippi in 1961, knowing rejection would be certain but deliberate.
- His admission required direct intervention by Attorney General Robert Kennedy and deployment of federal marshals to protect him on campus.
- Governor Ross Barnett led the state's massive resistance campaign, with riots erupting as Meredith arrived for his first day of classes.
- Endured severe social ostracism, threats, and physical isolation as the only Black student among thousands of hostile white peers.
- His presence at Ole Miss became a symbol of federal power enforcing civil rights law over state defiance.
- Continued his education and later became a prominent speaker on civil rights and Black nationalism.
