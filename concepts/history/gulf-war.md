## summary

The **Gulf War** (1990–1991), also called the *Persian Gulf War* or *Operation Desert Storm*, was a rapid military conflict in which a U.S.-led international coalition decisively defeated *Iraq* and expelled it from occupied *Kuwait*. Fought in the immediate aftermath of the *Cold War*, the war demonstrated American military supremacy in the post-Soviet era and left lasting consequences for Middle Eastern stability and American foreign policy. It lasted only 100 combat hours but killed tens of thousands.

## you gotta know

- *Saddam Hussein*'s Iraq invaded and annexed Kuwait in August 1990, threatening global oil supplies and regional stability.
- President *George H. W. Bush* assembled a broad international coalition, including Arab allies, and secured U.N. authorization for military action.
- The air campaign (Operation Desert Storm, January–February 1991) showcased advanced U.S. technology, including stealth aircraft and precision-guided munitions.
- The brief ground war lasted only 100 hours; Iraqi forces were rapidly routed from Kuwait, though Saddam remained in power.
- Resulted in perhaps 100,000+ Iraqi deaths (both civilian and military combined) versus fewer than 400 coalition casualties, a stark and lopsided disparity in military capability.
- Left unresolved tensions: Iraq remained a regional power, sanctions persisted, and residual instability contributed to future U.S. interventions.
- Demonstrated America's overwhelming military superiority in the post-Cold War era, reinforcing perceptions of U.S. dominance and raising public expectations for swift military solutions to regional conflicts.
- Left a lasting legacy of American military presence in the Middle East, including permanent bases in Saudi Arabia that became a significant grievance motivating terrorist attacks, including the 9/11 attacks on the United States.
