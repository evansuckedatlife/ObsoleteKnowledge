## summary

**Absolute monarchy**, or **absolutism**, is a political system and form of government in which unlimited, centralized sovereign authority is vested entirely in a single hereditary ruler. Reaching its zenith across continental Europe during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, it was most famously epitomized by King *Louis XIV* of [[france]], who systematically subordinated the feudal nobility to central royal administration. Grounded historically in the doctrine of the *divine right of kings*, absolutism fostered the creation of professional standing armies, expansive state bureaucracies, and mercantilist taxation systems before succumbing to the transformative pressures of the [[enlightenment]] and the [[french-revolution]].

## you gotta know

- King *Louis XIV* of [[france]], celebrated as the "Sun King," established the definitive model of European absolutist rule, reigning for seventy-two years and allegedly proclaiming *"L'État, c'est moi"* ("I am the State").
- Constructed the lavish *Palace of Versailles* outside Paris, compelling high-ranking aristocrats to reside at court and compete for ceremonial favor, effectively neutering noble rebellion and political resistance.
- Justified on theological grounds by the *divine right of kings*, formulated by Catholic theorists such as Bishop *Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet*, who argued that monarchs were God's earthly lieutenants answerable to no mortal law or parliament.
- Defended from a secular perspective by English philosopher *Thomas Hobbes* in his 1651 masterpiece *Leviathan*, which maintained that an undivided, absolute sovereign was the only safeguard against anarchy and violent civil war.
- Relied heavily on economic mercantilism orchestrated by state officials like French finance minister *Jean-Baptiste Colbert*, who enforced tariffs, monopolized industries, and acquired overseas colonies to fill royal treasuries.
- Adapted during the mid-eighteenth century into *enlightened absolutism*, under which sovereigns such as *Frederick the Great* of Prussia, *Catherine the Great* of the [[russian-empire]], and *Joseph II* of Austria applied rational administrative reforms, legal codification, and religious tolerance while fiercely retaining autocratic authority.
- Provoked fierce constitutional resistance in [[england]], where seventeenth-century attempts by *James I* and *Charles I* to govern without parliamentary consent led directly to the English Civil War, regicide, and the eventual passage of the 1689 Bill of Rights.
- Bypassed customary medieval institutions—including regional estates, parliaments, and guild jurisdictions—by establishing permanent standing armies and dispatching royal intendants to collect taxes and enforce central royal decrees.

## connections

- [[france]] — the archetype of early modern absolutist centralization under the Bourbon monarchy and the reign of *Louis XIV*.
- [[french-revolution]] — the explosive 1789 upheaval that toppled the Bourbon dynasty, demolished feudal privileges, and replaced absolutism with popular sovereignty.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical movement whose core doctrines of natural rights, social contracts, and separation of powers dismantled the intellectual legitimacy of absolutism.
- [[russian-empire]] — an enduring autocratic power where tsars like *Peter the Great* and *Catherine the Great* wielded absolute imperial command over state and serfdom.
- [[england]] — the primary European counter-example, whose parliamentary traditions and Glorious Revolution curbed royal authority and established a constitutional monarchy.
- [[spain]] — an early absolutist powerhouse under the Habsburg monarchs *Charles V* and *Philip II*, utilizing imperial silver to finance continental hegemony.
- [[colonialism]] — imperial expansion heavily driven by absolutist monarchs seeking bullion and captive markets through chartered trade monopolies.
