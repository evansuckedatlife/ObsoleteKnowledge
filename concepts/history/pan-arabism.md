## summary

**Pan-Arabism** is a political and cultural movement that advocates for the social, economic, and political unification of the nations and peoples of the Arab world across North Africa and West Asia. Emerging in the early twentieth century during the decline of the [[ottoman-empire]] and cresting during the 1950s and 1960s under Egyptian president *Gamal Abdel Nasser*, the movement positioned secular solidarity as the premier vehicle for collective liberation from European [[colonialism]]. It fundamentally reshaped the modern [[middle-east]], inspiring state mergers like the *United Arab Republic* and altering the course of the [[cold-war]] before collapsing in the wake of catastrophic defeats during the [[arab-israeli-conflict]].

## you gotta know

- Reached the peak of its popular influence under Egyptian president *Gamal Abdel Nasser*, whose defiance of Western powers during the 1956 Suez Crisis and charismatic radio addresses on *Voice of the Arabs* established Cairo as the political capital of Arab solidarity.
- Achieved its most significant institutional milestone through the *United Arab Republic*, an ambitious political union between Egypt and Syria established in 1958 that dissolved when a Syrian military coup seceded from the state in 1961.
- Suffered a catastrophic and permanent ideological decline following the 1967 Six-Day War, a watershed clash in the [[arab-israeli-conflict]] whose rapid Israeli victory discredited secular nationalist leaders and opened the path for the resurgence of political [[islam]].
- Formed the ideological foundation of the *Ba'ath Party*, co-founded in Damascus by *Michel Aflaq* and *Salah al-Din al-Bitar*, which paired national unity with state socialism and later produced competing authoritarian regimes in Syria and Iraq.
- Crystallized as an anti-imperialist mass movement following [[world-war-i]], when the secret 1916 Sykes-Picot Agreement saw the [[british-empire]] and [[france]] renege on wartime promises to the *Hashemite* dynasty by partitioning the Levant into colonial mandates.
- Played a pivotal role in the geopolitics of the [[cold-war]], as nationalist leaders sought international leverage by co-founding the *Non-Aligned Movement* while securing substantial military weapons and developmental aid from the [[soviet-union]].
- Found its primary intellectual formulation in the secular cultural theories of Ottoman-Syrian educator *Sati' al-Husri*, who argued that shared Arabic language and common historical experience defined nationhood regardless of religious identity.

## connections

- [[middle-east]] — the central geographic and cultural arena where the movement sought to eradicate imperial boundaries and forge political cohesion.
- [[cold-war]] — the global rivalry in which nationalist governments positioned themselves between superpowers to obtain financial subsidies and advanced armaments.
- [[arab-israeli-conflict]] — the enduring military and diplomatic struggle that served as the primary unifying cause for Arab solidarity until the 1967 war broke its momentum.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the imperial domain whose disintegration during the early twentieth century spurred intellectuals to define an independent Arab political identity.
- [[colonialism]] — the system of European mandate rule and economic extraction that Arab nationalists mobilized against in their quest for regional sovereignty.
- [[soviet-union]] — the communist superpower that provided essential diplomatic cover, economic assistance, and heavy weaponry to nationalist states.
- [[british-empire]] — the dominant colonial power whose diplomatic betrayals and military presence generated widespread anti-Western resistance across the region.
