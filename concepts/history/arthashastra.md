## summary

The **Arthashastra** (or **Arthaśāstra**) is an ancient Indian Sanskrit treatise on statecraft, political philosophy, economic administration, and military strategy. Attributed to the scholar and royal advisor *Kautilya* (also identified as *Chanakya* or *Vishnugupta*), the work is traditionally associated with the rise and governance of the *Mauryan Empire* during the late fourth century BCE, though its extant redactions date through the early centuries CE. Renowned for its uncompromising political realism and exhaustive bureaucratic detail, it explores the acquisition and preservation of dominion (*artha*), earning widespread comparison in world literature to the political philosophy of *Niccolò Machiavelli*.

## you gotta know

- Authorship is traditionally credited to *Kautilya* (also called *Chanakya*), the chief advisor and strategist who orchestrated the rise of *Chandragupta Maurya* to found the *Mauryan Empire*.
- Modern knowledge of the text began with its dramatic 1905 rediscovery by Sanskrit librarian *Rudrapaṭna Shamasastry*, who located a palm-leaf manuscript in Grantha script at Mysore and published the first translation in 1915.
- Formulates the *mandala* ("circle of states") theory of foreign relations, famously asserting that an immediate border neighbor is a natural adversary, while that rival's neighbor forms a natural geopolitical ally.
- Defines sovereignty through the *saptanga* ("seven limbs") doctrine, conceptualizing the state as an organic body composed of ruler (*swami*), ministers (*amatya*), territory (*janapada*), fortified city (*durga*), treasury (*kosha*), army (*danda*), and allies (*mitra*).
- Systematizes four primary instruments of diplomatic policy (*upayas*): conciliation (*sama*), economic concessions or gifts (*dana*), subversion and dividing enemies (*bheda*), and military force (*danda*).
- Mandates an omnipresent covert intelligence service, directing undercover agents, ascetics, courtesans, and poisoners to assess bureaucratic loyalty, weed out corruption, and destabilize enemy kingdoms.
- Spans fifteen books (*adhikaranas*) detailing economic statecraft, establishing state monopolies over mineral extraction, timber, and liquor while prescribing civil law codes, disaster management, and wartime logistics.

## connections

- [[buddhism]] — religion adopted by Chandragupta's grandson *Ashoka*, whose state edicts promoting moral piety and nonviolence (*ahimsa*) directly diverged from Kautilyan realpolitik.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Mediterranean culture linked to the Mauryan court via Seleucid diplomats such as *Megasthenes*, whose *Indica* complements the treatise's administrative descriptions.
- [[silk-road]] — trans-Eurasian trade network that carried Indian luxury manufactures regulated under the state trade guidelines and customs duties detailed in the text.
- [[china]] — civilization producing contemporaneous Legalist political philosophy under figures like *Han Feizi* and strategic military thought comparable to *Sun Tzu*.
- [[roman-empire]] — western power whose lucrative maritime trade with southern and western Indian ports engaged the commercial monitoring systems outlined in later recensions.
- [[enlightenment]] — modern intellectual movement whose thinkers investigated sovereignty and *raison d'état*, themes that nineteenth-century Indologists recognized in Kautilya's writing.
