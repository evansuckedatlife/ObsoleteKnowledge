## summary

The **Portuguese Empire** was the first European maritime empire, built through a systematic program of naval exploration and trade monopoly that established Portuguese dominance over Atlantic and Indian Ocean routes from the 15th to 20th centuries. Pioneered by *Prince Henry the Navigator* and sustained by technological innovation, military conquest, and ruthless trade monopoly, Portugal became Europe's earliest colonial superpower despite its small population and limited resources. Its footprint extended from Brazil to India to Africa, and fragments of Portuguese colonial territories persisted into the late 20th century.

## you gotta know

- *Prince Henry the Navigator* (1394–1460) founded a school of navigation and sponsored systematic exploration of the African coast, establishing trading posts and forts to monopolize the gold and spice trade.
- Portuguese navigators developed the caravel, a ship design that combined Mediterranean and Atlantic sailing techniques, enabling long-distance ocean voyages and exploration of regions others considered inaccessible.
- *Vasco da Gama's* voyage around Africa to India (1497–1499) was the culmination of decades of exploration; it opened the Indian Ocean to Portuguese trade and made the Portuguese the dominant European power in Asian commerce.
- The Portuguese established a "factory system" of fortified trading posts rather than large territorial holdings, maximizing profit through monopoly control of specific commodities (spices, gold, slaves) at key port cities.
- Portuguese colonialism in Brazil (from 1500) created the largest and most profitable European colony; Brazil's sugar plantations driven by slave labor became central to Portuguese wealth.
- The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) with Spain divided the world into Portuguese and Spanish hemispheres of colonial ambition, giving Portugal rights to Brazil and Asian routes.
- The Portuguese Empire, though small in European territory, claimed territories across six continents at its height and maintained colonial holdings longer than other European powers (until 1974 in Africa).
