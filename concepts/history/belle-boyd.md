## summary

**Belle Boyd** (1844–1900) was a Confederate spy and intelligence operative during the American Civil War, known for using her charm and social access to extract military information from Union officers. Operating in the Shenandoah Valley of Virginia, she gathered intelligence for Confederate General *Thomas Jackson* ("Stonewall Jackson") and earned the nickname "La Belle Rebelle." Though her espionage activities were eventually discovered and she was imprisoned, her daring and fearlessness made her one of the most celebrated female intelligence agents of the Civil War era.

## you gotta know

- Born in Martinsburg, Virginia, in 1844; came of age in an aristocratic planter family with strong secessionist sympathies.
- Began her intelligence work early in the war by gathering information from Union soldiers quartered near her family's home and passing it to Confederate commanders.
- Used social events, flirtation, and her position as a trusted member of the community to gain access to Union officers' plans and movements without arousing suspicion.
- Provided crucial intelligence about Union troop strength and positions to Confederate forces, particularly in the Shenandoah Valley campaign of 1862.
- Was arrested by Union authorities in 1862 and imprisoned in Old Capitol Prison in Washington, D.C.; later released in a prisoner exchange.
- Continued her intelligence work after her release, eventually arrested again in 1864; spent the remainder of the war in exile or under detention.
- After the war, became a public figure, writing memoirs, performing on stage, and capitalizing on her notoriety; died in Wisconsin in 1900.
