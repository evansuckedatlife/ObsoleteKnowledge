## summary

The **Stonewall riots**, also remembered as the **Stonewall uprising** or **Stonewall rebellion**, were a series of spontaneous demonstrations by members of the LGBT community against a police raid at the *Stonewall Inn* in *Greenwich Village*, [[new-york-city]], beginning on June 28, 1969. Occurring amidst widespread state persecution across the [[united-states]], the confrontation marked a decisive break from gradualist accommodationism toward confrontational liberation and unapologetic visibility. By galvanizing grassroots resistance against institutionalized oppression, the uprising catalyzed modern queer activism and permanently altered 20th-century political and cultural history.

## you gotta know

- Began in the early morning hours of June 28, 1969, when the *New York City Police Department* raided the *Stonewall Inn*, an unlicensed gay bar operated by the Genovese crime family on *Christopher Street* in *Greenwich Village*, [[new-york-city]].
- Ignited into violent resistance after officers aggressively handled patrons during arrests, reaching a boiling point when a handcuffed butch lesbian, widely identified as *Stormé DeLarverie*, scuffled with police and urged onlookers to fight back.
- Escalated across several nights into clashes involving hundreds of protesters who threw coins, cobblestones, bottles, and trash at officers barricaded inside the tavern, eventually prompting the tactical deployment of the city's helmeted *Tactical Patrol Force*.
- Propelled to national prominence by marginalized community members on the front lines, most notably transgender women and drag queens of color such as *Marsha P. Johnson* and *Sylvia Rivera*, who later organized *Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries*.
- Spurred the immediate formation of militant activist organizations, including the *Gay Liberation Front* and the *Gay Activists Alliance*, which rejected the cautious respectability politics of the older *Mattachine Society*.
- Commemorated on June 28, 1970, by the inaugural *Christopher Street Liberation Day* march in [[new-york-city]], an event organized by activist *Craig Rodwell* that established the tradition of annual global Pride celebrations.
- Covered with sensationalized hostility by the local underground newspaper *The Village Voice* in reports by *Howard Smith* and *Lucian Truscott IV*, provoking furious pickets outside its offices and prompting beat generation icon *Allen Ginsberg* to visit the scene and praise the neighborhood's newfound fearlessness.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — supplied tactical frameworks, civil disobedience strategies, and coalitional rhetoric directly adapted by post-uprising gay liberationists.
- [[new-york-city]] — the metropolitan crucible whose dense enclave of *Greenwich Village* and exploitative mafia tavern system fostered working-class queer solidarity.
- [[hiv-aids-crisis]] — the catastrophic health emergency that rekindled Stonewall-era direct-action militancy through organizations like ACT UP and Queer Nation.
- [[queer-theory]] — the academic discipline that systematically interrogates heteronormativity and identity essentialism, rooted in liberationist post-Stonewall politics.
- [[homosexuality-in-literature]] — the creative tradition that transitioned from tragic coded subtext into militant openness and political witness within post-1969 [[american-literature]].
- [[modernism]] — the intellectual and artistic movement whose radical critique of Victorian moral codes helped lay the cultural groundwork for sexual liberation.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional guarantee of equal protection and substantive due process central to litigation dismantling discriminatory state statutes across the [[united-states]].
