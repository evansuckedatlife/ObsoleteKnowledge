## summary

**Mobutu Sese Seko** (1930–1997) was a Congolese military dictator who seized power in 1965 and ruled with authoritarian control and endemic corruption for 32 years. His regime, propped up by Cold War geopolitics, became synonymous with kleptocracy, extracting the nation's vast mineral wealth while its people descended into poverty and his personal fortune grew into billions.

## you gotta know

- Rose from military ranks to seize power in a coup in November 1965, during the chaos of the *Congo Crisis* and competing Cold War faction struggles.
- Renamed the country *Zaire* (1971–1997) and adopted the grandiose name "Sese Seko" (meaning "the all-powerful warrior"), embodying his megalomaniacal vision of supreme national authority.
- Accumulated a personal fortune estimated at $4–5 billion through wholesale plunder of state resources, mining revenues, and foreign aid while the national economy collapsed.
- Maintained authoritarian control through a secret police (DGED), suppression of opposition parties, and periodic purges of potential rivals within the military and government.
- Exploited Cold War rivalries by positioning himself as an anti-communist ally of the United States and Western powers, securing military aid and diplomatic recognition despite his dictatorial rule.
- Presided over the catastrophic decline of Congo's infrastructure, health, and education systems—despite enormous mineral wealth, the country became impoverished under his rule.
- Was forced to flee in 1997 as rebel forces under Laurent-Désiré Kabila advanced, ending his regime as neighboring countries and regional powers withdrew support.
- His regime became the textbook case of kleptocratic authoritarianism in post-colonial Africa, demonstrating how Cold War patronage could enable one man's systematic theft of national resources while destroying a nation's institutions.
