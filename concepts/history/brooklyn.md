## summary

**Brooklyn** is a borough of New York City and a major urban center in the United States, located on the western tip of Long Island. Historically, it served as the site of crucial Revolutionary War battles and later became an industrial and immigrant gateway, shaping American urban development and culture. Brooklyn's strategic location, demographic diversity, and cultural influence have made it central to American history, economics, and identity.

## you gotta know

- Originally settled by the Dutch in the 17th century as part of New Netherland; came under English control in 1664 and was renamed King's County.
- Became a battleground during the American Revolution; the Battle of Long Island (August 1776) was fought largely in present-day Brooklyn and was a significant early defeat for the Continental Army.
- Developed as an agricultural and commercial region through the 19th century, connected to Manhattan by the Brooklyn Bridge (completed 1883), which catalyzed urbanization.
- A major receiving point for European immigrants in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; its neighborhoods—from Brooklyn Heights to Williamsburg to Red Hook—each developed distinct ethnic and class identities.
- Became an industrial powerhouse in the 19th–20th centuries, with shipyards, sugar refineries, iron foundries, and textile production; the Brooklyn Navy Yard was a crucial military facility.
- Home to a vibrant working-class culture reflected in sports (the Brooklyn Dodgers), music, and literature; its decline in the late 20th century and recent gentrification have made it a symbol of urban change.
