## summary

**Medieval warfare** encompasses the military practices, operational strategies, and combat technologies prevalent across Europe and the Mediterranean basin from the collapse of the [[roman-empire]] to the dawn of the early modern period. Dominated initially by feudal obligations, fortified stone strongholds, and shock charges of heavily armoured *knights*, European military doctrine evolved through continuous siege operations and destructive logistical raiding campaigns. The era fundamentally reshaped late medieval society as disciplined infantry formations, formidable ranged weapons like the *longbow*, and early gunpowder artillery gradually eroded the military and social supremacy of the noble equestrian class, laying the groundwork for centralized standing armies in [[france]] and [[england]].

## you gotta know

- The *Infantry Revolution* of the late Middle Ages fundamentally undermined the tactical supremacy of mounted *knights*, demonstrating that disciplined masses of foot soldiers armed with pikes, spears, or longbows could withstand and destroy elite heavy cavalry charges.
- English tactical doctrine during the *Hundred Years' War* paired dismounted men-at-arms with massed longbowmen deployed behind sharpened wooden stakes, securing devastating victories against French chivalric charges at *Crécy*, *Poitiers*, and *Agincourt*.
- Pitched battles were remarkably infrequent compared to protracted siege warfare, where commanders relied on counterweight *trebuchets*, battering rams, and subterranean sapping to starve out or breach fortified stone castles guarding strategic territory.
- Armies routinely prioritized destructive economic raiding expeditions known as *chevauchées*, deliberately burning rural villages, spoiling crops, and terrorizing peasant populations to deplete an enemy lord's revenues while evading decisive engagements.
- Escalating administrative costs and the severe limitations of traditional forty-day feudal levies compelled monarchs to contract mercenary free companies, professional Italian *condottieri*, and indentured retinues funded by direct state taxation.
- Combatants observed a rigid social dichotomy under the chivalric code, routinely sparing and exchanging captured noble knights for lucrative financial ransoms while summarily executing or starving common infantry, archers, and civilian townsfolk.
- The mid-fifteenth-century development of mobile bronze siege cannon and standardized cast-iron cannonballs rapidly rendered high vertical stone curtain walls obsolete, famously enabling the [[ottoman-empire]] to breach the defenses of Constantinople in 1453.

## connections

- [[england]] — perfected the tactical integration of massed longbowmen and defensive field fortifications to defeat superior aristocratic cavalry forces.
- [[france]] — overcame decades of catastrophic defeats to establish Western Europe's first permanent, state-financed standing army with the *Compagnies d'ordonnance*.
- [[roman-empire]] — established the classical military manuals, castrametation doctrines, and centralized administrative networks that disintegrated into localized feudal defense systems.
- [[renaissance]] — catalyzed the transition into early modern warfare through Swiss pike columns, Italian bastioned trace fortifications, and combined-arms pike-and-shot formations.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — deployed regular standing forces including the elite *Janissary* infantry alongside cutting-edge gunpowder artillery to dismantle medieval fortifications across Eastern Europe.
- [[christianity]] — provided ideological sanctification for warfare through the Crusading movement and chivalric orders while attempting to curb private conflicts via the *Truce of God*.
- [[italy]] — functioned as the premier battleground for professional mercenary contracts, innovative city-state crossbow militias, and sophisticated military engineering treatises.
