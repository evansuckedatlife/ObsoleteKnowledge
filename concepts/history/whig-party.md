# Whig Party

## summary

The **Whig Party** was a major American political party that flourished from the 1830s to the 1850s, formed in opposition to *President Andrew Jackson's* executive power and Democratic policies. The Whigs advocated for a stronger *Congress*, economic nationalism, and internal improvements, and absorbed the earlier *Anti-Masonic Party*; the party ultimately fractured over slavery and was succeeded by the *Republican Party*.

## you gotta know

- The Whigs emerged from the *National Republican Party* and opposition to Jackson's *spoils system* and aggressive use of presidential power, particularly his withdrawal of federal deposits from the *Second Bank of the United States*.
- The party was named after British Whigs, reflecting its alignment with parliamentary supremacy and checks on executive authority—a direct contrast to what Whigs saw as Jacksonian tyranny.
- *Henry Clay* was the leading Whig intellectual and politician, championing the *American System* of protective tariffs, federal investment in infrastructure, and national banking.
- The Whigs absorbed the *Anti-Masonic Party* (a single-issue movement opposing Freemasonry), expanding their base and establishing themselves as a genuine alternative to Democrats.
- Whig presidents included *William Henry Harrison* (1841, died in office), *John Tyler* (though he alienated Whig allies after becoming president), and *Zachary Taylor* (1849–1850).
- The party's platform included opposition to Jacksonian *Democracy*, support for a national bank, internal improvements (*canals*, *railroads*), and a gradualist approach to slavery that became untenable as sectional tensions escalated.
- The party collapsed in the 1850s as slavery consumed American politics: Northern Whigs migrated to the new *Republican Party*, while Southern Whigs became *Democrats* or dwindled into irrelevance.
