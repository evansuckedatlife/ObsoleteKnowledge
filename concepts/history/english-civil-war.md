# English Civil War

## summary

The **English Civil War** (1642–1651) was a conflict between *Charles I's* royalist forces and parliamentary armies that resulted in the king's execution, the abolition of the monarchy, and the establishment of a military dictatorship under *Oliver Cromwell*. The war represented the ultimate confrontation between absolute monarchy and parliamentary power, fundamentally transforming England's political system and establishing principles of parliamentary sovereignty that shaped modern Britain.

## you gotta know

- Root causes lay in *Charles I's* religious policies (attempts to impose Anglican practices on Scotland provoked rebellion), his dissolution of Parliament (1629–40), and his financial desperation, which forced him to recall Parliament in 1640; conflicts between Crown and Commons escalated into warfare.
- The war split England: royalists (aristocrats, Anglicans, "cavaliers") fought for the Crown; parliamentarians ("roundheads", led by *Oliver Cromwell*) fought for parliamentary supremacy and (increasingly) religious reform and popular representation.
- Cromwell's *New Model Army* (1645 onward) was a professional, disciplined force organized on meritocratic lines (officers promoted by ability, not birth); its victories at *Naseby* (1645) and subsequent campaigns crushed royalist resistance.
- After military victory, political tensions erupted: Parliament was divided between moderates (who wanted to negotiate with Charles) and radicals (who wanted to restructure government); *Cromwell's* army purged Parliament (the *Pride's Purge*, 1648) and put *Charles I* on trial for treason.
- *Charles I* was executed (January 1649), a shocking act that horrified European monarchies and demonstrated that parliamentary and popular forces had overcome divine-right absolutism in England.
- The subsequent *Commonwealth and Protectorate* (1649–60) saw Cromwell's military dictatorship, religious reformation, and colonial expansion; after Cromwell's death, the monarchy was restored (1660) under *Charles II*, but Parliament's authority was permanently established.
