## summary

**Paul Revere** was an *American* silversmith, engraver, merchant, and patriot courier who became an enduring folk hero of the [[american-revolution]]. Based in colonial *Boston*, *Massachusetts*, he organized early colonial resistance through clandestine intelligence networks and anti-British propaganda, most notably his famous engraving of the 1770 *Boston Massacre*. He achieved lasting historical renown for his April 1775 midnight ride alerting rural militias to advancing regulars from the [[british-empire]] before the battles of *Lexington* and *Concord*. Following the war, he became a pioneering industrialist whose foundries and rolling mills helped establish domestic manufacturing in the early [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Undertook his famous midnight ride on April 18–19, 1775, to warn colonial minutemen that troops of the [[british-empire]] were marching toward *Lexington* and *Concord*, arranging the two-lantern signal at *Boston*'s *Old North Church* ("one if by land, two if by sea") with sexton *Robert Newman*.
- Immortalized as a solitary savior in *Henry Wadsworth Longfellow*'s celebrated 1860 poem "*Paul Revere's Ride*", which popularized his nocturnal alarm while obscuring fellow couriers *William Dawes* and *Samuel Prescott*, the only rider who actually completed the mission to *Concord*.
- Engraved the incendiary and widely circulated political print *The Bloody Massacre in King-Street*, depicting British regulars firing into an unarmed crowd during the 1770 *Boston Massacre*, a composition copied without credit from artist *Henry Pelham*.
- Agitated against imperial rule as an active leader of the *Sons of Liberty*, helping orchestrate the December 1773 *Boston Tea Party* in which colonists dumped British East India Company tea into *Boston Harbor*.
- Commanded militia artillery during the disastrous 1779 *Penobscot Expedition* in *Maine*, the worst American naval defeat prior to the twentieth century, resulting in severe losses and court-martial charges of cowardice, though he was fully exonerated in 1782.
- Pioneered domestic manufacturing during the early [[industrial-revolution]] by opening a foundry that cast church bells and establishing the *Revere Copper Company*, which produced sheet-copper cladding for naval vessels including the frigate *USS Constitution*.
- Provided what is recognized as the earliest forensic dentistry in the [[united-states]], identifying the exhumed, decomposed body of Major General *Joseph Warren* at *Bunker Hill* using an artificial ivory tooth he had wired into Warren's jaw.

## connections

- [[american-revolution]] — the war of independence in which he served as an intelligence rider, artisan propagandist, and militia artillery officer.
- [[george-washington]] — commander-in-chief whose continental army relied on colonial intelligence networks and munitions supply routes Revere helped organize.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power whose occupying garrisons and taxation acts provoked the patriotic resistance Revere championed in *Boston*.
- [[england]] — the mother country across the sea against whose advancing regulars Revere raised the country alarm on the eve of war.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the manufacturing transition reflected in his post-war commercial ventures, including iron foundries and sheet-copper rolling.
- [[united-states]] — the fledgling republic whose early naval frigates like *Old Ironsides* were armored with copper produced in Revere's mills.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — fellow revolutionary statesman whose political philosophy aligned with the grassroots republican resistance Revere mobilized in *New England*.
