## summary

The **Fourth Amendment** is an amendment to the United States Constitution ratified in 1791 as part of the Bill of Rights, guaranteeing the right of people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures. Originating largely from colonial fury over British general warrants and writs of assistance, it requires warrants to be supported by probable cause and particularly describe the place to be searched. It stands as the cornerstone of privacy law, criminal procedure, and civil liberties in the United States.

## you gotta know

- Drafted by *James Madison* alongside the original Bill of Rights in response to Anti-Federalist demands during constitutional ratification debates.
- Historically inspired by colonial resistance against the crown's use of "writs of assistance," denounced famously by *James Otis* in 1761 in *Boston*.
- Standard of protection transitioned from a strict property-trespass theory to a "reasonable expectation of privacy" test in *Katz v. United States* (1967), authored by Justice *Potter Stewart*.
- Requires that search warrants issue only upon probable cause, supported by oath or affirmation, and describing with particularity the place, persons, or items to be seized.
- Created the "stop-and-frisk" exception for reasonable suspicion under *Terry v. Ohio* (1968), permitting brief investigative detentions and pat-downs for weapons.
- Established the "plain view" doctrine, vehicle exception (*Carroll v. United States*), and modern rules governing digital surveillance and cell phone tracking (*Carpenter v. United States*).
