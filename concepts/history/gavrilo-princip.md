## summary

**Gavrilo Princip** was a Bosnian-Serb nationalist whose assassination of *Archduke Franz Ferdinand* on June 28, 1914, precipitated the outbreak of *World War I*. At just 19 years old, this member of the revolutionary group *Black Hand* fired the shots that shattered the fragile peace of Europe and triggered one of history's deadliest conflicts.

## you gotta know

- Nineteen-year-old member of the *Black Hand*, a Serbian nationalist organization dedicated to freeing Slavic territories from Austro-Hungarian rule.
- Suffered from tuberculosis and joined nationalist organizations partly to give his short life meaning and purpose before illness claimed him.
- Part of a coordinated plot: six Serbian and Bosnian conspirators positioned along Franz Ferdinand's motorcade route in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914.
- The conspiracy involved Serbian military intelligence officers (*Dragutin Dimitrijevic*) and the *Black Hand*, linking the assassination to state-level actors in Serbia.
- Shot *Archduke Franz Ferdinand* and his wife *Sophie* during their motorcade in *Sarajevo* in Bosnia after an earlier bomb attack failed to kill the Archduke.
- Franz Ferdinand's visit to Sarajevo was intended as a show of Austro-Hungarian authority in newly annexed Bosnia-Herzegovina, which infuriated Serbian nationalists.
- Originally equipped with a cyanide capsule intended for suicide after the assassination, consistent with *Black Hand* protocols for martyrdom.
- Fired at close range from the sidewalk; the assassination was not instantaneous, and both the Archduke and Sophie died shortly after being rushed to hospital.
- Caught immediately after the shooting and tortured by Austro-Hungarian police; Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia and its allies set off the domino of declarations of war.
- Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia for the conspiracy and issued a harsh ultimatum; Serbia's rejection triggered Austro-Hungarian mobilization and the chain of alliances leading to general war.
- His age, youth, and idealism made him a symbol of youthful nationalist fervor; he became a propaganda figure for both Serbian resistance and Austro-Hungarian militarism.
- The Austro-Hungarian and German press portrayed him as a Serbian puppet; the Serbian and later Yugoslav press portrayed him as a noble martyr for South Slavic liberation.
- Died in prison in 1918 from tuberculosis, becoming a martyr figure in Yugoslav nationalism; his grave later became a pilgrimage site.
- Historical debate remains over whether he acted as an autonomous idealist or as a direct agent of Serbian state policy and the *Black Hand* leadership.
- His act demonstrated how nationalist zealotry and great-power entanglements could ignite global catastrophe.
- The assassination is often cited as the spark that transformed a regional crisis into a continent-wide war that killed millions.
