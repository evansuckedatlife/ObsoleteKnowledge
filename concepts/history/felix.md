## summary

**Charles-François Félix**, often known simply as **Félix** or **Charles-François Félix de Tassy**, was the chief royal surgeon to King *Louis XIV* of [[france]]. He attained lasting renown in medical history for his high-stakes surgical cure of the Sun King's debilitating anal fistula in 1686 at the Palace of [[versailles]]. His groundbreaking success transformed the social standing of surgery from a despised mechanical trade practiced by barber-surgeons into an esteemed medical science.

## you gotta know

- Successfully operated on *Louis XIV* on November 18, 1686, curing a severe anal fistula that had incapacitated the monarch and confounded the royal physicians.
- Invented an innovative, curved surgical scalpel featuring a retractable silver guard, commonly designated the royal bistoury (*bistouri à la royale*), designed specifically to spare healthy surrounding tissue.
- Rehearsed the perilous operation for months on dozens of impoverished patients and prisoners in the hospitals of Paris, refining his technique before touching the king.
- Rewarded by a grateful monarch with a massive fortune of over three hundred thousand livres, extensive estates, and hereditary nobility, taking the aristocratic designation *seigneur de Saint-Baudile*.
- Catalyzed an extraordinary medical fad throughout the royal court, where sycophantic courtiers at [[versailles]] proudly sported bandages and pleaded for identical operations to mimic their sovereign.
- Elevated the institutional and intellectual status of French surgery, laying the groundwork for the establishment of the Royal Academy of Surgery and breaking the monopoly of scholastic physicians.
- Served in a court environment shaped by ministers like Jean-Baptiste [[colbert]] and spaces originally laid out under the king's father, [[louis-xiii]].

## connections

- [[france]] — the kingdom whose absolute monarch he served as premier royal surgeon.
- [[versailles]] — the royal palace where the secret operation on the Sun King was performed.
- [[colbert]] — the principal minister whose state-building projects modernized French scientific institutions.
- [[louis-xiii]] — father of *Louis XIV*, whose original hunting lodge was transformed into the courtly stage of [[versailles]].
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual era that built upon the empirical and anatomical advances pioneered by early modern surgeons.
- [[french-revolution]] — the later upheaval that dismantled the ancient guilds and fully integrated surgery into modern medical faculties.
- [[feudal-contract]] — the ancient legal tradition of seigneurial privilege that royal grants of nobility and land gradually superseded.
