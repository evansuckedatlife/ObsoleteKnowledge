## summary

The **Capetian Dynasty** was a medieval European royal family that ruled France from 987 until 1328, and Spain (through cadet branches) for centuries afterward. Beginning with *Hugh Capet*, who claimed the French throne in 987, the Capetians gradually consolidated power, expanded their royal domain, and established the feudal hierarchy of medieval France. Through military conquest, strategic marriage alliances, and administrative reforms, successive Capetian kings transformed France from a feudal collection of semi-independent fiefdoms into a centralized kingdom, laying the foundation for the modern French state.

## you gotta know

- Founded when Hugh Capet was crowned King of France in 987, though his authority initially extended only over his personal domains (the Île-de-France) around Paris.
- Early Capetian kings were weak relative to many of the great feudal lords; the dynasty's survival depended on careful management and strategic alliances rather than immediate military superiority.
- *Louis VI* ("the Fat") and *Louis VII* began expanding royal power by asserting claims over vassals and managing conflicts with the powerful *House of Anjou*.
- *Philip II Augustus* (reigned 1180–1223) dramatically expanded Capetian power, defeating King *John of England* and seizing Normandy and other English-held lands in France; he nearly tripled the royal domain.
- *Louis IX* (later canonized as Saint Louis; reigned 1226–1270) consolidated royal authority through law and piety; he established royal courts as the highest legal authority and earned a reputation for justice and Christian virtue.
- *Philip IV the Fair* (reigned 1285–1314) transformed the monarchy into a centralized bureaucratic state, eliminating rival powers and establishing the Estates-General; he also clashed with the Papacy over papal authority.
- The dynasty ultimately produced the most powerful medieval French kings and shaped European feudal politics, though the direct line ended in 1328, leading to the succession crisis that sparked the Hundred Years' War.
