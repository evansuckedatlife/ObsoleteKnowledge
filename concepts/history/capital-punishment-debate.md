## summary

The **Capital Punishment Debate** encompasses the philosophical, legal, and moral contest over the authority of the sovereign state to execute convicted individuals. Originating in humanitarian critiques of judicial torture and corporal excess during the *Enlightenment*, modern discourse centers heavily on human rights charters, racial disparities, and constitutional limits on state power. In the legal history of the *United States*, this ongoing confrontation has produced pivotal jurisprudence interpreting fundamental guarantees against arbitrary, cruel, and unusual punishment.

## you gotta know

- *Cesare Beccaria* inaugurated the modern secular abolitionist movement with his landmark 1764 treatise *On Crimes and Punishments*, arguing that state execution violates the social contract and fails to deter crime.
- In *Furman v. Georgia* (1972), the Supreme Court of the *United States* struck down all existing death penalty statutes, holding that unguided jury discretion produced arbitrary and racially biased outcomes akin to being struck by lightning.
- Capital sentencing resumed nationally following *Gregg v. Georgia* (1976), which approved bifurcated trials separating the guilt phase from penalty deliberations governed by statutory aggravating and mitigating factors.
- Categorical exemptions emerged in the early twenty-first century, beginning with *Atkins v. Virginia* (2002), which held that executing persons with intellectual disabilities violates the Eighth Amendment.
- In the landmark decision *Roper v. Simmons* (2005), the high court barred the execution of juvenile offenders under age eighteen, citing evolving standards of decency and comparative international legal norms.
- The scope of the penalty for non-homicide offenses was restricted in *Coker v. Georgia* (1977) and reaffirmed in *Kennedy v. Louisiana* (2008), barring capital sentences for crimes against individuals that do not result in death.
- Procedural debates have increasingly focused on the mechanics of execution, with challenges over barbiturate shortages and botched lethal injections examined in decisions such as *Baze v. Rees* and *Glossip v. Gross*.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual movement whose utilitarian and social contract philosophies generated the earliest institutional critiques of execution.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — governing charter containing the Eighth Amendment prohibition against cruel and unusual punishments.
- [[14th-amendment]] — constitutional amendment incorporating federal protections against state-level due process and equal protection violations.
- [[united-states]] — democratic nation whose retention of the penalty distinguishes its domestic criminal code from most Western allies.
- [[french-revolution]] — historical upheaval where radical egalitarianism introduced the guillotine before generating intense legal pushback.
- [[england]] — common law jurisdiction that dismantled the harsh statutory framework known historically as the *Bloody Code*.
