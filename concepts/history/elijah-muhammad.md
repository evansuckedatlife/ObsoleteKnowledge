## summary

**Elijah Muhammad** (1897–1975), born **Elijah Robert Poole**, was an American religious leader who headed the *Nation of Islam* from 1934 until his death. Migrating from rural Georgia to Detroit during the Great Migration, he became the chosen successor of the movement's mysterious founder, *Wallace Fard Muhammad*. Muhammad built the organization into a prominent institution of Black nationalism, advocating complete racial separation, personal discipline, and economic self-sufficiency. Mentoring figures such as *Malcolm X* and *Muhammad Ali*, he offered a radical counterweight to mainstream integrationist goals during the *Civil Rights Movement*.

## you gotta know

- Directed the *Nation of Islam* for over four decades from Temple No. 2 in Chicago, transforming a local sect into an influential, multi-million-dollar national movement.
- Proclaimed founder *Wallace Fard Muhammad* to have been *Allah* incarnate and identified himself as the "Messenger of Allah" tasked with waking the Black nation.
- Mentored *Malcolm X*, who served as the movement's most famous spokesman until their high-profile theological and personal rift culminated in Malcolm's departure in 1964.
- Recruited and spiritually mentored world heavyweight boxing champion *Cassius Clay*, bestowing upon him his Islamic name, *Muhammad Ali*.
- Preached the doctrine of Black economic self-reliance, establishing an extensive network of Black-owned bakeries, grocery stores, real estate holdings, and thousands of acres of farmland.
- Founded the widely read newspaper *Muhammad Speaks*, which built a weekly circulation in the hundreds of thousands and financed organizational activities.
- Imprisoned for sedition and draft evasion during *World War II*, having urged his followers to refuse military service for a segregated nation.
- Fiercely opposed the integrationist philosophy of leaders like *Martin Luther King Jr.*, arguing that African Americans should build separate social, religious, and economic institutions.
