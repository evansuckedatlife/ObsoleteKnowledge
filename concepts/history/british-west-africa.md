## summary

**British West Africa** was the collective administrative grouping of colonies, protectorates, and trust territories governed by the *United Kingdom* in West Africa from the early nineteenth century until mid-twentieth-century decolonization. Encompassing the distinct territories of *The Gambia*, *Sierra Leone*, the *Gold Coast* (now Ghana), and *Nigeria*, it constituted a major theatre of British mercantile expansion, resource extraction, and administrative experiments such as indirect rule. Understanding this imperial grouping is essential for analyzing the dismantling of indigenous African polities like the *Asante Empire*, the dynamics of colonial exploitation, and the emergence of modern Pan-African nationalism.

## you gotta know

- Administered four separate geographical enclaves: *The Gambia*, *Sierra Leone*, the *Gold Coast*, and *Nigeria*, which were separated from one another by French and German colonial territories rather than forming a single contiguous landmass.
- Governed primarily through *Frederick Lugard*'s system of indirect rule, pioneered in the northern emirates of *Nigeria*, which co-opted traditional rulers and customary legal systems to maintain British control with minimal imperial personnel.
- Fought multiple military campaigns against the *Asante Empire* in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, culminating in the annexation of the Ashanti territory into the Gold Coast after the 1900 War of the Golden Stool led by Queen Mother *Yaa Asantewaa*.
- Maintained unified regional administrative and economic bodies, including the West African Currency Board (which issued the British West African pound), the West African Court of Appeal, and the Royal West African Frontier Force (*RWAFF*).
- Headquartered initially in *Freetown*, *Sierra Leone*, whose population included liberated captives from intercepted slave ships, known as the *Krio*, who became early civil servants, educators, and missionaries across the coast.
- Established higher education centers such as *Fourah Bay College* in Freetown and *Achimota College* in the Gold Coast, which trained an educated African elite that produced early anti-colonial theorists, legal scholars, and civil servants.
- Dissolved following *World War II* as individual territories agitated for sovereignty, beginning with the independence of the Gold Coast as Ghana under *Kwame Nkrumah* in 1957, followed by *Nigeria* in 1960, *Sierra Leone* in 1961, and *The Gambia* in 1965.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — the imperial global network that established and administered these West African colonial territories.
- [[colonialism]] — the overarching political and economic doctrine that justified the conquest and resource extraction across the continent.
- [[asante-empire]] — the powerful Akan confederacy repeatedly invaded and ultimately annexed by British forces in the Gold Coast.
- [[kwame-nkrumah]] — the pioneering nationalist who led the Gold Coast to independence, breaking the framework of British rule in the region.
- [[pan-africanism]] — the intellectual and political movement championed by West African intellectuals seeking liberation from colonial rule.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global war whose economic demands and conscription of West African troops accelerated postwar demands for independence.
- [[england]] — the imperial metropole whose colonial office directed executive authority and appointed governors across the four territories.
