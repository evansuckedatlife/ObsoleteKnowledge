## summary

The **Egyptian campaign**, formally designated as the **French campaign in Egypt and Syria** or the **Expédition d'Égypte**, was an overseas military and scientific expedition from 1798 to 1801 commanded by General *[[napoleon-bonaparte]]*. Undertaken during the *[[french-revolution|French Revolutionary Wars]]*, the enterprise aimed to disrupt British trade routes to India, establish a French colonial foothold in the *[[middle-east]]*, and introduce European scientific inquiry to North Africa. Although Napoleon won celebrated tactical victories on land, the annihilation of the French fleet by the British Royal Navy at the *Battle of the Nile* ultimately doomed the invasion to military capitulation while laying the foundation for modern Egyptology.

## you gotta know

- Captured the strategic Mediterranean island fortress of Malta from the *Knights Hospitaller* in June 1798 before making landfall near Alexandria to initiate the invasion of Ottoman Egypt.
- Won the decisive *Battle of the Pyramids* (Battle of Embabeh) on July 21, 1798, where French infantry squares shattered the elite Mamluk cavalry led by *Murad Bey*, prompting Napoleon's famous rallying cry invoking forty centuries of history gazing down from the monuments.
- Suffered a catastrophic strategic defeat at the *Battle of the Nile* (Abukir Bay) in August 1798, when British Rear-Admiral *[[nelson-horatio|Horatio Nelson]]* attacked the anchored French battle line from both sides, destroying thirteen warships and blowing up the flagship *L'Orient*.
- Accompanied by the *Commission des Sciences et des Arts d'Égypte*, a contingent of over 160 civilian mathematicians, naturalists, and cartographers who established the *Institut d'Égypte* in Cairo to map the country's geography, natural history, and antiquities.
- Discovered the *Rosetta Stone* in July 1799 during fortifications near Rashid, an ancient granodiorite stele inscribed in hieroglyphic, demotic, and Ancient Greek that later enabled *Jean-François Champollion* to decipher ancient Egyptian scripts.
- Expanded into the Levant during the 1799 Syrian expedition, capturing Jaffa but suffering a decisive check at the *Siege of Acre*, where Ottoman governor *Cezzar Pasha* and British naval commodore *Sidney Smith* successfully repulsed repeated French assaults.
- Concluded with Napoleon secretly abandoning his plague-stricken army in August 1799 to sail back to *[[france]]*, where he leveraged his domestic popularity to orchestrate the *Coup of 18 Brumaire* and seize power as First Consul.
- Ended in complete military capitulation under General *Jacques-François Menou* in 1801, though the scholars' extensive field research produced the monumental twenty-volume encyclopedic work *Description de l'Égypte*.

## connections

- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the ambitious young French general whose overall strategy, tactical commands, and political ascent hinged upon the expedition.
- [[nelson-horatio]] — the British naval commander who trapped the French army in North Africa by destroying its fleet at the Battle of the Nile.
- [[french-revolution]] — the political and military upheaval that empowered the French Directory to commission and finance the overseas expedition.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial rival whose lucrative commercial links to British India the French sought to sever by controlling the Isthmus of Suez.
- [[middle-east]] — the strategic crossroads of empires where the expedition introduced modern European imperial rivalry and military technology.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the sovereign imperial authority over Egypt that formed alliances with Britain and Russia to repel French occupation.
- [[egyptian-islamic-jihad]] — the modern militant movement whose anti-Western rhetoric frequently invoked the legacy of European military incursions into Egypt that began with Napoleon.
