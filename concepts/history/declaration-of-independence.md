## summary

The **Declaration of Independence**, adopted by the *Continental Congress* on July 4, 1776, announced the thirteen colonies' separation from Britain and justified that rupture on philosophical grounds. Drafted primarily by *Thomas Jefferson*, it enshrined revolutionary ideals of natural rights, popular sovereignty, and the consent of the governed. The document became the philosophical foundation of American political identity.

## you gotta know

- Approved July 4, 1776, in Philadelphia, after months of debate over the proper timing and language of independence.
- Written principally by *Jefferson*, with edits by *John Adams*, *Benjamin Franklin*, and others in the Continental Congress.
- Opens with the famous assertion that "all men are created equal" and possess inalienable rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.
- Grounds political legitimacy in a social contract: governments derive power from the consent of the governed, not divine right or conquest.
- Enumerates specific grievances against King *George III*—taxation without representation, dissolution of assemblies, quartering of troops—to justify rebellion.
- Uses *Enlightenment* reasoning to frame colonization and self-governance as rational principles, not merely colonial interest.
- Asserts the right of a people to alter or abolish government when it becomes destructive of their rights, a radical claim in 1776.
- Jefferson's original draft condemned the slave trade, but Congress excised this passage to appease southern and northern commercial interests.
- The Declaration was signed by 56 delegates, many of whom faced severe consequences—property confiscation, capture, even death—for their commitment to independence.
- Became a touchstone for subsequent democratic movements worldwide; oppressed peoples have invoked its language of natural rights to challenge imperial and autocratic rule.
