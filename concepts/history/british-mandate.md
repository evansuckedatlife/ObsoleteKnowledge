## summary

The **British Mandate**, officially designated as the **Mandate for Palestine**, was a geopolitical administrative entity entrusted to the United Kingdom by the League of Nations following the partition of the [[ottoman-empire]] after [[world-war-i]]. Spanning from 1920 until its chaotic termination in May 1948, the territory comprised the lands west of the Jordan River, having originally included the Emirate of Transjordan to the east. Governed under contradictory wartime pledges that promised self-determination to Arab populations while endorsing a Jewish national home, the mandate fostered escalating sectarian violence and laid the territorial foundation for the enduring modern [[arab-israeli-conflict]].

## you gotta know

- Established formally in the aftermath of the 1920 *San Remo Conference* and confirmed by the *League of Nations* Council in 1922, legitimizing British control over former Ottoman lands.
- Incorporated verbatim the text of the 1917 *Balfour Declaration*, in which British Foreign Secretary *Arthur Balfour* endorsed the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people.
- Contradicted secret wartime diplomatic understandings, specifically the British promises of independent Arab sovereignty in the *McMahon-Hussein Correspondence* and the imperial division outlined in the *Sykes-Picot Agreement*.
- Governed administratively through a Crown-appointed *High Commissioner for Palestine*, with prominent early leadership under diplomat *Herbert Samuel*, who established headquarters in Jerusalem.
- Separated in 1921 to sever the land east of the Jordan River into the distinct autonomous *Emirate of Transjordan* under Hashemite ruler *Abdullah I*.
- Confronted sustained communal and anti-colonial resistance during the *1929 Palestine riots* over holy site access, followed by the widespread, armed *1936–1939 Arab revolt* that paralyzed colonial infrastructure.
- Attempted to curb immigration and appease Arab unrest via the controversial *White Paper of 1939*, which strictly capped Jewish refugees on the eve of the Holocaust and alienated the Zionist movement.
- Ended abruptly on May 14, 1948, following Britain's referral of the crisis to the United Nations, precipitating the declaration of the State of Israel and the outbreak of the *1948 Arab-Israeli War*.
