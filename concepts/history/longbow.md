## summary

The **longbow** was a medieval weapon—a tall, simple bow made of yew wood, standing nearly as tall as a man and capable of firing arrows with devastating force and speed. Developed and perfected by Welsh and English archers, the longbow proved devastatingly effective against armored knights during the *Hundred Years' War*, particularly at *Crécy* (1346) and *Agincourt* (1415). The weapon's effectiveness in massed formation demonstrated that disciplined infantry could defeat mounted cavalry, fundamentally transforming medieval warfare and the balance of power between mounted nobility and common soldiers.

## you gotta know

- Required years of training and conditioning to master; English law mandated practice for all able-bodied men to maintain a trained force of archers.
- Could fire arrows at a rate of six to ten per minute when used in massed formations, creating devastating volleys before close combat even began.
- Arrows could penetrate armor and pierce knights from 200 yards away, destroying the medieval myth of cavalry invincibility.
- At the Battle of *Crécy* (1346), English and Welsh archers decimated French cavalry charges, helping establish English dominance in the early *Hundred Years' War*.
- At *Agincourt* (1415), *Henry V*'s archers again proved decisive, killing thousands of French knights and infantry in a mud-soaked valley where their advantage was maximized.
- The longbow eventually became obsolete as firearms and pike-and-shot formations emerged in the 16th century, but its tactical innovations influenced military thinking for centuries.
