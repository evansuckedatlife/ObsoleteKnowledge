## summary

**Apartheid** was South Africa's system of institutionalized racial segregation and discrimination, enforced by law from 1948 to the early 1990s, that systematized white minority rule over the Black majority and other non-white populations. The *National Party* government enshrined segregation into every aspect of life—housing, education, employment, and public facilities—while denying basic political rights to non-white citizens. The system became one of the twentieth century's most notorious human rights atrocities, ultimately overthrown through internal resistance led by *Nelson Mandela* and the *African National Congress*, combined with international sanctions and Cold War political shifts.

## you gotta know

- The term *apartheid* (Afrikaans for "separateness") was adopted officially by South Africa's National Party government in 1948, though racial segregation predated formal apartheid.
- Laws enforced rigid racial classification—white, Coloured, Indian, and Black—determining where people could live, what jobs they could hold, and what education they could access.
- The *Bantustan* system forcibly relocated millions of Black South Africans to artificial "homelands," denying them citizenship in white-controlled South Africa despite living and working there.
- *Nelson Mandela*, leader of the *African National Congress*, was imprisoned for 27 years (1962–1989) on charges of sabotage and treason, becoming a global symbol of anti-apartheid resistance.
- International isolation through economic sanctions, cultural boycotts, and UN resolutions steadily delegitimized the regime through the 1970s and 1980s.
- Apartheid ended formally with the 1994 democratic elections that installed *Mandela* as president; South Africa transitioned to majority rule and adopted a new constitution enshrining equality.
