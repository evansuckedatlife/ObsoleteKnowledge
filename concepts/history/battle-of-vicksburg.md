## summary

The **Battle of Vicksburg** (May–July 1863) was a pivotal *American Civil War* siege and battle in which Union forces under *Ulysses S. Grant* besieged and captured the Confederate stronghold of Vicksburg, Mississippi. The victory gave the North control of the *Mississippi River*, split the *Confederacy* in two, and proved Grant's military genius, marking a decisive turning point toward Union victory.

## you gotta know

- Fought May 18 – July 4, 1863; climaxed on the Fourth of July with Confederate surrender, yielding psychological victory on American independence day.
- Part of *Grant's* larger Vicksburg Campaign (Dec 1862–July 1863); involved feints, river operations coordinated with the U.S. Navy, and forced marches through Mississippi's difficult terrain.
- The siege lasted 47 days of relentless bombardment; Union forces surrounded the city from all sides while Confederates faced starvation, disease, and dwindling ammunition supplies—some soldiers ate mules and dogs.
- Combined victory: the *Battle of Gettysburg* (Pennsylvania, July 1–3, 1863) and Vicksburg (Mississippi, July 4) both concluded in the same week—a strategic turning point indicating the North's military ascendancy.
- Strategic significance: Vicksburg was the last Confederate stronghold on the Mississippi; its capture gave the Union complete control of the entire *Mississippi River*, effectively splitting Texas, Arkansas, and Louisiana from the Eastern Confederacy.
- Logistical innovation: *Grant's* campaign pioneered "living off the land"—moving without supply lines, foraging from the countryside to maintain mobility and speed, a tactic foreshadowing Sherman's later *March to the Sea*.
- Military reputation: Established *Ulysses S. Grant* as the North's most strategically capable field general; Lincoln recognized his talent and promoted him to Commanding General of all Union armies (1864).
- Casualties and prisoners: Approximately 10,000 Confederate prisoners of war captured; Union casualties ~10,000, Confederate ~9,000+ killed, wounded, or captured; Confederate relief attempts by General *Joe Johnston* failed.
- Symbol of Confederate decline: Vicksburg's fall demoralized the South and demonstrated Union capacity for coordinated strategy; combined with Gettysburg, it marked the beginning of the Confederacy's military decline.
