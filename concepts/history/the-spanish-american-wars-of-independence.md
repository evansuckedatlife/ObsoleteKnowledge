## summary

The **Spanish American Wars of Independence** (1808–1833) were the series of military campaigns and revolutions through which Spanish American colonies liberated themselves from European dominion and established independent republics. Sparked by Napoleon's invasion of Spain and the power vacuum it created, visionary military commanders like *Simón Bolívar* and *José de San Martín* led armies across continents, uniting diverse populations against Spanish colonial rule. By the 1830s, nearly all of Spanish America had broken free, reshaping the hemispheric political landscape and establishing the nation-states of modern Latin America.

## you gotta know

- The wars began after Napoleon's 1808 invasion of Spain destabilized Spanish authority and emboldened colonists yearning for independence.
- *Simón Bolívar* led revolutions across northern South America, liberating Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, and Bolivia, which was named in his honor.
- *José de San Martín* commanded the Southern Cone independence, liberating Argentina and Chile, then crossing the Andes to strike at Spanish Peru.
- *Bernardo O'Higgins*, son of an Irish-Chilean father, emerged as a key allied commander and first Chief Executive of independent Chile.
- The wars were ideologically inspired by the *American Revolution* and Enlightenment republicanism, though they faced geographic, economic, and social barriers the north had not.
- Spain initially resisted fiercely, deploying experienced troops to crush rebellions, but lacked the resources to reconquer so vast a territory simultaneously.
- By 1830, Spain retained only Cuba and Puerto Rico in the Americas—a stunning reversal from its prior three centuries of hemispheric dominance.

## connections

- [[spain]] — the colonial metropole whose authority the wars overthrew across the Americas.
- [[simon-bolivar]] — the revolutionary general who liberated much of northern South America.
- [[jose-de-san-martin]] — the military strategist who led southern South American independence and crossed the Andes to strike at Spain's heart.
- [[bernardo-ohiggins]] — the Chilean independence leader who allied with San Martín and became Chile's founding executive.
- [[latin-american-independence]] — the broader regional movement these wars instantiated across the hemisphere.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — whose invasion of Spain created the crisis that emboldened colonial independence movements.
- [[united-states]] — the prior American republic whose independence and example inspired Spanish American revolutionaries.
