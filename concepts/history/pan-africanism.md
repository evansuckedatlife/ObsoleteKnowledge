## summary

**Pan-Africanism** is a worldwide political and cultural movement dedicated to consolidating solidarity among all indigenous peoples and diasporas of African descent. Originating in the late nineteenth century among intellectuals resisting transatlantic racism and European [[colonialism]], the movement evolved throughout the twentieth century into an urgent programme for continental liberation, economic self-determination, and political unification. It reached its political zenith during the decolonization era following [[world-war-ii]], transforming from an exiled intellectual protest into the governing state ideology that guided the founding of sovereign African nations and durable continental alliances.

## you gotta know

- Championed by *Kwame Nkrumah*, who famously declared at Ghanaian independence that national freedom was meaningless unless linked to the total liberation of the continent, later inspiring the creation of the *Organization of African Unity* in 1963.
- Early diaspora leadership was shaped by Caribbean and American intellectuals, most prominently *W. E. B. Du Bois*, often celebrated as the father of modern Pan-Africanism, and *Marcus Garvey*, whose Universal Negro Improvement Association championed mass economic independence and the 'Back to Africa' movement.
- The watershed 1945 *Fifth Pan-African Congress* held in Manchester, [[england]], marked a decisive ideological shift from polite reformist petitions by diaspora elites to militant anti-colonial demands led by future African heads of state including *Jomo Kenyatta* and *Kwame Nkrumah*.
- Promoted across East Africa by *Julius Nyerere*, who synthesized continental unity with his socialist philosophy of *Ujamaa* and advocated for regional federations as essential, pragmatic stepping stones toward total African integration.
- Adopted distinct cultural expressions such as the *Négritude* literary and philosophical movement founded by *Léopold Sédar Senghor* and *Aimé Césaire* in [[france]], celebrating Black aesthetics and African cultural heritage as resistance to colonial assimilation.
- Deeply influenced and interacted with the American [[civil-rights-movement]] and the [[harlem-renaissance]], creating a vibrant ideological bridge between domestic anti-segregation campaigns in the [[united-states]] and liberation wars against European empires.
- Sparked the first formal international gathering at the 1900 *First Pan-African Conference* in London, organized by Trinidadian barrister *Henry Sylvester Williams* to protest racial disenfranchisement, colonial atrocities, and imperial land theft across the globe.

## connections

- [[kwame-nkrumah]] — Ghanaian independence leader who placed continental unity at the core of foreign policy and drove the establishment of the OAU.
- [[julius-nyerere]] — Tanzanian president who advanced Pan-African solidarity alongside his vision of agrarian socialist self-reliance.
- [[colonialism]] — the oppressive system of European imperial dominion whose eradication was the central objective of twentieth-century Pan-Africanists.
- [[ghana]] — the first sub-Saharan nation to achieve independence from colonial rule, becoming the operational headquarters of African liberation.
- [[british-empire]] — the global imperial power whose territories across the continent served as principal staging grounds for nationalist resistance.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — domestic American struggle that drew mutual tactical inspiration and philosophical solidarity from African decolonization.
- [[harlem-renaissance]] — artistic and literary awakening that forged transatlantic Black consciousness and celebrated shared African heritage.
- [[cold-war]] — international geopolitical rivalry that pressurized post-independence Pan-African alliances into non-aligned diplomatic strategies.
