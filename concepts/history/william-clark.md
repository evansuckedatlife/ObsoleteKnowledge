## summary

**William Clark** was an American explorer, soldier, and territorial governor best known for co-leading the *Corps of Discovery* expedition alongside *Meriwether Lewis* from 1804 to 1806. Commissioned by President *Thomas Jefferson* following the *Louisiana Purchase*, Clark served as the expedition's principal cartographer, river pilot, and practical field commander across the newly acquired western territories to the Pacific Ocean. His remarkably accurate navigational charts, extensive geographical surveys, and diplomatic negotiations with dozens of Native American tribes established United States claims to the Pacific Northwest and opened the trans-Mississippi frontier to future exploration and settlement.

## you gotta know

- Co-commanded the famous *Corps of Discovery* with *Meriwether Lewis* from *St. Louis* to the mouth of the Columbia River and back between 1804 and 1806, exercising shared leadership and mutual authority despite technically holding a lower commission as a second lieutenant of artillery.
- Served as the expedition's master cartographer, meticulously charting thousands of miles of the Missouri and Columbia River systems, mountain passes across the Continental Divide, and coastal terrain with astonishing spatial precision that guided decades of subsequent American expansion.
- Formed an enduring personal and working bond with the Lemhi Shoshone interpreter *Sacagawea* and her husband *Toussaint Charbonneau*, nicknaming their infant son *Jean Baptiste Charbonneau* "Pompey" and later assuming legal guardianship to finance the boy's classical education in *St. Louis*.
- Inscribed his signature and the date of July 25, 1806, into a massive sandstone promontory overlooking the Yellowstone River in modern-day Montana, naming the geological formation *Pompey's Pillar*, which survives today as the sole remaining physical mark of the expedition on the landscape.
- Kept voluminous daily journals noted for their idiosyncratic phonetic spelling, recording crucial astronomical readings, river depths, meteorological data, and first-hand scientific descriptions of numerous previously uncatalogued flora and fauna, including the grizzly bear and prairie dog.
- Brought his enslaved personal manservant, *York*, on the transcontinental journey, who proved vital as a scout, hunter, and diplomat among Plains tribes, and was permitted to cast an equal vote regarding the placement of *Fort Clatsop*, yet was denied manumission by Clark for many years after returning.
- Governed the Missouri Territory and served for two decades as federal Superintendent of Indian Affairs, presiding over major councils like the 1825 Treaty of *Prairie du Chien* and administering the forced relocation of eastern tribes to lands west of the Mississippi River.

## connections

- [[meriwether-lewis]] — his close personal friend, military comrade, and fellow captain of the *Corps of Discovery* with whom he shared undivided field command.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — the third president of the United States who conceived, funded, and instructed the transcontinental scientific expedition across North America.
- [[sacagawea]] — the Shoshone interpreter and guide who assisted the expedition across the northern plains and whose infant child Clark later mentored.
- [[louisiana-purchase]] — the vast western territory acquired from Napoleonic France that Clark was charged with exploring, surveying, and mapping.
- [[united-states]] — the expanding nation whose commercial interests and geopolitical claims to the Pacific Northwest were advanced by Clark's geographic work.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the worldwide historical epoch of maritime and continental discovery that provided the scientific and strategic blueprint for the expedition.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader ideological and imperial movement that drove American westward territorial displacement of Indigenous nations through treaties Clark administered.
