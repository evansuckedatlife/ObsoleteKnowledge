## summary

The **Battle of the Little Bighorn** (June 1876) was a devastating defeat inflicted on the United States 7th Cavalry regiment by combined Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho warriors led by *Sitting Bull* and *Crazy Horse*. The battle represented the Indian tribes' last major collective victory against American expansion, yet it also provoked a crushing federal response that shattered Native American resistance and led to the final conquest and reservation confinement of the Great Plains tribes. The battle became known as "Custer's Last Stand" after the death of Lieutenant Colonel *George Armstrong Custer*.

## you gotta know

- Occurred on June 25–26, 1876, in the Little Bighorn River valley in Montana, during the Great Sioux Wars.
- *Sitting Bull*, a Lakota holy man and strategist, organized a massive encampment of thousands of warriors from multiple tribes resisting forced reservation relocation.
- *George Armstrong Custer*, pursuing glory and Native American scalps, attacked without waiting for reinforcements or respecting Native American strength.
- Custer's regiment was outnumbered and overwhelmed; all 268 soldiers and scouts under his direct command were killed, including Custer himself.
- The shocking defeat galvanized American public opinion and military response; the federal government launched overwhelming campaigns that within months forced surviving tribes onto reservations.
- The battle became a symbol of Native American resistance and a turning point in the Indian Wars, after which tribal autonomy effectively ended on the Great Plains.
