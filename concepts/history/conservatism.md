## summary

**Conservatism** is a political ideology emphasizing tradition, gradual reform, social stability, and skepticism toward radical change. Emerging as a coherent reaction to the *French Revolution* and its promise of wholesale transformation through reason, conservatism argues that existing institutions embody accumulated wisdom, that rapid change breeds chaos and violence, and that liberty is best preserved through constitutional order rather than revolutionary rupture. Conservative thought shaped nineteenth- and twentieth-century politics, from the *Congress of Vienna*'s restoration of monarchy to contemporary arguments for limited government and cultural preservation.

## you gotta know

- Conservatism crystallized as a distinct ideology in the late eighteenth century, a reaction against Enlightenment rationalism and revolutionary change; thinkers like *Edmund Burke* argued that society is organic, not machinery to be rebuilt, and that gradual reform respects accumulated custom.
- The *Congress of Vienna* (1815) embodied conservative statecraft: *Metternich*, the Austrian diplomat, orchestrated the restoration of monarchy across Europe and established the *Concert of Europe* to suppress revolutionary movements and maintain the balance of power.
- Conservative thought distinguished between legitimate authority rooted in history and tradition versus revolutionary authority claimed by reason alone; conservatives defended monarchy, nobility, and the established church as natural and necessary hierarchies, not tyrannies to be overthrown.
- Burke's *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790) became the foundational conservative text: he argued the French Revolution's rationalist attempt to remake society from scratch would destroy irreplaceable social bonds and unleash terror—a prophecy that history seemed to confirm.
- Nineteenth-century conservatism adapted to industrialization and democratic pressure: British conservatism, more flexible than continental variants, accepted gradual expansion of voting rights and trade liberalization while resisting revolutionary socialism.
- Contemporary conservatism remains divided: some emphasize tradition and cultural continuity (social conservatism), others prioritize market economics and limited government (classical liberalism/libertarianism), yet others stress nationalist sovereignty and national character.
