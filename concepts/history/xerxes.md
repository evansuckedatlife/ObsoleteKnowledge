# Xerxes

## summary

**Xerxes I** (c. 519–465 BCE) was the Great King of the Achaemenid Persian Empire and the ruler who launched the massive invasion of Greece (480–479 BCE) to conquer the independent Greek city-states. Though his invasion was ultimately repelled at Marathon, Salamis, and Plataea, Xerxes personified Persian imperial ambition and the cultural confrontation between the autocratic Persian Empire and democratic Greek civilization that shaped Classical history.

## you gotta know

- Xerxes inherited the Persian throne from his father *Darius I*, who had already invaded Greece unsuccessfully at *Marathon* (490 BCE); Xerxes spent years preparing a vastly larger invasion force and mobilizing the empire's resources for a new attempt.
- The *Persian invasion of Greece* (480 BCE) was unprecedented in scale: over 100,000 soldiers (some accounts claim far more), accompanied by cavalry, chariots, engineers, and a fleet of warships; the expedition crossed the Hellespont on a pontoon bridge and advanced southward.
- At the *Battle of Thermopylae* (480 BCE), a Greek coalition under *Leonidas of Sparta* held a mountain pass against Xerxes' army for three days; though the Persians eventually outflanked and destroyed the Greek force, the defense became legendary as martyrdom and heroism.
- After Thermopylae, Xerxes captured and burned *Athens*; however, the *Battle of Salamis* (480 BCE), a naval engagement, saw the Greek fleet (inferior in numbers but better coordinated) defeat the Persian navy, encouraging Greek resistance.
- The following year, at *Plataea* (479 BCE), a combined Greek army crushed the remaining Persian forces on the Greek mainland; Xerxes and his remaining fleet retreated to Asia, ending the invasion.
- Xerxes ruled for another 30 years, but his reign was marked by palace intrigues, satrap rebellions, and loss of territory; he was eventually assassinated by a court official, and his death marked the beginning of the Achaemenid Empire's gradual decline.
