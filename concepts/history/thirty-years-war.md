## summary

The **Thirty Years' War** (1618–1648) was the devastating conflict that ravaged Central Europe, beginning as a religious struggle between Catholic and Protestant powers within the Holy Roman Empire and escalating into a pan-European war of dynastic ambition. Starting with a rebellion in Bohemia, the war pulled in Sweden, France, and most German territories into a grueling contest that killed roughly one-third of the German population and reshaped the political map of Europe. The [[Peace of Westphalia]] that concluded it ended both religious war in Christendom and Habsburg dominance, establishing France as Europe's paramount power.

## you gotta know

- The war began in 1618 when Protestant nobles in Bohemia rebelled against Hapsburg Catholic rule, triggering wider religious conflict across the Holy Roman Empire.
- The conflict evolved into a dynastic power struggle as France, under *Cardinal Richelieu* and *Louis XIII*, intervened to break Hapsburg dominance in Europe.
- Sweden entered the war militarily under *King Gustavus Adolphus* to defend Protestant interests and gain Swedish territory and influence.
- Mercenary armies devastated the German territories; entire regions were depopulated by military campaigns, famine, and disease outbreaks accompanying armies.
- Germany fragmented into hundreds of semi-independent principalities by war's end; the Holy Roman Empire never recovered its former authority.
- The *Peace of Westphalia* (1648) established principles of national sovereignty and religious toleration, marking the end of religiously-motivated wars in Europe.
- France emerged as the war's clear winner, absorbing Alsace, gaining prestige, and displacing Spain and Austria as Europe's dominant power.

## connections

- [[france]] — the nation that entered late but emerged victorious, establishing itself as Europe's paramount power.
- [[peace-of-westphalia]] — the treaties concluding the war and establishing the modern European state system.
- [[house-of-habsburg]] — the dynasty defending Catholic interests and seeking to maintain empire, ultimately weakened by the war.
- [[holy-roman-empire]] — the political entity torn apart by internal religious and political conflict across its territories.
- [[protestant-reformation]] — the religious schism that provided the initial ideological justification for the conflict.
- [[louis-xiii]] — the French king whose minister *Richelieu* pursued dynastic aggrandizement through military intervention.
- [[sweden]] — the rising Protestant power that militarily intervened and gained territory and status.
