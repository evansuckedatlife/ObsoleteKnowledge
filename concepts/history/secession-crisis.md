## summary

The **Secession Crisis** (or **United States Secession Crisis**) was the acute political and constitutional breakdown between the November 1860 election of *Abraham Lincoln* and the April 1861 bombardment of *Fort Sumter*. Convinced that the incoming Republican administration posed an existential threat to the institution of slavery, seven Deep South slaveholding states seceded during the winter, followed by four Upper South states in the spring, severing their ties with the [[united-states]]. The crisis paralyzed the federal government under lame-duck president *James Buchanan*, witnessed the establishment of the *Confederate States of America*, and shattered all legislative efforts at compromise. This collapse of democratic consensus transformed decades of escalating sectional friction into open military conflict, directly triggering the [[american-civil-war]].

## you gotta know

- Sparked by the 1860 presidential election of *Abraham Lincoln*, whose victory on a platform barring slavery from federal territories prompted deep-seated panic across the South.
- *South Carolina* was the first state to secede on December 20, 1860, unanimously adopting a formal ordinance of secession that repealed its ratification of the [[united-states-constitution]] and citing Northern hostility to slavery in its official declaration of causes.
- Six other Deep South states—*Mississippi*, *Florida*, *Alabama*, *Georgia*, *Louisiana*, and *Texas*—seceded by February 1, 1861, forming a contiguous Lower South rebellion.
- Secessionist delegates met in Montgomery, Alabama, in February 1861 to establish the *Confederate States of America*, adopting a provisional constitution and selecting *Jefferson Davis* as provisional president.
- Outgoing president *James Buchanan* adopted a paralyzed lame-duck posture, declaring secession unconstitutional in his annual address to Congress while simultaneously insisting that the federal government possessed no constitutional power to coerce a seceding state.
- The *Crittenden Compromise*, introduced by Kentucky senator *John J. Crittenden*, attempted to avert disunion by proposing unamendable constitutional amendments that would permanently protect slavery where it existed and re-establish the *Missouri Compromise* line to the Pacific Ocean, but it collapsed after Lincoln and congressional Republicans refused to permit territorial expansion of slavery.
- The *Peace Conference of 1861*, organized by Virginia and chaired by former president *John Tyler*, convened over one hundred delegates at the *Willard Hotel* in Washington but failed to gain congressional backing.
- Resolved into military catastrophe on April 12, 1861, when Confederate forces under General *P. G. T. Beauregard* opened fire on Major *Robert Anderson's* federal garrison at *Fort Sumter*, driving four Upper South states into secession.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the cataclysmic conflict that erupted immediately when the standoff culminated in the bombardment of *Fort Sumter*.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the governing charter whose legal permanence was challenged by Southern doctrines of state sovereignty and secession.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic that confronted imminent collapse and territorial partition during the winter of 1860–1861.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the postwar settlement that redefined federal authority and integrated the defeated rebel states back into the nation.
- [[abraham-lincoln]] — the newly elected president whose inaugural pledge to preserve the Union and hold federal property guided Northern policy.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the technological divergence between Northern free-labor manufacturing and Southern plantation agriculture that intensified sectional conflict.
- [[american-revolution]] — the historical precedent of anti-tyrannical revolt that Southern secessionists invoked to legitimize their rebellion.
