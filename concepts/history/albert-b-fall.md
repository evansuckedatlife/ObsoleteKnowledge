## summary

**Albert B. Fall** (full name **Albert Bacon Fall**) was an American politician, attorney, and rancher who served as a United States Senator from New Mexico and as the Secretary of the Interior under President *Warren G. Harding*. He achieved enduring historical infamy as the central figure of the *Teapot Dome scandal*, one of the most egregious corruption episodes in the political history of the United States. By secretly granting lucrative, non-competitive leases for federal naval petroleum reserves to private oil barons in exchange for massive personal bribes, Fall became the first former cabinet officer in American history convicted of a felony committed while in office and sentenced to imprisonment.

## you gotta know

- Convicted of bribery in 1929 and sentenced to a year in prison and a one-hundred-thousand-dollar fine, ultimately serving nine months in the New Mexico State Penitentiary to become the first cabinet member in United States history imprisoned for crimes committed in office.
- Orchestrated the 1921 transfer of strategic naval oil reserves from the Department of the Navy to the Department of the Interior, convincing President *Warren G. Harding* to sign an executive order granting him direct administrative jurisdiction over the public oil lands.
- Leased the *Teapot Dome* naval reserve in Wyoming to *Harry Sinclair* of *Mammoth Oil* and the *Elk Hills* reserve in California to *Edward L. Doheny* of the *Pan American Petroleum and Transport Company* without competitive bidding.
- Accepted approximately four hundred thousand dollars in illicit payments, including cash, Liberty Bonds, and livestock to improve his *Three Rivers* ranch in New Mexico, famously including a hundred-thousand-dollar cash loan delivered in a "little black bag" by Doheny's son.
- Exposed by a protracted congressional investigation led by Montana Senator *Thomas J. Walsh*, who relentlessly untangled Fall's sudden, lavish ranch acquisitions and traced the suspicious financial transactions back to the participating oil executives.
- Created a celebrated legal paradox when he was found guilty of accepting a bribe from *Edward L. Doheny*, even though Doheny was subsequently acquitted in a separate trial of giving that very same bribe to Fall.
- Represented New Mexico as one of its first two United States Senators after it achieved statehood in 1912, having previously served as a territorial legislator, judge, and captain in the Spanish-American War.
- Practiced law on the Southwestern frontier, where he gained widespread regional fame as the defense attorney who secured an acquittal for rancher *Wayne Brazel* in the 1908 murder trial of legendary lawman *Pat Garrett*.

## connections

- [[warren-g-harding]] — the president who appointed him Secretary of the Interior and signed the executive order transferring control of naval oil reserves.
- [[harry-sinclair]] — the oil tycoon who bribed Fall with Liberty Bonds and cash to secure exclusive drilling rights at Wyoming's Teapot Dome reserve.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose executive branch, public lands, and strategic naval fuel reserves Fall exploited for personal enrichment.
- [[world-war-i]] — the global conflict that demonstrated the vital importance of secure naval oil supplies, spurring the creation of the reserves Fall leased.
- [[new-york-city]] — the financial capital where Sinclair and Doheny arranged the clandestine bonds and banking transfers funneled to Fall.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict defining Fall's Kentucky childhood, instilling Southern Democratic loyalties before his Western migration and political switch to the Republicans.
