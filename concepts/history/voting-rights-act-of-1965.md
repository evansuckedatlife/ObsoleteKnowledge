# Voting Rights Act of 1965

## summary

The *Voting Rights Act of 1965* was landmark federal legislation passed during the height of the *Civil Rights Movement*, signed by President *Lyndon B. Johnson*, that suspended literacy tests and authorized federal oversight of voter registration in jurisdictions with histories of racial discrimination. It effectively dismantled the legal machinery of voter suppression in the American South and represents one of the most consequential civil rights protections ever enacted.

## you gotta know

- The Act was a direct response to the *Selma to Montgomery marches* in March 1965, where peaceful civil rights activists including *Martin Luther King Jr.* were brutally beaten by state police, creating national outrage that forced federal action.
- Section 5 of the Act required jurisdictions with histories of voter discrimination to obtain federal approval before implementing any changes to voting procedures, a "preclearance" requirement that remained law until 2013.
- The Act suspended literacy tests, grandfather clauses, poll taxes (complemented by the *24th Amendment*), and other ostensibly race-neutral mechanisms that Southern states had used since *Reconstruction* to disenfranchise Black citizens.
- Federal *examiners* were dispatched to the Deep South to register voters directly, bypassing local officials who had systematically rejected Black applicants; this federal authority was unprecedented in peacetime and seen by segregationists as unconstitutional overreach.
- The Act applied initially to seven Southern states and later to other jurisdictions; its passage triggered the Southern Strategy, wherein Republicans absorbed white Southern Democrats who abandoned the party of *Lincoln* in response to civil rights legislation.
- Subsequent challenges in the *Shelby County v. Holder* decision (2013) gutted the preclearance requirement, restoring voting suppression through new methods; the Act remains subject to ongoing legal and political battles.
