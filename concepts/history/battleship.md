## summary

**Battleships** are heavily armored, large-displacement warships designed for direct combat at sea, serving as the capital ships of naval fleets from the age of steam onward. They represent the peak of naval dominance in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, before aircraft fundamentally shifted naval warfare. The battleship embodies an entire era of naval strategy and international power projection.

## you gotta know

- Evolved from wooden sailing ships to ironclad vessels in the American Civil War, with the *USS Monitor* vs. *CSS Virginia* encounter revolutionizing naval combat.
- The *HMS Dreadnought* (launched 1906) was the first "all-big-gun" battleship with uniform main armament, sparking a global naval arms race.
- Characterized by thick armor plating, powerful main gun batteries, and exceptional displacement—the *Yamato* and *USS Iowa* were among the largest warships ever built.
- Pearl Harbor (December 7, 1941) demonstrated both the battleship's vulnerability to air attack and its continued strategic importance in naval operations.
- The Battle of the Coral Sea (1942) marked the first naval battle where opposing ships never came within sight of each other—aircraft did the fighting—signaling the end of the battleship era.
- Battleships remained in limited service through the Cold War, with bombardment and power projection roles, though submarines and aircraft carriers became the dominant warship types.
