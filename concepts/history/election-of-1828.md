## summary

The **Election of 1828**, formally the **United States presidential election of 1828**, was a bitterly contested political rematch between incumbent National Republican President *John Quincy Adams* and Democratic candidate *Andrew Jackson*. Propelled by widespread populist resentment over the contested 1824 election and the broadening of white male suffrage, Jackson secured a decisive popular and electoral victory. The campaign ended the nonpartisan *Era of Good Feelings*, inaugurated the *Second Party System*, and established the modern *Democratic Party* under the banner of *Jacksonian Democracy*.

## you gotta know

- Won decisively by *Andrew Jackson*, who claimed 178 electoral votes to *John Quincy Adams*'s 83 and swept the South, the West, and Pennsylvania.
- Regarded as one of the most vitriolic campaigns in American history, featuring vicious attacks on Jackson's frontier brawls and his wife *Rachel Jackson*, alongside charges that Adams was an elitist aristocrat.
- Avenged Jackson's controversial loss in 1824, which his supporters had spent four years denouncing as a "Corrupt Bargain" struck between Adams and House Speaker *Henry Clay*.
- Benefited from the elimination of property qualifications for voting across most states, causing total voter turnout to more than double from roughly 350,000 in 1824 to over 1.1 million.
- Formally launched the modern *Democratic Party*, organized through a national coalition of local clubs, partisan newspapers, and party bosses engineered by New York senator *Martin Van Buren*.
- Shifted executive power away from traditional Virginia and Massachusetts dynasties toward the western frontier, symbolizing the rise of mass popular democracy.
- Initiated the widespread federal adoption of the *spoils system*, replacing long-serving career civil servants with loyal partisan supporters.

- [[united-states-constitution]] — the governing charter whose Electoral College provisions facilitated Jackson's landslide victory.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — the founder of the Democratic-Republicans whose agrarian ideals Jackson claimed to resurrect against federal consolidation.
