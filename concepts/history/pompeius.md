## summary

**Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus**, anglicized as **Pompey** or **Pompey the Great** and historically recorded as **Pompeius**, was a dominant military commander and politician of the late *Roman Republic* whose campaigns transformed [[rome]] into a global power. Initially celebrated for suppressing Mediterranean piracy and conquering expansive territories across the [[middle-east]], he formed the influential political alliance known as the *First Triumvirate* alongside [[julius-caesar]] and *Marcus Licinius Crassus*. When that coalition fractured, he aligned himself with conservative senators like [[cato-the-younger]] and [[cicero]] to confront Caesar in civil war, culminating in his defeat at the *Battle of Pharsalus* in 48 BC. His meteoric rise through unprecedented extraordinary commands and his dramatic fall epitomize the crises that dismantled republican institutions and paved the way for the [[roman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Formed the *First Triumvirate* with [[julius-caesar]] and *Marcus Licinius Crassus* in 60 BC, consolidating vast influence and marrying Caesar's daughter *Julia* to cement their political pact.
- Clashed directly with Caesar during the Great Roman Civil War, abandoning [[italy]] to regroup in the Balkans before suffering a decisive defeat at the *Battle of Pharsalus* in 48 BC.
- Cleared the Mediterranean of entrenched Cilician pirates in roughly three months under the sweeping, extraordinary military powers granted to him by the *Lex Gabinia* of 67 BC.
- Concluded the Third Mithridatic War against King *Mithridates VI* of Pontus, annexing Syria, capturing Jerusalem, and establishing a wealthy provincial client-state network across the [[middle-east]].
- Constructed the lavish *Theatre of Pompey* in 55 BC, the first permanent stone theatre in [[rome]], whose complex included a senatorial curia where Caesar was later assassinated.
- Achieved early military fame under the dictator *Lucius Cornelius Sulla*, campaigning ruthlessly in [[italy]] and [[spain]] to earn the cognomen *Magnus* alongside the dark epithet *Adulescentulus Carnifex* ("the teenage butcher").
- Assumed an unprecedented sole consulship in 52 BC to restore public order following mob riots and the murder of *Publius Clodius Pulcher*, a measure backed by constitutionalists like [[cato-the-younger]].
- Fled to Egypt after Pharsalus, where he was immediately assassinated upon landing at Pelusium by regents of the young king *Ptolemy XIII*, who mistakenly thought the killing would win Caesar's favor.

## connections

- [[julius-caesar]] — longtime ally in the First Triumvirate whose crossing of the Rubicon sparked the civil war that ended Pompey's career.
- [[cicero]] — Roman orator who championed Pompey's eastern commands and reluctantly supported him during the civil war as the Republic's defender.
- [[cato-the-younger]] — uncompromising Stoic statesman who long opposed Pompey's autocratic ambitions before ultimately endorsing him as the lesser evil against Caesar.
- [[mark-antony]] — Caesar's top general who fought against Pompey at Pharsalus and later acquired his confiscated properties in Rome.
- [[cleopatra]] — Ptolemaic queen whose brother ordered Pompey's murder, prompting Caesar's intervention in the Egyptian dynastic struggle.
- [[rome]] — imperial capital whose grain supply, judicial assemblies, and monumental architecture were reshaped by his triumphs and building projects.
- [[roman-empire]] — imperial regime that grew from the Republic's ruins, building on the precedent of extraordinary individual commands established by Pompey.
