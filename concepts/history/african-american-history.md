## summary

**African-American history** encompasses the lived experience of Black Americans from enslavement through the present, marked by systematic oppression, resilience, and repeated struggles for freedom and equality. Beginning with the forced arrival of enslaved Africans in the 17th century, it traces forced labor, Emancipation, *Reconstruction*, *Jim Crow* segregation, the *Civil Rights Movement*, and ongoing efforts toward racial justice. This history is central to understanding American identity, constitutional evolution, and the meaning of democracy itself.

## you gotta know

- Africans arrived in Virginia in 1619 and were gradually enslaved in larger numbers as colonial economies demanded labor; by 1700, slavery had become a racial institution.
- Enslaved African Americans—over 4 million by 1860—were the largest forced population movement in human history.
- The *Civil War* and *13th Amendment* ended slavery, but *Reconstruction*-era progress was reversed by *Jim Crow Laws* that entrenched segregation and disenfranchisement.
- The *Civil Rights Movement* (1950s–1960s) challenged segregation through nonviolent protest, legislation, and legal victories like *Brown v. Board of Education*.
- *Martin Luther King Jr.* became the moral leader of the Civil Rights era, delivering his "I Have a Dream" speech during the 1963 March on Washington.
- The *14th Amendment* (1868) promised equal protection but was narrowly interpreted for nearly a century; civil rights law finally realized it in the 1960s.
- Economic disparities, mass incarceration, health inequities, and educational gaps persist as legacies of slavery and Jim Crow.
