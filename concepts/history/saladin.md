# Saladin

## summary

**Saladin** (1137–1193), born Salah al-Din al-Ayyubi, was a Kurdish-born Muslim military commander and diplomat who united the fragmented Islamic world, defeated the Crusaders, and retook Jerusalem. His chivalry and strategic brilliance earned respect from both Christian and Muslim contemporaries, and he became the defining figure of the later Crusades, embodying Islamic resistance and the eventual reversals of Crusader territorial gains.

## you gotta know

- Saladin rose from service in the army of *Nur ad-Din*, the Zengid ruler, and eventually seized control of Egypt, establishing the *Ayyubid dynasty* and using Egypt's wealth to finance a unified campaign against the Crusader kingdoms.
- His great victory at the *Battle of Hattin* (July 1187) destroyed the principal Crusader field army, killing or capturing thousands; the decisive defeat forced Crusader garrisons to surrender across the Levant.
- Saladin retook *Jerusalem* in October 1187, ending nearly a century of Crusader rule; his conduct—offering safe passage to Christian civilians, treating the city's Christian shrines with respect—contrasted sharply with the brutality of the First Crusade's conquest in 1099 and enhanced his reputation as a civilized warrior.
- His military reforms included standardizing armies, establishing professional soldier corps (the *Mamluks*), and investing in fortifications and naval power; he recognized that defeating the Crusaders required sustained military organization, not just battlefield victories.
- Saladin's diplomacy was as important as his warfare: he played Crusader states against each other, negotiated truces and ransoms, and maintained correspondence with European rulers; he projected strength while avoiding unnecessary bloodshed.
- Though the *Third Crusade* (1189–92) arrived in response to his victories, led by *Richard the Lionheart*, Saladin held the line; the resulting *Treaty of Jaffa* (1192) established a truce: Crusaders retained coastal enclaves, but Muslim control of Jerusalem and the interior was secure.
