## summary

**The Americas**, collectively known in historical contexts as the **New World** or simply the **Americas**, encompass the continents of North and South America, joined by the Isthmus of Panama. Long inhabited by diverse Indigenous societies with sophisticated agricultural, urban, and scientific achievements, the hemisphere entered a transformative epoch following the arrival of European expeditions in 1492. This encounter triggered the *Columbian Exchange*, a monumental transfer of food crops, livestock, cultures, and lethal Eurasian pathogens that radically altered global ecologies and caused catastrophic demographic collapse among native populations. European powers established extractive empires through [[colonialism]] and the transatlantic slave trade, sparking geopolitical rivalries and revolutionary movements that created modern independent republics.

## you gotta know

- *Christopher Columbus* made landfall in the Caribbean in 1492 under the sponsorship of [[spain]], inaugurating regular transatlantic voyages and permanent European colonization across the hemisphere.
- Eurasian epidemic diseases, most catastrophic among them *smallpox*, measles, and typhus, devastated Indigenous populations with estimated mortality rates reaching between 80 and 90 percent due to a lack of prior immunity.
- Formidable pre-Columbian imperial states, notably the *Aztec Empire* (*Mexica*) centered at *Tenochtitlan* and the *Inca Empire* across the Andean highlands, collapsed following invasions by Spanish conquistadors *Hernán Cortés* and *Francisco Pizarro*.
- The 1494 *Treaty of Tordesillas*, ratified by the Catholic Church, divided global spheres of colonial conquest between [[spain]] and *Portugal*, establishing the legal basis for Portuguese Brazil and Spanish hegemony throughout the rest of the landmass.
- Extractive colonial systems relied extensively on coerced Indigenous labor through institutions like the *encomienda* and *mita*, which were soon supplemented by the brutal transatlantic slave trade to fuel sugarcane plantations and silver mining at *Potosí*.
- North American colonization sparked intense imperial rivalries as [[england]], [[france]], and the Dutch Republic challenged Iberian territorial claims, establishing profitable fur-trading networks, agricultural settlements, and Caribbean plantation colonies.
- Hemispheric independence movements dismantled colonial rule between 1775 and 1830, beginning with the [[american-revolution]] and the Haitian Revolution, and culminating in South American liberation campaigns spearheaded by *Simón Bolívar* and *José de San Martín*.

## connections

- [[age-of-exploration]] — European maritime era of oceanic navigation and global mapping that connected the hemisphere permanently to Afro-Eurasian trade networks.
- [[colonialism]] — governing structure through which European powers seized Indigenous territories, established extractive resource economies, and imposed metropolitan laws.
- [[spain]] — early imperial hegemon that colonized vast territories across Central and South America, founding the wealthy Viceroyalties of *New Spain* and *Peru*.
- [[british-empire]] — maritime power that developed lucrative mercantile networks and permanent settler colonies along the eastern coast of North America.
- [[france]] — European rival that maintained valuable fur-trade networks across *New France* and lucrative sugar-producing Caribbean colonies like *Saint-Domingue*.
- [[american-revolution]] — decisive anti-colonial uprising that produced the hemisphere's first independent constitutional republic, creating the [[united-states]].
- [[christianity]] — religious tradition disseminated throughout the continents through intense Catholic missionary efforts and Protestant colonial settlements.
