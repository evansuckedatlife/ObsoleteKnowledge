## summary

**Babylon** was one of the ancient world's greatest cities and the political and cultural center of successive empires in *Mesopotamia*, most prominently during the reigns of *Hammurabi* (18th century BCE) and *Nebuchadnezzar II* (early 6th century BCE). Situated on the *Euphrates River* in what is now Iraq, Babylon served as a hub of trade, learning, administration, and religious authority, home to the temples of the god *Marduk*, and birthplace of sophisticated systems of law, astronomy, and mathematics. The city's walls, gates, and legendary gardens symbolized ancient Mesopotamian power and culture, and its name became synonymous with grandeur, chaos, and the limits of human ambition in religious and literary traditions.

## you gotta know

- Babylon emerged as a city-state around the 18th century BCE under *Hammurabi*, who conquered surrounding territories and established the *Babylonian Empire*; under Hammurabi, Babylon became the paramount power in Mesopotamia, known for centralized administration and codified law.
- The *Code of Hammurabi* (ca. 1754 BCE), one of history's earliest comprehensive legal codes, was erected as a stone pillar in Babylon; it established principles of justice, property, commerce, and crime that influenced subsequent legal systems across the ancient world.
- Babylon declined after Hammurabi's reign but was conquered and restored by the *Kassite* dynasty and later by the *Assyrian Empire*; it was then re-established as a great power under *Nebuchadnezzar II* (605–562 BCE), who rebuilt the city to monumental grandeur.
- Under Nebuchadnezzar II, Babylon reached its architectural and political height; the city featured the *Ishtar Gate* (dedicated to the goddess of love and war), glazed brick walls depicting bulls and dragons, and the famous *Hanging Gardens* (one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, though archaeological evidence remains debated).
- The *Tower of Babel*, mentioned in biblical tradition, may be an echo of or confusion with Babylon's *Etemenanki*, a great temple tower (ziggurat) dedicated to Marduk; the ziggurat was the religious center of the city and a marvel of ancient engineering.
- Babylon served as a center of mathematics, astronomy, and *cuneiform* scholarship; Babylonian astronomers developed sophisticated understanding of celestial bodies and created predictions of lunar and solar phenomena that influenced later Greek astronomy.
- The city's position on the *Euphrates* made it a crucial trade hub connecting the Persian Gulf to regions further west; Babylon controlled overland trade routes and river commerce, accumulating wealth and cultural influence.
- Babylon fell to the *Persian Empire* under Cyrus II (539 BCE) and later came under the control of successive empires (*Greek*, *Parthian*); its decline was gradual, and by the medieval period it had been abandoned and eventually lost to the sands.
- In Judeo-Christian tradition, Babylon became a symbol of worldly power, excess, and divine judgment, particularly after its conquest of Judea and the deportation of Jews (586 BCE); the biblical *Book of Revelation*'s apocalyptic "Babylon" draws on this historical and symbolic legacy.
