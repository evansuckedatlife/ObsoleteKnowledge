## summary

The **War of Spanish Succession** (1701–1714) was a major European conflict fought over the throne of Spain following the death of the last *Habsburg* king. *Louis XIV* of France supported his grandson, *Philip of Anjou*, for the Spanish throne, which would have united Spain and France under Bourbon rule—a prospect intolerable to other European powers. The resulting thirteen-year war saw England, Austria, and the Dutch United Provinces align against France and Spain, ending with the *Treaty of Utrecht* (1713) that reshaped European balance of power and established Britain's dominance in colonial affairs.

## you gotta know

- King *Charles II* of Spain died childless in 1700; Spanish inheritance had been disputed for decades as the *Habsburg* dynasty weakened.
- *Louis XIV*, seeking to advance Bourbon fortunes and weaken Austria, supported his grandson *Philip of Anjou*'s claim; other powers feared a Franco-Spanish union would dominate Europe.
- The conflict pitted England, Austria, and the Dutch against France and Spain; Portugal, Denmark, and other smaller powers switched sides opportunistically.
- English general *John Churchill*, Duke of Marlborough, emerged as Europe's most successful military commander, winning decisive battles at *Blenheim* (1704), *Ramillies* (1706), and *Malplaquet* (1709).
- French resources were drained by simultaneous conflict in North America (Queen Anne's War) and naval warfare with the British, weakening *Louis XIV*'s grip on power.
- The *Treaty of Utrecht* (1713) confirmed *Philip V* as King of Spain (the first Bourbon monarch) but required Spain to cede territories: Sicily, Sardinia, and the Spanish Netherlands to Austria; Gibraltar and Newfoundland to Britain.
- Britain emerged as the war's principal victor, gaining naval and colonial supremacy; France retained much territory but was financially exhausted.
- The war exemplified 18th-century dynastic politics, where thrones were dynastic possessions traded through marriage and negotiation rather than national sovereignties.
