## summary

The **Feudal Contract**, also termed the **Feudal Agreement** or **Lord-Vassal Contract**, was the reciprocal legal and customary pact that structured political authority, land tenure, and military obligation in medieval Europe. Established through formal rituals of commendation, homage, and fealty, it bound a lord to provide protection and maintenance, typically via a landed estate called a fief, in exchange for military service and political counsel from his vassal. This decentralized system defined governance across post-Carolingian realms before centralizing monarchs and monetary economies eroded its foundational bonds.

## you gotta know

- Sealed through sacred ceremonial rites including the act of homage (*homagium*), the oath of fealty sworn on Christian relics, and the formal investiture of the fief (*feodum*).
- Obligated the vassal to provide *auxilium et consilium* (aid and counsel), which encompassed forty days of annual military knight service, garrison duty, and attendance at the lord's judicial court.
- Imposed financial requirements known as feudal incidents on vassals, including ransom for a captured lord, knighting expenses for the lord's eldest son, dowries for his eldest daughter, and relief payments upon inheritance.
- Required the superior lord to guarantee military defense and judicial justice for his vassal, with any unjust refusal or tyranny considered an intolerable breach called felony (*felonia*).
- Allowed subinfeudation, wherein vassals subdivided their own estates to lesser vassals, generating intricate, overlapping chains of allegiances that frequently sparked jurisdictional conflict.
- Evolved into cash commutations known as scutage or shield money, allowing knights to purchase exemptions from active warfare and enabling monarchs to hire professional mercenary armies.
- Paralleled independent socio-military contracts elsewhere in world history, most notably the samurai and daimyo bonds forged in Japan during the [[edo-period]].

## connections

- [[france]] — the core heartland where classical feudal tenure and seigneurial institutions fully matured.
- [[england]] — the realm where William the Conqueror systematically imposed a centralized version of tenure following the Norman Conquest.
- [[magna-carta]] — the landmark 1215 charter forced upon King John to curb royal abuses of traditional feudal incidents and arbitrary taxation.
- [[frankish-empire]] — the early medieval dominion whose disintegration after Charlemagne fostered local lordships and military vassalage.
- [[edo-period]] — the Japanese historical era that maintained a comparable hereditary framework of oaths and land grants between shoguns and daimyo.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial civilization whose late practices of *patrocinium* and *beneficium* contributed legal roots to feudal tenure.
- [[felix]] — royal surgeon rewarded with seigneurial titles and estates under a monarch who transformed the remnants of feudal nobility into court sycophants.
