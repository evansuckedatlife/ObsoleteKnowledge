## summary

**Imperialism** refers to the policy and practice of extending a nation's power and dominion through military conquest, political control, and economic exploitation of foreign territories and peoples. Dominant especially during the 19th and early 20th centuries, imperialism transformed global geography and created hierarchies of power that persist today. European powers, the *United States*, and *Japan* competed for colonies and spheres of influence, reshaping continents and cultures under claims of civilization and racial superiority.

## you gotta know

- The ideological justification for imperialism invoked the "civilizing mission"—the supposed duty of advanced nations to elevate colonized peoples through European governance, religion, and culture.
- Industrial powers needed raw materials, markets for manufactured goods, and strategic territorial claims, making imperialism economically driven as well as political.
- The *British Empire* became the model and dominant imperial power, controlling nearly a quarter of the world's land by 1900, but France, Germany, the *Netherlands*, *Belgium*, and others competed aggressively.
- Imperial conquest was facilitated by technological superiority—steamships, rifles, artillery, and modern medicine gave Western powers decisive advantages over indigenous societies.
- "Scramble for Africa" (1880–1914) saw European nations partition the continent with minimal regard for existing societies, creating arbitrary borders that persist as sources of conflict.
- American imperialism expanded into the *Caribbean* and *Pacific*, driven by *Theodore Roosevelt* and the doctrine of the "Big Stick" and hemispheric dominance.
- Imperial rivalries contributed to *World War I*, as competing empires clashed over colonial possessions and geopolitical spheres.
- Anti-colonial movements grew throughout the 20th century, eventually dismantling formal empires through decolonization, though economic and political influence persisted.
- The legacy of imperialism continues to shape international relations, economic inequality between nations, and geopolitical tensions rooted in colonial-era boundaries and power asymmetries.
