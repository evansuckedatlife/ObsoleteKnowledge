## summary

The **Atlantic slave trade** was the forced migration of an estimated 12–15 million enslaved Africans to the Americas over four centuries (c. 1500–1867). Enabled by European maritime expansion, legitimized by racial ideology, and driven by demand for labor in New World plantations, it became the largest forced population transfer in history and the foundation of systemic racism in the Americas.

## you gotta know

- Began in the 15th century (1444) after Portuguese exploration of West Africa; accelerated dramatically with Caribbean sugar colonization (1500s+) and North American settlement (1600s+).
- Portuguese, Spanish, British, French, Dutch, and Swedish traders competed for control of the trade; African kingdoms (*Dahomey*, *Asante*) and Arab merchants in East Africa facilitated capture and sale of enslaved people.
- Central Triangle model: manufactured goods from Europe → enslaved Africans from coast → sugar, cotton, tobacco, rum from Americas back to Europe, creating a self-reinforcing economic cycle.
- Transported an estimated 12–15 million enslaved Africans across the Atlantic (1500–1867); millions more died in transit or in African/Arab slave markets before embarkation.
- Regional destinations: majority to sugar plantations in Caribbean (esp. Brazil, Haiti), cotton and tobacco fields in American South, gold/diamond mines in Brazil; smaller numbers to North America and Europe.
- *Middle Passage*: the horrific 6–8 week Atlantic crossing in packed, disease-ridden holds; mortality rates of 10–25% from dysentery, scurvy, malaria, and brutality; some captives jumped overboard to escape.
- Economic justification: European colonizers claimed Africans were "naturally suited" to slavery; pseudo-scientific racism developed post-hoc to justify the trade; *Atlantic creole* communities sometimes profited as middlemen.
- Enabled by *Treaty of Tordesillas* (1494), which divided the New World between Portuguese and Spanish empires, effectively licensing European conquest and labor systems.
- Abolished gradually through abolitionist movements: Denmark (1803), Britain (1807), United States (1865), Brazil (1888)—last major nation to abolish slavery; some Arab/Ottoman slave trade continued into 20th century.
- Foundation of Atlantic racial slavery and systemic racism; created vast wealth for European merchants and colonizers (accumulated capital financing Industrial Revolution); impoverished Africa and entrenched racial hierarchies lasting to present.
