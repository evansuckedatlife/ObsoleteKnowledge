## summary

**American government** is the federal constitutional republic established across North America following the [[american-revolution]], formally codified by the ratification of the [[united-states-constitution]] in 1788. Structured around the strict separation of powers among coequal legislative, executive, and judicial branches, the national authority operates in tandem with individual state sovereignties under an enduring framework of [[federalism]]. Drawing heavily upon political philosophy from the European [[enlightenment]], particularly *Montesquieu's* division of governing functions and *John Locke's* doctrine of the social contract, the system established checks and balances designed to guard against autocratic consolidation. It remains one of modern history's earliest and most influential implementations of constitutional democracy, serving as a worldwide model for written constitutional design and federal division of power.

## you gotta know

- Divides sovereign authority across three coequal federal branches under *Articles I, II, and III* of the [[united-states-constitution]]: the bicameral *Congress*, the executive headed by the [[presidency]], and the independent judiciary led by the *Supreme Court*.
- Balances national supremacy with state autonomy through [[federalism]], delegating enumerated powers to the central government while explicitly reserving undelegated powers to the states or the people under the *Tenth Amendment*.
- Selects the national chief executive through the *Electoral College*, an indirect mechanism weighting presidential ballots by state congressional representation, which has produced notable popular-vote divergences such as the [[election-of-1824]] and the [[election-of-2000]].
- Preserves institutional equilibrium through checks and balances, including presidential vetoes, congressional funding control and impeachment, and judicial review of legislation first established in the landmark 1803 decision *Marbury v. Madison*.
- Regulates lawmaking through two legislative chambers with distinct functions: the *House of Representatives*, apportioned by population to reflect the popular will, and the *Senate*, providing equal state suffrage with staggered six-year terms and procedural filibusters.
- Substantially expanded national civil rights authority following the [[american-civil-war]] via the Reconstruction amendments, most notably the [[14th-amendment]], which incorporated federal due process and equal protection restrictions against the states.
- Administers public policy through an extensive executive bureaucracy and civil service, which grew from the initial four cabinet departments convened by [[george-washington]] into fifteen modern departments and numerous regulatory agencies.
- Sustains a dominant two-party system that first coalesced during early constitutional debates between *Alexander Hamilton* and [[thomas-jefferson]], later institutionalized by single-member district winner-take-all electoral rules.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme legal charter that established the institutional architecture, separated powers, and enumerated federal authorities.
- [[presidency]] — the executive office established under Article II, charged with law enforcement, armed forces command, and international diplomacy.
- [[federalism]] — the foundational structural doctrine dividing sovereignty concurrently between the national government and individual states.
- [[american-revolution]] — the war of independence against the [[british-empire]] that precipitated the creation of an independent republican confederation.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the post-Civil War amendment that fundamentally broadened federal civil rights protections and restricted state infringement on citizens.
- [[george-washington]] — the first president whose administrative conduct, department creation, and voluntary retirement established crucial executive precedents.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — the third president whose anti-Federalist philosophy and election solidified the early party system and agrarian democratic ideals.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical movement whose principles of popular consent and separated powers directly inspired the framers of the republic.
