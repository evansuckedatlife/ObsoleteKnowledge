## summary

**Queer theory** is an interdisciplinary field of critical theory that emerged in the late 1980s and early 1990s to deconstruct normative categories of sex, gender, and desire. Rooted in the political struggles of the [[united-states]] and post-structuralist thought in [[france]], it grew out of feminist critiques and radical mobilization during the [[hiv-aids-crisis]]. By demonstrating that identity categories are regulatory fictions rather than innate biological truths, the field fundamentally transformed how the humanities interrogate power, cultural institutions, and literary representation.

## you gotta know

- Formulated its most famous foundational premise in *Gender Trouble* (1990) by [[judith-butler]], arguing that gender is not an expressive biological truth, but an iterative performativity sustained through stylized bodily acts and regulatory norms.
- Coined as a formal academic term by feminist film theorist *Teresa de Lauretis* at a 1990 Santa Cruz conference and in a 1991 issue of *Differences*, though she later abandoned the phrase after its rapid academic institutionalization.
- Advanced major literary methodologies through *Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick*, whose *Between Men* (1985) and *Epistemology of the Closet* (1990) analyzed homosocial desire and demonstrated that modern Western culture is fundamentally structured by homo/heterosexual secrecy.
- Drew its historical foundation from *Michel Foucault*'s *The History of Sexuality* (1976), which critiqued the repressive hypothesis and argued that nineteenth-century institutions created the modern homosexual as a distinct medicalized species rather than merely criminalizing sodomy.
- Emerged directly out of militant direct-action activism by ACT UP and Queer Nation during the catastrophic [[hiv-aids-crisis]], deliberately breaking from the assimilationist respectability politics of post-[[stonewall]] gay liberation in favor of confrontational visibility.
- Popularized the critique of *heteronormativity*, an analytical concept expanded by literary theorist *Michael Warner* in 1991 to expose how social institutions, legal codes, and everyday culture naturalize heterosexual romance, reproduction, and domestic life.
- Prompted the psychoanalytic and political "anti-social turn," exemplified by *Lee Edelman*'s *No Future* (2004) opposing the tyranny of "reproductive futurism," and *José Esteban Muñoz*'s *Cruising Utopia* (2009) reclaiming queerness as an open, backward-looking horizon of hope.

## connections

- [[judith-butler]] — established the foundational paradigm of gender performativity, reframing gender as repetitive discursive action rather than biological fact.
- [[existentialism]] — provided philosophical roots via *[[simone-de-beauvoir]]*, whose claim that womanhood is a social becoming prefigured queer anti-essentialism.
- [[hiv-aids-crisis]] — the catastrophic health emergency whose neglect sparked the militant, direct-action protests that galvanized the academic field.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — established legal frameworks, direct-action strategies, and coalitional protest models adapted by early gay liberation and queer activists.
- [[linguistics]] — supplied speech-act theory through *J. L. Austin*, which theorists adapted to explain how discursive declarations actively construct gendered subjects.
- [[modernism]] — offered early twentieth-century literary techniques that broke with Victorian domesticity, conventional narrative form, and fixed sexual identities.
- [[stonewall]] — the 1969 rebellion in [[new-york-city]] that launched militant gay liberation and provided historical inspiration for radical queer resistance.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — whose Renaissance sonnets and cross-dressing comedies became foundational subjects for queer literary scholars analyzing homosocial desire.
