## summary

**Montezuma II** (1480–1520) was the ninth Aztec emperor during the civilization's final crisis, ruling from 1502 to 1520. His reign saw the arrival of Spanish conquistadors under *Hernán Cortés* and the collapse of the Aztec Empire—a catastrophe hastened by his own hesitant diplomacy and the devastation of smallpox among his people.

## you gotta know

- Inherited the Aztec Empire at its territorial and cultural height, presiding over a sophisticated civilization of millions centered in Tenochtitlan, one of the world's largest cities.
- Initially received Cortés and the Spanish with hospitality rather than immediate military opposition, possibly viewing them as divine envoys—a hesitation later regretted when Spanish intentions became clear.
- Suffered from profound indecision and escalating conflict with Cortés, allowing the Spanish to consolidate power and form alliances with resentful tributary peoples chafing under Aztec rule.
- Allowed himself to be taken captive by Cortés and held as a hostage to control the Aztec state, an unprecedented humiliation that undermined his authority and religious prestige.
- Died in 1520 during the Spanish siege of Tenochtitlan, either from Spanish violence or wounds inflicted by his own people disgusted by his perceived betrayal and weakness.
- The Spanish conquest was accelerated by *smallpox* epidemics that killed far more Aztecs than combat, devastating the empire's population and military capacity within months.
- Became a figure of tragic irony in history—an emperor of a great civilization who faced an enemy he could not comprehend and made choices that hastened his empire's annihilation.
- Modern indigenous scholarship reassesses his legacy as a statesman facing impossible choices rather than a simple fool, complicating the European narrative of inevitable conquest and Spanish superiority.
