## summary

The **Free Exercise Clause** is the provision of the First Amendment to the *[[united-states-constitution]]* guaranteeing individuals the right to practice their religion freely without undue governmental interference. Ratified in 1791 alongside the Establishment Clause, it forms one half of the fundamental American dual framework regarding religious liberty developed out of *[[enlightenment]]* thought. Through selective incorporation via the *[[fourteen-amendment]]*, the clause restricts both state and federal legislative bodies from penalizing religious belief or targeting specific faith communities.

## you gotta know

- Textually situated in the First Amendment immediately following the Establishment Clause, stating that Congress shall make no law "prohibiting the free exercise thereof."
- Applied to state and local governments through selective incorporation under the Due Process Clause of the *[[fourteen-amendment]]* in the 1940 Supreme Court case *Cantwell v. Connecticut*.
- Historically distinguished between religious beliefs, which enjoy absolute protection from state sanction, and religious actions, which remain subject to valid governmental regulation.
- Tested in the landmark 1879 case *Reynolds v. United States*, where the Supreme Court ruled that criminal bans on polygamy did not violate the religious rights of Mormon practitioners.
- Subject to the strict scrutiny standard established in *Sherbert v. Verner* (1963) and *Wisconsin v. Yoder* (1972), requiring the government to demonstrate a compelling state interest when burdening religious practice.
- Reinterpreted in *Employment Division v. Smith* (1990), authored by Justice Antonin Scalia, which held that neutral, generally applicable laws do not violate religious freedom even if they incidentally burden faith practices.
- Prompted significant legislative response, including the federal Religious Freedom Restoration Act (RFRA) of 1993, which sought to restore the heightened *Sherbert* balancing test.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme legal framework containing the Bill of Rights in which this protection is enshrined.
- [[fourteen-amendment]] — the amendment whose Due Process Clause incorporated the clause against infringements by state governments.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement advocating freedom of conscience and toleration that inspired the founding generation.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — the Virginia statesman whose Statute for Religious Freedom directly influenced the drafting of federal religious protections.
- [[christianity]] — the dominant faith tradition in the early republic whose diverse denominations necessitated constitutional protections against state interference.
- [[judaism]] — a religious minority in the United States whose dietary, sabbath, and skullcap practices have frequently intersected with free exercise litigation.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic where free exercise disputes continue to shape civil liberties debates.
