## summary

The **Norman Conquest** was the invasion and takeover of *England* by *William the Conqueror* and his Norman forces between 1066 and the early 1070s. Triggered by a disputed succession to the English throne, the conquest decisively defeated the *Anglo-Saxon* resistance and replaced the English nobility with Norman-French lords. This single military event fundamentally transformed English language, culture, law, and institutional development for centuries to come.

## you gotta know

- The conquest began with *William of Normandy's* invasion in September 1066, motivated by his claim to the throne of *England* following the death of *Edward the Confessor*.
- The decisive *Battle of Hastings* (October 1066) saw *William* defeat and kill *Harold Godwinson*, the Anglo-Saxon king, and destroy the English noble class.
- The conquest took several years to consolidate; pockets of Anglo-Saxon and Scandinavian resistance persisted, especially in the North, until the early 1070s.
- Norman feudal institutions replaced Anglo-Saxon systems; the Normans introduced the knight, the castle, and the hierarchical feudal structure that defined medieval England.
- The Norman victory ended Scandinavian influence over *England*; it permanently aligned *England* with *France* culturally and politically, and tied English succession to Norman dynastic struggles.
- The Domesday Book (1086), commissioned by *William*, was an unprecedented administrative survey that asserted total royal dominion over all English lands and subjects.
- Norman French became the language of the English court and nobility for centuries; the blending of Norman and Anglo-Saxon cultures eventually produced English identity and the English language itself.
