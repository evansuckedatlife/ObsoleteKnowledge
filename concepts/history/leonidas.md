## summary

**Leonidas I** was a Spartan king (r. 480 BCE) who led Greece's most famous act of defiant resistance against *Xerxes*' invading Persian Empire. At the narrow pass of Thermopylae in 480 BCE, Leonidas commanded roughly 7,000 Greek hoplites, including 300 elite Spartan warriors, against an overwhelmingly larger Persian force. Though ultimately defeated and killed, Leonidas' stand became mythology's archetypal symbol of courage, sacrifice, and the triumph of determination over impossible odds.

## you gotta know

- One of two Spartan kings (Sparta maintained a dual kingship), reigning during the Persian Wars, the defining conflict of classical Greece in the 5th century BCE.
- Chose to hold the pass at Thermopylae with a small contingent when *Xerxes* invaded Greece in 480 BCE, blocking the only major route into central Greece through narrow mountain terrain.
- His 300 Spartan elite were reinforced by hoplites from other Greek city-states; together they held the narrow pass for two days against vastly larger Persian forces estimated at 100,000 or more.
- Died at Thermopylae when a traitor revealed a mountain path allowing Persians to outflank his position; he famously ordered the remaining Greeks to retreat while he held the pass to the death.
- The stand at Thermopylae gave other Greek forces critical time to organize their overall resistance and ultimately contributed to the Persian defeat at *Plataea* later that year.
- His legend epitomized Spartan values of honor, self-sacrifice, and unwavering commitment to duty, making him an immortal hero figure across Greek culture and Western tradition.
