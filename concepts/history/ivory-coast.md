## summary

**Ivory Coast**, officially designated as the Republic of **Côte d'Ivoire**, is a major West African coastal nation along the Gulf of Guinea that became the economic anchor of [[french-west-africa]] and subsequently one of post-colonial Africa's most prosperous states. Following independence from [[france]] in 1960 under the guidance of founding leader *Félix Houphouët-Boigny*, the country developed an agriculture-driven economy centred on cocoa and coffee exports that generated substantial economic expansion known as the *Ivorian Miracle*. The country served as a primary bulwark of Western influence and *Françafrique* diplomacy throughout the [[cold-war]], though subsequent disputes over national identity, economic downturns, and political succession ultimately triggered intense civil conflict in the early twenty-first century.

## you gotta know

- *Félix Houphouët-Boigny* governed the nation as its first president for thirty-three unbroken years until his death in 1993, maintaining an exceptionally close political, economic, and military relationship with [[france]].
- Surpassed all competitors to become the world's leading producer and exporter of cocoa beans, establishing an export-oriented cash-crop economy that financed massive infrastructure projects and rapid urbanization in the port city of *Abidjan*.
- *Yamoussoukro*, transformed into the official political capital by presidential decree in 1983, houses the massive *Basilica of Our Lady of Peace*, a monumental [[christianity|Christian]] edifice inspired by *St. Peter's Basilica* in [[rome]].
- The xenophobic ideology of *ivoirité* ("Ivorianness"), introduced by President *Henri Konan Bédié* to disenfranchise political rivals such as *Alassane Ouattara*, fractured national cohesion and exacerbated bitter sectarian conflict between the [[islam|Muslim]] north and Christian south.
- A contested 2010 presidential election resulted in the *Second Ivorian Civil War*, which concluded only when international peacekeepers and French forces stormed the presidential compound to arrest incumbent *Laurent Gbagbo* after his refusal to concede defeat.
- Anti-colonial resistance across the region was fiercely sustained by the *Malinké* ruler *Samori Ture*, founder of the *Wassoulou Empire*, who conducted a brilliant fifteen-year military campaign against French expansion before his capture in 1898.
- Nationalist mobilisations grew out of the *Syndicat Agricole Africain*, an indigenous planters' union founded in 1944 to protect native smallholders from discriminatory agricultural policies and forced labour mandates instituted during [[world-war-ii]].

## connections

- [[france]] — the former imperial ruler that established the colonial protectorate in 1893 and preserved extensive economic and military interventionism after formal independence.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader system of European rule that fixed national frontiers, mandated forced agricultural quotas, and reoriented local trade toward maritime export.
- [[french-west-africa]] — the colonial federation administered from *Dakar* in which this territory generated the highest export revenues alongside [[senegal]].
- [[senegal]] — key regional peer whose statesman *Léopold Sédar Senghor* clashed with *Houphouët-Boigny* regarding whether French West African colonies should confederate or pursue individual independence.
- [[cold-war]] — the ideological confrontation during which the state acted as a staunch anti-communist outpost and staunch diplomatic ally of the Western bloc.
- [[dahomey-benin]] — neighbouring French colony and fellow member of the *Conseil de l'Entente*, a regional cooperation forum spearheaded by Ivorian leadership in 1959.
- [[mali-empire]] — historic medieval realm whose commercial diaspora and *Dyula* trading networks integrated the northern regions into trans-Saharan caravan routes.
