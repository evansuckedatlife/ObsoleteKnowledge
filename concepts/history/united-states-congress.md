## summary

The **United States Congress** is the bicameral federal legislature of the [[united-states]], established under Article I of the [[united-states-constitution]] in 1789. Comprising the upper chamber, the *Senate*, and the lower chamber, the *House of Representatives*, it holds the exclusive national power to enact federal legislation, levy taxes, allocate public spending, and declare war. Throughout American history, the institution has served as the primary battleground for defining federal authority, resolving and deepening sectional tensions, and constructing the modern regulatory and welfare state.

## you gotta know

- Established by Article I of the [[united-states-constitution]] via the Great Compromise of 1787, balancing state sovereignty through equal representation in the *Senate* with popular sovereignty through proportional representation in the *House of Representatives*.
- Exercises substantial enumerated and implied constitutional powers, including control over the federal purse, interstate commerce regulation, armed forces maintenance, and the initiation of constitutional amendments.
- Enacted the foundational statutes of the American welfare and regulatory state, authorizing Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal relief programs during the Great Depression and Lyndon B. Johnson's Great Society anti-poverty measures.
- Passed monumental civil rights statutes during the [[civil-rights-movement]], relying upon its commerce power and constitutional enforcement clauses to enact the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.
- Dominated national governance during the [[reconstruction-era]], as a veto-proof Radical Republican majority passed the Reconstruction Acts over Andrew Johnson's vetoes, proposed the [[14th-amendment]], and conducted the first presidential impeachment trial.
- First assembled under the federal Constitution at Federal Hall in [[new-york-city]] in March 1789 under President [[george-washington]], where members established the federal court system and drafted the Bill of Rights.
- Mobilized national scientific resources during the [[cold-war]] and [[space-race]], enacting the National Aeronautics and Space Act of 1958 to establish [[nasa]] and funding the National Defense Education Act.
- Plagued by escalating sectional violence in the antebellum era, epitomized in 1856 when Representative Preston Brooks of South Carolina brutally caned abolitionist Senator Charles Sumner on the Senate floor before the [[american-civil-war]].

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — established the bicameral design, enumerated legislative powers, and structural limitations of the national legislature in Article I.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — period when Radical Republican majorities exercised unprecedented legislative supremacy to reshape Southern society and safeguard civil rights.
- [[american-civil-war]] — existential conflict preceded by decades of fragile legislative compromises over slavery, requiring wartime congressional measures to preserve the Union.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — grassroots social struggle that impelled lawmakers to enact comprehensive statutory bans against racial discrimination and disenfranchisement.
- [[14th-amendment]] — transformative constitutional amendment drafted by the Joint Committee on Reconstruction to guarantee birthright citizenship, due process, and equal protection under the law.
- [[george-washington]] — first chief executive, who worked directly with the inaugural Congress in [[new-york-city]] to organize the federal judiciary and cabinet departments.
- [[space-race]] — technological rivalry with the Soviet Union that spurred lawmakers to pass landmark legislation establishing [[nasa]] and expanding federal research grants.
- [[cold-war]] — prolonged geopolitical contest that shaped congressional appropriations, committee investigations, foreign aid doctrines, and national security institutions.
