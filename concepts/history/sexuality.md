## summary

**Sexuality**, encompassing **human sexuality**, refers to the capacity for erotic experiences, intimate desires, identities, and behaviors, functioning across human history as both an elemental aspect of personal life and a heavily contested social institution. In political and intellectual history, particularly within [[second-wave-feminism]], philosophy, and critical sociology, the subject shifted from a purely biological phenomenon into a primary domain of power dynamics, legal regulation, and bodily autonomy. Tracing the development of sexual norms highlights how religious doctrines, state surveillance, psychoanalytic doctrines, and grassroots liberation movements have shaped personhood across cultures.

## you gotta know

- *Michel Foucault* revolutionized modern historiography in *The History of Sexuality* (1976), challenging the common "repressive hypothesis" by arguing that modern Western institutions did not silence sex, but actively multiplied discourses and techniques of *biopower* to regulate it.
- The sexual revolution of the 1960s radically transformed Western society, spurred by the advent of the oral contraceptive pill in the [[united-states]] in 1960, legal victories regarding privacy, and the public emergence of modern gay liberation movements following the 1969 Stonewall riots in [[new-york-city]].
- Biologist *Alfred Kinsey* disrupted conventional moral assumptions through his landmark mid-century surveys on male and female sexual behavior, introducing the seven-point *Kinsey scale* to demonstrate that human erotic attraction operates along a fluid spectrum rather than an absolute binary.
- Leading theorists of [[second-wave-feminism]], including *Germaine Greer* in *The Female Eunuch* (1970) and *Simone de Beauvoir*, argued that patriarchal culture suppressed female sexual agency and pleasure, positioning bodily autonomy as indispensable to the [[womens-liberation]] movement.
- In works such as *Gender Trouble* (1990), philosopher *Judith Butler* and other architects of [[queer-theory]] challenged the naturalness of the sex-gender binary, contending that sexuality and gender are performatively instituted through repeated social conventions and compulsory heterosexuality.
- In *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality* (1905), *Sigmund Freud* posited that human *libido* is not merely an adult reproductive instinct but a foundational psychological drive that develops from infancy through polymorphous stages of erotic organization.
- Historical scholarship on [[ancient-greece]] and [[rome]] by classicists like *Kenneth Dover* revealed that premodern Mediterranean cultures organized sexual ethics around civic hierarchy and active versus passive roles, rather than modern identities based on the gender of one's partner.

## connections

- [[second-wave-feminism]] — asserted women's sexual pleasure, reproductive self-determination, and liberation from patriarchal control over their bodies.
- [[queer-theory]] — interrogated normative assumptions about desire, identity, and the cultural mechanisms that enforce heteronormativity.
- [[existentialism]] — shaped twentieth-century feminist philosophy by analyzing how bodily existence, personal freedom, and erotic objectification determine relations with the *Other*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — provided historical models where erotic practices were structured primarily by age, status, and civic authority rather than sexual orientation.
- [[christianity]] — established the theological paradigms, canon law restrictions, and moral framework governing marital fidelity and carnal acts in Western history.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — supplied crucial organizing strategies, rights discourse, and coalition paradigms adopted by gay rights and sexual liberation activists.
- [[united-states]] — served as the epicenter for empirical sexological surveys, high-profile constitutional privacy jurisprudence, and modern queer activism.
