## summary

The **Cuban Revolution** (**Revolución Cubana**) was an armed insurrection from 1953 to 1959 that ousted the authoritarian, US-backed regime of *Fulgencio Batista* and established a revolutionary socialist state under *Fidel Castro*. Centered on guerrilla warfare waged by the 26th of July Movement from the Sierra Maestra mountains, the victory fundamentally transformed Latin American geopolitics and made Cuba a central flashpoint of the [[cold-war]]. The new government aligned with the [[soviet-union]], enacted sweeping nationalizations and agrarian reforms, and prompted decades of American hostility, covert operations, and economic blockade.

## you gotta know

- Commenced on July 26, 1953, with the disastrous armed assault on the Moncada Barracks in Santiago de Cuba, leading to the arrest of *Fidel Castro*, who delivered his renowned courtroom defense titled "History Will Absolve Me" (*La historia me absolverá*).
- Launched into its decisive guerrilla phase in December 1956 when eighty-two rebels aboard the overcrowded yacht *Granma* landed at Playa Las Coloradas, suffering immediate ambush before survivors regrouped in the Sierra Maestra mountains.
- Commanded by key revolutionary figures including Argentine physician *Ernesto "Che" Guevara*, *Raúl Castro*, and *Camilo Cienfuegos*, who led guerrilla columns against superior federal troops and broadcast revolutionary propaganda over *Radio Rebelde*.
- Gained crucial domestic and international legitimacy following *Herbert Matthews*'s sympathetic 1957 front-page interviews with *Fidel Castro* published in *The New York Times*, disproving government claims of the rebels' deaths.
- Reached its climax in late December 1958 at the Battle of Santa Clara, where *Che Guevara*'s forces derailed an armored military train (*tren blindado*), prompting *Fulgencio Batista* to flee the country on New Year's Day, January 1, 1959.
- Triggered intense hostility from the [[united-states]], culminating in the failed CIA-sponsored Bay of Pigs Invasion (*Playa Girón*) in April 1961 and the catastrophic October 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis following Soviet nuclear deployments.
- Imposed rigorous social control and state persecution on nonconforming groups during the 1960s, dispatching artists, religious practitioners, and homosexuals to Military Units to Aid Production (*UMAP*) forced labor camps.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — transformed the Caribbean island into an ideological battleground and triggered the nuclear standoff of the Cuban Missile Crisis.
- [[soviet-union]] — provided military protection, economic subsidies, and political sponsorship after American boycotts severed trade.
- [[united-states]] — mounted repeated covert sabotage campaigns, the Bay of Pigs invasion, and a comprehensive trade embargo against the revolutionary state.
- [[colonialism]] — positioned itself ideologically as the completion of nineteenth-century anti-imperialist independence struggles against [[spain]] and American dominance.
- [[criminalization-of-homosexuality]] — implemented severe state repression against gay citizens through forced internment in labor camps under revolutionary morality campaigns.
