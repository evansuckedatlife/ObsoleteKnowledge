## summary

**Minos** was the legendary king of Crete in ancient Greek mythology, whose palace at [[Knossos]] dominated the Bronze Age Aegean. A son of *Zeus* and *Europa*, he became a figure of law and divine justice, though also infamous for exacting tribute from Athens in the form of youths sacrificed to the [[Minotaur]]. His reign represents the height of Minoan civilization and remains central to Greek myths of power, sovereignty, and the clash between civilization and monstrosity.

## you gotta know

- Son of *Zeus* and the Phoenician princess *Europa*, giving him divine authority and legitimacy to rule the island of Crete.
- Ruled from [[Knossos]], the greatest palace city on Crete, which served as the seat of Minoan civilization during the Bronze Age.
- Became a judge of the dead in the underworld—a role gained through his connection to *Zeus*—and his reputation for justice was legendary.
- Commissioned the craftsman [[Daedalus]] to build the Labyrinth to confine the [[Minotaur]]—his half-human, half-bull offspring with his wife *Pasiphae*, a curse from *Poseidon*.
- Imposed an annual tribute of seven young men and seven maidens from Athens to feed the Minotaur as punishment and as tribute to his power.
- His palace at Knossos, excavated by archaeologist Sir Arthur Evans, revealed the grandeur, engineering, and artistic sophistication of Bronze Age Minoan civilization.
- Defeated in combat by the Athenian hero [[Theseus]], who navigated the Labyrinth using Ariadne's thread, slew the Minotaur, and freed Athens from tribute.
- According to legend, died while pursuing the escaped [[Daedalus]] and his son [[Icarus]] to the court of the Sicilian king *Cocalus*, where he was killed.
