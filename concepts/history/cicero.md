## summary

**Cicero** (*Marcus Tullius Cicero*, 106–43 BCE) was the greatest orator of the Roman Republic and a statesman whose speeches, writings, and political career embodied the ideals of republican virtue. As consul during the conspiracy of *Catiline* and later as a senator opposing *Mark Antony*, he defended the Republic against tyranny through rhetoric and law. His work established the classical standard for Latin prose and political philosophy, influencing Western thought for two millennia.

## you gotta know

- Born in Arpinum to an equestrian (non-noble) family, he rose to supreme power through skill in oratory and law rather than aristocratic birth.
- Elected consul in 63 BCE, he uncovered and defeated the conspiracy of *Catiline*, a patrician who plotted to overthrow the Republic; his speeches exposing this threat (*Catilinarian Orations*) became legendary examples of political oratory.
- Composed the *Philippics*—fourteen speeches delivered against *Mark Antony* after the assassination of *Julius Caesar*—which attacked Antony as a tyrant and defended republican liberty; Antony had him executed for these words.
- His philosophical writings synthesized Greek Stoicism and Epicureanism for Roman audiences, introducing the *via media* (middle path) approach to ethics and politics.
- Perfected the Latin prose style (*Ciceronian style*) characterized by balanced periods, varied vocabulary, and rhythmic delivery; his works remain the gold standard for rhetorical training.
- Wrote extensively on rhetoric (*De Oratore*, *Brutus*, *Orator*), defining oratory as the art of persuading audiences through reason, emotion, and character.

## connections

- [[julius-caesar]] — political rival and alliance partner; Cicero opposed Caesar's consolidation of power while trying to maintain the Republic.
- [[mark-antony]] — the Triumvir and populist whom Cicero attacked in the *Philippics*, earning his death.
- [[cato-the-younger]] — fellow defender of republican virtue, with whom Cicero shared ideological alignment against imperial ambition.
- [[pompey]] — general and statesman aligned with Cicero in defense of the Senate and Republican institutions.
- [[roman-republic]] — the political system Cicero served and whose values he championed against tyranny.
- [[stoicism]] — the philosophical framework that influenced Cicero's ethics and his conception of duty and virtue.
- [[oratory]] — the art form Cicero mastered and defined for classical civilization.
