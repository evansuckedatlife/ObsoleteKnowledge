## summary

The **NAACP** (National Association for the Advancement of Colored People) was founded in 1909 as the first major civil rights organization committed to ending racial discrimination in America through legal action, political advocacy, and public education. Operating throughout the Jim Crow era and into the Civil Rights Movement, it pioneered strategies of litigation and institutional pressure that would define the movement's approach to racial justice. The NAACP became the most sustained and organizationally robust civil rights institution in American history.

## you gotta know

- Founded by an interracial coalition of African American and white activists, including W.E.B. Du Bois, in response to race riots and escalating racial violence across the country.
- Established local chapters nationwide, giving African Americans an organized vehicle for challenging segregation, disenfranchisement, and economic exploitation systematically rather than ad hoc.
- Pioneered the legal strategy of challenging "separate but equal" doctrine, setting the foundation for litigation that would culminate in *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954).
- Fought against lynching through publicizing atrocities, lobbying for federal legislation, and mobilizing public opinion, though federal anti-lynching laws remained elusive.
- Developed field organizers like Ella Baker and investigated racial violence like the murder of Medgar Evers, connecting national leadership with grassroots community work.
- Maintained institutional continuity and political influence across decades, transitioning from the early-20th-century era through the Civil Rights Movement into ongoing civil rights advocacy.

## connections

- [[ella-baker]] — NAACP organizer and conscience who later co-founded the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee.
- [[medgar-evers]] — NAACP field secretary in Mississippi whose assassination became a catalyst for movement radicalization.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — civil rights leader who worked alongside NAACP strategists and gained national prominence through NAACP connections.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the broader movement for racial justice that the NAACP helped launch and sustained through its institutions.
- [[desegregation]] — the legal and social goal the NAACP pursued through litigation and advocacy.
- [[united-states]] — the nation transformed by NAACP-driven civil rights struggles.
