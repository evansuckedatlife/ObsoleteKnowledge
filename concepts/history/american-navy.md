## summary

The **United States Navy**, commonly designated the **American Navy** or **U.S. Navy**, is the sea service branch of the armed forces of the *[[united-states]]*. Established in 1775 during the *[[american-revolution]]* and permanently reauthorized by Congress in 1794, it serves to maintain maritime security, protect international trade, and project sovereign power worldwide. Over two and a half centuries, the branch evolved from a modest force of wooden sailing frigates into the most formidable maritime fighting organization on Earth.

## you gotta know

- Achieved definitive global supremacy during *[[world-war-ii]]*, using aircraft carriers and naval aviation to defeat the naval forces of *[[japan]]* in milestone engagements at the Coral Sea, Midway, and Leyte Gulf.
- Traces its historical lineage to the Continental Navy formed by the Continental Congress in 1775, before being permanently reconstituted under the Naval Act of 1794, which commissioned six original frigates including *USS Constitution* (*"Old Ironsides"*).
- Enforced the Anaconda Plan blockade of Southern ports throughout the *[[american-civil-war]]*, introduced revolving-turret ironclads at the Battle of Hampton Roads, and secured strategic harbors under Admiral *David Farragut*.
- Expanded into a modern ocean-spanning fleet inspired by naval strategist *Alfred Thayer Mahan*, culminating in the victory over *[[spain]]* in 1898 and President *Theodore Roosevelt's* worldwide tour of the *"Great White Fleet"*.
- Pioneered naval nuclear propulsion under the direction of Admiral *Hyman G. Rickover*, deploying the submarine *USS Nautilus* and establishing an enduring ballistic missile deterrent against the *[[soviet-union]]* throughout the *[[cold-war]]*.
- Gained early international renown during the Barbary Wars, when Lieutenant *Stephen Decatur* destroyed the captured frigate *USS Philadelphia* in Tripoli harbor, and in the War of 1812 under Master Commandant *Oliver Hazard Perry*.
- Formally established as an executive cabinet department in 1798 under *Benjamin Stoddert* during the Quasi-War with *[[france]]*, having previously operated under the Department of War headed by *[[george-washington]]*.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the two-ocean conflict in which carrier aviation and amphibious landings established American mastery of the Pacific.
- [[american-revolution]] — the War of Independence during which the Continental Navy and commissioned privateers first challenged British maritime control.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the internal conflict that pioneered modern riverine warfare, coastal blockade operations, and ironclad ship designs.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical standoff that spurred the deployment of supercarrier strike groups and continuous undersea nuclear deterrence patrols.
- [[george-washington]] — the general and president who authorized early armed schooners in 1775 and signed the 1794 legislation creating the permanent fleet.
- [[japan]] — the naval adversary that mounted the surprise attack on Pearl Harbor before signing unconditional surrender documents on an American battleship.
- [[soviet-union]] — the primary maritime peer competitor whose expansive submarine forces were tracked across oceanic chokepoints for four decades.
- [[united-states]] — the sovereign republic whose global commerce, defense treaties, and territorial waters the maritime branch protects.
