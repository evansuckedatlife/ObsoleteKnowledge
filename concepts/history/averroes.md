## summary

**Averroes** (Ibn Rushd, 1126–1198) was a *Moorish* philosopher, physician, and theologian from *Al-Andalus* whose synthesis of *Aristotelian* logic with Islamic theology profoundly influenced both Islamic and European *Scholasticism*. Though censured in his lifetime for threatening religious orthodoxy, his commentaries on *Aristotle* preserved Greek philosophy and shaped medieval Christian thought.

## you gotta know

- Born in Córdoba (1126) in *Al-Andalus* (Islamic Spain) during the *Almohad Caliphate*; served as a court physician, qadi (Islamic judge), and chief jurist to Caliph Abu Yaqub Yusuf.
- Wrote comprehensive commentaries (short, middle, and long versions) on *Aristotle's* works, systematically defending Greek philosophy against charges of heresy within Islamic theological circles.
- Defended the compatibility of reason (*aql*) and revelation (*wahy*); argued for rational interpretation of Quranic scripture against literalism, engaging with *Al-Ghazali's* anti-rationalist theology.
- Concept of "double truth": argued that philosophical truth and religious truth could coexist on different levels of understanding—not a true contradiction but different modes of apprehension for different audiences.
- Persecuted late in life (1195) by the Almohad leadership for perceived threats to religious orthodoxy and rationalism; his works were burned in the Islamic world, though he was eventually pardoned shortly before death.
- Transmission via Latin translation (12th–13th centuries): his *Aristotle* commentaries were translated and became authoritative texts in European universities, making him a central (if controversial) authority in *Scholasticism*.
- Medieval Christian Scholastics (esp. at Oxford and Paris) debated "*Averroism*"—whether his ideas on the eternity of the world and "double truth" were compatible with Christian doctrine; the Church condemned Averroist positions in 1277.
- Influence shaped *Thomas Aquinas* and other Christian scholastics; his synthesis of Greek philosophy and monotheistic theology became the template for Christian engagement with Aristotle.
- Philosophical legacy: his defense of reason as compatible with faith influenced both Islamic and Christian intellectual traditions; his commentaries preserved Greek philosophical texts otherwise lost to medieval Europe.
