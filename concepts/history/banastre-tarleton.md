## summary

**Banastre Tarleton**, popularly remembered by the notorious monikers **Bloody Ban** and **The Green Dragoon**, was an aggressive *[[british-empire]]* cavalry officer who gained renown and infamy during the *[[american-revolution]]*. Operating as the dynamic spearhead of the British campaign across the American South, he commanded the loyalist *British Legion* in rapid, devastating pursuit operations against Continental regiments and local militia. His reputation for merciless ruthlessness, forged during the slaughter of surrendering soldiers, made his name a rallying cry of vengeance throughout the nascent *[[united-states]]*. Despite his tactical speed and operational audacity, his military career in North America was permanently eclipsed when his force was routed by *[[daniel-morgan]]* at *Cowpens*.

## you gotta know

- Infamous for the 1780 *Battle of Waxhaws*, where his cavalry sabered Virginia Continentals who were attempting to surrender under a white flag, giving rise to the bitter Patriot battle cry *"Tarleton's quarter"*, an ironic phrase denoting absolute refusal of mercy.
- Suffered a decisive tactical disaster against Brigadier General *[[daniel-morgan]]* at the *Battle of Cowpens* in January 1781, where a brilliant double envelopment routed his headlong cavalry charge, crippled his command, and stripped the British army of its vital light forces.
- Commanded the *British Legion*, a fearsome provincial unit composed of American Loyalists equipped as green-jacketed dragoons and light infantry, which served as the relentless advance guard for General *Charles Cornwallis* across the Carolinas and Virginia.
- Secured major early victories across the southern theater, including surprising and scattering American cavalry detachments at *Monck's Corner* and *Lenud's Ferry*, as well as orchestrating the merciless pursuit of retreating Continentals following the disastrous American defeat at *Camden*.
- Captured Continental Major General *Charles Lee* in December 1776 during a daring cavalry raid on a tavern in Basking Ridge, New Jersey, an audacious stroke that catapulted the twenty-two-year-old officer into instant fame throughout *[[england]]*.
- Surrendered his besieged outpost at Gloucester Point across from Yorktown in October 1781, where American officers harbored such visceral hatred for his past conduct that they notably denied him the traditional dinner invitations and parole courtesies granted to every other captured British officer.
- Returned to *[[england]]* as a decorated war hero and notorious socialite, later serving for over two decades in the British Parliament representing Liverpool, where he became one of the most aggressive legislative defenders of the transatlantic slave trade.

## connections

- [[daniel-morgan]] — the Continental commander whose brilliant defensive deployment at *Cowpens* shattered Tarleton's cavalry and inflicted his defining defeat.
- [[american-revolution]] — the armed conflict in which he established his legacy as Britain's most feared and despised cavalry leader.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power whose southern offensive strategy relied heavily on his lightning raids and mobile counter-insurgency tactics.
- [[george-washington]] — the Continental commander-in-chief whose allied Franco-American operations surrounded and compelled the final capitulation of Tarleton's forces at Yorktown.
- [[united-states]] — the fledgling nation where his alleged wartime atrocities solidified him in patriotic memory as a symbol of British tyranny.
- [[england]] — his native country, where he capitalized on military renown to secure high rank and serve as a long-term Member of Parliament.
