## summary

**English feudalism**, often characterized in its classical institutional form as **Anglo-Norman feudalism**, was the hierarchical socioeconomic and military structure introduced into [[england]] following the Norman Conquest of 1066. Under this regime, the sovereign claimed radical, ultimate ownership of all terrestrial territory, granting land as conditional tenures to tenant-in-chief barons in exchange for sworn fealty, military service, and financial dues. This systematic reorganization replaced decentralized Anglo-Saxon arrangements with a centralized monarchical pyramid that permanently reshaped English law, administration, and warfare throughout the medieval era.

## you gotta know

- Established systematically by *William the Conqueror* following his decisive victory at the Battle of Hastings, dispossessing native Anglo-Saxon thegns and distributing confiscated estates to Norman knights.
- Documented comprehensively across the kingdom in the 1086 *Domesday Book*, a monumental survey recording landholdings, taxable values, and feudal resource obligations.
- Required every major landholder in the realm to swear direct personal loyalty to the monarch at the Oath of Salisbury, ensuring allegiance to the crown superseded duties owed to immediate lords.
- Functioned through a network of tenure categories, including knight-service (*servitium debitum*), grand serjeanty, frankalmoign for church endowments, and agricultural socage.
- Relied upon manorialism at the rural economic foundation, compelling unfree serfs (*villeins*) to perform compulsory agricultural labor on the lord's demesne in return for strip-farming rights and manorial court protection.
- Evolved into a fiscalized system through the payment of *scutage* (shield money), which allowed feudal barons to commute direct military service into cash payments used by kings to hire professional mercenary companies.
- Sparked repeated baronial rebellions against extortionate feudal reliefs, wardships, and arbitrary exactions, directly culminating in the sealing of Magna Carta in 1215 and the gradual emergence of parliament.
- Abolished formally as a system of military tenures by the *Tenures Abolition Act 1660* following the English Civil War, transforming feudal landholdings into free and common socage.

## connections

- [[england]] — the realm whose legal, administrative, and agrarian foundations were permanently altered by the Norman tenurial settlement.
- [[france]] — the continental kingdom from which the Norman nobility imported advanced tenurial customs, cavalry tactics, and legal practices.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial global polity whose common law traditions of property rights, tenancy, and crown domain traced back to Norman manorial systems.
- [[colonialism]] — the overseas expansionist enterprise that frequently exported modified European tenurial titles and land-grant charters to conquered territories.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the dramatist whose English history plays chronicled the civil turbulence and baronial strife arising from fractured feudal oaths.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-war American period that debated land tenure and agrarian servitude comparable to historic tenant bondage.
