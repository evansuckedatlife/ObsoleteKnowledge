## summary

**Appomattox Court House** (often spelled **Appomattox Courthouse**) is a historic village in central *Virginia* where Confederate General *Robert E. Lee* surrendered the *Army of Northern Virginia* to Union General *Ulysses S. Grant* on April 9, 1865. The meeting inside the parlor of the *McLean House* effectively ended the [[american-civil-war]], triggering the collapse of the [[confederate-states-of-america]] and the preservation of the [[united-states]]. It remains one of the most iconic sites in American history, symbolizing national reunification and the beginning of the [[reconstruction-era]].

## you gotta know

- Site of the historic Palm Sunday conference on April 9, 1865, where General *Robert E. Lee* met Lieutenant General *Ulysses S. Grant* to surrender the Confederacy's principal military force.
- The formal terms of capitulation were drafted and signed inside the parlor of the *McLean House*, a private brick home owned by wholesale merchant *Wilmer McLean*.
- Homeowner *Wilmer McLean* had previously lived near Manassas, where the opening major clash of the war was fought across his property in 1861, prompting the legendary observation that the conflict began in his front yard and ended in his front parlor.
- *Ulysses S. Grant* offered remarkably generous terms, paroling Confederate soldiers rather than taking them prisoner, allowing officers to keep their sidearms, permitting cavalrymen and artillerymen to take home their horses and mules for spring farming, and ordering 25,000 rations sent to Lee's famished ranks.
- Preceded hours earlier by the *Battle of Appomattox Court House*, an engagement where Major General *John B. Gordon* attempted a final dawn breakthrough against Union cavalry under Major General *Philip Sheridan*, only to withdraw upon encountering two full Union infantry corps deployed across his front.
- The formal infantry surrender ceremony occurred on April 12, 1865, where Union Brigadier General *Joshua Chamberlain* famously called his troops to attention at "carry arms" as a salute of honor to their vanquished adversaries, a gesture returned by *John B. Gordon*.
- The village and surrounding battlegrounds are preserved today within *Appomattox Court House National Historical Park*, featuring the reconstructed courthouse and *McLean House*, which was originally dismantled in 1893 for an exhibition that never materialized.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the four-year national conflict brought to an effective military conclusion by the surrender at the village.
- [[ulysses-s-grant]] — the Union general-in-chief who accepted the surrender and drafted magnanimous terms in the *McLean House*.
- [[confederate-states-of-america]] — the secessionist government whose military collapse was sealed by the capitulation of Lee's army.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the transformative postwar era of national reunion and political rebuilding inaugurated after the surrender.
- [[battle-of-gettysburg]] — the turning-point engagement that broke Lee's northern offensive and set the stage for the final retreat to Appomattox.
- [[virginia]] — the state that hosted the courthouse village and served as the central theater of the conflict's eastern campaigns.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic preserved and fundamentally remade through the final surrender concluded at the site.
