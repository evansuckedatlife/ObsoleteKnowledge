## summary

The **Seventh Cavalry** (formally the **7th Cavalry Regiment**) is a historic and controversial unit of the [[united-states]] Army, established in 1866 during the [[reconstruction-era]]. Operating extensively across the Great Plains during the American Indian Wars, the regiment spearheaded federal military efforts to subjugate indigenous nations and enforce their confinement to reservations. Under the flamboyant leadership of Lieutenant Colonel *George Armstrong Custer*, the unit achieved enduring notoriety through its catastrophic defeat at the 1876 Battle of the [[little-bighorn]] and its later role in the horrific 1890 [[wounded-knee-massacre]].

## you gotta know

- Suffered a crushing defeat at the 1876 Battle of the [[little-bighorn]], where Lieutenant Colonel *George Armstrong Custer* and five companies under his immediate command were completely annihilated by a coalition of Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho warriors.
- Perpetrated the 1890 [[wounded-knee-massacre]] under the command of Colonel *James W. Forsyth*, during which soldiers deployed rapid-firing Hotchkiss guns against an encamped group of Miniconjou Lakota under Chief *Spotted Elk*, killing nearly three hundred Native Americans.
- Led by *George Armstrong Custer* during the 1868 Battle of Washita River, launching a dawn surprise attack that destroyed the winter encampment of peaceful Southern Cheyenne Chief *Black Kettle*.
- Fractured into three separate battalions during the Little Bighorn engagement, leaving surviving companies under Major *Marcus Reno* and Captain *Frederick Benteen* pinned down on Reno Hill until relief forces arrived.
- Organized at Fort Riley, Kansas, in 1866 following the [[american-civil-war]] under the command of Colonel *Andrew J. Smith*, adopting the spirited Irish drinking tune *"Garryowen"* as its enduring regimental song.
- Counted among its most famous figures *Comanche*, a horse belonging to Captain *Myles Keogh*, who survived severe wounds at Little Bighorn and became an honored ceremonial symbol of the regiment.
- Transformed into an air cavalry formation during the twentieth century, famously fighting at the 1965 Battle of Ia Drang in the Vietnam War under Lieutenant Colonel *Hal Moore*, an engagement chronicled in the book *We Were Soldiers Once... and Young*.

## connections

- [[little-bighorn]] — decisive 1876 engagement along the Greasy Grass where *George Armstrong Custer* and five companies were annihilated.
- [[wounded-knee-massacre]] — devastating 1890 assault by the regiment that resulted in the slaughter of Lakota Ghost Dancers in South Dakota.
- [[sitting-bull]] — revered Hunkpapa Lakota leader whose spiritual vision and tactical alliance inspired the crushing defeat of the regiment.
- [[american-civil-war]] — major conflict that concluded immediately prior to the 1866 reorganization of the army that established the regiment.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — postwar period during which the regiment was initially created, garrisoned, and assigned to frontier patrol duties.
- [[united-states]] — expanding nation whose federal government deployed the regiment to enforce territorial control and reservation boundaries.
- [[colonialism]] — overarching imperial policy of dispossession, forced assimilation, and territorial expansion executed by frontier cavalry forces.
