## summary

**False Dmitri** refers to a succession of impostors who laid claim to the Russian throne during the chaotic dynastic interregnum known as the *Time of Troubles*. Each pretender claimed to be the Tsarevich *Dmitri Ivanovich*, the youngest son of Tsar *Ivan the Terrible*, who had mysteriously perished from a throat wound in Uglich in 1591. The first and only successful claimant, known historically as **False Dmitry I** or **Pseudo-Demetrius**, ruled as Tsar of Russia from June 1605 until his violent assassination in May 1606.

## you gotta know

- Claimed to have escaped an assassination plot orchestrated by Regent *Boris Godunov* in 1591, alleging that another young boy had been murdered in his place while loyal guardians spirited him away.
- Identified by official Muscovite authorities and historians as *Grigory Otrepyev*, a runaway defrocked monk from the Chudov Monastery who fled across the border into the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
- Gained crucial military and diplomatic backing from Polish magnates such as *Jerzy Mniszech* after secretly converting to Roman Catholicism and pledging territorial concessions along with promises to reunite the Orthodox Church with *Rome*.
- Marched on Moscow in 1604 with an army of Polish mercenaries, Zaporozhian Cossacks, and disaffected boyars, entering the capital triumphantly following the sudden death of Tsar *Boris Godunov* and the murder of his young son *Feodor II*.
- Crowned Tsar of Russia in the Cathedral of the Dormition in July 1605, where his biological mother, *Maria Nagaya*, was pressured out of a convent to publicly acknowledge him as her surviving son.
- Alienated traditional Russian boyars and conservative clergy by displaying foreign Polish manners, refusing to sleep after the midday meal, neglecting Orthodox purification rituals, and marrying the Catholic noblewoman *Marina Mniszech*.
- Deposed and murdered after less than a year on the throne during a boyar rebellion led by Prince *Vasili Shuisky*, after which his corpse was publicly displayed, burned to ashes, mixed with gunpowder, and fired from the Tsar Cannon toward *Poland*.
- Succeeded by further pretenders, notably *False Dmitri II*, known as the *Thief of Tushino*, who maintained a rival rebel court outside Moscow until his murder in 1610.

## connections

- [[ivan-iv]] — the formidable Muscovite ruler whose deceased youngest son provided the royal identity usurped by the pretenders.
- [[russian-empire]] — the realm thrown into systemic crisis and external invasion before consolidating autocratic rule under the Romanov dynasty.
- [[time-of-troubles]] — the catastrophic interregnum of famine, rebellion, and foreign occupation triggered by the extinction of the Rurikid dynasty.
- [[mikhail-romanov]] — the young nobleman whose election to the throne in 1613 finally brought an end to the succession crises and foreign incursions.
- [[rome]] — the papal seat with which the first pretender established secret communion while seeking geopolitical sponsorship in Central Europe.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the contemporary playwright whose Elizabethan dramas mirrored the themes of regicide, dynastic imposture, and legitimate kingship seen in Muscovy.
