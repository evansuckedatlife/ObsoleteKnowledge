## summary

The **Labor Movement** is the collective efforts of workers to organize, strike, and advocate for better wages, working conditions, and rights in response to industrial capitalism's exploitation. Emerging in the 19th century during the *Industrial Revolution*, the movement grew from factory workers' resistance into political parties, trade unions, and a global force shaping modern labor law. Marxism and socialism provided ideological frameworks, while pragmatic unionists simply fought for shorter hours, safety, and pay.

## you gotta know

- Arose directly from the *Industrial Revolution*, as factory work concentrated workers and exposed them to harsh conditions, low pay, and child labor.
- Early movements were often repressed violently (e.g., *Peterloo Massacre*, strikes broken by police and militias), but workers persisted in organizing.
- Trade unions—organizations representing workers in specific trades or industries—became the primary vehicles for collective bargaining and negotiation with employers.
- Achieved major victories: the eight-hour workday, workplace safety regulations, child labor restrictions, and the right to organize (in most developed nations).
- Connected to socialist and Marxist ideology in some places (especially Europe); in the U.S., labor movements were often more pragmatic and nationalist than revolutionary.
- Remains vital today: unions represent workers' interests, though union membership has declined in recent decades in developed nations.
- Major American unions like the *AFL-CIO* leveraged membership numbers to win political representation and legislative victories improving working conditions across industries.
- Labor organizing often met state and corporate repression, yet iconic figures and movements—from the *Haymarket affair* to the *sit-down strikes* of the 1930s—built pressure for worker rights.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — the economic transformation that generated mass factory labor and provoked worker resistance.
- [[capitalism]] — the economic system against which workers organized, seeking to limit its exploitation.
- [[progressive-era]] — the American reform movement in which labor causes gained political allies and legislative victories.
- [[marxism]] — the ideology that provided theoretical justification for worker resistance and revolution.
- [[socialism]] — related ideology advocating collective ownership and equitable distribution, supported by many labor movements.
- [[working-class]] — the social class from which the labor movement emerged and which it represented.
