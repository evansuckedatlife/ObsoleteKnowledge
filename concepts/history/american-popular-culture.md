## summary

**American popular culture** encompasses the shared entertainment, values, and tastes of ordinary Americans, shaped by mass media and commercial forces. From *silent film* and *jazz* in the 1920s through radio, television, and now digital media, popular culture has defined American identity, aspiration, and social norms. It has democratized cultural experience, making entertainment and lifestyle accessible to the masses while reflecting and reinforcing American assumptions about success, gender, race, and consumption.

## you gotta know

- *Jazz*, born from African-American communities in New Orleans, became synonymous with the 1920s and defined an era's rebellious spirit and modernity.
- *Silent film* (1890s–1920s) created a global entertainment medium, with American studios (Hollywood) dominating production and export.
- *Readers' Digest* (founded 1922) and *The Saturday Evening Post* became mass-circulation magazines defining middle-class American taste and values.
- Radio emerged in the 1920s–1930s as the dominant mass medium, carrying news, entertainment, and advertising directly into American homes.
- Television, introduced widely in the 1950s, became the primary source of entertainment and information, with shows reflecting and shaping social assumptions.
- Hollywood studios developed the "star system," making actors into idols and driving consumer culture through fandom and celebrity.
- Advertising became a dominant force, using psychology and mass media to create desire and define the "good life" through consumption.
