## summary

The **Epic of Gilgamesh** is humanity's oldest known work of literature, composed in ancient Sumeria around the 2100s BCE and preserved in Akkadian. It recounts the adventures of *Gilgamesh*, a king of Uruk, and his companion *Enkidu*, exploring themes of friendship, mortality, and the human search for immortality.

## you gotta know

- Composed in cuneiform on clay tablets, originally in Sumerian, later transcribed into Akkadian, and preserved across multiple copies and versions spanning centuries.
- The narrative was initially orally transmitted before being written down, reflecting the epic traditions of Mesopotamian storytelling and theology.
- Centers on *Gilgamesh*, the tyrannical king of Uruk (a historical figure who ruled around 2750 BCE), who becomes humanized through his friendship with the wild man *Enkidu*.
- Chronicles *Gilgamesh*'s journey with *Enkidu* through adventures including the defeat of the monster *Humbaba* and the Bull of Heaven, sent by the goddess *Ishtar*.
- Follows *Enkidu*'s death, which traumatizes *Gilgamesh* and drives him to seek a path to eternal life and wisdom from *Utnapishtim*, the immortal survivor of the flood.
- Features the *Flood* narrative, an earlier version of similar stories found in later Mesopotamian and biblical texts, suggesting a shared cultural memory of catastrophic flooding.
- The text explores fundamental human concerns: mortality, friendship, the futility of seeking immortality, and the dignity of accepting one's fate as a human.
- Concludes with *Gilgamesh*'s acceptance of human mortality and his return to rule Uruk with new perspective and wisdom, the true treasure he gained from his journey.
- Preserved in the *Ashurbanipal* library at Nineveh (dating to the 7th century BCE), allowing its survival to the modern era and making it accessible to scholars through the collected royal archives.
- The eleven-tablet Standard Babylonian version, discovered in Nineveh in 1872, became the primary source for understanding this foundational work of world literature.
