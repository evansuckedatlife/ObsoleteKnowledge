## summary

The **Domesday Book** is a comprehensive manuscript record of the great survey of England and parts of Wales completed in 1086 by order of King *William the Conqueror*. Commissioned twenty years after the Norman Conquest, the survey systematically documented the ownership, extent, value, and taxable resources of landholdings across the realm. It represents an unprecedented administrative achievement in medieval European history and provides an invaluable demographic portrait of eleventh-century England.

## you gotta know

- Commissioned at Christmas in 1085 at Gloucester by King *William the Conqueror* to assess taxable wealth, determine feudal obligations, and solidify Norman control.
- Named popular by Anglo-Saxon subjects after the biblical *Day of Doom* (*Doomsday*) because its assessments and royal judgments were unappealable and final.
- Comprises two unequal volumes: *Great Domesday*, covering most of England in a single condensed hand, and *Little Domesday*, covering Essex, Norfolk, and Suffolk with greater, unsummarized detail.
- Gathered administrative data through royal commissioners who convened inquests in county shires and sworn regional juries of both Normans and native English jurors.
- Omitted certain northern regions such as Durham, Northumberland, and portions of the northwest, as well as the major autonomous cities of *London* and *Winchester*.
- Recorded agricultural livestock, mill yields, woodland coverage, fisheries, and social hierarchies ranging from great tenants-in-chief down to *villeins*, *cottars*, and enslaved laborers.
- Demonstrated that within two decades of the *Battle of Hastings*, the Anglo-Saxon ruling aristocracy had been almost completely dispossessed in favor of Norman barons.
- Preserved for centuries at the royal treasury in *Winchester* (hence its Latin title *Liber de Wintonia*) and now housed in the *National Archives* at Kew.
