## summary

The **Florida Cession**, achieved through the diplomatic ratification of the 1819 **Adams-Onís Treaty** (also formally designated the **Transcontinental Treaty**), was a pivotal territorial acquisition in which the Kingdom of [[spain]] ceded East Florida and West Florida to the [[united-states]]. Negotiated between American Secretary of State *John Quincy Adams* and Spanish envoy *Luis de Onís*, the settlement resolved protracted boundary quarrels dating back to the Louisiana Purchase and calmed regional crises exacerbated by the First Seminole War. In addition to securing the Florida peninsula, the treaty drew a definitive transcontinental boundary line extending all the way to the Pacific Ocean while releasing Spain from millions of dollars in private maritime and legal claims.

## you gotta know

- Negotiated primarily by Secretary of State *John Quincy Adams* and Spanish diplomat *Luis de Onís* under the administration of President *James Monroe*.
- Catalyzed by General *Andrew Jackson*'s aggressive and unauthorized 1818 invasion of Spanish Florida during the First Seminole War, where he seized Pensacola, ousted the Spanish governor, and executed two British subjects.
- Resolved contentious geographic ambiguities arising from the 1803 Louisiana Purchase, during which American officials had disputed whether West Florida had been included in the French sale.
- Established a definite border dividing Spanish colonial holdings from American territory along the Sabine, Red, and Arkansas Rivers, ascending northward to the 42nd parallel and terminating at the Pacific Ocean.
- Required the United States to relinquish its claims to Spanish Texas west of the Sabine River, an agreement that outraged southern expansionists and future proponents of Manifest Destiny.
- Obligated the United States government to assume five million dollars in debts and claims filed by American citizens against Spain rather than paying direct monetary compensation to the Spanish crown.
- Suffered substantial ratification delays until 1821, as Spain delayed formal approval while unsuccessfully attempting to prevent American diplomatic recognition of rebellious colonies across Latin America.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — growing republic that acquired peninsular Florida and established legal claims to the Pacific Northwest.
- [[spain]] — European global empire that yielded its northern American possessions in the face of domestic instability and colonial revolts.
- [[colonialism]] — imperial system of overseas territorial rivalry and boundary negotiations that governed early North American expansion.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — president whose 1803 purchase of Louisiana triggered decades of territorial friction over the borders of West Florida.
- [[george-washington]] — founding president whose early treaties with Spain first defined the disputed border along the 31st parallel.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — historical era during which Spanish explorers such as *Juan Ponce de León* first claimed the Florida peninsula for Spain.
