## summary

The **Indigenous Peoples of the Americas** were the diverse civilizations and societies inhabiting North and South America before European contact, numbering in the tens of millions. These peoples had developed sophisticated cultures, technologies, and political systems ranging from vast empires like the [[Aztec]] and [[Inca]] to smaller nations and tribes. European colonization initiated by figures like [[Christopher Columbus]] devastated these populations through disease, warfare, and subjugation, fundamentally transforming the hemisphere.

## you gotta know

- Inhabited the Americas for at least 15,000 years (some evidence suggests 20,000+), having migrated from Asia across the Bering Strait during the last ice age; they developed the hemisphere independently of Old World influence.
- Developed independent civilizations: the Aztec Empire in Mesoamerica, the Inca Empire spanning the length of South America, and hundreds of distinct nations north of [[Mexico]] ranging from the Iroquois Confederacy to the Mississippian culture.
- Possessed advanced knowledge in agriculture (domesticated maize, potatoes, chocolate, tomatoes), astronomy (Mayan calendars), architecture (pyramid temples, stone fortifications), and engineering (suspension bridges, canal systems, agricultural terracing).
- European contact initiated by *Christopher Columbus* in 1492 led to catastrophic demographic collapse, primarily through disease (smallpox, measles, influenza) but also through warfare, forced labor, and famine from disrupted food systems.
- Population estimates at contact ranged from 50 to 100 million; indigenous populations declined by 90% within the first century of colonization—one of history's deadliest catastrophes, mostly from disease unintentionally spread by Europeans.
- Colonial powers imposed their religions, languages, legal systems, and labor hierarchies (encomienda, plantation slavery), destroying traditional cultures, political structures, and knowledge systems; forced conversions and suppression of indigenous religions lasted centuries.
- Survived and persisted despite genocidal policies: modern indigenous nations (over 1,000 recognized groups) continue to claim territorial rights, preserve languages and traditions, and assert political autonomy; indigenous peoples constitute over 600 million people globally today.
