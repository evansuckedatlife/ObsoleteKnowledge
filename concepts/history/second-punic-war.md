## summary

The **Second Punic War** (218–201 BCE) was the titanic struggle between Rome and Carthage for dominion over the Mediterranean. [[Hannibal]], Carthage's brilliant general, invaded Italy with elephants and nearly destroyed Rome through a series of crushing victories, most devastatingly at [[Cannae]]. Yet Rome's resilience and [[Scipio Africanus]]'s strategic genius ultimately prevailed, decisively defeating Hannibal at [[Zama]] and establishing Rome as the Mediterranean's supreme power.

## you gotta know

- Began when [[Hannibal]] crossed the Alps with a force of about 26,000 soldiers and African war elephants to bring the war to Italy itself, shocking Rome.
- Hannibal won a series of stunning victories: at the Trebia River (218 BCE), Lake Trasimene (217 BCE), and most infamously at [[Cannae]] (216 BCE).
- At Cannae, Hannibal's tactical double envelopment perfectly annihilated a Roman army of 80,000—one of the worst defeats in Roman history—yet Rome refused to surrender.
- Rome responded by abandoning pitched battles, adopting a strategy of attrition and harassment under [[Fabius Maximus]], earning him the epithet "the Delayer" for his cautious strategy.
- The war devastated Italy for years as Hannibal marauded the countryside, but Rome controlled the seas and maintained supply lines and reinforcements from home.
- [[Scipio Africanus]] emerged as Rome's answer to Hannibal: a brilliant young general who trained new armies and invaded Carthage's North African homeland to draw Hannibal back.
- Scipio defeated Hannibal decisively at the [[Battle of Zama]] (202 BCE), forcing Carthage to surrender, pay a massive indemnity, and surrender its Mediterranean territories.
- The war ended Carthaginian power and left Rome the undisputed dominant power over the Mediterranean for the next two centuries, reshaping the ancient world's balance.
