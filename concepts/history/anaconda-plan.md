## summary

The **Anaconda Plan**, officially dubbed the *Scott Plan* and satirized as **Scott's Great Snake**, was the Union grand strategic outline devised by General-in-Chief *Winfield Scott* in early 1861 at the outbreak of the [[american-civil-war]]. Conceived to suppress the Confederacy with minimum bloodshed, it proposed a relentless economic stranglehold comprising a total naval blockade of Southern ports alongside an amphibious advance down the Mississippi River. Although initially mocked by politicians and journalists clamoring for an immediate march on Richmond, Scott's strategic principles correctly anticipated a prolonged struggle and became the guiding operational architecture of the [[united-states]] victory.

## you gotta know

- Proposed in early 1861 by veteran general-in-chief *Winfield Scott*, the aging hero known as *Old Fuss and Feathers*, who recognized that raw volunteers could not win a quick war.
- Called for a comprehensive naval blockade of over 3,500 miles of coastline stretching from Virginia to the Gulf of Mexico, cutting off cotton exports to *England* and *France* while denying military imports.
- Projected a combined army-navy thrust down the Mississippi River corridor to capture strategic strongholds and sever the Trans-Mississippi Confederacy.
- Severed food supplies, beef drives from Texas, and European munitions smuggled across the Rio Grande from reaching the eastern battlefields.
- Ridiculed relentlessly by Northern newspapers as passive and sluggish, inspiring political cartoonist *J.B. Elliott* to publish the famous caricature of a giant snake coiling around the South.
- Clashed with the impatient popular cry of *On to Richmond!*, which pushed President *Abraham Lincoln* into authorizing the disastrous early offensive at First Bull Run.
- Required immense industrial mobilization under Secretary of the Navy *Gideon Welles*, who built ironclad squadrons and converted merchant vessels to patrol shallow coastal inlets and suppress blockade runners.
- Achieved decisive strategic fruition in July 1863 when joint river operations culminated in *Ulysses S. Grant*'s capture of Vicksburg, permanently opening the Mississippi to the Union.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict for which *Winfield Scott* formulated the overarching strategic encirclement plan.
- [[united-states]] — the federal government whose naval and logistical mobilization brought the strategy into reality.
- [[ulysses-s-grant]] — Union commander whose Western campaigns systematically executed the Mississippi River component of the plan.
- [[confederate-army]] — the secessionist military forces gradually starved of vital ordnance, shoes, clothing, and rations by the blockade.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the Northern manufacturing supremacy that enabled the rapid construction of steam gunboats, ironclads, and naval ordnance.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-war era tasked with reintegrating a South devastated by four years of economic isolation and riverine ruin.
