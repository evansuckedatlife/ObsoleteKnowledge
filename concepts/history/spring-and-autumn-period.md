## summary

The **Spring and Autumn Period** was the transformative opening era of the Eastern [[zhou-dynasty]] in ancient [[china]], lasting from 770 to 476 BCE. Named after the court chronicle of the state of *Lu*, the era witnessed the collapse of royal Zhou authority and the decentralization of power into competing aristocratic fiefdoms governed by shifting hegemons. This dynamic fragmentation catalyzed intense diplomatic rivalry, chivalric chariot warfare, and the intellectual flourishing of the Hundred Schools of Thought, establishing the enduring cultural and philosophical foundations of traditional Chinese civilization.

## you gotta know

- Named after the *Spring and Autumn Annals*, an official historical chronicle of the state of *Lu* traditionally attributed to the editorial compilation of [[confucius]], which recorded court diplomacy, solar eclipses, and interstate warfare with terse moral judgment.
- Began when nomadic Rong invaders sacked the western capital of *Haojing* and forced King *Ping* to relocate east to *Chengzhou* in 770 BCE, reducing the royal court of the [[zhou-dynasty]] to ceremonial figureheads holding the [[mandate-of-heaven]] while regional lords (*zhuhou*) seized actual political power.
- Established the *Ba* or Hegemon system, first claimed by Duke *Huan* of *Qi* and his influential statesman *Guan Zhong*, who convened multi-state alliances under the diplomatic slogan of "respecting the king and repelling the barbarians" (*zunwang rangyi*) to preserve order across the Central Plains.
- Fought primarily through aristocratic chariot warfare between noble champions who observed strict ritual etiquette (*li*), prioritizing face-saving gestures, formalized challenges, and captive ransoms over the mass slaughter and territorial annexation of subsequent eras.
- Catalyzed the intellectual flowering known as the Hundred Schools of Thought as wandering scholars sought patrons among rival courts, notably [[confucius]], who advocated for a return to early Zhou moral rectitude, and *Sun Tzu*, who codified strategic theory in *The Art of War*.
- Witnessed fierce peripheral conflicts along the Yangtze River valley between the southern rivals *Wu* and *Yue*, culminating in King *Goujian* of Yue enduring humiliation and tasting gall to steel his resolve before utterly conquering King *Fuchai* of Wu.
- Concluded following the Battle of Jinyang and the eventual partition of the preeminent northern state of *Jin* among the ministerial houses of *Zhao*, *Wei*, and *Han* in 453 BCE, formally ushering in the total warfare and ruthless conquest of the [[warring-states-period]].

## connections

- [[confucius]] — the wandering moral philosopher whose traditional compilation of *Lu*'s historical chronicle provided the era with its name.
- [[zhou-dynasty]] — the ruling house whose political authority collapsed following nomadic sack and the eastward flight of King *Ping*.
- [[warring-states-period]] — the subsequent era of total mobilization, iron weapons, and mass conscription that replaced aristocratic chariot diplomacy.
- [[mandate-of-heaven]] — the divine legitimacy claimed by nominal Zhou rulers to command spiritual respect even as regional warlords ruled unchecked.
- [[qin-shi-huang]] — the legalist monarch who ultimately unified the warring regional fiefdoms and abolished the feudal aristocracies forged in this era.
- [[china]] — the civilization whose classical political philosophy, diplomatic conventions, and core cultural identity emerged from these multi-state conflicts.
- [[silk-road]] — the nascent commercial corridors that began expanding as regional states pioneered cast-iron metallurgy, minted bronze spade coinage, and traded silk.
