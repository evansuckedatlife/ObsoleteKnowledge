## summary

**Thomas Edison** (in full **Thomas Alva Edison**) was an American inventor, entrepreneur, and industrialist whose laboratory facilities established the archetype of modern corporate research during the *[[industrial-revolution]]*. Known as the "Wizard of Menlo Park," he held 1,093 United States patents covering revolutionary technologies including the incandescent electric light bulb, the phonograph, and the Kinetoscope. Edison's advocacy of direct current power distribution and his aggressive commercialization of motion pictures shaped the electrical grid and the emergence of global *[[silent-film]]*.

## you gotta know

- Established the Menlo Park laboratory complex in New Jersey in 1876, widely considered the first organized industrial research institution created to systematically churn out commercial innovations.
- Invented the tin-foil *phonograph* in 1877 by mechanically recording and playing back the nursery rhyme "Mary Had a Little Lamb," making sound recording a practical reality.
- Perfected a commercially viable incandescent light bulb in 1879 using a carbonized bamboo filament, developing alongside it the generators, cables, meters, and sockets needed for practical electric illumination.
- Built the Pearl Street Station in lower *[[new-york-city]]* in 1882, providing the world's first centralized investor-owned commercial electrical utility supplying low-voltage direct current.
- Waged the fierce "War of the Currents" during the late 1880s against *George Westinghouse* and *Nikola Tesla*, mounting sensational demonstrations and backing the alternating-current electric chair to disparage competing AC systems.
- Developed the *Kinetoscope* motion-picture viewer and *Kinetograph* camera with collaborator *William Kennedy Laurie Dickson*, filming early stage acts within the tar-papered, rotatable "Black Maria" studio.
- Formed the Motion Picture Patents Company in 1908 to enforce monopolistic licensing on cameras, projectors, and raw film stock, a trust whose aggressive East Coast lawsuits drove independent filmmakers west to southern California.
- Observed the Edison effect (thermionic emission) in 1883 inside evacuated light bulbs, an accidental finding that later enabled the invention of the vacuum tube and fundamentally advanced the understanding of the *[[electron]]*.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — institutionalized organized technological research and spearheaded mass urban electrification across the globe.
- [[silent-film]] — designed the Kinetoscope and Black Maria studio, establishing the technological foundation for commercial cinema.
- [[new-york-city]] — illuminated the Wall Street financial district with direct current through the landmark 1882 Pearl Street Station.
- [[united-states]] — epitomized Gilded Age industrial capitalism, technological ingenuity, and aggressive patent litigation.
- [[tecumseh]] — Shawnee leader whose Great Lakes frontier war zones encompassed the borderland territory where Edison's family later lived and rebelled.
- [[electron]] — documented thermionic emission of electrons across a vacuum filament gap, presaging modern electronic components.
- [[culture]] — fundamentally altered modern night life, domestic entertainment, and communication through recorded sound and commercial electric light.
