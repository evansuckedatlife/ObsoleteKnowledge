## summary

**George Armstrong Custer** was a [[united-states]] cavalry officer whose military career spanned the [[american-civil-war]] and the Plains [[indian-wars]]. Graduating last in his West Point class on the eve of rebellion, he achieved nationwide celebrity as an aggressive Union "Boy General" renowned for reckless mounted charges. He is most famous for the disastrous 1876 Battle of the [[little-bighorn]], where his overconfidence and tactical miscalculations led to the total annihilation of his immediate command by allied [[lakota-sioux]] and [[cheyenne]] warriors in what became immortalized as "Custer's Last Stand."

## you gotta know

- Annihilated alongside his entire immediate command of roughly two hundred and ten men on June 25, 1876, at the Battle of the [[little-bighorn]] after launching a premature surprise attack against a massive allied encampment of [[lakota-sioux]], [[cheyenne]], and *Arapaho* warriors.
- Achieved meteoric national fame during the [[american-civil-war]] when he was commissioned a brigadier general of volunteers at age twenty-three, earning the moniker "Boy General" and leading the *Michigan Cavalry Brigade* in daring charges at *Gettysburg* and *Yellow Tavern*.
- Led the controversial 1874 expedition into the sacred *Black Hills* of *Dakota Territory*, confirming the presence of gold and intentionally precipitating the *Black Hills Gold Rush*, an encroachment that violated the 1868 *Treaty of Fort Laramie* and sparked the Great Sioux War.
- Commanded the 7th Cavalry in the November 1868 dawn attack at the *Battle of Washita River*, destroying Chief *Black Kettle's* peaceful Southern [[cheyenne]] camp, killing noncombatants and ponies, and drawing intense internal military criticism for abandoning a detachment under Major *Joel Elliott*.
- Graduated at the very bottom of the thirty-four-member West Point class of June 1861, claiming the dubious distinction of class "goat" after amassing a staggering record of disciplinary demerits that brought him to the verge of court-martial.
- Served aggressively under General *Philip Sheridan* in the final Appomattox campaign, cutting off Confederate supply trains, receiving the white dishtowel carried as a flag of truce, and attending the historic surrender at Appomattox Court House.
- Transformed into an enduring American folk hero primarily through the tireless decades-long campaign of his widow, *Elizabeth Bacon Custer*, whose widely read books sanitized his vanity and tactical blunders into a legend of selfless martyrdom.

## connections

- [[little-bighorn]] — climactic Montana battle where his five cavalry companies were encircled and destroyed.
- [[sitting-bull]] — spiritual leader whose sun dance vision predicted soldiers falling into camp like grasshoppers.
- [[crazy-horse]] — prominent Oglala war chief who led the decisive countercharge up Custer Hill.
- [[lakota-sioux]] — northern Plains nation defending their homelands against his 7th Cavalry assault.
- [[cheyenne]] — indigenous nation whose settlements Custer attacked along the Washita and Little Bighorn rivers.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict where his fearless mounted charges made him one of the Union's celebrated heroes.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — era in which he policed occupied southern territories before redeploying to western frontier garrisons.
- [[colonialism]] — federal policy of military dispossession, treaty violation, and forced assimilation that shaped his frontier campaigns.
