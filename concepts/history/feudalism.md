## summary

**Feudalism** was a decentralized political and economic system that dominated medieval Europe and feudal Japan. Built on reciprocal oaths between lords and vassals, it organized society into hierarchies bound by personal loyalty and the conditional exchange of protection for service. Feudalism emerged from the collapse of centralized authority (especially the Roman Empire in the West) and persisted in modified forms until industrialization.

## you gotta know

- Organized around the *fief*—a grant of land (and the peasants who worked it) given by a lord to a vassal in exchange for military service and loyalty.
- The *feudal contract* bound lord and vassal by oaths: the lord promised protection; the vassal promised military service, counsel, and often financial payments.
- Peasants or *serfs* were bound to the land (*serfdom*) and owed labor obligations to the local lord, creating a three-tier hierarchy: lords, vassals, and serfs.
- Feudalism fragmented political authority; there was no single sovereign but rather overlapping networks of local power, each lord wielding near-complete authority within his domain.
- *Magna Carta* (1215) partly represented a feudal contract dispute—English barons asserting limits on the king's power, though it later became a symbol of universal rights.
- Feudalism in Japan (*samurai* and the *daimyo* system) operated on similar principles during the *Edo period*, though Japanese feudalism had distinct organizational features.
- The system began eroding with the rise of nation-states, professional armies, and centralized finance in the 16th–18th centuries, fully superseded by industrial capitalism.

## connections

- [[magna-carta]] — a feudal contract document that became the foundation myth for democratic constitutionalism.
- [[england]] — the primary feudal kingdom in Western Europe and source of legal innovations like Magna Carta.
- [[france]] — another feudal kingdom where local lords wielded fragmented power until centralization under the monarchy.
- [[edo-period]] — the Japanese period during which feudal structures (slightly adapted) persisted longer than in the West.
- [[frankish-empire]] — the early medieval kingdom where feudal relationships first crystallized in Europe.
- [[roman-empire]] — the centralized state whose collapse triggered the decentralization that feudalism represented.
