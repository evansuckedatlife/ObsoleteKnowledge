## summary

**William McKinley's Inauguration** on March 4, 1897, marked the commencement of the twenty-fifth presidency of the [[united-states]], taking place at the East Front of the United States Capitol in Washington, D.C. Administered by Chief Justice *Melville Fuller*, the event achieved lasting cultural fame as the earliest presidential swearing-in captured by a motion picture camera, heralding modern political media coverage. McKinley's inaugural address articulated a robust platform of economic nationalism, championing currency stability under the gold standard and high protective tariffs following the severe depression triggered by the Panic of 1893. This pivotal ceremony inaugurated the Fourth Party System and established an era of prolonged Republican electoral hegemony.

## you gotta know

- Celebrated as the first American presidential inauguration captured on motion picture film, documented by camera crews from the *Edison Manufacturing Company* who stationed kinetograph apparatuses around the Capitol grounds.
- Chief Justice *Melville Fuller* administered the presidential oath of office upon the East Portico of the Capitol, with McKinley kissing an ornate Bible opened to the passage from Second Chronicles chapter one, verse ten.
- Delivered an inaugural address that forcefully prioritized the restoration of domestic commercial prosperity, calling for immediate tariff revision that materialized months later in the passage of the protectionist *Dingley Tariff*.
- Outgoing two-term Democratic President *Grover Cleveland* accompanied McKinley in an open carriage to the Capitol, exemplifying a cordial and peaceful transfer of executive authority amidst intense nationwide labor and agrarian turmoil.
- Vice President *Garret Hobart* was inaugurated inside the Senate Chamber shortly before the outdoor public address, serving until his sudden death in 1899 necessitated the selection of *Theodore Roosevelt* as running mate for the 1901 ticket.
- Introduced the first glass-enclosed viewing pavilion constructed along Pennsylvania Avenue directly across from the White House, allowing dignitaries to observe the hours-long inaugural parade protected from harsh winter weather.
- The evening inaugural ball was celebrated within the soaring interior court of the *Pension Building*, where *William McKinley* drew public admiration for attentively caring for his wife, First Lady *Ida Saxton McKinley*, who suffered from epilepsy.
- Warned explicitly against foreign entanglement and territorial aggression in his speech, asserting that peace was preferable to conquest, an ironic prologue given the outbreak of war with [[spain]] just over a year later.

## connections

- [[silent-film]] — made pioneering media history when *Thomas Edison*'s operators filmed the presidential procession, proving the political utility of early motion picture technology.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — fulfilled Article II requirements for the executive oath of office, traditionally scheduled on March 4 until the Twentieth Amendment altered the date.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — reflected the zenith of American manufacturing interests, with an inaugural platform centered on protective tariffs, industrial expansion, and corporate enterprise.
- [[american-civil-war]] — marked the swearing-in of the last United States president who had served as an enlisted soldier and officer during the conflict.
- [[spain]] — the inaugural pledge to avoid foreign conquest was swiftly tested and broken by the naval and military campaigns of the Spanish-American War in 1898.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — completed the electoral transition away from Reconstruction-era regional battle lines toward the business-oriented coalition of the Fourth Party System.
- [[fdr-first-inauguration]] — stands alongside *Franklin D. Roosevelt*'s 1933 swearing-in as a landmark address responding directly to deep economic crisis and widespread banking panics.
