## summary

The **American Civil Liberties Union**, widely recognized as the **ACLU**, is a non-profit legal advocacy organization in the [[united-states]] dedicated to defending individual constitutional rights. Founded in 1920 in [[new-york-city]] in response to federal crackdowns on anti-war dissent, the organization provides legal counsel in landmark cases involving freedom of speech, racial equality, and procedural fairness. Its prolific litigation before the Supreme Court has fundamentally shaped modern constitutional law surrounding the [[bill-of-rights]] and the [[14th-amendment]].

## you gotta know

- Sponsored the legal defense of biology teacher *John T. Scopes* alongside attorney *Clarence Darrow* during the 1925 *Scopes Monkey Trial*, challenging a state statute prohibiting the instruction of human evolution in public schools.
- Defended the First Amendment rights of a neo-Nazi organization to demonstrate in *Skokie*, Illinois, in 1977, illustrating its commitment to content-neutral free speech despite intense public backlash and the loss of thousands of members.
- Represented public school students in the landmark 1969 ruling *Tinker v. Des Moines*, establishing that students do not shed their constitutional rights to freedom of speech or expression at the schoolhouse gate.
- Supported the defense in the 1966 milestone *Miranda v. Arizona*, which established mandatory warnings and procedural [[due-process]] safeguards against self-incrimination during custodial police interrogations.
- Aided the [[civil-rights-movement]] by submitting influential amicus curiae briefs in *Brown v. Board of Education* to overturn school segregation and directly representing the appellants in *Loving v. Virginia* to strike down state bans on interracial marriage.
- Challenged wartime civil rights abuses by contesting Japanese American exclusion orders during [[world-war-ii]] in *Korematsu v. United States*, arguing against military internment premised solely on ancestral heritage.
- Emerged in 1920 under the guidance of *Roger Baldwin*, *Crystal Eastman*, and social reformer *Jane Addams*, evolving out of the wartime National Civil Liberties Bureau created to protect conscientious objectors during [[world-war-i]].
- Became the first national organization to call publicly for the impeachment of President *Richard Nixon* in October 1973 following the Saturday Night Massacre during the [[watergate]] crisis.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme legal framework whose enumerated protections and institutional limits the organization litigates to preserve in court.
- [[first-amendment]] — principal constitutional battleground where the group asserts speech, press, assembly, and religious liberties regardless of public sentiment toward the speaker.
- [[14th-amendment]] — critical constitutional mechanism through which the organization incorporates federal civil liberties against state governments via the Due Process and Equal Protection clauses.
- [[due-process]] — procedural guarantee championed by the group across the criminal justice system to restrain state overreach and ensure fair treatment for the accused.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — era of mass social mobilization in which the organization partnered with legal activists to dismantle segregation laws and expand minority voting rights.
- [[world-war-i]] — historical conflict whose associated espionage legislation and subsequent Palmer Raids directly spurred the founding of the organization to protect civil liberties.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict during which the group stood almost alone among major legal institutions in contesting the constitutionality of Japanese American internment camps.
- [[watergate]] — political crisis that prompted the organization to adopt an unprecedented formal resolution advocating for presidential impeachment.
