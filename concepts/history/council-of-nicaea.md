## summary

The **Council of Nicaea**, recognized in church history as the **First Council of Nicaea** and the **First Ecumenical Council**, was an assembly of Christian bishops convened in 325 CE at Nicaea in Bithynia by Roman Emperor *Constantine the Great*. Gathered to resolve the theological firestorm sparked by the Alexandrian priest *Arius*, the council sought to unify a fractious church following centuries of Roman imperial persecution. It is a defining milestone in Western religious history because it formulated the foundational *Nicene Creed*, affirmed the consubstantial divinity of the Son, and initiated the enduring precedent of imperial intervention in ecclesiastical dogma.

## you gotta know

- Convened in 325 CE by Emperor *Constantine the Great*, who personally hosted the bishops at his imperial palace in Nicaea to ensure political and religious stability across the *[[roman-empire]]*.
- Condemned the teaching of *Arianism*, which posited that *[[jesus]]* was a created entity subordinate to the Father and encapsulated in the famous Arian slogan "there was a time when he was not."
- Promulgated the original *Nicene Creed*, which asserted that Christ is *homoousios* ("consubstantial" or of the same essence) with God the Father, a theological formula ardently defended by *Athanasius of Alexandria*.
- Settled the *Computus* dispute regarding the uniform calculation of the date of *Easter*, severing its ecclesiastical celebration from the lunar timing of the Jewish Passover.
- Enacted twenty canons of church law, establishing administrative hierarchy among the metropolitan sees of *[[rome]]*, Alexandria, and Antioch, and regulating the readmission of apostates who had yielded during imperial persecutions.
- Popular tradition claims that *Saint Nicholas of Myra*, the historical inspiration for Santa Claus, became so enraged during the proceedings that he physically slapped or punched *Arius* on the council floor.
- Exiled *Arius* along with the Libyan bishops *Theonas of Marmarica* and *Secundus of Ptolemais*, who refused to sign the council's creedal statement and anathemas.
- Many attending bishops bore visible physical scars, mutilations, and amputations suffered during the Great Persecution orchestrated two decades earlier by Emperor *[[diocletian]]*.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — global faith whose mainstream trinitarian theology and creedal orthodoxy were codified at the gathering.
- [[roman-empire]] — imperial state whose political authorities sponsored and enforced the council's theological decrees.
- [[rome]] — ancient imperial capital and Western patriarchal see represented at the council by the presbyters *Vitus* and *Vincentius* on behalf of Pope *Sylvester I*.
- [[jesus]] — central figure of Christian theology whose divine nature and ontological co-equality with the Father were affirmed.
- [[diocletian]] — emperor whose systematic persecutions immediately preceded Constantine's toleration and the emergence of the confessor bishops.
- [[edict-of-milan]] — landmark 313 CE proclamation granting religious toleration, setting the stage for open episcopal gatherings throughout the Mediterranean.
- [[battle-of-the-milvian-bridge]] — watershed military victory in 312 CE that brought Constantine to power under the Christian labarum.
