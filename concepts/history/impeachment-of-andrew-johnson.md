## summary

The **impeachment of Andrew Johnson** was the Senate trial of the 17th President in 1868 for allegedly violating the [[reconstruction-acts|Reconstruction Acts]] and the Tenure of Office Act. Driven by [[radical-republicans|Radical Republicans]] led by [[thaddeus-stevens|Thaddeus Stevens]] who sought to remove a president they viewed as obstructing Reconstruction and Black civil rights, the House impeached Johnson on 11 articles. The Senate fell one vote short of the two-thirds majority required for conviction, preserving the presidency but marking the first presidential impeachment and establishing a precedent for using impeachment as a political weapon.

## you gotta know

- Began in early 1868 when [[andrew-johnson|Johnson]] dismissed Secretary of War [[edwin-stanton|Edwin Stanton]], supposedly violating the Tenure of Office Act requiring Senate approval for cabinet dismissals.
- The Tenure of Office Act itself was constitutionally dubious and designed specifically to protect radical-aligned cabinet members from presidential removal.
- Radical Republicans were enraged by Johnson's lenient [[reconstruction-era|Reconstruction]] policies and his opposition to the [[14th-amendment|14th Amendment]], which guaranteed Black civil rights.
- Led by [[thaddeus-stevens|Stevens]], the House voted 126–47 to impeach, generating 11 articles of charges focused on the Stanton dismissal and obstruction of Reconstruction.
- The Senate trial lasted over two months; conviction required 36 votes out of 54 senators, a two-thirds supermajority.
- On the pivotal vote, the Senate fell exactly one vote short: 35 for conviction, 19 for acquittal—Edmund Ross and others broke party ranks to vote for acquittal.
- Johnson remained in office but was politically crippled; he never won nomination for a full presidential term and left office discredited.
