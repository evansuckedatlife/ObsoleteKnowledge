## summary

**Erwin von Rundstedt** was a prominent German field marshal who served as one of *[[germany]]*'s senior military commanders throughout *[[world-war-ii]]*. Representing the traditional Prussian aristocratic officer corps, he commanded Army Group South during the invasion of Poland and Army Group A during the fall of *[[france]]* in 1940. Later serving as Commander-in-Chief West during the Allied invasion of Normandy and the Ardennes Offensive, his career exemplified senior Wehrmacht complicity in aggressive war and criminal rear-area directives.

## you gotta know

- Commanded Army Group A during the 1940 Western Campaign, overseeing the decisive armored thrust through the Ardennes developed with *[[erich-von-manstein]]*.
- Validated the famous Halt Order outside Dunkirk in late May 1940, allowing the British Expeditionary Force time to evacuate across the English Channel.
- Led Army Group South during Operation Barbarossa against the *[[soviet-union]]* in 1941, capturing Kiev and advancing to Rostov before being dismissed for ordering a tactical retreat.
- Served repeatedly as Commander-in-Chief West (*Oberbefehlshaber West*), overseeing defensive preparations across Western Europe alongside *Erwin Rommel* prior to the D-Day landings.
- Endorsed the notorious Reichenau Order on the Eastern Front, commanding troops to cooperate with SS extermination squads against Jewish populations.
- Held nominal overall theater command during the German winter counter-offensive in December 1944, though he opposed the strategic ambition of what Western Allies termed the Battle of the Bulge.
- Arrested by Allied forces in May 1945, interned in British custody, and indicted for war crimes before being released in 1949 due to severe cardiovascular illness.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict in which he served as one of the Wehrmacht's senior operational field marshals.
- [[erich-von-manstein]] — his chief of staff who formulated the Fall Gelb sickle-cut invasion plan adopted for the 1940 French campaign.
- [[germany]] — his nation whose Prussian military traditions and subsequent Nazi regime he served across both world conflicts.
- [[france]] — the western theater where he directed German forces in 1940 and served as Commander-in-Chief West from 1942 to 1945.
- [[soviet-union]] — the eastern adversary against which he commanded Army Group South during Operation Barbarossa.
- [[world-war-i]] — the earlier conflict in which he served as a general staff officer on both the Eastern and Western fronts.
