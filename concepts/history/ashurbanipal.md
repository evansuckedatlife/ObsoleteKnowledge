## summary

**Ashurbanipal** (also rendered **Asurbanipal**, and remembered in classical Greco-Roman tradition as **Sardanapalus**) was the last great ruler of the Neo-Assyrian Empire, reigning from 669 BC to roughly 631 BC from his royal seat at *Nineveh* in the ancient [[middle-east]]. Renowned both for relentless imperial expansion and remarkable intellectual devotion, he assembled the celebrated royal library that preserved thousands of clay tablets containing Mesopotamia's richest literary, scientific, and religious texts. His reign marked both the zenith of Assyrian geopolitical supremacy and the cultural preservation that allowed the modern rediscovery of ancient Near Eastern literature, though his death triggered a swift imperial collapse.

## you gotta know

- Assembled the vast royal *Library of Ashurbanipal* at *Nineveh*, systematically collecting, copying, and cataloging more than thirty thousand cuneiform tablets from across Babylonia and Assyria, which preserved foundational masterworks such as the *[[epic-of-gilgamesh]]* and the creation epic *Enuma Elish*.
- Distinguished himself from other Mesopotamian warrior kings by boasting of his personal literacy, claiming he was initiated into scribal secrets, could solve advanced mathematical problems, and was capable of reading obscure cuneiform inscriptions written before the mythical deluge.
- Crushed a massive imperial civil war led by his older brother *Shamash-shum-ukin*, whom their father *Esarhaddon* had installed on the throne of *Babylon*, culminating in a grueling three-year siege that ended when *Babylon* fell and his brother perished in the flames of his burning palace.
- Raided and leveled the Elamite capital of *Susa*, systematically salting the earth, desecrating royal tombs, and putting an end to the kingdom of *Elam*, celebrated in a famous palace relief depicting him feasting with Queen *Libbali-sharrat* beneath the severed head of King *Teumman*.
- Maintained imperial dominion over *Egypt* early in his reign, suppressing stubborn local rebellions by pushing south to sack the sacred city of *Thebes*, an event remembered in biblical [[judaism]] as an emblem of devastating imperial conquest.
- Commissioned the intricate *Lion Hunt of Ashurbanipal* bas-reliefs in the *North Palace* at *Nineveh*, masterworks of ancient narrative sculpture depicting the monarch slaying lions with bows, spears, and daggers as a ritual demonstration of royal power over nature and chaos.
- Inspired the enduring classical myth of *Sardanapalus*, whom ancient writers in [[ancient-greece]] distorted into an effeminate and decadent ruler who committed suicide in a massive funeral pyre alongside his concubines and treasures when *Nineveh* fell, a legend that later inspired European Romantic artists.

## connections

- [[middle-east]] — cradle of ancient Mesopotamian civilization where he ruled the vast Neo-Assyrian domain from *Nineveh*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — locus of classical historians like *Ctesias* who transformed his historical persona into the legendary, tragic king *Sardanapalus*.
- [[judaism]] — religious and textual tradition whose biblical prophets documented Assyrian conquest and referenced the sack of *Thebes*.
- [[poetry]] — literary and artistic tradition embodied in the mythological verse, hymns, and royal epics preserved within his royal collection.
- [[linguistics]] — academic discipline revolutionized when modern philologists deciphered cuneiform Akkadian and Sumerian using tablets excavated from his library.
- [[epic-of-gilgamesh]] — landmark Mesopotamian epic poem whose standard twelve-tablet edition survived to modern times thanks to his library archives.
- [[rome]] — classical Mediterranean empire whose imperial administrators and later historians drew direct parallels with Assyrian militarism and imperial governance.
