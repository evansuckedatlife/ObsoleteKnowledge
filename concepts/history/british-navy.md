## summary

The **British Navy** (formally the Royal Navy) is one of history's most influential naval forces, achieving global naval dominance from the 18th century and maintaining technological and strategic leadership into the modern era. It pioneered naval tactics, ship design, and institutional organization that shaped modern naval warfare. The Navy was the primary instrument of British imperial power and remains central to understanding how maritime supremacy translated into global influence.

## you gotta know

- Rose to prominence after defeating the Spanish Armada (1588), but became the world's dominant naval power following victory at the Battle of the Nile (1798) and Trafalgar (1805).
- Under *Admiral Nelson*, the British Navy developed innovative tactical doctrines emphasizing massed firepower and close-quarters combat rather than rigid line formations.
- Ruled unopposed for nearly a century after Trafalgar (1805–1906), during which the British Navy projected power to every ocean and served as guarantor of British imperial interests.
- The *HMS Dreadnought* (1906) revolutionized battleship design; Britain's naval dominance shifted when Germany rapidly built dreadnoughts, sparking an arms race before World War I.
- In World War I and II, the Navy fought German submarines and surface raiders; developed convoy tactics, naval aviation, and carrier warfare in response to new threats.
- Shifted from battleship-centered to carrier-centered doctrine during World War II; remained a major maritime power through the Cold War despite losing imperial commitments.
