## summary

**Themistocles** was an Athenian politician and general (*strategos*) of the fifth century BC who transformed Athens into the preeminent naval power of [[ancient-greece]]. Rising to prominence as a populist leader, he foresaw the second Persian invasion and persuaded the Athenian assembly to invest the silver windfall from the mines of *Laurium* into building a massive fleet of triremes. His masterstroke at the naval *Battle of Salamis* in 480 BC lured the overwhelming armada of *Xerxes I* into narrow straits, securing a decisive victory that saved the Greek city-states and laid the foundation for the Athenian Empire. Though later ostracized by his fellow citizens and forced into exile in Persia, his strategic foresight permanently altered Mediterranean history.

## you gotta know

- Orchestrated the decisive Greek victory at the naval *Battle of Salamis* (480 BC) during the [[persian-wars]], crushing the Persian fleet under [[xerxes]] in the narrow straits between Attica and Salamis Island.
- Convinced the citizens of *Athens* to dedicate a rich silver vein discovered at *Laurium* to building two hundred triremes, forging the naval backbone that transformed Athens into a maritime empire.
- Interpreted the Delphic oracle's enigmatic prophecy that a "wooden wall" alone would survive the Persian onslaught as a divine mandate to rely upon the Athenian wooden fleet rather than the Acropolis walls.
- Dispatched his trusted slave and tutor *Sicinnus* with a secret message to the Persian king [[xerxes]], deceptively claiming friendship and warning that the allied Greek ships were planning to scatter, tricking the Persians into trapping themselves in the cramped narrows.
- Stalled Spartan ambassadors through deliberate diplomatic delays while the citizens of *Athens*, including women and children, hastily rebuilt and fortified the city's defensive perimeter, creating the *Themistoclean Wall*.
- Developed and fortified the deep-water harbor of *Piraeus*, replacing the exposed bay of *Phalerum* and linking the commercial lifeline directly to Athens.
- Orchestrated the political downfall and ostracism of his chief conservative rival, *Aristeides the Just*, before later falling out of public favor himself, suffering ostracism around 472 BC, and spending his final years serving the Persian king *Artaxerxes I* as governor of *Magnesia*.
- According to popular classical legend recorded by *Plutarch* and *Thucydides*, chose to die in exile by drinking bull's blood rather than fulfill Persian demands to command an expedition against his Greek homeland.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilizational sphere whose democratic institutions and naval supremacy he helped forge.
- [[persian-wars]] — the existential conflict between Greek city-states and the Achaemenid Empire where he served as master strategist.
- [[xerxes]] — the Persian Great King whom he outmaneuvered at Salamis using deception and geography.
- [[rome]] — the later Mediterranean power whose statesmen and historians studied his strategic precedents.
- [[middle-east]] — the imperial heartland of Persia where he spent his final years in exile as a regional governor.
- [[zeus]] — the supreme Greek deity honored at Olympia, where crowds famously celebrated his wartime achievements.
