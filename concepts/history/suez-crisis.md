## summary

The **Suez Crisis** of 1956 erupted when Egyptian President *Gamal Abdel Nasser* nationalized the Suez Canal, prompting a secret military invasion by *Britain*, *France*, and *Israel* who sought to reclaim the vital waterway and topple his regime. Cold War superpowers and international pressure forced a humiliating Allied withdrawal within weeks, marking a dramatic shift in global power: the decline of European colonialism and the rise of non-aligned nations in the postwar order.

## you gotta know

- *Nasser* nationalized the Suez Canal in July 1956 after the United States withdrew funding for the Aswan High Dam, a deliberate assertion of Egyptian sovereignty over the vital waterway.
- The canal had been under joint British-French control since its construction, generating enormous revenues and serving as a strategic artery of global commerce and imperial power projection.
- *Britain* and *France* coordinated a secret invasion plan with *Israel*, meeting at Sèvres in October 1956 to coordinate military action under the pretext of separating Israeli and Egyptian forces.
- *Israel* invaded Sinai on October 29 to seize the Gaza Strip and Eilat; *Britain* and *France* then bombed Egyptian airfields and landed paratroopers at Port Said.
- Soviet Premier *Nikita Khrushchev* threatened nuclear intervention on behalf of *Egypt*, while U.S. President *Eisenhower* opposed the invasion diplomatically and through UN channels, fracturing the Western alliance.
- UN emergency force troops deployed to maintain a ceasefire; *Britain*, *France*, and *Israel* withdrew by early 1957 without toppling *Nasser* or reclaiming the canal.
- The crisis marked the definitive end of European colonialism in the Middle East and established *Nasser* as the paramount Arab leader, reshaping Cold War alignments and regional politics for decades.
