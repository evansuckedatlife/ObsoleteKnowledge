## summary

**Fall River, Massachusetts** is an industrial port city situated along Mount Hope Bay and the Quequechan River in Bristol County. During the nineteenth century, the city leveraged its steep water falls and humid coastal climate to evolve into the premier textile manufacturing hub in the *United States*, operating over one hundred granite cotton mills. Despite its commanding industrial prominence, **Fall River** became permanently etched into American folklore in August 1892 as the scene of the sensational hatchet murders of *Andrew Borden* and his wife *Abby*.

## you gotta know

- Gained the moniker the *Spindle City* during the late nineteenth century when its massive granite mills along the Quequechan River produced more cotton cloth than any other city in the nation.
- Served as the epicenter of a notorious double homicide on August 4, 1892, when prominent banker *Andrew Borden* and his second wife *Abby Durfee Borden* were bludgeoned to death in their Second Street residence.
- Sparked a nationwide media circus and immortal children's jumping-rope rhyme centered around *Andrew's* thirty-two-year-old unmarried daughter, *Lizzie Borden*, who was arrested and indicted for the crimes.
- Held a sensational fifteen-day trial in June 1893 in nearby New Bedford that resulted in the complete acquittal of *Lizzie Borden*, leaving the case officially unsolved and fertile ground for true crime and *detective-fiction*.
- Built its industrial empire out of locally quarried Fall River granite rather than red brick, creating monumental, fortress-like factory structures that still dominate the urban skyline.
- Experienced devastating economic decline in the 1920s and 1930s as textile manufacturing migrated to non-union mills in the American South, a collapse exacerbated by catastrophic downtown fires in 1928.
- Absorbed vast influxes of European immigrants to work its spindles, establishing a vibrant Portuguese-American community that transformed the city into an enduring center of Azorean culture and cuisine in *New England*.
