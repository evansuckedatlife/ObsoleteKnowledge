## summary

**Gerald Ford** was the 38th President of the United States, assuming office in 1974 after *Richard Nixon's* resignation during the *Watergate* scandal. Though he served less than two years as president, his decision to pardon Nixon shaped the nation's reckoning with the crisis and defined his presidency.

## you gotta know

- Only U.S. president never elected to either the presidency or vice presidency; appointed VP after *Spiro Agnew's* resignation in 1973 and became president when Nixon resigned in 1974.
- Rise to power was extraordinarily rapid: became Vice President in December 1973 and President in August 1974, a span of just eight months.
- Immediately issued an unconditional pardon of Nixon in September 1974, granting him full forgiveness for crimes related to Watergate and potentially obstructing justice.
- Ford defended the pardon as necessary for national healing, but it appeared to many as a backroom deal struck when Ford accepted the vice presidency.
- The pardon proved deeply unpopular, with public approval ratings dropping sharply; it likely cost him the 1976 presidential election against *Jimmy Carter*, despite initially gaining sympathy as a stabilizing figure.
- Career congressman from Michigan for 25 years and House Minority Leader before his sudden elevation to the highest office.
- Known for integrity and bipartisan cooperation in Congress, but wielded little executive experience before assuming the presidency during a national crisis.
- Faced foreign policy crises including the fall of *Saigon* in 1975 (the end of American involvement in Vietnam) and the *Mayaguez* incident with Cambodia.
- Authorized the military response to the Mayaguez seizure (1975), an action seen as restoring American credibility after Vietnam but criticized for resulting in unnecessary American casualties.
- Emphasized healing and moving forward rather than prosecution and recrimination in addressing the nation's trauma from Watergate and Vietnam.
- His presidency, though cut short by electoral defeat, established precedents for transition of power and civilian control of government during constitutional crisis.
- Lived longer than any other U.S. president and was widely respected for his character, though his pardon of Nixon remained controversial until his death in 2006.
