## summary

**Charles IX** (*Charles-Maximilien*), crowned as **Charles IX of France**, was King of *France* from 1560 until his death in 1574, ascending the throne as a ten-year-old monarch of the House of *Valois-Angoulême*. His turbulent reign was completely dominated by the outbreak of the *French Wars of Religion*, a devastating series of sectarian civil conflicts between French Catholics and Calvinist *Protestants* known as [[huguenots]]. He is most notorious in European history for sanctioning the *St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre* of 1572 under the intense sway of his mother, *Catherine de' Medici*, an atrocity that resulted in the targeted slaughter of thousands of Huguenots across the realm.

## you gotta know

- Authorized the infamous *St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre* in August 1572 following the attempted assassination of Admiral *Gaspard de Coligny*, reportedly shouting in a fit of panic to kill all Huguenots so none remained to reproach him.
- Ascended the French throne in 1560 at only ten years of age after the early death of his elder brother *Francis II*, leaving his Italian mother, *Catherine de' Medici*, to govern the realm as queen regent during his minority.
- Reigned during the first four distinct conflicts of the *French Wars of Religion*, attempting multiple short-lived reconciliations such as the *Edict of Saint-Germain-en-Laye* and the *Peace of Amboise* before sectarian warfare resumed.
- Arranged the diplomatic wedding of his sister *Margaret of Valois* to the Protestant leader *Henry of Navarre*, a royal celebration that drew thousands of aristocratic Huguenots into *Paris* directly before the massacre.
- Maintained a serious devotion to the arts of the *French Renaissance*, serving as an enthusiastic royal patron to court poets of *La Pléiade*, especially *Pierre de Ronsard*, and chartering the *Académie Royale de Poésie et de Musique*.
- Penned an early technical treatise on royal hunting titled *La Chasse Royale*, reflecting his obsessive dedication to stag hunting and physical exertion, which courtiers feared exacerbated his chronic respiratory weaknesses.
- Died at age twenty-three in 1574 from tuberculosis at the *Château de Vincennes*, reportedly tortured on his deathbed by vivid hallucinations and remorse over the victims of the St. Bartholomew's Day bloodshed, passing the crown to his brother *Henry III*.

## connections

- [[catherine-de-medici]] — his formidable mother who governed France as regent and exerted immense influence over his decisions.
- [[huguenots]] — the French Protestant faction that suffered targeted persecution and mass murder under his nominal authority.
- [[french-wars-of-religion]] — the brutal religious civil wars that consumed his entire administration and destabilized the Valois dynasty.
- [[henry-of-navarre]] — his Protestant brother-in-law whose Parisian royal wedding served as the prelude to the August slaughter.
- [[france]] — the European kingdom he ruled amid catastrophic social division, factional violence, and dynastic decline.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural and literary movement whose poets and musical academies he personally financed and promoted.
- [[christianity]] — the overarching religion whose deep Catholic-Protestant theological rift tore his country apart.
