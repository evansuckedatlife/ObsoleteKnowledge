## summary

**Boethius** (c. 480–524) was a Roman philosopher, mathematician, and statesman who served the Ostrogothic king *Theodoric* before being imprisoned and executed under accusation of treason. Though his own works were modest, his translations of Aristotle and Neoplatonic texts preserved classical philosophy through the early medieval period. His *Consolation of Philosophy*, written in prison, became one of the Middle Ages' most influential intellectual works.

## you gotta know

- Born into the highest Roman aristocracy; received an elite classical education and rose to prominence as a statesman, magistrate, and trusted counselor to Theodoric.
- Began a systematic translation project of Greek philosophical works into Latin, including logic texts by Aristotle—these translations became the foundation for medieval scholastic philosophy.
- Fell from favor around 523 when accused of conspiring against Theodoric, though historians debate whether the charge was genuine or politically motivated by rivals.
- Wrote the *Consolation of Philosophy* while imprisoned, a dialogue combining poetry and prose in which Philosophy personified comforts him; the work reconciles free will with divine foreknowledge.
- Executed in 524, possibly by order or with permission of Theodoric; his death marked the execution of Rome's last great classical scholar.
- His *Consolation* was copied, translated, and commented on continuously through the medieval period; it profoundly influenced medieval intellectual life and appears in Dante's works.
