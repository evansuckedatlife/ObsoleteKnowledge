## summary

**Freedom of contract** is the legal doctrine asserting that private individuals and corporations possess an inherent right to negotiate terms of employment and commerce without undue state interference. In United States constitutional history, it served as the cornerstone of the controversial *Lochner* era, during which the Supreme Court invoked the *14th Amendment* to invalidate progressive workplace legislation. Closely linked with [[free-market]] capitalism, the doctrine dominated early industrial jurisprudence before its dramatic repudiation during the New Deal.

## you gotta know

- Defined the legal jurisprudence of the *Lochner era*, named after the landmark 1905 Supreme Court ruling *Lochner v. New York*.
- Derived by conservative jurists through the doctrine of *substantive due process* under the Due Process Clause of the *14th Amendment*.
- Used repeatedly to strike down state and federal labor laws, including maximum-hour rules, mandatory workplace safety regulations, and minimum wage statutes.
- Provoked celebrated dissents from Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr., who famously argued that the Constitution does not enact Herbert Spencer's social statics.
- Advanced by Supreme Court Justice Rufus Peckham, who asserted that bakers and bakery owners possessed equal and inviolable liberties to contract labor.
- Applied in *Adair v. United States* (1908) and *Coppage v. Kansas* (1915) to preserve "yellow-dog contracts," forbidding workers from joining labor unions.
- Formally overturned as an absolute constitutional barrier in *West Coast Hotel Co. v. Parrish* (1937), concluding the *Lochner* era and upholding state minimum wage laws.

## connections

- [[14th-amendment]] — constitutional amendment whose Due Process Clause was interpreted by conservative courts to protect unenumerated economic liberties.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — foundational charter within which federal judicial review examined the limits of state regulatory authority.
- [[free-market]] — economic philosophy of voluntary exchange and non-intervention that provided theoretical grounding for contractual autonomy.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — rapid rise of corporate monopolies and factory labor that spurred clashes between worker welfare and employer rights.
- [[united-states]] — nation where late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century courts extensively litigated the boundaries of police powers.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — formative constitutional period that produced the Fourteenth Amendment originally designed to secure civil equality.
