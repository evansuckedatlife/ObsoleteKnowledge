# Bronze Age

## summary

The **Bronze Age** (roughly 3300–1200 BCE, varying by region) was a prehistoric and ancient period defined by the widespread use of *bronze*—an alloy of copper and tin—for tools, weapons, and ornaments. The Bronze Age witnessed the rise of complex civilizations, long-distance trade networks, writing systems, and sophisticated social hierarchies, marking a leap from the *Stone Age* toward organized urban societies.

## you gotta know

- Bronze, being harder and more durable than copper or tin alone, revolutionized tool-making and warfare; bronze weapons and armor gave wielders decisive advantages, reshaping military organization and social power.
- The Bronze Age emerged at different times globally: roughly 3300 BCE in Mesopotamia and the Aegean, later in Europe and China; the *Shang Dynasty* in ancient China was one of the world's first literate Bronze Age civilizations.
- Major Bronze Age civilizations included the *Minoans* (Crete), the *Mycenaeans* (Greece), the *Hittites* (Anatolia), the *Egyptian Middle Kingdom*, and the *Indus Valley Civilization*—each creating distinct cultures yet linked by trade.
- Bronze Age trade networks connected the Mediterranean, Anatolia, Mesopotamia, Egypt, and beyond; merchants exchanged tin from Afghanistan, copper from Cyprus, cedar from Lebanon, and luxury goods (ivory, lapis lazuli).
- Writing emerged during the Bronze Age in multiple regions: cuneiform in Mesopotamia, hieroglyphics in Egypt, Linear A and B in the Aegean, oracle bone script in China—each adapted to recording administrative, religious, and economic information.
- The Bronze Age Collapse (around 1200 BCE) saw the abrupt fall of major civilizations (Mycenaean Greece, the Hittite empire, Egyptian New Kingdom decline) due to invasions, climate change, and economic disruption, fragmenting the interconnected Bronze Age world.
- The transition to the *Iron Age* occurred as iron-working technology spread, making iron tools and weapons more available and affordable than bronze, eventually replacing bronze as the primary metal.
