## summary

**Epaminondas** was a brilliant Theban statesman, philosopher, and military general of the fourth century BCE who shattered the long-standing myth of Spartan invincibility and briefly established Theban supremacy across classical *[[ancient-greece]]*. Renowned for his tactical genius and unyielding civic integrity, he invented the revolutionary oblique phalanx formation and led the elite *Sacred Band of Thebes* in a series of daring campaigns that permanently transformed the geopolitical balance of the Hellenic world. His decisive liberation of the enslaved Messenian helots broke the agrarian and economic backbone of *Sparta*, securing his enduring historical legacy as one of the most innovative, philosophical, and ethical commanders of the ancient Mediterranean.

## you gotta know

- Crushed the reputedly invincible Spartan army at the *Battle of Leuctra* in 371 BCE by massing an unprecedented fifty-rank-deep phalanx on his left wing against the Spartan king *Cleombrotus I*.
- Pioneered the tactical *oblique order* (*loxē phalanx*), advancing his reinforced striking wing while echeloning back his center and right wing to refuse engagement until the decisive breakthrough was achieved.
- Commanded alongside his political partner *Pelopidas*, who directly led the *Sacred Band of Thebes*, an elite troop of one hundred and fifty pairs of dedicated male warriors.
- Invaded the *Peloponnese* four times, liberating the *Messenians* after centuries of Spartan subjugation and building the fortified capital of *Messene* on Mount Ithome to guarantee their independence.
- Established the federal capital of *Megalopolis* in Arcadia, creating a powerful coalition of southern Greek city-states to permanently encircle and contain Spartan aggression.
- Fostered the education of the young hostage prince *Philip II of Macedon*, who resided in Thebes and absorbed Epaminondas's revolutionary tactical and operational doctrines.
- Declined to accumulate personal wealth despite his continental fame, living in austere poverty guided by the teachings of the Pythagorean philosopher *Lysis of Tarentum*.
- Mortally wounded by a spear while leading a decisive charge at the *Battle of Mantinea* in 362 BCE, dying only after confirming that his shield was safe and advising Thebans to make peace.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization whose geopolitical architecture and balance of power he radically restructured by ending centuries of Spartan dominance.
- [[sparta]] — the militaristic rival city-state whose regional hegemony and helot-dependent agrarian economy he permanently dismantled through repeated invasions.
- [[athens]] — the leading democratic city-state that formed an uneasy defensive coalition with its traditional enemy Sparta to counter Theban continental ascendancy.
- [[tragedy]] — the Athenian dramatic tradition whose greatest plays by *Sophocles* and *Aeschylus* frequently depicted the legendary kings and civil strife of Epaminondas's native Thebes.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the Elizabethan playwright whose classical historical works and political tragedies drew directly upon *Plutarch*'s biographical depictions of Hellenic commanders and moral leadership.
- [[english-law]] — the later Western legal and political tradition whose constitutional theorists drew upon classical Greek federations, civic virtue, and defensive alliances against tyranny.
