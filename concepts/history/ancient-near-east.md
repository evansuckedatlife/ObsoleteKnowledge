## summary

The **Ancient Near East** denotes the early civilizations encompassing *Mesopotamia*, *Egypt*, the *Levant*, *Anatolia*, and the *Iranian plateau* from the rise of agriculture through the conquests of *Alexander the Great*. Often recognized as the primary cradle of civilization, this interconnected macro-region pioneered sedentary urban settlements, codified legal systems, centralized monarchies, and the wheel. Its revolutionary development of writing scripts like *cuneiform* and the *Phoenician alphabet* provided the bureaucratic, literary, and commercial infrastructure that permanently shaped world history.

## you gotta know

- Anchored by the *Fertile Crescent*, the region hosted the *Neolithic Revolution*, which introduced agriculture, livestock domestication, and irrigation canal networks that sustained early urban centers like *Uruk*, *Ur*, and *Jericho*.
- It witnessed the birth of human writing, beginning in southern *Mesopotamia* with *Sumerian cuneiform*, an imprint script on wet clay tablets that recorded economic inventories, royal annals, and masterworks like the *Epic of Gilgamesh*.
- The world's earliest territorial state was forged here by *Sargon of Akkad*, inaugurating centuries of imperial cycles through the *Old Babylonian Empire*, the chariot-driven *Hittites*, the militarily relentless *Neo-Assyrian Empire*, and the expansive *Achaemenid Persian Empire*.
- Public statutory law emerged as a major instrument of statecraft, exemplified by the basalt stela of the *Code of Hammurabi*, which articulated *lex talionis* retribution, economic regulations, and class distinctions under the divine sanction of *Shamash*.
- Monumental architecture expressed religious and political cosmology, notably stepped mud-brick *ziggurats* dedicated to city patron deities such as *Marduk* and colossal human-headed winged-bull *lamassu* guarding Assyrian palace gates at *Nineveh* and *Nimrud*.
- The *Late Bronze Age* featured an intricate international diplomatic and trading network known as the *Amarna Period*, documented in cuneiform correspondence between "Great Kings" of *Egypt*, *Mitanni*, *Babylonia*, and *Hatti*.
- The trilingual *Behistun Inscription* carved into a cliff face in modern western Iran by *Darius the Great* provided parallel texts in *Old Persian*, *Elamite*, and *Babylonian* that enabled nineteenth-century orientalists like *Sir Henry Rawlinson* to decipher cuneiform.
- Groundbreaking prehistoric excavations across the northern periphery, particularly the monumental stone enclosures of *Göbekli Tepe* and the dense mudbrick settlement of *Çatalhöyük*, revealed that complex communal ceremonialism and symbolic art predated full agrarian urbanism.

## connections

- [[mesopotamia]] — the foundational river basin between the Tigris and Euphrates that served as the primary core for early urbanism and writing.
- [[akkadian-empire]] — the first multi-ethnic territorial empire in historical record, unifying Sumerian and Semitic populations under a centralized imperial administration.
- [[code-of-hammurabi]] — the celebrated Babylonian statutory corpus detailing civil, criminal, and commercial law inscribed under royal authority.
- [[middle-east]] — the overarching modern geographic territory that encompasses the historical heartlands, river valleys, and desert peripheries of the ancient empires.
- [[judaism]] — the biblical monotheistic religion that developed in the Levantine corridor amid direct political, legal, and theological interaction with imperial neighbors.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the Aegean civilization whose archaic art, alphabet, mythology, and philosophy were deeply enriched by trade contacts across the eastern Mediterranean.
- [[silk-road]] — the sprawling continental commercial network whose western termini connected through long-established ancient caravan routes across the Iranian plateau and Syrian desert.
- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline transformed by the comparative decipherment and classification of regional language families, including Semitic, Indo-European, and isolate tongues like *Sumerian*.
