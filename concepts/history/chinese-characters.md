## summary

**Chinese characters**, known natively as *Hanzi*, constitute the oldest continuously used logographic writing system in the world. Originating during the Shang dynasty as divinatory inscriptions on turtle shells and ox scapulae, the script evolved through standardized clerical and regular forms into the cultural bedrock of East Asian civilization. Because individual characters represent morphemes and conceptual meanings rather than purely phonetic values, the script unified diverse spoken dialects across *China* and served as an international medium of scholarship and statecraft across the Sinosphere.

## you gotta know

- Earliest verified attestations survive as oracle bone script (*jiaguwen*) from Anyang, where Shang dynasty rulers inscribed divinations regarding warfare, harvests, and ancestral rituals.
- Standardized under *Qin Shi Huang* and his chancellor *Li Si* during the Qin dynasty, unifying diverse regional scripts into the small seal script (*xiaozhuan*) to consolidate imperial authority.
- Transitioned into the flowing clerical script (*lishu*) and regular script (*kaishu*) during the Han dynasty, establishing the standard stroke order, balance, and proportions retained into modernity.
- Analyzed structurally by Han scholar *Xu Shen* in the foundational dictionary *Shuowen Jiezi*, which introduced radical indexing and classified characters into six structural principles (*liushu*), such as pictograms, ideograms, and phono-semantic compounds.
- Functioned as a pan-East Asian administrative and literary medium (*Hanmun* or Classical Chinese), adapting into Japanese *kanji*, Korean *hanja*, and Vietnamese *chữ Hán*.
- Split into two primary typographic standards in the 20th century: Traditional characters, retained in Taiwan and Hong Kong, and Simplified characters, promulgated by the People's Republic of *China* to accelerate mass literacy.
- Cultivated as high art through Chinese calligraphy (*shufa*), where brush control, ink balance, and expressive momentum made writing one of the celebrated Four Arts of the scholar-official.

## connections

- [[china]] — homeland, civilizational anchor, and linguistic birthplace of the logographic writing system.
- [[linguistics]] — subject of study for logographic, morphemic, and phonetic writing typology.
- [[japan]] — adapted the characters as *kanji* while deriving the phonetic *hiragana* and *katakana* syllabaries from them.
- [[silk-road]] — transcontinental trade routes that spread written records, papermaking, and translated religious manuscripts.
- [[buddhism]] — translated from Sanskrit into classical Chinese characters, spreading throughout East Asia.
- [[poetry]] — formal poetic genres such as Tang *shi* and Song *ci* drew heavily on the structural symmetry of the characters.
