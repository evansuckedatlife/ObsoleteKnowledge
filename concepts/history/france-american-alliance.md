## summary

The **Franco-American Alliance** was a military alliance between *France* and the *American* revolutionaries during the American War of Independence, formally established in 1778. French military support, naval power, and financial resources proved decisive in American victory and reshaped global geopolitics by weakening *Britain*'s dominance.

## you gotta know

- Began informally in 1775 as France supplied covert military aid and supplies to American rebels (gunpowder, weapons, uniforms), escalating to formal alliance after the American victory at *Saratoga* (1777).
- The victory at Saratoga convinced *King Louis XVI* and his ministers that American victory was possible, shifting France from cautious supplier to committed military partner.
- *Benjamin Franklin* played a crucial diplomatic role in Paris, cultivating French support through charm and deliberate cultivation of influential salon hosts and nobles.
- Franklin successfully negotiated the Treaty of Alliance (1778) and the Treaty of Commerce, formal documents binding France and America against Britain.
- French military involvement included troops (the *Comte de Rochambeau*'s expeditionary force of approximately 6,000 men), naval forces, and artillery, filling critical American gaps in training and equipment.
- *Comte de Rochambeau* coordinated operations with *George Washington* and proved instrumental in sieges like *Yorktown*, demonstrating sophisticated joint operations.
- Turned the tide decisively; American forces alone could not have defeated the British without French intervention and naval superiority, particularly in preventing British reinforcement.
- Enabled French naval victories that restricted British reinforcement and supply lines, particularly critical at *Yorktown* (1781), where French navy blockaded British escape.
- Created a debt burden that contributed to France's fiscal crisis (approximately 1.3 billion livres) and ultimately to the *French Revolution* a decade later, as France taxed its people to pay war costs.
