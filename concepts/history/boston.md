## summary

**Boston** is the capital and largest municipality of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, situated along Massachusetts Bay on the historic Shawmut Peninsula. Founded in 1630 by Puritan emigrants from [[england]], the maritime settlement rapidly developed into a thriving commercial seaport and the intellectual capital of New England. Renowned as the "Cradle of Liberty," the city served as the primary theater and ideological crucible for the opening hostilities of the [[american-revolution]], orchestrating defiance against parliamentary taxation and enduring prolonged military occupation by the [[british-empire]]. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, the city emerged as a crucial financial engine of the [[industrial-revolution]], a bastion of radical abolitionism, and one of the world's preeminent centers for higher education, medical research, and scientific innovation within the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Sparked the American War of Independence through fierce resistance to parliamentary taxation, culminating in the 1773 *Boston Tea Party*, when the *Sons of Liberty* dumped 342 chests of imported tea into the harbor to resist the *Tea Act*.
- Suffered the 1770 *Boston Massacre* on King Street outside the Old State House, where British soldiers fired into a hostile civilian mob, killing five colonists including the dockworker *Crispus Attucks*.
- Endured an eleven-month siege during the opening phase of the [[american-revolution]], which ended when Continental Army forces under General [[george-washington]] deployed heavy artillery atop Dorchester Heights, compelling the British fleet to evacuate.
- Dispatched the famous 1775 midnight alarm ride of *Paul Revere* and *William Dawes*, coordinated via two warning lanterns hung in the steeple of the *Old North Church* to signal that British regulars were moving toward Lexington and Concord.
- Led the intellectual and moral vanguard of the American abolitionist movement prior to the [[american-civil-war]], serving as the publication home of *William Lloyd Garrison*'s newspaper *The Liberator* and organizing the famed African American 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment.
- Established in 1630 by Puritan governor *John Winthrop*, whose sermon "A Model of Christian Charity" exhorted the community to stand as an exemplary "City upon a Hill," quickly leading to the founding of *Boston Latin School* in 1635 and *Harvard College* in 1636.
- Greatly expanded its land area during the nineteenth century through a massive public works project that leveled Mount Vernon and surrounding hills to fill the marshy tidal flats of the *Back Bay*.
- Endured the bizarre 1919 *Great Molasses Flood*, when a poorly constructed two-million-gallon storage tank in the North End ruptured, sending a deadly twenty-five-foot wave of molasses surging through the streets at thirty-five miles per hour.

## connections

- [[american-revolution]] — served as the political cockpit where the resistance to imperial tax laws and the Siege of Boston began.
- [[george-washington]] — general who outflanked British occupiers by fortifying Dorchester Heights with ordnance hauled from Fort Ticonderoga.
- [[british-empire]] — overseas power that garrisoned regiments in the city and shuttered its commercial harbor with the Port Act of 1774.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose constitutional traditions, early federal diplomacy, and naval history were profoundly shaped by the city.
- [[england]] — country of origin for the Puritan nonconformists who settled the Massachusetts Bay Colony in 1630.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict fueled by the city's radical anti-slavery agitation, transcendentalist literature, and Union financial backing.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformative era bankrolled by Boston merchant capital, which established pioneering textile mills throughout New England.
