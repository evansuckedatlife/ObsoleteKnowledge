## summary

The **Balkan Wars** (1912–1913) were two regional conflicts in southeastern Europe that dismantled Ottoman territorial holdings and exposed great-power rivalries that would ignite *World War I*. The First Balkan War saw alliance of Balkan nations defeat the declining *Ottoman Empire*; the Second Balkan War erupted between former allies over territorial division. These wars destabilized the region, emboldened nationalist movements, and created rivalries between *Austria-Hungary* and *Russia* that would ultimately trigger European war.

## you gotta know

- The First Balkan War (1912–1913) united *Serbia*, *Bulgaria*, *Greece*, and *Montenegro* against the Ottoman Empire, which lost nearly all remaining European territories, retaining only a small area around Istanbul and the Straits.
- Ottoman weakness had become apparent throughout the 19th century, inviting both local nationalist movements and great-power intervention in the Balkans.
- *Bulgaria*, dissatisfied with territorial gains in the First War, attacked its former allies *Serbia* and *Greece* in the Second Balkan War (1913), but was defeated and lost additional territory.
- *Serbia* emerged as the dominant Balkan Slavic power, strengthening nationalist sentiment and rivaling *Austria-Hungary* for regional influence and control of Slavic-majority territories.
- Great powers competed for influence: *Russia* supported Balkan Slavic nations; *Austria-Hungary* opposed Serbian expansion as a threat to its multi-ethnic empire; *Germany* backed Austria-Hungary.
- The wars exposed the fragility of Ottoman power and the volatility of nationalist aspirations in the region, creating conditions where local conflicts could escalate to world war.
- *Austria-Hungary's* annexation of Bosnia-Herzegovina (1908) and the Balkan Wars reinforced Serbian nationalism and anti-Habsburg sentiment, culminating in the assassination of Archduke *Franz Ferdinand* in 1914.
- The regional instability and ethnic tensions ignited by the Balkan Wars directly contributed to *World War I's* outbreak and deeply shaped the conflict's ideological dimensions.
