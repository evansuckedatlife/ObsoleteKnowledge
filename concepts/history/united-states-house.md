## summary

The **House of Representatives** is the lower chamber of the U.S. Congress, established by Article I of the Constitution with 435 voting members apportioned among the states by population, serving two-year terms. It functions alongside the Senate as the legislative branch, with power to originate revenue bills, impeach federal officials, and elect the president in case of Electoral College deadlock. Historically, the House has been a crucial battleground for civil rights legislation and represents the principle of direct popular sovereignty through proportional state representation.

## you gotta know

- The House was designed to be more directly responsive to the people than the Senate, with shorter two-year terms and representation based on state population; even the smallest states must have at least one representative.
- The number of representatives was fixed at 435 in 1913, though this number can theoretically be changed by *Congress*; representation is reapportioned every ten years following the decennial census.
- The *Speaker of the House* is elected by members and is second in the line of presidential succession after the Vice President; the Speaker's role combines legislative leadership with ceremonial authority.
- The House has the power of the *purse*: all revenue bills must originate in the House (the *Origination Clause*), and it controls federal appropriations, making it powerful in budgetary disputes with the executive.
- The House holds the exclusive power of *impeachment*—bringing formal charges against federal officials, including the president; the Senate then conducts the trial and votes on removal.
- Major civil rights legislation of the 1960s—including the *Civil Rights Act* (1964) and the *Voting Rights Act* (1965)—passed through the House after intense parliamentary struggles; the House's composition reflected regional and partisan divisions that made these victories significant.
- House membership has included many figures who became national leaders or whose committee work advanced major policy initiatives; the House Judiciary Committee and House Ways and Means Committee are particularly influential.
