## summary

The **Fifteenth Amendment** (also known as the **15th Amendment**) to the [[united-states-constitution]] prohibits the federal government and state governments from denying or abridging a citizen's right to vote based on "race, color, or previous condition of servitude." Ratified in February 1870 during the tumultuous [[reconstruction-era]] in the aftermath of the [[american-civil-war]], it stands as the third and final of the foundational Reconstruction Amendments. While it formally enfranchised African American men and empowered Congress to pass protective enforcement legislation, southern jurisdictions soon systematically circumvented its guarantees through discriminatory mechanisms, rendering its promises largely unfulfilled until the [[civil-rights-movement]] of the mid-twentieth century.

## you gotta know

- Ratified in 1870 as the crowning constitutional achievement of Radical Republican legislation during the [[reconstruction-era]], completing the trilogy of postwar amendments that began with the abolition of slavery.
- Section 1 guarantees that the voting rights of citizens of the [[united-states]] shall not be denied or abridged on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude, while Section 2 grants Congress enforcement powers through appropriate legislation.
- Created an acrimonious schism within the women's suffrage movement, causing *Susan B. Anthony* and *Elizabeth Cady Stanton* to oppose ratification because it failed to enfranchise women, while *Lucy Stone* and *Frederick Douglass* supported it as an urgent priority for Black survival.
- Catalyzed immediate political participation across the South, enabling millions of freedmen to register and electing historic figures such as *Hiram Revels* and *Blanche K. Bruce* to the Senate alongside hundreds of state legislators.
- Severely curtailed in 1876 by the Supreme Court in *United States v. Reese*, which ruled that the amendment did not confer an absolute right of suffrage but only prevented discrimination on specified grounds, opening the door for poll taxes and literacy tests.
- Eviscerated in practice across the Jim Crow South through violent intimidation and legal subterfuges such as grandfather clauses, which the Supreme Court finally invalidated in the 1915 decision *Guinn v. United States*.
- Reinvigorated during the [[civil-rights-movement]] by the passage of the *Voting Rights Act of 1965*, whose aggressive federal oversight provisions were explicitly sustained by Chief Justice *Earl Warren* in *South Carolina v. Katzenbach* as a valid exercise of Section 2 enforcement power.

## connections

- [[14th-amendment]] — preceding Reconstruction amendment that granted citizenship and equal protection, laying the constitutional groundwork for federal protection of civil liberties.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the transformative historical epoch of federal occupation and southern political reorganization that produced the amendment.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme legal charter of the nation, altered by the amendment to constrain state authority over elections.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — mass twentieth-century struggle that successfully demanded the genuine enforcement of the voting protections promised nine decades earlier.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the cataclysmic conflict that destroyed chattel slavery and compelled the federal government to redefine the boundaries of national citizenship.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose constitutional balance of power shifted dramatically toward national oversight of the electoral franchise.
