## summary

**Crispus Attucks**, also known under the alias **Michael Johnson**, was an American dockworker, sailor, and formerly enslaved man of mixed African and Wampanoag Indigenous descent who became the first casualty of the American Revolution. He was shot and killed by British soldiers during the Boston Massacre on March 5, 1770, instantly transforming him into a martyr for the anti-colonial cause. His sacrifice was later reclaimed by nineteenth-century abolitionists and twentieth-century civil rights activists as enduring proof of Black patriotism and leadership in the founding of the nation.

## you gotta know

- Struck by two lead balls in the chest on King Street in front of the Boston Custom House during the Boston Massacre, dying instantly as the first of five colonists killed that night.
- Escaped from enslavement around 1750 from Framingham, Massachusetts, where his owner *William Browne* placed advertisements in *The Boston Gazette* offering ten pounds for the return of a twenty-seven-year-old mixed-race man named "Crispus."
- Worked for two decades as a merchant seaman, whaler, and ropewalk dockworker, adopting the alias **Michael Johnson** to protect himself against recapture and kidnapping by British naval press gangs.
- Stood at the forefront of the angry confrontation on March 5, 1770, armed with a heavy cordwood stick or club, confronting soldiers of the British 29th Regiment commanded by Captain *Thomas Preston*.
- Vilified during the subsequent murder trial by defense attorney *John Adams*, who famously argued the soldiers acted in self-defense against a "motley rabble of saucy boys, negroes and mulattoes, Irish teagues and outlandish jack tars," singling out Attucks as the chief instigator.
- Buried alongside the other massacre victims (*Samuel Gray*, *James Caldwell*, *Samuel Maverick*, and *Patrick Carr*) in a shared grave at the Granary Burying Ground on Tremont Street in Boston.
- Omitted visually or depicted as indistinguishably white in *Paul Revere*'s iconic propaganda engraving *The Bloody Massacre*, an artistic erasure designed to appeal to conservative colonial prejudices.
- Reclaimed as a national hero by Black abolitionist *William Cooper Nell* in the 1850s, who established an annual "Crispus Attucks Day" in Boston and celebrated him as the earliest martyr of American liberty.
