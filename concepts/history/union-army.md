## summary

The **Union Army** was the military force of the Northern states during the American Civil War (1861–1865), fighting to preserve the federal union and, after 1863, to end slavery. Though initially ill-prepared and poorly led, the Union Army's superior numbers, industrial capacity, and eventually superior generalship under *Ulysses S. Grant* and *William Tecumseh Sherman* proved decisive. By war's end, it had grown from a small professional force into a mass conscript army of over a million men, remaking the conduct of warfare through coordinated campaigns and total war strategies.

## you gotta know

- The Union Army inherited the regular army structure of the pre-war United States, with many Confederate officers departing to serve the South, leaving the North initially understaffed in command.
- Early campaigns saw repeated Union defeats and retreats, particularly at *First Bull Run* (1861), which shattered Northern illusions of quick victory and forced massive mobilization and reorganization.
- The Army of the Potomac, the Union's primary force in the East under commanders like *George McClellan*, *Joseph Hooker*, and finally *George Meade*, struggled against *Robert E. Lee*'s Army of Northern Virginia until decisive victory at *Gettysburg* (1863).
- The Western Theater saw Union generals like *Ulysses S. Grant*, *William Tecumseh Sherman*, and *George Henry Thomas* achieve earlier strategic success by controlling the Mississippi River and splitting the Confederacy.
- *Grant*, appointed supreme commander in 1864, coordinated simultaneous offensives across multiple theaters, exploiting the Union's numerical superiority and denying the Confederacy the ability to concentrate forces.
- *Sherman's March to the Sea* (1864–1865) pioneered total war—burning infrastructure, destroying supply lines, and breaking civilian morale—strategies that modernized military practice.
- By war's end, the Union Army had suffered approximately 360,000 combat deaths and a further 225,000 from disease, yet replaced casualties with fresh conscripts; the Confederacy could not.
- The Union Army's postwar occupation of the South during [[reconstruction-era]] involved both humanitarian efforts and military enforcement of new civil rights, though these efforts faced fierce white Southern resistance.
