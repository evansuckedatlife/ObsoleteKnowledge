## summary

The **First Battle of Bull Run** (July 21, 1861), also called the *First Battle of Manassas*, was the first major military engagement of the American Civil War, fought near Manassas, Virginia. Though it ended in Confederate victory, it shattered Northern hopes for a swift, decisive victory and signaled that the war would be prolonged and costly.

## you gotta know

- Fought on July 21, 1861, less than three months after the firing on *Fort Sumter*, representing the Union's first major offensive operation of the war.
- Northern commanders, confident of victory and under political pressure to act, marched into Virginia expecting to overwhelm Confederate forces and recapture the strategic interior quickly.
- President *Lincoln* believed a swift victory would demoralize the South and end the rebellion, while Northern newspapers proclaimed a picnic-like atmosphere and easy triumph.
- Confederate forces, led by *P.G.T. Beauregard* and reinforced by *Joseph Johnston*'s army from the Shenandoah Valley, were well-positioned to defend the ground and staged an effective defense.
- *Thomas Jackson*'s unflinching stand on Henry House Hill earned him the nickname "Stonewall," becoming one of the war's great myths and establishing him as a military legend.
- Union forces initially seemed to be winning in the morning, flanking Confederate left flank, but Confederate reinforcements arrived at the critical moment via the Manassas Gap Railroad.
- Union forces, including many civilian observers who brought picnic baskets expecting an easy spectacle, were routed and retreated chaotically back toward Washington, shocking the North with the reality of warfare.
- Casualties were modest by later standards (approximately 600 killed from both sides) but striking to a nation unprepared for sustained warfare and the scale of modern combat.
- Demonstrated that the Confederacy was capable of serious military resistance and killed any illusion of a ninety-day war, forcing the North to prepare for prolonged conflict.
