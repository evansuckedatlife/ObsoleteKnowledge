## summary

**Ancient India** encompasses the civilizations, kingdoms, and cultural achievements of the **Indian subcontinent** from the *Indus Valley* settlements (~2600–1900 BCE) through the Mughal conquest (16th century), a span in which India developed sophisticated urban planning, philosophical schools, mathematical innovations, and religious systems that profoundly shaped world thought. The *Mauryan Empire* under *Ashoka* (3rd century BCE) unified much of the subcontinent and spread *Buddhism* across Asia; Indian mathematics introduced zero and the decimal system; Sanskrit literary tradition produced epics and drama of unparalleled richness.

## you gotta know

- The *Indus Valley Civilization* (c. 2600–1900 BCE) built planned cities like *Mohenjo-daro* with drainage systems and undeciphered script, rivaling Egypt and Mesopotamia in sophistication.
- *Vedic period* (c. 1500–500 BCE) saw the composition of the *Vedas*, Hinduism's oldest texts, which established caste hierarchy, ritual practice, and philosophical foundations still central to Indian culture.
- *Chandragupta Maurya* (c. 322–298 BCE) founded the *Mauryan Empire*, India's first great empire, centralizing administration and military power across the subcontinent.
- *Ashoka* (r. 268–232 BCE) ruled the Mauryan Empire at its height and became the first major royal patron of *Buddhism*, spreading it throughout Asia via missionary monks.
- Indian mathematicians invented *zero* and the decimal system (c. 5th century CE), enabling sophisticated calculations and eventually transmitted to the Islamic world and Europe.
- *Panini* (c. 4th century BCE) systematized Sanskrit grammar with such rigor that his work remains the gold standard for linguistic analysis.
- *Sanskrit* literature produced the great epics *Mahabharata* and *Ramayana*, philosophically rich dramas by *Kalidasa*, and Upanishads exploring metaphysics and consciousness.

## connections

- [[chandragupta-maurya]] — founder of the *Mauryan Empire* and architect of India's first imperial unification.
- [[ashoka]] — Mauryan emperor who embraced Buddhism and spread it across Asia, transforming religion and statecraft.
- [[mauryan-empire]] — India's first great empire, unifying the subcontinent under centralized rule.
- [[vedas]] — the foundational texts of *Hinduism* and the philosophical/liturgical core of Indian civilization.
- [[hinduism]] — the religious and philosophical tradition emerging from Vedic roots and dominating Indian culture.
- [[buddhism]] — religion founded in India that spread to Asia; patronized by Ashoka.
- [[sanskrit]] — the literary and ritual language of India, systematized by *Panini*.
- [[panini]] — the grammarian whose analysis of Sanskrit set standards for linguistic study.
