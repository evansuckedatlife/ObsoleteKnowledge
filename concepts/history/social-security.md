## summary

**Social Security** is a landmark federal social insurance program in the United States, established under the **Social Security Act** signed into law by President *Franklin D. Roosevelt* on August 14, 1935. Enacted as the legislative centerpiece of the Second *New Deal* during the economic devastation of the Great Depression, the system created a permanent safety net comprising old-age retirement pensions, joint federal-state unemployment insurance, and direct financial assistance for dependent children, the blind, and maternal health. By shifting the primary responsibility for social welfare from private charities and municipal governments to the federal level, the program established the cornerstone of the modern American welfare state and remains one of the most influential and widely debated domestic institutions in national history.

## you gotta know

- Signed into law on August 14, 1935, by President *Franklin D. Roosevelt* as the crowning achievement of the Second *New Deal*, creating guaranteed federal retirement benefits to alleviate widespread elder poverty during the *Great Depression*.
- Drafted under the direction of Secretary of Labor *Frances Perkins*, the first female cabinet secretary in American history, who chaired the *Committee on Economic Security* (*CES*) appointed by executive order in June 1934.
- Propelled through the legislative process by intense grassroots pressure from rival populist proposals, most notably California physician *Francis Townsend*'s proposed *Townsend Plan* of revolving monthly pensions and Louisiana Senator *Huey Long*'s radical *Share Our Wealth* movement.
- Structured as a compulsory self-funded social insurance program rather than a means-tested dole, financed through dedicated, equal payroll taxes levied on employers and employees under Title VIII, later codified as the *Federal Insurance Contributions Act* (*FICA*).
- Compromised early coverage by excluding agricultural laborers and domestic servants to overcome the resistance of powerful Southern Democratic committee chairs, effectively denying initial protection to more than sixty percent of African American workers nationwide.
- Survived severe constitutional challenges in May 1937 when the Supreme Court upheld the validity of federal payroll taxes and old-age benefits under Article I's General Welfare Clause in *Helvering v. Davis* and *Steward Machine Co. v. Davis*.
- Issued the first recurring monthly retirement benefit check on January 31, 1940, to *Ida May Fuller*, a retired legal secretary from Ludlow, Vermont, who received check number 00-000-001 for $22.54 after contributing only $24.75 in payroll taxes.
- Expanded significantly through later amendments administered by the *Social Security Administration*, adding dependent and survivor benefits in 1939, disability insurance (*SSDI*) under President *Dwight D. Eisenhower* in 1956, and automatic cost-of-living adjustments (*COLA*) pegged to inflation in 1972.

## connections

- [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] — the Democratic president who championed the legislation as the defining domestic achievement of his administration.
- [[great-depression]] — the catastrophic economic collapse that erased private savings, impoverished elderly citizens, and catalyzed national demand for structural social safety nets.
- [[united-states-congress]] — the legislative branch whose committees shaped the bill and negotiated controversial exclusions before passing it with overwhelming bipartisan majorities.
- [[executive-power]] — expanded significantly as the federal government assumed direct administrative authority over nationwide economic entitlements.
- [[works-progress-administration]] — complementary relief agency created in 1935 that supplied immediate emergency public employment while long-term pension structures were organized.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — provided the legal battleground where the General Welfare Clause was successfully invoked to affirm federal spending for social insurance.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformed an agrarian society into an urban wage-dependent economy, rendering elderly industrial laborers vulnerable to sudden destitution.
