## summary

The **Governor**, or **State Governor**, is the chief executive officer of a subnational state or territory, charged with administering civil law, commanding state militia forces, and overseeing the state bureaucratic apparatus. Within the federal system of the [[united-states]], the office serves as the constitutional counterweight to the state legislature, possessing varying degrees of line-item veto authority, executive clemency, and appointment powers. Historically evolving from appointed crown representatives under British [[colonialism]], the modern governorship has frequently served as a vital proving ground for national leadership and civil rights battles.

## you gotta know

- *P.B.S. Pinchback* of *Louisiana* became the first African American state executive in American history, serving as acting chief executive for thirty-five days during the [[reconstruction-era]] following the impeachment of *Henry Clay Warmoth*.
- Historically derived from colonial crown executives appointed by the monarchy, many of whom faced popular revolt such as *Sir Edmund Andros*, who was overthrown in Boston following the [[glorious-revolution]] in [[england]].
- Under early post-independence state constitutions, the office was deliberately weakened and subordinated to state legislatures due to widespread republican fears of executive tyranny sparked by royal rule.
- Possesses executive tools unique among American executives, including the *line-item veto* in forty-four states, which allows the rejection of specific budget appropriations without vetoing entire legislative bills.
- In 1989, *Douglas Wilder* of Virginia made history as the first African American candidate elected to the office by popular vote, while *Nellie Tayloe Ross* of Wyoming became the first woman to serve in 1925.
- The office served as a battleground during the [[civil-rights-movement]], as segregationist incumbents like *Orval Faubus* of Arkansas and *George Wallace* of Alabama resisted federal desegregation orders.
- Seventeen occupants of the office have advanced to become President of the [[united-states]], including *Thomas Jefferson*, *Franklin D. Roosevelt*, *Ronald Reagan*, and *Bill Clinton*.

## connections

- [[reconstruction-era]] — the postwar rebuilding period when Black political participation crested with *P.B.S. Pinchback*'s historic tenure in Louisiana.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose constitutional structure divides sovereign police and administrative powers between federal authorities and individual state executives.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme legal framework that reserves broad un-enumerated police powers to state executives under the Tenth Amendment.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional amendment whose equal protection clause empowered federal courts to constrain state executive authority over civil rights.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict in which northern and southern state executives managed critical logistics, conscription, and mobilization for both armies.
- [[glorious-revolution]] — the English parliamentary coup that inspired American colonists to depose autocratic royal executives across Massachusetts and New York.
- [[colonialism]] — the imperial administrative model from which the governorship originally emerged as an appointed royal vice-regal office.
