## summary

**Radio**, or **broadcast radio**, was the premier electronic mass medium of the early and mid-twentieth century, fundamentally reshaping global society by transmitting sound wirelessly into private living rooms. Emerging from late nineteenth-century experiments in wireless telegraphy and reaching cultural ubiquity during the interwar era, it dismantled regional isolation and created the first truly simultaneous national audiences. By delivering spoken journalism, symphonic performances, variety entertainment, and corporate advertising directly into domestic spaces, it transformed political statecraft, fueled the rise of consumer culture, and laid the institutional foundations for modern broadcast media.

## you gotta know

- *Franklin D. Roosevelt* famously bypassed hostile newspaper publishers by using his intimate *Fireside Chats* to speak directly to millions of citizens during the Great Depression and [[world-war-ii]].
- *Orson Welles* and the *Mercury Theatre on the Air* caused widespread panic and media sensation on Halloween in 1938 with a realistic mock-newscast adaptation of *H. G. Wells*'s science-fiction novel *The War of the Worlds*.
- Commercial broadcasting exploded across the [[united-states]] following the historic 1920 broadcast of presidential election results by station *KDKA* in Pittsburgh, which demonstrated the medium's unmatched immediacy.
- The medium catalyzed the commercial popularity of [[jazz]], big band swing, and country music, transforming localized musical traditions into national phenomena via live nightclub transmissions and barn dance programs.
- The emergence of private network giants like the *National Broadcasting Company* (*NBC*) and the *Columbia Broadcasting System* (*CBS*) established toll broadcasting, sponsor-backed programming, and serial soap operas as the economic foundation of twentieth-century entertainment.
- During the London Blitz, CBS correspondent *Edward R. Murrow* captivated foreign listeners with vivid, rooftop eyewitness reports that began with his trademark phrase, *This is London*, helping build American sympathy for [[england]].
- Italian inventor *Guglielmo Marconi* developed the first commercially viable wireless telegraphy apparatus in the 1890s, while Canadian engineer *Reginald Fessenden* and American inventor *Lee de Forest*—who invented the *Audion* vacuum tube—pioneered voice transmission.
- The *Radio Act of 1927* established the *Federal Radio Commission* (*FRC*) and codified the enduring legal principle that the electromagnetic spectrum is a scarce public resource, meaning broadcasters must operate in the public interest, convenience, and necessity.

## connections

- [[television]] — the visual successor medium that supplanted audio drama in the 1950s while directly adopting its corporate networks, sponsorship models, and star talent.
- [[jazz]] — the iconic American musical style whose syncopated rhythms and big band arrangements achieved mainstream cultural dominance through nationwide live broadcast remotes.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global struggle where wireless airwaves served as a critical psychological theater for home-front morale, Axis propaganda broadcasts, and live war reporting.
- [[silent-film]] — the dominant visual art form of the 1920s that faced fierce economic competition from free domestic audio programming before sound technology converged across both media.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the technological and manufacturing transformation that created mass-market consumer electronics, electrical grid expansion, and precision vacuum tubes.
- [[united-states]] — the nation that pioneered corporate commercial network broadcasting funded by advertising rather than government-administered license fees.
- [[modernism]] — the artistic and intellectual movement that reflected the altered sensory experience of space, time, and mass communication brought about by wireless signals.
- [[new-york-city]] — the primary headquarters of major broadcasting networks and talent agencies, anchoring national cultural production from facilities like *Radio City Music Hall*.
