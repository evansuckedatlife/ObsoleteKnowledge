## summary

The **Estates-General** (in French, the **États généraux**) was a representative legislative assembly of the three estates of the realm in *France*, first convoked in 1302 by King *Philip IV* and famously summoned in May 1789 by King *Louis XVI* to resolve an impending national bankruptcy. Dividing the kingdom into the First Estate (the clergy), the Second Estate (the nobility), and the Third Estate (the commoners representing roughly ninety-eight percent of the population), the assembly operated on an antiquated voting system where each order cast one collective ballot. When the Crown refused to grant voting by head, delegates of the Third Estate revolted, transforming the meeting into the *National Assembly* and initiating the [[french-revolution]].

## you gotta know

- Summoned to convene at *Versailles* on May 5, 1789, marking the first time the body had assembled since 1614 under *Marie de' Medici* and *Louis XIII*.
- Convoked by finance minister *Jacques Necker* and King *Louis XVI* to address catastrophic sovereign debt caused by French involvement in the [[american-revolution]] and systemic fiscal exemptions for privileged elites.
- The Third Estate, led by radical delegates and enlightened thinkers like *Emmanuel-Joseph Sieyès*, demanded "doubling of the Third" and voting *par tête* (by head) rather than *par ordre* (by order).
- Sparked nationwide political mobilization through the drafting of *cahiers de doléances* (books of grievances), in which communities across [[france]] cataloged local complaints and demands for institutional reform.
- Impasse over verification of credentials culminated on June 17, 1789, when the Third Estate, joined by sympathetic parish priests, declared itself the sovereign *National Assembly*.
- Locked out of their meeting hall at the *Hôtel des Menus-Plaisirs* on June 20, the delegates relocated to a nearby indoor court to swear the *Tennis Court Oath*, pledging not to disband until drafting a constitution.
- King *Louis XVI* attempted to assert royal authority during the *séance royale* on June 23, but deputy *Honoré Gabriel Riqueti, comte de Mirabeau* famously proclaimed they would not leave except by the force of bayonets.
- The capitulation of the monarchy and union of all three orders on June 27 effectively dismantled the *Ancien Régime* and established a constitutional legislature, directly precipitating the storming of the Bastille.

## connections

- [[french-revolution]] — the cataclysmic political upheaval sparked directly by the assembly's collapse.
- [[france]] — the kingdom transformed from an absolute monarchy into a constitutional and revolutionary state.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement whose concepts of popular sovereignty and natural rights animated the Third Estate.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the military commander whose subsequent rise was made possible by the destruction of the old social orders.
- [[american-revolution]] — the overseas conflict whose financial costs pushed royal coffers into the insolvency that forced the assembly's convocation.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — the American diplomat resident in Paris who witnessed the assembly and advised revolutionary leaders drafting reforms.
