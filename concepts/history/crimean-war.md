## summary

The **Crimean War** (1853–1856) was a conflict between the Russian Empire and an alliance of the Ottoman Empire, Britain, France, and Sardinia. Fought primarily on and around the Crimean Peninsula, it halted Russian expansion into Ottoman territories and marked a turning point in 19th-century geopolitics. The war's brutal trench warfare and technological innovations previewed the industrial carnage of the 20th century.

## you gotta know

- Began as a dispute over control of Orthodox holy sites in Palestine, with Russia claiming a protective role over Ottoman Christians.
- The *Siege of Sevastopol* (1854–1855) became the war's central episode—a grueling ten-month struggle that devastated both sides.
- Famously associated with *Florence Nightingale*, whose hospital reforms during the war pioneered modern nursing and statistics-driven healthcare.
- The Western alliance (particularly Britain and France) fought to prevent Russian domination of the Mediterranean and the Balkans.
- The *Treaty of Paris* (1856) stripped Russia of territorial gains and neutralized the Black Sea, a humiliating defeat for Nicholas I.
- Military innovations—ironclads, rifled muskets, trench warfare, and field telegraph—foreshadowed the mechanized conflicts to come.
- The conflict exposed the decay of the Ottoman Empire and accelerated Russian internal reform under Alexander II.
- Approximately 600,000 soldiers died, with disease and poor logistics accounting for the majority of casualties rather than combat itself.
- The war marked the beginning of the "Eastern Question"—a century-long diplomatic crisis over how the declining Ottoman Empire's European territories would be partitioned among Great Powers.

## connections

- [[ottoman-empire]] — the declining power Russia sought to partition, defended by Western allies.
- [[russian-empire]] — sought to expand southward; the war thwarted those ambitions for decades.
- [[british-empire]] — joined the alliance to preserve the balance of power and block Russian encroachment.
- [[france]] — fought alongside Britain to contain Russian territorial ambition in Europe.
- [[nicholas-i]] — the Russian autocrat whose expansionist policies triggered the conflict; he died before its end.
- [[alexander-ii]] — inherited the war, negotiated its settlement, and launched reforms in response to Russia's failure.
- [[henry-john-temple-duke-of-palmerston]] — the British Foreign Secretary whose interventionist strategy shaped the war's course.
