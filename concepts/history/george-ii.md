## summary

**George II**, born *George Augustus*, reigned as King of Great Britain and Ireland, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, and Prince-elector of the Holy Roman Empire from 1727 until his death in 1760. As the second Hanoverian monarch, he presided over an era of profound political stabilization, imperial expansion, and commercial growth orchestrated largely by parliamentary leaders like *Sir Robert Walpole*. He holds enduring renown as the last British monarch to lead troops in person on the field of battle during the War of the Austrian Succession.

## you gotta know

- Fought in person at the Battle of Dettingen in 1743 during the War of the Austrian Succession, cementing his legacy as the last reigning British sovereign to command troops directly under enemy fire.
- Maintained a famously stormy relationship with his father, *George I*, establishing a rival political court at Leicester House that became a gathering point for parliamentary opposition factions.
- Exercised political stability largely through the counsel of his intelligent and politically astute consort, *Caroline of Ansbach*, who persuaded him to retain *Sir Robert Walpole* as chief minister.
- Survived the major Jacobite Rising of 1745, when Charles Edward Stuart launched an invasion of [[england]] that was decisively crushed at the Battle of Culloden by the king's younger son, Prince William, Duke of Cumberland.
- Reigned during the global escalation of the Seven Years' War, witnessing the remarkable *Annus Mirabilis* of 1759 in which British forces scored sweeping victories across Europe, North America, and India.
- Maintained deep emotional and strategic ties to his ancestral electorate of Hanover in [[germany]], frequently shaping British diplomacy to protect continental interests and drawing persistent parliamentary criticism.
- Supported cultural patronage, enthusiastically endorsing George Frideric Handel, who composed the coronation anthem *Zadok the Priest* for his accession and the *Music for the Royal Fireworks* under royal commission.

## connections

- [[house-of-hanover]] — the royal dynasty that inherited the throne under the [[act-of-settlement]].
- [[robert-walpole]] — Britain's first recognized prime minister, who managed domestic affairs and royal finances throughout the early reign.
- [[england]] — the central kingdom of the realm whose parliamentary system consolidated power over royal prerogative.
- [[british-empire]] — the overseas imperial domain that expanded dramatically into India and North America during his reign.
- [[france]] — the primary geopolitical rival faced across the War of the Austrian Succession and the Seven Years' War.
- [[germany]] — the homeland of his hereditary electorate of Hanover, which deeply occupied his personal diplomatic focus.
