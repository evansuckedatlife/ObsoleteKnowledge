## summary

The **Battle of Guilford Court House** was a pivotal engagement of the Southern theater of the *[[american-revolution]]*, fought on March 15, 1781, at modern-day Greensboro, North Carolina. Major General *Nathanael Greene* deployed his Continental regulars and regional militia in three successive defensive lines against the advancing redcoats under Lieutenant General *Charles Cornwallis*. Although British troops secured tactical possession of the battlefield, the catastrophic casualties they suffered broke their strategic momentum in the Carolinas and set the stage for their surrender at the *[[battle-of-yorktown]]*.

## you gotta know

- Fought on March 15, 1781, between roughly 4,400 American troops under Major General *Nathanael Greene* and approximately 1,900 veteran British and Hessian regulars under Lieutenant General *Charles Cornwallis*.
- *Nathanael Greene* structured his defense on the tactical model created by *Daniel Morgan* at the *Battle of Cowpens*, arranging his army into three staggered defensive lines through dense timber and open fields to bleed the enemy advance.
- The first line consisted of North Carolina militia positioned behind a rail fence with orders to fire two effective volleys before retreating, though many panicked under British bayonet charges.
- The second line of Virginia militia under *Edward Stevens* and *Robert Lawson* mounted a bitter, prolonged resistance in dense woods that broke the cohesion of the attacking British columns.
- The third line comprised veteran Continental regulars from Maryland and Delaware, who delivered devastating musket fire and engaged in vicious hand-to-hand bayonet combat against the British Foot Guards.
- *Charles Cornwallis* infamously ordered his artillery to fire two three-pounder cannons loaded with grape shot directly into the melee of intertwined British Guards and Continental soldiers to halt the American counterattack.
- *Nathanael Greene* executed an orderly withdrawal with his core forces intact, while the British retained the field but lost over a quarter of their entire fighting force, leading Whig politician *Charles James Fox* to remark in the House of Commons that "another such victory would ruin the British army."
- The staggering attrition forced *Charles Cornwallis* to abandon his Carolina campaign, withdraw his battered troops to Wilmington to refit, and march north into Virginia, where he was ultimately trapped and besieged.
