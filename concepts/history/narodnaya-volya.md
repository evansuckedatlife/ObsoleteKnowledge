## summary

**Narodnaya Volya** (The People's Will) was a radical Russian revolutionary organization active in the 1870s–1880s that pioneered modern political assassination as a strategy to topple the Tsarist autocracy. Believing that targeted violence against state leaders would inspire mass uprising, they launched systematic attacks on high officials culminating in the 1881 assassination of Tsar Alexander II. Though the group was suppressed, it established a template for terrorism that would influence revolutionary movements worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Emerged from the populist movement's disillusionment with the possibility of peaceful reform or peasant-led revolution, pivoting toward elite-targeted violence.
- Developed an underground cell structure, secret printing presses, and systematic planning methods that became models for later revolutionary organizations.
- Conducted successful assassinations of Alexander II (1881), other officials, and police, treating terrorism as both a tactical necessity and a moral statement against autocratic oppression.
- Believed that the elimination of key state figures would either force democratic concessions or trigger the social collapse necessary for revolution—a theory proven wrong when the regime simply hardened repression.
- Used a mix of strategies: propaganda, armed robbery to finance operations, bomb-making, and political assassinations, representing an early comprehensive revolutionary toolkit.
- Suppressed after Alexander III's accession; survivors either fled into exile or were absorbed into Marxist socialism, which rejected their individualist terror strategy in favor of mass-based revolution.
