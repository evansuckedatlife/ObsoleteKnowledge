## summary

The **Electoral College** is the system by which Americans indirectly elect the president through state-based electors, rather than through a direct popular vote. Established by the *Constitution* in 1787 as a compromise between small and large states, it distributes electoral power according to representation in Congress. The College has produced periodic crises and remains one of American democracy's most debated institutions.

## you gotta know

- Each state receives a number of electors equal to its total representation in Congress (Senate seats plus House seats), currently totaling 538.
- To win the presidency, a candidate must secure an absolute majority: 270 electoral votes out of 538.
- In most states, electors are bound by law or party loyalty to vote for the candidate who won the popular vote in that state (*winner-take-all*).
- The College emerged from the Constitutional Convention's compromise between states seeking direct popular election and those wanting to protect state power.
- Critics argue that it gives disproportionate power to small states and swing states, while rendering voter preference in safe states irrelevant.
- The *Election of 1824* produced a constitutional crisis when no candidate won an electoral majority; the House decided the election, sparking charges of corruption.
- The *Election of 2000* saw *George W. Bush* win the presidency despite losing the national popular vote, reigniting demands for electoral reform.
- The "faithless elector" problem—electors voting contrary to state results or their pledges—has recurred sporadically but rarely changed outcomes due to the College's structural inefficiency.
- Small states enjoy disproportionate power; Wyoming, with 0.2% of the population, has roughly 1.8% of electoral votes, while California's electoral power per capita is reduced by the winner-take-all system.
- Proposed reforms range from abolishing the Electoral College entirely (requiring constitutional amendment, an exceedingly high bar) to adopting the National Popular Vote Interstate Compact, which would allocate votes to the national popular winner without amending the Constitution.
