## summary

**Chan Buddhism** is a major school of Mahayana *[[buddhism]]* that originated in *[[china]]* during the sixth century CE through the synthesis of Indian dhyāna (meditation) practices with Daoist natural philosophy. Rejecting mere reliance on scriptural scholasticism in favor of direct realization of one's own Buddha-nature, it emphasizes seated meditation, non-dual awareness, and master-to-disciple lineage transmissions. Chan spread across East Asia, establishing itself in Vietnam as Thiền, in Korea as Seon, and in *[[japan]]* as Zen, where it deeply shaped visual arts, literature, and monastic lifestyle.

## you gotta know

- Traditionally founded by the Indian monk *Bodhidharma*, the legendary first patriarch who arrived in southern *[[china]]* and reputedly spent nine years meditating facing a wall at the *Shaolin* Monastery.
- The fundamental ethos of the school is captured in the classic four-line verse: "A special transmission outside the scriptures; not founded upon words and letters; pointing directly to the human mind; seeing into one's nature and attaining Buddhahood."
- *Huineng*, the Sixth Patriarch, consolidated Southern Chan's doctrine of sudden enlightenment (*dunwu*), an egalitarian perspective detailed in the foundational text *The Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch*.
- The tradition developed distinctive pedagogical methods to shatter conceptual thinking, including *gong'an* (known in Japanese as *koan*, paradoxical riddles or dialogues) and startling physical acts by masters like shouting or striking.
- Divided into prominent rival traditions during the *Song* dynasty, most notably the *Linji* (Japanese *Rinzai*) school favoring dramatic koan contemplation and the *Caodong* (Japanese *Soto*) school emphasizing quiet sitting meditation (*zuochan* or *shikantaza*).
- Integrated deeply with everyday physical labor under the monastic rule established by *Baizhang Huaihai*, famous for the maxim: "A day without work is a day without food."
- Heavily influenced East Asian secular arts, cultivating minimalist aesthetic principles that informed ink-wash painting, calligraphic spontaneity, gardens, and rustic *[[ceramic-art]]* vessels.

## connections

- [[buddhism]] — the Indian philosophical and spiritual religion from which Chan arose as an East Asian Mahayana development.
- [[chinese-culture]] — the civilizational environment whose Daoist concepts of spontaneity and emptiness reshaped Indian Buddhist meditation.
- [[china]] — the geographic homeland where Chan patriarchs established their monastic centers and lineages.
- [[japan]] — the country where Chan took root as Zen through figures like Eisai and Dogen, transforming samurai ethics and aesthetics.
- [[ceramic-art]] — the craftsmanship discipline that produced austere, asymmetrical tea bowls and vessels revered by Zen practitioners.
- [[poetry]] — the literary medium frequently used by Chan hermits and monks, such as *Hanshan* (Cold Mountain), to articulate spiritual illumination.
- [[existentialism]] — the modern Western philosophical movement exploring subjectivity and being, often compared by scholars to Zen views on direct experience.
