## summary

The **Confederate Army** was the military force of the *Confederate States of America* during the American Civil War (1861–1865), consisting primarily of volunteers and conscripts from Southern states. Led by experienced officers and organized into regional armies, the Confederate forces achieved remarkable tactical victories and inflicted severe casualties despite being outnumbered and outmatched in industrial capacity and resources. Though militarily formidable under commanders like *Robert E. Lee*, the Confederacy ultimately could not sustain a prolonged war against the North's superior manpower and manufacturing capabilities.

## you gotta know

- Formally established in March 1861 as the military arm of the Confederacy; initially comprised state militias and volunteer units before transitioning to a conscript army as the war dragged on.
- Organized around regional armies: the *Army of Northern Virginia* (Lee's force, the most celebrated), the *Army of Tennessee*, the *Army of the Mississippi*, and smaller regional commands; maintained a decentralized command structure reflecting the Confederacy's weak federal authority.
- Benefited from the military experience of many officers who had served in the Mexican-American War or as career U.S. Army officers; leaders like Robert E. Lee brought institutional knowledge and battlefield proficiency.
- Achieved significant tactical victories early in the war, most notably at the *First Battle of Bull Run* (July 1861), which shattered Northern expectations of a quick victory and demonstrated Southern military competence.
- Relied heavily on interior lines of communication and the defensive advantage of fighting on home territory; conducted brilliant tactical maneuvers and held positions despite being outnumbered, particularly in Virginia and Tennessee.
- Suffered from chronic supply shortages, lack of industrial capacity to replace losses, inflation that eroded soldiers' pay, and declining morale as the war progressed and Union armies penetrated deeper into the South.
- Introduced conscription in 1862 (the first U.S. military draft), which caused resentment among conscripts and highlighted the Confederacy's desperation as volunteer recruitment dried up.
- Surrendered in phases between April and May 1865 as Union armies pressed from multiple directions; Robert E. Lee surrendered at *Appomattox Court House* in April 1865, effectively ending organized Confederate resistance.
