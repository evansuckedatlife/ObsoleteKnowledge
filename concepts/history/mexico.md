## summary

**Mexico** is a nation in North America with a history spanning pre-Columbian civilizations, Spanish colonial rule, independence, territorial conflict with the United States, and modern nationhood. The site of the Aztec Empire and later Spain's wealthiest American colony, Mexico inherited vast indigenous populations, Spanish culture, and Catholic religion. Its relationship with the United States—defined by the Mexican-American War, territorial loss, and ongoing regional dynamics—profoundly shaped both nations' development.

## you gotta know

- Home to advanced pre-Columbian civilizations, particularly the Aztec Empire centered in Tenochtitlan, which Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés conquered in 1521.
- Became Spain's most valuable American colony as New Spain, producing vast quantities of silver that enriched Spain and fueled global commerce for three centuries.
- Remained primarily agricultural and hierarchical under colonial rule, with Spanish, indigenous, and African populations creating a complex racial and social order.
- Gained independence from Spain in 1821 after a decade of wars, initially as an empire under Agustín de Iturbide, then as a republic.
- Lost half its territory to the United States through the Mexican-American War (1846-1848) and the subsequent Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, which transferred the Southwest to the U.S.
- Underwent violent revolution from 1910 to 1920, resulting in agrarian reform, constitutional changes, and a more redistributive political order.
- Developed a distinctive culture blending indigenous, Spanish, and modern influences, including Day of the Dead, muralism, and literary traditions that gained international significance.
