## summary

The **Twelfth Amendment** (or **12th Amendment**) to the [[united-states-constitution]] fundamentally restructured the American Electoral College by requiring electors to cast distinct, separate ballots for president and vice president. Ratified in *1804* in direct response to the bitter partisan deadlocks of the *1796* and *1800* presidential elections, it replaced the original framework under which the runner-up in presidential balloting automatically became vice president. By establishing unified party tickets and refining contingent election procedures in Congress, the amendment adapted the executive branch of the [[united-states]] to the emerging reality of national two-party politics.

## you gotta know

- Replaced the original Article II electoral mechanism where electors cast two votes for president, mandating instead that electors vote on *distinct ballots* specifically naming their choice for president and vice president.
- Prompted directly by the contentious election of *1800*, in which Democratic-Republicans [[thomas-jefferson]] and *Aaron Burr* tied in the Electoral College, precipitating a constitutional impasse that required thirty-six ballots in the House of Representatives to break.
- Prevented awkward bipartisan administrations like that produced in *1796*, when Federalist *John Adams* won the presidency while his foremost rival, Democratic-Republican [[thomas-jefferson]], finished second and became vice president.
- Altered contingent election protocols in the House of Representatives if no presidential candidate achieves an electoral majority, narrowing the field of candidates from the top five to the top three, with each state delegation possessing a single vote.
- Granted the Senate exclusive authority to select the vice president from the top two candidates if no vice-presidential contender obtains an outright electoral majority, requiring a two-thirds quorum and an absolute majority of senators.
- Mandated that vice presidents satisfy identical constitutional qualifications to presidents, ensuring that no person constitutionally ineligible to the presidency can serve as vice president.
- Decided the disputed election of *1824*, commonly remembered as the *Corrupt Bargain*, where the House of Representatives chose *John Quincy Adams* over *Andrew Jackson*, despite Jackson winning pluralities in both the popular and electoral votes.
- Invoked only once for a vice-presidential contingent election in *1837*, when the Senate elected *Richard Mentor Johnson* after Virginia's electors refused to back him over his open domestic relationship with the enslaved woman *Julia Chinn*.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme charter amended in 1804 to fix the defective executive election mechanisms set forth in Article II.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — the third president whose tied 1800 contest against his own running mate exposed the perils of unseparated balloting.
- [[george-washington]] — the first president whose unanimous selections initially obscured the structural instability inherent in the original electoral system.
- [[14th-amendment]] — subsequent constitutional amendment that updated voter qualification penalties and barred insurrectionists from holding executive or elector status.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose emergent two-party competition made party-ticket balloting an operational necessity.
- [[american-revolution]] — the independence struggle that forged the early constitutional republic and prompted these early institutional adjustments.
