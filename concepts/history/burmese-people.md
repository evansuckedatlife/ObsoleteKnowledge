## summary

The **Burmese people**, commonly known as the *Bamar* or *Burmans*, are the dominant Sino-Tibetan ethnic group native to modern Myanmar (historically Burma), comprising roughly two-thirds of the country's population. Settling predominantly along the fertile basin of the Irrawaddy River, the group established powerful regional dynasties—most notably the Pagan, Taungoo, and Konbaung empires—that synthesized Theravada *Buddhism* with royal governance. Their political ascendancy, cultural norms, and language shaped modern Burma while fueling prolonged friction with ethnic minorities and serving as the primary demographic base for anti-colonial movements and pro-democracy opposition.

## you gotta know

- Account for approximately sixty-eight percent of Myanmar's modern population, speaking the national Burmese language and inhabiting the central lowland plains.
- Practice Theravada *Buddhism* as the central pillar of communal life, seamlessly integrating it with the traditional veneration of thirty-seven pre-Buddhist spirits known as *nats*.
- Founded the Pagan Empire under King Anawrahta in 1044, who unified the Irrawaddy valley, established the capital at Bagan, and inaugurated centuries of monumental pagoda construction.
- Lost sovereign independence to the *British Empire* across three nineteenth-century conflicts, culminating in the 1885 exile of King Thibaw and the formal annexation of the Konbaung dynasty.
- Formed the core of the nationalist *Thakin* movement during the 1930s, spearheaded by General Aung San, who negotiated independence following complex alliances in *World War II*.
- Maintained dominant control over the post-independence military apparatus (*Tatmadaw*), which seized power under Ne Win in 1962 and fought peripheral minorities including the Karen, Shan, and Kachin.
- Served as the primary popular base for Aung San Suu Kyi's National League for Democracy, leading mass demonstrations during the 1988 Uprising and the 2007 Saffron Revolution.
- Maintain distinctive daily cultural customs such as applying *thanaka* cosmetic paste from ground tree bark and donning the wrapped cylindrical *longyi* garment.

## connections

- [[buddhism]] — faith adopted by King Anawrahta that remains central to the ethnic and moral identity of the Bamar majority.
- [[colonialism]] — British administrative subjugation that abolished the royal court, introduced migrant labor, and transformed the rural economy.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial power that governed Burma as an administrative province of British India until granting independence in 1948.
- [[world-war-ii]] — conflict in which Bamar nationalists initially partnered with imperial Japan before switching allegiance to the Allies in 1945.
- [[cold-war]] — era in which General Ne Win implemented the isolated "Burmese Way to Socialism" while combating communist and ethnic insurgencies.
- [[china]] — northern neighboring power that launched invasions against Pagan and the Konbaung dynasty and retains immense geopolitical influence in Myanmar.
