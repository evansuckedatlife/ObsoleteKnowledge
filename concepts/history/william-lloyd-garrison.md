## summary

**William Lloyd Garrison** was an influential nineteenth-century American journalist, social reformer, and prominent leader of the radical abolitionist crusade in the [[united-states]]. As the outspoken founder and editor of the anti-slavery newspaper *The Liberator*, he pioneered the demand for immediate, unconditional emancipation without financial compensation to enslavers. His uncompromising moral stance, advocacy for women's rights, and rejection of political compromise made him one of the most polarizing and pivotal figures leading up to the [[american-civil-war]].

## you gotta know

- Founded the seminal abolitionist weekly newspaper *The Liberator* in Boston in 1831 with printer *Isaac Knapp*, famously proclaiming in its inaugural issue that he would be "as harsh as truth, and as uncompromising as justice" and that he would "not retreat a single inch."
- Championed the doctrine of immediatism, demanding the immediate, unconditional emancipation of all enslaved people while repudiating gradual emancipation schemes and the colonization efforts of the *American Colonization Society*.
- Denounced the [[united-states-constitution]] as a pro-slavery compact, infamously describing it as "a covenant with death and an agreement with hell," and publicly burned a physical copy of the document at an 1854 Fourth of July gathering in Framingham, Massachusetts.
- Co-founded the *American Anti-Slavery Society* in Philadelphia in 1833, composing its foundational declaration of sentiments and advocating strict nonresistance, Christian pacifism, and moral suasion rather than participation in electoral politics.
- Provoked intense backlash across both the South and the North, prompting the state of Georgia to offer a cash reward for his arrest and surviving an assault by an angry anti-abolitionist Boston mob in 1835 that dragged him through the streets with a rope.
- Caused a major factional schism within the abolitionist movement in 1840 by insisting on the full participation of female activists such as *Abby Kelley*, which drove conservative members to break away and form the rival *American and Foreign Anti-Slavery Society*.
- Formed an influential early mentorship with escaped orator *Frederick Douglass*, whom he invited to join the lecture circuit, though the two later suffered a bitter public estrangement over political strategy and constitutional interpretation.
- Ceased publication of *The Liberator* in December 1865 following the ratification of the [[13th-amendment]], declaring that the legal destruction of chattel slavery completed the historic mission of his newspaper.

- [[american-civil-war]] — ideological catalyst whose decades of radical agitation intensified national sectional polarization.

- [[reconstruction-era]] — post-war period during which he turned his advocacy toward temperance, Native American rights, and women's suffrage.

- [[john-brown]] — militant abolitionist whose violent raid on Harpers Ferry he defended as heroic despite his own personal pacifism.
