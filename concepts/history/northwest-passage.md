## summary

The **Northwest Passage** is the legendary sea route through the Arctic waters of northern *Canada* connecting the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans. For centuries, explorers sought this shortcut to Asian markets, but the passage proved to be ice-choked, dangerous, and impassable except for brief seasonal windows. Its historical significance lies not in practical utility but in the exploratory ambitions, geographical knowledge, and imperial rivalry it generated.

## you gotta know

- European explorers pursued the Northwest Passage from the 16th century onward, driven by the hope of a faster trade route to Asia than around Africa or Cape Horn.
- *John Cabot* and other early explorers probed the North American coast searching for the passage, but were blocked by pack ice and did not recognize them as having reached a viable route.
- The *Lewis and Clark Expedition* (1804–1806) was partly motivated by the hope of finding a continuous water route to the Pacific; they discovered the passage did not exist as a practical navigable shortcut.
- Norwegian explorer *Roald Amundsen* became the first to successfully traverse the Northwest Passage between 1903 and 1906, using a small ship and Inuit knowledge of ice navigation.
- The passage remained commercially useless during the sailing era due to unpredictable ice and short navigation seasons; modern icebreaker technology has made it intermittently viable.
- Geographic knowledge of the Arctic and Canadian interior, gathered through the search for the passage, contributed to European understanding of the *North American* continent.
- The passage remains geopolitically significant today as climate change opens Arctic waters and nations compete for Arctic resources and shipping routes.
