# Valley Forge

## summary

**Valley Forge** was the winter encampment of the Continental Army from December 1777 to June 1778 near present-day Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, during the American Revolution. The camp became synonymous with suffering and endurance: thousands of soldiers faced starvation, disease, and cold with minimal supplies, yet emerged from the ordeal with improved discipline and confidence that would define their future victories.

## you gotta know

- After the *British victory at Brandywine* (September 1777) and loss of Philadelphia, General *George Washington* positioned the Continental Army at Valley Forge to monitor British movements while maintaining the army's cohesion through winter.
- Conditions were brutal: the army lacked adequate food, clothing, blankets, and shelter; disease (dysentery, typhoid, pneumonia) killed more soldiers than combat; morale depended on Washington's resolve and the commitment of officers like *Baron von Steuben*.
- *Baron von Steuben*, a Prussian officer, arrived in February 1778 and implemented rigorous training and standardized drill; his reforms transformed the Continental Army from a militia into a disciplined fighting force capable of matching British regulars.
- Supply shortages were catastrophic: soldiers went weeks without bread; horses starved; desertion rates climbed; the Continental Congress struggled to provision the army while financing the broader war effort.
- By spring, morale recovered as supplies improved, new recruits arrived, and foreign support (especially French aid) signaled the army's survival; the army left Valley Forge reinvigorated and went on to victories at *Monmouth* (June 1778) and later *Yorktown* (1781).
- Valley Forge symbolized the Continental Army's transformation from an improvised militia into a permanent, professional military force capable of sustained warfare against the British Empire.
