## summary

**Critical pedagogy** is an educational philosophy and teaching movement that treats schooling as an inherently political practice capable of either reinforcing social domination or advancing human liberation. Developed during the mid-to-late twentieth century, it arose from Brazilian educator *Paulo Freire*'s work in adult literacy campaigns and was subsequently adapted across the [[united-states]] through [[civil-rights-movement]] activism, feminist critique, and [[intersectionality]]. The framework remains central to modern social theory for dismantling institutional hierarchies and demonstrating how collaborative learning can cultivate critical consciousness across lines of [[race]] and [[class]].

## you gotta know

- Attacked the traditional *banking model of education* through *Paulo Freire*'s foundational 1968 text *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*, arguing that treating students as passive receptacles for deposited knowledge reinforces authoritarian social hierarchies and proposing problem-posing dialogue instead.
- Centers on the development of *conscientização*, or critical consciousness, an intellectual awakening in which marginalized learners perceive socioeconomic contradictions and actively intervene to transform their oppressive social reality.
- Reconceptualized in the [[united-states]] by feminist theorist *bell hooks* in *Teaching to Transgress: Education as the Practice of Freedom* (1994), who integrated [[intersectionality]] to theorize classroom teaching as a radical practice of freedom addressing [[race]], [[class]], and gender.
- Emphasizes the imperative of *praxis*, conceptualized as the continuous dialectical cycle of critical reflection and tangible action necessary to dismantle oppressive social structures rather than merely analyzing them theoretically.
- Originated in northeastern Brazil during early 1960s adult literacy campaigns, where *Freire* taught disenfranchised sugarcane laborers to read in weeks using politically charged generative words drawn directly from their working conditions.
- Drew intellectual inspiration from Western Marxism, the existential humanism of [[existentialism]], and the radical egalitarianism of Latin American liberation theology within [[christianity]], directly confronting the ongoing legacies of [[colonialism]].
- Expanded within North American academia during the [[cold-war]] by scholars like *Henry Giroux* and *Peter McLaren*, who demonstrated how public school curricula and standardized testing reproduce capitalist social stratification.
- Rejects the pretense of pedagogical neutrality, maintaining that all educational institutions either domesticate students into passive compliance with the dominant order or empower them to challenge systemic injustice.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — supplied practical models of freedom schools and grassroots literacy drives that demonstrated how education could foster radical political participation.
- [[colonialism]] — created the historical structures of cultural hegemony and epistemic violence that critical educators sought to interrogate and dismantle in postcolonial contexts.
- [[existentialism]] — provided philosophical concepts of human agency, authentic choice, and the ontological vocation of human beings to act upon their world.
- [[christianity]] — informed *Freire*'s worldview through Catholic liberation theology, which emphasized a moral imperative to defend the dignity of the oppressed.
- [[united-states]] — served as the primary academic environment where theorists synthesized Freirean principles with critical race theory, queer theory, and feminist pedagogy.
- [[cold-war]] — defined the polarized political atmosphere of military coups and anti-imperialist struggles across Latin America that shaped radical educational experiments.
- [[intersectionality]] — provided the structural framework utilized by *bell hooks* to demonstrate how race, gender, and economic exploitation operate simultaneously in academic settings.
