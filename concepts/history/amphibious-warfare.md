# Amphibious Warfare

## summary

**Amphibious warfare** is the military practice of utilizing naval ships and specialized watercraft to project landing forces ashore onto hostile or contested territory. Originating in ancient maritime raids and maturing into a complex combined-arms doctrine during the twentieth century, it integrates naval gunfire support, close air support, rapid sea-to-shore logistics, and specialized assault troops. Its execution proved decisive in major twentieth-century conflicts, shaping Allied strategy in [[world-war-ii]] across the Pacific and European theaters and providing dramatic strategic pivots during the [[cold-war]].

## you gotta know

- *Operation Overlord* (June 6, 1944): The Allied invasion of Normandy in [[france]] during [[world-war-ii]] stands as the largest amphibious assault in history, landing more than 150,000 soldiers across five code-named beachheads (*Utah*, *Omaha*, *Gold*, *Juno*, and *Sword*) under the supreme command of General *Dwight D. Eisenhower*.
- The Pacific "Island-Hopping" Campaign: The [[united-states]] military, led by the *United States Marine Corps* and the *US Army*, bypassed heavily fortified Japanese bastions to strike vulnerable outposts, refining amphibious doctrine through bloody assaults on *Guadalcanal*, *Tarawa*, *Iwo Jima*, and *Okinawa* against the Empire of [[japan]].
- The *Gallipoli Campaign* (1915): Allied forces from the [[british-empire]] and French forces landed on the Gallipoli peninsula against the [[ottoman-empire]] during [[world-war-i]]; the operation's disastrous logistical failures, trench deadlock, and eventual evacuation prompted comprehensive interwar doctrinal reform.
- The *Battle of Inchon* (*Operation Chromite*, September 1950): General *Douglas MacArthur* orchestrated a daring amphibious landing behind North Korean lines during the [[cold-war]], overcoming extreme twenty-nine-foot tidal variations and seawalls to recapture Seoul and dramatically reverse communist territorial gains.
- Critical Technological Innovations: The development of the shallow-draft *Landing Craft, Vehicle, Personnel* (*LCVP* or *Higgins boat*) designed by *Andrew Higgins*, tracked amphibious vehicles (*LVTs* or *Amtracs*), and floating artificial breakwaters known as *Mulberry harbours* solved critical bottlenecks of beachhead supply delivery.
- Ancient and Classical Antecedents: Early maritime powers conducted beachhead assaults for centuries, including the Persian landing at the *Battle of Marathon* in [[ancient-greece]], Athenian raids during the *Peloponnesian War*, and Julius Caesar's cross-channel expeditions into *Britannia* for the [[roman-empire]].
- The *Falklands War* (1982): British naval task forces conducted amphibious landings at *San Carlos Water* to retake the islands from Argentina, highlighting both the enduring relevance of expeditionary operations and the acute vulnerability of modern surface transport to land-based anti-ship missiles.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — Served as the defining crucible of modern landing doctrine, encompassing massive operations in Normandy, Sicily, and the Pacific.
- [[world-war-i]] — Demonstrated the perils of poor coastal coordination at Gallipoli, motivating interwar military theorists to develop systematic amphibious tactics.
- [[united-states]] — Institutionalized modern amphibious doctrine through the *United States Marine Corps* and mobilized immense industrial production of specialized landing craft.
- [[japan]] — Constructed formidable subterranean beach fortifications across Pacific islands, necessitating revolutionary improvements in preliminary bombardment.
- [[cold-war]] — Provided the backdrop for the dramatic landing at Inchon and spurred the adoption of helicopters for vertical envelopment tactics.
- [[british-empire]] — Possessed vast global naval power that conducted historic landings worldwide, from the assault on Quebec to modern operations in the South Atlantic.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — Mounted a stalwart coastal defense led by *Mustafa Kemal Atatürk* at Gallipoli, inflicting heavy casualties and repelling Allied invasion forces.
