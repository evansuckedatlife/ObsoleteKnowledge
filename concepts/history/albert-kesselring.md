## summary

**Albert Kesselring** was a German Field Marshal and skilled defensive strategist during *World War II*, famous for his command of Axis forces in *Italy* and southern Europe. Known for his ability to extract maximum resistance from limited resources, he orchestrated some of Nazi Germany's most effective defensive campaigns, particularly the stubborn *Gustav Line*. Though he achieved tactical victories, his strategic efforts ultimately could not overcome Allied material and manpower advantages.

## you gotta know

- Began his military career as a Luftwaffe (air force) officer before transitioning to command ground forces in North Africa and Italy.
- Took command of Axis forces in Italy in late 1943 and immediately began organizing a formidable defensive network.
- Constructed and skillfully defended the *Gustav Line*, a heavily fortified position across southern Italy that held the Allies for months.
- Frustrated Allied operations at *Anzio* in early 1944, counterattacking vigorously to contain and nearly destroy the beachhead.
- Coordinated the grueling *Battle of Monte Cassino* (January–May 1944), where German defenders held a strategic hilltop fortress.
- Known among German officers as "Smiling Albert" for his calm demeanor under pressure and his confident, optimistic manner.
- Later took command in southern France (*Operation Dragoon*) and maintained German presence until late 1944.
- Survived the war and was initially tried as a war criminal, though he was eventually acquitted.
