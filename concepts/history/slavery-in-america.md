## summary

**Slavery in America** was the institution by which millions of Africans and African Americans were held as property, forced to labor, and denied freedom from colonial times through 1865. Concentrated primarily in the agrarian South, slavery generated vast wealth for planters and became the foundational economic system of southern society, driving territorial expansion and political power. The incompatibility between slavery and northern industrial free labor made it an irreconcilable division that ultimately fractured the nation and culminated in the [[American Civil War]].

## you gotta know

- Slavery existed in all thirteen colonies but became concentrated in the South, where large plantations demanded constant labor to cultivate tobacco, rice, and cotton.
- The transatlantic slave trade forcibly transported over twelve million Africans to the Americas; roughly two million died en route in the Middle Passage.
- *Thomas Jefferson* famously condemned slavery philosophically while enslaving hundreds; this paradox epitomized the founding generation's moral hypocrisy.
- The *Three-Fifths Compromise* in the Constitution counted enslaved people as three-fifths of a person for representation, artificially inflating southern political power.
- *Cotton gin* mechanization in the late 1700s made cotton supremely profitable, dramatically increasing demand for enslaved labor and entrenching slavery deeper in the South.
- The Missouri Compromise, Compromise of 1850, and Kansas-Nebraska Act all failed to resolve whether slavery would expand into new territories, intensifying sectional conflict.
- By 1860, southern slaveholders viewed northern free-labor capitalism as an existential threat and secession as necessary self-preservation.
