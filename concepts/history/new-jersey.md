## summary

**New Jersey** is a state in the northeastern [[united-states]] with a pivotal role in the [[american-revolution]]. Located between the [[delaware-river]] and the Atlantic Ocean, it served as a crucial battleground during the War of Independence, hosting multiple pivotal engagements. The state's geography—narrow, densely settled, and strategically located between important centers—made it a contested zone where the fortunes of both the Continental Army and British forces rose and fell repeatedly.

## you gotta know

- Originally settled by Dutch colonists in the 17th century before being ceded to [[england|English]] control and eventually divided into East and West Jersey.
- Positioned strategically between the two major revolutionary centers of Philadelphia and New York City, making it perpetually vulnerable to invasion and occupation.
- Served as the site of the [[battle-of-trenton]] in 1776, where [[george-washington]] achieved a crucial winter victory that revived American morale and demonstrated strategic genius.
- Hosted the [[battle-of-princeton]] shortly after Trenton, another American victory that forced the British to abandon central New Jersey temporarily.
- Endured the [[battle-of-monmouth]] in 1778, a brutal engagement where Washington's army demonstrated that it could stand toe-to-toe with professional British soldiers.
- Was occupied for extended periods by British forces and served as a base for raids and foraging operations, making it a crucial theater of the [[american-revolution]].
- Endured harsh occupation policies that devastated civilian infrastructure and turned it into a battleground ravaged by both armies and competing local militias.
- Produced key revolutionary figures and contributed soldiers, supplies, and supplies to the Continental Army despite the hardships of occupation.
- Its strategic location meant that control of New Jersey often determined the outcome of major campaigns; Washington's genius lay partly in understanding this geography.

## connections

- [[delaware-river]] — the geographic feature defining New Jersey's western boundary and the site of Washington's daring Christmas crossing.
- [[american-revolution]] — the conflict that made New Jersey strategically vital and devastated much of its civilian population.
- [[battle-of-trenton]] — the first of three major battles fought on New Jersey soil that demonstrated American military capability.
- [[battle-of-princeton]] — followed Trenton as part of Washington's brilliant winter campaign of 1776–1777.
- [[battle-of-monmouth]] — the most significant engagement between the Continental Army and British forces in New Jersey.
- [[george-washington]] — whose military campaigns in New Jersey proved decisive for American independence.
