## summary

A **free market** is an economic system based on supply and demand where transactions occur between private buyers and sellers with minimal government intervention, price controls, or centralized planning. Rooted in eighteenth-century *Enlightenment* philosophy and classical political economy, it gained massive prominence alongside the *Industrial Revolution* in Great Britain and the United States. It forms the conceptual backbone of modern capitalism and remains one of the most fiercely debated paradigms in modern political and social history.

## you gotta know

- Popularized conceptually by Scottish philosopher *Adam Smith* in his 1776 foundational treatise *The Wealth of Nations*, which introduced the metaphor of the *invisible hand*.
- Emphasizes the price mechanism, where market forces of supply and demand determine the allocation of scarce resources rather than government mandates.
- Strongly associated with the French physiocratic doctrine of *laissez-faire*, which emerged in contrast to earlier mercantilist monopolies and colonial trade tariffs.
- Expanded dramatically across the globe during the *Industrial Revolution*, as factory production, international trade, and free-trade pacts displaced traditional agrarian guilds.
- Legally reinforced in nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century American jurisprudence through notions of private property and [[freedom-of-contract]].
- Subject to major structural critiques by Karl Marx, socialist thinkers, and later John Maynard Keynes, who analyzed recurring boom-and-bust business cycles and market failures.
- Played a central role in the geopolitical and ideological contests of the *Cold War*, contrasting Western capitalist economies with the centrally planned command economies of the *Soviet Union*.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical movement that championed individual liberty, property rights, and natural law.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — technological transformation that powered widespread factory industrialization and global commercial expansion.
- [[freedom-of-contract]] — legal doctrine granting private individuals the autonomy to negotiate economic agreements without state interference.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial powerhouse that championed global free trade policies such as the repeal of the Corn Laws.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose constitutional and commercial frameworks provided fertile ground for enterprise and market capitalism.
- [[french-revolution]] — historic rupture that abolished feudal guilds and internal trade barriers in Western Europe.
- [[cold-war]] — global ideological clash that pitted market-based economies against Soviet state socialism.
