## summary

**International Law** is the body of rules and treaties that govern relations between nation-states and define rights and obligations in war, trade, diplomacy, and shared domains like the ocean and space. Though ancient rulers made binding agreements, modern international law emerged from European treaties after the *Thirty Years' War* and evolved dramatically through the 20th century. The *League of Nations* and *United Nations* institutionalized this framework, while concepts like *war crimes* and *crimes against humanity* have slowly been enforced.

## you gotta know

- Originates in the *Peace of Westphalia* (1648), which established principles of state sovereignty and binding treaties between equals.
- *Treaties* are binding agreements that constitute the primary source of international law; *customary international law* develops through consistent state practice.
- The *League of Nations* (1920) was the first attempt to institutionalize international law; it failed to prevent World War II.
- The *United Nations* (1945) replaced the League and established the *United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea* and other frameworks for peaceful resolution of disputes.
- *War crimes* and *crimes against humanity* emerged as legal categories after World War II, enforced through the *Nuremberg Trials* and subsequent tribunals.
- Modern challenges include enforcing international law against powerful nations, addressing cyber warfare and terrorism, and adjudicating disputes through the *International Court of Justice*.
- The *Geneva Conventions* codify rules protecting civilians, prisoners of war, and the wounded during armed conflict, establishing universal standards of conduct in war.
- Sovereignty and non-intervention are central principles, yet humanitarian intervention and responsibility to protect have emerged as counterbalancing doctrines justifying intervention to stop atrocities.
