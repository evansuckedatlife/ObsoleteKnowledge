## summary

**Latin American Independence** was a series of revolutionary wars and political upheavals spanning roughly 1810–1825, through which the Spanish and Portuguese colonies in the Americas violently overthrew European colonial rule. Led by military commanders and intellectuals inspired by Enlightenment ideals and European upheaval, these independence movements liberated nearly 300 million people and created the modern nation-states of Latin America. The process fundamentally reshaped the hemisphere's political landscape and established a new international order in the Western Hemisphere.

## you gotta know

- Triggered by the Napoleonic Wars in Europe, which destabilized Spanish and Portuguese authority and created opportunities for colonial rebellion during the early 19th century.
- Led by legendary military commanders *Simón Bolívar* (Venezuela and northern South America) and *José de San Martín* (Chile and Argentina), whose campaigns liberated much of Spanish America through military genius.
- *Bolívar* liberated Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru across multiple campaigns; *San Martín* liberated Chile and Argentina before their paths crossed at the Peruvian front.
- Bolívar envisioned a unified Latin American federation, though political fragmentation and rival ambitions ultimately prevented continental unity after independence was achieved.
- Brazil's independence (1822) was the exception: it became an independent monarchy rather than a republic, maintaining imperial continuity under Portuguese royal succession and Portuguese prince *Pedro I*.
- Mexican independence (1821) achieved national sovereignty but fractured through internal division, constitutional conflicts, and military instability, eventually losing vast northern territories to the United States.
- The new republics inherited Spain's hierarchical colonial structures: vast inequality, powerful Catholic Church influence, military dominance, and chronic instability characterized their early decades and hindered development.
