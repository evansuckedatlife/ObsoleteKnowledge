## summary

**Nationalism** is a political ideology and social movement that emphasizes the interests, identity, and sovereignty of a nation or national group, often viewed as a collective bound by common language, culture, history, territory, or ethnicity. Emerging powerfully in the 19th century as a consequence of Enlightenment ideas and the *French Revolution*, nationalism transformed political life by replacing dynastic loyalty with loyalty to the nation; it became a driving force for both liberation (unification of Italy and Germany, decolonization) and imperialism, rivalry among powers, and eventually world war. Nationalism persists as one of the most potent forces in international relations and domestic politics.

## you gotta know

- Pre-modern political allegiance was primarily to dynasties and religious institutions; nationalism redirected loyalty toward imagined communities bound by national identity, creating the modern nation-state as a primary political unit.
- The *French Revolution* and *Napoleon Bonaparte* were pivotal; Napoleon's military dominance and the imposition of French law and administration across Europe actually sparked counter-nationalist movements as conquered peoples asserted national identity against foreign domination.
- *Romantic nationalism* in the 19th century emphasized the unique character, spirit, and genius of each nation; intellectuals celebrated folk traditions, national languages, and historical narratives as expressions of distinct national essence.
- Nationalism became the ideological fuel for unification movements: Italy (Cavour, Garibaldi) and Germany (Bismarck) were unified through nationalist fervor, creating powerful nation-states from fragmented regions and dynasties.
- *Imperialism* was often justified through nationalism; powerful nations claimed superiority and destiny to dominate others, framing conquest as civilizing missions; nationalism thus became an engine of imperial expansion and colonialism.
- The *Mexican Revolution* (1910–1920) was partly driven by nationalism; revolutionaries asserted Mexican control over Mexican resources and destiny against foreign economic domination and internal elitism.
- In sport, nationalism manifests as national pride in athletic competition; the *Olympic Games* became an arena for nations to compete and display superiority, often weaponized during the Cold War.
- Nationalism contributed directly to world wars; national rivalries, imperial competition, and mutual distrust among nationalist powers fueled the geopolitical tensions that exploded into *World War I* and *World War II*.
- Post-war decolonization movements invoked nationalism to justify independence from European empires; formerly colonized peoples claimed national self-determination and sovereignty based on **nationalist ideology**.
