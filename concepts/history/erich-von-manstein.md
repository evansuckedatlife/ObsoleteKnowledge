## summary

**Erich von Manstein** was widely regarded as the most gifted operational strategist in the German military during *[[world-war-ii]]*. He devised the audacious *Sichelschnitt* plan that smashed Allied defenses in *[[france]]* in 1940 and subsequently led major campaigns against the *[[soviet-union]]*, including the capture of Sevastopol and the counterstroke at Kharkov. Convicted of war crimes after the war for brutal treatment of civilians and partisans, he later advised West *[[germany]]* on rearmament and helped fabricate the myth of the clean Wehrmacht in his memoirs.

## you gotta know

- Conceived the unorthodox Ardennes armored penetration plan (*Fall Gelb*) while serving as chief of staff to *[[erwin-von-rundstedt]]*, leading directly to the rapid conquest of *[[france]]* in 1940.
- Commanded the 56th Panzer Corps during the initial onslaught against the *[[soviet-union]]* in 1941, securing vital bridgeheads across the Dvina River in a lightning advance.
- Led the 11th Army during the brutal siege of Sevastopol in the Crimean Peninsula in 1942, earning promotion to field marshal for capturing the fortress city.
- Commanded Army Group Don in late 1942 during Operation Winter Storm, a failed relief operation attempting to break the Soviet encirclement of the 6th Army at Stalingrad.
- Executed the brilliant mobile defense known as the "backhand blow" at the Third Battle of Kharkov in early 1943, stabilizing the shattered German southern front.
- Commanded the southern pincer forces at the massive armored Battle of Kursk (Operation Citadel) in July 1943 against deeply echeloned Soviet fortifications.
- Dismissed by Hitler in March 1944 following persistent strategic disagreements over mobile defense versus rigid static resistance.
- Tried in 1949 by a British military court for war crimes, including the enforcement of unlawful orders and complicity in atrocities, and sentenced to eighteen years' imprisonment before early release in 1953.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the conflict in which he directed many of the German military's most consequential campaigns.
- [[erwin-von-rundstedt]] — the field marshal under whom he served as chief of staff during the planning of the 1940 Western Campaign.
- [[germany]] — his country whose Imperial, Weimar, and Nazi armies he served before advising the postwar Bundeswehr.
- [[soviet-union]] — the eastern front superpower whose Red Army he battled across the Crimea, Kharkov, and Kursk.
- [[france]] — the battleground where his daring armored operational concept dismantled the Allied lines along the Meuse.
- [[world-war-i]] — the war in which he served as an infantry officer and staff officer after being wounded in Poland.
