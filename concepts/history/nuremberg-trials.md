## summary

The **Nuremberg Trials** were a landmark series of military tribunals convened between 1945 and 1946 in Nuremberg, [[germany]], in the aftermath of [[world-war-ii]]. Organized by the four Allied powers—the [[united-states]], Great Britain, the [[soviet-union]], and [[france]]—under the charter of the **International Military Tribunal**, the proceedings brought senior political, military, and economic leaders of the Nazi regime to justice. The trials fundamentally transformed international jurisprudence by establishing that state officials cannot claim sovereign immunity for atrocities and by codifying individual criminal responsibility under [[international-law]] for aggressive warfare, [[war-crimes]], and crimes against humanity.

## you gotta know

- Held in the *Palace of Justice* in Nuremberg, a venue selected because its courthouse and adjoining prison were largely intact and the city had served as the symbolic epicenter of Nazi propaganda rallies.
- Indicted twenty-four prominent leaders in the major war criminals trial, including *Reichsmarschall* *Hermann Göring*, who was sentenced to hang but cheated the gallows by ingesting potassium cyanide hours before execution.
- Led on the American prosecution side by Associate Supreme Court Justice *Robert H. Jackson*, whose eloquent opening statement asserted that civilization could not survive or tolerate the recurrence of such calculated wickedness.
- Structured indictments around four principal counts: conspiracy, crimes against peace (planning and waging aggressive war), conventional [[war-crimes]], and the newly formulated category of crimes against humanity.
- Decisively repudiated the defense of superior orders—commonly termed the *Nuremberg Defense*—ruling that obedience to government commands does not relieve an individual of personal legal and moral guilt.
- Succeeded by twelve Subsequent Nuremberg Proceedings conducted between 1946 and 1949 by United States military tribunals, including the *Doctors' Trial*, the *Judges' Trial*, and the *Einsatzgruppen Trial*.
- The *Doctors' Trial* yielded the *Nuremberg Code* of 1947, a foundational ten-point standard for human experimentation establishing voluntary informed consent as essential in biomedical ethics.
- Soviet judge *Iona Nikitchenko*, previously an architect of the 1930s Moscow show trials, entered formal dissents protesting the acquittals of *Hjalmar Schacht*, *Franz von Papen*, and *Hans Fritzsche*, while arguing *Rudolf Hess* deserved death instead of life imprisonment.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the cataclysmic conflict whose conclusion forced the Allies to construct a judicial mechanism for holding Axis leadership accountable.
- [[germany]] — the defeated sovereign nation whose government, military apparatus, and party organs stood trial in the ruined city of Nuremberg.
- [[united-states]] — leading Allied authority whose jurists shaped the adversarial trial structure and furnished chief prosecutor *Robert H. Jackson*.
- [[soviet-union]] — Allied signatory that captured Berlin, provided key judicial personnel, and supplied voluminous documentation of eastern atrocities.
- [[france]] — Allied tribunal member whose delegation focused prosecutorial arguments on western European occupation atrocities and forced labor.
- [[cold-war]] — deteriorating postwar relations among the four powers that disrupted joint prosecution efforts and left later trials solely to American tribunals.
- [[united-nations]] — global assembly that formalized the court's legacy into the Nuremberg Principles and the 1948 Genocide Convention.
- [[japan]] — Pacific Axis partner whose wartime leadership faced analogous prosecutions before the International Military Tribunal for the Far East in Tokyo.
