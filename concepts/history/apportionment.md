## summary

**Apportionment**, encompassing **congressional apportionment** and **legislative apportionment**, is the constitutional process of allocating political representation among geographic subdivisions according to population figures collected by a periodic census. In the *[[united-states]]*, this mechanism divides the 435 voting seats in the House of Representatives among the several states, while also establishing mandatory population parity standards for state legislative districts. Established under Article I of the *[[united-states-constitution]]* and transformed by twentieth-century rulings of the *[[federal-courts]]*, apportionment forms the structural cornerstone of representative democracy by translating demographic presence into equal political power.

## you gotta know

- The *[[civil-rights-movement]]* era landmark ruling in *Baker v. Carr* established that legislative malapportionment was justiciable in the *[[federal-courts]]*, leading to *Reynolds v. Sims* and *Wesberry v. Sanders*, which struck down skewed district maps and mandated substantially equal populations under the *one person, one vote* doctrine.
- The *Reapportionment Act of 1929* permanently fixed the size of the *[[united-states]]* House of Representatives at 435 members, creating a zero-sum distribution where growing states gain representation strictly at the expense of declining or slower-growing states.
- Article I, Section 2 of the *[[united-states-constitution]]* mandated a decennial enumeration to allocate congressional seats and direct taxes, originally incorporating the Three-Fifths Compromise until its formal abolition following the *[[american-civil-war]]*.
- Section 2 of the *[[14th-amendment]]*, ratified during the *[[reconstruction-era]]*, revised the formula to count the whole number of persons in each state, while creating an explicit penalty clause reducing representation for states that disenfranchised adult male citizens.
- President *[[george-washington]]* cast the first presidential veto in American history in 1792 to reject an apportionment bill that favored an algorithm designed by *Alexander Hamilton* over the divisor methodology championed by *[[thomas-jefferson]]*.
- Since 1941, federal statute has mandated the method of equal proportions (the *Huntington-Hill method*), which calculates priority values using geometric means to minimize relative percentage differences in district populations across different states.
- Mathematical calculations under the historical *Hamilton-Vinton method* created bizarre anomalies, notably after the 1880 census when increasing the total size of the House from 299 to 300 seats caused the state of Alabama to lose a representative in the famous *Alabama paradox*.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — Article I mandates a national decennial census to apportion federal House seats among the states.
- [[14th-amendment]] — eliminated the Three-Fifths Compromise during Reconstruction and mandated counting all persons in each state.
- [[federal-courts]] — discarded the political question doctrine in *Baker v. Carr* to review and invalidate malapportioned districts.
- [[voting-rights]] — works alongside constitutional apportionment mandates to prevent racial vote dilution in redistricting.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — supplied the legal impetus and political momentum that dismantled rural malapportionment across state legislatures.
- [[george-washington]] — exercised the first presidential veto in American history to reject an unfair apportionment bill in 1792.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — devised the alternative divisor-based allocation method used for the nation's earliest seat distributions.
