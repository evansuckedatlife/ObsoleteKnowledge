## summary

**Liberalism** is a political and philosophical ideology emphasizing individual liberty, limited government, representative democracy, and rule of law. Emerging from the [[enlightenment]] and crystallized by the [[french-revolution]], liberalism prioritizes protecting citizens from arbitrary state power through constitutional constraints and the separation of powers. As the dominant political ideology of the 19th and 20th centuries in Western democracies, liberalism shaped constitutional frameworks, expansions of suffrage, and social policy, though it took varied forms—from classical laissez-faire economics to modern social liberalism supporting welfare provisions.

## you gotta know

- The [[enlightenment]] philosophers, particularly *Locke* and *Montesquieu*, developed the intellectual foundations of liberalism: natural rights, separation of powers, and government by consent.
- **Liberal** ideology encompasses a range of political positions, but all emphasize individual liberty as the foundation; classical liberalism of the 19th century emphasized free markets, minimal government intervention, and individual property rights as prerequisites for human flourishing.
- Liberalism competed with [[conservatism]] in the post-Napoleonic era, with liberals advocating constitutional governance and conservatives resisting rapid social change.
- Social liberalism emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, accepting limited government intervention to provide education, welfare, and labor protections.
- Liberals championed democratic reforms including universal (male) suffrage, secret ballots, and representative institutions that challenged aristocratic monopolies on political power.
- Liberalism's emphasis on individual rights and rule of law provided the philosophical foundation for movements against slavery, colonialism, and authoritarianism.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement that provided liberalism's philosophical foundations through emphasis on reason and individual rights.
- [[french-revolution]] — the political upheaval that translated liberal principles into revolutionary practice, establishing republics and constitutional governance.
- [[conservatism]] — the competing ideology that resisted liberalism's rapid social changes and individualism, prioritizing stability and tradition.
- [[kant]] — the German philosopher whose work synthesized enlightenment rationalism with moral and political philosophy underpinning liberal thought.
- [[victorian-era]] — the period when liberalism achieved political dominance in Britain, shaping policy toward free trade, expanded suffrage, and constitutional reform.
- [[american-revolution]] — an earlier expression of liberal principles: individual rights, popular sovereignty, and constitutional limits on government.
- [[democracy]] — the political system liberals advocated for, though liberalism and democracy are distinct concepts sometimes in tension.
