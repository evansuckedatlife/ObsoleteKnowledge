## summary

The atomic bombings of **Hiroshima** (August 6, 1945) and **Nagasaki** (August 9, 1945) were catastrophic nuclear attacks by the United States on Japanese cities, killing approximately 210,000 people by year's end through immediate blast, thermal radiation, and radiation sickness. The bombings hastened Japan's surrender and initiated the *nuclear age*, raising enduring questions about military necessity, civilian casualties, and the moral legitimacy of weapons of mass destruction.

## you gotta know

- The bombs (nicknamed "Little Boy" over Hiroshima and "Fat Man" over Nagasaki) were the only nuclear weapons ever used in warfare; they represented the climactic application of atomic physics to military ends.
- The blast destroyed the vast majority of buildings within a mile radius; the intense heat ignited fires that created firestorms; radiation poisoning killed thousands in subsequent weeks and months.
- Estimates vary widely, but approximately 70,000–80,000 people died instantly at Hiroshima; total deaths from injuries and radiation by year-end reached ~140,000. Nagasaki's toll was ~74,000 total deaths.
- President *Harry Truman* authorized the bombings ostensibly to avoid a costly invasion of the Japanese home islands; however, Japan was already defeated and the Soviets were entering the Pacific War, so military necessity remains contested.
- The bombings accelerated Japan's surrender (August 15, 1945, formally signed September 2), ending World War II, but their strategic justification—whether a ground invasion would have cost more American lives—remains deeply debated.
- Survivors (*hibakusha*) suffered lifelong health complications and social stigma; the bombings' aftermath demonstrated the long-term human cost of radioactive exposure and nuclear war.
- The bombings galvanized the *nuclear age*: they motivated global concern about atomic weapons, contributed to the arms race, and shaped Cold War strategy around *mutually assured destruction*.
