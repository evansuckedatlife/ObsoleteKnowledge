## summary

**Feminism** is a social and political movement advocating for the equality and rights of women across all spheres of life—economic, political, social, and cultural. Emerging from Enlightenment ideals of universal rights, feminism crystallized in the 19th century through activism for suffrage and property rights, and evolved through successive "waves" addressing education, reproductive autonomy, workplace equality, and representation. It remains both a historical force and a living intellectual tradition.

## you gotta know

- First-wave feminism (mid-19th to early-20th century) focused on suffrage and legal personhood, exemplified by the *Seneca Falls Convention* (1848) and the fight for voting rights.
- Second-wave feminism (1960s–1980s) expanded the agenda to reproductive rights, workplace discrimination, sexual assault, and the concept of gender as socially constructed rather than biologically fixed.
- Simone de Beauvoir's *The Second Sex* (1949) provided philosophical foundations, arguing that women are not born subordinate but made so by male-dominated societies.
- The movement intersects with anti-racism, LGBT rights, and labor activism; working-class women and women of color developed distinct feminist frameworks addressing compounded oppression.
- Key legal victories include access to contraception, abortion rights, equal pay legislation, and Title IX protections in education—each contested and fragile.
- Feminist literary and film criticism revealed how cultural narratives construct and reinforce gender hierarchies, influencing both scholarship and creative practice.
- Debates within feminism persist: over essentialism versus social construction, the relationship between choice and patriarchy, and how to address global inequalities alongside Western concerns.
