## summary

**John Steinbeck** was a premier twentieth-century American author whose gritty realist works chronicled the economic dispossession, migratory upheaval, and social realities of the *Great Depression* in the [[united-states]]. Operating out of *Salinas*, California, he gave indelible narrative form to the dislocation of the [[dust-bowl]], winning the 1940 *Pulitzer Prize* and the 1962 *Nobel Prize in Literature*. His sharp moral scrutiny of labour migration, corporate agriculture, and human vulnerability established him as one of the definitive social chroniclers in modern world literature.

## you gotta know

- Wrote *The Grapes of Wrath* (1939), which traced the harrowing westward trek of the dispossessed *Joad* family along *Route 66* from Oklahoma to California, capturing the agricultural devastation of the [[dust-bowl]] and galvanising national debates over the [[new-deal]].
- Authored the celebrated Depression-era novella *Of Mice and Men* (1937), exploring the fractured dreams of displaced itinerant farmhands *George Milton* and *Lennie Small* amid the harsh realities of rural California.
- Set many of his most renowned literary masterpieces in the agricultural landscapes of his native *Salinas Valley* and *Monterey*, earning the region the enduring geographical moniker *Steinbeck Country* through works such as *Cannery Row* (1945) and *Tortilla Flat* (1935).
- Completed his sprawling, multi-generational epic *East of Eden* (1952), retelling the biblical narrative of *Cain* and *Abel* across the *Trask* and *Hamilton* families and investigating moral agency through the Hebrew theological concept of *timshel*.
- Served as a dedicated war correspondent during [[world-war-ii]] for the *New York Herald Tribune*, reporting from combat zones in the European and Mediterranean theatres, including *London*, *North Africa*, and *Italy*, before authoring the anti-fascist resistance novella *The Moon Is Down* (1942).
- Embarked on a notable 1947 journalistic journey through the [[soviet-union]] alongside Hungarian-American photojournalist *Robert Capa*, producing *A Russian Journal* to demystify everyday Soviet life for American readers at the onset of the [[cold-war]].
- Traversed forty American states in a custom truck camper named *Rocinante* accompanied by his French poodle *Charley*, producing the classic 1962 travelogue *Travels with Charley: In Search of America* shortly before winning the *Nobel Prize in Literature*.
- Collaborated closely with marine biologist *Ed Ricketts*, undertaking a pioneering biological expedition documented in *The Log from the Sea of Cortez* (1951), an interdisciplinary union of ecological philosophy, taxonomy, and literature that deeply shaped Steinbeck's ecological perspective.

## connections

- [[the-grapes-of-wrath]] — his defining novel depicting Oklahoma migrants fleeing environmental ruin and exploitation.
- [[united-states]] — his homeland whose economic turbulence, labor strife, and regional geographies anchored his work.
- [[dust-bowl]] — the ecological disaster that displaced thousands of tenant farmers whose plight he documented.
- [[modernism]] — the literary era in which his socially conscious naturalism challenged high-aesthetic formalist conventions.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict he reported on as an overseas war correspondent and anti-fascist propagandist.
- [[soviet-union]] — communist superpower he toured in 1947 to document ordinary postwar life behind the Iron Curtain.
- [[charles-dickens]] — Victorian literary predecessor whose crusading social realism and sympathy for the destitute anticipated Steinbeck's own method.
- [[new-york-city]] — major publishing capital where he spent his final decades and wrote his later observational essays.
