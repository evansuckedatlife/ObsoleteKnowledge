## summary

The **Battle of Appomattox Court House** (also designated the **Battle of Appomattox** or the **Surrender at Appomattox**) was fought on the morning of April 9, 1865, in Appomattox County, Virginia. It was the final engagement of Confederate General *Robert E. Lee*'s beleaguered Army of Northern Virginia before its capitulation to Union Lieutenant General *Ulysses S. Grant*. This climactic encounter effectively ended the military conflict of the [[american-civil-war]] in Virginia, triggered the collapse of remaining Confederate field armies throughout the South, and began the political transition toward the [[reconstruction-era]].

## you gotta know

- Concluded the Appomattox Campaign on Palm Sunday, April 9, 1865, when Confederate General *Robert E. Lee* surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia to Union Lieutenant General *Ulysses S. Grant*, establishing Union preservation for the [[united-states]].
- Hosted in the parlor of the *McLean House*, owned by grocer *Wilmer McLean*, whose previous home in Manassas served as Confederate headquarters during the First Battle of Bull Run, giving rise to the famous adage that the conflict began in his front yard and ended in his front parlor.
- Began with an early morning Confederate assault led by Major General *John B. Gordon*, who broke through Union cavalry lines before realizing his infantry was boxed in by the arrival of two Union corps under Major General *Edward Ord* and Brigadier General *Charles Griffin*.
- Yielded remarkably lenient terms crafted in accordance with the wishes of President *Abraham Lincoln*, allowing Confederate officers and enlisted men to return home on parole unmolested by federal treason charges so long as they obeyed their oaths.
- Permitted Confederate cavalrymen and artillerymen who brought their own horses and mules to keep their animals for spring agricultural planting, while Grant ordered twenty-five thousand rations distributed to feed Lee's famished soldiers.
- Included the famous personal contrast between the commanders: *Robert E. Lee* wore an immaculate full-dress uniform and an ornamental dress sword, whereas *Ulysses S. Grant* arrived in a mud-spattered field uniform with only general stars to denote his rank.
- Drafted and transcribed by Lieutenant Colonel *Ely S. Parker*, an indigenous Seneca lawyer and engineer serving as Grant's military secretary, to whom Lee remarked, "I am glad to see one real American here," prompting Parker's response, "We are all Americans."
- Culminated on April 12 in a formal stacking of arms presided over by Union Brigadier General *Joshua Chamberlain*, who ordered his federal soldiers to present arms in a respectful military salute, which Confederate General *John B. Gordon* acknowledged with his sword.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the four-year armed struggle between the Union and the Confederacy that effectively reached its military resolution through this surrender.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-war era of political reintegration, emancipation, and constitutional reform initiated in the immediate wake of Confederate collapse.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic preserved as a single nation through the military defeat of Southern secessionist forces.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the landmark Reconstruction constitutional amendment granting citizenship and equal protection, born from the Union's hard-fought victory.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the fundamental governing document whose federal supremacy and indivisibility were decisively established on the battlefield.
- [[robert-e-lee]] — the celebrated Confederate military commander whose surrender marked the operational end of the Southern rebellion.
- [[abraham-lincoln]] — the Union president whose generous, conciliatory instructions directly informed Grant's lenient surrender terms days before Lincoln was assassinated.
