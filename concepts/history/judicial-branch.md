## summary

The **Judicial Branch** is the system of federal courts, headed by the *Supreme Court*, that interprets and applies the law and reviews whether government actions comply with the Constitution. One of the three coordinate powers in the *separation of powers* framework, it can strike down laws and executive actions deemed unconstitutional. The judiciary's power of *judicial review*, though not explicitly mentioned in the Constitution, grew from *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) and became central to American governance.

## you gotta know

- Consists of the *Supreme Court*, federal courts of appeals, and district courts, creating a hierarchy of judicial authority.
- The *Supreme Court* has nine justices appointed for life; lower federal judges also serve for life, insulating them from political pressure.
- Exercises *judicial review*: the power to invalidate laws or government actions it deems unconstitutional, a practice established in *Marbury v. Madison*.
- Subject to checks: Congress can propose constitutional amendments, impeach judges, set the Court's size, and define its jurisdiction; the President appoints justices (with Senate confirmation).
- Interprets the Constitution and federal statutes, mediating disputes between individuals and government, and between federal and state power.
- Major Court decisions (e.g., *Plessy v. Ferguson*, *Brown v. Board of Education*, *Roe v. Wade*) have reshaped American society on race, education, and rights.
- Federal judges are appointed for life to protect judicial independence from political pressure, though this also creates accountability questions when judges remain on the bench for decades.
- The Supreme Court's ability to overturn laws and presidential actions makes it a powerful co-equal branch; appointments of new justices can shift jurisprudence for generations to come.

## connections

- [[separation-of-powers]] — the constitutional principle distributing federal authority among three branches, of which the judiciary is one.
- [[checks-and-balances]] — the system by which each branch constrains the others; Congress confirms judges and can impeach them.
- [[legislative-branch]] — the co-equal branch that passes laws the courts interpret and may invalidate.
- [[executive-branch]] — the co-equal branch that enforces laws; its actions are subject to judicial review.
- [[united-states-supreme-court]] — the highest court and final arbiter of constitutional meaning.
