## summary

The **Iron Curtain** was the metaphorical and literal barrier separating communist Eastern Europe from Western Europe during the *Cold War*. The term, popularized by Winston Churchill in 1946, described the ideological, political, and physical divisions established by the *Soviet Union* across the continent after World War II. The Iron Curtain symbolized the division of Europe into two hostile blocs and remained the defining symbol of Cold War division until its collapse in 1989.

## you gotta know

- The term emerged from Churchill's "Sinews of Peace" speech (March 1946), warning that an "iron curtain has descended across the Continent"—a vivid metaphor that captured Western fears of permanent Soviet dominance of Eastern Europe.
- Represented both an ideological divide between communism and capitalism and physical barriers: the [[Berlin Wall]], barbed wire fences, anti-tank obstacles, electrified fences, and heavily militarized borders guarded by armed soldiers under shoot-on-sight orders.
- The *Soviet Union* established satellite states across Eastern Europe (Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria, East Germany), subordinating them to Moscow's control through military occupation, communist puppet governments, and secret police (the Stasi, Securitate, etc.) that surveilled populations.
- Accompanied by strict travel restrictions: citizens were largely prevented from moving between East and West, with families separated for decades; escape attempts meant risking death from border guards—thousands died trying to cross the Iron Curtain.
- The [[Berlin Wall]], constructed in 1961, became the physical embodiment of the Iron Curtain, symbolizing the division of Europe and the world; it ran 96 miles through Berlin, separating the city and demonstrating the desperation of the Cold War.
- Economic disparity grew as Western European economies thrived under capitalism and Marshall Plan aid while Soviet-dominated Eastern Europe stagnated under central planning, resource extraction to benefit the USSR, and repression of entrepreneurship and innovation.
- Dissolved with the collapse of communism in 1989-1991: the Berlin Wall fell in November 1989, Soviet satellites gained independence (Poland, Czechoslovakia, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria), the Soviet Union collapsed in 1991, and the ideological barrier dissolved as Russia itself transformed.
