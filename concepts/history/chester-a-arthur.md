## summary

**Chester A. Arthur** was the twenty-first President of the United States, serving from 1881 to 1885 during a crucial period of Gilded Age political modernization and naval revitalization. Assuming the presidency under agonizing national circumstances following the assassination of his predecessor, he confounded low public expectations by conducting an honest, dignified, and reform-minded administration. His tenure is celebrated for signing the *Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act*, commissioning the first modern steel warships of the *New Navy*, and pursuing government corruption cases regardless of party affiliation.

## you gotta know

- Enacted the historic *Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act* in January 1883, which established the *Civil Service Commission* and outlawed political assessments on federal employees, initiating the modern professional civil service.
- Sparked the technological rebirth of the United States Navy by authorizing the construction of the nation's first steel-hulled steam warships, famously remembered as the *ABCD Ships* (*Atlanta*, *Boston*, *Chicago*, and *Dolphin*).
- Vetoed an initial twenty-year ban on Chinese laborers as an excessive violation of treaty commitments before signing an amended ten-year measure known as the *Chinese Exclusion Act* of 1882.
- Prosecuted the notorious *Star Route Frauds* with rigorous impartiality, aggressively pursuing federal postal officials and political insiders who had embezzled millions through rigged mail-delivery contracts.
- Urged tariff reform to curb mounting treasury surpluses, appointing a nonpartisan commission that resulted in the compromised *Mongrel Tariff* of 1883.
- Completely refurbished the deteriorating White House upon taking office, refusing to move in until redecorating the residence with designer *Louis Comfort Tiffany* and auctioning off twenty-four wagonloads of old furnishings.
- Declined to wage an aggressive reelection campaign in 1884 due to advanced *Bright's disease*, winning wide bipartisan respect that prompted Mark Twain to remark that "it would be hard indeed to better President Arthur's administration."
