## summary

**Hosni Mubarak** (formally *Muhammad Hosni El Sayed Mubarak*) was an Egyptian military commander and statesman who served as the fourth president of Egypt from 1981 until his ouster during the 2011 Arab Spring. Rising through the ranks of the Egyptian Air Force, he achieved national prominence during the 1973 October War and became vice president before assuming the presidency after the assassination of *Anwar Sadat*. Mubarak anchored Egypt's international standing by preserving the Camp David Accords in the [[arab-israeli-conflict]] and sustaining an intimate strategic partnership with the [[united-states]], while maintaining a pervasive internal police state governed under perpetual emergency law.

## you gotta know

- Assumed the presidency on October 14, 1981, following the assassination of *Anwar Sadat* by radical soldiers during a military parade commemorating the crossing of the Suez Canal, an attack in which Mubarak was wounded on the review stand.
- Forced from office on February 11, 2011, after eighteen days of historic, mass anti-government demonstrations centered in Cairo's Tahrir Square, ceding executive authority to the Supreme Council of the Armed Forces.
- Rose to national fame as commander of the Egyptian Air Force during the 1973 Yom Kippur War, orchestrating the opening surprise aerial offensive against Israeli installations in the Sinai Peninsula.
- Preserved Egypt's controversial 1979 peace treaty with Israel amid continuing turmoil in the [[arab-israeli-conflict]], restoring severed diplomatic relations with neighboring Arab capitals and converting Cairo into an indispensable intermediary for regional peace initiatives.
- Governed Egypt under an uninterrupted state of emergency via Law No. 162 of 1958, which suspended constitutional safeguards, curtailed press freedoms, enabled indefinite detentions without trial, and suppressed opposition organizations like the Muslim Brotherhood.
- Survived a notorious assassination attempt in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in June 1995, when armed gunmen affiliated with Egyptian Islamic Jihad and backed by Sudan ambushed his armored motorcade en route to an Organization of African Unity summit.
- Stood trial publicly following his deposition while confined to a hospital bed inside a metal courtroom cage in Cairo, initially receiving a life sentence in 2012 before being cleared of responsibility for protester deaths upon appeal and freed in 2017.

## connections

- [[arab-israeli-conflict]] — the defining dispute in the [[middle-east]] in which Mubarak steadfastly preserved the cold peace established with Israel by his predecessor.
- [[middle-east]] — the geopolitical crossroads where Egypt under Mubarak served as a pro-Western anchor and diplomatic heavyweight.
- [[united-states]] — the superpower partner that furnished billions of dollars in economic and military assistance to ensure Egyptian compliance with regional security agreements.
- [[cold-war]] — the global confrontation during which Mubarak guided Egypt firmly into Washington's orbit following decades of prior reliance on Moscow.
- [[soviet-union]] — the rival superpower where Mubarak received advanced aviation and staff instruction at the Frunze Military Academy in the 1960s.
- [[islam]] — the faith that formed the ideological foundation of Mubarak's domestic adversaries, encompassing nonviolent reformist groups as well as armed fundamentalist factions.
