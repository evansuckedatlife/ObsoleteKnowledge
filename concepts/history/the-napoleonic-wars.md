## summary

The *Napoleonic Wars* (1803–1815) were a series of military conflicts waged by *France* under *Napoleon Bonaparte* against various European coalitions seeking to contain French revolutionary and imperial expansion. The wars reshaped the political map of Europe, temporarily unified vast territories under French hegemony, and ultimately triggered a conservative reaction that established the foundations of the European state system lasting until *World War I*.

## you gotta know

- *Napoleon*, rising to power during the chaos of the *French Revolution*, transformed France's revolutionary cause into imperial ambition, fighting to export revolutionary ideals (and French dominance) while consolidating personal power; he declared himself Emperor in 1804.
- The *Continental System*—Napoleon's economic blockade of *Britain*—attempted to strangle the British economy and isolate it from European trade; this policy both prolonged the conflict and drove Britain to support anti-French coalitions.
- Napoleon's Grand Army invaded *Russia* in 1812 with over 600,000 troops, seeking to break Russian resistance; the Russian winter and scorched-earth tactics destroyed the Grande Armée, killing hundreds of thousands and shattering the myth of French military invincibility.
- The Wars of the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, and Seventh Coalitions pit European monarchies (Britain, Austria, Prussia, Russia) against France in successive waves; each coalition broke apart until the final coalitions overwhelming French resources.
- *Lord Nelson*'s victory at the *Battle of Trafalgar* (1805) secured British naval supremacy for over a century, preventing French invasion and forcing Napoleon to pursue continental dominance instead.
- Napoleon's defeat at *Waterloo* (June 1815), where British, Prussian, and Dutch forces under *Wellington* and *Blücher* routed the French, ended his Hundred Days after escape from exile and concluded the Wars; his abdication and exile to *Saint Helena* closed the revolutionary era in Europe.
