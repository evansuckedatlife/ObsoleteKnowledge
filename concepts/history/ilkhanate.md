## summary

The **Ilkhanate** was a southwestern successor state of the Mongol Empire, founded in the mid-thirteenth century by *Hulagu Khan*, grandson of *Genghis Khan*. Ruling over Persia, Iraq, Azerbaijan, and portions of Anatolia, it radically reshaped the political landscape of the Islamic world following the devastating 1258 sack of Baghdad. Over time, the dynasty integrated Persian administrative traditions, adopted *Islam* under *Ghazan Khan*, and sparked a dazzling cultural renaissance in miniature painting, architecture, and historical scholarship along the *Silk Road*.

## you gotta know

- Founded around 1256 by *Hulagu Khan* during the western campaigns commissioned by the Great Khan *Möngke*, establishing the title "Ilkhan" meaning subordinate khan.
- Captured Baghdad in 1258, executing the Abbasid caliph *al-Musta'sim* and terminating more than five centuries of the Abbasid Caliphate.
- Halted in its western expansion toward Egypt and North Africa at the 1260 Battle of Ain Jalut by the Mamluk Sultanate of Cairo.
- Frequently fought against its northern neighbor, the Golden Horde, forming diplomatic and military alliances with Western European Christian monarchs in an attempt to capture the Levant.
- Converted definitively to *Islam* under *Ghazan Khan* in 1295, a transformation that ended state preference for *Buddhism* and Nestorian *Christianity* and revitalized Persian bureaucratic institutions.
- Emitted significant literary and scholarly works under vizier *Rashid al-Din Hamadani*, who authored the *Jami al-Tawarikh* (*Compendium of Chronicles*), widely considered the world's first universal history.
- Constructed monumental Persian-Mongol architecture, best exemplified by the magnificent turquoise-domed Mausoleum of Oljaytu at the capital city of Soltaniyeh.
- Disintegrated rapidly in the 1330s following the death of its last effective ruler, *Abu Sa'id*, splintering into warring successor factions until conquered by *Timur* (*Tamerlane*).

## connections

- [[middle-east]] — the geographic theater across Persia and Mesopotamia where the Ilkhanate established sovereign control.
- [[silk-road]] — the transcontinental trade network revitalized under the Pax Mongolica, facilitating vibrant cultural and commercial exchange between East and West.
- [[islam]] — the faith embraced by *Ghazan Khan* in 1295, which became the official religion of the Ilkhanid realm.
- [[china]] — the eastern seat of the Mongol Empire governed by the Yuan dynasty, maintaining close diplomatic, dynastic, and artistic ties with the Ilkhans.
- [[buddhism]] — the initial religion practiced by early Ilkhans like *Hulagu* and *Arghun* before the state's official Islamic conversion.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the Anatolian state that rose to preeminence in the power vacuum left by the collapse of the Seljuks and Ilkhanids.
