## summary

**Philip VI**, also known as **Philip of Valois**, reigned as the first King of [[france]] from the cadet *House of Valois* from 1328 until his death in 1350. His controversial accession following the extinction of the senior *Capetian* male line prompted King [[edward-iii]] of [[england]] to press a direct claim to the French crown, precipitating the devastating [[hundred-years-war]]. Confronted by catastrophic military defeats, severe economic upheaval, and the catastrophic arrival of the *Black Death*, his reign marked a turbulent watershed that fundamentally reshaped medieval European monarchy and chivalric warfare.

## you gotta know

- Suffered a catastrophic defeat against King [[edward-iii]] of [[england]] at the *Battle of Crécy* in 1346, where disorganized French cavalry charges and Genoese mercenary crossbowmen were thoroughly obliterated by disciplined English defensive formations deploying the [[longbow]].
- Claimed the throne of [[france]] in 1328 upon the death of his cousin *Charles IV*, becoming the founding monarch of the *House of Valois* by invoking *Salic Law* to bar female succession and reject English dynastic inheritance.
- Confiscated the vast continental fiefdom of the *Duchy of Aquitaine* from [[edward-iii]] in 1337 on grounds of feudal contumacy and disloyalty, an aggressive assertion of sovereign royal rights that formally ignited the [[hundred-years-war]].
- Endured the devastating annihilation of the French fleet at the naval *Battle of Sluys* in 1340, surrendering maritime dominance over the English Channel to [[england]] and enabling unchecked English coastal raids and continental invasions.
- Ruled during the terrifying initial outbreak of the *Black Death* between 1348 and 1350, an epidemic that decimated more than a third of the population across [[france]], crippling agricultural labor, commerce, and royal tax revenues.
- Acquired the southeastern province of the *Dauphiné* in 1349 through purchase from *Humbert II*, initiating the enduring historical tradition whereby the French crown prince held the hereditary title of *Dauphin*.
- Institutionalized the *gabelle* in 1341, establishing an onerous permanent royal monopoly and mandatory excise tax on salt that generated indispensable wartime revenue despite stoking widespread peasant, urban, and noble resentment.
- Crushed a massive peasant uprising in the County of Flanders at the *Battle of Cassel* in 1328 early in his reign, securing the frontier and reinstating the loyal pro-French count *Louis I*.

## connections

- [[hundred-years-war]] — prolonged Anglo-French dynastic struggle sparked when he confiscated Gascony and disputed the French crown.
- [[edward-iii]] — English rival and Plantagenet nephew of *Charles IV* who claimed the French throne and routed his army at *Crécy*.
- [[france]] — sovereign realm he governed as the founder of the cadet *House of Valois*.
- [[england]] — rival kingdom whose monarchs challenged his legitimacy and launched devastating military campaigns across his territories.
- [[longbow]] — devastating missile weapon whose superior range and rate of fire dismantled his chivalric cavalry charges at *Crécy*.
- [[medieval-feudalism]] — socio-political and military framework that governed his vassal relations and defined his reliance on chivalric knightly levies.
- [[italy]] — Italian peninsula from which he recruited Genoese mercenary crossbowmen to support his royal army against English archers.
