## summary

**Hugh Thompson Jr.** was an American military aviator during the *Vietnam War* who courageously intervened to halt the slaughter of unarmed Vietnamese civilians during the 1968 *My Lai Massacre*. Flying an *OH-23 Raven* observation helicopter, he landed his aircraft directly between advancing infantrymen and terrified villagers, instructing his crew to train their weapons on fellow soldiers if the killings continued. Despite facing initial hostility, cover-ups, and threats of court-martial from military authorities, his defiance of illegal commands became a defining exemplar of battlefield ethics and the practical enforcement of modern [[humanitarian-law]].

## you gotta know

- Intervened during the *My Lai Massacre* on March 16, 1968, repeatedly setting his *OH-23 Raven* helicopter down in Son My village to rescue unarmed non-combatants from troops of Charlie Company, 1st Battalion, 20th Infantry Regiment.
- Ordered his crew members, door gunner *Lawrence Colburn* and crew chief *Glenn Andreotta*, to train their machine guns on American troops under Lieutenant *William Calley* and fire if the infantry attempted to execute civilians hiding inside an earthen bunker.
- Coordinated the extraction of imperiled villagers by signaling accompanying gunships to transport them to safety, directly saving at least ten Vietnamese non-combatants from imminent execution.
- Discovered an injured four-year-old child, *Do Ba*, clinging to life in an irrigation ditch filled with executed villagers, personally carrying the boy aboard his helicopter to fly him to an *Army* hospital at Quang Ngai.
- Radioed reports of civilian slaughter directly to his command post at landing zone Dottie, prompting an urgent cease-fire order from Lieutenant Colonel *Frank Barker* that halted the ground assault after more than five hundred civilians had been killed.
- Endured severe ostracism and death threats after testifying before Congress and military courts, including public condemnation from House Armed Services Committee chairman *L. Mendel Rivers*, who sought to court-martial him for treason.
- Awarded the *Soldier's Medal* along with *Lawrence Colburn* and the posthumously recognized *Glenn Andreotta* in 1998, three decades after the massacre, in formal recognition of their extraordinary heroism under fire.
- Lectured regularly in his later years at the *United States Military Academy* at West Point on the moral obligation of armed service members to recognize and disobey unlawful orders.
