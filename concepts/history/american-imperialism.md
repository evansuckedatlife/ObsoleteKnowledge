## summary

**American Imperialism** refers to the *United States*' expansionist foreign policy from the 1890s through the early twentieth century, asserting military, economic, and political dominance over weaker nations and territories in the Western Hemisphere and the Pacific. Championed by figures like *Theodore Roosevelt*, it drove the acquisition of colonial territories, intervention in Latin America and the Caribbean, and a vision of the U.S. as a great power destined to reshape the global order. This doctrine marked a departure from nineteenth-century isolationism and laid groundwork for American global supremacy.

## you gotta know

- Peaked during the *Spanish-American War* (1898), when the U.S. defeated Spain and acquired Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines as overseas territories, transforming America into a colonial power.
- *Theodore Roosevelt* crystallized the doctrine through the *Roosevelt Corollary* to the *Monroe Doctrine*, asserting the U.S. right to intervene throughout the Western Hemisphere to prevent European interference.
- Justified through contemporary ideologies of racial and cultural superiority—the "civilizing mission"—that rationalized control over non-European peoples and territories deemed incapable of self-government.
- Built infrastructure projects like the *Panama Canal* (completed 1914) to project American naval power, facilitate commerce, and reassert dominance over Central America and the Caribbean.
- Extended to military interventions in Cuba, Nicaragua, Haiti, and the Dominican Republic, installing friendly regimes and protecting American business interests, often against nationalist movements.
- Reflected broader American strategy to compete with European empires by emphasizing economic control and political influence over formal annexation, though substantial territorial acquisitions persisted in the Pacific.
- Waned after World War I as American focus shifted to European entanglements, though economic imperialism through corporate investment and diplomatic pressure continued throughout the twentieth century.
