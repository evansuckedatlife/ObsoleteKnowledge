## summary

**Donald Maclean** (1913–1983) was a Soviet intelligence agent who infiltrated the upper reaches of the British Foreign Office during the Cold War. As one of *the Cambridge Five*, the most damaging spy ring of the era, he passed classified diplomatic cables and atomic secrets to Moscow for decades before defecting to the USSR in 1951. His betrayal symbolized the Cambridge spy ring's penetration of British institutional power.

## you gotta know

- Recruited as a Soviet agent while at Cambridge University in the 1930s, part of a cohort of elite Cambridge intellectuals sympathetic to communism.
- Joined the Foreign Office in 1935 and rose through the ranks, gaining access to sensitive diplomatic cables and intelligence assessments.
- Served as Secretary to the British Ambassador in Washington (1944–1948), giving Soviet handlers direct access to American-British strategic planning.
- Passed atomic secrets to the USSR, including details of American atomic weapons development and the composition of British teams working on the bomb.
- Lived in growing paranoia and alcoholism as the FBI and MI5 closed in on the spy ring; Soviet handlers pressured him to defect.
- Fled to Moscow in 1951, one step ahead of arrest, becoming a propaganda asset for the Soviets and remaining abroad for the rest of his life.
- His defection exposed the fragility of British security and the vulnerability of the Cambridge system of recruiting gentleman-amateurs into sensitive posts.
- As part of the Cambridge Five, Maclean's espionage was among the most damaging intelligence breaches of the Cold War, jeopardizing British and American military secrets for an entire decade.
- His alcoholism and instability made him unreliable even for his Soviet handlers, who eventually exfiltrated him as security tightened around the spy ring.
- After fleeing to Moscow, Maclean lived under an assumed name, eventually dying in obscurity, a stark contrast to his prestigious position in the British establishment.
