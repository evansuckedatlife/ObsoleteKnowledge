## summary

The **Acropolis of Athens**, commonly known simply as the **Acropolis**, is an ancient citadel perched upon a steep limestone outcrop rising above the city of Athens in [[ancient-greece]]. Fortified since the Mycenaean Bronze Age, the sanctuary reached its artistic and political zenith during the fifth century BCE under the democratic statesman *Pericles*, who commissioned monuments celebrating civic identity and military victory. As the architectural home of the monumental [[parthenon]], the sacred hill remains the preeminent symbol of Classical Greek cultural achievement, monumental architecture, and civic pride in world history.

## you gotta know

- The monumental building program of the fifth century BCE was initiated by the Athenian statesman *Pericles* using funds from the Delian League, transforming the plateau into the crowning architectural showcase of classical democracy after its destruction by the Persians.
- The citadel's defining monument, the [[parthenon]], was dedicated to *Athena Parthenos* and designed by the architects *Ictinus* and *Callicrates* under the artistic direction of *Phidias*, who sculpted its colossal chryselephantine statue of gold and ivory.
- The *Erechtheion*, an asymmetric Ionic temple built to house the most sacred ancestral relics of Athens, features the famous *Porch of the Caryatids*, where six sculpted maiden statues serve as weight-bearing architectural columns.
- The monumental entrance complex is the *Propylaea*, designed by the architect *Mnesicles*, which stands adjacent to the delicate, amphiprostyle *Temple of Athena Nike* overlooking the primary western approach to the summit.
- The southern slope accommodated major performance venues, most notably the *Theatre of Dionysus*, where classical playwrights competed during civic religious festivals that fostered the birth of Western [[tragedy]].
- Between 1801 and 1812, the British diplomat *Lord Elgin* controversially removed roughly half of the surviving sculptures from the Parthenon and shipped them to [[england]], sparking an international cultural patrimony dispute that continues today.
- During the Morean War in 1687, Venetian forces under *Francesco Morosini* besieged the citadel and detonated an Ottoman gunpowder storehouse housed in the Parthenon, destroying the temple's roof and collapsing its inner cella.
- Nineteenth-century excavations revealed the *Perserschutt*, or Persian rubble, a ceremonial deposit of damaged Archaic votive statues—including the transitional *Kritios Boy* and the *Moschophoros*—ritually buried by returning citizens following the Persian sack of 480 BCE.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the civilization whose democratic Golden Age, classical philosophy, and architectural orders reached their peak on the hill.
- [[parthenon]] — the supreme Doric temple constructed on the citadel summit to honor Athena and safeguard the Athenian treasury.
- [[tragedy]] — the dramatic art form cultivated at the Theatre of Dionysus on the southern slope of the rocky sanctuary.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial realm that restored Athenian monuments, built the Odeon of Herodes Atticus, and constructed a temple to Augustus and Rome.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the ruling power that fortified the complex as a military stronghold, where an ammunition magazine catastrophically exploded in 1687.
- [[england]] — the nation whose British Museum houses the contested Elgin Marbles removed from the summit in the nineteenth century.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural revival whose scholars and architects rediscovered classical orders through drawings and descriptions of the Athenian acropolis.
