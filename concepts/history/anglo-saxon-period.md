## summary

The **Anglo-Saxon period** spans more than six centuries of British history, from the collapse of direct Roman administration in *Britannia* around 410 until the decisive Norman Conquest of [[england]] in 1066. Frequently designated as **Anglo-Saxon England**, this era witnessed the gradual migration and settlement of continental Germanic tribes—principally the *Angles*, *Saxons*, and *Jutes*—who carved out regional polities that ultimately coalesced into a single unified nation. It represents the foundational crucible of English common law, territorial administration, ecclesiastical heritage, vernacular literature, and national identity.

## you gotta know

- Concluded dramatically at the *Battle of Hastings* in 1066, where *William the Conqueror* defeated King *Harold Godwinson*, ending native Anglo-Saxon dynastic rule and inaugurating Norman feudal hegemony across [[england]].
- King *Alfred the Great* of *Wessex* repelled catastrophic Danish incursions, negotiated the partitioned territory known as the *Danelaw*, constructed fortified borough networks termed *burhs*, and sponsored vernacular literacy by commissioning the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*.
- Early political organisation coalesced around the *Heptarchy*, a shifting confederacy of seven regional kingdoms—*Northumbria*, *Mercia*, *East Anglia*, *Essex*, *Wessex*, *Sussex*, and *Kent*—whose most powerful monarchs claimed the overarching title of *bretwalda*.
- Produced masterworks of Old English [[poetry]], most famously the monumental heroic epic *Beowulf* preserved in the unique *Nowell Codex*, blending ancestral pagan Germanic martial codes with emerging Christian theological motifs.
- Underwent thorough spiritual transformation through the arrival of the Gregorian mission led by *Saint Augustine of Canterbury* in 597, alongside the flourishing of northern Insular monasticism centered at the holy island of *Lindisfarne*.
- Excavations at the seventh-century royal ship burial of *Sutton Hoo* in Suffolk revealed breathtaking garnet-inlaid gold regalia and an iconic crested iron helmet, thoroughly overturning nineteenth-century notions of the era as an illiterate "Dark Age."
- Documented extensively by the scholarly monk Saint *Bede* in his 731 masterpiece *Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum*, which popularized the *Anno Domini* dating convention and provided the primary narrative of early English ethnogenesis.
- Instituted enduring legal and governmental structures, including royal advisory assemblies known as the *witenagemot*, administrative subdivisions termed *shires* governed by executive *shire-reeves*, and codified restitution schedules called *wergild*.

## connections

- [[england]] — the unified realm forged through the defensive expansion and administrative consolidation of the House of Wessex against Viking threats.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical imperial power whose fifth-century withdrawal from the province of *Britannia* created the territorial vacuum settled by Germanic tribes.
- [[christianity]] — the institutional faith that unified disparate regional kings, introduced Latin literacy, and fostered renowned monastic centers of book illumination.
- [[france]] — the continental realm whose Norman aristocracy mounted the pivotal 1066 invasion, fundamentally redirecting English linguistic and political evolution.
- [[poetry]] — the celebrated artistic medium encompassing heroic verse, elegiac riddles, and devotional narratives composed in alliterative Old English meter.
- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline tracing how early West Germanic dialects brought across the North Sea developed into historical Old English.
- [[germany]] — the broader ancestral homeland in continental northwestern Europe from which Saxon, Anglian, and Jute warbands originally migrated.
