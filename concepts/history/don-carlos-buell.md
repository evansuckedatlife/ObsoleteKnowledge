## summary

**Don Carlos Buell** was an American regular army officer and Union major general during the *[[american-civil-war]]*, recognized for his leadership of the Army of the Ohio. A methodical organizer and strict disciplinarian, he played a pivotal role in the Western Theater by marching his forces to Pittsburg Landing on April 6, 1862, turning the tide on the second day of the Battle of Shiloh. However, his deliberate campaigning style, conservative approach to Southern civil property, and indecisive leadership at the Battle of Perryville alienated federal authorities, leading to his permanent removal from field command in late 1862.

## you gotta know

- Graduated from West Point in the class of 1841 and earned distinction during the Mexican-American War, receiving two brevet promotions for gallantry at Monterrey and Churubusco.
- Placed in charge of the Department of the Ohio in November 1861, applying exacting administrative rigor to forge raw volunteer recruits into one of the most disciplined field armies in the Union.
- Captured Nashville, Tennessee, in late February 1862 without significant opposition following the collapse of Confederate river defenses, securing an invaluable manufacturing and logistics hub.
- Rushed his divisions across flooded rivers to reach *[[ulysses-grant]]* at Pittsburg Landing, ferrying fresh troops across the Tennessee River on the evening of April 6 to lead the massive counteroffensive at Shiloh the next morning.
- Engaged in an acrimonious and lifelong public feud with *[[ulysses-grant]]* and *William Tecumseh Sherman*, vehemently asserting that his arrival had prevented the wholesale destruction of the Army of the Tennessee.
- Commanded the Union army during the Battle of Perryville on October 8, 1862, where an atmospheric "acoustic shadow" prevented sound from traveling to his headquarters two miles away, leaving him unaware of the pitched combat until late afternoon.
- Refused to pursue General *Braxton Bragg's* retreating Confederate Army of Mississippi into Tennessee after Perryville, citing logistics and the exhaustion of his troops, which infuriated President *Abraham Lincoln*.
- Strongly opposed the hard-war policies advocated by Radical Republicans, adhering strictly to a conciliatory stance that protected Southern property rights and prohibited Union soldiers from harboring escaped slaves.
- Relieved of command on October 24, 1862, and replaced by *William S. Rosecrans*, spending subsequent months testifying before an inconclusive military commission that ended his operational military career.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the national conflict in which he commanded the Army of the Ohio across critical campaigns in Kentucky and Tennessee.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose constitutional integrity and regular military establishment he served for over two decades.
- [[ulysses-grant]] — his peer and superior commander whose vulnerable lines at Shiloh were reinforced by Buell's timely arrival.
- [[albert-sidney-johnston]] — the Confederate general whose daring surprise offensive at Shiloh was blunted before Johnston was mortally wounded on the field.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the turbulent postwar era in which Buell managed mining enterprises in Kentucky and persistently defended his conservative military record.
- [[western-theater]] — the vast geographic theater between the Appalachian Mountains and the Mississippi River where Buell fought all his major Civil War engagements.
