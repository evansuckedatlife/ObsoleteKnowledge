## summary

The **Boxer Rebellion**, also known as the **Yihetuan Movement** or **Boxer Uprising**, was an anti-foreign, anti-colonial, and anti-Christian peasant uprising in northern [[china]] between 1899 and 1901 during the twilight of the Qing dynasty. Driven by the *Militia United in Righteousness*, a secret martial-arts society known colloquially as the "Boxers," the insurgents sought to violently expel foreign commercial, political, and spiritual influence. Encouraged by *Empress Dowager Cixi*, the unrest escalated into a dramatic fifty-five-day siege of foreign diplomatic legations in Beijing before being crushed by the multinational *Eight-Nation Alliance*. The punitive peace settlement severely weakened the Qing imperial court, deepened Western and Japanese imperial penetration, and hastened the revolutionary overthrow of the Chinese monarchy.

## you gotta know

- Sparked by the *Militia United in Righteousness* (*Yihequan*), whose practitioners performed martial rituals and calisthenics they believed bestowed supernatural invulnerability to Western bullets, artillery, and bladed weapons.
- Culminated in the fifty-five-day *Siege of the International Legations* in Beijing, during which hundreds of foreign diplomats, guards, and thousands of Chinese converts to [[christianity]] took refuge inside the fortified *Legation Quarter* and *Beitang Cathedral*.
- Endorsed by *Empress Dowager Cixi*, who reversed earlier court suppression, issued an imperial decree declaring war against all foreign powers simultaneously, and ordered imperial troops under *Ronglu* to coordinate with the insurgents.
- Crushed by the *Eight-Nation Alliance*, a coalition consisting of the [[british-empire]], [[russian-empire]], [[japan]], [[france]], [[united-states]], [[germany]], [[italy]], and Austria-Hungary that mounted amphibious landings, captured Tianjin, and looted Beijing.
- Defied internally by southern governors who formed the *Mutual Defense Pact of the Southeastern Provinces*, wherein regional leaders like *Li Hongzhang*, *Zhang Zhidong*, and *Yuan Shikai* suppressed Boxers and dismissed imperial war edicts as illegitimate.
- Concluded by the punitive *Boxer Protocol* of September 1901, requiring the Qing government to pay an indemnity of 450 million taels of silver, execute sympathetic ministers, and permit foreign troops to garrison transit routes to the sea.
- Leveraged diplomatically by the [[united-states]] through Secretary of State *John Hay* to promote the [[open-door-policy]], with remitted excess indemnity payments later funding the Boxer Indemnity Scholarship program for Chinese scholars.
- Triggered in part by escalating anti-missionary hostility following the 1897 *Juye Incident*, in which the murder of two German Catholic missionaries prompted [[germany]] to seize Jiaozhou Bay and claim Shandong as a sphere of influence.

## connections

- [[china]] — the imperial setting where devastating military defeat and indemnity debt eroded Qing authority, precipitating the 1911 Xinhai Revolution.
- [[colonialism]] — the aggressive expansion of Western and Japanese territorial concessions and economic spheres that generated intense domestic resentment.
- [[british-empire]] — primary maritime power in East Asia whose vice-admiral *Edward Seymour* led the first unsuccessful expedition to relieve besieged legations.
- [[russian-empire]] — coalition member that exploited the rebellion to deploy two hundred thousand troops into Manchuria, escalating tensions with [[japan]].
- [[japan]] — emerging East Asian empire that contributed the largest contingent of ground forces to the relief expedition, proving its modern military capability.
- [[germany]] — participant whose minister *Klemens von Ketteler* was slain in Beijing, prompting Kaiser *Wilhelm II* to deliver the infamous "Hun speech."
- [[united-states]] — participant that dispatched forces from the Philippines and utilized the diplomatic aftermath to advocate for the [[open-door-policy]].
- [[christianity]] — foreign religion whose missionaries and indigenous converts were targeted systematically by the insurgents across northern provinces.
