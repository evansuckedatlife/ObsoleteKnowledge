## summary

**Massachusetts** is a northeastern state that has played a disproportionate role in American history, from colonial settlement and revolutionary leadership through industrial innovation and cultural influence. The state's trajectory mirrors key turning points in American development: independence, constitutional crisis, and social transformation.

## you gotta know

- Settled by Puritan colonists in 1620, establishing deeply religious communities that shaped American moral culture and constitutionalism.
- Birthplace of the American Revolution, hosting the Boston Massacre, Boston Tea Party, and pivotal early battles of the War for Independence.
- Site of *Shays' Rebellion* (1786–1787), a debt-driven uprising that exposed the weakness of the Articles of Confederation and spurred constitutional reform.
- The Massachusetts Constitution (1780), drafted by John Adams, was a model for the federal Constitution and included the first explicit right to revolution.
- Became the industrial epicenter of American manufacturing in the 19th century, pioneering the textile mill system and attracting waves of immigrant labor.
- Home to major universities (Harvard, MIT) and intellectual movements that shaped American philosophy, transcendentalism, and academic culture.
- The state produced countless figures who shaped American law, politics, and society—from John Adams to Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.
- Abolished slavery in 1783 through judicial interpretation (Commonwealth v. Jennison), becoming the first state to formally end slavery, setting a model for abolition.
- The *Salem Witch Trials* (1692–1693) represented a dark episode of religious hysteria that killed over 20 people and left an indelible mark on American cultural memory and democratic cautionary tales.
