## summary

**Corporate law**, also known as **company law**, is the body of jurisprudence governing the formation, capitalization, governance, and dissolution of incorporated business organizations. Developing alongside the rise of commercial joint-stock enterprises and the factory systems of the *[[industrial-revolution]]* in *[[england]]* and the *[[united-states]]*, it defines the complex rights and duties binding shareholders, directors, officers, and creditors. The field provides the legal architecture of modern enterprise capitalism by facilitating large-scale capital aggregation, delegating managerial authority, and balancing entrepreneurial risk against investor vulnerability.

## you gotta know

- The doctrine of limited liability shields company shareholders from personal liability for business debts, ensuring an equity investor risks only the capital contributed to the firm.
- In the landmark English decision *Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd* (1897), the House of Lords upheld the separate legal personality of an incorporated business, establishing the legal "corporate veil" that insulates owners from corporate obligations.
- Modern United States corporate chartering is dominated by Delaware due to its dedicated *Court of Chancery*, its flexible *General Corporation Law*, and an extensive body of sophisticated chancery jurisprudence.
- Foundational legal scholars *Adolf Berle* and *Gardiner Means* articulated the "separation of ownership and control" in *The Modern Corporation and Private Property* (1932), analyzing how dispersed public shareholders lose operational supervision to entrenched management.
- The business judgment rule creates a presumption that corporate directors act on an informed basis, in good faith, and in the honest belief that an action serves the best interests of the company, shielding board members from judicial second-guessing.
- Fiduciary duties of loyalty and care prohibit managerial self-dealing, bad faith, and misappropriation of corporate opportunities, reinforced in takeover contexts by doctrines like the *Revlon* standard and *Unocal* test.
- The historic Supreme Court case *Dartmouth College v. Woodward* (1819) held that corporate charters granted by states constitute binding contracts protected under the *[[united-states-constitution]]*, barring arbitrary state revocation.
- Piercing the corporate veil represents an equitable judicial remedy invoked to hold individual shareholders or parent corporations directly liable when an entity is operated merely as an undercapitalized alter ego to commit fraud.

## connections

- [[corporate-personhood]] — legal status that treats the business entity as an independent person capable of bearing constitutional rights and liabilities.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of rapid technological and manufacturing expansion that necessitated limited liability and corporate capital pooling.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — organic law whose contracts clause and fourteenth amendment fundamentally constrained state regulation of corporate charters.
- [[14th-amendment]] — reconstruction amendment whose due process and equal protection guarantees were historically interpreted to protect corporate property and enterprise rights.
- [[united-states]] — jurisdiction whose state-level competition and federal securities statutes produced the modern regime of corporate governance.
- [[england]] — common-law birthplace of early joint-stock ventures, the South Sea *Bubble Act*, and modern corporate entity doctrines.
