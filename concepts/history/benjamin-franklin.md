## summary

**Benjamin Franklin** (1706–1790) was an American polymath and statesman whose versatility and diplomatic skill made him indispensable to the American Revolution and the founding of the nation. A printer, inventor, scientist, and philosopher, Franklin embodied Enlightenment values and served as a bridge between the colonial cause and European powers, particularly France. His pragmatism and charm secured crucial foreign support without which American independence would have failed.

## you gotta know

- Born in Boston to a large family of modest means; founded his own printing business in Philadelphia and became wealthy through newspapers, printing, and the *Poor Richard's Almanack*.
- Pioneered scientific investigation of electricity through the famous kite experiment (c. 1752), proving lightning was electrical and inventing the lightning rod—practical applications of theoretical inquiry.
- Founded the *Junto*, a civic improvement society; helped establish Philadelphia's library (first lending library in America), hospital, and academy (predecessor to University of Pennsylvania).
- Represented colonial interests in London as a diplomatic agent, becoming a voice for American grievances against British policy before and during the Revolutionary War.
- Key negotiator of the Treaty of Paris (1783), securing American independence and advantageous territorial terms; his reputation and French connections were essential to victory.
- Served as a delegate to the Constitutional Convention (1787) and supported ratification, despite reservations, helping forge consensus during the founding of the republic.
