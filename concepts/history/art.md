## summary

**Art**, in the context of visual and fine art practices, is the creation of objects and images intended to be experienced aesthetically—to move, provoke, communicate, or represent reality or imagination. The history of Western art is marked by revolutionary shifts in technique, subject matter, and philosophy: the Renaissance's marriage of artistic skill with scientific anatomical knowledge, the Baroque's dramatic emotionalism, the Dutch Golden Age's bourgeois celebration of ordinary life, Romanticism's embrace of nature and emotion, and modernism's rejection of realistic representation. Art has always reflected and shaped the societies that produce it.

## you gotta know

- Medieval and early Renaissance art was primarily religious; artworks were devotional objects meant to inspire faith rather than display technical mastery or individual vision.
- The Renaissance (14th–17th centuries) revolutionized art by combining artistic skill with scientific understanding, particularly anatomy; artists like *Leonardo da Vinci* dissected cadavers to understand human structure.
- Linear perspective—a mathematical technique for depicting three-dimensional space on a flat surface—was developed during the Renaissance and became fundamental to Western representational art.
- The Dutch Golden Age (17th century) produced an explosion of secular art—landscapes, still lifes, domestic scenes—reflecting the wealth and taste of a rising merchant class rather than aristocratic or religious patronage.
- The Industrial Revolution changed art patronage; new wealthy merchants and industrialists became collectors, creating demand for portraiture, landscapes, and historical scenes that appealed to bourgeois sensibilities.
- The 19th century saw successive artistic revolutions: Romanticism (emotion and nature), Realism (accurate depiction of ordinary life), Impressionism (light and color perception), and eventually modernism's radical departures from representation.
- Modernism in the 20th century rejected the assumption that art should depict reality; Cubism, Surrealism, Abstract art, and Expressionism treated art as a vehicle for exploring perception, psychology, and pure form.

## connections

- [[renaissance]] — the transformative period when art merged with scientific anatomical knowledge and achieved unprecedented naturalism.
- [[human-anatomy]] — the scientific knowledge that Renaissance and later artists mastered to depict the body with accuracy and expressiveness.
- [[dutch-golden-age]] — a historical period of artistic flourishing centered on secular, merchant-class art patronage.
- [[leonardo-da-vinci]] — the archetypal Renaissance artist who unified artistic skill, scientific investigation, and creative genius.
- [[painting]] — the primary medium of Western fine art; central to the visual arts.
- [[sculpture]] — the three-dimensional art form that paralleled and complemented painting in Western art history.
- [[modernism]] — the movement that rejected representational art and explored abstraction, psychology, and pure form.
- [[perspective]] — the mathematical technique that enabled realistic spatial representation and transformed Renaissance art.
