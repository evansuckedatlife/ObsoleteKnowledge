## summary

**Hammurabi** was the sixth king of the *First Babylonian Dynasty* (r. 1792–1750 BCE), whose reign transformed Babylon from a minor city-state into the dominant power of *Mesopotamia*. He is best remembered for creating the *Code of Hammurabi*, one of history's earliest comprehensive written legal systems, which established standardized principles of justice, commerce, and governance. His reign marks the transition from localized tribal law to centralized, rational administration grounded in written statute.

## you gotta know

- Ascended to the throne of Babylon in 1792 BCE and spent his early reign consolidating power within a fragmented Mesopotamian landscape of warring city-states.
- Conducted a series of military campaigns that extended Babylonian territory from the *Persian Gulf* northward into Assyria, making Babylon the hegemonic power in Mesopotamia by the end of his reign.
- Created the *Code of Hammurabi* (ca. 1754 BCE), comprising 282 laws inscribed on a stone pillar (stele); the code covered property rights, commerce, family relations, trade, debt, and criminal justice, with punishments scaled by social class.
- Introduced the principle of proportional justice: the famous "eye for an eye" formula derived from his code, reflecting both punishment and social order based on one's station in society.
- Standardized weights, measures, and currencies across his empire to facilitate trade and taxation, laying the groundwork for rational economic administration.
- Styled himself *king of kings* and portrayed himself in inscriptions as chosen by the god *Shamash* to deliver justice; this divine sanction legitimized centralized rule and written law over customary traditions.
- The Code of Hammurabi survived through antiquity because it was physically inscribed on durable stone; ancient copies preserved it, making it one of the most complete legal documents to survive from the ancient world.
