## summary

**Journalism** is the practice of gathering, investigating, and reporting factual information for public distribution through newspapers, magazines, broadcasts, and digital media. As both a profession and a cultural force, journalism has served as a watchdog on power, a vehicle for public discourse, and a shaper of collective understanding. Its legal protections, ethical standards, and economic models have continuously evolved to balance the public's right to information against competing interests like privacy, national security, and commercial viability.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a systematic practice during the 18th and 19th centuries alongside print technology and mass literacy expansion.
- The penny press of the 1830s–1840s democratized news by making newspapers affordable to ordinary readers rather than just elites.
- Developed professional codes of ethics emphasizing factual accuracy, source protection, and independence from political control and commercial influence.
- American journalism received constitutional protection through the [[first-amendment]]; [[new-york-times-co-v-sullivan]] (1964) clarified that public criticism is protected speech.
- Investigative journalism emerged as a discipline in the 19th and 20th centuries, exposing corruption and injustice through detailed reporting.
- Digital technology and the internet have transformed journalism's business model, distribution methods, and competitive landscape since the 1990s.
