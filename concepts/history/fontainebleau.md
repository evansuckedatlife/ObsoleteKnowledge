## summary

**Fontainebleau**, specifically the **Château de Fontainebleau** or **Palace of Fontainebleau**, is one of the grandest royal châteaux in [[france]], situated thirty-four miles southeast of central Paris in the heart of a vast ancient forest. Extensively transformed from a medieval fortress into an Italianate palace by King *Francis I* beginning in 1528, the estate served as the primary cradle of the French [[renaissance]] and gave birth to the influential Mannerist School of Fontainebleau. The palace functioned as an unbroken residence for French sovereigns across eight centuries, witnessing crucial events from the Wars of Religion to the dramatic abdication of [[napoleon-bonaparte]].

## you gotta know

- Transformed by King *Francis I* from a neglected medieval hunting lodge into a Renaissance showpiece, employing Italian artists such as *Rosso Fiorentino*, *Francesco Primaticcio*, and *Niccolò dell'Abbate*.
- Served as the home and namesake of the School of Fontainebleau, an artistic movement celebrated for combining intricate woodwork, decorative plaster stuccowork, and mythological Mannerist frescoes.
- Features the famous horseshoe-shaped exterior staircase designed by architect *Jean Androuet du Cerceau*, upon which [[napoleon-bonaparte]] addressed the Imperial Guard during his emotional farewell in 1814.
- Provided the historical venue where King *Louis XIV* signed the 1685 Edict of Fontainebleau, an executive decree that revoked the *Edict of Nantes* and banned Protestant worship throughout France.
- Served as a luxurious prison for *Pope Pius VII* from 1812 to 1814 during his bitter political struggle against Napoleon, who pressured the pontiff into signing the Concordat of Fontainebleau.
- Hosted the signing of Napoleon's first unconditional abdication on April 6, 1814, in the Salon of the Abdication following the occupation of Paris by the Sixth Coalition.
- Escaped structural destruction during the [[french-revolution]] despite the systematic dispersal and auctioning of its royal furnishings and fine decorative arts by revolutionary tribunals.

## connections

- [[france]] — kingdom and empire whose heads of state continuously occupied, embellished, and governed from the palace.
- [[renaissance]] — pan-European artistic and intellectual movement that *Francis I* deliberately imported from Italy to transform his court.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — emperor who revitalized the estate, designated it an imperial palace, and enacted his 1814 abdication within its chambers.
- [[french-revolution]] — major upheaval that stripped the residence of its historical furniture while preserving its structural fabric.
- [[italy]] — artistic peninsula from which *Francis I* recruited the master painters, sculptors, and bronze casters who decorated the palace.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical civilization whose monumental architecture, columns, and pagan mythologies provided motifs for the royal staterooms.
