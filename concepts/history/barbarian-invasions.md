## summary

The **Barbarian Invasions**, also known as the **Migration Period**, refers to the profound movement and military expansion of diverse peoples across Europe between the fourth and sixth centuries CE. Spurred in large part by the sudden arrival of the *Huns* from Central Asia, waves of Germanic and Eurasian confederations breached the historic frontiers of the [[roman-empire]]. These widespread migrations precipitated the political fragmentation and ultimate collapse of imperial rule in the West, establishing successor barbarian kingdoms that laid the territorial foundations of medieval Europe across modern [[italy]], [[france]], [[spain]], and [[germany]].

## you gotta know

- Culminated in the catastrophic sack of [[rome]] in 410 CE by *Alaric I* and the *Visigoths*, which shattered the aura of imperial invulnerability and inspired *Saint Augustine* to write *The City of God*.
- Marked the formal demise of the Western [[roman-empire]] in 476 CE when the Germanic warlord *Odoacer* deposed the child emperor *Romulus Augustulus* and sent the imperial regalia to Constantinople.
- Began in earnest after the westward push of the *Huns* drove displaced Gothic populations across the Danube River, culminating in the 378 CE Battle of Adrianople where the *Visigoths* killed Emperor *Valens*.
- Suffered a collapse of northern frontier defenses during the Crossing of the Rhine in the frozen winter of 406 CE, allowing a vast coalition of *Vandals*, *Suebi*, and *Alans* to overrun Gaul and invade [[spain]].
- Witnessed a second, more thorough sack of [[rome]] in 455 CE by the *Vandals* under King *Gaiseric*, who plundered the city from their newly established maritime kingdom in North Africa.
- Saw the rise of powerful post-imperial regimes such as the *Ostrogothic Kingdom* under *Theodoric the Great*, who ruled [[italy]] while deliberately preserving classical administrative and legal traditions.
- Led to the unification of Gaul under the *Frankish Kingdom* of *Clovis I*, who abandoned paganism directly for Nicene [[christianity]] to forge an enduring alliance with the local Gallo-Roman populace and the papacy.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the classical superpower whose western administrative structures and border frontiers disintegrated under sustained migratory pressures.
- [[rome]] — the historic imperial metropolis whose successive sieges and sacks symbolized the decline of classical antiquity.
- [[italy]] — the imperial heartland transformed into a patchwork of Germanic kingdoms ruled successively by *Odoacer*, the *Ostrogoths*, and the *Lombards*.
- [[france]] — the formerly Roman province of Gaul that became the seat of the Merovingian *Frankish Kingdom* founded by *Clovis I*.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian territory contested by early invaders before becoming the center of the enduring *Visigothic Kingdom*.
- [[germany]] — the forested trans-Rhenish territory north of the Danube that served as the ancestral home of numerous migratory confederations.
- [[christianity]] — the institutional faith adopted and championed by incoming rulers, preserving literacy and classical culture amidst political fragmentation.
