## summary

**Lucius Verus** (130–169 CE) was a Roman emperor and co-ruler with *Marcus Aurelius* during the early Antonine period. Adopted by Emperor *Antoninus Pius* as a potential heir, Lucius Verus shared imperial power with his brother Marcus Aurelius from 161 to 169 CE—a unique experiment in dual rulership that generally functioned well despite their strikingly different personalities. While Marcus Aurelius embodied philosophical restraint and duty, Lucius Verus enjoyed luxury and military campaigns. His death in 169 CE left Marcus Aurelius as sole emperor and ushered in the reign of the increasingly unstable *Commodus*.

## you gotta know

- Adopted by Emperor *Antoninus Pius* along with the young *Marcus Aurelius*, both groomed as potential heirs to stabilize the succession.
- Upon Antoninus Pius's death in 161 CE, both adopted sons became emperors, creating a rare dual-reign arrangement that the Senate accepted without significant controversy.
- Personally commanded Roman forces during the Parthian War (162–166 CE), achieving military victories but also exposing the empire to plague and disease from the East.
- Contrasted sharply with his co-emperor: while Marcus Aurelius devoted himself to Stoic philosophy and righteous governance, Lucius Verus pursued pleasure, entertainment, and martial glory.
- Despite their contrasting temperaments, the two worked effectively together, dividing responsibilities so that each handled matters suited to his talents and inclinations.
- Died in 169 CE, possibly from plague or overexertion, leaving Marcus Aurelius as sole emperor and setting up the succession of the troubled *Commodus*.
