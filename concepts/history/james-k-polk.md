## summary

**James K. Polk** was the 11th President of the *United States* (1845–1849), serving a single term marked by aggressive territorial expansion and the *Mexican-American War*. A staunch advocate of *Manifest Destiny*, Polk orchestrated the acquisition of vast western territories including present-day California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, and New Mexico. His presidency represented the apex of American expansionism in the 19th century and set the stage for sectional conflict over slavery in the newly acquired lands.

## you gotta know

- Elected in 1844 as a "dark horse" candidate on a platform of territorial expansion and aggressive nationalism, defeating Henry Clay (who opposed Manifest Destiny) with the rallying cry "Fifty-Four Forty or Fight!" regarding Oregon territory.
- Campaigned for the annexation of Texas (independent since 1836) and exclusive American control of Oregon (claimed by both U.S. and Britain), pledging to extend American territory to the Pacific Ocean in all directions.
- Successfully annexed Texas in 1845, precipitating Mexican hostility and setting conditions for war; Mexico viewed Texas as a rebellious province and considered American annexation an act of aggression justifying military response.
- Led the nation through the Mexican-American War (1846–1848), personally directing strategy despite lacking military experience; American forces under Generals Zachary Taylor and Winfield Scott advanced deep into Mexico, capturing Mexico City itself.
- Acquired over 525,000 square miles of territory through the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), nearly doubling American territorial extent; Mexico received $15 million in compensation but lost half its national territory, including present-day California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, and most of New Mexico.
- Settled the Oregon boundary dispute with Britain diplomatically in 1846, establishing the 49th parallel as the U.S.-Canada border in the Pacific Northwest (abandoning the "Fifty-Four Forty or Fight!" slogan when cooler heads prevailed).
- Promised to serve only one term and kept that promise, declining nomination in 1848 despite significant popularity during his presidency; he maintained he sought only to accomplish his expansionist goals and then retire—though some historians argue he exhausted himself and his presidency.
