## summary

**Collectivization** was the sweeping policy enforced by *Joseph Stalin* in the [[soviet-union]] between 1928 and 1940 to consolidate individual peasant landholdings into state-controlled agricultural cooperatives. Launched alongside the *First Five-Year Plan*, the campaign sought to boost agricultural export revenues to fund rapid heavy industry and integrate rural peasants into a planned economy. The policy crushed the traditional peasantry, triggered devastating man-made famines including the *Holodomor* in Ukraine and the Kazakh famine, and radically altered Soviet society.

## you gotta know

- Initiated as a core component of the *First Five-Year Plan* to overcome the grain procurement crisis of 1927 and replace the decentralized peasant agriculture of the *New Economic Policy*.
- Targeted prosperous independent peasants labeled *kulaks* through a campaign of *dekulakization*, which resulted in mass arrests, confiscation of property, forced deportations to the *Gulag*, and executions.
- Grouped peasant households into two primary farming models: the *kolkhoz*, a collective farm where workers shared residual yields, and the *sovkhoz*, a directly owned state farm where laborers received regular state wages.
- Relied on the *Twenty-Five Thousanders*, a cadre of industrial workers and party loyalists dispatched from urban factories into the countryside to violently break rural resistance and supervise grain seizures.
- Triggered widespread rural rebellion where peasants slaughtered over a third of the country's livestock, destroyed machinery, and burned fields rather than surrender them to collective management.
- Prompted *Stalin* to publish the deceitful article *Dizzy with Success* in *Pravda* in March 1930, temporarily blaming overzealous local cadres for coercion before resuming aggressive requisitions months later.
- Produced catastrophic human famine from 1932 to 1933, causing millions of deaths across Soviet Ukraine, the North Caucasus, the Volga region, and Kazakhstan, where pastoral nomadic society was decimated.
- Enforced draconian extraction quotas through measures like the *Law of Spikelets*, which decreed execution or ten-year prison terms for gleaning fallen grain ears from collective fields.

## connections

- [[soviet-union]] — the socialist state where the policy reshaped the entire agrarian economy and social structure.
- [[russian-empire]] — the imperial predecessor whose *mir* commune traditions and agrarian legacy preceded Soviet agricultural restructuring.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the rapid state-directed modernisation push financed through grain requisitions extracted under this system.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict during which the collective farming apparatus supplied food rations under extreme wartime duress.
- [[cold-war]] — the ideological standoff where post-war Soviet agricultural policy remained locked into the collective framework established during the 1930s.
