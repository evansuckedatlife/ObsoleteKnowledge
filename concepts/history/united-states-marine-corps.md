# United States Marine Corps

## summary

The **United States Marine Corps** (USMC) is an amphibious and expeditionary military branch established in 1775 that evolved from a small maritime guard into a global power-projection force. During World War II, the Marines became the icon of American offensive power in the Pacific, executing island-hopping campaigns against Japan and defining the doctrine and mythology of modern amphibious warfare.

## you gotta know

- Officially established in 1775 alongside the Continental Navy, the Marines initially served as shipboard security and boarding parties; by the 20th century, they had evolved into a distinct expeditionary force specialized in rapid-deployment operations.
- The Marine Corps philosophy emphasized aggressive small-unit tactics, leadership at all levels, and rapid decision-making; this culture made them effective in the chaotic, decentralized fighting of island warfare against Japanese forces.
- In World War II, the Marines executed the *island-hopping strategy* across the Pacific: *Guadalcanal* (1942–43), *Iwo Jima* (1945), *Okinawa* (1945)—brutal assaults against entrenched Japanese defenders that inflicted staggering casualties on both sides.
- *Iwo Jima* (February–March 1945) became the iconic Marine engagement: 70,000 Marines fought 22,000 Japanese defenders in brutal mountain and cave warfare; the iconic *flag-raising photograph* became the symbol of American sacrifice and victory.
- Marine casualties in the Pacific were extraordinary: tens of thousands killed and wounded; their aggressive tactics and willingness to accept heavy losses became both celebrated (as courage and commitment) and controversial (as potential waste).
- Post-1945, the Marines developed *amphibious assault doctrine*, helicopter-borne operations, and specialized training; their institutional culture—emphasizing unit cohesion, leadership, and aggressive offense—became central to their identity and public mythology.
