## summary

**Crazy Horse** (born **Tasunke Witko**) was an Oglala Lakota war leader who led armed resistance against the expansion of the [[united-states]] across the northern Great Plains during the late nineteenth century. Renowned for his tactical brilliance, martial discipline, and steadfast defense of traditional indigenous sovereignty against encroaching [[colonialism]], he never signed a treaty or surrendered the autonomy of his people willingly. His leadership in historic engagements such as the *Battle of the Little Bighorn* and the *Battle of the Rosebud* cemented his standing as one of the most revered military strategists and enduring icons in Native American history.

## you gotta know

- Decisively defeated the *7th Cavalry Regiment* under *George Armstrong Custer* at the *Battle of the Little Bighorn* in 1876, executing a devastating flanking charge that annihilated *Custer's* immediate detachment.
- Lured Captain *William J. Fetterman* and eighty federal soldiers into an ambush during the 1866 *Fetterman Fight* along the Bozeman Trail, acting as a decoy in the worst federal military disaster on the Great Plains prior to Little Bighorn.
- Checked the advance of Brigadier General *George Crook* at the *Battle of the Rosebud* eight days before Little Bighorn, forcing a federal withdrawal and isolating *Custer's* force from reinforcements.
- Surrendered at *Fort Robinson*, Nebraska, in May 1877 after severe winter conditions, declining bison populations, and persistent starvation left his followers unable to sustain further resistance.
- Fatally stabbed with a bayonet by an American soldier at *Fort Robinson* in September 1877 during a struggle after military officers attempted to lock him in a guardhouse.
- Guided by a sacred childhood vision in which an unadorned warrior passed unharmed through storms and gunfire, prompting him to wear only a red hawk feather in combat and shun victory ceremonies.
- Steadfastly refused to ever sit for a photograph or portrait throughout his life, believing that capturing his likeness would trap his shadow and diminish his spiritual power.
- Commemorated by the *Crazy Horse Memorial*, a monumental ongoing mountain carving in the Black Hills of South Dakota begun by sculptor *Korczak Ziolkowski* to celebrate indigenous resistance.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — federal power whose territorial expansion, gold rushes, and military campaigns displaced indigenous nations across the northern plains.
- [[colonialism]] — broader framework of settler expansion, forced treaties, and cultural assimilation that Lakota leaders mobilized to resist.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict whose veteran commanders, supply chains, and cavalry doctrine were redeployed across the western frontier.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — period during which federal military focus shifted away from southern occupation toward subjugating western plains tribes.
- [[red-cloud]] — Oglala chief who successfully commanded the Bozeman Trail conflict before choosing reservation diplomacy over continued armed warfare.
- [[geronimo]] — Chiricahua Apache leader whose prolonged guerrilla resistance in the Southwest paralleled his own northern campaigns against federal encroachment.
- [[chief-joseph]] — Nez Perce leader whose fighting retreat toward Canada occurred in 1877, the same year as his surrender and death.
