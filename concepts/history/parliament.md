## summary

**Parliament** is the legislative body of [[england]] and later the [[british-empire]], with roots extending back to medieval assemblies and a modern history as the world's oldest continuously functioning representative legislature. Emerging from feudal councils of nobles, it evolved into a bicameral body incorporating commons and lords, eventually becoming the model for democratic institutions across the globe. The principle of parliamentary sovereignty—that Parliament holds supreme authority—became fundamental to [[democracy|democratic]] governance and the rule of law.

## you gotta know

- Originated in the 13th century as assemblies of nobles summoned by the king to consent to taxation, with the [[magna-carta]] establishing the principle that even monarchs were subject to law.
- Evolved to include representatives from towns and shires alongside nobility, gradually becoming the primary legislative body by the 14th century.
- Played a central role in the [[english-civil-war]], with Parliament's assertion of authority against royal absolutism directly triggering the conflict.
- Emerged victorious from the [[english-civil-war]], establishing the principle that Parliament, not the king, held ultimate power in [[england]].
- Developed the concept of parliamentary sovereignty—the idea that Parliament represents the people and is the highest authority in the state.
- Became the model for [[democracy|democratic]] and representative institutions worldwide, influencing the [[american-revolution]] and republican movements globally.
- Developed a bicameral structure with the House of Commons (representing commoners) and House of Lords (representing nobility and clergy), each with distinct powers and prerogatives.
- Evolved the tradition of ministerial responsibility, where the executive branch must retain the confidence and support of Parliament to govern.
- Its traditions of debate, precedent, and procedure set the template for legislative bodies from the United States Congress to the Canadian Parliament to Commonwealth legislatures worldwide.

## connections

- [[magna-carta]] — the foundational document that constrained royal power and established the principle of rule by law.
- [[england]] — the political unit Parliament governed and for which it became a symbol of national identity.
- [[english-civil-war]] — the conflict that validated Parliament's claim to supremacy over the monarchy.
- [[democracy]] — the form of government that Parliament pioneered through representative legislation.
- [[liberal-party]] — the party that championed parliamentary democracy and expanded voting rights in the 19th century.
- [[oliver-cromwell]] — the military leader who initially replaced Parliament with military rule, demonstrating its importance to governance.
