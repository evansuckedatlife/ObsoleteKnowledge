## summary

The **civil rights backlash**, frequently referred to contemporarily as the white backlash, describes the political, cultural, and electoral counter-reaction among white Americans against the legislative and social advances of the [[civil-rights-movement]]. Gaining national momentum during the mid-to-late 1960s, this reaction manifested in widespread opposition to court-ordered school desegregation, affirmative action, and fair housing ordinances. It fundamentally reshaped party coalitions in the [[united-states]], ending the longstanding New Deal electoral consensus and ushering in a decades-long conservative realignment centered on appeals to law and order.

## you gotta know

- Crystallized in the 1968 presidential campaign of Alabama governor *George Wallace*, whose third-party American Independent Party run won five southern states and drew large working-class crowds in northern industrial cities.
- Adopted and refined by *Richard Nixon* through the Southern Strategy, which appealed to suburban and white working-class anxieties using racially coded rhetoric surrounding street crime and states' rights.
- Extended far beyond the American South into northern and western metropolises, provoking violent clashes over court-mandated busing policies in cities such as Boston and Chicago during the early 1970s.
- Mobilized fierce resistance against the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Fair Housing Act of 1968, sparking the formation of local taxpayers' leagues and white neighborhood defense councils.
- Mirrored the historical backlash that ended the [[reconstruction-era]], wherein conservative white redemption campaigns undermined post-war constitutional protections established under the [[14th-amendment]].
- Capitalized politically on urban riots in Watts, Detroit, and Newark, reframing the black liberation struggle in mass media as a crisis of social breakdown requiring punitive policing.
- Accelerated white flight from central city cores to newly constructed suburbs, depriving municipal school districts and public services of vital tax revenues.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — freedom struggle whose legislative breakthroughs provoked intense political resistance.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — leader whose northern desegregation campaigns encountered fierce residential pushback.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose postwar electoral map and political party systems were reorganized by the reaction.
- [[14th-amendment]] — constitutional guarantee of equal protection challenged by anti-busing and segregation advocates.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — earlier historical parallel where white resistance reversed federal civil rights enforcement.
- [[circumstantial-evidence]] — legal doctrine invoked in institutional civil rights cases evaluating systemic bias.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical rivalry that pressured federal authorities to balance foreign image against domestic unrest.
