## summary

The *Persian Wars* (circa 499–449 BCE) were a series of conflicts between the expanding *Persian Empire* under *Xerxes I* and the city-states of *ancient Greece*, culminating in Greek victories at *Marathon*, *Salamis*, and *Plataea*. These wars defined Greek identity, established Greek independence from Persian domination, and catalyzed the development of Athens as a democratic superpower that would dominate the Mediterranean in the subsequent Classical period.

## you gotta know

- The wars began with Greek city-states in *Ionia* (Asia Minor) rebelling against Persian rule circa 500 BCE; *Athens* and other mainland Greeks supported the Ionian revolt, drawing the ire of the Persian *Great King*.
- *Xerxes I*, son of *Darius*, mobilized an enormous army (ancient sources claim over a million soldiers; modern estimates range from 100,000 to 300,000) to invade mainland Greece, determined to punish Athens and expand Persian hegemony westward.
- The *Battle of Marathon* (490 BCE), fought before Xerxes' invasion, saw the Athenian general *Miltiades* achieve a stunning upset victory against a Persian expeditionary force; the victory boosted Greek confidence and demonstrated that Persian forces were not invincible.
- The *Battle of Salamis* (480 BCE) saw the Athenian navy under *Themistocles* destroy the Persian fleet through brilliant tactics and superior ship handling; the naval victory forced Xerxes to abandon his invasion and retreat to Asia Minor.
- The *Battle of Plataea* (479 BCE), the final major land engagement, saw a coalition of Greek hoplites under *Pausanias* decisively defeat the Persian army, effectively ending major Persian invasion attempts and confirming Greek independence.
- The *Treaty of Callias* (449 BCE) formalized peace between Athens and Persia, establishing mutual respect for spheres of influence and ending the constant warfare; this peace enabled Athens' ascendancy and the flowering of the Classical period.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the civilization defending itself against external domination and consolidating its identity.
- [[athens]] — the city-state whose naval power proved decisive and whose subsequent dominance was enabled by Persian Wars victory.
- [[sparta]] — the land power that complemented Athenian naval victory and shared leadership of the Greek resistance.
- [[xerxes]] — the Persian Great King whose invasion defined the wars and whose defeat established Persian limits.
- [[alexander-the-great]] — whose later Macedonian conquests of Persia represent the ultimate reversal of the Persian-Greek balance.
