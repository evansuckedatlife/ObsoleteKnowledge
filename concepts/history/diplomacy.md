## summary

**Diplomacy** is the art and practice of negotiation between sovereign states to resolve disputes, forge alliances, and conduct international relations without warfare. Emerging formally in Renaissance Italy and codified by the [[peace-of-westphalia]], diplomacy established the framework through which nation-states communicate, negotiate treaties, and manage their interests. It serves as both a stabilizing force that can prevent conflict and a mechanism through which power is exercised through means other than military force.

## you gotta know

- Early modern European diplomacy emerged from Italian city-states, with Venice and Florence pioneering resident ambassadors as permanent diplomatic presence.
- The [[peace-of-westphalia]] (1648) institutionalized diplomacy as the primary tool for managing interstate relations, replacing religious warfare with treaty negotiation.
- Diplomatic immunity—protection granted to ambassadors from host country jurisdiction—became a cornerstone principle enabling candid negotiation between adversaries.
- The [[concert-of-europe]], established after the Napoleonic Wars, represented a diplomatic system where major powers met to discuss European affairs collectively.
- Multilateral diplomacy expanded through institutions like the [[league-of-nations]] and [[united-nations]], creating frameworks for ongoing diplomatic engagement beyond bilateral treaties.
- Modern diplomacy encompasses secret negotiations ("back channels"), economic incentives (sanctions and trade), and public diplomacy (cultural and information campaigns).

## connections

- [[peace-of-westphalia]] — the foundational treaty that established diplomacy as the framework for interstate relations.
- [[treaty-of-utrecht]] — exemplary of how diplomatic negotiation resolved complex territorial and religious disputes across Europe.
- [[collective-security]] — a diplomatic concept in which nations agree to defend one another against aggression.
- [[balance-of-power]] — the diplomatic strategy of maintaining equilibrium among competing nation-states to prevent any single power from dominating.
- [[united-nations]] — the modern institutional embodiment of multilateral diplomacy on a global scale.
- [[cold-war]] — a period of heightened diplomatic tension and "proxy" conflicts despite the absence of direct warfare.
- [[congress-of-vienna]] — a major diplomatic congress that reshaped Europe's political order after Napoleon's defeat.
