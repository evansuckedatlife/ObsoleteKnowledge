## summary

**Bruno Richard Hauptmann**, commonly known as **Bruno Hauptmann**, was a German-born carpenter and convicted felon whose trial for the abduction and murder of *Charles Lindbergh Jr.* was dubbed the "Trial of the Century". Arrested in New York in 1934 after passing a marked gold certificate from the ransom cache, Hauptmann steadfastly maintained his innocence throughout his sensational court proceedings. He was convicted of first-degree murder in Flemington, New Jersey, and died in the electric chair at Trenton State Prison in 1936, leaving a contentious legacy that catalyzed modern forensic and legal standards.

## you gotta know

- Captured by federal and municipal investigators after spending a distinctive ten-dollar gold certificate at a gas station in the Bronx, leading police directly to his home.
- Found in possession of over fourteen thousand dollars of the original ransom money hidden inside his garage walls and oil cans, money he claimed was left by an associate named *Isidor Fisch*.
- Linked to the crime through the specialized testimony of government wood technologist *Arthur Koehler*, who demonstrated that wood from the crude homemade kidnap ladder matched floorboards in Hauptmann's attic.
- Prosecuted in Flemington, New Jersey, by state attorney general *David Wilentz* before presiding judge *Thomas Trenchard*, while defended by celebrity attorney *Edward J. Reilly*.
- Identified by the aviator *Charles Lindbergh*, who testified that he recognized Hauptmann's distinctive German accent from words shouted in a cemetery during the midnight ransom handover.
- Subject of intense modern skepticism by legal scholars and investigative historians who cite severe evidence tampering, suppressed witness reports, and fabricated police testimony.
- Executed by electrocution in the electric chair in April 1936 after rejecting a life-sentence clemency offer conditioned upon confessing to the kidnapping.
