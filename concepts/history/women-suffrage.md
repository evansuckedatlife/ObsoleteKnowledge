## summary

**Women Suffrage** is the political right of women to vote, a struggle spanning nearly a century in the United States before culminating in the *19th Amendment* (1920). Though women were promised equality in the nation's founding, they were systematically excluded from political participation, property ownership, and legal personhood. The women's suffrage movement, beginning in earnest in 1848 at *Seneca Falls* and carried forward by generations of activists, became the central struggle of the Progressive era, linking women's votes to broader demands for equality, labor rights, and reproductive autonomy.

## you gotta know

- The first organized demand for women's voting rights came at the *Seneca Falls Convention* (1848), where *Elizabeth Cady Stanton* and *Lucretia Mott* drafted the *Declaration of Sentiments*, modeled on the Declaration of Independence.
- *Susan B. Anthony* and *Elizabeth Cady Stanton* co-founded the National Woman Suffrage Association (1869), pioneering militant tactics including civil disobedience and symbolic arrests.
- The *19th Amendment* was first introduced in Congress in 1878 and regularly reintroduced for 42 years before finally passing in 1920—a remarkable persistence through multiple generational turnover.
- *Carrie Chapman Catt*, leading the National American Woman Suffrage Association, adopted a pragmatic state-by-state strategy that secured voting rights in numerous states before the federal amendment.
- Wyoming (1869) became the first territory to grant women the vote; by 1920, fifteen states had already enfranchised women, proving a political opening existed.
- The suffrage movement intersected with temperance, labor rights, and pacifism; many suffragists were also Progressive reformers seeking to use women's votes to advance social legislation.
- Southern white suffragists often collaborated with white segregationists and actively opposed Black women's voting rights, fracturing the movement's moral coalition.
- The *19th Amendment*, ratified in 1920, guaranteed that voting rights could not be denied on account of sex—though women's full political participation took decades more, faced with cultural barriers and voting suppression targeting Black women.
