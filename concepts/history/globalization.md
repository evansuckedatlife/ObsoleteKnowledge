## summary

**globalization** refers to the historical process of accelerating interconnectedness and interdependence among world economies, societies, cultures, and governance systems. Driven by modern telecommunications, transportation breakthroughs, and trade liberalization, the phenomenon surged dramatically following the collapse of the *[[soviet-union]]* and the conclusion of the *[[cold-war]]*. It transformed contemporary production, politics, and daily life through international commerce, digital networks, and multinational supply chains while prompting heated debate over inequality and cultural homogenization.

## you gotta know

- Accelerated historically in phases, from ancient cross-continental commerce along the *[[silk-road]]* to the mercantilist shipping surges of the *[[age-of-exploration]]*.
- Catalyzed technologically during the *[[industrial-revolution]]* through steamships, railroads, and the transatlantic telegraph, creating an integrated global market.
- Institutionalized after 1944 via the Bretton Woods system, establishing the International Monetary Fund, World Bank, and later the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade.
- Expanded rapidly during the 1990s through neoliberal market policies known as the Washington Consensus, which emphasized deregulation and privatization.
- Solidified across regional blocs through major free trade treaties, notably the North American Free Trade Agreement under *[[bill-clinton]]* and the creation of the European Union.
- Reshaped by the 1995 establishment of the World Trade Organization and the rapid integration of manufacturing powerhouses like *[[china]]*.
- Sparked widespread backlash seen in the 1999 Seattle WTO protests and populist political movements defending domestic manufacturing against offshoring.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — provided the technological infrastructure of rapid transport and mass production enabling global markets.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical rivalry whose peaceful resolution unlocked worldwide market expansion and supply integration.
- [[silk-road]] — ancient network of Eurasian overland and maritime trade routes serving as an early ancestor of global exchange.
- [[colonialism]] — imperial extraction and trade networks that forcibly knit disparate global regions into European economies.
- [[china]] — modern superpower whose export-led industrialization and accession to the WTO transformed global manufacturing.
- [[united-states]] — primary architect and guarantor of the post-1945 international financial and trade architecture.
