## summary

The **Chattanooga Campaign**, also recorded in military histories as the **Battles for Chattanooga**, was a decisive series of engagements fought during October and November 1863 around Chattanooga, Tennessee, during the [[american-civil-war]]. Following their catastrophic defeat at Chickamauga, Union forces found themselves besieged inside the city until Major General *Ulysses S. Grant* arrived to coordinate reinforcements, establish a lifeline of supplies, and execute a brilliant counteroffensive against Confederate mountain positions. The dramatic Union victory lifted the siege, drove General *Braxton Bragg*'s forces into retreat, and unlocked the strategic doorway to the heart of the Confederacy.

## you gotta know

- Began in late September 1863 after the Confederate victory at the *Battle of Chickamauga* bottled up the Union *Army of the Cumberland* under General *William Rosecrans* in a starving siege inside Chattanooga.
- Led President *Abraham Lincoln* to create the *Military Division of the Mississippi*, appointing *Ulysses S. Grant* to supreme command across the Western Theater and replacing Rosecrans with General *George H. Thomas*.
- Reopened vital Union supply routes into the starving city through the establishment of the famous "Cracker Line," secured by a surprise amphibious landing at *Brown's Ferry* and victory at *Wauhatchie*.
- Featured the legendary "Battle Above the Clouds" on November 24, 1863, where Major General *Joseph Hooker* led Union troops through thick fog to capture fortified Confederate lines on *Lookout Mountain*.
- Reached its climax on November 25, 1863, with the assault on *Missionary Ridge*, where Thomas's men, ordered only to capture lower rifle pits, spontaneously surged up the sheer slopes shouting "Chickamauga!" to rout Bragg's army.
- Transformed Chattanooga into the primary Union logistical springboard for *William Tecumseh Sherman*'s *Atlanta Campaign* and subsequent March to the Sea in 1864.
- Cemented the supreme military reputation of *Ulysses S. Grant*, prompting Congress and Lincoln to revive the three-star rank of Lieutenant General and place Grant in command of all Union armies.
