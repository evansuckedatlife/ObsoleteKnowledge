## summary

**Kamikaze**, known formally in the Japanese military as the **Tokkōtai** or **Special Attack Units**, were state-sanctioned aerial and naval suicide tactics deployed by the *Empire of Japan* against Allied forces during the final stages of [[world-war-ii]]. First mobilized systematically in October 1944 during the liberation of the Philippines, the strategy involved aviators deliberately piloting bomb-laden aircraft directly into Allied warships to inflict catastrophic damage. Designed to compensate for crippling industrial deficits, fuel shortages, and the loss of veteran aviators, this doctrine embodied the extreme militarization of imperial ideology before the war ended in 1945.

## you gotta know

- Took its name from the Japanese term for *divine wind*, referencing legendary thirteenth-century typhoons that destroyed the invasion fleets of *Kublai Khan* and saved [[japan]] from Mongol conquest.
- Conceived and organized as formal operational doctrine by Vice Admiral *Takijirō Ōnishi*, who oversaw the first successful deployment at the *Battle of Leyte Gulf*, where a suicide plane sank the escort carrier USS *St. Lo*.
- Reached its maximum intensity during the bloody 1945 *Battle of Okinawa*, where the Japanese launched ten massive coordinated waves of mass attacks known as *Operation Kikusui* (*Floating Chrysanthemum*), sinking dozens of ships.
- Employed adapted conventional warplanes such as the *Mitsubishi A6M Zero*, alongside specialized rocket-powered suicide gliders known as the *Yokosuka MXY-7 Ohka* (*Baka bomb* to Allied forces) and explosive *Shin'yō* suicide motorboats.
- Drew heavily from impressionable young university student conscripts and adolescent cadets rather than elite veterans, as military authorities exploited patriotism and romanticized warrior traditions of *bushido* to ensure compliance.
- Marked departures with solemn rituals where doomed pilots drank ceremonial cups of *sake* or water, received thousand-stitch cloths called *senninbari*, wore white *hachimaki* headbands, and composed death poems (*jisei*) to immortalize their sacrifice.
- Incurred diminishing tactical success as the [[united-states]] Navy refined defensive doctrines, relying on shipboard radar picket destroyers, combat air patrols of *F6F Hellcats*, and anti-aircraft weapons equipped with proximity fuzes.
- Culminated in the ritual suicide (*seppuku*) of Vice Admiral *Ōnishi* on the day of Japanese surrender, leaving a controversial legacy that sparked intense debates over military coercion versus genuine national devotion.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the catastrophic global conflict whose Pacific Theater witnessed the emergence and climax of organized suicide tactics.
- [[japan]] — the island nation whose imperial armed forces instituted suicide attacks to ward off imminent Allied invasion.
- [[united-states]] — the primary naval power whose Pacific Fleet carriers, destroyers, and amphibious forces bore the brunt of the strikes.
- [[battle-of-leyte-gulf]] — the decisive October 1944 naval clash where organized suicide squads claimed their first warship sinking.
- [[battle-of-okinawa]] — the costly 1945 campaign characterized by the highest volume of massed aerial suicide assaults.
- [[bushido]] — the idealized samurai moral code adapted by military propagandists to glorify self-immolation in defense of the Emperor.
- [[pearl-harbor]] — the opening surprise strike in 1941 that initiated the Pacific War and established the aircraft carrier as the dominant naval weapon.
