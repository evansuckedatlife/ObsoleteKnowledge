## summary

The **Greek City-States**, known natively as the *poleis*, were autonomous, urban-centered sovereign communities that defined Hellenic civilization from the Archaic period through the Classical era. Across mainland *Greece*, the Aegean islands, and colonial shores spanning Southern Italy and the Mediterranean basin, these independent republics and monarchies shared a common language, religion, and literature while maintaining fiercely competitive political autonomy. Their innovative experiments with governance produced seminal models of classical democracy, oligarchy, philosophy, and civic architecture that laid the foundations for Western political thought.

## you gotta know

- Structured around an urban center featuring an elevated defensive citadel (*acropolis*) and a central marketplace and civic assembly space (*agora*), ringed by agrarian hinterlands (*chora*).
- Characterized by extreme political fragmentation and diversity of constitutions, including Athenian direct democracy, Spartan militarized oligarchy (*diarchy*), Corinthian commercial tyranny, and aristocratic councils.
- Formed temporary alliances (*symmachiai*) such as the Hellenic League during the Greco-Persian Wars, which later evolved into rival imperial leagues like the Athenian-led Delian League and the Spartan-dominated Peloponnesian League.
- Fought the destructive *Peloponnesian War* (431–404 BCE), a pan-Hellenic conflict chronicled by *Thucydides* that exhausted Athenian maritime supremacy, weakened the autonomy of the city-states, and briefly established Spartan hegemony before Thebes rose to power.
- Promoted shared cultural unity through pan-Hellenic institutions, including the Olympic Games at Olympia, the Pythian Games at Delphi, sacred amphictyonies, and common Homeric epics, despite chronic interstate warfare.
- Founded numerous overseas colonies (*apoikiai*) across the Mediterranean, including settlements in Sicily (*Magna Graecia*), the Black Sea, and Cyrene on the North African coast, establishing long-distance trade routes.
- Subjugated by *Philip II of Macedon* after the decisive Battle of Chaeronea in 338 BCE, which united them under the League of Corinth and marked the eclipse of autonomous classical civic independence.
