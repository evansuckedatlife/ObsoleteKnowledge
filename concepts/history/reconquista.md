# Reconquista

## summary

The **Reconquista** was the centuries-long Christian reconquest of the *Iberian Peninsula* from *Muslim* rule, spanning roughly from the 8th century (after the *Umayyad conquest*) to 1492 when the final *Islamic* *Sultanate of Granada* fell to the Christian *Catholic Monarchs*. It fundamentally reshaped medieval Europe, driving the development of Christian kingdoms in northern Iberia and culminating in the emergence of *Spain* as a major European power.

## you gotta know

- The Reconquista began in earnest after the 8th-century *Umayyad conquest of Iberia* left most of the peninsula under Islamic control; Christian kingdoms persisted in the north (*Asturias*, *Leon*, *Castile*, *Aragon*, *Portugal*).
- *Al-Andalus*, the Islamic civilization in Iberia, was culturally sophisticated and religiously pluralistic (though non-Muslims faced *dhimmi* status); its libraries, scholars, and architecture influenced medieval Europe profoundly.
- The *Battle of Tours* (732) in what is now France—though not technically Iberian—marked the symbolic limit of *Islamic expansion* northward and energized Christian resistance mythology.
- Progress was fitful: the Reconquista advanced and retreated over centuries; the *Taifa period* (11th century) saw Islamic Iberia fragment into petty kingdoms, enabling Christian advances; subsequent waves of North African Muslim invaders (*Almoravids*, *Almohads*) reversed some gains.
- The *Catholic Monarchs* *Isabella and Ferdinand* unified *Castile and Aragon* through marriage and orchestrated the final siege of *Granada* (1482–1492), expelling the last Muslim rulers and establishing Christian supremacy.
- The Reconquista was intertwined with the *Crusades* ideologically—it was framed as holy war against Islam—and fueled anti-Islamic sentiment that persisted into the modern era.
- After 1492, the monarchs expelled *Jews* and *Muslims* who refused conversion, and the Spanish *Inquisition* targeted converts, marking a violent end to *medieval pluralism* in Iberia.
