## summary

**Nonviolence** is a political and moral philosophy that rejects physical force and harm as means of achieving social, political, or religious change. Rooted in ancient religious traditions (Buddhism, Hinduism, Christianity) and systematized in the 20th century by *Mahatma Gandhi* and *Martin Luther King Jr.*, nonviolence became a powerful strategy for movements challenging entrenched power. It operates on the principle that moral force, dignity, and exposing injustice prove more transformative than violence, which corrupts movements and alienates potential allies.

## you gotta know

- *Ahimsa* (non-harming) emerged from Hindu and Jain traditions thousands of years old, providing philosophical foundations for later political applications of nonviolence.
- Gandhi systematized nonviolence into *satyagraha* ("truth-force"), combining civil disobedience, economic boycotts, and strategic moral suasion to challenge British colonial rule in India.
- Martin Luther King Jr. adapted Gandhian nonviolence to the American Civil Rights Movement, insisting that racial justice must be pursued without weapons or hatred, despite violent provocation from segregationists.
- Nonviolent movements prove historically effective: the Indian independence movement, civil rights victories, Eastern European transitions from communism, and anti-apartheid struggle all achieved major change through organized nonviolence.
- Distinguishes between passive acceptance of injustice and active nonviolent resistance—the latter requires courage, discipline, and willingness to absorb violence without retaliation.
- Critics argue nonviolence privileges the moral comfort of the oppressed's conscience over immediate material liberation, though success in major movements validates its strategic efficacy.

## connections

- [[gandhi]] — the pioneering theorist and practitioner of political nonviolence in India.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — American civil rights leader who centered nonviolence as both moral principle and strategic tool.
- [[civil-disobedience]] — the tactic of deliberately breaking unjust laws nonviolently, core to nonviolent resistance movements.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the American struggle for racial justice defined by its (contested) commitment to nonviolence.
- [[indian-independence-movement]] — independence achieved through Gandhian nonviolent mass mobilization against the British Empire.
- [[satyagraha]] — Gandhi's specific formulation of truth-force and organized nonviolent resistance.
