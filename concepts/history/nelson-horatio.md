## summary

**Horatio Nelson** (1758–1805) was the legendary British admiral whose tactical genius and fearless leadership established British naval supremacy during the Napoleonic Wars. Despite losing an arm and an eye in combat, he won decisive victories against French and Spanish fleets, most famously at [[Trafalgar]], where he died in the moment of triumph. His legacy transformed naval warfare and made Britain's control of the seas absolute for the century to come.

## you gotta know

- Rose through the navy during the American Revolution and the wars against Revolutionary France, earning a reputation for bold aggression and innovative tactical thinking.
- Lost his right arm at the [[Siege of Tenerife]] (1797) and his left eye from shrapnel in earlier combat, yet continued to command with undiminished vigor and prescient vision.
- Achieved his first great victory at the [[Battle of the Nile]] (1798), destroying the French Mediterranean fleet and cutting off [[Napoleon's]] crucial supply lines to Egypt.
- Famously disobeyed explicit orders at the [[Battle of Copenhagen]] (1801), reportedly holding his telescope to his blind eye and claiming he could see no signal to retreat.
- Fought [[Napoleon's]] combined Franco-Spanish fleet at the [[Battle of Trafalgar]] (1805), where his innovative "break the line" tactics devastatingly crushed enemy naval power and dominance.
- Mortally wounded during Trafalgar by a French sharpshooter but lived long enough to learn of victory, expiring with the words "Thank God I have done my duty."
- Became a towering national icon in Britain, instantly celebrated across the nation for his ultimate sacrifice; his famous column still stands in Trafalgar Square, London.
