## summary

**Daniel O'Connell**, historically celebrated as **The Liberator** and **The Emancipator**, was an Irish barrister, orator, and parliamentary statesman who revolutionized democratic mass mobilization in the nineteenth century. By organizing the disenfranchised Catholic peasantry through disciplined nonviolent resistance, he compelled the British government to pass the historic *Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829*. He subsequently directed the national crusade to repeal the *Act of Union 1800*, establishing pioneering strategies of peaceful extra-parliamentary assembly that profoundly reshaped constitutional politics throughout the *British Empire*.

## you gotta know

- Won election to the British *Parliament* as an MP for *County Clare* in 1828 while legally barred from taking his seat due to anti-Catholic parliamentary oaths, precipitating a constitutional crisis that forced *Sir Robert Peel* and the *Duke of Wellington* to concede emancipation.
- Created the *Catholic Association* in 1823, funding populist political agitation through the "Catholic Rent" of a penny a month, which empowered millions of impoverished tenants as active political participants.
- Organized gigantic "Monster Meetings" across Ireland during the 1840s that assembled hundreds of thousands of unarmed citizens, culminating in a historic gathering at the ancient royal summit on the *Hill of Tara*.
- Cancelled the scheduled Monster Meeting at *Clontarf* in October 1843 when British authorities dispatched warships and artillery to forbid it, holding firm to his central moral tenet that no political achievement was worth shedding a single drop of human blood.
- Established the *Loyal National Repeal Association* to demand the peaceful dissolution of the *Act of Union 1800* and the re-establishment of an autonomous Irish parliament in Dublin.
- Championed universal abolitionism with uncompromising fervor, vocally denouncing chattel slavery in America and refusing financial aid from Southern slaveholders, earning the deep admiration of *Frederick Douglass*.
- Studied law in continental Europe at Douai and Saint-Omer, where witnessing the violent upheavals of the *French Revolution* instilled in him a permanent horror of mob rule and armed insurrection.
- Clashed ideological swords with the younger intellectuals of the *Young Ireland* faction, whose romantic nationalism and eventual willingness to contemplate armed rebellion ruptured the Repeal movement on the eve of the Great Famine.
