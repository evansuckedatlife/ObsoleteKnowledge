## summary

**F. W. de Klerk** was the seventh and final State President of South Africa under the minority-rule regime, governing from 1989 to 1994. Recognizing that political isolation, economic sanctions, and domestic resistance rendered the racial segregation system unsustainable, he unbanned liberation movements, released political prisoners including *Nelson Mandela*, and initiated the negotiations that dismantled *apartheid*. For their joint efforts to negotiate a peaceful transition to non-racial democracy, **Frederik Willem de Klerk** and *Nelson Mandela* were jointly awarded the 1993 Nobel Peace Prize.

## you gotta know

- Delivered a historic address to the South African Parliament on February 2, 1990, announcing the unconditional unbanning of the *African National Congress*, the Pan Africanist Congress, and the South African Communist Party, as well as the imminent release of *Nelson Mandela*.
- Succeeded *P. W. Botha* as leader of the National Party in February 1989 and as State President in September 1989 after *Botha* suffered a stroke and was pressured out of office by the cabinet.
- Staged an all-white referendum in March 1992 asking voters whether they supported the ongoing reform process aimed at a negotiated constitution, winning an overwhelming sixty-nine percent mandate that sidelined right-wing hardliners.
- Oversaw the secret dismantling of South Africa's nuclear weapons program, making the nation the first state in world history to voluntarily assemble and subsequently destroy its own nuclear arsenal before transferring power.
- Clashed bitterly with *Nelson Mandela* during the Convention for a Democratic South Africa talks, notably enduring a blistering public rebuke from *Mandela* at the December 1991 plenary session over covert state sponsorship of third-force township violence.
- Served as one of two Executive Deputy Presidents under *Nelson Mandela* from 1994 until 1996 in the Government of National Unity, eventually withdrawing the National Party from the coalition over disputes regarding the newly drafted constitution.
- Testified before the Truth and Reconciliation Commission chaired by Archbishop *Desmond Tutu*, offering an apology for the pain caused by segregationist policies while controversial denials of state-sponsored atrocities drew severe criticism.

## connections

- [[nelson-mandela]] — his primary negotiating partner, rival, and predecessor as Nobel laureate who defeated him in the historic 1994 democratic election.
- [[apartheid]] — the institutionalized system of racial segregation and white supremacy that he dismantled as state president.
- [[cold-war]] — the global geopolitical conflict whose resolution diminished Western fear of communist influence and accelerated pressure on Pretoria.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader historical pattern of European imperial settlement and dominion across Southern Africa that entrenched white minority rule.
- [[african-national-congress]] — the banned liberation movement that he legalized and negotiated with to establish universal franchise.
- [[south-africa]] — the nation he governed through constitutional transformation from an international pariah into a multi-ethnic democracy.
