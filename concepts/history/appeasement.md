## summary

**Appeasement** was the diplomatic strategy pursued primarily by *Britain* and [[france]] during the 1930s that sought to prevent European war by granting negotiated concessions to revisionist authoritarian regimes, most notably [[germany]] under *Adolf Hitler*. Rooted in the lingering trauma of [[world-war-i]], severe domestic economic distress, and widespread dread of aerial bombardment, the policy culminated in the dismemberment of democratic Czechoslovakia at the 1938 [[munich-conference]]. Far from sating aggressive appetites, these unilateral compromises dismantled the collective security framework of the [[league-of-nations]] and accelerated the path toward [[world-war-ii]].

## you gotta know

- Associated primarily with British Prime Minister *Neville Chamberlain*, who returned from the [[munich-conference]] brandishing the Anglo-German declaration and proclaiming to cheering crowds that he had preserved "peace for our time."
- Deeply rooted in the catastrophic trauma of [[world-war-i]], which left public opinion and political elites across [[england]] and [[france]] determined to avoid another slaughter of a generation at virtually any diplomatic cost.
- Enabled the systematic destruction of the Treaty of Versailles through unchecked revisionist actions, including the 1935 rearmament announcements, the 1936 remilitarization of the Rhineland, and the 1938 *Anschluss* with Austria.
- Prompted the tragic abandonment of Czechoslovakia, whose heavily fortified Sudeten border regions were ceded to Nazi rule without Czechoslovak representatives even being admitted into the negotiation chambers.
- Shattered decisively in March 1939 when German armored columns invaded and occupied the rump provinces of Bohemia and Moravia, demonstrating beyond doubt that Hitler's ambitions were not limited to ethnic German populations.
- Fiercely opposed from the backbenches by *Winston Churchill*, who famously declared: "You were given the choice between war and dishonour. You chose dishonour, and you will have war."
- Shaped by acute strategic and military anxieties, as British imperial defense chiefs warned that the armed forces were unprepared to fight a simultaneous two-ocean conflict against [[germany]], [[italy]], and [[japan]].
- Compounded by persistent Western hostility toward the [[soviet-union]], which derailed proposed tripartite negotiations in Moscow and directly incentivized *Joseph Stalin* to negotiate the cynical 1939 Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the global cataclysm precipitated when territorial concessions failed to restrain fascist expansionism.
- [[munich-conference]] — the notorious September 1938 summit where European leaders sacrificed Czechoslovak sovereignty for temporary peace.
- [[world-war-i]] — the industrial conflict whose horrific casualties convinced interwar statesmen that modern war had to be averted at all costs.
- [[england]] — the imperial power whose conservative cabinet spearheaded foreign policies intended to buy critical time for rearmament.
- [[france]] — the continental ally that abandoned its defense treaties with Prague in deference to British diplomatic leadership.
- [[germany]] — the expansionist fascist dictatorship that exploited Western hesitancy to overturn European borders through coercion.
- [[soviet-union]] — the socialist power alienated by Western exclusion, which subsequently abandoned collective security for a non-aggression pact.
- [[league-of-nations]] — the Geneva-based institution whose founding mission of collective deterrence was thoroughly bypassed by bilateral bargaining.
