## summary

The **guillotine** was an execution apparatus utilizing a heavy, angled blade dropped between vertical uprights to carry out decapitation, becoming the grim mechanical emblem of the *[[french-revolution]]*. Conceived in late 1789 as a humanitarian and egalitarian reform inspired by the *[[enlightenment]]*, the device replaced agonizing aristocratic privileges and torture with an instantaneous, uniform method of capital punishment for all social classes. During the radical phase known as the *Reign of Terror*, it severed thousands of lives in public squares across *[[france]]*, fundamentally altering the cultural and political spectacle of state violence.

## you gotta know

- Proposed to the National Assembly in October 1789 by physician *Joseph-Ignace Guillotin*, who sought an egalitarian, uniform, and painless method of capital punishment to eliminate aristocratic distinction in death.
- Designed and engineered by the royal surgeon *Antoine Louis* and built by German harpsichord maker *Tobias Schmidt*, leading it initially to be nicknamed the *Louisette* or *Louison*.
- Featured an oblique, angled blade rather than a crescent or horizontal edge, a modification advocated to ensure a clean, shearing bisection of the neck through mechanical efficiency.
- First used on an actual human subject on April 25, 1792, to execute the convicted highwayman *Nicolas Jacques Pelletier* outside the Hôtel de Ville in Paris, where spectators complained it was too fast to provide entertainment.
- Operated as the primary instrument of state terror during 1793–1794 under the Committee of Public Safety, claiming high-profile victims including King *Louis XVI*, Queen *Marie Antoinette*, *Georges Danton*, and *Maximilien Robespierre*.
- Popularly personified under colorful revolutionary sobriquets such as "The National Razor" (*Le Rasoir National*), "Madame Guillotine", and "The Widow" (*La Veuve*).
- Created a persistent biological debate regarding whether severed heads retain consciousness, fueled by experiments on freshly executed individuals such as *Charlotte Corday*, who allegedly blushed when slapped by the executioner's assistant.
- Remained the official method of execution in *[[france]]* for nearly two centuries, with its final public execution taking place in 1939 (*Eugen Weidmann*) and its final private execution in 1977 (*Hamida Djandoubi*) before capital punishment was abolished in 1981.

## connections

- [[french-revolution]] — the political upheaval whose radical phase and radical justice were permanently defined by mass public executions using the apparatus.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement promoting egalitarianism, rational reform, and the mitigation of cruel physical torture that inspired the design.
- [[france]] — the sovereign nation where the machine was invented, codified into penal law, and utilized as the sole method of civil execution until 1981.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the military leader whose rise to power ended revolutionary factional violence and regularized French administrative and penal codes.
- [[romanticism]] — the artistic and literary movement that fixated on the macabre aesthetic, sublime terror, and psychological horror of revolutionary execution.
- [[realism]] — the subsequent literary and artistic movement in which authors examined the bleak social conditions, criminal underworlds, and grim realities of the penal system.
