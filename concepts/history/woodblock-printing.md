## summary

**Woodblock printing**, also known as **xylography**, is a relief printing technique in which text, images, or patterns are carved onto wooden blocks, inked, and transferred onto paper or fabric. Originating in *[[china]]* during the *Tang dynasty*, the method revolutionized the mass reproduction of literature, bureaucratic records, and religious scriptures. The subsequent proliferation of the craft during the *Song dynasty* dramatically lowered the cost of books, transforming education, civil service examinations, and information sharing across East Asia centuries before the European *[[renaissance]]*.

## you gotta know

- The earliest dated, complete printed book in human history produced with this method is the *Diamond Sutra* (*Jingang Jing*), created in *[[china]]* in 868 CE during the *Tang dynasty* and discovered in the *Mogao Caves* of Dunhuang along the *[[silk-road]]*.
- While invented during the *Tang dynasty* primarily to propagate *[[buddhism]]*, the technology was refined, standardized, and commercially expanded under the *Song dynasty*, leading to state-sponsored encyclopedias, dynastic histories, and the world's first government-issued paper currency, *jiaozi*.
- Transmitted rapidly across East Asia, the method flourished in *[[japan]]* from the early eighth-century *Hyakumanto Darani* dharani charms to the vibrant, multicolored *ukiyo-e* prints of the Edo period created by masters like *Hokusai* and *Hiroshige*.
- In medieval Korea, artisans mastered woodblock carving to produce the *Tripitaka Koreana*, a monumental thirteenth-century collection of over eighty-one thousand carved birch and magnolia woodblocks preserving Buddhist scripture at the Haeinsa temple.
- The manual process involved pasting a written manuscript face-down onto an even block of wood, carefully carving away all negative space with gouges so that only the reversed characters remained raised, and applying water-based ink before pressing paper onto the relief.
- Although Chinese artisan *Bi Sheng* later invented ceramic movable type during the Northern Song period, woodblock printing remained the dominant mode of East Asian text production for centuries because its calligraphic flexibility better accommodated thousands of distinct logographic characters.
- The rapid proliferation of inexpensive woodblock books fueled an unprecedented expansion of the scholar-official class through the imperial civil examination system (*keju*), fundamentally altering social mobility and governance in imperial *[[china]]*.

## connections

- [[china]] — birthplace of the technology during the *Tang dynasty* and site of its commercial zenith under the *Song dynasty*.
- [[buddhism]] — primary religious motivation behind early block carving, inspiring the mass dissemination of sacred sutras and protective charms.
- [[silk-road]] — overland conduit along which early printed artifacts, including the *Diamond Sutra* at Dunhuang, were preserved and shared.
- [[japan]] — neighboring realm that embraced xylography for Buddhist devotional charms and later adapted it into iconic polychrome *ukiyo-e* art.
- [[renaissance]] — European cultural epoch that later witnessed its own print revolution through movable metal type, paralleling East Asian dissemination.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — modern transformative era that ultimately replaced traditional manual woodblock carving with mechanized cylinder presses.
