## summary

The **Mexican-American War** (1846–1848) was a military conflict between the *United States* and *Mexico* that resulted in American victory and the largest territorial acquisition by the U.S. since westward expansion began. Driven by American expansionism, particularly the ideology of *Manifest Destiny*, the war was triggered by a border clash in disputed Texas territory and culminated in the *Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo*, which transferred nearly half of Mexico's territory—present-day California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico, and portions of Colorado and Wyoming—to the United States. The war was bitterly controversial in American politics, deepening sectional tensions over slavery expansion into new territories.

## you gotta know

- The war began in May 1846 after American forces under General *Zachary Taylor* clashed with Mexican troops in the disputed region between the *Nueces River* (claimed as the Texas border by Mexico) and the *Rio Grande* (claimed by Texas and the U.S.).
- President *James K. Polk* framed the conflict as defensive, claiming Mexican forces had "shed American blood upon American soil"; this framing mobilized American public opinion, though many Whig politicians and intellectuals opposed the war as unjust.
- The U.S. rapidly defeated Mexican forces through a combination of superior military organization and technology; American soldiers, though fewer in number, executed coordinated campaigns more effectively than fragmented Mexican forces.
- General *Zachary Taylor* emerged from the war as a national hero, winning major battles and subsequently running successfully for president; his military reputation made him nationally prominent despite minimal political experience.
- The Mexican government, led by *Antonio López de Santa Anna*, lost successive campaigns and eventually sued for peace; Mexico's military exhaustion and internal political instability accelerated its defeat.
- The *Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo* (1848) required Mexico to cede approximately 525,000 square miles; in compensation, the U.S. paid Mexico $15 million and assumed $3.25 million in Mexican debts to American citizens.
- The war intensified American debate over slavery; Northern Whigs feared that vast new territories would become slave states, tipping the sectional balance in Congress; the *Wilmot Proviso*, proposing to exclude slavery from conquered lands, became a focal point of bitter sectional conflict.
- The territorial gains set the stage for the *American Civil War*; disputes over whether new territories would permit slavery directly contributed to the sectional crisis of the 1850s, culminating in secession and civil war.
