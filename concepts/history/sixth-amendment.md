## summary

The **Sixth Amendment** is part of the [[bill-of-rights]], ratified in 1791, establishing fundamental protections for criminal defendants in the [[united-states]]. It guarantees the rights to speedy and public trial, confrontation with accusers, compulsory process to obtain witnesses, and crucially, representation by counsel. Interpreted and expanded through landmark Supreme Court cases like [[gideon-v-wainwright]] and [[miranda-v-arizona]], the Sixth Amendment has become the constitutional foundation for criminal justice fairness and the development of professional defense counsel systems throughout American jurisprudence.

## you gotta know

- The Sixth Amendment's text guarantees "the assistance of counsel for his defense," though courts initially interpreted this as a right to hire a lawyer, not receive one at public expense.
- *Gideon v. Wainwright* (1963) held that states must provide counsel to indigent defendants, establishing that the Sixth Amendment requires publicly-funded defense lawyers.
- The "speedy trial" right protects defendants from indefinite delay, though courts must balance this against defense preparation time; delays exceeding 70 months have been deemed unconstitutional.
- The confrontation clause permits defendants to cross-examine witnesses, a practice dating to English common law; the amendment constitutionalizes this ancient protection against secret testimony.
- The "compulsory process" clause allows defendants to subpoena witnesses in their favor, shifting power from prosecution to defense in witness acquisition.
- The jury trial right, paired with the Sixth Amendment, requires that juries be composed of peers and be reasonably informed, establishing jury composition standards and discovery obligations.

## connections

- [[bill-of-rights]] — the constitutional amendments of which the Sixth Amendment is a cornerstone, protecting fundamental liberties.
- [[criminal-procedure]] — the field of law governing criminal trials, in which the Sixth Amendment establishes baseline protections and procedural requirements.
- [[gideon-v-wainwright]] — the landmark case that transformed the Sixth Amendment from theoretical right to mandatory public defense system.
- [[miranda-v-arizona]] — the case establishing suspects' right to counsel before questioning, implementing Sixth Amendment protections during interrogation.
- [[due-process]] — the broader constitutional principle underlying the Sixth Amendment's criminal protections and the Fifth Amendment's self-incrimination safeguard.
- [[fifth-amendment]] — related protections including double-jeopardy and self-incrimination rights that companion the Sixth Amendment's trial protections.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the governing document of which the Sixth Amendment is a critical component.
