## summary

**Scottish Independence** describes the sovereign nationhood and political autonomy of the medieval Kingdom of *Scotland*, successfully asserted and defended against English feudal overlordship during the late thirteenth and early fourteenth centuries. Precipitated by the succession crisis of the *Great Cause* and subsequent invasions by *Edward I* of *England*, this status was preserved through the protracted *Wars of Scottish Independence*. Led by figures including *William Wallace* and *Robert the Bruce*, the kingdom established enduring diplomatic ties through the *Auld Alliance* with *France* and formulated foundational claims regarding national self-determination. Officially recognized in the 1328 *Treaty of Edinburgh-Northampton*, this hard-won independence maintained Scotland as a distinct sovereign realm until parliamentary union in the eighteenth century.

## you gotta know

- *Robert the Bruce* secured de facto independence by crushing the numerically superior forces of *Edward II* at the *Battle of Bannockburn* in 1314, establishing undisputed Scottish military control and consolidating his royal authority as King of Scots.
- *William Wallace* emerged as the preeminent champion of early resistance following the 1296 English invasion, annihilating an occupying army at the *Battle of Stirling Bridge* in 1297 before his betrayal, capture, and brutal execution in *London*.
- The *Declaration of Arbroath*, drafted in 1320 by Scottish barons and churchmen for Pope *John XXII*, famously affirmed that Scotland fought neither for glory nor riches but solely for liberty, producing one of the medieval era's most celebrated statements of national sovereignty.
- De jure recognition of Scotland's sovereignty was achieved under the 1328 *Treaty of Edinburgh-Northampton*, in which the English crown formally acknowledged *Robert I* as legitimate king and renounced all historical claims of feudal suzerainty over *Scotland*.
- Scotland safeguarded its autonomy by entering into the *Auld Alliance* with *France* in 1295, creating a mutual-defense compact stipulating that an English invasion of either kingdom would trigger military intervention by the other.
- The independence crisis began with the 1286 death of *Alexander III* and the subsequent demise of his infant heir, the *Maid of Norway*, which prompted *Edward I* of *England* to claim feudal overlordship during the arbitration known as the *Great Cause*.
- Sovereignty was defended again during the *Second War of Scottish Independence* (1332–1357), when *Edward III* supported the pretender *Edward Balliol*, culminating in the capture of King *David II* and his eventual release under the *Treaty of Berwick*.

## connections

- [[robert-the-bruce]] — the monarch whose triumph at Bannockburn established de facto sovereignty and secured the crown.
- [[william-wallace]] — the Guardian of Scotland who mobilized armed popular resistance against early English domination.
- [[edward-ii]] — the English king whose decisive humiliation at Bannockburn wrecked England's occupation campaign.
- [[scotland]] — the northern kingdom that preserved its sovereign identity, distinctive parliament, and separate legal traditions.
- [[england]] — the aggressive southern neighbor whose Plantagenet monarchs repeatedly sought to impose feudal vassalage.
- [[france]] — the continental ally bound to Scotland by the military provisions of the Auld Alliance.
- [[british-empire]] — the global power that later incorporated Scotland following the parliamentary union of 1707.
