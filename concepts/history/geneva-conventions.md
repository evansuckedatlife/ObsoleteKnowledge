## summary

The **Geneva Conventions** are a series of international treaties that establish humanitarian standards for the treatment of combatants, prisoners of war, and civilians during armed conflict. First codified in 1864 following the horrors of modern industrial warfare, these conventions represent humanity's attempt to impose moral and legal restraints on the conduct of war through binding international agreement.

## you gotta know

- First Geneva Convention (1864) was inspired by *Henri Dunant's* witnessing of the suffering at the Battle of Solferino and led to the founding of the *Red Cross*.
- Established that wounded soldiers must be treated humanely regardless of nationality; created protections for medical personnel and facilities marked with the Red Cross symbol.
- The 1864 convention emerged from the humanitarian impulse to impose ethical limits on industrial warfare after observing mass casualties in the Franco-Austrian War.
- Progressively expanded through four major conventions adopted in 1949 (after *World War II*), covering: wounded and sick combatants, prisoners of war, civilians in wartime, and protection of cultural property.
- The 1949 protocols reflected lessons from *World War II* atrocities; they extended protections to resistance fighters, partisans, and other non-uniformed combatants.
- Require nations to distinguish between combatants and civilians, prohibit torture and summary execution, mandate fair trials for prisoners, and protect medical workers.
- Established that all parties to a conflict are bound by these rules; violations constitute *war crimes* subject to prosecution before international courts.
- Two additional protocols adopted in 1977 extended protections for victims of non-international armed conflicts and established rules for warfare in occupied territories.
- Enforcement relies on signatory nations' commitment and international monitoring through the *International Committee of the Red Cross* (ICRC).
- Violations during conflicts from Vietnam to the Balkans to the Middle East have proven enforcement challenges; courts struggle with jurisdiction and political will to prosecute.
- Represent a foundational principle of *international humanitarian law* and form the basis for modern war crimes prosecutions at the *International Criminal Court*.
- Over 190 nations have ratified the conventions, making them among the most widely adopted international treaties.
