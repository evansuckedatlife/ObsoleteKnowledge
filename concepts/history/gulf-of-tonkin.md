## summary

The **Gulf of Tonkin** incident denotes two naval encounters in August 1964 off the coast of North Vietnam that catalyzed open American military intervention in the *Vietnam War*. While the first skirmish between North Vietnamese torpedo boats and the destroyer *USS Maddox* occurred during covert intelligence gathering, the second alleged attack on August 4 was based on phantom radar returns and misinterpreted signals intelligence. Seizing on the disputed reports, President *Lyndon B. Johnson* secured the **Gulf of Tonkin Resolution**, granting sweeping executive power to conduct military operations in Southeast Asia without an official congressional declaration of war.

## you gotta know

- Occurred in early August 1964 in the body of water bordered by North Vietnam, southern *[[china]]*, and Hainan Island during covert maritime operations known as *OPLAN 34A*.
- On August 2, 1964, the destroyer *USS Maddox* engaged three North Vietnamese P-4 torpedo boats while conducting a *DESOTO* electronic espionage patrol, exchanging fire and receiving air support from four *F-8 Crusader* jets from the aircraft carrier *USS Ticonderoga*.
- On the dark, stormy night of August 4, 1964, the *USS Maddox* and the *USS Turner Joy* reported coming under sustained torpedo attack after tracking dozens of erratic high-speed radar contacts and sonar pings.
- Task force commander Captain *John J. Herrick* sent urgent follow-up cables within hours cautioning that freak weather effects, overeager sonar operators, and dark seas created false impressions, stating that "many reported contacts and torpedoes fired appear doubtful."
- Naval aviator *James Stockdale*, who flew directly over the destroyers that night searching for enemy vessels, confirmed that he saw no enemy craft, wakes, or muzzle flashes anywhere in the gulf.
- Secretary of Defense *Robert McNamara* and military commanders selectively presented the incident to Congress as unprovoked aggression in international waters, concealing both the covert South Vietnamese raids on nearby islands and the deep doubts regarding the second attack.
- Congress passed the **Gulf of Tonkin Resolution** on August 7, 1964, with unanimous approval in the House of Representatives and only two dissenting votes in the Senate, cast by *Wayne Morse* and *Ernest Gruening*.
- The resolution served as the statutory substitute for a declaration of war, allowing the rapid deployment of combat troops and unleashing massive bombing campaigns like *[[operation-rolling-thunder]]*.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — the global ideological conflict that framed American policy makers' containment doctrine and Domino Theory in Southeast Asia.
- [[united-states]] — the superpower whose military, political, and cultural landscape was fundamentally reshaped by the ensuing protracted ground and air war.
- [[anti-war-movement]] — the widespread domestic protest movement that erupted across campuses and cities as casualty lists mounted and the war's premise was questioned.
- [[tet-offensive]] — the massive 1968 communist campaign that permanently eroded public confidence in official claims of military victory.
- [[operation-rolling-thunder]] — the sustained aerial bombardment campaign of North Vietnam initiated directly as an escalatory response after the incident.
- [[china]] — the neighboring communist giant providing vital logistical, economic, and anti-aircraft support to North Vietnam throughout the conflict.
- [[soviet-union]] — the rival superpower that supplied North Vietnam with advanced surface-to-air missiles, radar systems, and military equipment.
