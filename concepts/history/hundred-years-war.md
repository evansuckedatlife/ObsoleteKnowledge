# Hundred Years' War

## summary

The **Hundred Years' War** (1337–1453) was a prolonged conflict between England and France over succession to the French throne, territorial control, and dynastic prestige. The war devastated French territory, witnessed the rise of professional armies and artillery, saw *Joan of Arc* become a national martyr, and ultimately ended with English loss of nearly all continental holdings, reshaping the political map of northwestern Europe and the identity of both nations.

## you gotta know

- Rooted in the *Norman conquest* and dynastic overlap (English kings held French territories and claimed the French throne), the war erupted when *Edward III of England* asserted his claim to the French crown in 1337, challenging the *Valois* succession.
- Early English victories—*Crécy* (1346), *Agincourt* (1415)—demonstrated the effectiveness of the English longbow and pike formations against French cavalry, crushing the myth of aristocratic military superiority and establishing a new warfare paradigm.
- The war was not continuous but rather a series of campaigns interspersed with truces; *Joan of Arc* (1412–31), a peasant mystic, rallied French forces at *Orléans* (1429) and catalyzed French resistance, though captured and executed by English allies; her martyrdom inspired French national identity and recovery.
- French forces regrouped under *Charles VII* and pushed English armies out of Normandy and Aquitaine; by 1453, England retained only the port city of Calais in France (lost finally in 1558).
- The war spurred the development of centralized nation-states: France consolidated territory and identity around its monarchy; England redirected ambitions eastward (toward Scotland and eventually colonialism) and inward (toward the *Wars of the Roses*, a dynastic civil war).
- Artillery became decisive in sieges and field battles; mercenary companies, standing armies, and professional military organization replaced feudal levies, accelerating the transition from medieval to early modern warfare.
