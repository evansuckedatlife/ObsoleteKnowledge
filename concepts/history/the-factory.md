## summary

**The Factory** was the legendary Midtown [[new-york-city]] studio founded by artist *Andy Warhol* in 1962, functioning as an influential workspace, countercultural salon, and bohemian gathering hub. Operating on an assembly-line model that mirrored contemporary capitalist production, the studio churned out mass-market silkscreen prints, experimental films, musical projects, and social celebrities while systematically eroding traditional boundaries between commercial commerce and fine art. As a cornerstone of postwar [[modernism]], the studio redefined the nature of artistic authorship in an era shaped by television, mass marketing, and booming American [[consumerism]].

## you gotta know

- Founded by *Andy Warhol* in 1962 on East 47th Street in [[new-york-city]], the studio served as his primary creative headquarters before relocating to Union Square West and later Broadway.
- Widely known as the **Silver Factory**, the original fifth-floor loft was transformed by photographer and interior designer *Billy Name*, who encrusted the walls, ceilings, and structural pipes in silver foil, industrial spray paint, and mirror shards to craft an immersive space-age salon.
- Operated on a quasi-industrial assembly-line methodology where Warhol and assistants used commercial [[silkscreen]] techniques to print repetitive grids of consumer goods like *Campbell's Soup Cans* and portraits of celebrities such as *Marilyn Monroe* and *Elvis Presley*.
- Functioned as a musical laboratory and rehearsal venue for the rock band *The Velvet Underground*, whose live performances were integrated into Warhol's traveling multimedia light-and-sound exhibition *Exploding Plastic Inevitable*.
- Established an avant-garde cinema workshop that produced hundreds of three-minute *Screen Tests* alongside radical feature-length films, including the stationary eight-hour continuous view of the Empire State Building in *Empire* and the split-screen underground hit *Chelsea Girls*.
- Fostered a revolving, flamboyant social circle known as the "Warhol Superstars," including figures like *Edie Sedgwick*, *Nico*, *Viva*, *Ultra Violet*, and *Candy Darling*, who dissolved private life into theatrical public performance and pioneered modern celebrity culture.
- Suffered a violent crisis on June 3, 1968, when radical feminist writer *Valerie Solanas*, founder of the *SCUM* movement, shot Warhol inside the studio, nearly ending his life and prompting a transition toward a more tightly secured, professionalized enterprise.
- Embodied the historical deindustrialization of the [[united-states]] as heavy manufacturing fled metropolitan centers, enabling postwar artists to colonize abandoned manufacturing lofts and repurpose industrial assembly techniques for the commodification of culture.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — the dominant twentieth-century aesthetic framework whose emphasis on authentic emotional expression and unique authorship was subverted by mechanical replication.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical era of factory mechanization whose division of labor and production-line systems were ironically adapted into fine-art fabrication.
- [[new-york-city]] — the post-World War II cultural capital whose abundant, inexpensive commercial loft spaces provided the physical architecture for collaborative studio experiments.
- [[silent-film]] — the foundational cinematic format that directly informed Warhol's unedited, stationary camera work, standard-speed projection choices, and silent screen portraits.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical contest whose domestic prosperity and ideological promotion of Western consumer culture provided both the subject matter and international audience for Pop Art.
- [[united-states]] — the postwar industrial superpower whose advertising culture, mass supermarket commodities, and Hollywood mythology supplied the studio's primary visual iconography.
- [[pop-art]] — the international movement headquartered at the studio that dismantled entrenched hierarchies between high culture and mass commercial advertising.
