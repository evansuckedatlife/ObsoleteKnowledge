## summary

**Ida B. Wells** was an American investigative journalist, educator, and early civil rights leader who spearheaded the national anti-lynching crusade during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Born into slavery in Mississippi during the *American Civil War*, she used rigorous statistical reporting and investigative journalism to expose white supremacist violence and debunk racist pretexts for mob murder. A co-founder of the *National Association for the Advancement of Colored People* (*NAACP*) and a militant suffragist, she spent much of her career organizing political and civic reforms from Chicago, *Illinois*.

## you gotta know

- Published *Southern Horrors: Lynch Law in All Its Phases* and *A Red Record*, pioneering works of investigative data journalism that demonstrated lynching was an economic and political tool to suppress Black progress rather than retribution for criminal acts.
- Co-owned and edited the Memphis newspaper *The Free Speech and Headlight*, whose offices were burned down by an armed white mob in 1892 while she was traveling out of state.
- Sued the Chesapeake, Ohio and Southwestern Railroad Company in 1884 after being forcibly dragged from a first-class ladies' train car for refusing to give up her seat, winning at the circuit court level before the decision was overturned by the Tennessee Supreme Court.
- Traveled extensively across the *United Kingdom* in 1893 and 1894 on speaking tours to organize international boycotts against American cotton, establishing the British Anti-Lynching Committee.
- Boycotted the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago alongside *Frederick Douglass* and *Ferdinand Lee Barnett*, co-authoring and distributing the protest pamphlet *The Reason Why the Colored American Is Not in the World's Columbian Exposition*.
- Refused to march at the back of the segregated 1913 Woman Suffrage Parade in Washington, D.C., organized by *Alice Paul*, waiting along the parade route to step directly into the *Illinois* delegation between two white supporters.
- Founded the Alpha Suffrage Club in Chicago in 1913, the first Black women's suffrage organization in *Illinois*, which played a pivotal role in electing *Oscar De Priest* as the city's first Black alderman.
- Posthumously awarded a Pulitzer Prize Special Citation in 2020 for her groundbreaking reporting on the horrific and vicious violence against African Americans during the lynching era.
