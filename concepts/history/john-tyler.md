## summary

**John Tyler** was the first vice president to assume the presidency after a sitting president's death, establishing a constitutional precedent that would guide succession for over a century. When William Henry Harrison died in 1841, Tyler asserted his right to the full presidency rather than merely acting as a caretaker, an act that redefined executive power. Though a political outsider despised by his own party, his administration oversaw territorial expansion and modernized the Office of the President.

## you gotta know

- Born in Virginia in 1790; served in Congress and as a U.S. Senator before becoming vice president in 1841.
- Became president upon Harrison's death just 31 days into his term, claiming the office was constitutionally his, not a temporary acting role.
- Expelled from the Whig Party after refusing to adopt their economic agenda, becoming a president without a party.
- Oversaw the annexation of Texas in 1845, a pivotal act that intensified sectional tensions over slavery's expansion.
- Strengthened presidential authority by asserting executive prerogatives against congressional demands, expanding the modern presidency.
- Retired to Virginia after his term and later returned to politics as a pro-secession Confederate congressman.
