## summary

**Justinian I** was a 6th-century Byzantine emperor whose ambitions to restore Roman imperial power across the Mediterranean shaped the medieval world for centuries. He commissioned monumental works like the [[hagia-sophia]], reformed the law through the *Justinian Code*, and launched military campaigns that temporarily reconquered vast territories in North Africa and the Mediterranean. Though his reign expanded Byzantine power to its greatest territorial extent, plague, warfare, and economic strain ultimately undermined his vision and left the empire exhausted.

## you gotta know

- Born around 482; became emperor in 527 and ruled until 565, a reign spanning four decades of constant warfare and ambitious building.
- Appointed his best general, Belisarius, to reconquer former Roman territories: North Africa, Sicily, and Italy were reclaimed from barbarian kingdoms.
- Commissioned the *Justinian Code*, a comprehensive legal compilation that organized Roman law and became the foundation of European law for centuries.
- Built the Hagia Sophia (532–537), the greatest architectural achievement of the Byzantine age and an enduring symbol of Byzantine civilization.
- His reign coincided with the [[plague-of-justinian]] (541–549), which killed millions and undermined his military and economic achievements.
- By his death, the empire had expanded to include North Africa, the Mediterranean coast, and reconquered Rome itself, but was financially and militarily exhausted.
