## summary

**Lester B. Pearson** was a Canadian scholar, diplomat, and statesman who served as the 14th prime minister of Canada from 1963 to 1968. Before leading the country, he was awarded the 1957 *Nobel Peace Prize* for conceptualising and deploying the *United Nations Emergency Force* to resolve the 1956 *Suez Crisis*. Despite heading back-to-back minority governments, his premiership fundamentally established modern Canadian social democracy, introducing universal healthcare, the *Canada Pension Plan*, and the iconic maple leaf national flag.

## you gotta know

- Awarded the *Nobel Peace Prize* in 1957 for creating the modern doctrine of international peacekeeping, deploying armed UN peacekeepers to resolve the *Suez Crisis* and defuse hostilities between Britain, France, Israel, and Egypt.
- Governed entirely without a parliamentary majority across two successive terms between 1963 and 1968, relying on cooperation with the *New Democratic Party* (*NDP*) led by *Tommy Douglas*.
- Spearheaded the adoption of the distinctive red-and-white maple leaf as Canada's official national flag in 1965, prevailing in the fierce parliamentary and public debate known as the "Great Flag Debate" over supporters of the traditional Red Ensign.
- Established the core pillars of Canada's social welfare safety net, presiding over the passage of the *Medical Care Act* of 1966 that created universal publicly funded healthcare, as well as the *Canada Pension Plan* and *Canada Student Loans*.
- Established the landmark *Royal Commission on Bilingualism and Biculturalism* in 1963 to address rising nationalism in Quebec, laying the groundwork for federal bilingual legislation later enacted by his successor, [[pierre-elliott-trudeau]].
- Recruited influential intellectuals into federal politics—most notably the "Three Wise Men" (*Gérard Pelletier*, *Jean Marchand*, and [[pierre-elliott-trudeau]])—ensuring a powerful federalist presence in Quebec.
- Served in both [[world-war-i]] as an ambulance driver and pilot, and [[world-war-ii]] as a senior diplomat, later helping draft the founding charter of the *North Atlantic Treaty Organization* (*NATO*).

## connections

- [[pierre-elliott-trudeau]] — cabinet protégé appointed by Pearson as minister of justice, who succeeded him as prime minister and leader of the *Liberal Party*.
- [[justin-trudeau]] — modern Liberal prime minister who frequently cites Pearsonian internationalism and social programs as foundational to his party's identity.
- [[cold-war]] — global geopolitical confrontation during which Pearson pioneered UN peacekeeping and helped establish collective security via *NATO*.
- [[world-war-ii]] — worldwide conflict during which Pearson held vital diplomatic postings in Washington and London, preparing him for postwar multilateral diplomacy.
- [[world-war-i]] — conflict in which Pearson served overseas, an experience that shaped his lifelong commitment to international conflict resolution.
- [[british-empire]] — historic empire whose colonial symbols Pearson replaced in Canada, notably replacing the colonial Red Ensign with the independent maple leaf flag.
- [[united-states]] — crucial southern ally with whom Pearson maintained pragmatic relations, signing the landmark 1965 *Auto Pact* despite tensions with *Lyndon B. Johnson* over the Vietnam War.
