## summary

**Eugene Debs** was an American socialist political leader, anti-war activist, and perennial presidential nominee whose campaigns transformed electoral radicalism during the Progressive Era. Running as the standard-bearer for the *Socialist Party of America* in five presidential elections between 1900 and 1920, he articulated a distinctly American brand of democratic socialism grounded in the Declaration of Independence, abolitionism, and Christian ethics. He achieved his greatest electoral success in the historic four-way election of 1912, capturing six percent of the popular vote, and later mounted his famous 1920 campaign as "Convict No. 9653" while incarcerated in federal prison for opposing American involvement in [[world-war-i]].

## you gotta know

- Ran for President of the [[united-states]] five times (1900, 1904, 1908, 1912, and 1920), touring the nation in 1908 aboard a dedicated campaign train dubbed the *Red Special*.
- Captured over 900,000 votes (six percent of the popular tally) in the legendary election of 1912, challenging [[woodrow-wilson]], [[theodore-roosevelt]], and [[william-howard-taft]] in the high-water mark for American democratic socialism.
- Delivered a fiery anti-war address in Canton, Ohio, on June 16, 1918, denouncing imperialist militarism and declaring that the working class always fights the wars declared by ruling elites.
- Arrested two weeks after the Canton speech and charged with ten counts of sedition under the *Espionage Act of 1917*, which criminalized public opposition to the draft or military operations.
- Made a celebrated statement to the court at his sentencing: "While there is a lower class, I am in it; while there is a criminal element, I am of it; while there is a soul in prison, I am not free."
- Had his ten-year prison sentence unanimously affirmed by the Supreme Court in *Debs v. United States* (1919), with Justice *Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.* invoking the "clear and present danger" doctrine.
- Polled over 913,000 votes while locked inside the *Atlanta Federal Penitentiary* in the 1920 presidential election won by *Warren G. Harding*.
- Had his prison sentence commuted on Christmas Day in 1921 by President *Warren G. Harding*, who invited him to the White House and warmly greeted him as a patriotic man of honor.
