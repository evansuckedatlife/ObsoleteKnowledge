## summary

**Joan of Arc**, known as *Jeanne d'Arc*, was a French military leader and saint who played a pivotal role in defending [[france]] during the later stages of the [[hundred-years-war]]. Born a peasant girl in eastern [[france]], she claimed divine guidance and led French forces to crucial victories against [[england|English]] occupation. Her remarkable military career, trial for heresy, and martyrdom at the stake made her a national symbol of French resistance and spiritual conviction.

## you gotta know

- Born around 1412 in the village of Domrémy in eastern [[france]] to a peasant family with no military background or connections.
- Claimed to receive visions and divine voices from saints, instructing her to lead [[france]] to victory during a period of military defeat and occupation.
- Convinced the weak [[france|French]] dauphin (later [[charles-vii]]) of her divine mission and secured command of an army despite her youth and gender.
- Achieved her most celebrated victory at Orléans in 1429, rallying [[france|French]] forces along the [[loire-river]] and turning the tide of the [[hundred-years-war]].
- Captured by Burgundian forces and sold to the [[england|English]], who tried her for heresy and witchcraft in 1431.
- Burned at the stake in Rouen in 1431 at age 19, becoming a martyr; later canonized as a saint and national heroine of [[france]].
- Her brief military career lasted only about a year and a half, yet it fundamentally shifted the momentum of a war that had lasted nearly a century.
- Her trial record is one of the best-documented medieval proceedings and provides insight into medieval warfare, gender roles, and religious belief.
- Became an enduring symbol of French nationalism, female heroism, and religious conviction; her legacy influenced literature, art, and political thought for centuries.

## connections
