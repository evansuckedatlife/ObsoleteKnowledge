## summary

**Ironclad ships** were armored warships covered with iron or steel plating, representing a revolutionary shift in naval technology during the mid-19th century. Replacing wooden sailing ships vulnerable to cannon fire, ironclads combined steam power with armor protection, fundamentally transforming naval warfare. The [[Battle of Hampton Roads]] (1862) between the USS Monitor and CSS Virginia (Merrimack) demonstrated the obsolescence of wooden warships and launched the ironclad era.

## you gotta know

- Emerged in the 1850s as navies developed steam engines and sought protection against increasingly powerful cannon weaponry; the vulnerability of wooden ships to modern artillery made innovation imperative.
- Early ironclads like the French [[La Gloire]] (1859) and British HMS Warrior (1860) prompted naval arms races as nations rushed to build armored fleets; these ships proved far superior to wooden warships in combat trials.
- Faced significant technical challenges: armor plating added enormous weight, requiring more powerful engines; this reduced speed and maneuverability, making ironclads slow, difficult to handle, and poor sailors in heavy seas.
- The [[Battle of Hampton Roads]] in March 1862 pitted the Union's ironclad USS Monitor (with rotating turret) against the Confederate CSS Virginia (converted from USS Merrimack), with neither gaining decisive victory but shocking observers that wooden warships were now obsolete and armored vessels dominated.
- Rendered wooden-hulled sailing ships and earlier warship designs completely obsolete, forcing comprehensive naval modernization worldwide; established navies had to scrap fleets of ships built within recent years as they suddenly became militarily worthless.
- Featured gun turrets (rotating mounts for cannon), allowing broader firing arcs and the ability to engage targets without repositioning the entire ship—a major advantage compared to fixed guns on broadside-armed wooden warships.
- Remained dominant until the HMS Dreadnought (1906) introduced the "all-big-gun" battleship with uniform large-caliber armament and steam turbine propulsion, triggering another naval arms race and making previous ironclad designs obsolete in turn.
