## summary

The **Embargo Act** was an 1807 legislative measure passed by the United States Congress and signed into law by President *[[thomas-jefferson]]*. Intended as a form of non-violent economic coercion against Great Britain and Napoleonic France, the act closed American ports to foreign commerce and banned exports to all foreign destinations. The policy devastated the domestic economy of the *[[united-states]]*, particularly merchant shipping in New England, while failing to compel European powers to respect American neutral rights.

## you gotta know

- Signed into law by *[[thomas-jefferson]]* in December 1807 in response to British and French maritime violations during the Napoleonic Wars.
- Direct reaction to the *Chesapeake-Leopard* affair of 1807, in which a British warship attacked a US frigate and impressed American sailors into royal naval service.
- Banned all American merchant vessels from clearing for foreign ports and severely restricted foreign ships from taking on cargo in American harbors.
- Sparked pervasive domestic outrage, widespread smuggling, and satirical political cartoons depicting the embargo as a biting snapping turtle named "Ograbme" ("embargo" spelled backwards).
- Inflicted severe economic depression across mercantile hubs in New England, driving unemployment and revitalizing the moribund Federalist Party.
- Inadvertently stimulated domestic American manufacturing by cutting off British textile and factory imports, spurring early elements of the *[[industrial-revolution]]*.
- Repealed in March 1809 in the closing days of Jefferson's presidency, replaced by the less comprehensive Non-Intercourse Act.

## connections

- [[thomas-jefferson]] — championed the policy as an alternative to war, but suffered severe reputational damage from its enforcement.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — issued the Continental System decrees that prompted escalating naval tensions with neutral American trade.
- [[united-states]] — young republic whose commercial autonomy and sovereignty were imperiled by European naval warfare.
- [[england]] — targeted by the act due to its aggressive blockades and regular impressment of American sailors.
- [[france]] — confiscated neutral American shipping under Napoleonic Berlin and Milan decrees.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — unintended beneficiary, as domestic New England manufacturing arose to substitute for embargoed imports.
