## summary

**African-American culture**, also termed **Black American culture**, encompasses the distinct artistic, literary, musical, linguistic, and social traditions developed by people of African descent in the [[united-states]]. Emerging from the crucible of chattel slavery and systemic racial oppression, this resilient tradition syncretized West and Central African customs with European influences while generating profoundly innovative forms of expression. Its enduring contributions—from spirituals and folklore to the global dominance of contemporary popular music and literature—have fundamentally redefined American identity and international culture.

## you gotta know

- Pioneered foundational musical genres including spirituals, the blues, [[jazz]], gospel, rhythm and blues, and hip-hop, characterized by signature techniques such as syncopation, blue notes, call-and-response, and polyrhythmic improvisation.
- Sparked the 1920s [[harlem-renaissance]] in [[new-york-city]], an artistic and literary flourishing led by figures such as *Langston Hughes*, *Zora Neale Hurston*, and *Alain Locke*, who anthologized the creative emergence of the "New Negro."
- Organized civic leadership and social resilience through the Black church, especially independent denominations like the African Methodist Episcopal Church founded by *Richard Allen*, which later served as structural headquarters for the [[civil-rights-movement]].
- Maintained rich oral traditions and folklore featuring animal trickster cycles like *Br'er Rabbit*, derived from West African *Anansi* tales, alongside pervasive rhetorical practices such as "signifying" and the competitive insult game known as the "dozens."
- Established distinct commemorations including *Juneteenth*, which marks the delayed emancipation of enslaved people in Texas following the [[american-civil-war]], and *Black History Month*, established in 1926 by historian *Carter G. Woodson* as Negro History Week.
- Reshaped urban demographic and cultural landscapes through the *Great Migration*, wherein over six million Black Americans relocated from the rural South, pioneering the urban electric blues of Chicago and northern community art centers.
- Developed distinct regional culinary traditions known as *soul food*, transforming rations and discarded animal parts into celebrated dishes while incorporating traditional African agricultural contributions such as okra, black-eyed peas, yams, and sesame.
- Preserved ancestral African religious and performative customs through the *ring shout*, an ecstatic counterclockwise circle dance driven by stick-beating and foot-shuffling, surviving most intact among the isolated coastal *Gullah Geechee* communities.

## connections

- [[harlem-renaissance]] — the landmark cultural explosion centered in northern Manhattan that asserted Black creative autonomy and artistic pride.
- [[jazz]] — the revolutionary, syncopated musical tradition born in New Orleans that became one of America's primary cultural exports.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the political crusade against segregation that drew its primary strength, rhetoric, and hymns from Black communal institutions.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the critical post-war epoch in which newly freed people established independent schools, churches, and civic bodies.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict that brought the legal destruction of chattel slavery, inaugurating a new era of public cultural creation.
- [[american-literature]] — the national literary tradition deeply transformed by Black authors investigating the color line, democracy, and identity.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose popular music, vernacular language, dance, and spiritual traditions were centrally defined by African Americans.
