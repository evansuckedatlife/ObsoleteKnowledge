## summary

**Conquistador** (*Spanish: "conqueror"*) refers to the Spanish military adventurers and soldiers who conquered vast territories in the Americas in the sixteenth century, toppling the *Aztec* and *Inca* empires and establishing Spanish colonial rule across Mesoamerica and South America. Armed with steel weapons, horses, and a willingness to commit atrocities, conquistadores like *Hernán Cortés* and *Francisco Pizarro* transformed the geopolitical map, enslaved millions of indigenous peoples, and initiated the demographic catastrophe of disease and colonization that reshaped the Americas forever.

## you gotta know

- Conquistadores were typically Spanish soldiers of fortune, religious zealots, and ambitious adventurers seeking wealth, land, and honor; Spain officially sanctioned conquests through *encomienda* grants—permits to conquer territory and extract indigenous labor.
- *Hernán Cortés* conquered the *Aztec Empire* (1519–1521) with fewer than 600 men by exploiting indigenous political divisions, indigenous fears of European technology, and relentless violence; the conquest triggered epidemics that killed far more than Spanish swords.
- *Francisco Pizarro* conquered the *Inca Empire* (1532–1572) with similar tactics: small Spanish forces leveraged internal Incan conflicts, captured the Inca ruler *Atahualpa*, and dominated through superior military technology and indigenous allies seeking to overthrow Incan rule.
- Conquistadores established encomienda—a labor system that enslaved indigenous peoples under the pretense of Christian conversion; it was slavery in all but name, worked to death in mines extracting silver for Spanish wealth.
- The conquistador era depended on indigenous labor and knowledge: Spanish forces relied on indigenous guides, porters, and warriors who outnumbered Spaniards by orders of magnitude; indigenous intermediaries translated and negotiated; without them, conquests would have been impossible.
- The demographic consequence was catastrophic: disease introduced by Europeans (smallpox, measles) killed an estimated 90% of the indigenous population within a century; this plague preceded Spanish conquest in many regions, weakening indigenous resistance and enabling Spanish dominance.
