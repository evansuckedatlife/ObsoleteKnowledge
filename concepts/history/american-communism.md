## summary

**American Communism** refers to the Marxist-Leninist political movement in the [[united-states]], predominantly organized under the **Communist Party USA** (**CPUSA**), which emerged in 1919 when revolutionary left-wing factions split from the reformist *Socialist Party of America* following the Bolshevik Revolution. The movement functioned as the primary ideological rival to democratic socialism, seeking revolutionary transformation under the guidance of the *Communist International* (*Comintern*) while embedding itself in domestic labor and racial justice struggles. Though severely repressed during successive *Red Scares* and compromised by financial and ideological subservience to the [[soviet-union]], it exerted significant influence on twentieth-century industrial unionism, civil rights legal defense, and left-wing culture.

## you gotta know

- Faced severe federal prosecution during the [[cold-war]], culminating in *Smith Act* convictions upheld in *Dennis v. United States*, widespread blacklisting by the *House Un-American Activities Committee*, and the execution of *Julius and Ethel Rosenberg* for atomic espionage.
- Originated in September 1919 during a contentious convention split in Chicago, where radicals led by *Charles Ruthenberg* and journalist *John Reed* abandoned the *Socialist Party of America* to align directly with Moscow's *Comintern*, triggering the immediate roundups of the *Palmer Raids*.
- Achieved peak domestic popularity during the *Popular Front* of the 1930s under general secretary *Earl Browder*, who championed the slogan "Communism is twentieth-century Americanism" and embedded thousands of party members into the industrial organizing drives of the *CIO*.
- Led early anti-racist and civil rights legal battles through its legal affiliate, the *International Labor Defense*, most famously orchestrating the global defense campaign for the *Scottsboro Boys* in Alabama while advancing self-determination theories for the southern *Black Belt*.
- Maintained extensive underground apparatuses and espionage networks funded by secret Moscow subsidies, a relationship conclusively documented after the [[cold-war]] through the release of decrypted Soviet cables in the *Venona project*.
- Suffered a devastating internal crisis in 1956 when *Nikita Khrushchev's* "Secret Speech" exposing *Joseph Stalin's* atrocities and the Soviet suppression of the Hungarian Revolution prompted the mass resignation of over half its membership, including key staff at the *Daily Worker*.
- Remained under the rigid, pro-Soviet leadership of *Gus Hall* from 1959 to 2000, who ran four presidential campaigns with activist *Angela Davis* as his vice-presidential candidate before Soviet collapse permanently eliminated the party's institutional backing.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — prompted federal anti-subversion trials, loyalty screenings, and congressional investigations that effectively dismantled party influence.
- [[soviet-union]] — provided strategic oversight through the *Comintern*, covert operational funding, and the ideological dogma that dictated party policy.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — inherited key activist networks, legal strategies, and labor alliances forged by communist organizers fighting Jim Crow segregation.
- [[world-war-ii]] — fostered a brief period of domestic legitimacy when the wartime alliance with Moscow allowed communists to enthusiastically back the national war effort.
- [[harlem-renaissance]] — engaged prominent Black intellectuals and literary figures, including *Claude McKay* and *Langston Hughes*, who explored anti-imperialist Marxist ideas.
- [[world-war-i]] — catalyzed the wartime state crackdowns on dissent and the radicalization that fractured American socialism into rival revolutionary and reformist camps.
- [[new-york-city]] — served as the national headquarters, intellectual epicenter, and publishing home of the movement's flagship periodical, the *Daily Worker*.
