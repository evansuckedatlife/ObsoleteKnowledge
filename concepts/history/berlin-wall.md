# Berlin Wall

## summary

The **Berlin Wall** was a concrete barrier that divided *East* and *West Berlin* from 1961 to 1989, erected by the *German Democratic Republic* (East Germany) to prevent its citizens from fleeing to the West. It became the physical and symbolic embodiment of the *Cold War* and the *Iron Curtain*, and its fall in 1989 marked the beginning of the end of communist control in Eastern Europe.

## you gotta know

- The wall was built in August 1961 by the *Soviet Union* and East German authorities to stop the massive exodus of East Germans fleeing communist rule; hundreds of thousands had already escaped through Berlin, embarrassing the regime.
- The wall divided families, separated siblings and spouses, and created one of the most haunting symbols of the Cold War—a physical manifestation of ideological division that made escape to the West tantamount to betrayal in the communist state's eyes.
- *East Germany* guarded the wall with shoot-on-sight orders; hundreds died attempting to cross (estimates range from 140 to over 200), making it a deadly barrier that tested Western resolve and communism's moral bankruptcy.
- The wall's construction prompted fears in the West that it might presage Soviet action against *West Berlin*, but *President John F. Kennedy* affirmed American commitment to defend the city, avoiding military confrontation.
- The wall became a focus of Cold War symbolism: *Ronald Reagan*'s 1987 speech ("Mr. Gorbachev, tear down this wall!") and the *Fall of the Berlin Wall* (November 1989) marked the collapse of Soviet control and the imminent end of the Cold War.
- The wall's demolition was largely spontaneous—crowds celebrated, hacked at the concrete, and toppled it in a joyous release—and symbolized the power of popular pressure against authoritarian rule.
- The fall of the Berlin Wall led directly to German reunification (1990) and accelerated the dissolution of the *Soviet Union* and the end of communist hegemony in Eastern Europe.
