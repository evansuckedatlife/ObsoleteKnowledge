## summary

**Henry VII** was the Welsh-born king who defeated *Richard III* at the [[battle-of-bosworth-field|Battle of Bosworth Field]] in 1485 and founded the [[tudor-dynasty|Tudor Dynasty]], ending the Wars of the Roses. His reign marked a transition from medieval to early modern English kingship, establishing centralized royal authority, reducing the power of the nobility, and laying the foundation for the Tudor golden age. Though less famous than his granddaughter *Elizabeth I* or his son *Henry VIII*, Henry VII was a master political operator whose shrewd governance unified a fractured realm.

## you gotta know

- Born Henry Tudor, a Welsh nobleman with a tenuous claim to the English throne through his mother's bloodline.
- Defeated *Richard III* at Bosworth Field, a decisive victory that allowed him to seize the crown and establish the Tudor line.
- Married *Elizabeth of York*, daughter of *Edward IV*, uniting the rival York and Lancaster houses symbolically through matrimony.
- Established the [[tudor-dynasty|Tudor Dynasty]], which would rule England for over a century and produce some of England's most famous monarchs.
- Strengthened royal finances through careful management and avoided costly foreign adventures, building a stable treasury.
- Reduced the power of overmighty nobles by breaking up private armies and using the Court of Star Chamber to enforce royal justice.
- Sponsored explorer [[john-cabot|John Cabot]]'s expedition to North America in 1497, establishing a claim to territory in the New World.
