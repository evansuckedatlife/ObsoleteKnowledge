## summary

The **Holy Alliance** (French: *Sainte-Alliance*; German: *Heilige Allianz*) was a conservative diplomatic coalition formed in Paris in September 1815 by the monarchs of Russia, Austria, and Prussia in the immediate wake of the Napoleonic Wars. Instigated by *Tsar Alexander I* of the [[russian-empire]], the pact bound signatory sovereigns to govern their subjects and conduct international statecraft in accordance with the tenets of [[christianity]]. Over the subsequent decades, the coalition became an enduring symbol of reactionary absolutism and political intervention, operating as an ideological bulwark designed to crush liberal and constitutional revolutions across Restoration-era Europe.

## you gotta know

- Established in September 1815 in Paris by *Tsar Alexander I* of Russia, *Emperor Francis I* of Austria, and *King Frederick William III* of Prussia following the final defeat of [[napoleon-bonaparte]] at Waterloo.
- Championed by the deeply mystical *Alexander I*, whose worldview had become intensely pious under the spiritual guidance of Baltic German pietist *Baroness Barbara von Krüdener*.
- Dismissed privately by Austrian state chancellor *Prince Klemens von Metternich* as a "loud-sounding nothing" and "sublime mysticism," though *Metternich* adroitly weaponized the treaty's fraternal rhetoric to coordinate conservative police actions and defend Austrian hegemony.
- Dedicated governmental authority explicitly to the "Holy and Undivided Trinity," declaring that the allied rulers constituted a single Christian brotherhood tasked with preserving order, justice, and peace.
- Acceded to by almost every reigning monarch in continental Europe, while *Pope Pius VII* conspicuously refused to sign due to its ecumenical blurring of Catholic, Protestant, and Orthodox lines, and the sultan of the [[ottoman-empire]] was excluded as a non-Christian ruler.
- Spurned by Great Britain, whose foreign secretary *Lord Castlereagh* famously derided it as a piece of "sublime mysticism and nonsense" incompatible with parliamentary government, leading Britain to favor the secular, treaty-based Quadruple Alliance.
- Furnished the ideological pretext for multilateral military crackdowns authorized at the Congresses of Troppau, Laibach, and Verona, dispatching Austrian armies into [[italy]] to crush constitutionalists in Naples and Piedmont and licensing [[france]] to suppress the liberal Trienio Liberal in [[spain]].
- Provoked the formulation of the 1823 Monroe Doctrine by the [[united-states]], which feared that the reactionary European monarchies would attempt armed interventions in the Western Hemisphere to restore Spanish imperial authority over newly independent Latin American republics.

## connections

- [[russian-empire]] — the expansive Eurasian power whose monarch, *Tsar Alexander I*, envisioned and drafted the spiritual compact.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the deposed French emperor whose continent-spanning conquests prompted conservative monarchs to establish a unified post-war security framework.
- [[christianity]] — the theological framework invoked by the treaty to bind Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant rulers against secular revolutionary philosophy.
- [[france]] — the restored Bourbon monarchy that joined the conservative coalition and deployed armed forces into [[spain]] under its auspices in 1823.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian state where French forces operating under the alliance's mandate crushed the constitutional government and restored *King Ferdinand VII*.
- [[romanticism]] — the cultural and intellectual movement whose medievalist revival and emotional mysticism profoundly colored *Alexander I*'s religious imagination.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the Muslim-ruled empire explicitly shut out from the alliance's fraternal Christian compact.
- [[united-states]] — the American republic whose Monroe Doctrine emerged in direct opposition to potential alliance expansion into the Americas.
