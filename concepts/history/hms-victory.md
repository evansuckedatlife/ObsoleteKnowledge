## summary

**HMS Victory** is a 104-gun first-rate ship of the line of the Royal Navy, renowned worldwide as the flagship of Vice Admiral *Lord Horatio Nelson* at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805. Launched in 1765 during the zenith of British maritime expansion, the vessel saw extensive service across multiple global conflicts before cementing British naval supremacy in the Napoleonic Wars. Preserved today in Portsmouth Historic Dockyard, she remains the world's oldest commissioned naval warship and serves as the official flagship of the First Sea Lord.

## you gotta know

- Designed by master shipwright *Sir Thomas Slade* and constructed at Chatham Dockyard using thousands of mature English oak trees, carrying over one hundred bronze and iron smoothbore cannon.
- Served as the command flagship of Vice Admiral *Lord Horatio Nelson* at the decisive Battle of Trafalgar on October 21, 1805, where the British Mediterranean Fleet routed the combined French and Spanish fleets.
- Nelson was mortally wounded on her quarterdeck during the engagement by a French sharpshooter firing from the mizzentop of the *Redoutable*, dying in her cramped orlop deck after learning of the fleet's victory.
- Flying from her halliards prior to engaging the enemy line was Nelson's celebrated signal to the fleet: "England expects that every man will do his duty."
- Experienced combat throughout the American War of Independence, fighting at the First and Second Battles of Ushant under Admirals *Augustus Keppel* and *Richard Kempenfelt*.
- Served as the flagship of Admiral *Sir John Jervis* at the Battle of Cape St. Vincent in 1797, which earned Jervis an earldom and highlighted Nelson's audacious boarding tactics.
- Moved into dry dock number 2 at Portsmouth in 1922 following a major public preservation campaign led by the Society for Nautical Research, where she continues to undergo monumental timber restoration.
