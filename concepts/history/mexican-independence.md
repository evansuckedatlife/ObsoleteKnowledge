# Mexican Independence

## summary

**Mexican Independence** was the successful rebellion against *Spanish* colonial rule that culminated in *Mexico's* independence in 1821, ending three centuries of colonial domination. The war (1810–1821) was brutal and transformative, involving a popular uprising led by rural clergy, a prolonged guerrilla conflict, and ultimately a negotiated transfer of power that preserved monarchical institutions.

## you gotta know

- The war began with *Miguel Hidalgo*'s *Grito de Dolores* (September 1810), a call to rebellion that sparked a popular uprising among indigenous, mestizo, and criollo (Spanish-American) populations chafing under colonial rule and Spanish economic monopolies.
- Hidalgo mobilized peasants and indigenous peoples through appeals to Catholic faith and Mexican identity; his army briefly captured several Mexican cities but was defeated and executed by Spanish forces in 1811.
- The war evolved through phases: initial popular insurgency under Hidalgo and *José María Morelos* (1810–1815), Spanish suppression and counterinsurgency, and eventually a conservative independence movement among criollo elites.
- *Agustín de Iturbide*, a Spanish-born military officer, became the unlikely champion of independence; he negotiated the *Plan of Iguala* (1821), which united disparate factions (including ex-insurgents and Spanish forces) behind independence and a constitutional monarchy.
- Iturbide briefly ruled as Emperor of Mexico before being overthrown; Mexico then became a republic, though instability and civil conflict plagued the new nation throughout the 19th century.
- Spanish colonial rule had concentrated wealth in Spanish hands, excluded criollos and indigenous peoples from political power, and imposed mercantilist economic policies; independence removed Spanish authority but did not resolve these structural inequalities.
- The independence war killed hundreds of thousands and devastated the Mexican economy; the new nation faced immediate challenges: foreign debt, internal divisions (conservatives vs. liberals), and land disputes.
