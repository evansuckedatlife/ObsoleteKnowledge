## summary

The **Radical Republicans** were a Congressional faction that dominated Republican politics during the *Reconstruction* period (1865–1877) following the *American Civil War*. Committed to rigorous enforcement of emancipation, equal rights for freedmen, and punishment of the South for rebellion, Radicals rejected moderate approaches to reconciliation. They pushed through constitutional amendments, federal protection of civil rights, and direct federal intervention in Southern affairs, establishing a vision of national citizenship and racial equality that would not be fully realized until the 20th century.

## you gotta know

- The Radical faction included figures like *Thaddeus Stevens* and *Charles Sumner*, who believed the Civil War was a crusade for human freedom and that Reconstruction should ensure freedmen's rights, not merely restore Union.
- Radicals opposed President *Andrew Johnson's* lenient Reconstruction policies, which allowed Southern states to rapidly return to the Union with minimal change and no guarantee of freedmen's rights or safety.
- Radical Republicans authored the *14th Amendment* (1868), establishing national citizenship and equal protection of the laws, and the *15th Amendment* (1870), forbidding racial discrimination in voting rights.
- The *Reconstruction Acts* (1867) were Radical-imposed federal laws that placed Southern states under military governance until they ratified the 14th Amendment and established Black voting rights.
- Radicals created the *Freedmen's Bureau*, a federal agency to protect freedmen's rights, distribute land and aid, and organize education—representing an unprecedented federal intervention in social welfare.
- The impeachment of *Andrew Johnson* (1868) was driven by Radical determination to prevent his obstruction of Reconstruction; though Johnson survived removal by one Senate vote, Radicals gained tighter control afterward.
- Radicals' vision of federal enforcement of civil rights and equality was largely abandoned after 1877; Southern Democrats reasserted control, dismantled Reconstruction protections, and imposed Jim Crow segregation.
