## summary

**Ida B. Tarbell** was an American investigative journalist, biographer, and lecturer who pioneered the muckraking movement during the Progressive Era. Her landmark nineteen-part serial for *McClure's Magazine*, later published as *The History of the Standard Oil Company*, exposed the aggressive monopolistic practices of *John D. Rockefeller*. Her thorough, document-driven reporting catalyzed public outrage, set a foundational precedent for modern investigative journalism, and directly accelerated antitrust legislation in the United States.

## you gotta know

- Authored *The History of the Standard Oil Company* (1904), a seminal expose that meticulously documented corporate espionage, predatory pricing, and secret railroad rebates.
- Spurred federal antitrust action that ultimately culminated in the landmark 1911 Supreme Court decision to dismantle the *Standard Oil* trust under the Sherman Antitrust Act.
- Served as a leading writer and editor for *McClure's Magazine*, working alongside fellow muckrakers such as *Lincoln Steffens* and *Ray Stannard Baker*.
- Grew up in the oil fields of northwestern Pennsylvania, where her father's small petroleum business was devastated by the South Improvement Company scheme engineered by *Rockefeller*.
- Studied at Allegheny College as the only woman in the freshman class of 1876, originally training in biology before pursuing professional journalism.
- Wrote extensive historical biographies, including widely read multivolume studies of *Abraham Lincoln* and *Napoleon Bonaparte*.
- Held complex personal views on gender, supporting women's higher education while opposing woman suffrage, which estranged her from some contemporary feminists.
