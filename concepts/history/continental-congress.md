## summary

The **Continental Congress** was the governing body of the thirteen American colonies during the Revolutionary War and immediate aftermath, first convened in 1774 to coordinate resistance to British policies. It served as both a diplomatic instrument and the de facto government of the emerging United States, drafting the Declaration of Independence and the Articles of Confederation. The Congress embodied colonial unity in the face of imperial authority and gave form to revolutionary ideals.

## you gotta know

- The First Continental Congress met in Philadelphia in 1774 to protest the Coercive (Intolerable) Acts and coordinate colonial response.
- The Second Continental Congress convened in May 1775 after fighting began at Lexington and Concord, and assumed war powers.
- Approved the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776, the primary political document justifying American independence.
- Adopted the Articles of Confederation in 1777, the first constitution, which proved too weak to bind the states effectively.
- Appointed *George Washington* as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army and managed the war effort from afar.
- Comprised delegates from all thirteen colonies, each with one vote, making decisions a matter of negotiation and compromise.
- Dissolved and reconstituted as Congress under the federal Constitution in 1789, marking the transition from revolutionary to constitutional government.
- Financing the war was a persistent struggle; Congress issued paper currency (Continentals) that rapidly depreciated, creating economic chaos and deepening state rivalries.
- Negotiated the Treaty of Paris (1783) ending the Revolutionary War, with *Benjamin Franklin*, *John Adams*, and *John Jay* as lead negotiators securing favorable terms.
- Created committees to manage diplomacy, procurement, and military affairs, establishing bureaucratic structures that prefigured the federal executive departments.
- The Congress's power remained limited; it could not impose taxes or enforce decisions, making it dependent on state compliance and domestic loans.
