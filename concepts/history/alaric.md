## summary

**Alaric** (c. 370–410 CE), also designated as **Alaric I**, was the founding king of the *Visigoths* and the military commander who led the catastrophic sack of [[rome]] in 410 CE. Rising to prominence as a chieftain of Gothic federated auxiliaries fighting for the [[roman-empire]], he led his people through decades of rebellion and migration across the Balkans and [[italy]] in a desperate search for security, grain, and legitimate imperial status. His dramatic breach and three-day plunder of the imperial capital stunned the Mediterranean world, shattered the myth of Roman invulnerability, and accelerated the terminal decline of the Western [[roman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Captured and pillaged [[rome]] in August 410 CE, entering through the *Salarian Gate* after a protracted naval and overland blockade brought the populace to starvation.
- Led the *Visigoths* as their first unified monarch, forging disparate Gothic clans into a powerful military coalition whose descendants eventually established enduring successor kingdoms in [[france]] and [[spain]].
- Served initially as an allied Roman commander under Emperor *Theodosius I*, commanding Gothic auxiliaries at the bloody *Battle of the Frigidus* in 394 CE, where crippling casualties convinced him that Romans treated his soldiers as expendable cannon fodder.
- Clashed repeatedly with the Western Roman regent *Flavius Stilicho*, who checked his incursions across [[italy]] at the battles of *Pollentia* and *Verona* before Stilicho's political execution in 408 CE left the Western court defenseless.
- Demanded substantial annual subsidies of gold, grain, and formal land grants in Noricum or Venetia from Emperor *Honorius* at *Ravenna*, seeking recognition as an imperial magistrate rather than aiming to destroy Roman civilization.
- Ordered strict protection for Christian holy places during the 410 sack, commanding his warriors to spare refugees inside the basilicas of *St. Peter* and *St. Paul* because he practiced Arian [[christianity]].
- Invaded the Balkan peninsula and ravaged [[ancient-greece]] between 395 and 397 CE, sacking classic settlements such as *Corinth*, *Argos*, and *Sparta* while desecrating the ancient sanctuary of *Eleusis*.
- Died suddenly from a violent illness in late 410 CE near *Consentia* while preparing an invasion of North Africa, prompting his followers to divert the *Busento River* to bury him with his spoils before returning the waters to hide his grave.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — served as an allied military officer before leading devastating insurrections that crippled Western imperial administrative authority.
- [[rome]] — encircled the ancient capital through three successive sieges before unleashing his army to plunder it for three days in 410 CE.
- [[italy]] — conducted sweeping military operations across the peninsula for a decade, marching from the northern borders down to the Calabrian coast.
- [[spain]] — established the royal Gothic dynasty whose subsequent generations migrated across the Pyrenees to build a lasting Iberian realm.
- [[france]] — led the migrant nation whose immediate successors negotiated the establishment of the Visigothic Kingdom centered in Aquitaine.
- [[christianity]] — adhered to the Arian Christian faith, which motivated his strict orders to spare major holy basilicas during the sack.
- [[ancient-greece]] — plundered the Peloponnese and central Hellenic regions during his initial campaign against the Eastern imperial court.
