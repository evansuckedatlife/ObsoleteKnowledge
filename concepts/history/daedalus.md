## summary

**Daedalus** was the legendary craftsman and inventor of ancient Greek mythology, renowned for his ingenious mechanical designs and artistic mastery. Most famously, he built the *Labyrinth* on Crete for King *Minos* to house the *Minotaur*. His story blends the mythological with the proto-historical, connecting him to the palace of Knossos and Minoan civilization, and serves as a symbol of human ingenuity and its tragic consequences.

## you gotta know

- Built the *Labyrinth* at Knossos—an elaborate maze so intricate that even its architect could barely navigate it—for King *Minos* to confine the *Minotaur*.
- Created the wooden cow that enabled Pasiphae to deceive a bull, leading to the birth of the *Minotaur* itself.
- Trapped on Crete by King *Minos* after helping *Ariadne* and *Theseus* escape the Labyrinth, he devised an ingenious escape.
- Fashioned artificial wings from feathers and wax for himself and his son *Icarus* so they could flee the island by air.
- His son *Icarus* fell to his death when he flew too close to the sun and the wax melted—the cautionary tale of hubris and overreach.
- Represented in ancient texts as a figure of unparalleled technical skill, often invoked as the patron of craftsmen and inventors.
- His name appears inscribed on archaeological finds from Bronze Age Crete, suggesting a historical memory layered beneath the myth.

## connections

- [[theseus]] — the hero who used Daedalus's Labyrinth design (via Ariadne's thread) to slay the Minotaur.
- [[minotaur]] — the creature Daedalus imprisoned in his greatest work, the Labyrinth.
- [[minos]] — the Cretan king who both commissioned Daedalus and imprisoned him on the island.
- [[knossos]] — the historical palace site traditionally identified with Minos's court and the Labyrinth's location.
- [[icarus]] — his son, famous for his fatal flight when wax wings failed.
- [[ariadne]] — princess who helped Theseus escape by rewarding Daedalus's design secrets.
- [[crete]] — the island where Daedalus spent much of his life and created his masterworks.
