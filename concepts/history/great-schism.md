## summary

The **Great Schism**, also known as the *East-West Schism*, was the formal rupture in 1054 between the Western Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church. Centered in *Rome* and *Constantinople*, the split arose from centuries of theological divergence, ecclesiastical rivalry, and political friction between Western Europe and the *Byzantine Empire*. This division permanently altered medieval geopolitics, shaping the distinct spiritual and cultural trajectories of Western Christendom and Eastern Orthodox civilization.

## you gotta know

- Precipitated in 1054 when papal legate *Cardinal Humbert of Silva Candida* placed a papal bull of excommunication against Ecumenical Patriarch *Michael Cerularius* on the high altar of the *Hagia Sophia*, after which *Cerularius* responded by excommunicating the papal legates.
- Centers on theological controversies, primarily the Western insertion of the Latin term *filioque* ("and the Son") into the *Nicene Creed*, which Eastern theologians rejected as unauthorized and doctrinally erroneous regarding the procession of the Holy Spirit.
- Involved disputes over ritual practices and ecclesiastical customs, such as the Western use of unleavened bread (*azymes*) for the Eucharist, clerical celibacy in the West, and differences regarding fasting rules.
- Driven by the fundamental conflict over papal supremacy, where the Bishop of *Rome* claimed universal jurisdiction as the successor of Saint Peter, while Eastern patriarchs favored a conciliar pentarchy where *Rome* held only a primacy of honor.
- Irrevocably solidified in 1204 during the Fourth Crusade, when Western crusaders brutally sacked the Byzantine capital of *Constantinople* and established a Latin Empire.
- Mutual excommunications remained officially in place until 1965, when *Pope Paul VI* and Patriarch *Athenagoras I* formally rescinded them during a historic joint declaration in Jerusalem.

## connections
