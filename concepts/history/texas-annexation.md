## summary

The **Texas Annexation** was the 1845 incorporation of the *Republic of Texas* into the *[[united-states]]* as the twenty-eighth state. Driven forward by expansionist fervor and the ideology of *Manifest Destiny*, the acquisition was orchestrated through a contentious joint congressional resolution signed by outgoing President *John Tyler* and finalized under President *James K. Polk*. By extending American borders across the Southwest, the annexation intensified acute sectional conflicts over the westward spread of chattel slavery and directly triggered the *Mexican-American War*, irrevocably paving the path toward the *[[american-civil-war]]*.

## you gotta know

- *James K. Polk* made the acquisition of Texas a cornerstone of his victorious 1844 presidential campaign, effectively framing continental expansion to the Pacific as the nation's inevitable *Manifest Destiny* and defeating his rival *Henry Clay* of the *[[whig-party]]*.
- Outgoing President *John Tyler* pushed the measure through Congress in March 1845 via an unprecedented joint resolution, a tactical maneuver requiring only simple majorities in both chambers that bypassed the strict two-thirds Senate threshold previously required for treaties.
- The annexation directly sparked the *Mexican-American War* in 1846 following armed skirmishes along the disputed boundary between the *Nueces River*, recognized by Mexico as the historical border, and the *Rio Grande*, claimed by Texas and Washington.
- Northern abolitionists, Free Soilers, and conscience politicians such as *John Quincy Adams* vehemently denounced the annexation, characterizing it as an aggressive plot by the southern *Slave Power* to acquire fresh agricultural territory and entrench legislative dominance for slaveholding interests.
- The *Republic of Texas*, under revolutionary leader and president *Sam Houston*, had overwhelmingly voted in favor of American annexation immediately after securing independence from Mexico in 1836, yet presidents *Andrew Jackson* and *Martin Van Buren* initially rebuffed the overture to avoid inflaming domestic sectional divisions.
- Intense diplomatic activity by *England* and *[[france]]*, who sought to sponsor an independent, abolitionist Texas to serve as a commercial barrier against American growth and an alternate cotton supplier, spurred American politicians to accelerate annexation before European influence took hold.
- Under the exceptional terms of the admission agreement approved in December 1845, Texas was permitted to retain all unappropriated public lands within its borders to pay off its substantial republic-era sovereign debts, along with an explicit provision allowing it to subdivide into as many as five separate states.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the federal republic that formally admitted Texas as its twenty-eighth state in December 1845 under President *James K. Polk*.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the devastating sectional conflict sparked in large measure by subsequent crises over the status of slavery in lands gained through annexation.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the governing charter whose treaty-making clauses were sidestepped when Congress relied on a simple-majority joint resolution instead.
- [[whig-party]] — the major opposition party that mounted vigorous moral and constitutional resistance against annexation to prevent territorial aggression and slavery expansion.
- [[american-politics]] — the national political arena fundamentally reordered by expansionist mandates, the 1844 election, and rising regional polarization.
- [[colonialism]] — the overarching historical current of territorial conquest, settler migration, and displacement of indigenous and Mexican populations across the continent.
- [[spain]] — the European colonial power that exercised imperial dominion over the region before Mexican independence in 1821.
