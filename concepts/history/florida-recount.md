## summary

The **Florida Recount** of 2000 was a highly contested effort to recount votes in Florida following the 2000 presidential election, where the state's 25 electoral votes determined the presidency. Disputes over ballot design, counting methods, and legal authority led to Supreme Court intervention and gave the presidency to *George W. Bush* over *Al Gore*, reshaping American politics and constitutional law.

## you gotta know

- Florida's election results were extremely close, with *Bush* initially ahead by approximately 537 votes out of six million cast, making Florida's 25 electoral votes decisive.
- Many Florida ballots featured confusing "butterfly" ballot designs that led voters to punch the wrong hole for their intended candidate; others had chads only partially detached (*hanging chads*), raising interpretation questions.
- Palm Beach County's design was particularly problematic, with *Al Gore* appearing on the left but his punch position being in the center, causing many *Gore* voters to accidentally vote for *Pat Buchanan*.
- Recounts in select counties revealed that standards for what constituted a valid vote varied dramatically, with different officials interpreting "voter intent" in fundamentally different ways across the state.
- The *Gore* campaign requested hand recounts in four strategically chosen Democratic counties, while *Bush*'s campaign fought to limit recounts and certify results quickly before judges could intervene.
- *Katherine Harris*, Florida's Secretary of State and a *Bush* supporter, enforced a strict deadline for certification on November 14, 2000, before manual recounts could proceed in many counties.
- *Bush v. Gore* reached the *Supreme Court*, which halted the Florida recount on December 9, 2000, in a deeply divided 5-4 decision, effectively awarding the presidency to *Bush*.
- The Court's reasoning was that continuing recounts would violate equal protection (inconsistent standards) and that *Bush* would be injured by the recount process itself.
- The decision proved deeply controversial, with critics arguing the Court had abandoned judicial restraint and compromised its impartiality by inserting itself into political disputes.
