## summary

**Braxton Bragg** (1817–1876) was a Confederate general during the American Civil War, noted for tactical competence but crippled by poor judgment, excessive caution, and a volatile personality that alienated subordinates and superiors alike. His command of the Army of Tennessee saw mixed success in crucial battles across the western theater, and he remains controversial among historians—sometimes praised for fighting well against odds, more often critiqued for squandering opportunities and failing to capitalize on victories.

## you gotta know

- A regular Army officer who graduated from West Point (1837) and served with distinction in the Mexican-American War, earning a reputation as a skilled artillery commander.
- Transferred to Confederate service in 1861 and commanded forces at Fort Sumter, then rose rapidly through ranks despite limited political connections; his professionalism initially impressed President Davis.
- Assumed command of the Army of Tennessee after Albert Sidney Johnston's death at Shiloh (April 1862), leading the army for two crucial years across the western theater.
- Won the Battle of Chickamauga (September 1863) through superior numbers and tactical coordination, but failed to press his advantage or pursue the defeated Union army effectively.
- Repeatedly clashed with his generals—*James Longstreet*, *Leonidas Polk*, and others—over tactics and resources; his contentious nature eroded morale and cohesion in his command.
- After his removal from field command in 1864, served as military advisor to President Davis; died in poverty in 1876, largely forgotten despite his significant role in the war.
