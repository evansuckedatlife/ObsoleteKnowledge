## summary

The **Aztec Empire** was a sophisticated Mesoamerican civilization centered in what is now *Mexico*, flourishing from roughly 1300 to 1521 CE. Built on an island in Lake Texcoco, the capital *Tenochtitlan* became one of the world's largest and most remarkable cities, featuring advanced agriculture, engineering, and astronomical knowledge. Conquered by Spanish forces under *Hernán Cortés*, the Aztec Empire's fall marked one of history's most consequential collisions between Old World and New World civilizations, reshaping the Americas forever.

## you gotta know

- The Aztecs were the *Mexica*, who migrated to the Valley of Mexico and established Tenochtitlan on an island, developing a vast empire through military conquest and tributary networks.
- Tenochtitlan's population exceeded 200,000 by the early 1500s, rivaling the largest European cities and featuring sophisticated infrastructure including canals, aqueducts, and chinampas (floating gardens).
- Aztec society was highly stratified: emperors (*Huey Tlatoani*), nobles, warriors, merchants, commoners, and slaves; human sacrifice was central to their cosmology and religious practice.
- *Montezuma II* was the emperor when *Cortés* arrived in 1519; his initial indecision and diplomatic approach gave the Spanish crucial time to consolidate alliances with indigenous rivals.
- Spanish conquest exploited internal divisions, political rivalries, and epidemic disease; Cortés had fewer than 600 soldiers but recruited thousands of indigenous allies resentful of Aztec tributary demands.
- Smallpox, introduced by Spanish forces, devastated the Aztec population with no immunity to the pathogen, turning it into an ally more powerful than Spanish weapons.
- The fall of Tenochtitlan in 1521 ended Aztec sovereignty and initiated Spanish colonization of Mexico, fundamentally altering the hemisphere's political and cultural landscape.
- Aztec knowledge, art, agriculture, and cultural practices were suppressed under colonial rule but persisted in hybrid forms and influenced modern Mexican identity.
