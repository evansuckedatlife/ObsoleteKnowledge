## summary

**Louis XV** (r. 1715–1774) was a French king whose long reign began with prosperity and military dominance but ended in financial crisis and declining power. Initially beloved by the French people as "the Well-Beloved," Louis XV gradually squandered his inheritance through costly wars, court extravagance, and inability to reform an ossified absolutist system. His reign witnessed the erosion of French power relative to rival European states and left the royal treasury bankrupt—a condition his grandson *Louis XVI* inherited and failed to remedy, contributing directly to the French Revolution.

## you gotta know

- Ascended at age five following the death of his great-grandfather *Louis XIV*; governed through regency until reaching maturity.
- Initially presided over a period of peace and relative prosperity, earning the epithet "the Well-Beloved" (*le Bien-Aimé*) from his grateful subjects.
- Became infatuated with the ambitious courtier *Madame de Pompadour*, whose influence over royal decisions for over two decades scandalized France and distracted the king from governance.
- Fought costly wars including the War of Austrian Succession and the Seven Years' War, both of which depleted the royal treasury and yielded little territorial gain for France.
- Failed to implement structural reforms to the French tax system, maintaining an archaic system that protected nobility and clergy while crushing the common people.
- Left the royal finances in catastrophic disorder; the debt crisis and fiscal paralysis he passed to his successor made comprehensive reform impossible and contributed to revolutionary ferment.
