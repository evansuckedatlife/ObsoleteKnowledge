# Harold Godwinson

## summary

**Harold Godwinson** (c. 1022–1066) was the last Anglo-Saxon king of England, ruling for only nine months before his death at the *Battle of Hastings* in 1066. His defeat by *William the Conqueror* and the Norman invasion marked the end of Anglo-Saxon rule and initiated the profound cultural and political transformation that shaped medieval and modern England.

## you gotta know

- Harold was born into the powerful Godwin family, the dominant English aristocracy under *Edward the Confessor*; he rose to become Earl of Wessex and the most influential magnate in the kingdom.
- On his deathbed in January 1066, King *Edward the Confessor* named Harold as his successor (though the exact circumstances and his own role in this decision were disputed); Harold was crowned king and claimed legitimate succession to the English throne.
- Multiple claimants contested this succession: *William, Duke of Normandy* claimed Edward had once promised him the throne; *Harald Hardrada*, the Norwegian king, also pressed claims; Harold faced simultaneous threats from three directions.
- Harold won a decisive victory over *Harald Hardrada* and the Earls of Mercia and Northumbria at *Stamford Bridge* (September 25, 1066), reportedly killing the Norwegian king; this triumph exhausted Harold's army just as William's invasion fleet landed in Sussex.
- At the *Battle of Hastings* (October 14, 1066), Harold's forces faced Norman knights and archers; Harold was killed in battle (legend says by an arrow through his eye), his army was routed, and the Norman conquest of England began.
- Harold's death and defeat severed Anglo-Saxon royal lineage; the Norman dynasty under William established a new nobility, feudal system, and cultural synthesis of Norman-French and Anglo-Saxon elements that defined England's future.
