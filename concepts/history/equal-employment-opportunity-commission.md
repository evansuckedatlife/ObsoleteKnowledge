## summary

The **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission** (*EEOC*) is a federal agency of the *[[united-states]]* charged with enforcing civil rights laws against workplace discrimination. Created under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and opening its doors in 1965, the agency investigates claims of bias based on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, disability, and age. Its landmark conciliations, federal enforcement lawsuits, and regulatory guidance have shaped American labor policy and corporate employment standards for decades.

## you gotta know

- Established under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, signed into law by *Lyndon B. Johnson* following pressure from the broader *[[civil-rights-movement]]*.
- Opened operational headquarters in 1965 under its first chairman, *Franklin D. Roosevelt Jr.*
- Investigates formal charges filed by workers, issues "right-to-sue" letters when mediation fails, and litigates high-impact employment discrimination lawsuits in federal court.
- Enforces major workplace statutes beyond Title VII, including the Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967, the Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990, and the Equal Pay Act of 1963.
- Its initial reluctance to vigorously enforce Title VII's ban on sex discrimination led activists like *Betty Friedan* to establish the National Organization for Women in 1966 to lobby for systemic remedies like the *[[equal-rights-amendment]]*.
- Secured major milestone settlements in corporate America, including the landmark 1973 consent decree against AT&T that provided millions in back pay and opened craft roles to women and minorities.
- Led in subsequent decades by notable chairs including *Eleanor Holmes Norton*, who issued historic guidelines recognizing sexual harassment as unlawful sex discrimination in 1980, and future Supreme Court Justice *Clarence Thomas*.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the historic mass struggle that forced passage of Title VII and created the agency.
- [[united-states]] — the federal government whose executive branch houses this independent regulatory commission.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional guarantee of equal protection that underpins federal anti-discrimination statutes.
- [[equal-rights-amendment]] — constitutional push heavily driven by activists dissatisfied with early agency inaction on sex bias.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — civil rights leader whose campaigns for racial and economic justice catalyzed federal employment protections.
