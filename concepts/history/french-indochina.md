## summary

**French Indochina** was a grouping of French colonial territories in Southeast Asia established in 1887, ultimately comprising the protectorates of Annam, Tonkin, Cambodia, and Laos, alongside the direct colony of Cochinchina. Administered under a centralized governor-general in Hanoi and Saigon, the federation exploited local labor and agricultural wealth through state monopolies on salt, opium, and alcohol. Its occupation by *Japan* during *World War II* fatally eroded European prestige, culminating in the First Indochina War, the decisive French defeat at Dien Bien Phu, and the emergence of independent nation-states shaped by the global *Cold War*.

## you gotta know

- The federation collapsed militarily following the 1954 Siege of Dien Bien Phu, where Viet Minh forces under General *Vo Nguyen Giap* surrounded and overran the French garrison, forcing the Geneva Accords to partition Vietnam along the 17th parallel.
- Formally constituted as the **Indochinese Union** in 1887 from Cochinchina, Annam, Tonkin, and Cambodia, the colony later incorporated Laos following the 1893 Franco-Siamese crisis and annexed the leased Chinese port of Guangzhouwan in 1898.
- Governor-General *Paul Doumer* reorganized the administration in the late 1890s, imposing brutal taxation policies and establishing state-controlled monopolies over alcohol, salt, and opium to make the colony financially self-sufficient.
- Rubber became a primary cash crop, with vast concessions granted to private conglomerates like *Michelin*, whose brutal working conditions on plantations fueled widespread communist recruitment and peasant revolts.
- During *World War II*, the collaborationist Vichy administration permitted imperial *Japan* to station troops and use airbases throughout the territory, maintaining nominal French administrative control until the Japanese staged a violent coup de force in March 1945.
- The power vacuum caused by the Japanese surrender in August 1945 triggered the August Revolution, during which *Ho Chi Minh* proclaimed the independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in Hanoi.
- Gunboat diplomacy during the 1893 Franco-Siamese War saw French warships ascend the Chao Phraya River to Bangkok, compelling Siam's *Chakri Dynasty* to cede all Lao territories east of the Mekong River to preserve Siamese independence.
- Early resistance culminated in events such as the 1930 Yen Bai mutiny organized by the nationalist *VNQDD* and the simultaneous Nghe-Tinh soviets, both crushed through aerial bombardment and executions by colonial authorities.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — stands as a primary example of late nineteenth-century European imperial exploitation and resource extraction in Asia.
- [[france]] — the imperial metropolis that colonized the region and fought an exhaustive eight-year war to retain it after 1945.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical standoff that turned post-colonial Vietnam into a primary ideological battleground between superpowers.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the conflict whose Pacific theater saw Japanese occupation shatter the myth of European colonial invulnerability.
- [[japan]] — the Axis power that dominated the territory militarily during the Pacific War and dismantled French rule in 1945.
- [[china]] — the northern neighbor whose border regions provided sanctuaries, training, and heavy artillery to anticolonial Viet Minh forces.
- [[buddhism]] — the predominant cultural and religious tradition across Cambodia, Laos, and Vietnam that often clashed with colonial policies.
- [[french-resistance]] — metropolitan resistance networks whose leaders, including *Charles de Gaulle*, sought to reassert French sovereignty across Indochina after liberation.
