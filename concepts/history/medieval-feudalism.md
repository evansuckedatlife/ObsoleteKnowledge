## summary

**Feudalism** was the dominant social, economic, and political system in [[medieval-europe]], particularly in Western Europe from the 9th to the 15th centuries. It was built on a hierarchical network of mutual obligations between lords and vassals, where land ownership and military service formed the foundation of power and wealth. Though often romanticized as a chivalric order, feudalism was fundamentally a practical response to the fragmentation of central authority following the fall of the [[roman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Emerged gradually during the early medieval period as local strongmen offered protection to peasants in exchange for labor and loyalty when centralized authority collapsed.
- Organized society into a pyramid: the king at the top, followed by great nobles, lesser knights, and peasants (often unfree serfs bound to the land).
- Based on the exchange of land (called a fief) for military service and loyalty; a vassal swore homage to his lord and was bound by sacred oath to obey.
- Fractured political power into thousands of small territories, each controlled by a feudal lord answerable only to his superior in the hierarchy.
- Began to decline after the [[black-death]] dramatically reduced the peasant population, giving surviving serfs increased bargaining power and mobility.
- Was gradually replaced by centralized national monarchies, professional armies, and commercial economies during the transition to the [[renaissance]].
- The feudal contract was theoretically reciprocal—vassals owed service, but lords owed protection and fair governance, though this balance shifted with power dynamics.
- Tied land ownership inextricably to military obligation and noble status, making landholding the primary source of both wealth and political authority.
- Created a complex web of overlapping feudal relationships where a single lord could be vassal to multiple superiors, sometimes leading to conflicts of loyalty and civil wars.

## connections

- [[medieval-europe]] — the broader historical period of which feudalism was the defining characteristic.
- [[magna-carta]] — a document that constrained feudal royal authority and established accountability to nobles and common law.
- [[black-death]] — triggered the social breakdown that weakened feudal bonds and hastened the system's decline.
- [[chivalry]] — the ideological code that gave feudalism its moral legitimacy among the warrior nobility.
- [[england]] — where feudalism took root after the [[norman-conquest]] and shaped English political development.
- [[france]] — another stronghold of feudal organization and the birthplace of chivalric culture.
