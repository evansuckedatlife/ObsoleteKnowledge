## summary

**Mississippi** is a state in the Deep South whose history epitomizes the American struggle with slavery, racial injustice, and gradual democratization. As one of the most economically dependent on enslaved labor, Mississippi experienced the traumatic Reconstruction period and led resistance to civil rights, making it a crucial battleground in American racial history.

## you gotta know

- Admitted to the Union in 1817, Mississippi became one of the wealthiest states through its massive plantation economy based entirely on enslaved African American labor.
- Held a larger percentage of enslaved people than any other state, with plantations dominating the political economy and culture by the antebellum era.
- Seceded in January 1861 to form the Confederate States, with slavery and white supremacy as its stated justification, making it central to the Civil War's cause.
- During *Reconstruction* (1865–1877), Mississippi briefly experienced Black political participation, with *Hiram Revels* and *Blanche Bruce* elected as U.S. Senators.
- The *Compromise of 1877* and the subsequent withdrawal of federal troops enabled a reactionary Democratic takeover and the restoration of white political hegemony.
- Implemented aggressive *Jim Crow* laws more thoroughly than any state, creating an exceptionally violent system of racial segregation and disenfranchisement lasting into the 1960s.
- Became the focal point of the Civil Rights Movement in the 1960s, with figures like *Medgar Evers* murdered by white segregationists and the *Freedom Rides* facing fierce local resistance.
- The state's brutal resistance to desegregation and voting rights, exemplified by the 1964 murder of civil rights workers (Chaney, Goodman, and Schwerner), exposed the violent depths of American racism.
