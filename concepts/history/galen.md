## summary

**Galen** of Pergamon was a towering Greek physician, surgeon, and philosopher in the [[roman-empire]] whose extensive medical treatises formed the unquestioned foundation of Western and Islamic medicine for over a millennium. Serving as court physician to emperors including *Marcus Aurelius*, he synthesized the clinical philosophy of *Hippocrates* with extensive animal dissection and experimental physiology. His anatomical doctrines and humoral theories shaped clinical practice until revolutionary critiques arose during the European [[renaissance]].

## you gotta know

- Born in Pergamon in Asia Minor, he received an extensive education in philosophy and rhetoric before studying medicine in major intellectual centers such as Alexandria.
- Gained early surgical fame in Pergamon by serving as chief physician to the gladiators, pioneering treatments for trauma, severed tendons, and abdominal wounds with remarkably low mortality rates.
- Relocated to [[rome]], where he rose to prominence as imperial physician to Emperor *Marcus Aurelius* and his son *Commodus*, solidifying his medical authority across the [[roman-empire]].
- Witnessed, survived, and extensively documented the *Antonine Plague* (likely smallpox), which ravaged the Roman army and civilian populations across Europe and the [[middle-east]].
- Elaborated the doctrine of the four bodily humors—blood, yellow bile, black bile, and phlegm—pairing them with specific temperaments and elements to explain human illness and psychological disposition.
- Conducted daring vivisections on animals, famously severing the recurrent laryngeal nerve of a squealing pig to demonstrate that voice originates in the brain rather than the heart.
- Authored monumental texts including *Method of Medicine* and *On the Usefulness of the Parts of the Body*, which survived through Syriac and Arabic translations before re-emerging in Medieval and [[renaissance]] Europe.
- Held a teleological view that every bodily organ was designed by a divine Creator for a specific purpose, which later endeared his treatises to scholars of both [[christianity]] and [[islam]].

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial civilization where he served as imperial court physician and conducted public anatomical demonstrations.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the intellectual and philosophical tradition within which he was trained, writing his medical treatises in Attic Greek.
- [[rome]] — the imperial capital where he achieved philosophical renown, debated rivals, and treated high-ranking patricians.
- [[renaissance]] — the era when modern anatomists such as *Andreas Vesalius* challenged his human anatomical assertions by dissecting actual cadavers.
- [[christianity]] — the faith whose medieval scholars preserved and venerated his anatomical teleology and philosophical doctrines.
- [[islam]] — the civilization whose Golden Age translators, notably *Hunayn ibn Ishaq*, preserved his medical corpus and disseminated it to the wider world.
- [[middle-east]] — the region where his writings were meticulously copied, commented upon, and integrated into early hospital systems.
