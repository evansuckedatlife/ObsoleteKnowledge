## summary

The **Hanging Gardens of Babylon** were one of the traditional Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, celebrated as an extraordinary marvel of terraced engineering, exotic horticulture, and irrigation in Mesopotamia. Classical Greek and Roman historians traditionally attributed their construction to King *Nebuchadnezzar II* around 600 BCE to comfort his Median wife *Amytis*, who missed the lush mountain landscapes of her homeland. Because no unambiguous archaeological traces or contemporary Babylonian cuneiform records explicitly mention them, their exact physical location and historical reality remain among the greatest mysteries of antiquity.

## you gotta know

- Ranked alongside iconic monuments such as the Colossus of Rhodes and the Great Pyramid of Giza as one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.
- Classical accounts state that King *Nebuchadnezzar II* erected the gardens for Queen *Amytis of Media*, who longed for the forested hills and green valleys of northwestern Iran.
- Described by Hellenistic and Roman chroniclers such as *Strabo*, *Diodorus Siculus*, and *Philo of Byzantium* as a stepped series of vaulted stone terraces planted with mature trees, fragrant shrubs, and cascading flora.
- Relied on sophisticated water-lifting mechanisms, widely conjectured to be early variants of the Archimedes screw or mechanical chain pumps, drawing directly from the nearby *Euphrates River*.
- Modern archaeological excavations by *Robert Koldewey* at the site of ancient Babylon uncovered massive vaulted foundations, yet failed to yield definitive epigraphic proof confirming the gardens.
- Assyriologist *Stephanie Dalley* proposed the prominent alternative theory that the legendary wonder was actually built at Nineveh by the Neo-Assyrian king *Sennacherib*, confusing Babylon with the Assyrian capital.
- The term "hanging" is derived from the Greek word *kremastos* or Latin *pensilis*, referring to plants suspended over elevated stone cantilevers and balconies rather than hanging by ropes.
