## summary

The **Wars of the Roses** (or **War of the Roses**) were a series of dynastic civil wars fought between 1455 and 1487 for control of the throne of [[england]]. The conflict pitted two rival cadet branches of the royal *House of Plantagenet* against each other: the *House of Lancaster*, symbolized by the red rose, and the *House of York*, symbolized by the white rose. Decades of factional warfare destroyed the feudal nobility, extinguished the Plantagenet male line, and established the Tudor dynasty under *Henry VII*, inaugurating the English [[renaissance]].

## you gotta know

- Fought between the rival cadet branches of the royal *House of Plantagenet*—the *House of York* and the *House of Lancaster*, symbolized by white and red roses—ending when *Henry Tudor* married *Elizabeth of York* to unite their emblems into the Tudor rose.
- The decisive climax arrived at the 1485 *Battle of Bosworth Field*, where the last Plantagenet king, *Richard III*, died fighting hand-to-hand after the battlefield betrayal of the *Stanley* family, enabling the coronation of *Henry VII*.
- The mysterious disappearance of the young *Princes in the Tower*—King *Edward V* and his brother *Richard, Duke of York*—fueled accusations that their uncle murdered them to usurp the crown as *Richard III*.
- The powerful nobleman *Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick*, earned the title *the Kingmaker* by using his private armies to depose the Lancastrian *Henry VI* for *Edward IV*, before defecting to briefly restore *Henry VI*.
- Sparked by England's humiliating defeat in [[france]] at the end of the Hundred Years' War, combined with the catastrophic mental breakdowns and weak governance of the pious Lancastrian king *Henry VI*.
- The 1461 *Battle of Towton*, fought during a Palm Sunday snowstorm, stands as the bloodiest battle ever waged on English soil, causing an estimated twenty-eight thousand deaths and securing the crown for *Edward IV*.
- Hostilities opened in 1455 at the *First Battle of St Albans*, where *Richard, Duke of York*, attacked the royal forces, wounded *Henry VI*, and killed his chief rival, *Edmund Beaufort, Duke of Somerset*.
- The fighting officially ended in 1487 at the *Battle of Stoke Field*, where *Henry VII* crushed a Yorkist rebel army fighting on behalf of the pretender *Lambert Simnel*.

## connections

- [[england]] — the realm whose medieval feudal nobility was decimated by three decades of dynastic civil war.
- [[france]] — the rival nation whose victory in the Hundred Years' War caused financial ruin that ignited English unrest.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the dramatist whose history plays chronicling *Henry VI* and *Richard III* created the enduring popular mythos.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural flowering that took root under the centralized Tudor peace established after the conflict.
- [[british-empire]] — the global power made possible by the strong administrative monarchy that replaced fractured feudal governance.
- [[american-civil-war]] — a later civil conflict between regional factions that fundamentally redefined national sovereignty.
- [[french-revolution]] — a subsequent revolution that similarly dismantled the political dominance and hereditary privileges of a feudal aristocracy.
