## summary

**Edmund Burke** (1729–1797) was an Irish-born British politician, philosopher, and orator whose critique of the *French Revolution* shaped modern conservatism. A Whig reformer in his earlier career, Burke became the voice of conservative reaction against revolutionary radicalism, arguing that society's ancient institutions embodied hard-won wisdom. His *Reflections on the Revolution in France* became the defining conservative manifesto.

## you gotta know

- Served in Parliament for nearly three decades and was known for eloquent speeches defending Irish Catholics and American colonists against imperial overreach.
- Initially sympathetic to reform and democratic principles, but recoiled from the revolutionary violence and ideological extremism of 1789 onwards.
- Published *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790), a withering critique of revolutionary rationalism and a defense of traditional hierarchy and gradual reform.
- Argued that society is an inherited trust passed from past generations through the present to future generations—not a blank slate for philosophical refashioning.
- Asserted that the Revolution's attempt to reconstruct society from rational first principles was hubris; traditions embody the accumulated experience of centuries.
- Warned that revolutionary ideals, unchecked by prudence and reverence for the past, would lead to terror—a prediction vindicated by the *Reign of Terror*.
- Became the intellectual founder of modern conservatism; his arguments influenced conservative thought across Europe and America for two centuries.
- Championed the rights of oppressed groups (Irish Catholics, American colonists) but opposed revolutionary overthrow of established institutions, creating a paradox in his political thought.
- His famous phrase "the social contract" emphasized stability, continuity, and prudent reform over radical rupture with the past.
- *Mary Wollstonecraft's* *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman* (1792) was written in direct refutation of Burke's *Reflections*, making Burke's work a foundational text for modern feminist theory as well as conservatism.
