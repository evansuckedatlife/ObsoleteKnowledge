## summary

The **Alien and Sedition Acts** were a series of restrictive laws passed by a *Federalist*-controlled Congress in 1798 during the quasi-war with France, intended to suppress political opposition and immigrant influence. The Sedition Act criminalized criticism of the federal government and made it illegal to publish "false, scandalous, and malicious" statements. Though brief in duration, these acts became a lightning rod for constitutional controversy, violating principles of free speech and galvanizing opposition that contributed to the *election of 1800*.

## you gotta know

- Passed as four separate acts: the Alien Act, Alien Enemies Act, Naturalization Act, and Sedition Act, all enacted in summer 1798.
- The Sedition Act made it a crime to "write, print, utter or publish" any "false, scandalous, and malicious writing" against the government, Congress, or the president.
- About 25 people were prosecuted under the Sedition Act; some were jailed, and the law enabled suppression of *Democratic-Republican* newspapers and speech.
- Newspapers were the primary targets; editors critical of *John Adams* and *Federalist* policies faced prosecution and imprisonment.
- The acts expired in 1801 after *Thomas Jefferson*'s election; Jefferson later pardoned those convicted and Congress refunded their fines.
- They sparked state responses, particularly the *Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions*, which asserted state sovereignty against federal overreach.
- Constitutional scholars view them as a clear violation of the *First Amendment*, setting a precedent for later free-speech jurisprudence.
