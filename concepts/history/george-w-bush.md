# George W. Bush

## summary

**George W. Bush** was the 43rd President of the United States (2001–2009), whose presidency was defined by the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001, and the subsequent "War on Terror." His election in 2000 followed a controversial Supreme Court decision that effectively decided the Florida recount dispute. Bush's administration prosecuted wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, reshaped American national security strategy around preemption and unilateral action, and expanded presidential executive powers, leaving a contested legacy that remains central to debates about American military interventionism and constitutional governance.

## you gotta know

- Bush's election in 2000 was decided by *Bush v. Gore* (2000), a Supreme Court decision that halted Florida's recount and awarded the presidency to Bush despite losing the popular vote to *Al Gore*; this decision remains one of the most controversial Supreme Court interventions in American politics.
- The *terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001*, fundamentally transformed Bush's presidency; the attacks killed nearly 3,000 people and prompted Bush to declare a global "War on Terror" reshaping American foreign and domestic policy.
- The *invasion of Afghanistan* (2001) was the first military response to 9/11, targeting *Al-Qaeda* and the *Taliban* regime harboring them; it became the longest American war and remained unresolved when Bush left office.
- The *invasion of Iraq* (2003), launched on claims of weapons of mass destruction that were later unverified, became deeply unpopular and intensified Middle Eastern anti-American sentiment; it was a hallmark of Bush's doctrine of preemptive warfare.
- Bush's foreign policy emphasized *neoconservatism*: unilateral American action, skepticism of international institutions like the *United Nations*, and the belief that U.S. military power could reshape the Middle East; this doctrine faced growing criticism as both wars stalled.
- Domestically, Bush faced the *Great Recession* (2008), the worst economic crisis since the *Great Depression*; his administration approved massive financial bailouts that proved politically controversial and reshaped the regulatory landscape.
- Bush championed conservative social policies including restrictions on abortion funding and same-sex marriage, and appointed two conservative Supreme Court justices (*John Roberts* and *Samuel Alito*).
- The expansion of executive power under Bush—including warrantless wiretapping, enhanced interrogation techniques, and indefinite detention—prompted fierce constitutional debate and contributed to post-presidency scrutiny of presidential authority.
