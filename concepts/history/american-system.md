## summary

The **American System** was an integrated economic policy championed by *Henry Clay* in the early 19th century, combining protective tariffs, federal investment in infrastructure, and a national bank. It represented an ambitious vision for transforming the young United States into a self-sufficient industrial nation, contrasting sharply with the agrarian Free Trade philosophy of the South.

## you gotta know

- Promoted by *Henry Clay* during his time as *Speaker of the House* and presidential candidate, most notably in the 1820s-1830s.
- Central planks: high protective tariffs (esp. the Tariff of 1828, colloquially the "Tariff of Abominations"), federal funding for roads and canals, and a strong national banking system (*Second Bank of the United States*).
- Designed to create domestic manufacturing independence and compete with British industrial dominance by sheltering American infant industries.
- Opposed fiercely by the *American South*, which saw tariffs as enriching Northern manufacturers at the expense of their agricultural exports and forced them to buy expensive domestic goods.
- The tariffs sparked the *Nullification Crisis* (1832), when South Carolina challenged federal tariff authority, nearly leading to secession.
- Became the platform of the *Whig Party* and later the *Republican Party* under *Lincoln*, who supported protectionist tariffs during the Civil War era.
- Federal investment component included plans for a "national road" (Cumberland Road) and canal systems to bind the nation economically.
- Presaged later 20th-century American protectionist and interventionist economic models, influencing debates on free trade vs. protectionism into modern times.
- Contrasted sharply with Southern *David Ricardo* free-trade economics and Northern industrial ambitions, a fundamental split in American economic ideology that persisted into the 20th century.
- Opposition unified diverse Northern interests: manufacturers seeking tariff protection, workers fearing foreign competition, and infrastructure investors seeking federal support for internal improvements.
- Southern opposition was equally unified: planters dependent on selling cotton and tobacco abroad feared foreign tariff retaliation; they preferred low tariffs to maximize global trade.
- The system's failure to prevent the *Civil War* demonstrated that economic integration alone could not resolve the slavery question, the core conflict between North and South.
