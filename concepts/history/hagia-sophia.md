## summary

**Hagia Sophia**, historically known as the **Church of the Holy Wisdom** and later designated as **Ayasofya**, is a monumental sixth-century structure in *Istanbul* that represents the supreme architectural achievement of the [[byzantine-empire]]. Commissioned by the emperor *Justinian I* following the destruction of an earlier basilica during the 532 CE *Nika riots*, the cathedral was engineered by the physicist *Anthemius of Tralles* and the mathematician *Isidore of Miletus*. For nearly a millennium it stood as the grandest cathedral in Christendom and the spiritual heart of the [[eastern-orthodox-church]], before serving as a premier imperial mosque under the [[ottoman-empire]] and later as a world-renowned museum.

## you gotta know

- Built between 532 and 537 CE under *Justinian I*, the monumental church was designed with a groundbreaking forty-windowed pendentive dome that contemporary court historian *Procopius* famously described as looking as though it were suspended by a golden chain from heaven.
- Converted into an imperial mosque immediately following the 1453 [[fall-of-constantinople]] by Sultan *Mehmed II*, who added its first minaret, preserved its colossal marble columns, and initiated the plastering over of its figurative Byzantine mosaics.
- Upon entering the completed sanctuary at its consecration on Christmas Day in 537 CE, *Justinian I* supposedly gazed upon its immense nave and cried out, *“Solomon, I have surpassed thee!”*
- Plundered and stripped of its golden relics during the Fourth Crusade in 1204, the cathedral was temporarily converted into a Latin Catholic seat until the Byzantine reconquest in 1261.
- The original shallow dome collapsed following devastating earthquakes in 558 CE and was redesigned with a higher curvature and forty exterior buttressed ribs by *Isidore the Younger*.
- Served as the coronation site for Byzantine monarchs, whose emperors were crowned atop the *Omphalion*, an intricate opus sectile floor pavement composed of circular porphyry, serpentine, and granite stones.
- Houses famed Byzantine mosaics including the thirteenth-century *Deësis*, depicting *Christ Pantocrator* flanked by the Virgin Mary and John the Baptist, as well as ninth-century runic graffiti etched into a southern balustrade by a member of the imperial *Varangian Guard* named *Halfdan*.

## connections

- [[justinian-i]] — Byzantine emperor who commissioned the structure in 532 CE to solidify imperial authority following the *Nika riots*.
- [[byzantine-empire]] — civilization whose religious, ceremonial, and architectural zenith was embodied by the cathedral for over nine centuries.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — Islamic power that conquered the city in 1453, repurposing the edifice as an imperial mosque and adding grand minarets designed by *Mimar Sinan*.
- [[fall-of-constantinople]] — catastrophic siege led by *Mehmed II* that ended Byzantine rule and led directly to the building's conversion into an Islamic worship space.
- [[christianity]] — faith for which the cathedral served as the theological and ceremonial focal point of the Eastern Orthodox tradition.
- [[islam]] — religion whose liturgical needs added mihrabs, minbars, and enormous calligraphic roundels bearing sacred Arabic names to the sanctuary.
- [[roman-empire]] — imperial state whose architectural legacy of monumental arches, vaults, and civic scale reached its ultimate culmination in the complex.
