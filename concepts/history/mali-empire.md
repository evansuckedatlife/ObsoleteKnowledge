## summary

The **Mali Empire** (historically known as the **Manden Kurufaba**) was a powerful medieval West African state founded around 1230 by the warrior-king *Sundiata Keita* that extended across the Western Sudan along the Niger River basin to the [[senegal]] River valley. Renowned for its vast wealth in gold and control of lucrative trans-Saharan trade routes, the empire transformed thriving urban centers such as *Timbuktu* and *Djenné* into leading hubs of commerce, Islamic architecture, and intellectual scholarship. At its peak under the fourteenth-century ruler *Mansa Musa*, the realm captured global renown across Europe and the [[middle-east]], establishing an enduring civilizational legacy that fundamentally shaped West African political structures, oral literature, and religious traditions.

## you gotta know

- *Mansa Musa*, famously recognized as one of history's wealthiest figures, embarked on an extravagant hajj to Mecca in 1324, dispensing vast quantities of gold that depressed bullion markets in Cairo and across the [[middle-east]] for over a decade.
- Founded by *Sundiata Keita*, the legendary "Lion King" who unified Mandinka clans and defeated the Sosso sorcerer-king *Soumaoro Kanté* at the Battle of Kirina in 1235, an achievement commemorated in the oral epic tradition of the *Epic of Sundiata*.
- Transformed the city of *Timbuktu* into an intellectual capital of Islamic scholarship, centering on the prestigious University of Sankore and monumental mud-brick structures like the Djinguereber Mosque, which attracted jurists, mathematicians, and astronomers from across the Islamic world.
- Monopolized the trans-Saharan trade routes across West Africa, accumulating vast state wealth through tariffs on rock salt transported from northern desert deposits and gold panned from rich southern alluvial fields at Bambuk and Bure.
- Codified constitutional governance through the *Kouroukan Fouga*, an oral charter established at Kurukan Fuga that structured the imperial federation, recognized hereditary clan lineages, regulated trade guilds, and established fundamental social and civic rights.
- Administered by monarchs bearing the Mandinka title *mansa*, who governed through provincial military governors called *farin*, coordinated royal councils, and deployed standing cavalry forces to safeguard vital merchant pathways across the savanna.
- Succumbed to gradual internal fragmentation and outside pressure after succession crises weakened central authority, suffering raids by Mossi and Tuareg forces before being eclipsed and conquered by the rising *Songhai Empire* in the late fifteenth century.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the adopted religion of the imperial court, inspiring its legal administration, scholarship, architecture, and international diplomatic prestige.
- [[middle-east]] — destination of *Mansa Musa*'s famous pilgrimage, binding West African commerce directly to Mediterranean, Egyptian, and Arabian markets.
- [[senegal]] — modern nation whose river valley and coastal territories formed the empire's western sphere of cultural and political hegemony.
- [[colonialism]] — the nineteenth-century European partition that dismantled the fragmented Mandinka successor states and disrupted traditional trans-Saharan commerce.
- [[france]] — the European imperial power that colonized the former imperial heartland as French Sudan, establishing colonial rule from Bamako.
- [[french-west-africa]] — colonial federation that consolidated the historic riverine and savanna territories formerly governed by medieval West African empires.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — European maritime expansion spurred partly by Portuguese ambitions to access West African gold markets directly by sea.
