## summary

The *Korean War* (1950–1953) was the first large-scale military confrontation of the *Cold War*, fought between the communist *North Korea* (backed by the *Soviet Union* and *China*) and the non-communist *South Korea* (backed by the *United States* and the *United Nations*). The conflict resulted in millions of casualties, the destruction of much of the Korean peninsula, and ended in an armistice that left the country divided along a demilitarized zone—a division that persists today.

## you gotta know

- *North Korea*, established by the Soviet Union as a satellite state after *World War II*, invaded the South on June 25, 1950; *President Harry Truman* committed American forces immediately, framing the conflict as a test of Cold War containment.
- *General Douglas MacArthur* led the initial American response with a brilliant amphibious landing at *Inchon* in September 1950, which nearly unified the peninsula under South Korean control before Chinese intervention reversed the war's trajectory.
- *China*, fearing American encroachment on its borders, entered the war in October 1950 with hundreds of thousands of troops, driving American and UN forces back to the 38th parallel and prolonging the conflict into a grueling war of attrition.
- MacArthur, commanding American forces, clashed with *President Truman* over strategy and publicly criticized the President's policies; Truman relieved MacArthur of command in April 1951, a dramatic civilian-military confrontation that shaped Cold War civil-military relations.
- The war killed approximately 2.5 million people: roughly 400,000 Chinese troops, 500,000 North Korean soldiers, 100,000 South Korean soldiers, and 37,000 American troops, making it one of the bloodiest conflicts of the era.
- The armistice of July 1953 ended active fighting but never formally concluded the war; North and South Korea remain technically at war, separated by the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), one of the world's most fortified borders.
