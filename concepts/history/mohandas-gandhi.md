## summary

**Mohandas Gandhi** (1869–1948), revered as *Mahatma* (the Great Soul), was the spiritual and political leader of India's independence movement against British colonial rule. His philosophy of *nonviolent resistance* (*satyagraha*) transformed anti-colonial struggle, inspired global civil rights movements, and established him as an icon of moral conscience in the twentieth century.

## you gotta know

- Developed the philosophy of *satyagraha* (truth-force), nonviolent civil disobedience that challenged the moral authority of colonial rule without matching Britain's military power.
- Led the *Salt March* (1930), a 240-mile march to the sea to produce salt in defiance of the British salt monopoly, galvanizing mass support and international attention.
- Organized the *Quit India Movement* (1942), demanding immediate British departure, which accelerated negotiations leading to independence in 1947.
- Endured repeated imprisonment, beatings, and torture by British authorities, whose violent suppression of his nonviolent protests exposed colonialism's brutality to the world.
- The *Jallianwala Bagh massacre* (1919), where British troops killed hundreds of Indian protesters, crystallized opposition to British rule and vindicated Gandhi's moral authority.
- Fasted as a political weapon, using his personal sacrifice to resolve religious communal violence and pressure political rivals, demonstrating ascetic spiritual authority.
- Assassinated in January 1948 by a Hindu extremist opposed to his communal reconciliation efforts, martyring him at the moment of Indian independence.
- Developed *khadi* (homespun cloth) as a symbol of Indian self-reliance and independence, turning textile production into a spiritual and political practice that unified the independence movement.
- Influenced global movements far beyond India: the American civil rights movement, anti-apartheid activism in South Africa, and peace movements worldwide drew inspiration from his philosophy of moral force.
