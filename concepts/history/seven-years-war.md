## summary

The **Seven Years' War** (1756–1763) was a vast global military conflict fought primarily between rival coalitions led by the [[british-empire]] and the Kingdom of [[france]], spanning theaters across Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa, and the Indian subcontinent. Triggered by escalating colonial confrontations in the Ohio River Valley and reshaped by the *Diplomatic Revolution* of 1756—which paired Britain with *Prussia* against Austria, [[france]], and the [[russian-empire]]—the struggle tested sovereign states across multiple continents. Widely considered by historians to be the first true world war, its resolution permanently altered the global balance of power by ending French hegemony in North America, establishing British maritime supremacy, and driving fiscal crises that culminated in the [[french-revolution]] and the [[american-revolution]].

## you gotta know

- Ignited in North America as the *French and Indian War* when colonial forces under [[george-washington]] ambushed French troops at the *Battle of Jumonville Glen* and subsequently surrendered at *Fort Necessity*.
- Great Britain achieved sweeping worldwide victories through the strategic vision of *William Pitt the Elder*, who heavily subsidized continental allies while using the Royal Navy to isolate and seize overseas French colonies.
- *Prussia* survived near-annihilation against overwhelming allied forces through the brilliant tactical maneuvers of *Frederick the Great* at major engagements like the *Battle of Rossbach* and the *Battle of Leuthen*.
- The pivotal climax in North America occurred in 1759, celebrated in London as the *Annus Mirabilis*, when British troops under General *James Wolfe* defeated the *Marquis de Montcalm* on the *Plains of Abraham* outside *Quebec*.
- In South Asia, the 1757 *Battle of Plassey* saw *Robert Clive* and the *East India Company* defeat the Nawab of Bengal and his French allies, establishing direct British territorial hegemony that displaced rivals across the subcontinent and opened commercial avenues toward [[china]].
- Prussia was miraculously saved from military collapse in 1762 by the *Miracle of the House of Brandenburg*, when *Empress Elizabeth* died and was succeeded by *Tsar Peter III*, an ardent admirer of Frederick who promptly pulled Russia out of the war.
- The conflict concluded with the 1763 *Treaty of Paris*, which forced [[france]] to cede Canada and claims east of the Mississippi River to Britain, while the separate *Treaty of Hubertusburg* preserved Prussian possession of the rich province of *Silesia*.
- Massive imperial debts incurred by Great Britain during the war prompted Parliament to levy new revenues through legislation such as the *Stamp Act*, provoking fierce resistance among the Thirteen Colonies and precipitating the [[american-revolution]].

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — emerged as the world's foremost colonial and maritime power while accumulating staggering national debt that destabilized imperial governance.
- [[france]] — suffered the humiliating loss of New France and catastrophic financial exhaustion that paved the path toward the [[french-revolution]].
- [[george-washington]] — commenced his military career in the Ohio Country clashes that ignited the initial conflict in North America.
- [[american-revolution]] — directly resulted from British efforts to enforce post-war imperial taxation upon American colonists without colonial representation.
- [[russian-empire]] — joined the anti-Prussian coalition and occupied Berlin before Peter III executed an abrupt reversal of foreign policy.
- [[colonialism]] — accelerated worldwide as the British Crown established commercial monopolies and territorial dominance across India and the Americas.
- [[spain]] — entered the war late under the Bourbon Family Compact, temporarily relinquishing Havana and Manila while receiving French Louisiana.
