## summary

**Criminal justice** is the system through which a society detects, prosecutes, and punishes violations of law, balancing state power against individual rights. In the United States, this system is grounded in constitutional protections—presumption of innocence, due process, right to counsel—that developed over centuries of common law and political struggle. The tension between law enforcement and civil liberties defines modern criminal justice.

## you gotta know

- The system comprises police, prosecutors, courts, and corrections officials working to enforce criminal statutes.
- Fundamental rights—protection against self-incrimination, the right to counsel, the right to confront witnesses—are enshrined in the *Fifth*, *Sixth*, and *Fourteenth* Amendments.
- The burden of proof rests on the state; a defendant is innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Due process requires that procedures be fair, transparent, and applied equally—not merely that laws exist.
- Criminal procedure encompasses arrest, interrogation, evidence rules, trial procedure, and appeals.
- Capital punishment represents the most severe punishment and has historically been the subject of intense moral and constitutional debate.
- Miscarriages of justice have prompted reforms: mandatory counsel for indigent defendants, Miranda warnings, suppression of illegally obtained evidence.
- The exclusionary rule, established in *Mapp v. Ohio* (1961), suppresses evidence obtained through unconstitutional searches and seizures.
- Sentencing guidelines and mandatory minimums attempt to standardize punishment but have been criticized for creating inconsistencies and fueling mass incarceration.
- The criminal justice system operates unevenly across socioeconomic lines; low-income defendants and minorities often face harsher treatment and outcomes than affluent accused persons.
- The death penalty remains legal in most American states, though its application has become increasingly rare and controversial on moral and constitutionality grounds.
- Prison reform movements have highlighted failures in rehabilitation, prison overcrowding, and recidivism rates, prompting debates over whether criminal justice prioritizes punishment or rehabilitation.
