## summary

**Messalina** (c. 17–48 CE) was a Roman empress and wife of Emperor *Claudius*, infamous for her political scheming, sexual exploits, and ultimate downfall during the chaotic first century of imperial Rome. Her life and death exemplified the dangers and intrigues of the imperial court, where power and survival were inseparable.

## you gotta know

- Married Emperor Claudius as his third wife around 41 CE, gaining enormous influence over the aging, somewhat naive ruler.
- Bore Claudius two children: Britannicus (whom she sought to elevate) and Octavia, cementing her power base within the imperial household.
- Used her position to eliminate political rivals and accumulate wealth through confiscations and bribes, becoming one of Rome's most feared power brokers.
- Conducted numerous affairs with senators, soldiers, and other prominent figures while her husband remained largely oblivious or unwilling to act.
- According to hostile sources (particularly historian Tacitus), engaged in legendary sexual escapades, though later accounts likely exaggerated her behavior for dramatic effect.
- Orchestrated the execution of prominent figures, including the wealthy senator Silius and others who fell from her favor or posed threats to her power.
- Was executed in 48 CE on Claudius's orders after her intrigues were exposed, becoming a cautionary tale of imperial feminine ambition and imperial politics.
- Her infamy was partly constructed by hostile male historians; modern scholars debate how much of her reputation reflects reality versus patriarchal propaganda designed to defame ambitious women in power.
- Represented a type of imperial consort power that both fascinated and terrified Roman elites who saw feminine political agency as threatening to male-centered state authority and masculine virtue.

## connections

- [[claudius]] — her husband, the emperor whose court she dominated and whose paranoia she exploited.
- [[roman-empire]] — the political system where women of the imperial family wielded tremendous informal power.
- [[agrippina-the-younger]] — Claudius's subsequent wife who succeeded in elevating her own son Nero to the throne.
- [[nero]] — her stepson, whose mother Agrippina later became Claudius's wife.
