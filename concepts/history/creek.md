## summary

The **Creek**, or **Muscogee** (**Mvskoke**), are an Indigenous confederacy originating in the southeastern woodlands of North America, primarily across modern-day Alabama, Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina. Counted historically by Euro-Americans among the "Five Civilized Tribes," they forged a sophisticated multi-town diplomatic network that resisted European encroachments by the [[british-empire]], [[spain]], and the nascent [[united-states]]. Their political autonomy was shattered in the early nineteenth century by internal conflict, military defeat, and violent forced removal under the Indian Removal Act to Indian Territory.

## you gotta know

- Forced from their southeastern ancestral lands along the Trail of Tears following the Indian Removal Act of 1830, establishing a new capital at Okmulgee in modern Oklahoma after suffering immense casualties from disease and exposure.
- Fractured during the Creek War of 1813–1814, a conflict that intersected with the War of 1812 and pitted traditionalist Red Sticks (*Upper Creek*) against US-allied *Lower Creek* forces.
- Suffered a catastrophic defeat at the March 1814 Battle of Horseshoe Bend (*Tohopeka*) on the Tallapoosa River, where future president *Andrew Jackson* and allied Cherokee and Creek warriors overwhelmed the fortifications of the Red Stick leader *Menawa*.
- Forced to sign the 1814 Treaty of Fort Jackson by *Andrew Jackson*, surrendering more than twenty million acres of territory across present-day Georgia and Alabama, even though allied Creeks had fought alongside US troops.
- Earlier secured the 1790 Treaty of New York, negotiated by *George Washington*, Secretary of War *Henry Knox*, and mixed-race Creek leader *Alexander McGillivray*, which recognized Creek border sovereignty before being undermined by land speculators.
- Divided politically between the *Upper Towns* along the Coosa and Tallapoosa Rivers and the *Lower Towns* along the Chattahoochee and Flint Rivers, reflecting differing geographic exposure and diplomatic alignments toward white settlers.
- Experienced internal civil execution when Chief *William McIntosh* signed the fraudulent 1825 Treaty of Indian Springs ceding remaining lands; the Creek National Council condemned him under tribal law, dispatching *Menawa* to execute *McIntosh* at his plantation.
- Won a landmark modern legal victory in the Supreme Court case *McGirt v. Oklahoma* (2020), which ruled that Congress had never disestablished the reservation of the **Muscogee Creek Nation**, dramatically redefining tribal criminal jurisdiction across eastern Oklahoma.
