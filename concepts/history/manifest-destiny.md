# Manifest Destiny

## summary

**Manifest Destiny** was the 19th-century American ideology that the United States was divinely ordained to expand across the North American continent from the Atlantic to the Pacific Ocean. This belief justified territorial conquest, Indian removal, and aggressive expansionism, shaping American foreign policy and dramatically altering the geopolitical landscape, though it resulted in the displacement and suffering of indigenous populations.

## you gotta know

- The term "Manifest Destiny" was coined in 1845 to articulate a belief held by many Americans that territorial expansion was inevitable, justified, and morally right—an ideological justification for westward conquest.
- The ideology drew on *Protestant* eschatology, nationalist fervor, and pseudoscientific racial theories (*Anglo-Saxon* or *Anglo-American* racial superiority), framing expansion as a civilizing and Christian mission.
- Manifest Destiny motivated the *Louisiana Purchase* (1803), territorial acquisition from *Spain* (*Florida*, *Texas*), the *Mexican-American War* (1846–1848) which seized the Southwest, and the *Oregon Treaty* (1846) with *Britain*.
- The expansion came at catastrophic cost to *Native American* peoples: the *Indian Removal Act* (1830), *Trail of Tears*, forced relocations, broken treaties, and genocidal warfare displaced millions from their ancestral lands.
- Manifest Destiny exacerbated sectional tensions over slavery: each new territory prompted conflicts over whether slavery would be permitted, driving the country toward civil war.
- The ideology extended internationally: American imperialists applied Manifest Destiny logic to justify interventions in *Latin America* and the *Pacific*, including the *Spanish-American War* (1898) and subsequent colonial acquisitions.
- Modern scholars critique Manifest Destiny as a retrospective rationalization for colonialism and genocide, though the ideology deeply shaped American self-identity and territorial ambitions for generations.
