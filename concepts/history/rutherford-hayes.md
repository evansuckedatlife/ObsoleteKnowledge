## summary

**Rutherford B. Hayes** was the nineteenth president of the [[united-states]], serving from 1877 to 1881 following the most bitterly contested presidential election in the nation's history. A former Ohio governor and Union general during the [[american-civil-war]], **Rutherford Hayes** entered the executive mansion through the [[compromise-of-1877]], an unwritten political bargain that awarded him disputed electoral returns in exchange for withdrawing federal troops from the South and ending the [[reconstruction-era]]. His single term was defined by resolute efforts to restore integrity to the executive branch, initiating landmark civil service reforms against entrenched party machines, managing the national crisis of the *Great Railroad Strike of 1877*, and returning the nation to the gold standard.

## you gotta know

- Ascended to the presidency through the disputed [[election-of-1876]] against Democrat [[samuel-tilden]], clinching the office when an eight-to-seven partisan Electoral Commission awarded him twenty contested electoral votes following the decisive [[compromise-of-1877]].
- Derided by outraged Democrats with the satirical monikers *His Fraudulency* and *Rutherfraud B. Hayes* due to his contentious installation, though he preserved his personal reputation by strictly honoring his pledge to serve only a single four-year term.
- Presided over a dry White House alongside his educated and devout wife, *Lucy Webb Hayes*, whose ardent advocacy of the temperance movement earned her the popular moniker *Lemonade Lucy*.
- Championed early federal civil service reform by taking on the dominant *Stalwart* faction of the Republican Party, defiantly firing future president *Chester A. Arthur* from his lucrative patronage post as Collector of the Port of New York.
- Dispatched federal troops to major rail centers across West Virginia, Maryland, and Pennsylvania to suppress widespread labor unrest during the *Great Railroad Strike of 1877*, establishing a precedent as the first peacetime deployment of the military against striking American workers.
- Championed financial conservatism by overseeing the full execution of the *Specie Resumption Act* to restore gold backing to paper currency, while unsuccessfully vetoing the inflationary silver-purchasing *Bland-Allison Act*.
- Wounded five separate times during the [[american-civil-war]], most famously at the Battle of South Mountain while commanding the 23rd Ohio Infantry alongside future president *William McKinley*, after having earlier defended runaway enslaved people in Cincinnati as an abolitionist-leaning lawyer.

## connections

- [[compromise-of-1877]] — the informal political bargain that resolved his disputed election and withdrew federal troops from the South.
- [[election-of-1876]] — the contentious presidential contest against Samuel Tilden that resulted in twenty contested electoral votes.
- [[samuel-tilden]] — the Democratic nominee who won the popular vote but lost the presidency by one electoral vote.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-war transformative period whose federal military phase concluded with his withdrawal of troops.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict in which he served as a brave Union general and sustained five combat injuries.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional amendment whose Southern civil rights guarantees lost federal military backing after 1877.
- [[united-states]] — the republic he led through a critical post-war reconciliation era while advancing executive independence.
