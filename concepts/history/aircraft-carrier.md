## summary

The **aircraft carrier** is a capital warship designed to carry and launch aircraft, representing a revolutionary shift in naval power from battleships to air-based warfare. Emerging as a crucial weapon during the interwar period and reaching maturity in *World War II*, carriers became the dominant force in naval combat. The carrier replaced the battleship as the Navy's most powerful vessel and remains central to modern military strategy and deterrence.

## you gotta know

- The first purpose-built aircraft carrier, HMS *Hermes*, was launched by Britain in 1924; the USS *Lexington* followed for the United States.
- The *Battle of the Coral Sea* (May 1942) was the first naval battle where the opposing ships never came within sight of each other—all combat was carried out by aircraft.
- The *Battle of Midway* (June 1942) confirmed the carrier's dominance: it was decided entirely by carrier-based aircraft, marking the end of the battleship era.
- American Essex-class carriers, built during WWII, proved so durable that many served into the Cold War and beyond.
- The USS *Enterprise* (CV-6) became one of the most decorated ships in US Navy history, participating in countless Pacific battles.
- Carriers require extensive support: escort ships, supply vessels, and fighter squadrons, making them the centerpiece of modern naval task forces.
- The size and cost of modern nuclear-powered carriers make them symbols of national power; the US Navy maintains roughly 11 active carriers.
