## summary

**Eleanor of Aquitaine** (c. 1122–1204), ruling as **Duchess of Aquitaine** in her own right, was one of the wealthiest, most powerful, and politically formidable figures of high-medieval Europe. Through consecutive dynastic marriages, she served first as Queen of *France* alongside King *Louis VII* and subsequently as Queen of *England* alongside King *Henry II*. Her vast inherited estates in southwestern France anchored the creation of the transatlantic *Angevin Empire*. A tireless political actor and celebrated patron of chivalric literature, she led crusaders, staged rebellions alongside her royal sons, and governed as English regent.

## you gotta know

- Reigned consecutively as Queen of *France* and Queen of *England*, making her the only woman in European history to wear both crowns.
- Inherited the sprawling *Duchy of Aquitaine*—stretching from the Loire River to the Pyrenees—upon the death of her father *William X*, making her the most sought-after heiress in Latin Christendom at age fifteen.
- Joined her first husband *Louis VII* on the military campaign of the *Second Crusade* to Antioch and Jerusalem, personally leading a large armed contingent of Aquitanian vassals.
- Secured a papal annulment from *Louis VII* on the canonical grounds of consanguinity in 1152, successfully retaining her ancestral duchy and marrying the future *Henry II* of *England* eight weeks later.
- Sided with her sons *Richard the Lionheart* (*Richard I*), *Henry the Young King*, and *Geoffrey II* against *Henry II* during the Great Revolt of 1173–1174, resulting in her imprisonment under royal guard for fifteen years.
- Served as de facto ruler and regent of *England* following *Henry II*'s death, holding the realm secure while *Richard I* fought on the *Third Crusade*.
- Personally negotiated, gathered, and delivered the colossal hundred-thousand-mark ransom demanded by Holy Roman Emperor *Henry VI* after *Richard I* was captured returning from Palestine.
- Presided over a renowned cultural court at *Poitiers*, where she and her daughter *Marie of Champagne* nurtured troubadours and fostered the literary traditions of chivalric courtly love.

## connections

- [[england]] — the island kingdom she helped govern as queen consort and later as regent for her sons.
- [[france]] — her continental homeland and the kingdom she ruled during her first marriage to *Louis VII*.
- [[christianity]] — the religious authority whose papacy dissolved her first marriage and under whose auspices she crusaded.
- [[richard-i]] — her favorite son, whose release from German captivity and claim to the English crown she secured.
- [[king-john]] — her youngest son, whose disputed continental claims against *Philip II* she defended into her eighties.
- [[thomas-becket]] — the Archbishop of Canterbury whose bitter constitutional conflict with *Henry II* destabilized the Angevin court.
- [[house-of-plantagenet]] — the royal dynasty established through her marriage to *Henry II* that held the English crown for centuries.
