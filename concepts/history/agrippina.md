## summary

**Agrippina the Elder**, historically known as **Vipsania Agrippina** and **Agrippina Major**, was an influential Roman noblewoman of the early Julio-Claudian dynasty who lived during the formative decades of the [[roman-empire]]. As the direct granddaughter of Emperor *Augustus* and the devoted wife of the celebrated general *Germanicus*, she earned widespread admiration and military loyalty across [[rome]] and its frontier provinces. Following the sudden death of her husband, she tirelessly championed her children's dynastic rights against Emperor *Tiberius* and the ambitious prefect *Sejanus*. Her unrelenting struggle ultimately helped secure the imperial purple for her son *Caligula*, though it cost her freedom, health, and life.

## you gotta know

- Daughter of *Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa* and *Julia the Elder*, making her the legitimate maternal granddaughter of *Augustus*, the mother of Emperor *Caligula*, and the maternal grandmother of Emperor *Nero*.
- Accompanied her husband *Germanicus* on arduous military expeditions across [[germany]], famously preventing panic among legionaries at the Rhine by forbidding the demolition of a crucial bridge at Castra Vetera.
- Gave birth to nine children, six of whom survived to adulthood, earning widespread public praise across [[italy]] as an exemplar of traditional Roman matronly virtue and aristocratic fecundity.
- Transported the ashes of *Germanicus* back to [[rome]] from Antioch following his suspicious poisoning in 19 AD, dramatically disembarking at Brundisium before grieving crowds to demand justice against governor *Gnaeus Calpurnius Piso*.
- Led a prominent political faction in the Roman Senate known as the Agrippina party, openly defying Emperor *Tiberius* and warning that his powerful praetorian prefect *Sejanus* intended to destroy the Julian line.
- Exiled in 29 AD by the Senate under *Tiberius* to the barren Tyrrhenian island of *Pandateria*, where her mother had once been banished, suffering brutal beatings from a centurion that cost her an eye.
- Died of starvation while imprisoned in 33 AD, either through forced deprivation or an act of suicide through hunger strike, exactly on the anniversary of her husband's death.
- Posthumously rehabilitated when *Caligula* ascended the throne in 37 AD; he personally sailed into winter storms to recover her remains, interred them in the Mausoleum of Augustus, and struck coinage in her honour.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the realm whose dynastic succession she shaped through her lineage and fierce opposition to *Tiberius*.
- [[rome]] — the capital city where she rallied public sentiment, contested Senate politics, and was ultimately entombed with imperial honors.
- [[germany]] — the turbulent northern frontier province where she stood alongside *Germanicus* and safeguarded the Rhine crossing.
- [[italy]] — the homeland where she returned carrying her husband's funeral urn to widespread popular mourning.
- [[middle-east]] — the region encompassing Syria where *Germanicus* died under mysterious circumstances, sparking her lifelong campaign for vengeance.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the eastern Mediterranean territories she toured with her husband before his fatal illness in Antioch.
- [[tragedy]] — the literary and historical mode employed by Roman historians such as *Tacitus* to narrate her dramatic downfall.
