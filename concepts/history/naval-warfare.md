## summary

**Naval warfare** encompasses the tactics, technologies, and strategies of combat on water, from galley ramming in antiquity to industrial-age battleship engagements and modern submarine warfare. Maritime dominance has been decisive in shaping empires, controlling trade routes, and determining military outcomes throughout recorded history. The evolution from sail to steam to steel revolutionized naval power and the strategic value of sea control.

## you gotta know

- Ancient naval tactics centered on ramming and boarding—the trireme, a three-tiered oared warship, defined Mediterranean power for centuries.
- The invention of the cannon fundamentally altered naval architecture; warships evolved from broad, high-castled vessels to lower, gun-heavy designs optimized for broadside firepower.
- The British Royal Navy's dominance from the 18th century onward was built on ship-of-the-line tactics, where heavily armed wooden ships fought in rigid battle lines.
- *Ironclad ships*, introduced during the American Civil War, made wooden warships obsolete overnight; *HMS Dreadnought* (1906) set the template for all subsequent battleships with its uniform big-gun armament.
- The *Battle of the Atlantic* in World War II demonstrated that submarines and aircraft had superseded battleships as the decisive naval weapons.
- Carrier task forces replaced battleships as the dominant naval formation; modern naval warfare involves anti-ship missiles, naval aviation, and electronic warfare over vast distances.
- Naval blockades—cutting off an enemy's sea trade—have proven as devastating as direct combat, starving nations into surrender or negotiation.
