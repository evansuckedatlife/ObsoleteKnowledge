## summary

**Qin Shi Huang** (born **Ying Zheng**), also known as **Shi Huangdi**, was the founder of the *Qin dynasty* and the first monarch to rule a unified *[[china]]*, reigning as emperor from 221 to 210 BCE. After methodically conquering the rival realms of the *[[warring-states-period]]*, he abolished hereditary feudalism, centralized governance under strict Legalist doctrines, and standardized weights, measures, coinage, and the written script. His transformative yet tyrannical reign established the bureaucratic architecture of imperial *[[china]]* that endured for more than two millennia, while producing monumental works including his vast mausoleum complex and early frontier walls.

## you gotta know

- Unified the fractured kingdoms of the *[[warring-states-period]]* by subduing the state of *Qi* in 221 BCE, ending centuries of chronic warfare and creating the title *huangdi* ("emperor") to signify a monarch surpassing the ancient kings.
- Commissioned the legendary *[[terra-cotta-army]]*, an enormous subterranean necropolis near modern *Xi'an* featuring thousands of life-sized ceramic infantrymen, archers, chariots, and horses constructed to safeguard his spirit in the afterlife.
- Ordered the linkage and expansion of disparate regional ramparts along the northern frontier into an early precursor of the *[[great-wall-of-china]]* to repel raids from the nomadic *Xiongnu* confederation.
- Implemented sweeping bureaucratic standardization orchestrated by Chancellor *Li Si*, enforcing uniform cart axle widths, regional measurements, copper coinage, and the standardized Small Seal script across all administrative commanderies.
- Instigated the infamous suppression known as the "burning of books and burying of scholars" (*fenshu kengru*) in 213–212 BCE, destroying rival philosophical texts and executing dissenting scholars to enforce ideological conformity.
- Replaced traditional aristocratic feudalism with rigorous Legalism (*Fajia*), an authoritarian governance model that relied on codification of strict penal statutes, universal conscription, and collective family responsibility for legal violations.
- Became obsessively consumed with seeking physical immortality in his twilight years, dispatching maritime expeditions under *Xu Fu* to find mystical islands and ingesting toxic mercury compounds compounded by court alchemists.
- Died abruptly at *Shaqiu* while on an inspection tour in 210 BCE, leading conspirators *Zhao Gao* and *Li Si* to transport his decaying body alongside carts of rotten fish to conceal his death until a forged succession decree was issued.

## connections

- [[china]] — the unified civilization and imperial state structure created through his sweeping political and territorial consolidation.
- [[han-dynasty]] — successor dynasty that retained Qin bureaucratic commanderies and legal apparatus while adopting Confucianism as moral state philosophy.
- [[silk-road]] — ancient Eurasian overland commercial artery whose precursor routes and garrisons were stabilized by Qin territorial cohesion.
- [[terra-cotta-army]] — subterranean funerary garrison of thousands of individualized clay soldiers buried within his mausoleum necropolis at *Mount Li*.
- [[great-wall-of-china]] — northern border barrier consolidated under his reign by connecting preexisting territorial walls using hundreds of thousands of laborers.
- [[warring-states-period]] — era of fragmentation and warfare among rival Chinese states that concluded when his armies completed total conquest.
- [[zhou-dynasty]] — ancient feudal dynasty whose decentralized political model and ritual hegemony he extinguished to establish absolute imperial autocracy.
