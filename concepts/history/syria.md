## summary

**Syria** is a nation in the Levant region of the Middle East, strategically situated on trade routes between Europe, Africa, and Asia. Its capital, *Damascus*, is one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities. In modern history, Syria emerged from Ottoman rule after World War I, but has been dominated by a single authoritarian dynasty—the Alawite *Baath Party* under *Hafez al-Assad* and his son *Bashar al-Assad*—making it a focal point of regional conflict and geopolitics.

## you gotta know

- Ancient Syria was the cultural heart of the Levant; Damascus was a major trading hub on the *Silk Road* and a center of Islamic civilization.
- The region was part of the *Ottoman Empire* for four centuries (1516–1918); after Ottoman collapse in World War I, Syria became a French League of Nations mandate (1920–1943).
- Syria gained independence in 1943 but remained politically unstable, experiencing multiple coups and military governments; the country saw the rise of Arab nationalism and anti-Western sentiment.
- The *Baath Party* took power in a 1963 coup and has ruled Syria ever since; under *Hafez al-Assad* (1970–2000), the party became synonymous with secularist Arab socialism and Alawite minority rule.
- Syria fought in the 1967 Six-Day War against *Israel*, losing the *Golan Heights*; this defeat became a defining national trauma and shaped decades of Syrian hostility toward Israel.
- Syria became a client state of the *Soviet Union* during the Cold War, receiving military aid and political support; after 1991, it shifted toward more pragmatic regional diplomacy.
- The Syrian Civil War (2011–present) emerged from the Arab Spring and evolved into a complex proxy conflict involving the Assad regime, various rebel factions, *ISIS*, regional powers (*Iran*, Turkey), and the United States.
