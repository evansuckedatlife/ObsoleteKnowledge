## summary

The **Girondins** were a moderate republican faction in the *French Revolution* who favored decentralized power, constitutional limits on executive authority, and a gradualist approach to reform. Opposed by the more radical *Montagnards*, they dominated the early Legislative Assembly but were purged from power during the *Reign of Terror*, with many executed for their perceived counterrevolutionary sympathies.

## you gotta know

- Named for the Gironde department in southwestern France where many leaders originated, though the faction was actually led from Paris (many leaders were deputies from Gironde or allied provinces).
- Advocated for a federal system of regional autonomy rather than centralized power, fearing Paris domination and mob rule by radical sans-culottes.
- Supported the constitutional monarchy under *Louis XVI* and opposed regicide, believing gradual reform preferable to violent rupture of the established order.
- Drew support from bourgeois merchants, lawyers, and provincial interests; represented commercially-oriented French regions outside of Paris.
- Controlled the Legislative Assembly (1791–1792) and initially dominated the National Convention (September 1792–spring 1793), pushing for war against Austria to rally the nation.
- Believed war would rally moderates and discredit the radical left; instead, military setbacks strengthened radical influence and made the Girondins appear incompetent and defeatist.
- Gradually lost ground to the *Montagnards*, who appealed to urban poor and radical ideology with promises of economic intervention, price controls, and revolutionary justice.
- The Montagnards controlled the Paris Commune and the sans-culottes militias, giving them effective power over the Convention despite fewer formal votes.
- Purged between 1793 and 1794 as Robespierre and the *Committee of Public Safety* executed 21 Girondin leaders in October 1793 and imprisoned dozens more as traitors and counterrevolutionaries.
- Their purge (the *purge of the Girondins*) marked the beginning of the *Reign of Terror* and consolidated Montagnard/Jacobin control.
- Survivors became symbols of moderation and principle; posthumously rehabilitated as victims of revolutionary extremism and Robespierre's paranoia.
- Their fate exemplifies the revolutionary dynamic of escalating violence; moderates were outpaced by more radical movements and ultimately destroyed.
