## summary

The **Victorian era** (1837–1901) was the reign of *Queen Victoria* of Britain, spanning 64 years of unprecedented industrial, economic, and imperial expansion. The period witnessed Britain's ascendancy as the world's dominant industrial and military power, moral debates over propriety and progress, literary and artistic flourishing, and deep social anxieties over modernization, class, and the empire's moral foundations.

## you gotta know

- *Queen Victoria*'s accession (1837) initiated an era of conservative moralism and emphasis on duty, propriety, and family values—"Victorianism" became synonymous with sexual restraint and social hierarchy.
- The *Industrial Revolution* reached its apex during Victoria's reign: steam power, railways, factories, and urban growth transformed Britain from agrarian to industrial, creating unprecedented wealth and dislocation.
- Britain became the world's premier imperial power, controlling India, vast African territories, Australia, and dominions; the Raj represented both industrial organization and racial hierarchy, glorified at home but destabilizing abroad.
- The *Great Exhibition* (1851) showcased British industrial achievement and was the most visited event of the era; it symbolized confidence in progress, technology, and British superiority.
- Class tensions mounted as industrialization created a powerful industrial bourgeoisie, a growing working class, and a destabilized landed aristocracy; political reform expanded the franchise in 1867 and 1884, gradually democratizing the political system.
- Literature flourished: *Charles Dickens*, *George Eliot*, *Thomas Hardy*, *Oscar Wilde*, and *George Bernard Shaw* created masterpieces grappling with modernization, morality, hypocrisy, and social injustice.
- The era's end was marked by anxiety: the Boer War (1899–1902) shattered assumptions of British invincibility, and the death of Victoria (1901) closed a psychological epoch.
