## summary

**Pausanias** designates two essential figures of *[[ancient-greece]]*: **Pausanias the Regent**, the 5th-century BCE Spartan general who commanded the Hellenic coalition to victory over *[[mardonius]]* at the Battle of Plataea, and **Pausanias the Geographer**, the 2nd-century CE travel writer whose *Description of Greece* provides an indispensable firsthand survey of classical art and sacred topography. The Spartan commander broke the land power of the Achaemenid invasion during the *[[persian-wars]]* through tactical discipline on the battlefield, though his subsequent autocratic ambitions at Byzantium led to his disgrace and starvation at the Temple of Athena of the Brazen House. Centuries later under the *[[roman-empire]]*, the geographer journeyed across mainland Greece to systematically document religious sanctuaries, legendary monuments, and civic traditions, preserving key insights into masterpieces such as the colossal statue of *[[zeus]]* crafted by *[[phidias]]*. Together, the two figures represent the military defense of Greek liberty at its height and the literary preservation of Hellenic cultural memory for future generations.

## you gotta know

- Commanded the allied Greek army as regent of *Sparta* at the decisive Battle of Plataea in 479 BCE, routing the Persian army and slaying the general *[[mardonius]]* to permanently end the second Persian invasion of Greece.
- Authored the *Description of Greece* (*Hellados Periegesis*) in ten books during the 2nd century CE, creating a comprehensive topographical travelogue of central Greece and the Peloponnese during the Antonine era of the *[[roman-empire]]*.
- Provided the primary surviving eyewitness account of the chryselephantine Statue of *[[zeus]]* at Olympia created by *[[phidias]]*, alongside meticulous records of the surrounding Altis sanctuary and early *[[olympic-games]]* traditions.
- Captured Byzantium from the Persians in 478 BCE but alienated the allied Greek fleet through tyrannical behavior, Persian dress, and alleged treasonous correspondence with *Xerxes I*, prompting the formation of the Delian League under Athenian leadership.
- Incurred widespread outrage by engraving an arrogant personal dedication on the Golden Tripod supported by the *Serpent Column* at Delphi, which the Spartan government hastily erased and replaced with the names of all contributing Greek cities.
- Starved to death inside the precinct of the Temple of Athena of the Brazen House (*Chalkioikos*) after Spartan magistrates and ephors cornered him for plotting a helot rebellion, only carrying his body outside just before he expired to prevent ritual pollution.
- Served as the indispensable topographical roadmap for modern 19th-century archaeology, directly guiding *Heinrich Schliemann* in excavating the royal shaft graves at Mycenae and German excavators in uncovering the ancient ruins of Olympia.

## connections

- [[persian-wars]] — the generational clash in which the Spartan regent served as supreme commander of the Hellenic land forces.
- [[mardonius]] — the Persian general whose defeat and death at Plataea sealed the triumph of the Spartan general's coalition.
- [[phidias]] — the classical sculptor whose monumental chryselephantine works at Olympia and Athens were exhaustively described by the geographer.
- [[zeus]] — the king of the Olympian gods whose sanctuary at Elis forms the focal center of the geographer's fifth and sixth books.
- [[olympic-games]] — the quadrennial pan-Hellenic festival whose athletic monuments, victory statues, and sacred history were recorded in the *Description of Greece*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization whose military defense and cultural monuments define the historical significance of both namesakes.
- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean superpower whose imperial peace enabled the geographer to travel freely and compose his exhaustive regional survey.
