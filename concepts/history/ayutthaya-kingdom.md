## summary

The **Ayutthaya Kingdom** (also known as the **Kingdom of Ayutthaya** or simply **Ayutthaya**) was a dominant Siamese realm centered on an island city in the Chao Phraya river basin from 1351 to 1767. Celebrated as one of the wealthiest and most cosmopolitan trading emporiums in early modern Asia, the empire wielded profound political and cultural hegemony across mainland Southeast Asia. Its dramatic sack by invading Burmese armies ended four centuries of continuous dynastic rule, triggering the rapid reorganization of Siam that culminated in the rise of the [[chakri-dynasty]].

## you gotta know

- Sacked and utterly destroyed in 1767 by the Burmese *Konbaung Dynasty* under King *Hsinbyushin*, bringing an end to four centuries of Siamese dominion and spurring King *Taksin* to establish the brief *Thonburi Kingdom*.
- King *Naresuan the Great* declared Siamese independence from Burmese Taungoo vassalage in 1584, achieving folkloric fame by slaying the Burmese crown prince *Mingyi Swa* during a climactic elephant duel at the Battle of *Nong Sarai* in 1593.
- Experienced a golden age of diplomatic engagement under King *Narai the Great*, whose Greek first minister *Constantine Phaulkon* facilitated grand embassies with King *Louis XIV* of [[france]] before being overthrown in the anti-European *Siamese Revolution of 1688*.
- Founded in 1351 by King *Ramathibodi I* (*Uthong*), who positioned the capital on a strategic, flood-protected island confluence of the *Chao Phraya*, *Pa Sak*, and *Lopburi* rivers and codified the first unified legal statutes of Siam.
- Flourished as a global entrepôt during the [[age-of-exploration]], accommodating self-governing trade quarters for merchants and mercenaries hailing from [[china]], [[japan]], Persia, Portugal, and the Dutch East India Company.
- Structured its administration around the intricate *sakdina* social hierarchy codified by King *Borommatrailokanat*, which ranked every subject from peasant to prince through numerical allocations of land, legal standing, and compulsory corvée labor.
- Patronized Theravada [[buddhism]] as the paramount foundation of royal legitimacy and architecture, commissioning soaring stone prangs and majestic monastic complexes including *Wat Phra Si Sanphet*, *Wat Mahathat*, and *Wat Chaiwatthanaram*.

## connections

- [[chakri-dynasty]] — the successor ruling line that established Bangkok and deliberately modeled its administrative, legal, and artistic heritage on the fallen capital.
- [[buddhism]] — the primary state religion whose moral ideals cast the monarch as a righteous *dhammaraja* and protector of the monastic order.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the period of expanding international trade during which European merchants and envoys established competitive commercial factories along the Chao Phraya.
- [[france]] — the western power that sent diplomatic missions under *Louis XIV* to convert King *Narai* and garrison Siamese fortresses prior to the 1688 expulsion.
- [[china]] — the imperial realm with which Siamese rulers maintained crucial tributary relations and profitable maritime merchant junk commerce throughout the Ming and Qing eras.
- [[japan]] — the island power whose expatriates and exiled Christian samurai, notably led by *Yamada Nagamasa*, served as royal bodyguards and prominent overseas traders.
- [[colonialism]] — the emerging European imperial expansion across Southeast Asia that the Siamese royal court countered through flexible statecraft and commercial balance.
