# Ghost Dance

## summary

The **Ghost Dance** was a late nineteenth-century spiritual revitalization movement founded in 1889 by the Northern Paiute prophet *Wovoka* in Nevada. Blending traditional indigenous circular dancing with apocalyptic prophecy and elements of [[christianity]], it promised that righteous living, peaceful cooperation, and ceremonial devotion would reunite the living with their ancestors, restore vanished bison herds, and reverse the devastation wrought by [[colonialism]]. As the movement spread rapidly eastward across the Great Plains to the Lakota people, mounting hysteria among federal authorities and the [[united-states]] military culminated in the killing of [[sitting-bull]] and the catastrophic 1890 massacre at [[wounded-knee]].

## you gotta know

- The movement's suppression directly precipitated the December 29, 1890 massacre at [[wounded-knee]], where the reconstructed [[seventh-cavalry]] slaughtered an estimated three hundred Miniconjou Lakota men, women, and children under *Spotted Elk*, an atrocity widely regarded by historians as marking the conclusion of the armed Indian Wars.
- Lakota adherents adopted sacred white garments known as ghost shirts, which leaders such as *Kicking Bear* and *Short Bull* preached would spiritually protect dancers by repelling the bullets of white soldiers.
- Federal apprehension over the ritual led Indian Agency police to attempt the arrest of the revered Hunkpapa Lakota leader [[sitting-bull]] on December 15, 1890, triggering a bloody standoff at the *Standing Rock* Reservation that resulted in his death.
- The spiritual movement was initiated by *Wovoka*, a Northern Paiute religious leader also known as *Jack Wilson*, who experienced a transformative vision during the solar eclipse of January 1, 1889, in Nevada.
- Despite being perceived by the War Department as a hostile military conspiracy, *Wovoka* strictly taught an ethic of non-violence, industriousness, and harmonious coexistence, drawing on both native traditions and teachings from [[christianity]] to preach spiritual renewal.
- The 1889 movement was the second iteration of the dance, building directly upon the earlier 1870 Ghost Dance led by *Wovoka*'s father or mentor, the Paiute prophet *Tavibo*, which had spread through California and Oregon.
- Smithsonian ethnologist *James Mooney* conducted extensive fieldwork among participating tribes in the immediate aftermath of the tragedy, publishing the foundational 1896 monograph *The Ghost-Dance Religion and the Sioux Outbreak of 1890*, which preserved songs, ceremonies, and firsthand accounts.

## connections

- [[wounded-knee]] — the site of the 1890 military massacre of Lakota dancers that decisively ended the movement.
- [[sitting-bull]] — the legendary Lakota leader assassinated by reservation police over fears he would endorse the dance.
- [[colonialism]] — the overarching system of dispossession, forced assimilation, and land confinement that spurred the revitalist movement.
- [[united-states]] — the federal government whose military forces and reservation agents violently suppressed the peaceful revival.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition whose apocalyptic theology and resurrection motifs strongly influenced *Wovoka*'s messianic message.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-Civil War period during which accelerated western settlement and railroad expansion decimated native lands.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict whose veteran officers, tactical doctrines, and demobilized troops were redirected to subjugate Plains tribes.
