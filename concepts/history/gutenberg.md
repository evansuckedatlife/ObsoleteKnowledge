## summary

**Johannes Gutenberg** was a German inventor, blacksmith, and goldsmith who introduced movable type mechanical printing to Europe around 1440. By developing durable metal punch-cut matrices, an oil-based printing ink, and an adapted wooden screw press, he established typography as a viable mass-production industry. Gutenberg's innovation initiated a media revolution that democratized literacy, accelerated the spread of religious and scientific ideas, and formed the technological cornerstone of the European [[renaissance]] and Reformation.

## you gotta know

- Born *Johannes Gensfleisch zur Laden zum Gutenberg* in the German city of *Mainz* into an aristocratic patrician family with ties to the archbishopric mint.
- Perfected movable type by formulating an alloy of lead, tin, and antimony that melted at low temperatures, expanded slightly upon cooling, and withstood repeated pressing.
- Invented an adjustable hand mould that permitted the rapid and uniform casting of interchangeable, identical metal letters and punctuation sorts.
- Produced the monumental *Gutenberg Bible* (also called the *42-Line Bible* or *B42*) around 1455, printing roughly one hundred and eighty copies on paper and vellum.
- Financed his ambitious workshop through massive loans from the financier *Johann Fust*, who eventually sued Gutenberg, foreclosing on the print shop and taking control alongside Gutenberg's foreman, *Peter Schöffer*.
- Precipitated the *Incunabula* era, a half-century boom running until 1501 during which printing workshops proliferated across Europe, issuing millions of books.
- Honored late in life by the Archbishop of Mainz, *Adolf von Nassau*, who granted him the courtly title of *Hofmann* with a yearly pension of grain, wine, and tax exemptions.

## connections

- [[germany]] — the Holy Roman Empire territory where Gutenberg developed, financed, and operated his print shop.
- [[renaissance]] — the intellectual and cultural rebirth propelled forward by the sudden abundance of classical texts and scholarly translations.
- [[christianity]] — the dominant religious faith whose sacred scriptures were first mass-produced via the landmark forty-two-line Latin Vulgate Bible.
- [[gunpowder]] — a fellow transformative invention alongside printing that Francis Bacon famously identified as having altered the whole face of the world.
- [[enlightenment]] — the later philosophical movement made possible by the wide distribution of uncensored vernacular literature and scientific treatises.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the era of automated mass manufacture that drew inspiration from Gutenberg's early modular, interchangeable typecasting.
