## summary

The **Concert of Europe** was a system of collective great-power diplomacy established after the *Napoleonic Wars* (1815) in which Britain, Russia, Austria, France, and Prussia agreed to manage European affairs through periodic conferences rather than unilateral action. Built on principles of balance-of-power and shared interest in stability, the Concert suppressed revolutionary movements, partitioned Poland, and maintained conservative monarchies—making it both a mechanism of international cooperation and an instrument of authoritarian repression. Though lasting formally until the 1870s, the Concert gradually dissolved as nationalist ambitions and industrial rivalry fractured the post-Napoleonic consensus.

## you gotta know

- Emerged from the *Congress of Vienna* (1814–1815), where the victorious powers reorganized Europe after Napoleon's defeat.
- *Metternich* (Austrian foreign minister) was the Concert's principal architect and ideologue, using it to isolate revolutionary movements and preserve autocratic monarchy.
- Based on the principle that major powers should consult before unilateral action and that the stability of one great power benefits all—a forerunner of modern multilateralism.
- Intervened collectively: the Concert authorized intervention in Spain (1823), Greece (to recognize independence, 1829), and the Balkans, overriding national sovereignty.
- Preserved *balance-of-power* by preventing any single nation from dominating Europe; this diplomatic mechanism restrained but did not prevent future wars and conflicts.
- Suppressed liberal nationalism: the Concert opposed Italian unification and Greek independence initially, viewing them as threats to conservative order.
- Declined after the Crimean War (1853–1856) and *German unification*: rising nationalism and intense industrial competition undermined the consensual foundations.
