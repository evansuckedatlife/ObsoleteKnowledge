## summary

**Earl Warren** was an American jurist and politician who served as the fourteenth Chief Justice of the *[[united-states]]* Supreme Court from 1953 to 1969. Previously the Republican Governor of California, his tenure as head of the judicial branch ushered in the transformative "Warren Court" era, which radically expanded civil rights, civil liberties, and federal judicial power. His consensus-building leadership famously forged a unanimous ruling declaring racial segregation in public schools unconstitutional, fundamentally reshaping constitutional interpretation in modern American life.

## you gotta know

- Authored the unanimous landmark opinion in *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954), famously holding that "separate educational facilities are inherently unequal" under the *[[14th-amendment]]*.
- Orchestrated complete unanimity among the nine justices in *Brown* through extensive private diplomacy, preventing dissenting opinions that could have fueled southern resistance.
- Presided over the criminal procedure revolution that mandated legal counsel for indigent defendants in *Gideon v. Wainwright* (1963) and required procedural police warnings in *Miranda v. Arizona* (1966).
- Established the constitutional "one person, one vote" standard for legislative redistricting in *Reynolds v. Sims* (1964), a decision he personally considered his most important.
- Struck down state anti-miscegenation statutes prohibiting interracial marriage in the milestone civil rights case *Loving v. Virginia* (1967).
- Chaired the *Warren Commission*, appointed by President *Lyndon B. Johnson* in 1963 to investigate the assassination of President *John F. Kennedy*, which concluded that *Lee Harvey Oswald* acted alone.
- Served three terms as Governor of California, during which he supported the controversial wartime internment of Japanese Americans, an action he publicly expressed deep regret over in his posthumous memoirs.
