## summary

**Chivalry** was the ideological code of conduct governing medieval aristocratic warriors, blending martial prowess with Christian virtue, courtly refinement, and honor. Emerging in the 12th century, it elevated knights above mere soldiers, framing military service as a moral calling bound by loyalty to feudal lords, protection of the weak, and romantic love. Though the code was often honored more in literature than practice, chivalry shaped medieval culture and left an enduring imprint on Western ideas of honor and nobility.

## you gotta know

- Chivalry originated among Norman and Frankish warriors in the 11th–12th centuries, shaped by the Crusades and the need to impose moral order on feudal violence.
- The code emphasized several intertwined virtues: *loyalty* to one's lord (feudal obedience), *martial valor* in battle, *protection* of the weak and defenceless, *Christian piety* and service to the Church, and *courtly love*—idealized romantic devotion to a noble lady.
- Knights were supposed to protect the Church, defend the poor, maintain justice, and conduct themselves with *courtesy*—refined, dignified behavior that distinguished aristocrats from commoners.
- Tournaments and trials of combat became theatrical displays of chivalric virtue; knights proved their worth through formal contests, elaborate armor, and heraldic display.
- Chivalric ideals were romanticized in literature—Arthurian legends, romances of *King Arthur* and *Lancelot*, songs of courtly love—which often depicted knights more nobly than historical reality warranted.
- In practice, many medieval knights were brutal, rapacious, and mercenary; the gap between the chivalric ideal and actual conduct created enduring irony and critique.
- *Miguel de Cervantes'* *Don Quixote* (1605) satirized the chivalric code, depicting an aging knight attempting to live by archaic ideals in a world that had abandoned them—a death knell for chivalry's cultural dominance.

- [[middle-ages]] — the historical period in which chivalry emerged, flourished, and declined.
- [[knights]] — the warrior class bound by the chivalric code and its ideals.
- [[king-arthur]] — the legendary king whose court became the ultimate symbol of chivalric ideals in Western literature.
- [[cervantes]] — the author whose *Don Quixote* satirized and effectively ended the cultural dominance of chivalric romance.
- [[tournament]] — the ritualized combat through which knights performed and tested their chivalric virtue.
- [[france]] — the region (Aquitaine, Burgundy) where chivalry was most elaborately developed and practiced.
- [[england]] — the nation that inherited and adapted chivalric traditions, especially through the Arthurian legends.
