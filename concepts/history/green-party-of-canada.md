## summary

The **Green Party of Canada** is a federal political party founded in 1983 dedicated to environmentalism, non-violence, social justice, and ecological wisdom. Emerging from grassroots peace and conservation movements during the late [[cold-war]], the party evolved from a peripheral protest organization into a parliamentary force in Canadian politics. Under the long-time leadership of *Elizabeth May*, it achieved historic breakthroughs by securing representation in the House of Commons and shifting national debate toward climate policy.

## you gotta know

- Founded in Ottawa in 1983 at Carleton University, inspired by the rise of green political movements across Western Europe and environmental activism in British Columbia.
- Operates on the *Global Greens Charter*, emphasizing six core tenets: ecological wisdom, social justice, participatory democracy, non-violence, sustainability, and respect for diversity.
- Achieved a historic electoral breakthrough in the 2011 federal election when party leader *Elizabeth May* won the riding of *Saanich—Gulf Islands*, becoming the first elected Green member of Parliament.
- Gained its first sitting member of Parliament in 2008 when former Liberal MP *Blair Wilson* crossed the floor to sit as a Green Party member shortly before that year's general election.
- Advocates unconventional monetary and fiscal policies, including proposals to direct the [[governor-of-the-bank-of-canada]] to support sustainable municipal infrastructure through green bonds.
- Positioned itself against the traditional resource-extraction platforms championed by older parties, contrasting with the legacy of leaders such as [[john-diefenbaker]] and [[joe-clark]].
- Reached its peak popular vote performance in the 2019 federal election, electing three members of Parliament and capturing over one million votes across the country.

## connections

- [[governor-of-the-bank-of-canada]] — central monetary office that Green Party policy platforms frequently propose leveraging to fund climate transition infrastructure.
- [[joe-clark]] — Progressive Conservative prime minister whose western conservative base formed the dominant regional establishment that Green candidates challenged in British Columbia.
- [[john-diefenbaker]] — twentieth-century prime minister whose resource-focused *Northern Vision* development contrasted with green conservation ethics.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical era whose anti-nuclear and disarmament demonstrations directly catalyzed the founding of the Canadian green movement.
- [[culture]] — broader societal attitudes and environmental philosophies that reshaped modern public policy and electoral competition.
- [[british-empire]] — historic source of the Westminster parliamentary system and first-past-the-post electoral mechanics that hindered third-party seat conversion.
