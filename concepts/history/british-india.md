## summary

**British India**, commonly known as the **British Raj**, was the system of direct imperial governance exercised by the British Crown over the Indian subcontinent between 1858 and 1947. Instituted in the wake of the 1857 Indian Rebellion, which dissolved the administrative rule of the East India Company, the Raj incorporated modern-day India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh, along with regions of Burma. As the prized "Jewel in the Crown" of the [[british-empire]], it transformed global trade, institutionalized racial hierarchy, and galvanized one of the most influential anti-colonial nationalist struggles in world history.

## you gotta know

- Instituted through the *Government of India Act 1858*, which liquidated the rule of the *Honourable East India Company* and transferred sovereign authority directly to *Queen Victoria*.
- Governed on the ground by a Crown-appointed *Viceroy and Governor-General*, with policy established in London by the cabinet post of *Secretary of State for India* and executed locally through the competitive, elite *Indian Civil Service*.
- Partitioned formally at midnight on August 15, 1947, along religious lines into two sovereign dominions, India and Pakistan, under the oversight of the final viceroy, *Lord Mountbatten*, and boundary commissioner *Cyril Radcliffe*.
- Triggered massive anti-colonial resistance through the *Indian National Congress* led by *Mahatma Gandhi* and *Jawaharlal Nehru*, as well as the *All-India Muslim League* headed by *Muhammad Ali Jinnah*.
- Exposed the violent underpinnings of colonial rule during the 1919 *Jallianwala Bagh massacre* in Amritsar, where troops under *Reginald Dyer* fired on unarmed protestors assembling against the repressive *Rowlatt Acts*.
- Reorganized princely states through the doctrine of subsidiary alliances, directly administering approximately three-fifths of the territory while exercising suzerainty over hundreds of internal autonomous native rulers.
- Deployed imperial armed forces, including the native *British Indian Army*, across global fronts in [[world-war-i]] and [[world-war-ii]], while requisitioning massive domestic grain supplies that exacerbated the catastrophic 1943 *Bengal famine*.
- Relocated its imperial capital in 1911 from Calcutta to the purpose-built administrative city of New Delhi, designed by architects *Edwin Lutyens* and *Herbert Baker*.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — the overarching global imperial system for which the subcontinent served as the economic and military anchor.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader ideological and economic apparatus of territorial domination exemplified by Crown rule in South Asia.
- [[british-military]] — the institutional arm that maintained internal coercion and drew heavily upon sepoy regiments for overseas projection.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflict in which over one million Indian soldiers deployed abroad, intensifying post-war demands for self-determination.
- [[world-war-ii]] — worldwide conflagration that devastated British imperial finances and precipitated the rapid transfer of power in 1947.
- [[british-mandate]] — parallel imperial governance framework administered in the Near East during the interwar era using administrative methods honed in the subcontinent.
- [[england]] — the imperial metropole that directed colonial commerce, civil service recruitment, and judicial appeals through London.
