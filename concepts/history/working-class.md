## summary

The **working class** (or **Proletariat**) refers to people who earn wages by selling their labor rather than owning productive capital—factory workers, miners, farmhands, and service workers whose collective muscle and skill powered the *Industrial Revolution* and modern economies. Defined socially by their economic dependence and subordination to employers, the working class became the revolutionary agent in *Marxist* theory and the political base of labor movements and socialist parties throughout the 19th and 20th centuries. Though materially better-off in wealthy nations today, class divisions remain fundamental to how wealth, power, and culture are distributed.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a distinct class during industrialization: *enclosure movements* displaced peasants, driving mass migration to factory towns where workers sold labor by the hour.
- Worked in dangerous, unsanitary conditions for subsistence wages; child labor, 12-hour shifts, and industrial injuries were routine until reform movements intervened.
- Developed collective consciousness through the *labor-movement*: trade unions, strikes, and political organizing gave workers bargaining power against employers.
- *Karl Marx* theorized that capitalist exploitation of workers would inevitably generate class conflict and revolution—the working class as history's liberating force.
- Different from the historical *peasantry*: workers in industrial societies were atomized, concentrated in cities, and dependent on cash wages rather than land.
- Supported the rise of *socialist* and *communist* parties (e.g., the *Labour Party* in Britain) that explicitly claimed to represent working-class interests.
- Cultural identity: working-class communities developed distinct dialects, music, literature, and values—from *jazz* in New Orleans to *punk-rock* in 1970s London.
