## summary

The **Fifth Amendment** to the United States Constitution (ratified 1791 as part of the *Bill of Rights*) protects individuals against self-incrimination and the deprivation of life, liberty, or property without due process of law. It grants grand jury protections, double jeopardy prohibitions, and eminent domain limits. The Fifth Amendment stands as one of the most powerful shields against state power in American law.

## you gotta know

- The Self-Incrimination Clause ("No person... shall be compelled in any criminal case to be a witness against himself") is its most recognizable provision, invoked when witnesses "take the Fifth."
- The *Due Process Clause* ("nor be deprived of life, liberty, or property, without due process of law") has spawned centuries of constitutional litigation over the scope of fundamental rights.
- The *Double Jeopardy* prohibition prevents prosecution twice for the same offense, ensuring finality in criminal adjudication.
- The *Grand Jury* requirement (in federal felony cases) mandates that serious charges be reviewed by citizens before prosecution proceeds.
- The *Takings Clause* ("nor shall private property be taken for public use, without just compensation") limits eminent domain and has evolved to address environmental and zoning disputes.
- *Miranda v. Arizona* (1966) operationalized the Fifth Amendment's protections by requiring police to inform suspects of their right to remain silent and have counsel.
- The amendment has been selectively incorporated into the Fourteenth Amendment, applying most (though not all) of its protections to the states.
