## summary

**Ancient literature**, particularly the corpus of **ancient Near Eastern literature**, encompasses the earliest recorded creative, mythological, and philosophical writings in human history, originating in the river valleys of [[mesopotamia]]. Inscribed in cuneiform script across clay tablets beginning in the third millennium BCE, these texts established foundational narrative forms ranging from cosmological poetry and royal hymns to legal codes and existential epics. Preserved through the scribal traditions of [[sumerian-civilization]], the [[akkadian-empire]], and the [[assyrian-empire]], these seminal works profoundly shaped the later literary, religious, and philosophical frameworks of the [[middle-east]], [[judaism]], and [[ancient-greece]].

## you gotta know

- *The Epic of Gilgamesh* is the most celebrated epic of antiquity, narrating the heroic exploits of the king of Uruk alongside his companion *Enkidu*, their battle against the cedar guardian *Humbaba*, and Gilgamesh's subsequent search for immortality from the flood survivor *Utnapishtim*.
- *Enheduanna*, high priestess of the moon god *Nanna* at Ur and daughter of *Sargon of Akkad*, is recognised as the earliest named individual author in recorded history, celebrated for composing the *Exaltation of Inanna* (*Nin-me-sar-ra*) and an influential cycle of temple hymns.
- *The Enuma Elish*, the Babylonian creation myth recited annually during the *Akitu* New Year festival, details the elevation of the patron deity *Marduk*, who slays the primordial chaos dragon *Tiamat* and constructs the heavens and earth from her severed body.
- The royal library of *Ashurbanipal* at Nineveh, rediscovered in the nineteenth century, preserved tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets, including the twelve-tablet Standard Babylonian edition of the Gilgamesh epic compiled by the priest-scholar *Sin-leqi-unninni*.
- *The Atrahasis Epic* recounts the creation of humankind from clay mixed with the blood of a slaughtered god to relieve the deities of toil, followed by the storm god *Enlil* attempting to annihilate mankind through disease, drought, and a great deluge.
- *The Descent of Ishtar to the Netherworld*, paralleling the older Sumerian poem concerning *Inanna*, recounts the love goddess journeying to confront her sister *Ereshkigal*, submitting to having her garments and jewels removed at seven successive gates of the underworld.
- Sumerian and Akkadian wisdom literature, including the didactic *Instructions of Shuruppak* and the satirical *Dialogue of Pessimism*, established enduring genres of father-to-son counsel, proverbs, and philosophical disputations on the problem of human suffering.

## connections

- [[mesopotamia]] — the river valley heartland where the invention of cuneiform script on clay tablets inaugurated written literature and historical recordkeeping.
- [[sumerian-civilization]] — the southern civilization that developed the cuneiform writing system, the earliest heroic cycles of *Bilgames*, and pioneering liturgies.
- [[akkadian-empire]] — the Semitic power established by *Sargon* whose scribes patronised *Enheduanna* and adapted Sumerian literary forms into East Semitic poetry.
- [[assyrian-empire]] — the northern realm whose monarch *Ashurbanipal* systematically gathered, copied, and catalogued thousands of ancient cuneiform texts in his Nineveh library.
- [[middle-east]] — the broader cultural sphere whose scribal traditions fostered shared motifs of primeval floods, dynastic legitimisation, and royal law codes.
- [[poetry]] — the primary rhythmic, formulaic art form utilized by ancient bards and scribes to memorize and transmit mythological and devotional texts.
- [[judaism]] — the Levantine religious tradition whose scripture features striking cosmological and thematic affinities with Mesopotamian flood narratives and law collections.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the Mediterranean civilization whose epic poets and mythographers adapted Near Eastern motifs of succession myths, underworld journeys, and divine assemblies.
