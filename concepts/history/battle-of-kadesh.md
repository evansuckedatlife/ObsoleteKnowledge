## summary

The **Battle of Kadesh** (also known as the **Battle of Qadesh**) was a monumental clash fought in *1274 BC* between the New Kingdom of [[egypt]], led by Pharaoh *Ramesses II*, and the Hittite Empire under King *Muwatalli II*. Taking place along the *Orontes River* near the Syrian fortress of *Kadesh* in the ancient [[middle-east]], the confrontation is widely celebrated as the largest chariot engagement in military history, fielding an estimated five to six thousand chariots. Although tactical deception and sudden ambushes yielded a bloody stalemate rather than the decisive triumph claimed by both monarchs, the conflict reshaped regional diplomacy and directly precipitated the *Eternal Treaty*—recognized as the earliest surviving international peace treaty in world history.

## you gotta know

- Widely renowned for culminating in the *Treaty of Kadesh*, concluded sixteen years later between *Ramesses II* and *Hattusili III* to establish mutual non-aggression, an official replica of which is displayed at the United Nations headquarters in [[new-york-city]].
- Considered the largest chariot clash ever fought in antiquity, pitting nimble two-man Egyptian reconnaissance platforms against heavier three-man Hittite shock chariots.
- *Ramesses II* was severely deceived by captured *Shasu* Bedouin spies secretly serving *Muwatalli II*, who falsely claimed the Hittite army was far away near *Aleppo*, prompting the pharaoh to advance with only the division of *Amun*.
- Concealed behind the tell of *Kadesh*, the Hittite army launched a surprise flanking charge that routed the marching Egyptian division of *Ra*, sweeping directly into the royal camp before defenses could be formed.
- Facing encirclement, *Ramesses II* rallied his household guard and led desperate countercharges against the Hittites, who had broken formation to plunder the Egyptian encampment.
- The Egyptian force was saved from destruction by the timely arrival of the *Ne'arin*, an elite coastal reserve corps that struck the disordered Hittites from the rear and drove them into the *Orontes River*.
- Egyptian propaganda celebrated the clash as a personal triumph through the literary *Poem of Pentaur* and the official *Bulletin*, carved onto monumental temple walls at *Karnak*, *Luxor*, *Abu Simbel*, and the *Ramesseum*.
- Modern historians view the outcome as an Egyptian tactical survival but a strategic Hittite victory, as the Hittites maintained control of *Kadesh* and halted Egyptian expansion into Syria.
