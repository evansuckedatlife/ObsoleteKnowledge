## summary

The **Executive Branch** is one of the three coordinate powers of the U.S. federal government, headed by the President and charged with enforcing the laws Congress enacts. It comprises the President, Cabinet, federal agencies, and bureaucracy that carry out the nation's day-to-day governance. The separation of this power from legislative and judicial authority is foundational to American constitutional design.

## you gotta know

- Headed by the *President*, who is commander-in-chief of the armed forces and chief executive officer of the federal government.
- Includes the *Cabinet*, a group of department heads (State, Defense, Treasury, Interior, etc.) who advise the President and manage major administrative functions.
- Enforces federal laws, negotiates treaties (subject to Senate ratification), and appoints federal judges and senior officials.
- Subject to checks by Congress (which can override vetoes, impeach, and control funding) and the courts (which review executive action for constitutionality).
- The President can veto legislation, invoke executive orders, and delegate authority to agencies and subordinates.
- Grew dramatically in size and scope during the 20th century, particularly under *Franklin D. Roosevelt* during the Depression and World War II.
- Includes the President's staff (the White House Office) and specialized agencies like the Office of Management and Budget that advise and support executive decision-making.
- Presidents have assumed broader powers over time through executive orders, national emergencies, and military actions, sometimes sparking constitutional disputes with Congress.
- Modern presidents command vast bureaucratic apparatus managing everything from social security to environmental regulation, making the Executive Branch the largest of the three branches in actual staffing.

## connections

- [[separation-of-powers]] — the constitutional principle that distributes federal authority among three branches.
- [[checks-and-balances]] — the system by which each branch constrains the others; Congress checks executive power through oversight and appropriation.
- [[legislative-branch]] — the co-equal branch that makes law; the President must work with Congress to govern.
- [[judicial-branch]] — the courts that review executive action and decide constitutional limits on presidential power.
- [[president]] — the elected head of the Executive Branch.
- [[impeachment]] — the House power to charge a President with "high crimes and misdemeanors."
