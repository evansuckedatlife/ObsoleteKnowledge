## summary

The **Iowa-class** battleships were the largest and most powerful battleships ever built by any nation, representing the culmination of battleship design. Built primarily during World War II, four ships of the class—USS Iowa, USS New Jersey, USS Missouri, and USS Wisconsin—served as the capital ships of the American Navy. These vessels combined massive firepower, heavy armor, and unprecedented speed, embodying the height of 20th-century naval technology before the carrier became the dominant warship.

## you gotta know

- Displaced 45,000 tons fully loaded, making them the heaviest battleships in operation; each carried nine 16-inch guns as primary armament capable of firing 2,700-pound shells over 20 miles—the most powerful naval artillery of their era.
- Featured a revolutionary hull design allowing speeds of 33 knots (38 mph), making them the fastest battleships of their era; this "fast battleship" design allowed them to operate with carrier task forces rather than as separate battle lines.
- Entered service between 1943 and 1944 (USS Iowa, New Jersey, Missouri, Wisconsin), arriving late enough to see limited combat service in the Pacific but carrying significant firepower in fleet operations and shore bombardment.
- [[USS Missouri]] is the most famous of the class, serving as the site of the formal Japanese surrender ceremony in September 1945 in Tokyo Bay; the ship became a symbol of American naval supremacy and remains a tourist attraction today.
- Post-World War II, the class was extensively modernized with upgraded missiles, fire-control systems, and sensors; they continued service through the *Cold War* as symbols of American naval might, with some remaining active into the 1990s (USS Wisconsin and Missouri served in the 1991 Persian Gulf War).
- Combined the traditional big-gun firepower of the battleship (which battleship enthusiasts valued for psychological impact and survivability) with modern radar, fire-control systems, guided missiles, and anti-aircraft armament—embodying the transition from gun-based to missile-based naval warfare.
- Represented an enormous investment of resources during wartime (each cost $100+ million in 1940s dollars); their construction demonstrated American industrial capacity, naval ambition, and the belief that battleships would remain the dominant warship—though carrier warfare proved this assumption wrong.
