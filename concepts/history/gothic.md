## summary

The **Gothic** identity originally designated the **Goths**, an influential confederation of East Germanic peoples who played a pivotal role in the collapse of the Western [[roman-empire]] and the transformation of the Mediterranean world. Migrating from the Baltic basin toward the Black Sea, they divided into the *Visigoths* and *Ostrogoths*, alternately serving as Roman federates and military antagonists. After crossing the Danube under pressure from the *Huns*, their sack of [[rome]] in 410 CE under *Alaric I* shattered the illusion of imperial invincibility and led to the establishment of durable post-Roman kingdoms in [[italy]] and [[spain]].

## you gotta know

- Divided historically into two primary confederations: the *Visigoths* (Western Goths), who founded an enduring realm in [[spain]] and southwestern [[france]], and the *Ostrogoths* (Eastern Goths), who established a kingdom in [[italy]].
- Dealt the [[roman-empire]] one of its most catastrophic military defeats at the Battle of *Adrianople* in 378 CE, where Gothic heavy cavalry and infantry crushed the imperial army and killed Emperor *Valens*.
- Sacked the city of [[rome]] in 410 CE under the Visigothic king *Alaric I*, an event that shocked the ancient Mediterranean world and prompted *Saint Augustine* to compose *The City of God*.
- Converted early to *Arianism*, a non-Nicene branch of [[christianity]], largely through the missionary efforts of Bishop *Ulfilas*, who created a dedicated alphabet to translate the Bible into the Gothic language.
- Reached an artistic and administrative zenith in [[italy]] under the Ostrogothic monarch *Theodoric the Great*, who preserved classical Roman law, urban infrastructure, and administrative traditions from his capital at Ravenna.
- Suffered imperial subjugation and dispersion during Emperor *Justinian I*'s destructive *Gothic War* (535–554 CE), which devastated [[italy]] and eliminated the Ostrogothic kingdom.
- The term was repurposed pejoratively during the [[renaissance]] by Italian humanists like *Giorgio Vasari* to denigrate medieval pointed-arch architecture as barbaric, later inspiring 18th-century [[gothic-literature]].

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the classical Mediterranean superpower whose western provinces were transformed and partitioned by migrating Gothic confederations.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization whose eastern Roman territories in Thrace and Greece were pillaged by Gothic incursions before the march on the West.
- [[christianity]] — the faith adopted by the Goths in its Arian theological form, generating long-standing religious tension with orthodox Nicene subjects.
- [[italy]] — the central Mediterranean peninsula governed by *Theodoric the Great* from Ravenna before being ravaged during the campaigns of *Belisarius*.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian region where the Visigothic kingdom established its royal capital at Toledo, lasting until the Umayyad conquest in 711 CE.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the late-eighteenth-century literary movement that drew romantic atmospheric inspiration from medieval ruins and supernatural horror.
- [[glorious-revolution]] — the 1688 English settlement celebrated by Whig historians who romanticized ancient Gothic freedom and representative assemblies as roots of parliamentary liberty.
