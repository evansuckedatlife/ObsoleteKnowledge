## summary

**Black Power** was a political, cultural, and social movement that emerged in the *[[united-states]]* during the mid-1960s as a major ideological shift within the broader *[[civil-rights-movement]]*. Popularized by organizers who grew disillusioned with nonviolent direct action and slow legal reform, the movement emphasized racial pride, economic self-sufficiency, and community autonomy. By prioritizing self-determination, institution-building, and armed self-defense against racial violence, it permanently reshaped African American politics and cultural consciousness.

## you gotta know

- Popularized nationally during the 1966 *March Against Fear* in Mississippi, when *Stokely Carmichael* of the *Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee* (*SNCC*) led crowds in chanting the slogan.
- Dramatically showcased on the global stage at the 1968 Summer Olympics in Mexico City, where American medalists *Tommie Smith* and *John Carlos* raised black-gloved fists atop the victory podium.
- Embodied by the *Black Panther Party for Self-Defense*, founded in Oakland in 1966 by *Huey P. Newton* and *Bobby Seale*, who organized armed neighborhood patrols and community survival programs such as free breakfasts for schoolchildren.
- Formulated in philosophical opposition to the strict integrationist, church-led nonviolence championed by *[[martin-luther-king-jr]]*, drawing deep ideological inspiration instead from the nationalist speeches of *Malcolm X*.
- Generated the vibrant *Black Arts Movement*, an aesthetic renaissance led by figures such as *Amiri Baraka* that celebrated African heritage and revolutionary pride through *[[poetry]]*, theater, and literature.
- Targeted aggressively by the *Federal Bureau of Investigation* (*FBI*) under its counterintelligence operation *COINTELPRO*, which sought to disrupt organizations, sow internal factional strife, and assassinate or imprison prominent leaders like *Fred Hampton*.
- Connected domestic racial oppression to global struggles against *[[colonialism]]*, establishing direct solidarity with liberation struggles in post-colonial Africa, Asia, and Latin America.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the broader reform campaign from which this radical faction broke over tactical and philosophical divides.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — the celebrated nonviolent leader whose integrationist approach was challenged by younger, more militant advocates.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose systemic disenfranchisement and racial violence provoked the movement's radical turn.
- [[harlem-renaissance]] — the earlier literary and artistic awakening that established foundational themes of cultural pride and self-definition.
- [[colonialism]] — the international system of imperial subjugation that organizers cited as the worldwide counterpart to domestic racial oppression.
- [[cold-war]] — the international geopolitical rivalry during which American intelligence agencies surveilled radical domestic activists as potential subversives.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional guarantee of equal protection whose gradual enforcement spurred demands for collective autonomy.
