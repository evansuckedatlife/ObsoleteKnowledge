## summary

The **Wars of the Roses** (also called the **War of the Roses**) were a series of fifteenth-century civil wars fought between 1455 and 1487 for control of the throne of *England*. The conflict pitted two rival cadet branches of the royal *House of Plantagenet* against each other: the *House of Lancaster*, traditionally symbolized by the red rose, and the *House of York*, represented by the white rose. Decades of violent factionalism, sudden dynastic usurpations, and shifting aristocratic loyalties culminated in the death of King *Richard III* at the *Battle of Bosworth Field* in 1485. The triumph of the Lancastrian claimant *Henry Tudor* established the *Tudor dynasty*, effectively extinguishing the direct male Plantagenet line and inaugurating the early modern era of centralized English monarchical governance.

## you gotta know

- Fought between the rival *House of Lancaster* and *House of York*, whose heraldic badges of the red and white rose inspired the conflict's modern name, largely popularized centuries later by *William Shakespeare* and Sir *Walter Scott*.
- Ended decisively on the battlefield at the *Battle of Bosworth Field* in 1485, where *Richard III* was slain in combat and the victorious *Henry Tudor* ascended as King *Henry VII*, uniting the factions by marrying *Elizabeth of York* and creating the iconic *Tudor Rose*.
- Haunted by the infamous disappearance of the *Princes in the Tower*, the young King *Edward V* and his brother *Richard, Duke of York*, who vanished from the *Tower of London* after their uncle seized the throne as *Richard III*.
- Dominated during its middle phase by *Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick*, known as *Warwick the Kingmaker*, who utilized his vast military wealth to depose the Lancastrian *Henry VI* in favor of the Yorkist *Edward IV*, before defecting back to Lancaster to briefly reinstate *Henry VI* during the *Readeption*.
- Sparked initially by the weak rule and mental incapacitation of King *Henry VI*, whose catastrophic bouts of catatonia led to a bitter power struggle between his formidable queen, *Margaret of Anjou*, and *Richard, Duke of York*, who had served as *Lord Protector*.
- Produced extraordinary bloodshed at the *Battle of Towton* in 1461, where an estimated twenty-eight thousand soldiers perished amidst a blinding snowstorm, making it the deadliest single-day battle ever fought on English soil.
- Concluded in military terms at the often-overlooked *Battle of Stoke Field* in 1487, where *Henry VII* crushed the final major Yorkist armed uprising, which had rallied behind the pretender *Lambert Simnel*.
