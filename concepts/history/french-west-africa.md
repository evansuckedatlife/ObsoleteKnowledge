## summary

**French West Africa** was a colonial federation of eight French territories in West Africa, administered as a single entity from 1895 to 1960. This vast administrative bloc served France's strategic and economic interests by centralizing control over resource extraction, military recruitment, and trade, while imposing French cultural and political systems on diverse indigenous populations.

## you gotta know

- Officially known as *Afrique Occidentale Française* (AOF); established through French colonial expansion and military conquest in the late 19th century.
- Comprised eight territories: Mauritania, Senegal, French Guinea, Mali (French Sudan), Ivory Coast, Burkina Faso (Upper Volta), Dahomey (Benin), and Niger.
- Ruled from *Dakar*, *Senegal*, with a French governor-general administering colonial policy across linguistically and culturally distinct regions.
- The administrative structure centralized control: a single governor-general oversaw all territories from Dakar, subordinating regional chiefs and eliminating indigenous governance systems.
- Extracted wealth through agricultural production (peanuts, cotton), forced labor conscription, and military service imposed on colonized populations.
- Implemented the *mission civilisatrice* (civilizing mission) ideology: mandatory French education, suppression of local languages, and imposition of French legal codes.
- Formed the headquarters of French colonial Africa and a model for administrative centralization that privileged French language, law, and culture.
- The *Four Communes* of Senegal (Saint-Louis, Gorée, Dakar, Rufisque) enjoyed limited voting rights and representation, creating a French-educated African elite that later led independence movements.
- Economic integration tied the federation to French markets; French companies extracted raw materials while the colonies purchased manufactured French goods at inflated prices.
- Dissolved between 1958 and 1960 as independence movements swept West Africa, with most territories becoming independent nations within a decade.
- The federation's dissolution reflected both nationalist resistance and France's shift toward *la Communauté Française*, attempting to maintain control through looser association.
- Left enduring legacies: French as official language, administrative structures modeled on French systems, economic dependencies on former colonizer, and ongoing disputes over language and postcolonial identity.
