## summary

**Systemic racism**, also known as **institutional racism**, refers to the policies, practices, and cultural norms embedded within societal structures that systematically disadvantage racial minorities while privileging the dominant racial group. Formulated prominently during the 1960s civil rights era in the [[united-states]], the concept shifts the analysis of racial inequality from individual prejudice to entrenched legal, economic, and governmental systems. Understanding systemic racism is vital in historical and political study for explaining how vast disparities in generational wealth, housing, healthcare, criminal justice, and political enfranchisement endure long after the formal abolition of explicit discriminatory statutes like slavery and *Jim Crow*.

## you gotta know

- Popularized as a formal analytical framework by civil rights activist *Stokely Carmichael* (later *Kwame Ture*) and political scientist *Charles V. Hamilton* in their landmark 1967 text *Black Power: The Politics of Liberation in America*.
- Endorsed by the federal government's *Kerner Commission* in 1968, which investigated nationwide urban unrest during the [[civil-rights-movement]] and famously concluded that white societal institutions were directly responsible for creating and maintaining impoverished Black ghettos.
- Perpetuated economically through twentieth-century federal housing policies such as redlining, where the Home Owners' Loan Corporation and the Federal Housing Administration systematically denied mortgage backing to minority neighborhoods, locking generations of Black citizens out of postwar suburban property equity.
- Rooted historically in the post-[[reconstruction-era]] institutional backlash, where Southern legislatures enacted *Black Codes* and *Jim Crow* ordinances designed to circumvent the [[14th-amendment]] through convict leasing, poll taxes, literacy tests, and enforced racial segregation.
- Reinforced by criminal justice policies throughout the late twentieth century, notably mandatory minimum sentencing and the severe statutory disparity between crack and powder cocaine established in 1986, which drove disproportionate rates of mass incarceration.
- Persisted within public education through de facto residential segregation and localized property tax funding models, maintaining deep resource disparities between school districts decades after the Supreme Court's ruling in *Brown v. Board of Education*.
- Evidenced in environmental policy as environmental racism, a phenomenon formally brought to national attention by the 1987 United Church of Christ report *Toxic Wastes and Race*, which documented the disproportionate placement of hazardous waste sites and polluting industrial facilities in minority communities.

## connections

- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-Civil War period whose political collapse enabled Southern state governments to construct institutionalized segregation and voter suppression.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the mass mobilization that dismantled de jure segregation through landmark federal legislation while confronting entrenched structural oppression.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional amendment providing equal protection and due process, which formed the primary judicial foundation for challenging discriminatory state policies.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — civil rights leader whose later initiatives, including the Poor People's Campaign, transitioned from opposing legal segregation to directly combating economic and systemic inequality.
- [[colonialism]] — the worldwide structure of territorial domination and economic exploitation that formulated global racial hierarchies to justify subjugating indigenous and enslaved populations.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the sectional conflict that destroyed the institution of chattel slavery, after which institutional racism evolved into new legal and economic mechanisms.
- [[united-states]] — the constitutional republic whose federal, state, and private institutions historically codified and perpetuated racial disparities.
