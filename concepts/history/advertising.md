## summary

**Advertising** is the commercial practice of using media—print, radio, television, and later digital channels—to promote products and influence consumer behavior. As mass production created surplus goods in the early 20th century, advertising evolved from simple notices into a sophisticated industry of persuasion. It became central to American capitalism, shaping culture, values, and consumption patterns while generating enormous wealth for publishers and ad agencies.

## you gotta know

- Advertising emerged in modern form during the Industrial Revolution; printed newspapers and magazines provided media for commercial messages, but scale was limited until mass circulation grew in the late 1800s.
- The rise of department stores and packaged goods created demand for mass advertising; companies like *Procter & Gamble* pioneered brand-building through repeated commercial messaging.
- The early 20th century saw advertising professionalize into an industry; ad agencies like *J. Walter Thompson* and *Ogilvy & Mather* employed creative strategists who scientifically targeted demographics.
- Print media—newspapers, magazines like *The Saturday Evening Post*—became dependent on advertising revenue; advertisers gained enormous influence over editorial content and cultural messaging.
- Radio (1920s–1940s) and television (1950s onward) became advertising's primary vehicles; sponsored programs and commercial breaks made broadcasting profitable and shaped the medium's content.
- Mid-20th-century advertising became increasingly psychological; advertisers studied consumer motivations and deployed Freudian imagery to link products to subconscious desires and status.
- Advertising created the concept of *consumer culture*—the notion that happiness and identity were purchasable commodities; it fundamentally transformed Americans from producers to consumers and normalized the practice of continuous consumption as a way of life.
