## summary

The **Imperial Japanese Army** was the military ground force of *Imperial Japan*, operating from the *Meiji Restoration* through World War II. Organized around samurai traditions, bushido ethics, and nationalist ideology, it became one of the war's most powerful and brutal forces. The army committed widespread atrocities and maintained a fighting spirit that prolonged the Pacific War even as Japanese defeat became inevitable.

## you gotta know

- Originated in the Meiji era as a conscript-based, Western-trained institution that combined European military science with samurai honor codes, creating a unique martial tradition emphasizing duty, loyalty, and honor unto death.
- Became increasingly politicized in the 1920s and 1930s, with radical factions plotting coups and pushing aggressive foreign policy; military cliques gradually gained control over civilian government, marginalizing democratic institutions.
- Conquered vast territory in China and Manchuria, establishing the puppet state of Manchukuo and fighting the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945), which killed an estimated 20 million Chinese civilians and soldiers.
- Known for extreme brutality, including mass atrocities like the *Nanjing Massacre* (1937), where Japanese soldiers killed 300,000 Chinese civilians and prisoners in weeks, and systematic torture, rape, and abuse of prisoners of war from multiple nations.
- Fought major Pacific campaigns including [[Guadalcanal]] (1942–1943), [[Iwo-Jima]] (1945), and [[Okinawa]] (1945), inflicting heavy casualties on Allied forces but suffering devastating defeats as American industrial capacity overwhelmed Japanese resources.
- Organized suicide-attack units (kamikaze), embodying the code that surrender was dishonorable and death was preferable; over 3,000 kamikaze pilots died in deliberate suicide attacks against Allied ships in the final year of the war.
- Totaled approximately 3 million military deaths during World War II; Japanese and occupied territories' civilian casualties (from combat, disease, starvation, forced labor) far exceeded military deaths, with historians estimating 10–20 million deaths across Asia.
