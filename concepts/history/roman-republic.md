## summary

The **Roman Republic** was the political system that governed Rome from approximately 509 BCE to 27 BCE, creating the world's first large-scale representative government with elected magistrates, a Senate of aristocratic families, and complex constitutional mechanisms. It expanded Rome from a city-state to master of the Mediterranean, accumulated vast wealth, and developed legal and governmental structures that profoundly influenced Western democracy. The Republic's internal contradictions—between patrician and plebeian classes, between republic and military strongmen—ultimately destabilized it, collapsing into civil war and empire.

## you gotta know

- Established after the overthrow of the last king, Tarquinius Superbus, in 509 BCE, establishing an aristocratic government based on the Senate and magistrates (consuls, tribunes, praetors).
- Developed early democratic institutions including the assemblies where citizens voted, the tribunes representing plebeian interests, and laws preventing any magistrate from holding unchecked power.
- Expanded through conquest and alliance, gradually absorbing Italy, then defeating Carthage in the Punic Wars, finally dominating the entire Mediterranean and Near East.
- Created a mixed constitution balancing monarchy (consuls), aristocracy (Senate), and democracy (assemblies)—a model later admired by Montesquieu and incorporated into American government.
- Suffered increasing internal conflict between factions, escalating civil wars, and the rise of military strongmen (Pompey, Caesar, Octavian) whose power undermined republican institutions.
- Produced extraordinary literary and philosophical achievement, including Cicero's orations, Livy's histories, and Vergil's Aeneid, which celebrated and questioned republican ideals.
- Formally ended in 27 BCE when Octavian, as Augustus, received the title of princeps and consolidated power, creating the Roman Empire while maintaining republican forms.
