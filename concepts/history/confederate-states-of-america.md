## summary

The **Confederate States of America** was a short-lived nation formed by eleven Southern U.S. states that seceded from the Union in 1860–1861 in response to the election of *Abraham Lincoln* and Northern attempts to restrict slavery's expansion. Lasting only four years (1861–1865), the Confederacy built its political identity and economy around slavery and state sovereignty, fought the American Civil War against the Union, and was ultimately defeated and dissolved. The attempt to create a permanent slave-holding nation ended the institution of slavery in North America and fundamentally redefined the relationship between federal and state power in the United States.

## you gotta know

- Formed after Lincoln's election in November 1860 by seven Deep South states (South Carolina, Mississippi, Florida, Alabama, Georgia, Louisiana, and Texas) that seceded from the Union; four Upper South states (Virginia, Arkansas, Tennessee, and North Carolina) joined after war began at *Fort Sumter*.
- Adopted a provisional constitution in February 1861 that explicitly protected slavery and emphasized state sovereignty; elected *Jefferson Davis* as president (provisional, then permanent) and *Alexander Stephens* as vice president.
- Declared its founding principle to be slavery: Vice President Stephens stated that the Confederacy was built upon the "great truth that the negro is not equal to the white man; that slavery, subordination to the superior race, is his natural and normal condition."
- Attempted to secure international recognition from Britain and France, hoping that cotton exports would compel foreign powers to recognize the Confederacy as a legitimate nation; this strategy ultimately failed.
- Built a conscript army (the Confederate Army) and attempted to sustain a war effort despite severe disadvantages in population, industrial capacity, and transportation infrastructure.
- Suffered from chronic political divisions between state governments (which jealously guarded sovereignty) and central authority, weakening unified war effort and economic mobilization.
- Experienced severe inflation, currency collapse, and economic breakdown as Union blockades strangled trade and agricultural production, combined with massive military expenditures.
- Collapsed militarily between April and May 1865 as Union armies penetrated the heartland; Robert E. Lee surrendered in April, effectively ending the Confederacy as a political entity.
- Was formally dissolved and the Southern states were placed under military Reconstruction (1865–1877); the 13th Amendment, ratified in December 1865, abolished slavery nationwide.
