## summary

**James Longstreet** (1821–1904) was the *Confederacy's* most capable general and *Robert E. Lee's* closest and most trusted lieutenant, earning the nickname "Old Warhorse" for his steadiness in battle and sound counsel. Known for his tactical conservatism and deep skepticism of aggressive offensives, he often clashed with Lee over strategic decisions but proved invaluable in defensive operations. After the war, his acceptance of Reconstruction and political alliances with Republicans made him deeply controversial in the South; he lived out his years as a U.S. diplomat.

## you gotta know

- A West Point graduate (1842) with significant Mexican-American War experience, a trusted and well-respected officer in the pre-war regular army.
- Served as Lee's primary subordinate and most trusted corps commander for much of the *American Civil War*, fighting in major campaigns including *Antietam* and *Gettysburg*.
- Advocated defensive strategy at *Gettysburg*, opposing the assaults that weakened Confederate forces; after the battle, he supported a retreat.
- At *Chickamauga* (1863), his reinforcements and aggressive counterattack briefly achieved a Confederate victory, one of the South's last significant triumphs.
- Survived multiple severe wounds and combat injuries throughout the war but was temporarily incapacitated by friendly fire at the *Wilderness*.
- After the war, became a Republican and supporter of Reconstruction, which made him reviled by former Confederate colleagues and lost him popularity in the white South.
- Served in Republican administrations as a diplomat and Indian Affairs official, deliberately associating with the party of Lincoln and Union victory.
- His willingness to accept defeat and move forward earned him respect in Northern circles but permanent enmity from unreconstructed Southerners, making him a controversial figure long after his death.
