## summary

**Harry Thaw** was a wealthy American coal and railroad heir from Pittsburgh whose 1906 murder of premier architect *Stanford White* at Madison Square Garden ignited one of the first modern media circuses in the [[united-states]]. Consumed by pathological jealousy over White's previous sexual relationship with his young wife, the Gibson Girl model and actress *Evelyn Nesbit*, Thaw shot White point-blank during a musical theatre performance in [[new-york-city]]. His sensational court battles introduced pioneering psychiatric defenses of temporary insanity and exposed the decadent excesses of high society during the American Gilded Age.

## you gotta know

- Shot and killed famed architect *Stanford White* on June 25, 1906, on the rooftop theatre of the old Madison Square Garden—a building White himself had designed—during a performance of *Mam'zelle Champagne*.
- Uttered the dramatic declaration, "You've ruined my life," or "You have ruined my wife," as he discharged three revolver shots directly into White's face in front of hundreds of wealthy theatregoers.
- Married the prominent teenage artist's model and chorus girl *Evelyn Nesbit* in 1905, becoming obsessed with extracting detailed confessions about her seduction by White when she was sixteen.
- Tried twice in high-profile courtroom spectacles dubbed the original "Trial of the Century," heavily sensationalized by the yellow journalism of *William Randolph Hearst*'s newspapers.
- Escaped the electric chair after defense attorney *Delphin Delmas* coined the legal term "brainstorm" to describe a temporary, manic loss of reason that rendered him legally insane.
- Committed to the Matteawan State Hospital for the Criminally Insane in Fishkill, New York, from which he made a daring escape across the border to Canada in 1913 before being extradited.
- Inherited a massive family fortune originating in Pennsylvania coal mines and the Pennsylvania Railroad, enabling his family to spend hundreds of thousands of dollars on his legal defenses and corrupt confinement privileges.
- Re-evaluated in popular culture as a principal character in *E. L. Doctorow*'s historical novel *Ragtime*, which portrays his violent obsession alongside other iconic Gilded Age figures.
