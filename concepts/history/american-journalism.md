## summary

**American journalism** developed as a distinct institutional practice shaped by commercial printing, mass literacy, and democratic ideals, evolving from partisan pamphlets to professionalised newspapers and magazines. The nineteenth and twentieth centuries saw the rise of muckraking investigative reporting, literary magazines of serious criticism, and eventually a culture of journalistic objectivity intended to separate news from opinion. American journalism's ambitions and practices became a model for democratic accountability worldwide, though often constrained by economic pressures and political interests.

## you gotta know

- Started as party-aligned newspapers in the early Republic; the *First Amendment* theoretically protected press freedom while partisan rivalry and economic interests shaped editorial content and bias.
- *Yellow journalism* emerged in the 1890s, with sensationalist tactics like *Joseph Pulitzer*'s New York World and *William Randolph Hearst*'s Journal competing for readership through lurid headlines and crusading exposés.
- *Muckraking* journalism of the *Progressive Era* (1890s–1910s) exposed corporate corruption, labor abuses, and political machines, leading to regulatory reforms, antitrust legislation, and food safety laws.
- Literary magazines like *Harper's*, *The Atlantic*, and *The New Yorker* pioneered serious arts criticism, fiction publication, and long-form essays as journalism, elevating the cultural prestige of magazine writing.
- Radio and television news transformed journalism in the mid-twentieth century, creating national news broadcasts (NBC, CBS, ABC) and bringing current events directly into American homes nightly.
- Professional journalism schools, wire services (AP, Reuters, UPI), and ethics codes (SPJ) codified standards of accuracy, objectivity, and editorial independence that became the profession's aspirational ideals.
- Faced recurring challenges from sensationalism, bias, corporate ownership consolidation, and the tension between profit motives and commitment to public accountability and truth-telling.
