## summary

**William Howard Taft** (1857–1930) was the 27th President of the United States (1909–1913), serving a single term between two Theodore Roosevelt administrations. Originally hand-picked by *Roosevelt* as his successor, Taft disappointed Roosevelt by pursuing conservative policies and alienating the President's Progressive wing of the Republican party. Their bitter 1912 split—with Roosevelt running as a third-party candidate on the *Bull Moose* ticket—fractured the Republican vote and allowed Democrat *Woodrow Wilson* to win. Despite an unpopular presidency, Taft later served as Chief Justice of the United States (1921–1930), a role that proved his true calling.

## you gotta know

- Born in Cincinnati, Ohio; trained as a lawyer and judge; first gained prominence as governor-general of the Philippines following the Spanish-American War, implementing American colonial policy.
- *Theodore Roosevelt*, seeking a successor who would continue his policies while limiting executive power, handpicked Taft as the Republican presidential nominee in 1908; Taft won easily, as Roosevelt remained popular.
- Promised to continue Roosevelt's trust-busting agenda but proved more cautious and legalistic; filed more antitrust suits than Roosevelt but with less fanfare, earning a reputation for passivity.
- The firing of conservationist *Gifford Pinchot* in 1909 alienated Progressives who saw it as capitulation to conservative Republicans; the incident symbolized Taft's perceived abandonment of Roosevelt's reform agenda.
- Taft's conservative judicial philosophy clashed with Roosevelt's expansive executive power; he opposed Roosevelt's view that the presidency should do whatever was not explicitly forbidden.
- Roosevelt, disgusted with Taft's presidency, announced his 1912 return to politics; when Taft was renominated, Roosevelt bolted to form the *Progressive Party*, splitting the Republican vote.
- The 1912 three-way race between Taft (conservative Republican), Roosevelt (Progressive), and *Woodrow Wilson* (Democrat) resulted in Wilson's victory and the Republicans' descent into factionalism for a generation.
- After defeat, Taft moved to Yale Law School; in 1921, President *Warren Harding* appointed him Chief Justice, a position Taft considered his finest role and where he shaped constitutional jurisprudence for a decade.
