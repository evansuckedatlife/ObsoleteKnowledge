## summary

**Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson** (1824–1863) was a Confederate general during the [[american-civil-war]], renowned for his aggressive tactics, rapid movements, and unwavering devotion to [[robert-e-lee]]. Acquiring his famous nickname at the [[first-battle-of-bull-run]], Jackson became Lee's most trusted lieutenant and achieved legendary status through victories at Chancellorsville, the Shenandoah Valley campaigns, and numerous other engagements. His death from complications following accidental wounding at Chancellorsville (May 1863) marked a turning point in Confederate fortunes, removing the general Lee deemed most essential to Southern victory and weakening Confederate command structure at a critical moment.

## you gotta know

- Jackson's nickname "Stonewall" originated during the First Battle of Bull Run (1861), where his steadfast defensive position inspired Confederate confidence and became emblematic of his later reputation.
- Jackson's Shenandoah Valley campaigns (spring 1862) demonstrated his tactical brilliance: employing rapid marches, terrain knowledge, and surprise attacks to defeat three larger Union armies without being decisively defeated.
- He was one of the Confederacy's few accomplished offensive strategists and tacticians; his aggressive doctrines and lightning movements contrasted with more cautious Confederate generals.
- Jackson became [[robert-e-lee]]'s "right arm," trusted above all other commanders; Lee famously said after Jackson's wounding: "I have lost my right arm" and later "I have lost my left arm" (prophetic given the subsequent loss).
- Jackson maintained religious devotion throughout his military career, viewing himself as an instrument of Providence, leading to pious personal behavior and deep Christian conviction.
- He was shot accidentally by his own troops during the Battle of Chancellorsville while conducting a night reconnaissance; he died nine days later (May 1863), a severe blow to Confederate morale and strategy.

## connections

- [[robert-e-lee]] — the Confederate commander and Jackson's superior, who regarded Jackson as his indispensable military partner.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict in which Jackson rose to prominence as one of the Confederacy's most significant military figures.
- [[james-longstreet]] — fellow corps commander under Lee, though lacking Jackson's reputation for aggressive maneuvering.
- [[belle-boyd]] — the Confederate scout and spy who supported Jackson's operations with intelligence gathering.
- [[confederacy]] — the nation Jackson served, whose military fortunes depended heavily on his capabilities.
- [[battle-of-chickamauga]] — subsequent battle in which the absence of Jackson's command presence may have affected outcomes.
- [[virginia]] — Jackson's home state, where he conducted many of his famous campaigns and built his reputation.
