## summary

**Lake Trasimene** is a lake in central Italy that hosted one of the most devastating ambush victories in military history. In 217 BCE, *Hannibal* of Carthage crushed the Roman legions at this site, achieving his greatest tactical triumph through masterful use of terrain and troop positioning. The battle stands as a defining moment of the Second Punic War and a testament to Hannibal's genius for maneuver warfare.

## you gotta know

- Located in Umbria, central Italy, Lake Trasimene is the largest lake on the Italian peninsula, providing Hannibal with narrow passes and terrain advantages.
- *Hannibal* lured the Roman army under *Gaius Flaminius* into a devastating ambush on the lake's shore in 217 BCE during his invasion of Italy.
- The Roman forces suffered one of their worst defeats, with approximately 15,000 soldiers killed compared to roughly 1,500 Carthaginian casualties, making it one of history's most lopsided defeats.
- Hannibal's victory demonstrated his mastery of combined-arms tactics: concealing infantry in hills and using cavalry to encircle the enemy force, trapping Romans against the shore.
- The Roman commander *Flaminius* is remembered for his impulsiveness and arrogance; he ignored omens and advice to engage hastily rather than wait for reinforcements.
- The disaster forced Rome to abandon its strategy of direct confrontation and adopt *Fabius Maximus*'s defensive strategy of attrition rather than pitched battles.
- After Trasimene, Rome's strategy shifted to harassment and avoiding Hannibal's armies, demonstrating that overwhelming numerical advantage was not always sufficient to guarantee victory.
- The battle remains a textbook example of tactical ambush and the advantage of knowing terrain intimately in warfare.
