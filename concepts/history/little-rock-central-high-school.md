## summary

**Little Rock Central High School** is an Arkansas public secondary school that became the focal point of the 1957 Little Rock Crisis during the [[civil-rights-movement]]. Following the landmark Supreme Court ruling in *Brown v. Board of Education*, Arkansas Governor *Orval Faubus* deployed state troops to block nine African American teenagers—the *Little Rock Nine*—from entering the building. In response, President *Dwight D. Eisenhower* issued *Executive Order 10730*, federalizing the state guard and dispatching the *101st Airborne Division* to enforce desegregation under the [[14th-amendment]]. The crisis proved to be a watershed moment, demonstrating that the executive branch would use armed military force to enforce federal judicial mandates against state defiance.

## you gotta know

- Enrolled nine Black students, famously known as the *Little Rock Nine* and organized by *Arkansas NAACP* president *Daisy Bates*, to initiate court-ordered desegregation in September 1957.
- Prompted President *Dwight D. Eisenhower* to issue *Executive Order 10730*, federalizing the *Arkansas National Guard* and deploying the *101st Airborne Division* to escort the students, marking the first use of federal troops to protect civil rights since the [[reconstruction-era]].
- Blockaded initially by the *Arkansas National Guard* on orders from Governor *Orval Faubus*, who openly defied federal court integration orders under the claim of preserving public peace.
- Provided the backdrop for *Will Counts*'s iconic photograph showing fifteen-year-old *Elizabeth Eckford* walking alone through a hostile white crowd while being heckled by classmate *Hazel Bryan*.
- Saw senior *Ernest Green* become the school's first African American graduate in May 1958, a milestone quietly attended by civil rights leader [[martin-luther-king-jr]].
- Witnessed severe ongoing student harassment that led to the suspension and expulsion of *Minnijean Brown*, who was provoked by white students and retaliated by dropping a bowl of chili on her tormentors in the cafeteria.
- Prompted the landmark Supreme Court decision *Cooper v. Aaron* in 1958, which barred states from evading desegregation orders and led Governor *Faubus* to shut down all Little Rock public high schools during the 1958–1959 *Lost Year*.
- Commanded during the federal occupation by Major General *Edwin Walker*, an ardent segregationist who enforced the president's orders but later resigned from the military and led violent mobs opposing integration at the [[university-of-mississippi]].

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — broader national campaign against racial segregation and disenfranchisement that reached a pivotal crisis at the school.
- [[14th-amendment]] — constitutional guarantee of equal protection that served as the legal foundation for federal court orders against segregated schooling.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — supreme law of the land whose authority over state defiance was vindicated by presidential enforcement.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — prior historical epoch of federal military intervention in the South, invoked when troops returned to protect Black citizens in 1957.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — prominent civil rights leader who encouraged the nine students and attended the commencement of their first graduate.
- [[cold-war]] — international ideological conflict that turned Southern racial violence into a damaging propaganda liability for American foreign policy.
- [[university-of-mississippi]] — later integration battle in 1962 that served as a direct successor to the military precedent established in Little Rock.
- [[united-states]] — federal government whose executive power clashed directly with Arkansas state sovereignty.
