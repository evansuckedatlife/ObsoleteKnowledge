## summary

The **Moscow Principality** (also known as the Grand Principality of Moscow) was the medieval Russian state that emerged from the fragmented territories under Mongol rule and grew into the nucleus of the Russian Empire. From a minor settlement in the 12th century, Moscow rose to dominance through strategic location, diplomatic maneuvering, and military conquest, ultimately unifying the Russian lands and throwing off foreign control.

## you gotta know

- Founded as a small fortified settlement in the 12th century by Prince Yuri Dolgoruki, Moscow lay at the intersection of major trade routes and surrounded by forests offering defensive advantages.
- Rose to prominence in the 14th century under Ivan I (Ivan Moneybag), who secured the title of Grand Prince and established Moscow as the seat of the Russian Orthodox Church's metropolitan.
- *Ivan III* (1462–1505), known as the Gatherer of Lands, dramatically expanded Moscow's power, subduing rival principalities, throwing off Mongol overlordship, and establishing Moscow's hegemony.
- Defeated the *Golden Horde*, the Mongol state that had dominated Russian territories for over two centuries, declaring independence and establishing Moscow as the leader of Russian liberation.
- *Ivan IV* (1530–1584), known as *Ivan the Terrible*, transformed the Moscow Principality into the Russian Tsardom, conquering additional territories and centralizing autocratic power.
- The principality's rulers adopted Byzantine imperial symbols and married into Byzantine royalty, positioning Moscow as the heir to Constantinople's legacy and claiming "Third Rome" status.
- Developed sophisticated bureaucratic structures, military institutions, and cultural achievements that established the foundation for the Russian Empire's later rise to great-power status.
