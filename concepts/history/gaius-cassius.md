## summary

**Gaius Cassius**, full name **Gaius Cassius Longinus**, was a leading Roman senator, general, and the principal ideological architect behind the conspiracy to assassinate *Julius Caesar* on the Ides of March in 44 BCE. A staunch defender of the traditional republican constitution and an adherent of Epicurean philosophy, **Cassius** persuaded *Marcus Junius Brutus* to join the plot to provide moral authority to the tyrannicide. His subsequent military campaign against the Second Triumvirate ended in disaster at the Battle of Philippi in 42 BCE, where his suicide cemented the collapse of the Roman Republic and cleared the path for the [[roman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- First achieved major military renown during the ill-fated Parthian campaign of *Marcus Licinius Crassus* (53 BCE), where he survived the catastrophe of Carrhae, organized the retreat, and successfully defended Roman Syria.
- Supported *Pompey the Great* during the Great Roman Civil War, commanding a substantial fleet in the Aegean and Adriatic before surrendering to *Julius Caesar* in the Hellespont and receiving a formal pardon.
- Formed the core conspiracy of more than sixty senators calling themselves the *Liberatores*, identifying Caesar's perpetual dictatorship as an intolerable monarchical tyranny.
- Depicted famously in *[[william-shakespeare]]*'s [[tragedy]] *Julius Caesar* as an austere schemer whom Caesar distrusts for having a "lean and hungry look."
- Sacked the ancient maritime republic of Rhodes and extorted heavy tribute across Asia Minor to amass a formidable treasury and navy for the republican cause.
- Served as praetor peregrinus in 44 BCE alongside his brother-in-law *Brutus*, with whom he shared overall military command of the eastern provinces following their departure from Italy.
- Committed suicide at the first engagement of the Battle of Philippi in Macedonia after mistakenly believing his camp had fallen and that *Brutus* had suffered total defeat.
- Eulogized by *Brutus* as "the last of the Romans," a phrase later preserved by imperial historians such as *Tacitus* to commemorate the death of civic liberty.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — sought through political violence to preserve republican liberty, but his actions precipitated the triumviral civil wars that established imperial monarchy.
- [[rome]] — served as a senator, quaestor, and praetor within the city before orchestrating the assassination of Caesar in the Curia of Pompey.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — immortalized in the tragedy *Julius Caesar* as the pragmatic, passionate foil to the idealistic *Brutus*.
- [[tragedy]] — celebrated across European dramatic and literary traditions as the quintessential political conspirator brought low by fate and miscalculation.
- [[ancient-greece]] — conducted his final military operations across Macedonia and Thrace, meeting his demise at Philippi.
- [[gaiseric]] — marked an early turning point in the militarization of Roman rule that centuries later left the Western Empire defenseless against Germanic warlords like [[gaiseric]].
