## summary

**Stalin** (1878–1953) was the totalitarian dictator of the Soviet Union who transformed it into an industrial superpower while perpetrating some of history's most systematic atrocities. Rising to power after *Lenin*'s death, he consolidated authority through ruthless purges, collectivized agriculture causing mass starvation, and governed through terror and propaganda. As Soviet leader during World War II, he rallied the nation against Nazi invasion and emerged as a paramount figure in the Cold War that followed his death.

## you gotta know

- Stalin was born in Georgia as *Iosif Dzhugashvili* and adopted "Stalin" (meaning "man of steel") as a revolutionary pseudonym.
- After *Lenin*'s death in 1924, Stalin outmaneuvered rival *Leon Trotsky* for control of the Bolshevik Party through cunning political maneuvering and alliance-building.
- Stalin's policy of *collectivization* forced peasants into collective farms; agricultural chaos and deliberate requisitions triggered the *Holodomor*, a man-made famine killing millions in Ukraine.
- The *Great Purge* (1936–1938) saw Stalin eliminate suspected enemies within the Communist Party, military, and intelligentsia—hundreds of thousands were executed or sent to gulags.
- Stalin rebuilt the Soviet military and industrialized the economy through brutal "Five-Year Plans," prioritizing heavy industry and military capacity over consumer goods.
- During World War II, Stalin mobilized the Soviet Union against Nazi Germany; Soviet sacrifice proved decisive in defeating fascism, though the war cost over 27 million Soviet lives.
- After 1945, Stalin imposed communist regimes throughout Eastern Europe and confronted the Western Allies in the nascent Cold War.
