## summary

**Cuba**, the largest Caribbean island, occupied a strategic position in American foreign policy from the nineteenth century onward. First as a Spanish colony vulnerable to American expansion, then as a newly independent nation embroiled in Cold War conflict, Cuba became a flashpoint of superpower competition. The island's history embodies the tension between national sovereignty and great-power intervention.

## you gotta know

- Spanish colonial possession from 1492 until 1898, Cuba supplied sugar wealth to Madrid and hosted slavery on a massive scale.
- American interests in Cuban independence grew in the nineteenth century, driven by geographic proximity and investment in sugar plantations.
- The *Spanish-American War* (1898) was ostensibly fought on Cuba's behalf, though American occupation followed independence, subordinating the island to Washington's oversight.
- *Fidel Castro's* revolution in 1959 severed Cuba from American dominance and aligned the island with the Soviet Union during the *Cold War*.
- The *Cuban Missile Crisis* (1962) brought the superpowers to the brink of nuclear war over Soviet missiles deployed on the island.
- American embargo (beginning 1962) isolated Cuba economically, making the island a symbol of anti-imperialism in the global south.
- Castro's regime, though isolating Cuba internationally, pursued land reform and educational expansion, complicating assessments of its legacy.
- The Bay of Pigs invasion (1961), a CIA-sponsored attempt to overthrow Castro, failed spectacularly and deepened Cuban-American hostility.
- Cuba's alliance with the Soviet Union made it a crucial strategic asset during the Cold War, hosting Soviet troops and missiles aimed at American cities.
- The U.S. embargo, maintained for over six decades despite diplomatic opening under *Barack Obama*, remains one of the world's longest-standing trade sanctions on a single nation.
