## summary

The **atomic bomb** is a nuclear weapon deriving explosive force from the fission of uranium or plutonium nuclei, released as an uncontrolled chain reaction. Developed during World War II by the United States in the secret Manhattan Project, it was first used militarily against Japan in August 1945, destroying Hiroshima and Nagasaki and killing over 200,000 people. Its emergence fundamentally altered warfare, international relations, and human vulnerability, ushering in the nuclear age.

## you gotta know

- Resulted from theoretical physics breakthroughs in the 1930s, particularly Einstein and Szilard's demonstration that nuclear fission could release enormous energy and trigger chain reactions.
- Developed in secret by the Manhattan Project (1942-1945), employing over 130,000 workers and scientists across multiple sites at enormous cost—a technological and industrial triumph of extraordinary magnitude.
- Required uranium enrichment and plutonium production technologies that did not previously exist, pushing the boundaries of chemistry, physics, and industrial processes.
- First tested successfully on July 16, 1945, in New Mexico, producing an explosive force equivalent to 20,000 tons of TNT and generating a mushroom cloud visible for miles.
- Dropped by the United States on Hiroshima on August 6, 1945, and Nagasaki on August 9, killing at minimum 200,000 people and inducing Japanese surrender, ending World War II.
- Sparked immediate moral and strategic reconsideration: the weapon was so destructive that it seemed to transcend conventional military utility and threatened civilization itself.
- Became central to Cold War strategy, with nuclear deterrence based on the threat of mutual annihilation, shaping international relations and military doctrine for the rest of the century.
