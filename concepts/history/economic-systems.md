## summary

An **economic system** is the structured institutional arrangement by which a human society organizes the production, distribution, and consumption of goods, labor, and scarce resources. Across world history, societies have transitioned from subsistence hunter-gatherer and agrarian modes through feudalism and state-monopolized mercantilism to modern industrial frameworks dominated by market capitalism, command socialism, and diverse mixed economies. The ideological contest over how to order these systems defined major revolutions, fueled imperial rivalries, and determined the outcomes of modern global conflicts.

## you gotta know

- The four broad structural typologies recognized across economic history comprise traditional economies, market economies, command or planned economies, and mixed economies that synthesize private initiative with state regulation.
- Mercantilism dominated European statecraft from the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries, asserting that global wealth was static and that national power required accumulating bullion through protective tariffs and colonial extraction.
- The intellectual foundation of free-market capitalism emerged during the *Enlightenment*, formulated by *Adam Smith* in *The Wealth of Nations* (1776) through concepts of the division of labor, self-interest, and the "invisible hand."
- The disruptive rise of industrial factory production spurred radical critiques, culminating in *Karl Marx* and *Friedrich Engels* articulating scientific socialism and predicting that internal capitalist contradictions would inevitably yield proletarian revolution.
- Command economies reached their historical zenith in the twentieth-century *Soviet Union*, relying on central state planners such as Gosplan to set strict production quotas, state-mandated pricing, and complete public ownership of heavy industry.
- The Great Depression catalyzed a transformative revolution in macroeconomic governance led by *John Maynard Keynes*, who championed counter-cyclical fiscal intervention, deficit spending, and regulatory oversight to stabilize market fluctuations.
- Postwar international agreements established the Bretton Woods architecture, institutionalizing international trade networks, the International Monetary Fund, and the World Bank to prevent competitive currency devaluations and market collapse.
- In post-conflict and post-crisis environments, transitioning an economy requires [[economic-reconstruction]] to stabilize currency, overhaul property regimes, and restore physical industrial networks.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — the technological and social upheaval that supplanted agrarian craft production with mechanized factories, giving birth to modern capitalism.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical movement that developed classical liberal political economy, championing free markets, private property, and commercial liberty.
- [[soviet-union]] — the twentieth-century socialist superpower that operated the world's largest experiment in centralized command economic planning.
- [[united-states]] — the foremost champion of democratic corporate capitalism, private property rights, and global consumer markets.
- [[cold-war]] — the multi-decade geopolitical struggle fought between capitalist market democracies and Marxist-Leninist planned economies.
- [[british-empire]] — the global imperial hegemon that initially enriched itself through mercantilism before becoming the world's leading evangelist of free trade.
- [[economic-reconstruction]] — the systematic process of rehabilitating damaged industrial capacity and restructuring national economic mechanisms after devastating wars.
