## summary

The *Bill of Rights* consists of the first ten amendments to the *United States Constitution*, ratified in 1791. Drafted primarily by *James Madison* in response to Anti-Federalist demands for explicit protections of individual liberties, the Bill of Rights enshrines fundamental freedoms—speech, religion, press, assembly, petition, due process, and jury trial—that define American civil rights and remain the foundation of constitutional law governing personal freedoms.

## you gotta know

- *James Madison* initially opposed a bill of rights, arguing that the Constitution's structure and enumerated powers already protected liberty; he changed his mind under political pressure from *Thomas Jefferson* and Anti-Federalists who feared unchecked federal power.
- The *First Amendment* protects freedoms of religion, speech, press, assembly, and petition; it is the most litigated amendment and the foundation of American free speech doctrine.
- The *Second Amendment* protects the right to bear arms; its interpretation—whether it protects an individual right or a collective right tied to militia service—remains hotly contested in contemporary gun-control debates.
- The *Fourth Amendment* protects against unreasonable searches and seizures, establishing the foundation for privacy rights and requiring warrants based on probable cause; it has spawned vast jurisprudence on wiretapping, digital privacy, and police conduct.
- The *Fifth Amendment* provides due process protections, protections against self-incrimination, and guarantees against double jeopardy; the phrase "taking the Fifth" enters popular speech through this amendment.
- The *Sixth Amendment* guarantees the right to counsel, speedy trial, and confrontation of witnesses; the *Seventh Amendment* preserves jury trial in civil cases; the *Eighth Amendment* prohibits cruel and unusual punishment; the *Ninth and Tenth Amendments* reserve rights and powers to the people and states.
