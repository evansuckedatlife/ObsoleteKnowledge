## summary

**Desegregation** is the process of ending racial segregation in public institutions, most notably schools, following the principle of equal protection under law. Driven by the [[civil-rights-movement]] of the mid-20th century, it challenged the legal doctrine of "separate but equal" and required the integration of facilities, transportation, and services that had been forcibly separated by race. Desegregation remained incomplete and contentious decades after its legal mandate, representing one of America's most protracted social transformations.

## you gotta know

- *Plessy v. Ferguson* (1896) established "separate but equal" as legal justification for racial segregation, creating a foundation for Jim Crow laws across the South.
- *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954) declared separate schools inherently unequal and unconstitutional, reversing Plessy and serving as the legal mandate for desegregation.
- Desegregation faced massive resistance in the South, including [[national-guard]] intervention at [[university-of-mississippi]] (1962) and federal troops deployed to [[little-rock-central-high-school]] (1957).
- The term "busing" came to describe the controversial practice of transporting students across district lines to achieve racial balance in schools.
- Desegregation extended beyond schools to lunch counters, swimming pools, hotels, and public transportation through sit-ins, freedom rides, and legal action.
- Many Northern cities practiced de facto segregation through residential patterns and school district boundaries, complicating the desegregation process nationwide.
- White resistance to desegregation often took organized forms, from White Citizens' Councils opposing integration to massive resistance movements that closed public schools rather than desegregate them.

## connections

- [[brown-v-board-of-education]] — the Supreme Court decision that made desegregation legally mandatory.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the broader social movement that pushed for desegregation across all public life.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — the primary leader who championed nonviolent desegregation through marches and civil disobedience.
- [[plessy-v-ferguson]] — the 1896 precedent that desegregation was designed to overturn.
- [[fourteen-amendment]] — the constitutional basis for equal protection claims in desegregation cases.
- [[voting-rights-act-of-1965]] — legislation that enforced desegregation in voting access.
- [[jim-crow-laws]] — the segregationist legal regime that desegregation sought to dismantle.
