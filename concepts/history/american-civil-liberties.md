## summary

**American civil liberties** are the fundamental rights and freedoms of individuals protected by the *US Constitution* and *Bill of Rights*, including free speech, religious practice, due process, and protection from unreasonable searches. Throughout American history, these liberties have been contested, restricted in crises, and gradually expanded through court decisions and constitutional amendments. The ongoing tension between security and freedom, majority will and individual rights, defines much of American constitutional law and politics.

## you gotta know

- The *Bill of Rights* (first ten amendments, ratified 1791) established the core civil liberties: free speech, religion, press, assembly, and petition.
- The *14th Amendment* (1868) extended rights protections against state governments, not just the federal government, through the "incorporation doctrine."
- Civil liberties have repeatedly contracted in crises: the *Alien and Sedition Acts* (1798), internment during *World War II*, and McCarthyism (1950s) all saw suspension of speech and due process.
- The *American Civil Liberties Union*, founded 1920, became the primary defender of civil liberties through litigation and advocacy.
- The Warren Court (1953–1969) dramatically expanded civil liberties protections: *Miranda v. Arizona*, *Gideon v. Wainwright*, and desegregation decisions.
- *Watergate* (1973–1974) was catalyzed partly by civil liberties concerns about executive overreach and unconstitutional government surveillance.
- Modern debates pit civil liberties (speech, privacy) against security concerns in contexts of terrorism, immigration, and digital surveillance.
