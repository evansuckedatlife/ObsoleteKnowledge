## summary

**Chester Arthur** was an American attorney, political organizer, and factional leader who rose through the ranks of the New York Republican machine to become the nation's twenty-first Vice President and twenty-first President. Long regarded as the consummate instrument of political patronage under Senator *Roscoe Conkling*, he was selected for the national ticket in 1880 to placate the party's hardline *Stalwart* faction. When the assassination of *James A. Garfield* unexpectedly elevated him to the presidency, Arthur astonished both allies and critics by rising above machine loyalties and presiding over monumental civil service reform.

## you gotta know

- Directed the lucrative *Custom House* of [[new-york-city]] as its Collector from 1871 to 1878, managing the ultimate engine of spoils system patronage until President *Rutherford B. Hayes* removed him during a high-profile reform campaign.
- Served as the chief organizational lieutenant to New York party boss *Roscoe Conkling*, anchoring the conservative *Stalwart* faction that fiercely defended party patronage and backed a third term for *Ulysses S. Grant*.
- Chosen as the vice-presidential nominee at the 1880 Republican National Convention to unify party factions alongside the compromise presidential candidate, *James A. Garfield*.
- Succeeded to the presidency in September 1881 after deranged office-seeker *Charles J. Guiteau* shot Garfield, with the assassin infamously shouting, "I am a Stalwart, and Arthur is President!"
- Broke decisively with his political patrons by signing the landmark *Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act* of 1883, transforming the federal bureaucracy into a competitive, merit-based system.
- Established his early legal reputation in the 1850s as a committed abolitionist attorney, successfully representing *Elizabeth Jennings Graham* after she was forcibly expelled from a segregated streetcar, leading to the desegregation of New York transit.
- Kept his fatal diagnosis of *Bright's disease* strictly hidden from the American public throughout his presidency, suffering privately from kidney failure that sapped his physical energy and led to his death in 1886.
