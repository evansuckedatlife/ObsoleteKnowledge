# Assassination of William McKinley

## summary

President *William McKinley* was shot by anarchist **Leon Czolgosz** on September 6, 1901, in Buffalo, New York, and died eight days later, making him the third U.S. president assassinated in the nineteenth century. **Leon Czolgosz**, motivated by anarchist ideology that rejected all government authority, approached *McKinley* during a public reception and fired two shots. The assassination brought *Theodore Roosevelt*, then Vice President at age 42, to the presidency, transforming American politics and launching one of the nation's most dynamic and expansionist presidencies.

## you gotta know

- **Leon Czolgosz**, a factory worker and anarchist sympathizer, shot President *McKinley* during a public reception at the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo on September 6, 1901.
- *McKinley* initially survived the wounds and doctors believed he would recover; however, complications from the gunshot wounds (likely infection) led to his death on September 14, 1901.
- *Czolgosz* was captured immediately, tried within weeks, convicted of murder, and executed by electric chair in October 1901—a swift execution that reflected public hysteria about anarchism.
- The shooting elevated *Theodore Roosevelt*, then Vice President at 42, to the presidency, making him the youngest president in U.S. history and transforming American foreign and domestic policy.
- The assassination sparked a national panic about anarchist violence and radical immigration; Congress passed legislation restricting anarchists from entering or residing in the United States.
- *McKinley*'s death followed the assassinations of *Abraham Lincoln* (1865) and *James A. Garfield* (1881), establishing a traumatic pattern of presidential violence in nineteenth-century American life.
- The event galvanized progressive reforms aimed at protecting public figures and controlling radical political violence, though anarchism's underlying grievances remained unaddressed by official responses.
