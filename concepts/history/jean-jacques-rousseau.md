## summary

**Jean-Jacques Rousseau** was a Genevan philosopher whose radical challenge to rationalism and emphasis on natural feeling, popular sovereignty, and collective will shaped Enlightenment thought and the ideological foundations of revolution. Through works like *The Social Contract* and *Emile*, he argued that legitimate government rests on the consent of the governed and that human nature is naturally good but corrupted by civilization. His ideas profoundly influenced the [[french-revolution]] and modern democratic theory, though his philosophy remains deeply contested.

## you gotta know

- Born in Geneva in 1712; spent formative years as a wanderer, musician, and household tutor across Europe.
- Published *Discourse on Inequality* (1755), arguing that private property and social hierarchy corrupted natural human equality.
- Wrote *The Social Contract* (1762), proposing that legitimate government derives from a social contract between free individuals; the "general will" supersedes individual rights.
- Argued in *Emile* (1762) that education should nurture natural human development rather than impose conformity; children learn through experience and feeling.
- His ideas on nature and natural feeling rejected Cartesian rationalism, elevating emotion and intuition as valid paths to knowledge.
- Fled France and Switzerland as his writings drew condemnation from authorities; died in exile in Paris in 1778.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — his work redefined the movement's philosophical boundaries and political implications.
- [[social-contract]] — his theory of legitimate government through collective will became foundational to modern political thought.
- [[voltaire]] — fellow Enlightenment giant with whom Rousseau sharply disagreed on reason, nature, and society.
- [[french-revolution]] — revolutionary leaders drew directly on his vision of popular sovereignty and the general will.
- [[mary-wollstonecraft]] — feminist philosopher who both drew from and critiqued Rousseau's ideas on education and society.
- [[john-locke]] — predecessor whose social contract theory Rousseau revised and radicalized.
