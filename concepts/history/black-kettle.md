## summary

**Black Kettle** (*Mo'ôhtavetoo'o*) was a prominent Southern Cheyenne peace chief who led his people during the volatile Plains Indian Wars of the 1860s in the western [[united-states]]. Renowned for his unwavering commitment to diplomatic reconciliation, he repeatedly sought nonviolent coexistence with expanding white settlers and government authorities despite relentless encroachments on Cheyenne territory. His tragic legacy was cemented when his peaceful encampments were attacked twice by federal forces—first surviving the treacherous Sand Creek Massacre in 1864, before being killed four years later by troops under Lieutenant Colonel *George Armstrong Custer* at the Washita River.

## you gotta know

- Survived the horrific *Sand Creek Massacre* in November 1864, where Colonel *John Chivington* and Colorado volunteers slaughtered over two hundred peaceful Cheyenne and Arapaho villagers beneath an *American flag* and white truce banner.
- Died during the *Battle of Washita River* in November 1868 when the 7th Cavalry under *George Armstrong Custer* launched a dawn assault against his sleeping encampment in Indian Territory.
- Advocated persistent diplomacy as a member of the Southern Cheyenne Council of Forty-Four, consistently urging accommodation to shield his band from military destruction by the expanding [[united-states]].
- Signed the controversial 1861 *Treaty of Fort Wise*, which drastically ceded Cheyenne and Arapaho lands in Colorado to a fraction of the territory recognized by earlier accords.
- Met with Territorial Governor *John Evans* and military officers at Camp Weld in 1864, departing with firm assurances that peaceful Indians camping near Fort Lyon would remain unharmed.
- Negotiated and signed the 1865 *Treaty of the Little Arkansas* and the 1867 *Treaty of Medicine Lodge*, endeavoring to establish secure reservations for Southern Plains tribes.
- Endured severe friction with younger militant Cheyenne warrior societies, particularly the *Dog Soldiers*, who fiercely condemned his policy of appeasement toward American expansion.
- Escaped the Sand Creek slaughter alongside his wife, *Medicine Woman Later*, who survived multiple gunshot wounds there only for both to perish together during Custer's Washita attack.
