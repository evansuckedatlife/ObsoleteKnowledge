# Zionism

## summary

**Zionism** is a political and ideological movement advocating for the establishment and support of a Jewish homeland or nation-state, historically focused on the region of Palestine, ancient Judea. Emerging in the late 19th century as a response to *antisemitism* and Jewish dispersion (the *diaspora*), Zionism gained momentum through the 20th century, particularly after the *Holocaust*, culminating in the creation of the State of *Israel* in 1948. The movement encompasses diverse strands—religious, socialist, revisionist—and remains central to Middle Eastern politics, Israeli identity, and international debates over rights, territory, and historical justice.

## you gotta know

- Zionism emerged formally in the late 19th century during a period of intensifying antisemitism in Europe, particularly in Eastern Europe and *Russia*; the movement argued that Jews needed a sovereign homeland for safety and self-determination.
- *Theodor Herzl*, an *Austro-Hungarian* journalist and thinker, founded modern political Zionism in 1896–1897; his vision of a Jewish state was crystallized in the *First Zionist Congress* (1897), which established organizational structures and pursued diplomatic channels to realize this goal.
- Early Zionist settlement in Palestine (then part of the *Ottoman Empire*) proceeded through purchasing land and establishing agricultural colonies and communities, gradually building institutions, infrastructure, and a Hebrew-speaking culture.
- The *Balfour Declaration* (1917), issued by the British government during World War I, expressed support for a "Jewish national home" in Palestine, though it also stated that nothing should prejudice the rights of existing non-Jewish populations—a contradiction that presaged future conflict.
- Zionism received international legitimacy through the *League of Nations* *British Mandate* over Palestine (1920–1948), which nominally facilitated Jewish immigration and institution-building while maintaining British control; immigration accelerated, especially after the *Holocaust*.
- Jewish migration to Palestine intensified dramatically after the *Holocaust* (1933–1945), in which Nazi Germany systematically murdered six million Jews; the Holocaust's horror became the moral argument for Jewish statehood and accelerated international support for a Jewish homeland.
- The *Arab-Israeli conflict* emerged as Palestinian Arabs and neighboring Arab states opposed Zionist settlement and the displacement of Palestinians; the conflict has continued from Israel's 1948 independence through the present, encompassing wars, occupation, and humanitarian crises.
- Different strands of Zionism developed: *socialist Zionism* (kibbutz movement), *religious Zionism* (linking Jewish faith to territorial claims), and *revisionist Zionism* (more militantly nationalist); these divergent ideologies have shaped Israeli society and policy.
