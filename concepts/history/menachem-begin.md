## summary

**Menachem Begin** (1913–1992) was an Israeli statesman and military commander who led the right-wing Likud Party to electoral victory and served as Prime Minister from 1977 to 1983. His tenure marked a watershed in Middle Eastern politics, signing the first peace treaty between Israel and an Arab state while simultaneously expanding Israeli settlements on contested territories.

## you gotta know

- Led the Irgun, a militant Jewish underground organization fighting British mandate forces in Palestine before Israeli independence in 1948.
- As Prime Minister, signed the *Camp David Accords* (1978) with Egyptian President *Anwar Sadat*, brokered by President Jimmy Carter, establishing peace between Israel and Egypt.
- The Camp David agreement provided for Israeli withdrawal from the Sinai Peninsula, but Israeli territorial claims in the West Bank and Gaza remained unresolved.
- Orchestrated the *invasion of Lebanon* (1982) to eliminate the Palestine Liberation Organization's presence, a decision that drew international criticism and domestic opposition.
- Pursued hardline policies on Israeli settlements in Palestinian territories, believing Jews had a historical and religious right to all land west of the Jordan River.
- His hawkish ideology contrasted sharply with his willingness to negotiate with Egypt, showing pragmatism in pursuit of strategic advantage despite his ideological rigidity.
- Became deeply depressed over the suffering caused by the Lebanon War and resigned in 1983, leaving office during a period of national controversy.
- Survived multiple assassination attempts and suffered torture during his years in the resistance, experiences that shaped his inflexible commitment to Israeli security and territorial claims.
