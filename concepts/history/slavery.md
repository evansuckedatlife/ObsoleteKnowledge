## summary

**Slavery** in America was the forced labor system binding millions of people of African descent to perpetual servitude in the southern states from the 1600s through 1865. It formed the economic foundation of the American South, structuring society, law, and culture around the enslavement of Black people for agricultural production. The moral, political, and constitutional crisis slavery created directly precipitated the Civil War and shaped American history long after its abolition.

## you gotta know

- Emerged gradually in colonial America during the 1600s as planters in Virginia and the Carolinas shifted from indentured servitude to permanent racial slavery.
- Justified by pseudoscientific racism and selective biblical interpretation that claimed Africans were inherently suited to enslavement.
- Generated vast wealth for southern planters and northern merchants through the Atlantic slave trade, enriching Northern industrial development alongside Southern agriculture.
- Created fundamentally different economic and social systems: the North industrializing while the South remained agrarian and rigidly hierarchical.
- Sparked the Missouri Compromise, the Compromise of 1850, and the Kansas-Nebraska Act as the nation attempted and failed to manage expansion into new territories.
- Made the Civil War inevitable when the American political system could no longer contain the conflict between free and slave states.
- Abolished constitutionally by the 13th Amendment in 1865, yet its legacy persisted through Reconstruction's failure and Jim Crow racial segregation.
