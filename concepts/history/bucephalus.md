## summary

**Bucephalus**, also spelled *Boukephalas*, was the legendary warhorse of *Alexander the Great* and one of the most celebrated mounts in world history. Described in classical accounts as an unmanageable, fierce Thessalian stallion with a distinctive ox-head brand or broad forehead, the horse was tamed by a young Alexander when no royal groom could master it. Bucephalus accompanied the conqueror across thousands of miles of campaign routes through the Levant, Egypt, Mesopotamia, and Central Asia until the stallion's death following the *Battle of the Hydaspes*.

## you gotta know

- Tamed by a twelve-year-old *Alexander* after the prince astutely observed that the ferocious stallion was rearing in panic at the sight of its own cast shadow.
- Prompted his father King *Philip II of Macedon* to weep with pride and declare that the boy must seek a kingdom worthy of him, as *Macedon* was too small.
- Named from Greek roots translating to "ox-head", an appellation derived either from a brand burned into its flank, a white blaze upon its dark coat, or the peculiar broad shape of its skull.
- Offered originally to the Macedonian court by the horse dealer *Philonicus of Thessaly* for the enormous sum of thirteen talents, a purchase Philip initially refused due to the animal's wild intractability.
- Carried *Alexander* through critical victories against *Darius III* and the *Achaemenid Empire*, including the decisive clashes at the *Battle of Issus* and the *Battle of Gaugamela*.
- Stolen by the predatory Uxians during a mountain expedition, provoking Alexander to threaten the complete extermination of their tribe until the animal was safely returned.
- Died in the Punjab in 326 BCE from wounds or old age following the brutal engagement with King *Porus* and his war elephants at the *Battle of the Hydaspes*.
- Honored posthumously when Alexander founded the garrison city of *Alexandria Bucephala* along the banks of the Jhelum River in modern-day Pakistan.
