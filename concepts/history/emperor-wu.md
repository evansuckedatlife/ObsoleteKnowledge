## summary

**Emperor Wu** of Han, personal name *Liu Che*, was the seventh emperor of the Han dynasty, ruling imperial *[[china]]* from 141 BCE to 87 BCE. His reign of fifty-four years established the administrative, territorial, and intellectual blueprint of imperial China for millennia. Best known for crushing nomadic incursions, elevating Confucianism to state orthodoxy, and opening vast trade networks, he transformed the Han into an expansive imperial power.

## you gotta know

- Instigated the diplomatic expedition of envoy *Zhang Qian* to the western regions, directly establishing the trans-Eurasian trade routes known as the *[[silk-road]]*.
- Adopted Confucianism as the sole official ideological foundation of the imperial court on the advice of scholar *Dong Zhongshu*, sidelining rival Legalist and Daoist factions.
- Launched aggressive offensive campaigns against the northern nomadic confederation of the *Xiongnu*, ending decades of appeasement and tribute policies known as *heqin*.
- Created state-run monopolies over salt, iron, and liquor alongside price-stabilization schemes (*pingzhun*) to finance expensive military expansion without bankrupting the state.
- Expanded imperial borders deep into the Korean Peninsula, modern northern Vietnam, and the Tarim Basin in Central Asia.
- Established the Imperial Academy (*Taixue*) to educate prospective imperial bureaucrats in the Confucian classics, laying the groundwork for the civil service exam system.
- Marred his final years with the devastating "Witchcraft Catastrophe" (*wugu*), an imperial succession crisis fueled by paranoia that resulted in the suicide of his crown prince.

## connections

- [[china]] — expanded its borders, centralized its bureaucracy, and cemented its traditional political philosophy.
- [[silk-road]] — initiated official state expeditions to the West that opened continental Eurasian commerce.
- [[roman-empire]] — contemporary civilization at the opposing western terminus of early Eurasian trade networks.
- [[buddhism]] — western territorial conquests facilitated the eventual introduction of this faith into China.
