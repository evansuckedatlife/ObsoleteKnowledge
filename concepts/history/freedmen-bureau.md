## summary

The **Freedmen's Bureau** (officially the Bureau of Refugees, Freedmen, and Abandoned Lands) was the first major federal welfare agency, established in 1865 to assist the millions of formerly enslaved people in the South. Operating during *Reconstruction*, it distributed food and clothing, established schools, negotiated labor contracts, and protected freedmen's rights. President *Andrew Johnson* opposed and undermined the Bureau, while *Radical Republicans* defended it as essential to ensuring the freedom and citizenship of African Americans.

## you gotta know

- Created under President *Abraham Lincoln* in 1865, with operations extending into the early 1870s during Reconstruction.
- Distributed rations to destitute freedmen and poor whites, operated hospitals and schools, and settled disputes over labor and property.
- Represented the federal government's most ambitious attempt to aid formerly enslaved people in their transition to freedom and citizenship.
- *Andrew Johnson* opposed the Bureau, viewing it as overreach and a threat to state authority, and vetoed legislation to expand it.
- Provided crucial education: the Bureau founded thousands of schools and eventually helped establish historically Black colleges.
- Gradually lost funding and authority as white Southern resistance mounted; effectively ended by 1872 as Reconstruction collapsed.
- Negotiated labor contracts between freedmen and former owners, attempting to establish free labor rather than slavery or sharecropping (though sharecropping ultimately prevailed across the South).
- Faced fierce opposition from white Southerners who saw it as federal tyranny interfering in local affairs; when Republican political power waned, Reconstruction measures were systematically dismantled.
- Its failure to achieve lasting economic independence for freedmen remains one of Reconstruction's most significant unfulfilled promises and contributed to the persistent wealth gap between Black and white Americans.
