# Time of Troubles

## summary

The **Time of Troubles** (1598–1613) was a period of civil strife, foreign invasion, and dynastic confusion in Russia following the death of *Tsar Ivan the Terrible*. The chaos included succession crises, impostor claimants, Polish-Lithuanian occupation of Moscow, famine, and peasant rebellions, ending only with the election of *Mikhail Romanov* as tsar and the founding of the Romanov dynasty that would rule Russia until 1917.

## you gotta know

- After *Ivan IV's* death in 1584, his weak son Feodor I inherited an exhausted realm; Boris Godunov, a powerful regent, consolidated control and was elected tsar after Feodor's death (1598), but faced immediate opposition and legitimacy questions.
- A succession of false claimants (impostor "Dmitris") emerged, especially the *False Dmitri* (1603–1605), who claimed to be Ivan's younger son; with Polish-Lithuanian backing, the false Dmitri briefly occupied the throne, highlighting the weakness of Godunov's regime.
- Swedish and Polish forces invaded Russia, exploiting the chaos: the Poles occupied Moscow and installed their own puppet tsar; Russia faced fragmentation, famine (particularly harsh 1601–03), and widespread social breakdown.
- Peasant rebellions erupted, including Cossack uprisings under *Stenka Razin*; the traditional social hierarchy fractured as nobles, merchants, and peasants vied for advantage during the power vacuum.
- The Russian Orthodox Church became a symbol of national continuity; Patriarch *Filaret* (later *Mikhail Romanov's* father) mobilized religious authority and popular sentiment against foreign occupation and illegitimate rulers.
- In 1613, the *Zemsky Sobor* (assembly of estates) elected *Mikhail Romanov*, a young noble whose mother was the daughter of Tsar Ivan IV's advisor, as tsar; his election restored a sense of legitimate succession and native rule.
