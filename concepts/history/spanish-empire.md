# Spanish Empire

## summary

The **Spanish Empire** was a global imperial power spanning the 16th through 19th centuries, at its height the largest empire in the world by territorial extent, spanning vast regions of the Americas, the Caribbean, much of Europe (including the Netherlands and parts of Italy), Africa, and Asia (notably the Philippines). Built on the foundations of *Columbus*'s trans-Atlantic voyages and the *Reconquista* of the Iberian Peninsula from Muslim rule, the Spanish Empire accumulated vast wealth from American silver and sugar, established Spanish as a global language, and shaped the cultural, religious, and political landscape of the Americas for centuries.

## you gotta know

- Spain's empire began to coalesce after the marriage of *Ferdinand and Isabella* (1469), which unified the Spanish crowns; their sponsorship of *Columbus* in 1492 inaugurated Spanish colonization of the Americas and initiated Europe's global expansion.
- Spanish conquistadors—most notably *Hernán Cortés* (who conquered the *Aztec Empire*) and *Francisco Pizarro* (who conquered the *Inca Empire*)—rapidly subjugated vast populations in the 16th century, extracting enormous wealth in gold and silver.
- The wealth of the Americas, particularly the silver mines of *Potosí* in Peru, enriched Spain and financed its wars and court; Spain became the wealthiest power in Europe, though the influx of silver eventually contributed to inflation and economic decline.
- Spain's American empire relied on the *encomienda* system, a labor arrangement that granted Spanish settlers control over Indigenous populations; this system, along with disease and warfare, devastated American populations and created a legacy of exploitation and racial stratification.
- The Spanish king became *King Philip II* (1556–1598), who consolidated imperial power and launched the *Spanish Armada* (1588) against *England*; the Armada's defeat signaled Spain's inability to remain unchallenged at sea and marked the beginning of Spanish decline relative to rising powers like *England* and the *Netherlands*.
- Spain maintained vast territorial claims throughout the 17th and 18th centuries despite losing ground to other European powers; the Spanish succession crisis (1701–1714) further diminished Spanish power and territory in Europe.
- The Spanish Empire reached from *Mexico* and the Caribbean in the north to *Chile* and *Argentina* in the south; Spanish colonial administration, Catholic missions, and Spanish language created a lasting cultural unity across these regions.
- By the 19th century, Spanish America had experienced waves of independence movements led by figures like *Simón Bolívar*; by the end of the century, Spain retained only a few colonial holdings (Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam, the Philippines) until the *Spanish-American War* (1898) ended most remaining empire.
