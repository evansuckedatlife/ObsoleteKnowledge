## summary

**Alp Arslan** was the second sultan of the [[seljuk-turks]], ruling an expansive empire across Persia, Iraq, and the wider [[middle-east]] from 1063 until his death in 1072. Revered as an exceptional military commander and dedicated champion of Sunni [[islam]], he governed alongside his Persian grand vizier *Nizam al-Mulk* to centralize imperial administration, stabilize internal borders, and foster Islamic learning. His defining historical triumph occurred at the *Battle of Manzikert* in 1071, where his army routed the [[byzantine-empire]] and captured Emperor *Romanos IV Diogenes*. This historic victory shattered Byzantine military hegemony in Asia Minor, opening Anatolia to permanent Turkish settlement and irrevocably transforming the geopolitical landscape of the medieval world.

## you gotta know

- Decisively defeated the imperial army of the [[byzantine-empire]] at the *Battle of Manzikert* in 1071 using feigned retreats and encirclement, culminating in the shocking capture of Emperor *Romanos IV Diogenes*.
- Famously treated his captured imperial rival with personal respect and royal courtesy, dining with *Romanos*, negotiating a generous peace treaty and annual tribute, and releasing him with an armed escort rather than executing or humiliating him.
- Shattered Byzantine frontier defenses and triggered the large-scale migration of Oghuz Turkic tribes into Asia Minor, laying the direct demographic and political foundations for the Sultanate of Rum and the eventual rise of the [[ottoman-empire]].
- Prompted the eventual appeal by Byzantine Emperor *Alexios I Komnenos* to Western Christendom for urgent military assistance, which directly catalyzed the [[crusades]] summoned by Pope *Urban II* at the Council of Clermont.
- Entrusted civil governance, fiscal reform, and administrative justice to his Persian grand vizier *Nizam al-Mulk*, who institutionalized Sunni governance and founded the influential *Nizamiyya* madrasa network across major cities in the [[middle-east]].
- Succeeded his uncle *Tughril Beg* in 1063 after suppressing succession challenges by his brother *Qawurd* and other claimants, consolidating Seljuk authority from Central Asia to the borders of the [[roman-empire]].
- Born *Muhammad bin Dawud Chaghri*, his royal Turkic honorific translates to "Heroic Lion," reflecting the martial prowess shown while governing Khurasan alongside his father *Chaghri Beg* and securing trade along the [[silk-road]].
- Fatally stabbed along the Oxus River in 1072 by *Yusuf al-Khwarazmi*, a captured fortress commander brought before him; on his deathbed, he famously lamented that pride in his own archery and physical prowess had caused him to let down his guard.

## connections

- [[byzantine-empire]] — the Christian imperial power whose army he routed at Manzikert, precipitating its permanent loss of the Anatolian heartland.
- [[seljuk-turks]] — the ruling Turkic-Persian dynasty he led as its second sultan, establishing it as the dominant military power in Western Asia.
- [[crusades]] — the European military expeditions triggered by Byzantine appeals for aid against the rapid Turkish expansion set in motion by his victory.
- [[islam]] — the faith whose orthodox Sunni Abbasid caliphate he defended, checking the political influence of the rival Fatimid Caliphate across the Levant.
- [[middle-east]] — the central geographic realm of his empire, encompassing united domains across Persia, Mesopotamia, and Syria.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the later imperial state founded by Turkic lineages whose settlement in Anatolia began under his reign.
- [[roman-empire]] — the ancient predecessor whose surviving eastern realm suffered an irreversible military and territorial collapse after Manzikert.
