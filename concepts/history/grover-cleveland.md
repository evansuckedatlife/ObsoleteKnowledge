## summary

**Grover Cleveland**, born **Stephen Grover Cleveland**, was an American attorney and statesman who served as the 22nd and 24th President of the [[united-states]]. He is the only chief executive in American history to serve two non-consecutive terms in the White House (1885–1889 and 1893–1897) and the sole Democrat elected to the presidency during the long Republican ascendancy following the [[reconstruction-era]]. Celebrated by contemporaries for his unyielding honesty and fierce devotion to classical liberal governance, he staunchly opposed protective tariffs, political corruption, and paper money inflation.

## you gotta know

- Won the presidency in 1884 against Republican candidate *James G. Blaine* with the crucial backing of reformist Republicans dubbed *Mugwumps*, aided when a Protestant minister insulted Democrats as the party of "Rum, Romanism, and Rebellion."
- Deployed the presidential veto power with unprecedented frequency, issuing 414 vetoes in his first term alone to strike down fraudulent Civil War veterans' pensions and rejecting the *Texas Seed Bill* on strict constitutional grounds.
- Wed twenty-one-year-old *Frances Folsom* in the Blue Room in 1886, making him the only sitting president to marry inside the White House, while public affection for their daughter *Ruth Cleveland* later inspired commercial popular culture.
- Concealed an emergency surgery performed aboard the yacht *Oneida* in Long Island Sound during the summer of 1893 to remove a cancerous tumor from his upper jaw, avoiding panic on Wall Street.
- Reaffirmed his commitment to the *gold standard* amid the crushing economic depression of the *Panic of 1893*, repealing the *Sherman Silver Purchase Act* and negotiating a controversial $65 million gold loan from financier *J. P. Morgan*.
- Dispatched federal soldiers to Chicago to suppress the 1894 *Pullman Strike* over the vigorous protests of Illinois Governor *John Peter Altgeld*, claiming the strike obstructed the delivery of federal mail.
- Signed major federal regulatory legislation during his first administration, notably the *Interstate Commerce Act* of 1887 creating the Interstate Commerce Commission and the *Dawes Act* concerning indigenous lands.
- Invoked the Monroe Doctrine aggressively against the [[british-empire]] during the 1895 Venezuelan boundary dispute, insisting that American arbitration govern the boundary dispute.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the federal republic he governed across two distinct administrations during the height of the Gilded Age.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the formative period of post-war political turbulence that shaped the Democratic coalition that eventually elected him.
- [[william-mckinley]] — the champion of high protective tariffs and gold-standard monetary policy who succeeded him following the 1896 election.
- [[benjamin-harrison]] — the Republican leader who ousted him in the Electoral College in 1888 before losing their rematch in 1892.
- [[pullman-strike]] — the nationwide railway strike led by *Eugene V. Debs* that his administration crushed using federal troops and injunctions.
- [[panic-of-1893]] — the catastrophic financial panic that triggered bank runs and shattered the popularity of his second administration.
- [[gold-standard]] — the hard-money monetary framework he dogmatically defended against the free silver faction of his own party.
