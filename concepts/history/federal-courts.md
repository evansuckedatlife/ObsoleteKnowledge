## summary

The **federal courts** comprise the system of courts established under the *U.S. Constitution* to interpret federal law and resolve disputes involving federal issues. Headed by the *Supreme Court*, the federal judiciary serves as a co-equal branch alongside the executive and legislative branches, wielding the power of judicial review.

## you gotta know

- Established by *Article III* of the *Constitution*, creating the *Supreme Court* and authorizing Congress to create lower federal courts as needed to handle federal business.
- Operate separately from state court systems; federal courts hear cases involving federal law, interstate disputes, constitutional questions, and disputes between states.
- Include district courts (trial courts with fact-finding jurisdiction), courts of appeals (appellate courts reviewing district decisions), and specialized courts such as bankruptcy, tax, and patent courts.
- The *Supreme Court* serves as the ultimate arbiter of constitutional interpretation, with decisions binding on all lower courts and fundamentally shaping American governance.
- Gained power to declare laws unconstitutional through *Marbury v. Madison* (1803), establishing *judicial review*, a transformative power that made courts co-equal with legislatures.
- Limited by the requirement of *standing* — plaintiffs must demonstrate concrete injury — and justiciability doctrines that prevent advisory opinions and limit courts to actual cases or controversies.
- Appointment of federal judges is controlled by the President with Senate confirmation, shaping jurisprudence across generations; federal judges serve for life during good behavior.
- Federal courts can overturn state laws that violate federal law or the *Constitution*, making them instruments of national authority over state legislatures.
- Serve as final arbiter of constitutional disputes, their interpretation of the Constitution effectively amending it without formal amendment, giving the judiciary immense power.
