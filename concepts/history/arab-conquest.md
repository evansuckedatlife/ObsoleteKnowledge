## summary

The **Arab conquest of Spain**, also known as the **Umayyad conquest of Hispania**, was an eight-year military campaign beginning in 711 CE that dismantled the Visigothic Kingdom and brought the vast majority of the Iberian Peninsula under Islamic dominion. Initiated by the Berber commander *Tariq ibn Ziyad* and reinforced by the governor *Musa ibn Nusayr*, the offensive established the province of *Al-Andalus* and integrated southwestern Europe into the burgeoning [[islam]]ic world. The expedition dismantled centuries of Germanic rule, creating a sophisticated multi-confessional society in medieval [[spain]] while inaugurating the fierce, centuries-long territorial dispute remembered as the [[reconquista]].

## you gotta know

- *Tariq ibn Ziyad* initiated the invasion in 711 CE by ferrying approximately seven thousand Berber soldiers across the Strait of Gibraltar, landing at the iconic promontory that bears his name (*Jabal Tariq*).
- The decisive engagement occurred at the *Battle of Guadalete* (711 CE), where Tariq's forces routed the royal Visigothic army, leading to the death or disappearance of King *Roderic* and the catastrophic collapse of centralized Visigothic resistance.
- The Umayyad governor of Ifriqiya, *Musa ibn Nusayr*, crossed into Iberia in 712 CE with an army of eighteen thousand predominantly Arab troops to conquer Seville, Mérida, and the Visigothic capital of Toledo.
- Expansion across the Pyrenees into the territory of [[france]] was permanently checked in 732 CE at the *Battle of Tours*, where Frankish leader *Charles Martel* defeated the governor *Abdul Rahman Al Ghafiqi*.
- Resistance in the mountainous northern fringe of Asturias coalesced under the nobleman *Pelagius* at the *Battle of Covadonga* (c. 718–722 CE), which later Christian chroniclers celebrated as the opening salvos of the [[reconquista]].
- Rather than imposing forced conversions, Muslim commanders instituted the *dhimmi* system, exemplified by the *Pact of Theodemir* (713 CE), which guaranteed Christians and Jews religious freedom and property security under [[christianity]] and [[judaism]] in exchange for annual poll taxes.
- The campaign benefited enormously from deep domestic fissures within the Visigothic elite, particularly the lingering succession dispute between *Roderic* and the partisans of his predecessor *Wittiza*, alongside legendary assistance from *Count Julian* of Ceuta.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the spiritual and political banner uniting Arab governors and Berber soldiers, establishing the legal and social framework of *Al-Andalus*.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian theater profoundly transformed from an unstable Germanic kingdom into a flourishing center of medieval civilization.
- [[reconquista]] — the protracted military movement by northern Christian kingdoms aimed at reversing the conquests and reclaiming the peninsula.
- [[umayyad-caliphate]] — the Syrian-centered imperial caliphate that financed, sanctioned, and formally administered the newly conquered European territories.
- [[france]] — the neighboring Frankish realm whose borderlands served as the ultimate northern boundary of Umayyad military expansion.
- [[christianity]] — the established faith of the Visigoths, whose adherents remained the demographically dominant population throughout early Muslim rule.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical predecessor whose surviving paved highways, bridges, and urban fortifications enabled rapid military transit across Hispania.
- [[judaism]] — the heavily persecuted minority under late Visigothic law that welcomed the invading forces as liberators and flourished as key civil administrators.
