## summary

**Al-Azhar** is a historic mosque and prestigious center of higher learning in Cairo, Egypt, widely regarded as the intellectual heart of Sunni [[islam]]. Commissioned in 970 CE by the Fatimid general *Jawhar al-Siqilli* following the conquest of Egypt, it was named in honor of the Prophet's daughter *Fatima az-Zahra* and formally opened for prayers and instruction in 972 CE. Although founded to propagate Isma'ili Shi'ite theology, it was transformed into a premier Sunni institution under the Ayyubid and Mamluk dynasties, ultimately evolving into one of the world's oldest continuously operating universities. Across a millennium of political transformation across the [[middle-east]], the complex has served as an authoritative arbiter of Islamic jurisprudence, a sanctuary for Arabic scholarship, and a focal point of resistance against foreign empires.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 970 CE by the Sicilian-born Fatimid general *Jawhar al-Siqilli* on the orders of Caliph *al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah* during the establishment of Cairo (*al-Qahira*).
- Named **Al-Azhar Mosque** (*al-Jami' al-Azhar*, "the Most Resplendent Mosque") as an allusion to *Fatima az-Zahra*, the daughter of the Prophet Muhammad and ancestor of the Fatimid dynasty.
- Served as the headquarters of the 1798 Cairo Revolt against the military occupation of Egypt by [[france]] under [[napoleon-bonaparte]], which provoked French artillery bombardment of the sanctuary and the stabling of cavalry horses inside its courtyard.
- Transformed into a modern state university in 1961 by Egyptian President *Gamal Abdel Nasser*, who nationalized its endowments and established secular faculties for medicine, agriculture, and engineering alongside traditional faculties of Islamic theology (*usul al-din*) and law (*sharia*).
- Suppressed and stripped of state patronage by *Saladin* (*Salah ad-Din*) after the fall of the Fatimids in 1171, leading to a nearly century-long suspension of its congregational Friday prayers in favor of newly established Sunni madrasas.
- Restored to prominence under the Mamluk Sultanate, particularly by Sultan *Baybars*, who reinstated its Friday sermon, endowed its teaching chairs, and established it as the foremost center for teaching all four Sunni legal schools (*madhhabs*).
- Headed by the *Shaykh al-Azhar* (Grand Imam of Al-Azhar), an office historically chosen by senior scholars that carries immense theological prestige and moral authority across the Muslim world.
- Hosted lectures by renowned medieval intellectual luminaries, including the pioneering optical scientist *Alhazen* (*Ibn al-Haytham*) and the celebrated North African historiographer *Ibn Khaldun*, who taught jurisprudence within its arcades.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the global faith whose legal, theological, and linguistic traditions **Al-Azhar** has preserved, systematized, and taught for over a thousand years.
- [[middle-east]] — the geographic and cultural region where Cairo and Al-Azhar served as an unrivaled intellectual capital and spiritual center.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the imperial power that ruled Egypt from 1517 to 1798, during which the office of the Grand Imam (*Shaykh al-Azhar*) was formally established.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — French general whose 1798 invasion provoked an armed insurrection centered at the mosque, prompting artillery bombardment of the sanctuary.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power occupying Egypt from 1882 to 1956, which faced fierce nationalist resistance organized by scholars and students of **Al-Azhar University**.
- [[silk-road]] — the trade and pilgrimage network that carried scholars, pilgrims, and theological manuscripts between North Africa, Andalusia, and Central Asia to Cairo.
