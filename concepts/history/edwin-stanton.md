## summary

**Edwin Stanton**, officially **Edwin McMasters Stanton**, was an American lawyer and statesman who served as the *United States Secretary of War* under Presidents *[[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]* and *[[andrew-johnson|Andrew Johnson]]* throughout the *[[american-civil-war]]* and early *[[reconstruction-era]]*. Celebrated for his administrative vigor, organizational mastery, and strict intolerance of financial waste, he reorganized federal logistical lines and directed the union's military resources toward final victory. His defiant refusal to vacate his post following his dismissal by President Johnson led to Johnson's impeachment in 1868, making Stanton the central figure in one of the deepest constitutional crises in *[[united-states]]* history.

## you gotta know

- Served as *Secretary of War* from 1862 through 1868, replacing the corrupt and inefficient *Simon Cameron* and transforming the War Department into an efficient logistical machine that managed troop deployments, rail supply networks, and military communications.
- Took personal charge of the national emergency and directed the extensive manhunt for *John Wilkes Booth* and the co-conspirators immediately following the assassination of *[[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]* at *Ford's Theatre*.
- Uttered the memorable phrase at Lincoln's bedside following the president's passing on April 15, 1865, commonly recorded as "*Now he belongs to the ages*", though some witnesses reported hearing "*angels*".
- Served as the flashpoint for the first presidential impeachment when *[[andrew-johnson|Andrew Johnson]]* attempted to fire him in violation of the *Tenure of Office Act*, prompting Stanton to barricade himself inside his War Department office for weeks while federal troops guarded the perimeter.
- Acted as Attorney General during the final months of *James Buchanan*'s administration in 1860, where he resisted southern secessionist demands and secretly passed critical intelligence to Republican leaders to protect federal forts.
- Gained national legal fame prior to public service in landmark trials, including defending Congressman *Daniel Sickles* in 1859 by deploying the nation's first successful plea of temporary insanity, and snubbing a young Illinois lawyer named *[[abraham-lincoln|Abraham Lincoln]]* in the *McCormick v. Manny* patent litigation.
- Nominated by President *Ulysses S. Grant* and confirmed by the Senate to the *United States Supreme Court* as an Associate Justice in December 1869, only to die of coronary asthma four days later before taking his seat on the bench.

## connections

- [[abraham-lincoln]] — the president who appointed him to head the War Department and forged a close working partnership with him.
- [[andrew-johnson]] — the successor president whose attempt to dismiss him triggered the first presidential impeachment trial.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict in which he directed federal military logistics, telegraph communications, and officer management.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-war period during which he advocated for Radical Republican military protection in the South.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the constitutional framework tested by his tenure and the resulting separation-of-powers struggle.
- [[tenure-of-office-act]] — the federal law enacted by Congress over Johnson's veto that forbade firing cabinet secretaries without Senate consent.
