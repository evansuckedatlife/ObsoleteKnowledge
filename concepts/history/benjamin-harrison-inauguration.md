## summary

The **Inauguration of Benjamin Harrison** took place on March 4, 1889, at the East Portico of the United States Capitol in Washington, D.C., inaugurating *Benjamin Harrison* as the twenty-third president of the *[[united-states]]*. Notable for occurring during a torrential rainstorm, the ceremony saw outgoing president *Grover Cleveland* hold an umbrella over the incoming leader as Chief Justice *Melville Fuller* administered the oath of office. The event marked the centennial of the American presidency under the *[[united-states-constitution]]*, exactly one hundred years after *[[george-washington]]* assumed office in *[[new-york-city]]*. In his address, Harrison articulated a vigorous vision of national expansion, advocating for protective tariffs, naval modernization, the admission of new states, and federal protection for Black voting rights.

## you gotta know

- Endured a relentless, freezing rainstorm that drenched thousands of spectators, leading outgoing president *Grover Cleveland* to hold an umbrella over *Harrison* during the oath administered by Chief Justice *Melville Fuller*.
- Commemorated the centennial anniversary of the executive branch under the *[[united-states-constitution]]*, arriving a century after *[[george-washington]]* stood on the balcony of Federal Hall in 1789.
- Carried profound family significance as *Benjamin Harrison* was the grandson of *William Henry Harrison*, whose own drenched 1841 inauguration speech famously preceded fatal illness.
- Outlined an ambitious domestic agenda promoting protective tariffs to defend domestic labor during the *[[industrial-revolution]]* while urging the rapid admission of several Western territories into the union.
- Addressed the persistent suppression of Southern Black voters following the *[[reconstruction-era]]*, forcefully arguing that federal authority must guarantee constitutional rights and ballot security for all citizens.
- Welcomed *Levi P. Morton* into office as the twenty-second vice president, who took his separate oath inside the Senate Chamber before the rain-soaked crowd assembled outside.
- Heard musical performances from the United States Marine Band directed by *John Philip Sousa*, whose musicians played through blinding rain with waterlogged brass instruments.
- Culminated in an opulent inaugural ball inside the newly constructed Pension Building, where thousands of guests danced amidst electric chandeliers and elaborate floral displays.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — framed the centennial anniversary celebrated by the 1889 inaugural ceremonies and governed the oath administered to the new executive.
- [[george-washington]] — served as the first president whose 1789 inauguration provided the historic benchmark and inspiration for Harrison's centennial address.
- [[american-civil-war]] — influenced Harrison's personal outlook as a former Union brigadier general and reinforced his dedication to veterans' pensions.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — left unresolved civil rights struggles that prompted Harrison to advocate strongly for federal enforcement of voting protections.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — drove the economic growth and domestic manufacturing that Harrison sought to nurture through high protective tariffs.
- [[new-york-city]] — hosted the original 1789 inaugural rites, prompting Harrison to attend lavish follow-up centennial commemorations there in April 1889.
- [[lincoln-second-inauguration]] — provided an earlier famous precedent of an American president taking the oath amidst heavy rains and muddy Washington thoroughfares.
