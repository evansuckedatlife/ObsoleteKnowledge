## summary

**Sweden** is a Scandinavian nation that rose from medieval obscurity to major European power status during the early modern period. Particularly through military prowess during the [[thirty-years-war]] under King Gustavus Adolphus, Sweden acquired significant Baltic territories at the [[peace-of-westphalia]] (1648) and established itself as a prominent force in northern European politics. While Swedish power declined from its 18th-century peak, Sweden maintained a distinct cultural and political identity, and in the modern era developed notable democratic institutions, cultural achievements, and a tradition of neutrality in European conflicts.

## you gotta know

- Sweden's entry into the [[thirty-years-war]] under *Gustavus Adolphus* (1630) transformed the conflict, introducing innovative military tactics (cavalry tactics, rapid artillery deployment) that influenced European warfare for centuries.
- The [[peace-of-westphalia]] awarded Sweden significant Baltic territories: **Swedish** Pomerania, Bremen-Verden, and confirmation of earlier Baltic conquests, elevating Sweden to major-power status.
- Swedish expansion during the 17th century created the Swedish Empire, controlling lands around the Baltic Sea and competing with Russia, Poland, and Denmark for regional dominance.
- Sweden's cultural flowering during this period, though less famous than Italian or French achievements, produced notable painters, architects, and a distinctive aesthetic blending Baroque and northern traditions.
- The Great Northern War (1700–1721) pitted Sweden against Russia, Prussia, and allied powers; Swedish defeat marked the beginning of Swedish power decline but Sweden remained a significant actor.
- Modern Sweden developed constitutional monarchy, parliamentary democracy, extensive social welfare systems, and cultural exports (literature, film, design) that extended Swedish influence into the 20th and 21st centuries.

## connections

- [[peace-of-westphalia]] — the treaty that elevated Sweden to major-power status through territorial acquisition and international recognition.
- [[thirty-years-war]] — the religious conflict in which Sweden's military intervention established its reputation as a formidable military power.
- [[gustavus-adolphus]] — the king whose military innovations and leadership transformed Sweden into a major power.
- [[norway]] — the neighboring Scandinavian nation with which Sweden shared a complex political history, including union and eventual separation.
- [[baltic-region]] — the strategic maritime zone around which Sweden built its early modern power, competing for control.
- [[russia]] — Sweden's principal competitor for Baltic dominance throughout the early modern period and rival major power.
- [[northern-europe]] — the region where Sweden's cultural and political identity developed, distinct from southern European patterns.
