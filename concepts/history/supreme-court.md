## summary

The **Supreme Court** is the highest federal judicial authority in the United States, established by the *Constitution* (1787) as the apex of the judicial branch. It interprets the Constitution and federal law, has final appellate authority over state and federal courts, and through landmark rulings has shaped American civil rights, federalism, and governance for over two centuries.

## you gotta know

- The Court consists of nine justices (one Chief Justice and eight Associate Justices) appointed for life by the President with Senate confirmation, insulating them from electoral pressure.
- *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) established *judicial review*—the power to strike down laws and executive actions as unconstitutional—cementing the Court's role as guardian of the Constitution.
- The Court's decisions in the 19th and early 20th centuries often protected property and limited federal regulation (e.g., striking down labor laws), but shifted during the *New Deal* to uphold expansive government programs.
- *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954) ruled that school *segregation* violated the *14th Amendment*, making the Court a crucial engine of the *civil-rights-movement*.
- The Court has navigated major constitutional questions: the scope of federal power, individual rights (speech, religion, due process), state versus federal authority, and the reach of presidential power during emergencies.
- Justices write *majority* and *dissenting opinions*, creating binding precedent and legal reasoning that shapes American law; dissents often presage future reversals.
- The Court's docket is highly selective (only ~70 cases heard annually from thousands of petitions), making it a strategically important filter for American jurisprudence.
