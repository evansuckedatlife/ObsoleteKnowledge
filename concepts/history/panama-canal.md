# Panama Canal

## summary

The **Panama Canal** is an artificial waterway connecting the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans across the Isthmus of Panama, engineered and built by the United States between 1904 and 1914. It revolutionized global shipping by eliminating the need to circumnavigate South America and became one of the most strategically important infrastructure projects of the 20th century, though its construction and American control sparked lasting geopolitical tensions in Latin America.

## you gotta know

- The canal was built under *President Theodore Roosevelt*'s administration as a crown jewel of American imperialism and naval strategy; it opened in 1914 but was controlled by the U.S. until 1977.
- The French attempted to build the canal under *Ferdinand de Lesseps* (1881–1889) but abandoned the project after massive casualties from disease and engineering failures.
- The U.S. obtained the right to build the canal by supporting Panama's independence from *Colombia* in 1903, then signed the *Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty* granting the U.S. a perpetual lease and control of the canal zone.
- Construction was a monumental feat: the canal required *locks* (massive chambers that raise and lower ships), excavation of the *Culebra Cut* through the continental divide, and construction of *Gatun Lake*, an artificial lake created to enable ships to pass.
- The canal reduced travel time from Asia to the U.S. East Coast by roughly half, fundamentally altering global trade and naval deployment strategies; both world wars demonstrated its strategic value.
- *Disease* (yellow fever and malaria) killed thousands during construction until American engineers, led by *Colonel William Gorgas*, implemented sanitation reforms that broke the cycle of epidemics.
- The canal's construction displaced indigenous *Panamanian* populations and environmental degradation was severe; American control became a symbol of Yankee imperialism in Central America.
