## summary

**Al-Ghazali** (1058–1111) was a Persian Islamic philosopher, theologian, and jurist who became the greatest intellectual authority of the medieval Islamic world. Known as the "Proof of Islam," he synthesized Aristotelian logic with Islamic theology, defending orthodox Islamic doctrine against rationalist challenges. His massive philosophical and devotional works profoundly shaped Islamic thought, Sufism, and Islamic law for centuries, establishing him as one of the most influential thinkers in Islamic history.

## you gotta know

- Born in Khorasan, Persia; trained in Islamic jurisprudence, theology, and philosophy in Baghdad, then became a teacher and advisor to the Abbasid caliph.
- Became the principal defender of orthodox Islam against the rationalist *Mutazilite* school, which argued that reason alone could derive Islamic truth without revelation.
- Authored *Incoherence of the Philosophers* (*Tahafut al-Falasifa*), a systematic refutation of Aristotelian philosophy as applied to Islamic doctrine; argued that philosophers could not prove the eternity of the world or deny divine omniscience.
- Stressed the limits of reason in theology; asserted that matters like the nature of God's attributes and divine predestination must be accepted on faith, not rationalism.
- Became deeply influenced by Sufism (Islamic mysticism) and integrated contemplative, devotional practice into orthodox Islamic theology—a synthesis that elevated Sufism's status within Islam.
- Authored *The Revival of Islamic Sciences* (*Ihya' Ulum al-Din*), an encyclopedic synthesis of Islamic law, ethics, and spirituality that became a canonical reference for centuries.
- Engaged in intellectual debates with philosophers like *Averroes*, who represented the continued rationalist tradition in Islamic Spain; Averroes replied to al-Ghazali's *Incoherence* with his own *Incoherence of the Incoherence*.
