## summary

The **printing press** is a transformative mechanical technology for transferring text and imagery onto paper, cloth, or parchment, superseding manual scribal transcription. Originating in [[china]] through *Tang dynasty* woodblock printing and the eleventh-century invention of **movable type** by *Bi Sheng*, the medium reached global prominence when *Johannes Gutenberg* introduced the movable-metal-type screw press in fifteenth-century [[germany]]. By enabling rapid textual duplication, standard pagination, and affordable distribution, it dismantled aristocratic monopolies on knowledge, catalyzed the [[renaissance]] and the Protestant Reformation, and fueled scientific progress across the early modern world.

## you gotta know

- Perfected in Europe around 1440 by the Mainz goldsmith *Johannes Gutenberg*, who combined durable lead-tin alloy type, an adjustable hand mold, oil-based ink, and an adapted agricultural screw press to produce the famed forty-two-line *Gutenberg Bible*.
- Replaced the slow, labor-intensive craft of manuscript copying by medieval monks and professional scribes, drastically lowering production costs while enforcing orthographic consistency and reliable cross-copy standardization.
- Propelled the Protestant Reformation across [[germany]] and Europe by mechanically duplicating vernacular translations of the scriptures and polemical tracts written by *Martin Luther*, outstripping ecclesiastical attempts at censorship.
- Revolutionized vernacular European literature and early modern drama, providing English printer *William Caxton* and subsequent London publishing houses the infrastructure to preserve canonical collections such as the *First Folio* of [[william-shakespeare]].
- Emerged centuries prior in imperial [[china]], where woodblock printing on mulberry paper flourished under the *Tang dynasty*, yielding the world's earliest complete, dated printed work, the *Diamond Sutra*, in 868 CE to disseminate the scriptures of [[buddhism]].
- Evolved into modular typography during the *Song dynasty*, when commoner *Bi Sheng* fashioned reusable baked-clay characters set into iron frames, a system later refined with wooden fonts by *Wang Zhen* to mass-produce civil service examination treatises and works of [[neo-confucianism]].
- Achieved early movable metal casting in thirteenth-century *Goryeo* Korea, where state printers cast bronze types to stamp state records and produced the Buddhist anthology *Jikji* in 1377, decades before Gutenberg's independent European invention.

## connections

- [[china]] — cradle of papermaking, woodblock printing, and the original ceramic modular characters designed to spread administrative and religious texts.
- [[germany]] — regional home of Mainz, Strasbourg, and Nuremberg, where metal casting and screw-press mechanics sparked the European print revolution.
- [[renaissance]] — cultural rebirth whose humanist scholars relied on printed editions of classical Greek and Latin literature to circulate humanist philosophy across [[italy]] and the continent.
- [[christianity]] — world religion deeply altered when mass-market vernacular editions of the [[hebrew-bible|Old Testament]] and New Testament fractured ecclesiastical authority and fostered individual literacy.
- [[buddhism]] — devotional faith whose monastic communities commissioned the earliest woodblock sutras and printed devotional icons across East Asia as acts of spiritual merit.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — celebrated playwright whose dramatic corpus was immortalized, commodified, and standardized across the Anglophone world through the print trade.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual era powered by the widespread circulation of independent newspapers, political broadsides, scientific treatises, and cooperative encyclopedias.
