## summary

**Joe Clark** is a Canadian statesman and diplomat who served as the sixteenth prime minister of Canada from 1979 to 1980. Entering office at the age of thirty-nine, he became the youngest prime minister in the country's history after defeating *Pierre Trudeau*'s long-ruling Liberals. Although his minority government fell after only nine months over a controversial austerity budget, he later distinguished himself as a highly respected Secretary of State for External Affairs during the end of the [[cold-war]].

## you gotta know

- Became the youngest prime minister in Canadian history when sworn into office in June 1979, representing the Progressive Conservative Party as an Alberta member of Parliament.
- His minority administration collapsed in December 1979 when the House of Commons passed a motion of non-confidence against his government's budget, which introduced an eighteen-cent excise tax on gasoline.
- Worked during his premiership alongside the [[governor-of-the-bank-of-canada]], *Gerald Bouey*, during an era of stagflation, spiraling energy prices, and double-digit interest rates.
- Oversaw Canadian authorization of the covert *Canadian Caper* in 1979–1980, coordinating with Ambassador *Ken Taylor* in Tehran to rescue six American diplomats during the Iran hostage crisis.
- Served as Secretary of State for External Affairs under Prime Minister *Brian Mulroney* from 1984 to 1991, playing an active diplomatic role as the [[cold-war]] concluded.
- Led aggressive Canadian diplomatic pressure within the Commonwealth against apartheid in South Africa, working with international leaders against racial segregation.
- Returned as leader of the federal Progressive Conservative Party from 1998 to 2003, opposing the party's eventual merger with the *Canadian Alliance*.

## connections

- [[john-diefenbaker]] — antecedent Progressive Conservative prime minister from Western Canada whose prairie populist base paved the way for Clark's rise.
- [[governor-of-the-bank-of-canada]] — central banking authority that implemented severe anti-inflationary rate hikes during Clark's turbulent minority tenure.
- [[green-party-of-canada]] — party whose founding in 1983 was catalyzed in part by public backlash to conventional energy policies debated during his era.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical rivalry framing his foreign affairs leadership during the fall of the Berlin Wall and the collapse of the *Soviet Union*.
- [[united-states]] — continental ally whose diplomats were sheltered and rescued by Canadian personnel in Tehran under Clark's government.
- [[british-empire]] — historic institutional network whose Commonwealth summit meetings Clark utilized to mobilize diplomatic sanctions against apartheid.
