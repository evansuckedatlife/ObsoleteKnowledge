## summary

The **Monroe Doctrine** was President *James Monroe*'s declaration in 1823 that the Western Hemisphere was closed to further European colonization and that any attempt by European powers to interfere in the Americas would be regarded as a threat to United States security. Though the U.S. lacked the military power to enforce it, the doctrine became foundational to American foreign policy and hemispheric supremacy, undergirding a century of U.S. intervention in Latin America.

## you gotta know

- Announced by President [[James Monroe]] in December 1823 in his annual message to Congress, drafted primarily by Secretary of State [[John Quincy Adams]].
- Declared that European powers could not establish new colonies in the Western Hemisphere and that the U.S. would interpret such attempts as hostile acts against American sovereignty.
- Asserted that the U.S. would not interfere in European colonial possessions or the internal affairs of existing European nations, staking a position of non-interference in Europe.
- Responded to Russian expansion claims in the Pacific Northwest, the threat of European intervention in Spanish America, and the wave of Latin American independence movements.
- Initially had little enforcement power but gained credibility as U.S. naval and economic strength grew throughout the 19th century.
- Became the foundation for American claims to hemispheric hegemony and shaped U.S. relations with Latin America for generations, establishing the U.S. as the dominant regional power.
- Invoked and reinterpreted by later presidents, most notably [[Theodore Roosevelt]], who added the Roosevelt Corollary asserting the U.S. right to intervene in Latin American affairs to maintain order.
- Remains an iconic statement of U.S. foreign policy, cited throughout history to justify both intervention and non-intervention alike, shaping American diplomatic tradition.
