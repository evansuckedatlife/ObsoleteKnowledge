## summary

**Democracy** is a form of government in which political power rests with the people, exercised either directly or through elected representatives. While the concept traces its philosophical roots to ancient thought, it emerged as a practical political system in [[athens]] and remained largely dormant until revolutionary movements of the 18th century revived and globalized it. Modern democracy stands as the dominant legitimizing principle for political authority across the developed world.

## you gotta know

- Originated as direct democracy in ancient [[athens]] around the 5th century BCE, where male citizens voted directly on major decisions in the assembly.
- Distinct from tyranny, oligarchy, and monarchy—the three forms of rule that Aristotle and other Greek philosophers contrasted with democratic systems.
- Largely disappeared after the fall of [[ancient-greece]], replaced by hierarchical monarchies and feudal systems during the [[medieval-europe|medieval period]].
- Revived as a political ideal during the [[american-revolution]], which established the first modern democratic republic based on representative government.
- Requires the rule of law, separation of powers, and protection of individual rights to function as a stable system rather than degenerating into mob rule.
- Became intertwined with liberal political thought and capitalism, though philosophers debated whether democracy and economic freedom necessarily go together.
- The 19th century witnessed an expansion of voting rights in the West, with franchise reform movements pushing for universal male suffrage and eventually women's suffrage.
- Distinguished by its emphasis on legitimacy derived from the consent of the governed rather than from divine right, hereditary privilege, or military force.
- Modern democracy typically combines representative institutions, regular elections, multiple competing parties, and civil liberties protections to prevent tyranny of majorities.

## connections

- [[athens]] — the birthplace and historical exemplar of democratic practice in the ancient world.
- [[american-revolution]] — established democracy as a viable modern system through constitutional government.
- [[liberal-party]] — championed democratic reform and expanded voting rights in the 19th century.
- [[magna-carta]] — a foundational document constraining arbitrary power and establishing the principle of rule by law.
- [[republic]] — the governmental form that democracy typically takes in practice.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual movement that provided the philosophical justification for democratic systems.
