# Trail of Tears

## summary

The **Trail of Tears** was the catastrophic forced removal of Native American tribes—primarily the Cherokee, Creek, Chickasaw, Choctaw, and Seminole—from their ancestral homelands in the southeastern United States to designated territories in present-day Oklahoma between 1831 and 1850. Thousands died during the journey from disease, starvation, and exposure, making it one of the deadliest ethnic cleansings in American history and a defining tragedy of Indian removal policy.

## you gotta know

- Authorized by the *Indian Removal Act* of 1830 under President *Andrew Jackson*, the policy was built on the premise of "civilizing" and "concentrating" Native peoples away from expanding white settlements.
- The *Cherokee removal* (1838–39) followed the *Trail of Tears* proper: over 4,000 Cherokee died in winter conditions during the forced march from present-day North Carolina and Georgia to Indian Territory (Oklahoma), out of roughly 16,000 displaced.
- The *Creek War* (1813–14) preceding removal saw brutal conflict between factions; the *Treaty of Fort Jackson* (1814) stripped the Creek Nation of millions of acres, setting the precedent for mass dispossession.
- Other nations forcibly removed included the *Choctaw* (1831–33, with roughly 4,000 deaths), *Chickasaw* (1837–38), *Seminole* (1838–42, the most violent removal), and *Creek* (1834–37).
- The removals were justified by pseudo-legal treaties often imposed under duress or signed by unrepresentative factions; the Cherokee fought removal in court (*Worcester v. Georgia*, 1831) but were ignored by Jackson's administration.
- The destination—Indian Territory—was portrayed as permanent, but western expansion and the *Oklahoma Land Run* (1889) soon invaded this refuge, repeating the cycle of dispossession.
