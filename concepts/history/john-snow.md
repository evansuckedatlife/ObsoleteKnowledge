## summary

**John Snow** was an English physician who became the founder of modern epidemiology through his investigation of the 1854 cholera outbreak in London. Using maps, statistics, and rigorous fieldwork, he identified contaminated water as the disease vector—contrary to the prevailing "miasma" theory that blamed bad air—and his work established the scientific method in public health. Though he died before germ theory triumphed, his legacy transformed disease prevention and urban sanitation worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Born in Yorkshire in 1813; became a physician during the early industrial revolution when infectious disease ravaged cities.
- Investigated multiple cholera outbreaks, meticulously documenting cases and their spatial patterns to identify transmission routes.
- During the 1854 Soho outbreak, traced the source to contaminated water from the Broad Street pump through careful interviews and mapping.
- Convinced the local parish to remove the pump handle, ending the outbreak and dramatically reducing cases.
- Published *On the Mode of Communication of Cholera* (1855), proposing water-borne transmission through scientific evidence.
- His work preceded germ theory but provided the empirical foundation that later validated Pasteur and Koch's discoveries.
