## summary

**Henry Luce** was an American publisher and media mogul who founded *Time* magazine and later *Life* magazine, fundamentally shaping how twentieth-century Americans consumed news and visual information. Born to missionary parents in China, Luce pioneered the weekly news digest format and pioneered the large-format photojournalism magazine, making both publications primary cultural forces that shaped public opinion and visual literacy. His vision—that Americans needed accessible, compelling storytelling about world events—created a media empire that dominated American journalism for generations.

## you gotta know

- Founded *Time* magazine in 1923 with partner Briton Hadden, inventing the weekly news digest format to make world events digestible for busy readers.
- Pioneered the "Man of the Year" (now "Person of the Year") concept, which became a cultural touchstone for marking historical significance.
- Founded *Life* magazine in 1936 as a large-format photojournalism publication, revolutionizing visual storytelling and making photography central to news media.
- Built Time Inc. into a massive publishing empire that also included *Fortune* and other influential magazines.
- Held deeply conservative political views, using his magazines as platforms for his anti-communist ideology during the *Cold War*.
- Championed what he called the "American Century"—the idea that American cultural and political dominance was inevitable and desirable in the postwar world.
- Employed renowned photographers like Margaret Bourke-White, whose stunning work helped define the visual aesthetic of twentieth-century journalism.
