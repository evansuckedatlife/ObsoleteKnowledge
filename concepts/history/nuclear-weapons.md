## summary

**Nuclear weapons** are explosive devices whose destructive power derives from atomic fission or fusion reactions, capable of destroying cities and killing millions. Developed in secret during World War II and deployed against Japan in August 1945, they inaugurated an era of unprecedented military and strategic power. Nuclear weapons transformed international relations, deterrence theory, and the calculus of war by introducing the possibility of civilizational annihilation, reshaping geopolitics for the remainder of the 20th century.

## you gotta know

- Two types exist: fission (atomic) bombs, which derive energy from uranium-235 or plutonium-239 splitting, and fusion (thermonuclear) bombs, which derive energy from hydrogen isotope fusion and are many times more powerful.
- The atomic bombs dropped on Hiroshima (6 August 1945) and Nagasaki (9 August 1945) killed approximately 200,000 people instantly and through radiation and burn injuries in the following weeks, including many civilians.
- Demonstrated unprecedented destructive power: a single bomb destroyed an entire city, killed mostly civilians, and introduced radiation sickness and long-term health consequences, raising moral and strategic questions that persist.
- Cold War accumulation saw both superpowers acquiring thousands of warheads and delivery systems (missiles, bombers, submarines), creating arsenals capable of destroying human civilization multiple times over.
- Nuclear weapons changed military strategy by making warfare between nuclear powers existentially risky; conventional wars persisted, but superpowers avoided direct military conflict ("proxy wars" instead).
- Proliferation concerns emerged as more nations acquired weapons: Soviet Union (1949), Britain (1952), France (1960), China (1964), and others, complicating international security and deterrence calculations.
