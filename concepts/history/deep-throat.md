## summary

**Deep Throat** was the pseudonym assigned to the clandestine confidential source who provided critical investigative leaks to *The Washington Post* during the *Watergate* scandal in the [[united-states]]. In 2005, former Associate Director of the FBI **W. Mark Felt** (commonly called **Mark Felt**) came forward to reveal that he had served as the covert informant who steered reporters *Bob Woodward* and *Carl Bernstein*. His disclosures confirmed unlawful surveillance and White House interference, precipitating the fall of the *Richard Nixon* administration during the height of the [[cold-war]].

## you gotta know

- Provided essential verification and leads to reporter *Bob Woodward* during late-night meetings in an underground parking garage in Rosslyn, Virginia, confirming executive branch orchestration of the *Watergate* break-in cover-up.
- Received the pseudonym **Deep Throat** from *The Washington Post* managing editor *Howard Simons*, referencing a famous 1972 pornographic motion picture to reflect deep-background source guidelines.
- Employed secret signaling protocols evocative of classic [[detective-fiction]], such as having Woodward reposition an empty flowerpot containing a red flag on his apartment balcony and marking page numbers in daily copies of *The New York Times*.
- Popularized the famous investigative adage *follow the money*, a line introduced into popular culture by screenwriter *William Goldman* in the 1976 film *All the President's Men* to summarize Felt's investigative guidance.
- Guarded his identity for over three decades until May 2005, when an article in *Vanity Fair* authored by attorney *John O'Connor* publicly confirmed his role with the consent of Felt and his family.
- Rose through the *Federal Bureau of Investigation* under longtime director *J. Edgar Hoover*, leaking evidence partly out of frustration after President *Richard Nixon* bypassed career bureau officials to appoint loyalist *L. Patrick Gray* as acting director.
- Received a criminal conviction in 1980 for ordering unlawful warrantless searches against the homes of friends and family of the radical *Weather Underground*, later obtaining a full presidential pardon from *Ronald Reagan*.

## connections

- [[richard-nixon]] — the president whose administration's illicit surveillance, campaign sabotage, and obstruction efforts Felt systematically exposed.
- [[gerald-ford]] — the vice president who assumed office upon Nixon's resulting resignation and subsequently granted Nixon a controversial full pardon.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose political culture, federal law enforcement ethics, and press protections were transformed by the Watergate affair.
- [[cold-war]] — the global era of state secrecy and intelligence operations during which executive abuse of domestic surveillance took place.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational document whose separation of powers and executive privilege boundaries were tested before the Supreme Court.
- [[detective-fiction]] — the narrative genre whose conventions of shadowy figures, coded missives, and subterranean rendezvous mirrored Felt's tradecraft.
