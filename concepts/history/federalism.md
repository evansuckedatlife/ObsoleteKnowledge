## summary

**Federalism** is a system of governance that divides political authority between a central government and constituent regional governments, each sovereign within its assigned sphere. Developed systematically in the American Constitution of 1787, federalism attempted to balance national unity with local autonomy, preventing both tyrannical centralization and destructive fragmentation. It became a foundational principle of American government and influenced constitutions worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Arose from the failure of the Articles of Confederation, which had created a weak central government subordinate to states, proving inadequate for national defense and commerce.
- Designed by the Constitutional Convention to create a "more perfect union" with sufficient federal power for national purposes while preserving state sovereignty in internal affairs.
- Divided powers explicitly: Congress has authority over interstate commerce, coinage, foreign relations; states retain police powers and sovereignty in local matters.
- Required ratification by nine of thirteen states and sparked intense debate between Federalists (supporting a strong center) and Anti-Federalists (fearing centralized tyranny).
- Incorporated separation of powers and checks and balances to prevent any single branch of government from dominating.
- Evolved through Supreme Court interpretation, particularly John Marshall's decisions like McCulloch v. Maryland, which expanded federal authority through implied powers.
- Created ongoing tension between national and state authority that produced constitutional crises, from nullification in the 1830s to secession and Civil War.
