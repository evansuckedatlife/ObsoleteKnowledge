## summary

**Firdawsi** (also transliterated as **Ferdowsi** or **Hakim Abul-Qasim Ferdowsi Tousi**) was a celebrated Persian epic poet of the medieval Islamic Golden Age who authored the *Shahnameh* ("Book of Kings"), the national epic of Greater Iran. Composed primarily under the patronage of the Ghaznavid ruler *Mahmud of Ghazni* between roughly 977 and 1010 CE, the monumental text spans fifty thousand rhyming couplets that chronicle the mythological origins, legendary champions, and historical dynasties of ancient Persia up to the Muslim conquest. His extraordinary literary labor revitalized Iranian cultural memory and Persian literary traditions after centuries of Arab political and linguistic dominance, securing his reputation as a foundational figure in world literature and across the [[middle-east]].

## you gotta know

- Authored the *Shahnameh*, an immense epic masterpiece that traces Iranian history from the primordial creation of humanity and mythical kings through the historical Sasanian monarchs.
- Deliberately composed the vast majority of his epic in New Persian while consciously avoiding Arabic loanwords, an extraordinary linguistic achievement that stabilized and revived the Persian vernacular.
- Completed the epic in dedication to the Ghaznavid conqueror *Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni*, who according to longstanding tradition broke his royal promise by sending meager silver dirhams rather than gold dinars as payment.
- Authored a blistering verse [[satire]] condemning *Sultan Mahmud* as base-born and unfit for kingship before fleeing Ghazni to escape the sultan's wrath.
- Memorialized iconic folk heroes such as the legendary champion *Rostam*, his miraculous horse *Rakhsh*, and the tragic battle against his own son *Sohrab*, whom he unwittingly slayed in single combat.
- Detailed mythological figures and benevolent creatures such as the wise mythical bird *Simurgh*, who nurtured the discarded albino youth *Zal* in her mountaintop nest.
- Faced severe religious hostility from local Sunni authorities in Tus who condemned him for his suspected Shi'ite inclinations and his lavish praise of Zoroastrian monarchs, briefly barring his corpse from the communal Muslim cemetery.
