## summary

The **Iran-Iraq War** (1980–1988) was a brutal eight-year conflict between the Islamic Republic of *Iran* under *Ayatollah Khomeini* and the Iraqi Ba'athist regime of *Saddam Hussein*. Rooted in territorial disputes, ideological antagonism, and regional power struggles, the war became one of the deadliest conflicts of the late 20th century, with combined casualties exceeding one million and leaving both nations economically devastated. The conflict drew in superpowers and regional actors, reshaped Middle Eastern geopolitics, and illustrated the limits of conventional warfare in the *Cold War* era.

## you gotta know

- Initiated by *Iraq* in September 1980, ostensibly over control of the *Shatt al-Arab* waterway; Saddam Hussein sought to exploit perceived weakness following Iran's 1979 Islamic Revolution and prevent Iranian expansionism.
- Iran, isolated internationally and economically weakened by the revolution, unexpectedly repelled Iraqi advances and eventually launched counteroffensives that pushed the war into Iraqi territory, forcing prolonged stalemate.
- The war featured trench warfare, chemical weapons (deployed by Iraq), naval blockade, and "tanker wars" targeting petroleum commerce in the Persian Gulf; civilian populations suffered tremendously from air raids and artillery bombardment.
- The *Soviet Union* supplied weapons and technical support to Iraq; the *United States*, officially neutral but strategically aligned against Iran, provided intelligence and indirect support to Iraq through intermediaries and allies.
- The *Iran-Contra affair* exposed covert U.S. arms sales to Iran (nominally embargo'ed) while simultaneously supporting Iraq, revealing the complex hidden diplomacy of the conflict.
- Both nations suffered catastrophic economic losses; Iraq accumulated massive foreign debt; Iran's development stalled and its population endured widespread deprivation and displacement.
- Ended inconclusively in August 1988 with a UN-brokered ceasefire that restored pre-war borders, leaving neither side victorious but both exhausted; Khomeini's acceptance of the ceasefire was described by him as drinking "a cup of poison."
