## summary

The **Domino Theory** was an influential foreign policy doctrine prominent in the *[[united-states]]* throughout the *[[cold-war]]*, positing that the fall of a single sovereign state to communism would inevitably trigger a chain reaction of collapses in surrounding nations. First articulated metaphorically by President *Dwight D. Eisenhower* in 1954 regarding Southeast Asia, the concept built upon earlier containment doctrines and provided the strategic imperative for expanding American military commitments across the Pacific. The principle governed Washington's strategic calculus from the *[[korean-war]]* to the intervention in Vietnam, asserting that failing to halt ideological expansion abroad would compromise global security.

## you gotta know

- Popularized by *Dwight D. Eisenhower* during an April 7, 1954 press conference addressing the *First Indochina War*, where he compared vulnerable Southeast Asian countries to a row of standing dominoes that would tumble consecutively if the first was knocked over.
- Conceptually rooted in *George F. Kennan's* 1946 Long Telegram and containment strategy, which argued that Soviet expansionism had to be vigilantly contained at every border point to cause its eventual internal collapse.
- Prefigured by *Dean Acheson*, who famously utilized the related metaphor of "rotten apples" in a single barrel corrupting the entire container when urging assistance to Greece and Turkey under the *[[harry-s-truman]]* administration.
- Provided the central operational justification for the escalation of American troop deployments and air strikes in Indochina under Presidents *John F. Kennedy* and *Lyndon B. Johnson*.
- Prompted the creation of mutual defense pacts such as the *Southeast Asia Treaty Organization* (SEATO) to erect a coordinated diplomatic and military cordon around emergent non-communist regimes.
- Underpinned covert operations and interventionist initiatives across Latin America, Africa, and Asia designed to prevent newly independent post-colonial states from aligning with the *[[soviet-union]]*.
- Criticized extensively by contemporary diplomats and later historians who argued the model wrongly conflated distinct local anti-colonial struggles with monolithic international communism while ignoring historical rivalries, such as tensions between *[[china]]* and Vietnam.
- Challenged empirically after the 1975 fall of Saigon; while communism did spread to neighboring Laos and Cambodia, it failed to trigger the predicted regional collapse of other Southeast Asian nations such as Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — the global ideological and geopolitical confrontation that provided the entire foundation and justification for domino modeling.
- [[soviet-union]] — the primary superpower rival whose perceived goal of world communist revolution the doctrine sought to counter.
- [[united-states]] — the global power whose executive branch and national security apparatus transformed the metaphor into formal statecraft.
- [[korean-war]] — an early armed conflict waged on the premise that unchecked communist aggression on the Korean peninsula would destabilize East Asia.
- [[harry-s-truman]] — the president whose namesake doctrine and anti-communist containment policies laid the groundwork for the theory.
- [[colonialism]] — the decaying imperial system whose sudden dissolution across Asia and Africa left power vacuums vulnerable to revolutionary insurgencies.
- [[china]] — the communist triumph under *Mao Zedong* in 1949 that heightened Western anxieties regarding an unstoppable red tide in Asia.
