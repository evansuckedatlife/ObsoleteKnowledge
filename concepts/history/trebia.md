## summary

The **Battle of the Trebia** (or simply the **Trebia**) was the first major military engagement of the Second Punic War, fought in late December of 218 BC between the Carthaginian invasion army of *Hannibal Barca* and the Roman Republic led by consul *Tiberius Sempronius Longus*. Taking place along the floodplains of the Trebia River near modern Piacenza in northern [[italy]], the battle highlighted *Hannibal*'s extraordinary ability to exploit terrain, weather, and the psychological flaws of his opponents. The crushing Carthaginian triumph shattered Roman military confidence, established *Hannibal*'s reputation as a tactical master, and prompted the Celtic tribes of Cisalpine Gaul to revolt against [[rome]] and supply thousands of auxiliary troops to the Carthaginian cause.

## you gotta know

- Fought in December 218 BC, the battle marked the first major pitched confrontation and decisive victory of *Hannibal Barca* against [[rome]] after his historic crossing of the Alps.
- *Hannibal* deliberately provoked the rash and aggressive Roman consul *Tiberius Sempronius Longus* into battle by sending Numidian light cavalry to skirmish at the gates of the Roman camp at daybreak.
- Unprepared and unbreakfasted Roman soldiers were ordered to pursue the retreating skirmishers directly across the freezing, chest-deep waters of the swollen Trebia River, leaving them hypothermic, exhausted, and demoralized before battle commenced.
- *Hannibal* ensured his own men were well-fed, rested, and gathered around fires rubbing themselves with olive oil to insulate their bodies against the freezing winter sleet and biting river winds.
- A hidden ambush detachment commanded by *Hannibal*'s younger brother *Mago Barca*, consisting of two thousand veteran cavalry and infantry concealed in a brush-choked watercourse, emerged at the climax of the fight to strike the Roman rear.
- Carthaginian war elephants, heavy Celtic infantry, and superior Numidian cavalry overwhelmed the Roman flanks, cutting off retreat and driving thousands of panicked legionaries back into the deadly river current.
- Demonstrating remarkable discipline despite complete encirclement, a central formation of approximately ten thousand Roman heavy infantry pierced through *Hannibal*'s Gallic center and retreated in good order to the fortress colony of *Placentia*.
- Co-consul *Publius Cornelius Scipio*—the father of the future victor of [[zama]], *Scipio Africanus*—had strongly urged caution while convalescing from wounds suffered at the Ticinus River, but *Sempronius* overruled him in pursuit of personal electoral glory.

## connections

- [[cannae]] — *Hannibal*'s tactical masterpiece two years later in southern [[italy]], where he achieved the legendary double encirclement of an entire Roman army.
- [[lake-trasimene]] — the catastrophic Roman defeat in 217 BC in which *Hannibal* concealed his army along a lakeside ridge and destroyed the legions of *Gaius Flaminius*.
- [[zama]] — the climactic 202 BC engagement in North Africa where *Scipio Africanus* defeated *Hannibal* to permanently break the military power of Carthage.
- [[rome]] — the resilient ancient republic whose citizens and senate refused to surrender despite suffering catastrophic casualties in the early years of the war.
- [[italy]] — the Italian peninsula that served as the primary theater of devastating military operations and strategic maneuvering for over fifteen grueling years.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian stronghold that provided the Carthaginian empire with wealthy silver mines, elite mercenary troops, and the operational staging ground for the Italian expedition.
- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean superpower whose later imperial dominance was directly shaped by the bitter existential survival lessons learned against Carthage.
