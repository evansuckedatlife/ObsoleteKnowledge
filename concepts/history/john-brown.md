## summary

**John Brown** was a radical abolitionist whose violent tactics and ultimate execution transformed him into a martyr for the antislavery cause. His 1859 raid on the federal armory at Harpers Ferry, Virginia, intended to spark a slave rebellion, failed militarily but succeeded in galvanizing the nation toward civil war. Hailed as a hero by abolitionists and condemned as a terrorist by the South, Brown embodied the polarization that made peaceful compromise impossible.

## you gotta know

- Born in Connecticut in 1800; became deeply religious and devoted himself entirely to the abolition of slavery.
- Believed that slavery could only be destroyed through violence and was willing to shed blood—both slaveholders' and his own—to achieve emancipation.
- Participated in pro-abolition militias during the Kansas-Nebraska violence of the 1850s, earning the nickname "Old Brown."
- Led an armed band of followers, including his own sons, in the October 1859 raid on the Harpers Ferry armory seeking to arm enslaved people.
- Captured after a three-day siege; tried for treason against Virginia and executed by hanging in December 1859.
- His execution made him a symbol of northern commitment to antislavery and a rallying cry for southern secession.
