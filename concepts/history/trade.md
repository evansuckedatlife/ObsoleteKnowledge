## summary

**Trade** is the exchange of goods and services between individuals, regions, or nations—an economic activity that has driven human civilization, cultural exchange, and geopolitical power since antiquity. From the ancient [[Silk Road]] connecting East and West to the modern global economy, trade has stimulated innovation, enriched merchant classes, sparked imperial expansion, and woven distant peoples into networks of interdependence. Commercial networks created both prosperity and exploitation, making trade a force as culturally transformative as it was economically vital.

## you gotta know

- The [[Silk Road]] was not a single route but a network of overland and maritime trade routes connecting China, Central Asia, India, the Middle East, and Europe for over a millennium.
- Medieval Venice and Genoa grew wealthy by controlling Mediterranean trade routes and serving as intermediaries between Europe and the Islamic world.
- The Dutch Golden Age (seventeenth century) emerged from maritime trade dominance; the Dutch East India Company became history's first true multinational corporation.
- European mercantilism theorized that national wealth derived from accumulating precious metals through trade surpluses, driving colonization to secure resources and markets.
- The Columbian Exchange, initiated by Columbus's voyages, created new transatlantic trade networks in sugar, tobacco, and eventually slaves—transforming both hemispheres.
- The Industrial Revolution dramatically accelerated trade through mechanized production, steam-powered transportation, and imperial networks that extracted resources from colonies.
- Trade networks often embedded power hierarchies: wealthy nations and trading companies exploited colonial resources and labor, concentrating wealth while impoverishing peripheries.
