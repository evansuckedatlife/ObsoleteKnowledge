## summary

The **Barbary Wars** were two naval conflicts fought between the fledgling [[united-states]] and the North African corsair regencies of Tripoli, Algiers, and Tunis—nominally autonomous territories of the [[ottoman-empire]]—alongside Morocco from 1801 to 1815. Consisting of the **First Barbary War** (or **Tripolitanian War**) and the **Second Barbary War**, the struggle erupted when American leadership refused to continue paying tribute and ransoms to protect merchant shipping from Mediterranean privateers. The wars marked the first foreign military operations of the young republic, established the operational reputation of the nascent American navy, and successfully brought an end to customary pirate tribute demands.

## you gotta know

- First Lieutenant *Presley O'Bannon* and consul *William Eaton* led a daring overland march across the Libyan desert to capture Derna in 1805, securing the first foreign land victory for the [[united-states]] and inspiring the phrase "to the shores of Tripoli" in the Marines' Hymn.
- Lieutenant *Stephen Decatur* led a nocturnal raid into Tripoli harbor in 1804 aboard the captured ketch *Intrepid* to burn the grounded frigate *Philadelphia*, an exploit that British Admiral *Lord Nelson* famously hailed as the "most bold and daring act of the age."
- President *[[thomas-jefferson]]* triggered the conflict by refusing to pay increased extortion fees to *Yusuf Karamanli*, the Pasha of Tripoli, who dramatized his declaration of war by cutting down the flagpole of the American consulate.
- Captain *William Bainbridge* ran the heavy frigate *Philadelphia* aground on an uncharted reef off Tripoli in 1803, leading to the capture of the ship and the nineteen-month imprisonment of its crew of over three hundred men.
- Commodore *Edward Preble* commanded the Mediterranean squadron from the *USS Constitution*, directing repeated naval bombardments of Tripoli and mentoring an aggressive cadre of junior officers known as "Preble's Boys" who later led the fleet during the *[[war-of-1812]]*.
- Commodore *Stephen Decatur* resolved the conflict during the 1815 Algerine expedition by defeating the flagship *Meshuda*, killing corsair admiral *Raïs Hamidou*, and forcing the Dey of Algiers to sign an immediate treaty ending tribute payments and releasing all captives.
- European maritime powers like the *[[british-empire]]*, *[[france]]*, and *[[spain]]* had previously normalized paying annual tribute and ransoms to the North African regencies for over a century rather than maintaining continuous naval blockades.

## connections

- [[thomas-jefferson]] — president who rejected tribute demands and deployed naval squadrons to the Mediterranean.
- [[united-states]] — young republic whose early maritime identity and overseas military projection were established in the conflicts.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — nominal imperial sovereign over the semi-autonomous North African regencies of Tripoli, Tunis, and Algiers.
- [[war-of-1812]] — major Anglo-American war that temporarily interrupted American Mediterranean naval operations.
- [[american-revolution]] — war of independence that stripped American merchant shipping of British naval protection.
- [[british-empire]] — primary naval power whose historical practice of paying tribute established the regional status quo.
- [[spain]] — Mediterranean power whose ports, such as Syracuse and Gibraltar, provided essential bases for the American fleet.
