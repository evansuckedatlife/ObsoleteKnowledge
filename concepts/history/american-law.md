## summary

**American law**, or the **law of the United States**, comprises the multifaceted legal traditions, statutory codes, judicial doctrines, and regulatory frameworks operative throughout the [[united-states]]. Rooted primarily in the common law heritage imported from [[england]], it is distinguished by a dual sovereign system of federal and state jurisdictions under the supreme authority of the [[united-states-constitution]]. It plays an indispensable role in global jurisprudence through its development of constitutional judicial review, fundamental rights protections, commercial regulation, and procedural litigation practices.

## you gotta know

- The doctrine of judicial review was firmly established in the landmark 1803 decision *Marbury v. Madison*, confirming that the *Supreme Court* led by *Chief Justice John Marshall* possessed the final power to void acts of *Congress* that conflict with the [[united-states-constitution]].
- Federalism establishes a dual court system in which state judiciaries handle state statutory and common law claims, while federal courts exercise jurisdiction over federal statutes, constitutional controversies, and diversity cases between citizens of different states.
- Deriving directly from traditions in [[england]] during the colonial rule of the [[british-empire]], the system relies on an adversarial court process and the foundational principle of *stare decisis*, requiring courts to adhere to binding legal precedents.
- The adoption of the [[14th-amendment]] during the [[reconstruction-era]] following the [[american-civil-war]] revolutionized constitutional law by applying federal due process and equal protection limits to state governments, empowering subsequent litigation in the [[civil-rights-movement]].
- Twentieth-century jurisprudence was reshaped by *legal realism*, an influential intellectual movement championed by jurists like *Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.* and *Roscoe Pound* that viewed legal rules as pragmatic instruments of social policy rather than static formal logic.
- To address legal divergence across state borders, private scholarly bodies created influential unifying texts including the *Restatements of the Law* published by the *American Law Institute* and the *Uniform Commercial Code*, which standardizes interstate commercial and contract law.
- Distinctive procedural aspects include the preservation of civil jury trials under the *Seventh Amendment*, wide-ranging pretrial evidentiary discovery, widespread use of class actions, and the *American rule*, which obligates each party to pay their own legal counsel.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — provides the supreme legal framework, structural division of powers, and primary source of federal judicial authority.
- [[14th-amendment]] — incorporated the federal *Bill of Rights* against the individual states and established constitutional mandates for equal protection.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — drove major statutory and constitutional litigation that dismantled de jure racial segregation and expanded federal civil rights enforcement.
- [[england]] — supplied the fundamental common law precedents, adversarial trial customs, and jury conventions upon which early colonial jurisprudence was modeled.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — generated the defining constitutional amendments that expanded national citizenship, federal court jurisdiction, and civil rights protections.
- [[american-revolution]] — separated the colonies from British sovereignty and prompted the newly independent states to replace royal charters with written constitutions.
- [[enlightenment]] — provided the political philosophy of natural rights, popular sovereignty, and separation of powers that animated early constitutional design.
