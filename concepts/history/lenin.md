## summary

**Vladimir Lenin** (1870–1924) was the revolutionary leader who founded the Soviet Union and the first communist state in the world. A committed Marxist who adapted communist theory to Russian conditions, he led the Bolshevik faction to power during the October Revolution of 1917 and created a totalitarian state built on single-party rule. His theories and methods of revolutionary organization transformed global politics and inspired communist movements across the twentieth century.

## you gotta know

- Born *Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov* in rural Russia; adopted the pseudonym "Lenin" as a revolutionary.
- His elder brother was hanged for plotting against the Tsar, an event that radicalized young Vladimir and pushed him toward revolutionary politics.
- Moved through illegal agitation and multiple exiles before returning to Russia in April 1917 during the chaos of World War I.
- Published *The State and Revolution* (1917), a foundational text arguing that the working class could not use existing capitalist states but must destroy and replace them entirely.
- Led the Bolsheviks—the revolutionary faction of Russian Marxists—in the October (November) Revolution that toppled the liberal provisional government.
- Consolidated Bolshevik power through the Russian Civil War (1918–1922) against anti-communist "White" forces backed by Western powers and intervening Allied armies.
- Established "democratic centralism" as the organizational principle: absolute obedience to central party leadership once decisions were made, silencing dissent within the party.
- Implemented war communism during the civil war—seizing grain and resources—and the New Economic Policy (NEP) to stabilize the economy in the 1920s.
- Suffered a series of strokes from 1922 onward; his final years were marked by illness and concern about succession as he grew increasingly wary of [[Stalin]].
- Died in 1924, leaving his successor [[Stalin]] to transform the Soviet state into a totalitarian superpower, reversing some of Lenin's more moderate policies.
