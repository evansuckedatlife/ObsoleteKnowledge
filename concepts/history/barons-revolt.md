## summary

The **Barons' Revolt**, historically designated as the **First Barons' War**, was an armed civil conflict from 1215 to 1217 fought between a confederation of English landholders and the autocratic crown under *King John*. Erupting after the monarch repudiated the landmark *Magna Carta* drafted at *Runnymede*, the conflict led the insurgent barons to offer the crown to Prince *Louis* of [[france]], who landed troops and occupied London. The crisis fundamentally transformed medieval constitutional governance by demonstrating that the English monarchy could be militarily compelled to respect customary liberties, establishing an enduring legal precedent that eventually shaped the governance of the [[british-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Precipitated by the confrontation at *Runnymede* in June 1215, where twenty-five rebel barons forced *King John* to grant *Magna Carta* ("the Great Charter") after the insurgents seized London and cut off royal revenues.
- Escalated into full-scale war after *Pope Innocent III* issued a papal bull annulling *Magna Carta* at John's behest, declaring the document illegal and excommunicating the rebel leadership.
- Led the dissident barons, operating under the command of *Robert Fitzwalter* as "Marshal of the Army of God", to formally invite Prince *Louis* of [[france]] to invade [[england]] and claim the vacant throne.
- Transformed completely upon the sudden death of *King John* from dysentery at *Newark Castle* in October 1216, removing the main target of baronial anger and leaving the realm to his nine-year-old son *Henry III*.
- Prompted the royalist protector *William Marshal*, *1st Earl of Pembroke*, to reassert Plantagenet legitimacy by reissuing an amended version of *Magna Carta* alongside the *Charter of the Forest*, successfully swaying defectors away from French allegiance.
- Shattered the rebel cause in May 1217 when eighty-year-old *William Marshal* led a daring counterattack at the *Battle of Lincoln*, routing rebel forces in a chaotic city assault satirized by chroniclers as the "Lincoln Fair".
- Cut off foreign reinforcement in August 1217 at the naval *Battle of Sandwich*, where royal ships under *Hubert de Burgh* blinded French crews with unslaked lime and captured the dreaded mercenary *Eustace the Monk*.
- Concluded diplomatically with the *Treaty of Lambeth* (or *Treaty of Kingston*) in September 1217, which granted full amnesty to the rebels, secured Prince *Louis*'s withdrawal with a cash indemnity, and permanently reaffirmed the charter.

## connections

- [[england]] — the island realm whose royal finances, aristocratic liberties, and common law traditions were permanently reshaped by the civil war.
- [[france]] — the rival Capetian kingdom whose crown prince invaded Kent, entered London, and briefly ruled half of the country before defeat.
- [[christianity]] — the overarching spiritual authority represented by the Papacy, which backed *King John*, suspended the Archbishop of Canterbury, and excommunicated the rebels.
- [[british-empire]] — the later global power whose constitutional foundations and parliamentary checks on sovereign authority directly descend from this thirteenth-century baronial challenge.
- [[king-john]] — the Angevin monarch whose military collapses in Normandy, exorbitant scutage taxes, and arbitrary exactions pushed the baronage into open rebellion.
- [[feudalism]] — the customary system of reciprocal land tenure, baronial privileges, and military duty that the rebellion sought to protect against autocratic royal encroachment.
- [[william-marshal]] — the legendary knight, statesman, and royalist regent whose military leadership and timely reissuance of the charter saved the young king's realm.
