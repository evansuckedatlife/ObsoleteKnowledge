## summary

The **Federalist Party** was the first major political party in the United States, dominant from 1789 to the early 1800s, advocating for a strong central government and commercial development. Led by *Alexander Hamilton* and *John Adams*, it represented merchants, manufacturers, and coastal elites who believed national cohesion and economic strength required centralized federal authority. The party collapsed after the pivotal *Election of 1800*, when *Thomas Jefferson* and the rival *Democratic-Republicans* defeated them decisively.

## you gotta know

- Formed around *Alexander Hamilton*'s compelling vision of a strong federal government with a national bank, tariffs, and active economic management.
- Backed ratification of the *United States Constitution* and won the nation's early elections through the 1790s.
- Supported *John Adams* throughout his presidency (1797–1801), during which Federalists passed the controversial *Alien and Sedition Acts* to suppress dissent.
- Opposed by *Thomas Jefferson*'s *Democratic-Republicans*, who favored state power and agrarian interests.
- Collapsed irreversibly after the *Election of 1800*, when Jefferson defeated Adams and the party fragmented into irrelevance.
- Its core ideas—a strong executive, fiscal responsibility, and federal commercial power—influenced American conservatism for centuries.
- Drew strong support from merchants, shipowners, and urban commercial interests; opposed by farmers and southern planters who deeply feared federal intrusion on state autonomy.
- Promoted a strong national military, a national debt as an economic tool, and protective tariffs to build American manufacturing independence and shield the nation from foreign economic domination.
- The party's demise after 1800 did not end its ideology: Federalist principles of energetic central government and commercial capitalism deeply influenced later Republican parties and American statecraft throughout the 19th and 20th centuries.
