## summary

**William Henry Harrison** (1773–1841) was the ninth President of the United States, though he served the shortest tenure in presidential history—just 31 days before dying of illness. A military officer famous for his victory against Native American forces at *Tippecanoe* (1811), Harrison was elected in 1840 as a anti-establishment candidate opposing President *Martin Van Buren*. His sudden death precipitated the first presidential succession and left the nation with an unelected vice president, *John Tyler*, who assumed powers unprecedented in American constitutional practice.

## you gotta know

- Born in Virginia as the son of a Founding Father; entered the military and became an Indian fighter and territorial governor of the Northwest Territory during the early republic.
- Commanded U.S. forces at the *Battle of Tippecanoe* (1811) in Indiana Territory against a *Tecumseh*-led confederacy of Native Americans; though tactically inconclusive, he won popular acclaim as an "Indian fighter."
- Became a national hero during the War of 1812, commanding successful campaigns against British forces and their Native American allies.
- Political career began in the House of Representatives and later served as minister to Colombia; remained relatively obscure until the 1840 presidential campaign.
- Elected president in 1840 on an anti-Van Buren, anti-executive-power platform; the [[anti-masonic-party]] supported him, combining with other anti-Democratic forces.
- Delivered the longest presidential inaugural address in history (~8,400 words) despite cold, wet weather without proper clothing—tradition claims his illness stemmed from this exposure.
- Died on April 4, 1841, of pneumonia or enteric illness after only 31 days in office; his death triggered a constitutional crisis about presidential succession.
- *John Tyler*, his vice president, assumed the full powers of the presidency against the expectations of Congressional Whigs who expected Tyler to serve merely as acting president.
