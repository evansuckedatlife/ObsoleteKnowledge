## summary

Soviet intelligence agencies, most famously the *KGB*, served as the political police, secret service, and espionage apparatus of the *Soviet Union* throughout its existence. These organizations were instruments of state control, conducting surveillance on the Soviet population, conducting massive counterintelligence operations during the *Cold War*, and maintaining a vast network of foreign agents worldwide to advance Soviet interests and gather intelligence on Western powers.

## you gotta know

- The *Cheka*, established by *Vladimir Lenin* immediately after the *Russian Revolution*, was the Soviet Union's first secret police; it evolved through successive reorganizations into the *NKVD* under *Stalin* and eventually the *KGB* under *Nikita Khrushchev*.
- Soviet intelligence played a defining role in the Cold War, recruiting deep-cover agents in Western governments, militaries, and intelligence agencies; the *Cambridge Five* spy ring, which included members of the British upper class, was a spectacular Soviet espionage victory in post-war Britain.
- The *KGB* under *Yuri Andropov* became a state within the state during the 1970s and 1980s, wielding enormous power over the Soviet leadership and conducting pervasive domestic surveillance that created a climate of fear and suspicion among the Soviet population.
- Soviet intelligence employed extensive networks of *informants* (доносчики) who reported on neighbors, coworkers, and even family members, making everyday life a landscape of betrayal and suspicion throughout the Soviet era.
- The *GRU* (military intelligence) operated parallel to the KGB, conducting espionage missions globally and recruiting military attachés and defectors; the two agencies competed for resources and sometimes clashed in their operations.
- Soviet defectors—*Oleg Penkovskiy*, *Andrei Sakharov*, *Vitaly Shcherbakov*—risked their lives to expose Soviet espionage operations and internal repression to the West, fundamentally shaping Western understanding of Soviet threats.
