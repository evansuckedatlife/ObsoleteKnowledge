## summary

**Containment** was the cornerstone geopolitical strategy of American foreign policy during the [[cold-war]], designed to halt the geopolitical and ideological expansion of the [[soviet-union]]. Articulated in 1946 and 1947 by American diplomat *George F. Kennan*, the doctrine argued that Soviet power was inherently expansionist but opportunistic, and that persistent resistance would eventually cause it to mellow or collapse. Implemented through diplomatic, economic, and military programs across the globe, the policy guided the [[united-states]] across four decades from the aftermath of [[world-war-ii]] until the dissolution of the Soviet bloc.

## you gotta know

- Formulated by *George F. Kennan* in the 1946 *Long Telegram* from Moscow, and later published anonymously as the *Sources of Soviet Conduct* (the *X Article*) in *Foreign Affairs*.
- Operationally inaugurated by the *Truman Doctrine* in 1947, which pledged American financial and military assistance to Greece and Turkey to prevent communist takeovers.
- Supported economically by the *Marshall Plan*, which injected billions of dollars into Western Europe to stabilize democratic institutions and rebuild shattered postwar industries.
- Resulted in the 1949 creation of *NATO* (*North Atlantic Treaty Organization*), providing a collective security umbrella against potential Soviet conventional aggression.
- Militarized and generalized globally through *NSC-68*, a top-secret policy paper drafted by *Paul Nitze* that urged rapid American rearmament and deterrence worldwide.
- Led directly to military commitments in Asia, notably during the Korean War and the Vietnam War under the perceived threat of the *domino theory*.
- Contrasted with alternative strategic proposals of the era, such as right-wing *rollback* or isolationist disengagement.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — defined the overarching grand strategy pursued by Western powers throughout the decades-long confrontation.
- [[soviet-union]] — served as the primary geopolitical rival whose political influence and military reach the policy sought to check.
- [[united-states]] — transformed the nation's peacetime foreign policy from historic neutrality into permanent worldwide alliance systems.
- [[world-war-ii]] — emerged directly out of the collapse of the wartime Grand Alliance and disagreements over the political reconstruction of Europe.
- [[germany]] — formed the geographic crucible of the policy, sparking the Berlin Airlift and the partition into democratic and communist states.
- [[congressional-black-caucus]] — faced scrutiny from progressive legislators and CBC leaders who criticized military overextension and interventions in postcolonial nations.
