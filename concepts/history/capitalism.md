## summary

**Capitalism** is an economic system based on private ownership of capital, market exchange, wage labor, and the accumulation of profit as the primary economic incentive. Emerging gradually from medieval Europe and crystallizing during the 18th and 19th centuries, it became the dominant global economic system. Adam Smith's theories of the "invisible hand" and self-interest provided intellectual justification, while industrialization and colonialism provided the material infrastructure. Capitalism generated unprecedented wealth but also created stark inequality, labor exploitation, and periodic crises.

## you gotta know

- Emerged gradually from mercantilism and the commercial revolution, accelerating with the enclosure of common lands, the rise of joint-stock companies, and the development of credit and banking systems.
- Assumes that private individuals pursuing self-interest through market competition produces efficient allocation of resources and raises living standards.
- Requires capital accumulation—reinvestment of profits into productive assets—as the engine of growth, distinguishing it from earlier economic systems focused on subsistence or aristocratic patronage.
- Expanded dramatically during the Industrial Revolution, with mechanized production, factory labor, and mass markets creating unprecedented wealth and economic dynamism.
- Integrated with colonialism and slavery, extracting resources and labor from colonies to enrich metropolitan capitalists and fuel industrialization.
- Generated persistent critiques by thinkers like Karl Marx, who argued capitalism contained inherent contradictions—overproduction, worker alienation, and crises—that would eventually cause its collapse.
- Drove technological innovation, urbanization, and rising standards of living in developed nations, while simultaneously creating industrial cities with brutal working conditions and vast wealth disparities.
