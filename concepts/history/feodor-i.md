## summary

**Feodor I**, also known as **Feodor I of Russia**, **Fyodor I**, or **Feodor the Bellringer**, was the final Tsar of Russia from the centuries-old Rurikid dynasty, reigning from 1584 until his death in 1598. Pious, frail, and widely considered intellectually incapable of independent governance, his regime was effectively steered by his ambitious brother-in-law and regent, *Boris Godunov*. His childless death extinguished the direct dynastic line of *Ivan the Terrible*, plunging the Russian state into the severe social, economic, and political catastrophe known as the Time of Troubles.

## you gotta know

- Inherited the Russian throne following the death of his tyrannical father, *Ivan the Terrible*, who had killed his elder brother *Ivan Ivanovich* in a fit of rage three years prior.
- Earned the popular epithet *the Bellringer* due to his extreme religious devotion, spending hours traveling to monasteries and ringing church bells across Moscow rather than attending to state affairs.
- Relegated supreme administrative and foreign policy powers to his regent *Boris Godunov*, whose sister *Irina Godunova* was married to the tsar.
- Oversaw the establishment of the autonomous Patriarchate of Moscow in 1589 under *Job*, achieving spiritual independence for the Russian Orthodox Church from Constantinople.
- Witnessed the suspicious 1591 death of his nine-year-old half-brother, *Tsarevich Dmitry of Uglich*, an event widely blamed on *Boris Godunov* that fueled future pretenders.
- Concluded the Russo-Swedish War of 1590–1595 with the Treaty of Teusina, regaining lost Baltic coastline territories for Muscovy.
- Died in January 1598 without surviving offspring, bringing the seven-century Rurikid dynasty to an end and initiating the dynastic crisis of the Time of Troubles.

## connections

- [[russian-empire]] — the expansive Eurasian power that emerged out of the Tsardom of Muscovy after the Rurikid line collapsed.
- [[christianity]] — the Orthodox faith whose ecclesiastical prestige in Russia surged with the elevation of Moscow to a full patriarchate in 1589.
- [[female-leaders]] — represented in his reign by his consort *Tsarina Irina Godunova*, who briefly held public sovereign authority following his demise.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the imperial power governing Constantinople, from whose ecumenical patriarch the Muscovite regency secured patriarchal autocephaly.
- [[england]] — the commercial partner with whom the tsar's government maintained trade monopolies via the Muscovy Company.
- [[tragedy]] — the dramatic genre that immortalized his court intrigue in Alexander Pushkin's verse drama *Boris Godunov*.
