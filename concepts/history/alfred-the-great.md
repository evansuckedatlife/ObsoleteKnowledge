## summary

**Alfred the Great** was King of the West Saxons from 871 to 899, celebrated for successfully defending his realm against the catastrophic invasions of the Great Heathen Army of Danish Vikings. Through tactical resilience, fort building, and diplomacy, he preserved Wessex as the last surviving independent Anglo-Saxon realm and laid the political framework for a unified [[england]]. His reign was equally distinguished by comprehensive legal reforms, religious devotion, and a profound intellectual renaissance that revitalized literacy and vernacular scholarship across southern Britain.

## you gotta know

- Decisively defeated the Danish Viking king *Guthrum* at the *Battle of Edington* in 878, forcing the invaders to retreat, surrender hostages, and accept peace terms.
- Concluded the *Treaty of Wedmore* and subsequent pacts that established the *Danelaw*, dividing control of Britain and compelling *Guthrum* to undergo conversion to [[christianity]] with Alfred serving as his godfather.
- Retreated during winter 878 to the Somerset marshes at *Athelney*, from which legendary folklore arose depicting the disguised monarch carelessly allowing an unsuspecting peasant woman's cakes to burn on her hearth.
- Revolutionized Anglo-Saxon territorial defense by instituting the *burghal system*, a strategic network of fortified urban centers (*burhs*) garrisoned by royal troops, as recorded in the administrative document known as the *Burghal Hidage*.
- Reorganized the agrarian military levy into a rotational standing army and commissioned fleets of newly designed longships to combat Scandinavian raids, earning him recognition as a traditional precursor to the maritime forces of [[england]].
- Fostered a sweeping cultural revival to remedy the decay of Latin literacy, advocating that young freemen learn to read Old English and personally assisting in translating canonical Latin works, including *Consolation of Philosophy* by *Boethius* and *Pastoral Care* by Pope *Gregory the Great*.
- Promulgated the *Doom Book* (*Dōmbōc*), an extensive legal code synthesizing biblical Mosaic commandments with the ancient Wessex legislation of King *Ine* and Mercian customs of King *Offa*.
- Commissioned the initial compilation of the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* and served as the direct subject of a celebrated contemporary Latin biography, *The Life of King Alfred*, penned by the Welsh monk *Asser*.

## connections

- [[england]] — the unified realm whose territorial integrity and national identity trace back to his preservation of Wessex against Viking conquest.
- [[christianity]] — the religious faith that formed the spiritual core of his rule, inspiring his educational missions, monastic restorations, and diplomatic baptisms.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical civilization whose imperial administration, literary heritage, and legal authority provided the intellectual model for his court.
- [[franks]] — the continental empire whose Carolingian scholarship provided foreign scholars, ecclesiastical models, and inspiration for Alfred's palace school.
- [[british-empire]] — the global naval power whose later chroniclers embraced him as a heroic mythic forefather and the originator of British sea power.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the English national dramatist whose history plays chronicled the ongoing evolution of the medieval crown Alfred fought to preserve.
- [[charles-dickens]] — the Victorian novelist who popularized idealized romantic depictions of the monarch's piety and humble trials in his historical sketches.
