## summary

The **Democratic realignment**, also termed the **New Deal realignment** or **1932 realignment**, was a transformative critical election cycle that ended decades of Republican presidential dominance and established a durable national majority for the Democratic Party. Catalyzed by the catastrophic economic collapse of the Great Depression and the reformist platform of *Franklin D. Roosevelt*, this shift forged the potent New Deal coalition consisting of industrial labor unions, urban ethnic minorities, farmers, progressive intellectuals, and the traditional Solid South. The realignment fundamentally redefined American federalism, economic interventionism, and modern social welfare politics for generations.

## you gotta know

- Triggered by the landslide 1932 presidential election, in which *Franklin D. Roosevelt* defeated incumbent Republican *Herbert Hoover* amidst economic collapse.
- Theorized by political scientist *V. O. Key Jr.* in 1955 as a critical election, characterized by sharp, durable changes in party loyalty and voting coalitions.
- Consolidated the diverse New Deal coalition, bringing together blue-collar industrial workers, Catholic and Jewish immigrants, African American voters, and rural agrarians.
- Reversed the long-standing Republican hegemony established in the 1896 realignment between *William McKinley* and *William Jennings Bryan*.
- Manifested a major demographic migration of Black voters from the Republican Party of *Abraham Lincoln* to the Democrats, accelerating through subsequent civil rights battles.
- Enabled the enactment of landmark domestic welfare legislation, including the Social Security Act of 1935, the Wagner Act, and comprehensive financial regulations.
- Sparked constitutional crises with the conservative Supreme Court, which struck down early legislation and prompted *Roosevelt's* controversial 1937 court-packing plan.
- Began fracturing decades later over federal civil rights intervention and decentralized self-governance, anticipating territorial and regional tensions seen in British *[[devolution]]*.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose political parties and electoral landscape were restructured by the 1932 critical election.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational legal framework whose Commerce Clause and Tenth Amendment were reinterpreted to validate New Deal programs.
- [[devolution]] — the parallel constitutional mechanism of devolving administrative power to regional bodies when centralized national party coalitions experience friction.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the social uprising whose federal legislative victories in the 1960s finally dissolved the southern segment of the New Deal coalition.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional amendment whose equal protection and due process clauses underpinned subsequent federal expansions of citizen welfare and rights.
- [[great-depression]] — the severe global economic crisis that discredited orthodox laissez-faire governance and facilitated massive voter realignment.
