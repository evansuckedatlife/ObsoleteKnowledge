## summary

**Georgy Zhukov** was the Soviet Union's most celebrated military commander during *World War II*, serving as Chief of the General Staff and Marshal of the Soviet Union. His strategic brilliance in coordinating massive defensive operations and counteroffensives proved instrumental in turning the tide against Nazi Germany on the Eastern Front.

## you gotta know

- Rose from cavalry officer to become *Stalin's* principal military strategist, surviving the dictator's purges when many generals perished; appointed Chief of the General Staff in 1941.
- Impressed Stalin with his strategic insight and ability to implement large-scale operations across vast theaters of war.
- Orchestrated the defense at *Stalingrad* in 1942–1943, coordinating the Soviet counteroffensive (Operation Uranus) that encircled and destroyed the German Sixth Army and portions of the Fourth Panzer Army.
- At Stalingrad, managed coordination between multiple Soviet army groups across hundreds of kilometers, demonstrating mastery of complex logistics and multi-front coordination.
- Engineered the victory at *Kursk* in 1943, the largest tank battle in history, where Soviet intelligence and preparation defeated a German offensive (Operation Citadel).
- At Kursk, Soviet intelligence from the *Lucy Ring* spy network provided advance warning of German plans, allowing Zhukov to prepare massive defensive positions and prepare counterattacks.
- Directed *Operation Bagration* in 1944, a coordinated offensive that liberated Belarus and destroyed German Army Group Center with over 400,000 casualties.
- Master of large-scale coordination: combining intelligence, reserve forces, and artillery concentration to overwhelm German defenses; pioneered the Soviet doctrine of deep battle.
- His tactics emphasized massed artillery, coordinated reserve forces, and breakthrough tactics that became model for Soviet military doctrine for decades.
- Fell from favor after the war due to his growing prestige and *Stalin's* paranoia, though rehabilitated under *Nikita Khrushchev* and recognized as a hero of the Soviet Union.
- Lived to see recognition of his contributions; died in 1974 and received a state funeral, cementing his legacy as the architect of Soviet victory.
