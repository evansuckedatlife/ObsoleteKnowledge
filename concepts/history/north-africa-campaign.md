## summary

The **North Africa Campaign**, also known as the **Desert War**, was a major theater of [[world-war-ii]] fought across the deserts of Egypt, Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, and Morocco between June 1940 and May 1943. The campaign pitted Allied forces, spearheaded initially by the [[british-empire]] and later reinforced by the [[united-states]], against the Axis powers of Fascist [[italy]] and Nazi [[germany]]. Allied victory in North Africa secured crucial shipping lanes through the Suez Canal, safeguarded oil reserves in the [[middle-east]], and served as the vital springboard for the invasion of [[italy]] and the ultimate liberation of southern Europe.

## you gotta know

- *Erwin Rommel*, the "Desert Fox," gained legendary military fame commanding the German *Afrika Korps*, executing daring armored thrusts across the Libyan desert before being halted and pushed back.
- The decisive turning point occurred at the Second *Battle of El Alamein* in late 1942, where British Lieutenant-General *Bernard Montgomery* decisively defeated Axis forces in Egypt, permanently shifting the strategic initiative to the Allies.
- *Operation Torch* in November 1942 marked the first major combat commitment of ground forces from the [[united-states]] in the European and Mediterranean spheres, landing troops in Morocco and Algeria under General *Dwight D. Eisenhower*.
- The conflict began in June 1940 when Italian forces invaded Egypt from Libya, ending in catastrophic failure during *Operation Compass* when British troops captured over one hundred thousand prisoners and forced *Adolf Hitler* to intervene.
- The besieged fortress of *Tobruk* became an enduring symbol of Allied defiance during an eight-month siege in 1941, held resolutely by the Australian 9th Division, who proudly embraced the German propaganda slur *Rats of Tobruk*.
- American forces suffered a stinging tactical defeat against experienced German panzers at the *Battle of Kasserine Pass* in February 1943, prompting sweeping military reorganizations and the appointment of *George S. Patton* to command II Corps.
- The campaign concluded in May 1943 with the total collapse of Axis resistance in the *Tunisian Campaign*, where more than two hundred and fifty thousand German and Italian soldiers surrendered, eliminating the Axis presence on the African continent.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the overarching global conflict in which desert fighting formed the primary Western Allied ground campaign against Axis land forces from 1940 through 1943.
- [[germany]] — deployed the expeditionary *Afrika Korps* under *Erwin Rommel* to prevent Italian collapse and threaten British positions across North Africa.
- [[italy]] — instigated hostilities to forge a modern Roman empire and capture Egypt, but suffered devastating reverses that compelled total reliance on German military support.
- [[british-empire]] — marshaled imperial and commonwealth armies from Australia, India, and New Zealand to guard the Suez Canal and protect vital supply routes.
- [[united-states]] — entered the Mediterranean land war through large-scale amphibious landings, gaining hard-won tactical and logistical experience necessary for future assaults on Europe.
- [[middle-east]] — the strategic prize whose oilfields and supply corridors to the [[soviet-union]] remained safely out of reach of Axis conquerors.
- [[france]] — saw its North African protectorates and colonies transition from *Vichy* neutrality to active participation in the Allied coalition following *Operation Torch*.
