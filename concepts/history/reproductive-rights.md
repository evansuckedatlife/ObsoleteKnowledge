# Reproductive Rights

## summary

**Reproductive Rights** are the legal and political freedoms to make decisions about reproduction, including access to contraception and abortion. The movement for reproductive rights has been central to women's liberation struggles globally, from *Margaret Sanger*'s birth control activism in the early 20th century to the landmark *Roe v. Wade* decision (1973) that legalized abortion nationwide in the United States.

## you gotta know

- *Margaret Sanger* pioneered the birth control movement in the 1920s–1930s, challenging laws that criminalized contraception and opening clinics to provide access to working-class women.
- Access to *contraception* was restricted or illegal in many U.S. states until the 1960s; the *Griswold v. Connecticut* decision (1965) established a constitutional right to use contraception, laying groundwork for broader reproductive privacy.
- *Roe v. Wade* (1973) struck down state bans on abortion, establishing a constitutional right to abortion subject to state regulation at different pregnancy trimesters—a transformative decision that energized both reproductive rights advocates and anti-abortion activists.
- The *Women's Liberation Movement* of the 1960s–1970s made reproductive rights a cornerstone issue, linking women's bodily autonomy to economic and social freedom.
- Anti-abortion movements have sought to restrict or overturn abortion access through state legislation, constitutional amendments, and Supreme Court challenges; *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization* (2022) overturned Roe and returned abortion regulation to the states.
- Reproductive rights activism has intersected with racial justice, disability rights, and economic justice movements—recognizing that reproductive freedom is unevenly distributed across race and class lines.
- Global reproductive rights activism continues: debates over access to contraception, abortion legality, and women's bodily autonomy remain contentious in many countries and religious communities.
