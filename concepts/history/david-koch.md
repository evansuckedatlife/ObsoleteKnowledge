## summary

**David Koch** (born **David Hamilton Koch**) was an American chemical engineer, billionaire industrialist, and political donor who served as executive vice president of *Koch Industries*. Emerging as a national political figure when he ran as the vice-presidential nominee for the Libertarian Party in 1980, he spent subsequent decades constructing an expansive apparatus of free-market advocacy groups, policy think tanks, and philanthropic foundations across the [[united-states]]. Along with his brother *Charles Koch*, he reshaped modern American conservative political organizing, financial networks, and cultural philanthropy in [[new-york-city]].

## you gotta know

- Ran as the Libertarian Party vice-presidential nominee in the 1980 presidential election alongside *Ed Clark*, personally providing over two million dollars to secure ballot access and promote [[american-third-parties]] nationally.
- Built, in partnership with his older brother *Charles Koch*, an enormous donor network often described by commentators as the *Koch network*, which directed hundreds of millions of dollars toward free-market advocacy through organizations such as *Americans for Prosperity*.
- Co-founded and subsidized major conservative and market-oriented think tanks, including the *Cato Institute*, the *Mercatus Center*, and the *Reason Foundation*, anchoring modern institutional [[libertarianism]].
- Survived the 1991 *USAir Flight 1493* collision at *Los Angeles International Airport*, escaping a burning aircraft cabin that killed dozens of passengers, an experience he cited as the primary catalyst for his extensive medical philanthropy.
- Endowed major civic and artistic renovations in [[new-york-city]], providing principal funding for the *David H. Koch Theater* at the *Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts*, the *Metropolitan Museum of Art* outdoor plaza, and the dinosaur wing at the *American Museum of Natural History*.
- Financed cancer research and medical facilities following his own diagnosis, establishing the *David H. Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research* at MIT and funding clinical wings at *Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center*.
- Played collegiate basketball at the *Massachusetts Institute of Technology*, serving as team captain and setting a school single-game scoring record of forty-one points in 1962 that stood for decades.

## connections

- [[libertarianism]] — the free-market and anti-regulatory philosophy that anchored his 1980 campaign and guided his lifelong political investments.
- [[american-third-parties]] — alternative political movements whose ballot access and profile he advanced through his self-funded 1980 national candidacy.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose political system, regulatory statutes, and taxation policies he sought to reshape through private advocacy.
- [[new-york-city]] — his longtime place of residence and the primary beneficiary of his large-scale civic and performing arts donations.
- [[richard-nixon]] — the president whose wage-and-price controls and environmental regulations spurred the Koch family to finance free-market institutions.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical contest during which Koch Industries grew rapidly and its leadership developed staunch anti-collectivist economic positions.
