## summary

The **University of Mississippi**, popularly known as **Ole Miss**, is a public university in *Oxford, Mississippi*, that became the flashpoint of massive resistance against desegregation in the American South. In the autumn of 1962, military veteran *James Meredith* sought to exercise his constitutional right to enroll as the university's first African American student, triggering an armed standoff between state authorities and the federal government. The ensuing crisis culminated in the deadly *Ole Miss riot of 1962*, forcing President *John F. Kennedy* to dispatch federal troops to uphold the supremacy of the [[united-states-constitution]] and the legal gains of the [[civil-rights-movement]].

## you gotta know

- *James Meredith*, an Air Force veteran, initiated his legal fight to desegregate the university following President *John F. Kennedy*'s inaugural address, receiving legal representation from *Constance Baker Motley* and the *NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund*.
- Mississippi Governor *Ross Barnett* fiercely resisted court-ordered desegregation, invoking the doctrine of interposition in a statewide radio address and personally blocking Meredith from entering the campus administration building, the *Lyceum*.
- Supreme Court Justice *Hugo Black* issued a decisive order requiring the university to admit Meredith, striking down obstructionist rulings from the *Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals* and rejecting the state's sovereignty claims.
- President *John F. Kennedy* and Attorney General *Robert F. Kennedy* conducted tense telephone negotiations with Barnett before issuing *Executive Order 11053*, which federalized the Mississippi *National Guard* and dispatched U.S. Marshals to *Oxford*.
- A massive riot erupted across campus on September 30, 1962, as thousands of armed segregationists besieged the *Lyceum*, forcing Deputy Attorney General *Nicholas Katzenbach* and federal marshals to repel assaults using tear gas under sustained sniper fire.
- The campus violence claimed the lives of two civilians: French journalist *Paul Guihard*, who was fatally shot in the back while covering the crisis, and local Oxford resident *Ray Gunter*, who was killed by a stray bullet.
- Former U.S. Army Major General *Edwin Walker*, an outspoken segregationist who had previously overseen federal troops at [[little-rock-central-high-school]], arrived in Oxford to exhort the crowd and led violent charges against federal law enforcement.
- Meredith successfully registered for classes on October 1, 1962, attended lectures under twenty-four-hour armed guard by military police, and graduated in August 1963 with a bachelor's degree in political science.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the historic struggle against racial segregation in which Meredith's enrollment represented a decisive victory over Southern defiance.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional guarantee of equal protection that formed the legal foundation for court-ordered university desegregation.
- [[medgar-evers]] — Mississippi *NAACP* field secretary who advised Meredith, coordinated his legal security, and was assassinated in Jackson months later.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — national civil rights leader who supported Meredith's struggle and pressed the Kennedy administration to act aggressively against segregationist violence.
- [[little-rock-central-high-school]] — Arkansas secondary school integrated by federal troops five years earlier, serving as the direct precedent for military intervention in Oxford.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the supreme legal charter whose supremacy clause nullified state attempts to block federal court decrees through interposition.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — post-Civil War period of federal civil rights enforcement whose unfulfilled promises and subsequent rollback shaped Mississippi's segregated legal order.
