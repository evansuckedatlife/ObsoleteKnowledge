# Fascism

## summary

**Fascism** is an authoritarian political ideology that emerged in early 20th-century Europe, characterized by nationalism, militarism, hierarchical social organization, and centralized state control under a charismatic leader. Fascism rejected democracy and liberalism, embraced violence and propaganda, and achieved power in *Italy* (*Benito Mussolini*), *Germany* (*Adolf Hitler*), and elsewhere, resulting in some of history's most destructive regimes.

## you gotta know

- Fascism originated in early 1920s *Italy* under *Benito Mussolini*, who blended nationalist fervor, syndicalist corporatism, and authoritarian hierarchy; it claimed to transcend both capitalism and communism through organic national unity.
- Key fascist characteristics include extreme nationalism (sometimes racial), militarism, cult of personality around the leader, suppression of opposition, corporatist economic organization, and rejection of democratic values and individual rights.
- *Nazi Germany* under *Adolf Hitler* adapted fascism to include antisemitism and racial pseudoscience, producing the most destructive fascist regime; the Nazis merged fascism with racial ideology and launched *World War II*.
- Fascism appeals to insecure middle classes, nationalist sentiment, and desire for order amid chaos; it emerged in countries experiencing economic crisis, national humiliation, or social instability (post-*World War I* Europe, *Italy*, *Germany*).
- Fascist movements used mass propaganda, spectacle, violence (paramilitary street gangs), and control of media to consolidate power and suppress opposition—tactics that influenced later authoritarian movements.
- Fascism's alliance with capitalism and opposition to communism attracted business elites and conservative politicians who initially underestimated fascism's radicalism; fascist regimes ultimately subordinated capitalism to state control.
- Fascism is now widely viewed as morally and politically repugnant; postwar democracies adopted constitutional protections against fascist movements, though fascistic tendencies periodically reemerge in political discourse.
