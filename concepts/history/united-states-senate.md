## summary

The **United States Senate** is one of two chambers of the U.S. Congress, established by the *Constitution* as the upper house of the legislative branch with two senators per state. Designed as a more deliberative body than the *House of Representatives*, the Senate holds unique powers including treaty ratification, impeachment trials, and Supreme Court confirmation. It has evolved from a body exclusively composed of white men to a more representative assembly, particularly following the *Civil Rights Movement*.

## you gotta know

- Established by the *Constitution* with two senators per state, regardless of population, giving every state equal representation in the upper chamber and protecting smaller states' interests.
- Initially, senators were elected by state legislatures, not by popular vote; the *17th Amendment* (1913) shifted power to direct popular election, democratizing Senate selection.
- Holds exclusive powers to ratify treaties (requiring a two-thirds supermajority), confirm presidential appointments (cabinet secretaries, federal judges, Supreme Court justices), and try impeachment cases.
- The filibuster rule allows senators to delay or prevent votes through extended debate; breaking a filibuster requires a 60-vote supermajority to invoke cloture, giving obstruction formidable power.
- *Reconstruction-era* amendments and voting rights acts opened the Senate to Black senators like *Hiram Revels* (1870) and *Blanche Bruce* (1875), though segregation and discrimination delayed true integration for a century.
- Undergoes periodic constitutional crises when presidents face impeachment trials, most notably *Andrew Johnson* (1868), *Richard Nixon* (resigned 1974), and *Donald Trump* (2021, 2024).
- Operates with 100 members total (50 states × 2); each senator serves a six-year staggered term, with roughly one-third elected every two years, ensuring continuity and resistance to electoral waves.
