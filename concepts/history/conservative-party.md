## summary

The **Conservative Party** is one of the two major political forces in British history, emerging as a modern organized party during the 19th century. It traditionally represents property owners, the established church, and social stability, opposing radical reform. The party has shaped British governance for nearly two centuries and remains central to UK politics, alternating power with its rival, the [[liberal-party]].

## you gotta know

- Traces its ideological roots to the *Tory* faction of the 17th century, which supported royal authority during the [[english-civil-war]].
- Formally crystallized as a modern party under [[benjamin-disraeli]] in the 1870s, when he repositioned it as an imperialist force appealing to working-class voters.
- Dominated 19th-century politics through leaders like [[robert-peel]], who championed gradual reform while resisting revolutionary change.
- Won support from both the landed aristocracy and urban middle classes by embracing imperial expansion and opposing radical socialism.
- Reached its peak under [[margaret-thatcher]] in the 1980s, when her government pursued radical free-market economics and weakened trade unions.
- Stands in perpetual tension with the [[liberal-party]] and later the Labour Party as the right-wing pole of British two-party politics.
- Core ideology emphasizes tradition, property rights, national sovereignty, and a hierarchical social order tempered by noblesse oblige and constitutional governance.
- Major policy platforms include opposition to the welfare state (though accepting its basic existence), skepticism of European integration, and emphasis on law and order.
- Alternated power with the Liberals and later Labour throughout the 20th century, dominating the 1920s–1930s, 1950s–1960s, and 1980s–1990s.

## connections

- [[benjamin-disraeli]] — modernized the party into a mass political machine and rebranded it for imperial ambitions.
- [[robert-peel]] — established the intellectual foundation for conservative gradualism and parliamentary pragmatism.
- [[margaret-thatcher]] — embodied radical conservative ideology in the late 20th century.
- [[liberal-party]] — its great rival throughout the 19th century, representing progressive reform.
- [[england]] — the geographic base and political arena where the party has wielded power.
- [[parliament]] — the institution where conservative majorities have governed.
