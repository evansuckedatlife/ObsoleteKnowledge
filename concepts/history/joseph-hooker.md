## summary

**Joseph "Fighting Joe" Hooker** was a Union general whose aggressive tactics and confidence earned him his famous nickname, yet whose inability to execute complex maneuvers led to decisive defeats. Commanding the Army of the Potomac at the [[battle-of-chancellorsville]] in 1863, he faced Robert E. Lee while outnumbering the Confederates but was outmaneuvered and forced to retreat. His career exemplifies the challenges of translating military boldness into operational success during the Civil War.

## you gotta know

- Born in Massachusetts in 1814; graduated from West Point and served in the Mexican-American War before the Civil War.
- Earned his "Fighting Joe" nickname through aggressive brigade-level tactics in early Union campaigns.
- Appointed commander of the Army of the Potomac in early 1863 with a reputation for aggressive maneuvering.
- Devised a bold flanking strategy at Chancellorsville but failed in execution as Lee and Jackson maneuvered brilliantly.
- Suffered a crushing defeat at Chancellorsville in May 1863 despite numerical superiority of two-to-one.
- Relieved of command and later transferred west, where he had greater success as a subordinate officer.
