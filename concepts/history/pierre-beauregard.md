## summary

**Pierre Beauregard**, widely known as **P. G. T. Beauregard** and born **Pierre Gustave Toutant-Beauregard**, was a Louisiana-born officer who served as one of the premier generals of the [[confederate-army]] during the [[american-civil-war]]. He achieved instant acclaim across the South by ordering the bombardment of Fort Sumter and routing federal forces at the First Battle of Bull Run. Although ongoing personal disputes with Confederate President *Jefferson Davis* later limited his influence, his defensive operations at Petersburg and his progressive political activities in the [[reconstruction-era]] established him as a notable American military figure.

## you gotta know

- Commanded the Confederate forces in Charleston that bombarded Fort Sumter in April 1861, compelling the surrender of Union Major *Robert Anderson* and commencing the [[american-civil-war]].
- Routed the [[union-army]] commanded by General [[irvin-mcdowell]] at the First Battle of Bull Run in July 1861 alongside General *Joseph E. Johnston*, becoming the Confederacy's foremost war hero.
- Promoted the design of the square Confederate Battle Flag alongside *William Porcher Miles* after noticing that the "Stars and Bars" caused hazardous confusion with federal banners in battle smoke.
- Assumed command of the Army of the Mississippi at the Battle of Shiloh following the death of General *Albert Sidney Johnston*, halting the evening assault before being repelled by *Ulysses S. Grant*.
- Bottled up Union General *Benjamin Butler* and his Army of the James on the Bermuda Hundred peninsula in 1864, neutralizing a direct threat to the Confederate capital of Richmond.
- Conducted a brilliant defensive stand during the opening phase of the Siege of Petersburg in June 1864, repelling larger Union forces until General *Robert E. Lee* arrived with reinforcements.
- Earned the nicknames "The Little Creole" and "Little Napoleon" due to his French Louisiana lineage, theatrical temperament, and reliance on *Napoleonic* strategic maneuvers.
- Promoted racial equality and voting rights as a leader of the Louisiana Unification Movement during the [[reconstruction-era]], later amassing wealth as supervisor of the Louisiana State Lottery.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the four-year conflict in which he served as a full general, directing the bombardment of Fort Sumter and commanding across multiple theaters.
- [[confederate-army]] — the armed service in which he was appointed the fifth full general, commanding field armies in Virginia, Tennessee, and the Carolinas.
- [[union-army]] — the federal military he opposed at Fort Sumter, First Bull Run, Shiloh, Bermuda Hundred, and the defenses around Petersburg.
- [[irvin-mcdowell]] — the opposing Union general whom he outmaneuvered and routed during the First Battle of Bull Run in July 1861.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-war period during which he returned to Louisiana, supported civil rights for freedmen, and managed commercial enterprises.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic he served as an engineer in the Mexican-American War before resigning his commission to join the Confederacy.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the French emperor whose military treatises shaped his tactical concepts and earned him the nickname "Little Napoleon".
