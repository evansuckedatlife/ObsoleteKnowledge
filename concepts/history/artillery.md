## summary

The **Continental Artillery**, also known as the **Continental Army Artillery**, was the specialized ordnance, gunnery, and siege arm of the [[continental-army]] during the [[american-revolution]]. Established in late 1775 under the command of Boston bookseller-turned-soldier *Henry Knox*, the corps transformed an amateur colonial rebellion into a disciplined fighting force capable of facing the formidable *Royal Artillery* of the [[british-empire]]. Despite crippling shortages of powder, foundries, and trained artificers, the artillery served as an essential pillar of *General* [[george-washington]]'s tactical doctrine across eight years of campaigning. Its gunners preserved fragile American defensive lines from Long Island to Monmouth and delivered the overwhelming bombardment during the [[yorktown-campaign]], clinching sovereign independence for the fledgling [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- *The Noble Train of Artillery*: In the bitter winter of 1775–1776, *Henry Knox* led an expedition that dragged fifty-nine captured British cannons, howitzers, and mortars from *Fort Ticonderoga* over three hundred miles of frozen rivers and mountain passes to Boston. Their placement on *Dorchester Heights* compelled British forces to evacuate the city.
- *Urban dominance at Trenton*: On Christmas night in 1776, Knox's men skillfully ferried eighteen field pieces across the ice-strewn Delaware River, stationing batteries commanded by *Alexander Hamilton* and other young officers at key crossroads to sweep Trenton's streets with canister shot and shatter Hessian counterattacks.
- *Decisive siege fire at Yorktown*: Operating in close coordination with French siege units under *General Rochambeau*, American gunners during the [[yorktown-campaign]] dug progressive parallels and poured thousands of heavy rounds into British redoubts, convincing *Lord Cornwallis* that resistance was futile.
- *Four continental regiments*: The branch expanded from an initial Massachusetts regiment under *Richard Gridley* into four national regiments commanded by colonels *John Lamb*, *John Crane*, *Thomas Proctor*, and *Charles Harrison*, distributing mobile field batteries among Continental infantry brigades.
- *The Pluckemin academy and artillery park*: During the winter cantonment of 1778–1779 in *Pluckemin*, New Jersey, Knox established the new nation's first military academy, constructing lecture halls and workshops to instruct officers in ballistics, trajectory mathematics, geometry, and pyrotechnics.
- *French matériel and standardisation*: Critical supplies of brass cannons, siege mortars, and gunpowder arrived through covert French aid, while foreign engineers like *Louis Lebègue Duportail* introduced *Gribeauval* doctrinal reforms that improved carriage mobility and standardized ammunition calibers.
- *Logistical strain and foundry production*: Because cannon founding required vast amounts of scarce copper and tin, the army established domestic foundries at *Springfield*, Massachusetts, and *Philadelphia*, relying on dedicated corps of artificers, blacksmiths, and teamsters to sustain operations.

## connections

- [[american-revolution]] — the war of independence in which artillery provided indispensable heavy firepower to counter British regulars.
- [[george-washington]] — commander-in-chief who recognized the branch's strategic necessity and placed immense trust in Knox's tactical leadership.
- [[continental-army]] — the unified colonial military force of which Knox's artillery served as a specialized, permanent combat branch.
- [[yorktown-campaign]] — the decisive 1781 siege where massed American and French artillery battered British earthworks and forced capitulation.
- [[france]] — allied European monarchy that provided vital siege guns, field cannons, gunpowder shipments, and trained military engineers.
- [[british-empire]] — primary adversary whose Royal Artillery was initially far superior in equipment, training, and logistical support.
- [[united-states]] — the newly sovereign republic whose hard-won victory was anchored by the technical proficiency of Continental gunners.
