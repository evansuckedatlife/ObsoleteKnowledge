## summary

**Law** is a system of rules enforced by state authority to regulate behavior, resolve disputes, and maintain order. Spanning from ancient legal codes like the *Code of Hammurabi* and the *Ten Commandments* to modern constitutions and criminal statutes, law reflects both practical necessity and philosophical ideals about justice. The *Roman Empire* created a sophisticated legal tradition that profoundly influenced Western jurisprudence, while modern democracies ground law in written constitutions and the rule of law as a check on tyranny.

## you gotta know

- Among humanity's oldest social inventions: early civilizations (Egypt, Mesopotamia, Greece) developed codes to regularize justice and social order.
- The *Ten Commandments* represented moral law in Judeo-Christian tradition, influencing Western legal thought with commands against harm and theft.
- *Roman Law* developed a sophisticated system distinguishing civil law, criminal law, and property; its ideas influenced European and U.S. legal systems.
- Natural law theory, articulated by *Aristotle*, *Aquinas*, and *Locke*, posits that law reflects universal moral principles discoverable through reason.
- Modern legal systems distinguish between statutory law (enacted by legislatures), case law (judge-made through precedent), and constitutional law (the framework governing governance).
- The *rule of law*—the principle that law applies equally to all, including rulers—is foundational to democracy and human rights, though often violated in practice.
- Common law systems (Britain, U.S.) rely on precedent and judicial interpretation to develop legal doctrine over time; civil law systems (continental Europe) emphasize written statutes and codes.
- International law, human rights law, and domestic criminal and civil law all operate within these frameworks, creating complex overlapping jurisdictions and the perennial tension between justice and power.
