## summary

**Hessian soldiers**, commonly known as **Hessians** or **Hessian mercenaries**, were eighteenth-century German auxiliary troops contracted by the *Kingdom of Great Britain* to fight during the *American Revolutionary War*. Numbering roughly thirty thousand men, the vast majority were leased from sovereign German rulers, most notably the *Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel*, to augment British land forces across North America. Their disciplined infantry tactics, distinctive brass-fronted mitre caps, and feared reputation made them central figures in major engagements from New York to Trenton, leaving an enduring mark on early American wartime lore.

## you gotta know

- Deployed extensively by *King George III* after the *British Empire* struggled to recruit enough domestic soldiers to suppress the rebellion in the *Thirteen Colonies*.
- Surprised and decisively routed by General *George Washington* following his iconic crossing of the icy *Delaware River* on *Christmas* night in 1776 at the *Battle of Trenton*.
- Commanded at Trenton by Colonel *Johann Rall*, who was mortally wounded during the street fighting after ignoring earlier warnings of the impending continental assault.
- Composed primarily of conscripts and professional soldiers from *Hesse-Kassel* and *Hesse-Hanau*, alongside contingents from *Brunswick*, *Ansbach-Bayreuth*, *Waldeck*, and *Anhalt-Zerbst*.
- Commanded overall in North America by *Lieutenant General Wilhelm von Knyphausen*, who led forces through pivotal campaigns including *White Plains*, *Fort Washington*, and *Brandywine*.
- Feared by American colonists as brutal mercenaries, an outrage explicitly condemned in the *United States Declaration of Independence* for transporting foreign armies to complete works of death and desolation.
- Offered land incentives and naturalization by the *Continental Congress* to encourage desertion, leading approximately five thousand German troops to remain in the *United States* permanently after the conflict.
