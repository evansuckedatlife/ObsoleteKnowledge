## summary

**Ashoka** (r. 268–232 BCE) was the third and most renowned emperor of the *Mauryan Empire*, who transformed himself from a military conqueror into a devoted advocate of *Buddhism* and nonviolence. His edicts, inscribed on pillars across India, represent one of history's first explicit commitments to ethical governance and the spread of a universal religion.

## you gotta know

- Early reign (c. 268 BCE onward) marked by aggressive military expansion, including the bloody conquest of *Kalinga* (260 BCE), which killed an estimated 100,000+ and enslaved 150,000 others.
- Horrified by his own carnage and mass suffering, *Ashoka* underwent a profound spiritual conversion to *Buddhism*, renouncing violence and adopting the doctrine of *dharma* (righteousness and moral order).
- Issued the *Ashoka Edicts* (c. 256 BCE)—inscribed on stone pillars and rock faces across the *Mauryan Empire*—promoting dharma, vegetarianism, animal welfare, religious tolerance of all sects, and opposition to animal sacrifice.
- Sent Buddhist missionaries throughout Asia (*Sri Lanka*, *Egypt*, *Syria*, Central Asia) to spread *Buddhism* via the *Silk Road*; widely credited with transforming *Buddhism* from a minor sect into a major world religion.
- Built approximately 84,000 *stupas* (Buddhist dome monuments) to house relics of the *Buddha* and other venerated monks; used architecture as religious propaganda and made pilgrimage to stupas central to Buddhist practice.
- Established a just legal system emphasizing clemency and rehabilitation over harsh punishment; funded public hospitals (including animal hospitals—unprecedented); created a tax system based on moral principles, reducing tax burdens on the poor.
- Governance innovation: appointed officials (*dharma-mahamattas*) specifically to spread moral teachings and ensure ethical administration, creating an early ideological bureaucracy.
- Reign marked the apex of *Mauryan Empire* power and territorial reach; his edicts reveal one of history's first explicit attempts to ground imperial ideology in ethics rather than military force.
- Legacy: his conversion sparked a major religious transformation; the *Mauryan Empire* under his successors declined, but his patronage of *Buddhism* shaped Asia's intellectual and spiritual development for millennia.
