## summary

The **Equal Rights Amendment** (*ERA*) is a proposed amendment to the *[[united-states-constitution]]* designed to guarantee that equal legal rights shall not be denied or abridged on account of sex. First drafted in 1923 by suffragist *Alice Paul* after the ratification of the Nineteenth Amendment, it gained sweeping bipartisan momentum during the second-wave feminist movement and passed both houses of Congress in 1972. However, a ferocious conservative counter-mobilization stalled ratification just short of the necessary three-fourths state threshold before the extended 1982 deadline expired, sparking enduring constitutional debates.

## you gotta know

- Penned by militant suffragist *Alice Paul* and feminist leader *Crystal Eastman* in 1923, initially titled the "Lucretia Mott Amendment" when introduced in Seneca Falls.
- Stated in its essential Section 1: "Equality of rights under the law shall not be denied or abridged by the *[[united-states]]* or by any State on account of sex."
- Passed both chambers of Congress with overwhelming bipartisan majorities in 1971 and 1972, gaining support from presidents *Richard Nixon*, *Gerald Ford*, and *Jimmy Carter*.
- Rapidly won ratification in thirty states within a year, propelled by coalition organizations such as the National Organization for Women (*NOW*), which protested inadequate enforcement by the *[[equal-employment-opportunity-commission]]*.
- Stalled in the mid-1970s due to the "STOP ERA" campaign spearheaded by conservative activist *Phyllis Schlafly*, who argued it would strip protective labor laws, mandate women in military combat, and invalidate alimony.
- Congress controversially voted to extend the original seven-year ratification window from 1979 to June 30, 1982, yet no additional states ratified within that extension period, leaving it three states short at thirty-five.
- Re-entered national legal debate in the late 2010s and 2020s when Nevada, Illinois, and Virginia became the 36th, 37th, and 38th states to ratify, triggering ongoing litigation over ratification deadlines and state rescissions under the *[[14th-amendment]]*.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — the governing charter that the measure sought to amend to explicitly prohibit sex discrimination.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose state legislatures and Congress were the arena for the ratification fight.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the Reconstruction amendment whose Equal Protection Clause has been used to adjudicate gender bias in the absence of the ERA.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the parallel mass movement whose legal strategies and legislative victories deeply inspired second-wave feminist organizers.
- [[equal-employment-opportunity-commission]] — the agency whose early neglect of workplace sex bias catalyzed nationwide feminist pressure for constitutional equality.
