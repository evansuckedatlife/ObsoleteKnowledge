## summary

The **Thermidorian Reaction**, frequently referenced simply as **Thermidor**, was the parliamentary coup d'état on *9 Thermidor Year II* (27 July 1794) in Paris that overthrew [[robespierre]] and abruptly brought an end to the [[reign-of-terror]] during the [[french-revolution]]. Precipitated by frightened deputies of the *National Convention* who feared they were marked for execution, the revolt dismantled the authoritarian wartime government directed by the *Committee of Public Safety* and broke the political ascendancy of the *Jacobins*. The ensuing transition established a moderate republican regime, abolished rigid wartime economic regulations such as the *General Maximum*, and initiated a broad conservative retrenchment across [[france]]. It stands as an essential turning point in modern political history, providing a universal historical archetype for revolutionary fatigue and paving the road toward the rise of [[napoleon-bonaparte]].

## you gotta know

- Ended the [[reign-of-terror]] when the *National Convention* voted to arrest [[robespierre]], his brother *Augustin*, *Louis Antoine de Saint-Just*, and their radical allies on *27 July 1794*.
- Culminated in the public execution of *Robespierre* and twenty-one associates at the *Place de la Révolution* without trial on *10 Thermidor*, having been declared outlaws following a brief standoff at the *Hôtel de Ville*.
- Precipitated by *Robespierre's* threatening speech on *8 Thermidor*, in which he warned of an internal conspiracy among unnamed legislators, inadvertently uniting terrified moderates and vulnerable radicals against him.
- Shuttered instruments of terror by repealing the draconian *Law of 22 Prairial*, releasing thousands of political prisoners, curtailing the *Revolutionary Tribunal*, and permanently closing the *Jacobin Club* of Paris.
- Unleashed the reactionary *White Terror* across provincial [[france]], where royalists, aggrieved victims, and organized gangs of well-dressed bourgeois youths known as the *Muscadins* assaulted and murdered former Jacobin militants.
- Destabilized the urban economy by terminating the *General Maximum* price caps on grain and provisions, prompting catastrophic currency depreciation of *assignats* and sparking the desperate, suppressed worker uprisings of *Germinal* and *Prairial*.
- Replaced the democratic constitution with the *Constitution of Year III* in 1795, abolishing universal male suffrage in favor of strict property requirements and establishing the executive *French Directory*.
- Inspired a lasting political concept popularized by later historians and theorists, notably *Leon Trotsky*, who used the event as shorthand for any counter-revolutionary degeneration or post-radical stabilization.

## connections

- [[french-revolution]] — the overarching political upheaval whose radical phase ended with the fall of the Montagnards.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the young military commander who defended the post-Thermidorian Directory before seizing power in the coup of 18 Brumaire.
- [[france]] — the nation transformed by the retreat from Jacobin egalitarianism toward a bourgeois republic.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical tradition whose competing visions of virtue, liberty, and order clashed during the crisis.
- [[robespierre]] — the incorruptible Jacobin dictator whose denunciation, arrest, and execution marked the turning point of the coup.
- [[danton]] — the charismatic moderate whose trial and execution earlier in 1794 convinced convention deputies that none among them were safe.
- [[reign-of-terror]] — the systematic state repression and wave of executions terminated immediately following 9 Thermidor.
