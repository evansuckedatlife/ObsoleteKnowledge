## summary

**Harriet Tubman** was an enslaved woman who escaped bondage and became the most famous conductor of the [[underground-railroad|Underground Railroad]], risking her life repeatedly to guide approximately 70 enslaved people to freedom in the North. Born in Maryland's Eastern Shore, she endured severe brutality but escaped in 1849, then returned to the South at least 13 times to lead others to liberty. During the *American Civil War*, she served the Union Army as a nurse, cook, and scout, and became a legendary symbol of Black resistance and self-determination.

## you gotta know

- Suffered brutal treatment under multiple enslavers, including whippings so severe they left permanent scars and neurological damage.
- Escaped to Philadelphia in 1849 after attempting escape years earlier and being recaptured and sold farther south.
- Earned the nickname "Moses" for leading enslaved people out of bondage, comparing herself to the Biblical figure who freed the Hebrews.
- Had an extraordinarily high success rate—all of her passengers reached freedom safely, a remarkable feat that earned her deep trust and legendary status.
- Used the North Star and hidden safe houses (stations) of the Underground Railroad network to navigate hundreds of miles to Pennsylvania, New York, and Canada.
- During the Civil War, served at Fort Wagner as a nurse and cook, and participated in the Combahee River Raid (1863), which liberated over 700 enslaved people.
- Lived until 1913, continuing activism for women's suffrage and African American education in her later years.
