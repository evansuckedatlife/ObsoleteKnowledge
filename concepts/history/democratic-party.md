## summary

The **Democratic Party** is the *United States'* oldest continuously operating political party, tracing its origins to factions within the early Republic that coalesced around *Andrew Jackson's* populist campaign in the 1820s. Evolving from the Democratic-Republican alliance through Jacksonian Democrats, the party has represented diverse coalitions ranging from rural and labor interests to urban progressives, with dramatic realignments between regional, class, and racial constituencies. The party's history reflects fundamental tensions between populism and elitism, federalism and state power, and competing visions of American democracy.

## you gotta know

- Early Democratic organization emerged from supporters of *Andrew Jackson*, who actively positioned himself as defender of ordinary citizens against aristocratic and banking elites.
- The election of 1828 marked the first modern political party campaign, with the Democrats using new organizational techniques, patronage networks, and mass mobilization to bring Jackson to power.
- *Martin Van Buren*, a shrewd Democratic organizer and New York politician, systematized party machinery and established principles of party discipline that influenced American politics thereafter.
- Jackson's Democrats were sectionally diverse: Southern slaveholders, Northern laborers, and Western settlers united against the National Republican and later Whig parties.
- The party fractured profoundly over slavery's expansion into Western territories; the Republican Party's emergence in 1854 shattered the Democrat-dominated national consensus on territorial slavery governance.
- Post-Civil War Democrats were regionally concentrated in the *South*, transformed into a regional defensive party opposing Reconstruction and federal civil rights enforcement.
- The election of 1932 initiated a realignment where urban, immigrant, labor, and Black voters joined the Democratic coalition supporting *Franklin D. Roosevelt's* New Deal, creating a new majority.
- Subsequent decades witnessed profound regional realignment: Southern white Democrats gradually shifted to Republicans over civil rights opposition; Republicans gained conservative strength; Democrats became increasingly identified with civil rights and progressive social liberalism.
