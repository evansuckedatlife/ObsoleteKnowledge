## summary

**Serbian nationalism** was a militant political and ideological movement in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries that sought the liberation and political unification of South Slavic populations from imperial rule into a single sovereign state, frequently envisioned as **Greater Serbia**. Centered in Belgrade following autonomous self-rule and eventual independence from the [[ottoman-empire]], the movement grew increasingly radicalized against the neighboring Austro-Hungarian Empire, whose rule over large Slavic populations directly thwarted Serbian territorial ambitions. Backed by diplomatic and military patronage from the [[russian-empire]], this persistent agitation destabilized the Balkan Peninsula and ultimately ignited [[world-war-i]] after nationalist conspirators organized the [[assassination-of-archduke-franz-ferdinand]] in 1914.

## you gotta know

- Reached its world-historical zenith on 28 June 1914 when *Gavrilo Princip*, a young Bosnian Serb student affiliated with the revolutionary group *Young Bosnia* (*Mlada Bosna*), carried out the fatal [[assassination-of-archduke-franz-ferdinand]] in Sarajevo on the sacred Serbian commemorative holiday of *St. Vitus Day* (*Vidovdan*).
- Found its most dangerous and violent institutional expression through the secret military society officially named *Unification or Death*, popularised as the [[black-hand]], which was commanded by the Serbian chief of military intelligence, *Colonel Dragutin Dimitrijević*, widely known by the codename *Apis*.
- Provoked the dangerous *Bosnian Crisis* of 1908 following the Austro-Hungarian annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, an event that provoked widespread outrage across Belgrade and spurred the establishment of *Narodna Odbrana*, a patriotic civilian defense society devoted to anti-Habsburg resistance.
- Expanded national frontiers dramatically during the *Balkan Wars* of 1912 and 1913, in which the Serbian army defeated the [[ottoman-empire]] and subsequently repelled Bulgarian forces, nearly doubling the kingdom's territorial extent and fostering an aggressive martial pride that alarmed neighboring Austria-Hungary and [[germany]].
- Transformed into a militantly anti-Austrian posture after the violent *May Coup* of 1903, wherein conspiratorial army officers brutally assassinated the pro-Habsburg *King Alexander Obrenović* and *Queen Draga*, thereby returning the rival *Karađorđević* dynasty to power under *King Peter I*.
- Depended strategically upon the ideological concept of Pan-Slavism and sustained political backing from the [[russian-empire]], whose sense of religious and cultural obligation toward Orthodox Balkan Slavs ultimately transformed an Austrian punitive strike against Belgrade into a catastrophic continental war.
- Derived its foundational geopolitical manifesto from *Načertanije* ("The Draft"), a clandestine 1844 foreign policy memorandum composed by interior minister *Ilija Garašanin* that urged the systematic liberation of Serbs throughout the Balkans and revitalized the historical memory of the 1389 *Battle of Kosovo*.

## connections

- [[assassination-of-archduke-franz-ferdinand]] — the climactic terrorist act in Sarajevo that severed Austro-Serbian relations and initiated the July Crisis.
- [[archduke-franz-ferdinand]] — the Habsburg heir whose proposed trialist restructuring of Austria-Hungary threatened nationalist ambitions by appeasing South Slav grievances.
- [[world-war-i]] — the catastrophic global conflict ignited when the Austro-Hungarian invasion of Serbia activated the European mutual defense alliances.
- [[black-hand]] — the clandestine nationalist officer network that trained, armed, and smuggled the Sarajevo conspirators across the border.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — the retreating Islamic imperial power whose centuries of Balkan hegemony originally forged Serbian romantic resistance and territorial claims.
- [[russian-empire]] — the Eastern European imperial power that positioned itself as the protector of Balkan Slavs, guaranteeing Serbian military resistance.
- [[germany]] — the imperial power whose issuance of the blank check encouraged Austria-Hungary to seek the total military destruction of Serbia.
