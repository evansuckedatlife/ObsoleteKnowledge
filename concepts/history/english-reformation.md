## summary

The **English Reformation** was *Henry VIII*'s break with Rome and the Catholic Church, undertaken primarily to secure an annulment for his marriage. Spanning the 16th century, it transformed England into a Protestant nation with the monarch as head of the Church of England, reshaping religious, political, and social life across the realm.

## you gotta know

- Initiated by *Henry VIII* in the 1530s, chiefly to circumvent papal refusal to annul his marriage to *Catherine of Aragon*, daughter of Spanish monarchs, whose union had produced no male heir.
- *Thomas Cromwell* orchestrated the break from Rome and directed the suppression of monasteries, seizing their vast wealth for the Crown and enriching both himself and the nobility.
- Introduced the *Act of Supremacy* (1534), declaring the monarch, not the Pope, as supreme head of the Church of England, a radical assertion of royal authority over spiritual matters.
- Dissolved the monasteries (1536–1541), destroying centuries of religious institutions and redirecting land and resources to the nobility, fundamentally reshaping English landholding patterns.
- Established the Church of England through the *Church of England Act* (1534), creating a national church nominally Catholic in doctrine but Protestant in governance.
- Replaced Latin Mass with an English-language *Book of Common Prayer* (first introduced 1549), making worship accessible to ordinary people and advancing English as a liturgical language.
- Henry's personal religious views remained ambiguous: he rejected papal authority but maintained Catholic doctrine on several sacraments, calling himself "Defender of the Faith."
- Shifted religious character under different Tudor heirs: *Edward VI* pushed more radical Protestantism, *Mary I* briefly restored Catholicism and persecuted Protestants, and *Elizabeth I* established a moderate middle way.
- Established the Church of England as distinct from both Rome and continental Protestantism, laying the foundation for Anglicanism and creating a unique church structure that blended Catholic hierarchy with Protestant theology.
- Resulted in significant loss of life: figures like *Thomas More* and *John Fisher* were executed for refusing the oath of supremacy, symbolizing the spiritual struggle over royal authority.
