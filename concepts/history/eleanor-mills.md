## summary

**Eleanor Mills**, born **Eleanor Reinhardt Mills**, was an American church choir singer whose sensational unsolved murder in New Brunswick, New Jersey, alongside her lover, the Episcopal rector *[[edward-hall|Edward Wheeler Hall]]*, became the definitive tabloid spectacle of the Jazz Age. Found slain beneath a crabapple tree with their clandestine love letters scattered over their bodies, the pair became the center of a national media frenzy fueled by sensationalist reporting and high-society intrigue. The sensational 1926 murder trial of Hall's wealthy widow captivated the *[[united-states]]*, profoundly shaping the evolution of sensationalist journalism and *[[detective-fiction]]*.

## you gotta know

- Discovered dead alongside her married paramour, *[[edward-hall|Reverend Edward Wheeler Hall]]*, on September 16, 1922, on an abandoned farm along *De Russey's Lane* in Franklin Township near New Brunswick, New Jersey.
- Displayed gruesome injuries indicating intense personal malice: she had been shot three times in the head, her throat was slashed from ear to ear, and her vocal cords and larynx were surgically excised by the killer.
- Found in a bizarrely theatrical tableau where *[[edward-hall|Hall]]*'s calling card was placed resting against his feet, while torn fragments of their intimate romantic correspondence were arranged across their bodies.
- Served as a choir singer at the *Church of St. John the Evangelist*, where her husband *James Mills* worked as the church sexton and janitor, while Hall was married to the wealthy heiress *Frances Noel Stevens*.
- Became the focus of testimony by the eccentric local witness *Jane Gibson*, dubbed the "*Pig Woman*" by press outlets, who claimed she observed suspicious figures by flashlight and testified while lying in a hospital bed inside the courtroom.
- Saw the cold case dramatically reopened four years later in 1926 through an aggressive investigation bankrolled by editor *Philip Payne* of the sensationalist tabloid *New York Daily Mirror*.
- Prompted the celebrated 1926 murder trial of *Frances Stevens Hall* and her eccentric brothers *Henry Stevens* and *Willie Stevens*, which ended in complete acquittals largely due to Willie Stevens's unexpectedly composed and credible testimony.
- Mirrored the rapid expansion of mass-market tabloid culture, high-society criminal scandals, and yellow journalism that directly inspired contemporary American authors and early modern *[[detective-fiction]]*.
