## summary

The **Celtic Peoples** were an ethno-linguistic collection of tribal societies that inhabited large expanses of Iron Age and early historic Europe, extending from the British Isles and the Iberian Peninsula across Gaul to the Danube basin and Anatolia. United by related languages, metalworking traditions, and distinct religious systems, they clashed repeatedly with Mediterranean city-states during classical antiquity. Following conquests by the *Roman Empire* and subsequent Germanic migrations, distinct Celtic languages and institutions survived primarily along the Atlantic fringe of northwestern Europe.

## you gotta know

- Associated archeologically with the Central European *Hallstatt* and *La Tène* material cultures, famed for intricate swirling knotwork, torcs, and sophisticated iron weapons.
- Sacked Rome around 390 BCE under the war chief *Brennus*, an event that deeply scarred early Roman military consciousness.
- The Gauls formed the largest continental concentration of these tribes, fiercely resisting Roman legions until their defeat at the Siege of Alesia in 52 BCE by Julius Caesar.
- In Britain, the Iceni queen *Boudica* led a devastating anti-Roman uprising in 60–61 CE, burning settlements before her eventual defeat.
- Maintained an influential priestly and intellectual class known as *druids*, who presided over sacrificial rites, judicial arbitrations, and oral lore.
- Divided linguistically into Goidelic (Gaelic) branches covering Ireland, Scotland, and the Isle of Man, and Brythonic (Brittonic) branches covering Wales, Cornwall, and Brittany.
- Developed an early medieval monastic tradition in Ireland and western Britain that preserved classical manuscripts and re-evangelized parts of post-Roman continental Europe.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean empire that subjugated continental Gaul and southern Britain.
- [[france]] — the modern nation-state encompassing the territory of ancient Gaul.
- [[england]] — the island region where incoming Anglo-Saxons pushed Brythonic speech westward into Wales and Cornwall.
- [[carolingian]] — the Frankish empire that clashed with and annexed Brythonic-speaking Brittany.
- [[christianity]] — the religion embraced by Insular Celtic regions, fostering a vibrant illuminated manuscript culture.
- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline that classifies the surviving and extinct Celtic tongues within the Indo-European family.
