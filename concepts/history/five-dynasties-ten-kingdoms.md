## summary

The **Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms** (formally chronicled in Chinese historiography as the **Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period** or **Wudai Shiguo**) was an era of intense political fragmentation and military strife in tenth-century [[china]]. Spanning from the collapse of the Tang dynasty in 907 CE to the consolidation of the Song dynasty in 960–979 CE, the epoch saw five ephemeral military dynasties succeed each other across the northern Yellow River basin while more than ten regional autonomous kingdoms partitioned southern and western China. Despite relentless frontier warfare and frequent palace coups, the period witnessed remarkable economic dynamism, flourishing maritime commerce, and major cultural milestones including the rise of lyric poetry and the state-sponsored printing of classical texts.

## you gotta know

- Began in 907 CE when the military warlord *Zhu Wen* forced the abdication of Emperor *Aidi*, bringing the celebrated Tang dynasty to an end and founding the Later Liang dynasty.
- Characterized in the north by five rapidly succeeding dynasties centered in the Central Plains: the Later Liang, Later Tang, Later Jin, Later Han, and Later Zhou.
- Dominated by military commanders of Shatuo Turkic descent, who established three of the five northern dynasties (Later Tang, Later Jin, and Later Han) and transformed northern military organization.
- Marked by the surrender of the vital Sixteen Prefectures to the Khitan Liao dynasty by *Shi Jingtang* of the Later Jin, an act that deprived China of natural defensive borders for centuries.
- Concluded in 960 CE when general *Zhao Kuangyin* led the Chenqiao Mutiny against the Later Zhou, ascending the throne as Emperor *Taizu* and establishing the Song dynasty to re-unify China.
- Fostered the cultural brilliance of southern regimes like the Southern Tang, whose final sovereign *Li Yu* became a legendary figure in the composition of delicate *ci* lyric [[poetry]].
- Advanced the technology of woodblock printing through the visionary state project organized by minister *Feng Dao*, who oversaw the first mass printing of the Confucian classics.

## connections

- [[china]] — imperial civilization that endured internal fracture before reunifying under the centralizing civil reforms of the Song dynasty.
- [[buddhism]] — religious tradition that suffered extensive property seizures and temple closures under the anti-Buddhist persecutions of the Later Zhou emperor *Shizong*.
- [[silk-road]] — continental commercial routes navigated by northwestern border regimes such as the Guiyi Circuit and Ganzhou Uyghur Kingdom.
- [[poetry]] — literary discipline deeply transformed during this era through the blossoming of the *ci* poetic genre in the courts of southern kingdoms.
- [[linguistics]] — field tracing dialectal differentiation, rhyme tables, and phonetic developments that occurred during regional political division.
