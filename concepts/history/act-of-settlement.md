## summary

The **Act of Settlement** (formally the **Act of Settlement 1701**) was a landmark constitutional statute passed by the Parliament of [[england]] to resolve the succession to the English and Irish thrones. Enacted during the reign of King *William III* following the untimely death of the sole surviving child of Princess *Anne*, the law barred Roman Catholics from the line of succession and settled the crown upon the Protestant Electress *Sophia of Hanover* and her non-Catholic descendants. Beyond determining the dynastic future of Great Britain under the [[house-of-hanover]], the statute established enduring constitutional tenets, including statutory judicial independence and parliamentary oversight of foreign wars.

## you gotta know

- Settled the royal succession to the thrones of [[england]] and *Ireland* exclusively upon the Protestant Electress *Sophia of Hanover*—a granddaughter of *James I* through *Elizabeth of Bohemia*—and her descendants, ensuring the rise of the [[house-of-hanover]].
- Explicitly disqualified any person who was a Roman Catholic, or who married a Catholic, from inheriting the crown, requiring the reigning monarch to join in formal communion with the Church of [[england]].
- Prompted by a succession crisis when eleven-year-old Prince *William*, Duke of Gloucester—the only surviving child of the future Queen *Anne*—died in 1700, leaving no direct Protestant Stuart heirs to succeed *Anne* and the childless *William III*.
- Established the constitutional foundation of judicial independence by dictating that judges hold office during good behavior (*quamdiu se bene gesserint*) with fixed salaries, removable only by an address from both Houses of Parliament rather than by royal prerogative.
- Decreed that no royal pardon under the Great Seal could be pleaded in bar of an impeachment by the *House of Commons*, preventing monarchs from shielding corrupt ministers from parliamentary accountability.
- Mandated that [[england]] was not obliged to engage in war for the defense of any dominions not belonging to the English crown without parliamentary consent, addressing concerns over monarchical entanglements in German continental conflicts.
- Catalyzed the political unification of Great Britain, as English insistence on Hanoverian succession prompted Scotland's retaliatory Act of Security 1704, which directly drove negotiations culminating in the [[act-of-union-1707]].

## connections

- [[england]] — the realm whose parliament enacted the statute to secure Protestant succession and limit monarchical prerogative.
- [[house-of-hanover]] — the German royal dynasty that inherited the crown in 1714 as direct beneficiaries of the act.
- [[george-i]] — the Elector of Hanover who peacefully ascended the British throne pursuant to the statute after the death of Queen *Anne*.
- [[robert-walpole]] — the first de facto prime minister, who consolidated parliamentary governance under the Hanoverian monarchs established by the act.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power whose stability and constitutional monarchy were anchored by parliamentary control of the succession.
- [[glorious-revolution]] — the 1688 uprising that deposed *James II* and created the precedent for parliament regulating the line of succession.
- [[christianity]] — the doctrinal divide between Catholicism and Protestantism that shaped British politics and motivated the statute's religious exclusions.
