## summary

The **Arapaho** (historically rendered as **Arapahoe**) are an Algonquian-speaking Indigenous people of the Great Plains whose traditional territory spanned vast portions of modern Colorado, Wyoming, Nebraska, and Kansas. Operating in close military, diplomatic, and kinship alliance with the [[cheyenne]], they formed one of the most formidable coalitions resisting westward expansion by the [[united-states]] during the nineteenth century. Subjected to catastrophic violence during the Plains [[indian-wars]]—most notoriously at the Sand Creek Massacre—the **Arapaho people** were subsequently divided into two distinct, enduring sovereign nations: the Northern Arapaho of Wyoming and the Southern Arapaho of Oklahoma.

## you gotta know

- Suffered catastrophic losses at the *Sand Creek Massacre* in November 1864, when Colonel *John Chivington* led the Third Colorado Cavalry in a surprise dawn attack on a peaceful encampment, murdering over one hundred and fifty people under chiefs *Left Hand* and *Black Kettle*.
- Maintained a profound military, economic, and social alliance with the [[cheyenne]], coordinating seasonal bison hunts across the High Plains, establishing intertribal trade networks, and jointly defending traditional hunting territories against encroachment.
- Fought alongside allied Lakota and Cheyenne warriors in prominent engagements of the Plains [[indian-wars]], including the *Colorado War*, *Red Cloud's War*, and the defeat of Lieutenant Colonel *George Armstrong Custer* at the *Battle of the Little Bighorn*.
- Negotiated major diplomatic pacts with the federal government, including the 1851 *Treaty of Fort Laramie* delineating tribal lands and the 1867 *Medicine Lodge Treaty*, which compelled the southern bands onto reservations in Indian Territory.
- Separated permanently into two distinct sovereign entities during the reservation era: the *Northern Arapaho Tribe*, which was placed on the *Wind River Reservation* alongside their historic rivals the *Eastern Shoshone*, and the *Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes* of Oklahoma.
- Became fervent practitioners and prominent apostles of the *Ghost Dance* religious movement in the late 1880s, following the teachings of the prophet *Wovoka* under the spiritual leadership of *Sitting Bull the Arapaho*.
- Structured traditional male civic and military life around eight hierarchical age-grade societies—progressing from junior warrior associations to the governing *Water-Pouring Old Men*—while anchoring tribal cosmology to the annual *Sun Dance* ceremony and the veneration of the sacred *Flat Pipe*.

## connections

- [[cheyenne]] — close historical allies in Plains warfare, diplomacy, and shared reservation administration.
- [[united-states]] — the expanding imperial nation whose treaties, military operations, and reservation policies altered their ancestral way of life.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict that diverted federal regulars east, leaving Western frontier posts to undisciplined territorial militia units.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader geopolitical regime of territorial dispossession, forced cultural assimilation, and institutional subjugation imposed on native populations.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the transformative postwar period characterized by aggressive transcontinental railroad expansion and intensified federal military subjugation of Plains tribes.
- [[linguistics]] — the scholarly discipline that classifies their polysynthetic speech as an exceptionally divergent member of the Algonquian family.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional measure guaranteeing birthright citizenship that initially excluded untaxed Native American tribes maintaining internal sovereignty.
