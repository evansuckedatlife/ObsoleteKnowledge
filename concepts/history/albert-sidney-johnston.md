## summary

**Albert Sidney Johnston** was an American military officer who served as a senior general in the Confederate States Army during the early months of the [[american-civil-war]]. Esteemed by Confederate President *Jefferson Davis* as the South's finest soldier, he was entrusted with the sprawling and vulnerable Western Department, defending a perimeter stretching from the Appalachian Mountains across Kentucky and Tennessee to the Mississippi River. His sudden death at the Battle of Shiloh in April 1862 eliminated the Confederacy's highest-ranking field commander and proved to be an irreplaceable strategic blow to Southern military fortunes in the Western theater.

## you gotta know

- Died from a severed popliteal artery behind his right knee during the Battle of Shiloh on April 6, 1862, while rallying retreating troops near a peach orchard during the Confederate assault on Pittsburg Landing.
- Stands as the highest-ranking military officer on either side killed in action throughout the entire [[american-civil-war]], having been commissioned as one of only eight full generals in the Confederate service.
- Bled to death within minutes because he had ordered his personal physician, Dr. *David W. Yandell*, to leave his side to treat a group of wounded Union prisoners, leaving no surgeon to apply a simple tourniquet when Johnston's boot quietly filled with blood.
- Faced severe public outrage and calls for his dismissal in early 1862 after his defensive cordons collapsed when Union forces under Major General [[ulysses-grant]] captured Fort Henry on the Tennessee River and Fort Donelson on the Cumberland River.
- Redeemed his operational reputation by masterminding a clandestine concentration of over forty thousand troops at Corinth, Mississippi, and launching an overwhelming dawn surprise attack intended to crush Grant's army before Major General *Don Carlos Buell* could arrive.
- Relinquished supreme battlefield command upon his mortal wounding to General *P. G. T. Beauregard*, who halted the evening assaults at Shiloh; the subsequent Union counterattack the following day drove Confederate forces from the field in defeat.
- Commanded the federal Utah Expedition during the Utah War between 1857 and 1858, deftly navigating severe winter weather, logistical collapse, and armed Mormon resistance to establish federal military authority outside Salt Lake City without widespread bloodshed.
- Fought in the Texas Revolution and later served as the senior commander and Secretary of War for the Republic of Texas, where he was severely wounded in the pelvis during a famous 1837 duel with rival officer *Felix Huston*.

## connections

- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict in which he commanded the Confederate Western Department until his death.
- [[ulysses-grant]] — opposing Union general who pierced Johnston's defensive line in Tennessee and survived his dawn assault at Shiloh.
- [[jefferson-davis]] — Confederate president and close personal friend who fiercely defended Johnston's command against widespread public censure.
- [[robert-e-lee]] — West Point associate whose appointment as top field commander followed Johnston's demise in the West.
- [[united-states]] — nation he served for over three decades as a West Point graduate and cavalry officer before his resignation.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — postwar period during which *Lost Cause* writers romanticized Johnston's death as the decisive turning point that doomed the Confederacy.
