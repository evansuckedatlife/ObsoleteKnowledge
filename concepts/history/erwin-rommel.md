## summary

**Erwin Rommel** (1891–1944) was a legendary German field marshal renowned for his tactical brilliance and earned respect from both allies and enemies. Nicknamed "The Desert Fox," he commanded Axis forces in North Africa and became one of World War II's most celebrated military commanders before his involvement in the July 20 plot against Hitler cost him his life.

## you gotta know

- Born in 1891 in southern Germany; rose through German ranks during World War I as an infantry officer and gained prominence as a panzer tactician and theorist before World War II.
- Published *Infanterie Greift An* (1937), a influential tactical manual that established his reputation as a military theorist and innovator.
- Commanded the *Afrika Korps* in North Africa (1941–1943), achieving remarkable victories against numerically superior British forces through mobile warfare and logistical innovation.
- Adapted desert warfare tactics using speed and concentration of armored force, becoming famous for aggressive maneuvers and rapid redeployment despite supply limitations.
- Suffered his only major setback at the *Battle of El Alamein* (1942), where he was defeated by *Bernard Montgomery* and forced into retreat across Libya and Tunisia.
- Commanded Army Group B defending Normandy during the *D-Day* invasion (1944), though his efforts were hamstrung by Hitler's refusal to commit reserves and micromanagement of strategy.
- Strongly opposed Hitler's ideology in private and grew increasingly critical of Nazi policies, particularly the persecution of Jews, though he was not openly resistant until late in the war.
- Respected by opponents for his conduct and chivalry; British and American commanders admired his military prowess and battlefield ethics, calling him an honorable soldier.
- Implicated in the *July 20 plot* (1944) to assassinate *Hitler*, though his actual involvement remains disputed by historians—he may have known of the plot but did not actively participate.
- Ordered to commit suicide by Hitler in 1944 rather than face public trial, allowing him to die with honor and receive a state funeral, cementing his complicated legacy.
