## summary

**French absolutism** was the system of centralized monarchical governance in France that reached its height under the Bourbon dynasty during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. Under this model, the monarch held supreme sovereign authority unrestrained by legislative assemblies, deriving legitimacy from the divine right of kings as articulated by figures like Bishop Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet. Its institutional apex under *Louis XIV* forged a dominant continental empire, but its financial rigidity and social inequalities ultimately ignited the *French Revolution*.

## you gotta know

- Architected fundamentally by Cardinal *Richelieu* and Cardinal *Mazarin*, who subdued rebellious noble factions, centralized administrative authority, and established the system of regional *intendants*.
- Reached its absolute zenith under *Louis XIV*, known as the "Sun King," who famously centralized aristocratic life and royal power at the Palace of Versailles.
- Justified ideologically through the *divine right of kings*, asserting that royal authority came directly from God and that subjects owed unconditional obedience.
- Undermined traditional regional assemblies and suppressed the national *Estates-General*, which was never summoned between 1614 and the onset of revolution in 1789.
- Enforced religious uniformity through actions such as the 1685 Edict of Fontainebleau, which revoked the Edict of Nantes and expelled hundreds of thousands of Huguenots.
- Provoked trenchant criticism from *Enlightenment* philosophers such as *Voltaire*, *Montesquieu*, and *Jean-Jacques Rousseau*, who challenged arbitrary power and advocated separation of powers.
- Collapsed during the reign of *Louis XVI*, whose inability to resolve catastrophic state debts and fiscal privileges led directly to the *French Revolution*.

## connections

- [[france]] — the European kingdom whose political system and social hierarchy were shaped by Bourbon monarchical centralization.
- [[french-revolution]] — the cataclysmic uprising that dismantled the monarchy, feudal privileges, and absolutist institutions.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual movement that critiqued royal despotism, clerical authority, and the absence of civil liberties.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — military commander and statesman who restructured French administrative centralism in the wake of the monarchy's fall.
- [[england]] — rival European power that rejected royal absolutism in favor of parliamentary supremacy following the Glorious Revolution.
- [[spain]] — allied Bourbon realm closely tied through diplomatic pacts, dynastic succession wars, and continental balances of power.
