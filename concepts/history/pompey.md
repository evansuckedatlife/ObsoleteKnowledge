## summary

**Pompey** (*Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus*), widely celebrated as **Pompey the Great**, was one of the preeminent statesmen and military commanders of the late [[roman-republic]]. Rising to supreme power during the civil wars of the first century BC, he transformed the Mediterranean world by clearing endemic piracy, conquering vast lands across the [[middle-east]], and forming the First Triumvirate with [[julius-caesar]] and *Marcus Licinius Crassus*. Although his unprecedented battlefield triumphs made him Rome's leading citizen, his subsequent alliance with conservative senators against Caesar plunged the state into catastrophic civil strife. Defeated at the Battle of Pharsalus and treacherously murdered in Egypt, his tragic downfall hastened the collapse of the Republic and the dawn of imperial autocracy.

## you gotta know

- Commanded the senatorial armies against [[julius-caesar]] in the [[roman-civil-war]], suffering a ruinous and decisive defeat at the *Battle of Pharsalus* in 48 BC despite possessing superior numbers.
- Established the dominant informal coalition known as the First Triumvirate in 60 BC with [[julius-caesar]] and *Marcus Licinius Crassus*, cementing the alliance by marrying Caesar's daughter *Julia*.
- Cleared the Mediterranean Sea of Cilician pirates in just three months under the extraordinary sweeping command granted to him by the controversial *Lex Gabinia* of 67 BC.
- Subjugated the [[middle-east]] in the Third Mithridatic War, defeating *Mithridates VI of Pontus*, annexing Syria, and famously entering the Holy of Holies inside the Temple of Jerusalem.
- Assassinated upon landing on the Egyptian coast following Pharsalus, where regents of *Ptolemy XIII* severed his head; Caesar wept in horror upon receiving his rival's signet ring.
- Constructed the monumental Theater of Pompey in the Campus Martius as the first permanent stone theater in [[rome]], incorporating an adjoining curia that famously became the site of Caesar's assassination.
- Celebrated three magnificent Roman triumphs over three distinct continents—Africa for subduing Marian loyalists, Europe across [[spain]], and Asia—an unprecedented feat that symbolized the global extent of his victories.
- Rose to early prominence as a ruthless partisan of the dictator *Lucius Cornelius Sulla*, earning the cognomen *Magnus* ("the Great") while political opponents derided the young commander as the *adulescentulus carnifex* ("teenage butcher").

## connections

- [[julius-caesar]] — his partner in the First Triumvirate and beloved father-in-law who became his ultimate rival and conqueror in the civil war.
- [[roman-republic]] — the constitutional order whose legal conventions he frequently bypassed and later died defending as general.
- [[roman-civil-war]] — the factional conflict sparked when Caesar crossed the Rubicon that drove Pompey from the capital into overseas exile.
- [[rome]] — the republic's capital where he erected its first stone theater and celebrated three historic triumphs.
- [[italy]] — the peninsula he evacuated in a strategic retreat to assemble senatorial legions across the Adriatic Sea.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the campaign theater where he repulsed Caesar at Dyrrhachium before suffering total defeat at Pharsalus.
- [[middle-east]] — the region he permanently reorganized into Roman provinces and client states after crushing Mithridates.
- [[spain]] — the western province where he fought *Quintus Sertorius* and where his sons made their final resistance.
