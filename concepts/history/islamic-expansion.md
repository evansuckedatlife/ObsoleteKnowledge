## summary

The **Islamic expansion**, also known as the **early Muslim conquests**, refers to the rapid military, political, and religious outward expansion originating from the Arabian Peninsula in the seventh and eighth centuries following the death of the Prophet *Muhammad*. Spearheaded first by the *Rashidun Caliphate* and subsequently extended under the *Umayyad Caliphate*, these campaigns dismantled the *Sasanian Empire* in the [[middle-east]] and seized vast territories from the [[byzantine-empire]], including Syria and Egypt. Extending across three continents from the borders of [[china]] to [[spain]] and southern [[france]], the movement fundamentally restructured Mediterranean geopolitics, trade, and culture, ending late antiquity and establishing a unified Islamic sphere.

## you gotta know

- Began immediately after the death of *Muhammad* in 632, as the first caliph, *Abu Bakr*, unified Arabia through the *Ridda Wars* before directing forces outward against the exhausted Byzantine and Sasanian empires.
- Defeated the eastern Roman armies at the decisive *Battle of Yarmouk* in 636, stripping the [[byzantine-empire]] of Syria and Palestine and facilitating the surrender of Jerusalem.
- Overthrew the entire *Sasanian Empire* within two decades through major victories at *al-Qadisiyyah* and *Nihavand*, integrating Persia and Mesopotamia into the caliphate.
- Crossed the Strait of Gibraltar under *Tariq ibn Ziyad* in 711, destroying the Visigothic Kingdom of [[spain]] and founding the Islamic realm of *al-Andalus*.
- Reached its northernmost advance into Western Europe before being checked by Frankish leader *Charles Martel* near Poitiers at the *Battle of Tours* in 732.
- Repelled at the walls of Constantinople during two major sieges in 674–678 and 717–718, where Byzantine defenses employing *Greek fire* preserved southeastern Europe.
- Clashed with Tang imperial forces at the *Battle of Talas* in 751 near [[china]], halting eastern military expansion while opening the [[silk-road]] to paper manufacturing.
- Established fortified garrison cities called *amsar*, such as *Kufa*, *Basra*, and *Fustat*, to house Arab armies, administer conquered subjects, and collect the *jizya* tax from non-Muslim populations.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the monotheistic religion whose spiritual message and political unification provided the foundational ideology for rapid territorial conquest.
- [[middle-east]] — the central theater where Arab armies permanently supplanted Greco-Roman and Persian dominance with Islamic cultural and linguistic institutions.
- [[roman-empire]] — the ancient Mediterranean power whose Byzantine continuation lost its wealthiest grain-producing and trading provinces during the onslaught.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian territory conquered in 711, giving rise to centuries of Islamic culture, scholarship, and architecture under *al-Andalus*.
- [[france]] — the Frankish realm whose forces halted caliphal incursions into continental Europe at the watershed *Battle of Tours*.
- [[china]] — the Asian empire whose Tang army fought the caliphate at the *Battle of Talas*, cementing Muslim control over Central Asia.
- [[silk-road]] — the transcontinental trade network revitalized and protected under a single imperial economic zone stretching from Central Asia to the Atlantic.
- [[christianity]] — the prevailing religion of the Mediterranean, which saw ancient patriarchates like Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem fall under Muslim rule.
