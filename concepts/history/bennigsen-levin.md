## summary

**Levin August von Bennigsen**, also known in Russian as **Leonty Leontyevich Bennigsen** or simply **Levin von Bennigsen**, was a German-born general of cavalry who served the [[russian-empire]] with distinction throughout the Napoleonic Wars. He achieved international prominence as the supreme commander of coalition forces during the War of the Fourth Coalition, fighting [[napoleon-bonaparte]] to a brutal, legendary standstill at the Battle of Eylau in early 1807 before suffering a catastrophic defeat at the Battle of Friedland later that campaign. Renowned for his tactical tenacity and volatile temper, he also played a decisive role in the 1801 assassination of Tsar *Paul I*, fought at Borodino and Tarutino during the 1812 French invasion of Russia, and commanded the Army of Poland during the decisive allied victory at the Battle of Leipzig.

## you gotta know

- Suffered a crushing defeat against [[napoleon-bonaparte]] at the Battle of Friedland in June 1807 after trapping his own army with its back against the Alle River, an error that destroyed the Fourth Coalition and compelled Tsar *Alexander I* to sign the Treaty of Tilsit.
- Commanded Russian and Prussian troops to a ferocious, blood-soaked tactical draw against [[napoleon-bonaparte]] at the Battle of Eylau in February 1807, inflicting catastrophic French casualties amidst a blinding blizzard and shattering the myth of French invincibility.
- Directed the Russian Army of Poland during the War of the Sixth Coalition, playing a pivotal role on the allied right wing in crushing French forces at the epic Battle of Leipzig in 1813, which earned him elevation to imperial count.
- Scored an important tactical triumph during the 1812 French retreat from Moscow by surprising and defeating Marshal *Joachim Murat* at the Battle of Tarutino, capturing dozens of cannons and igniting allied momentum.
- Acted as Chief of Staff to Field Marshal *Mikhail Kutuzov* during the 1812 French invasion, clashing fiercely with Kutuzov and vehemently opposing the surrender of Moscow during the historic Council of Fili.
- Conspired as a primary ringleader in the March 1801 palace coup that culminated in the violent assassination of Emperor *Paul I*, an intrigue that installed *Alexander I* on the Russian throne and revived Bennigsen's military fortunes.
- Began his life as a Hanoverian nobleman and guardsman in [[germany]] before joining the imperial army of Catherine the Great in 1773, gaining distinction against the [[ottoman-empire]] at the storming of Ochakov and during the Kościuszko Uprising in Poland.

## connections

- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — legendary French emperor whom he battled at Eylau, Friedland, and Leipzig.
- [[russian-empire]] — adopted imperial realm whose armies he commanded across four major continental campaigns.
- [[france]] — rival imperial powerhouse whose armies he contested across East Prussia, Russia, and Central Europe.
- [[germany]] — native homeland where he was raised in Hanover and where he concluded his service besieging Hamburg.
- [[french-revolution]] — continent-spanning political crisis whose resulting wars fueled his rapid rise to supreme field command.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — southern imperial adversary against whom he won early combat renown at the siege of Ochakov.
- [[romanticism]] — cultural and literary movement that dramatized his headquarters disputes, notably in Leo Tolstoy's *War and Peace*.
