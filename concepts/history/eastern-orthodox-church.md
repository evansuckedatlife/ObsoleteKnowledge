## summary

The **Eastern Orthodox Church** is one of the three major branches of Christianity, with roots in the apostolic churches of the Eastern Mediterranean and the *Byzantine Empire*. After the *Great Schism* of 1054 separated it from the Roman Catholic Church, Orthodoxy developed distinctive theology, liturgy, and iconography centered on Constantinople and later Russia. It remains the dominant Christian tradition in Eastern Europe, Russia, and parts of the Middle East.

## you gotta know

- Traces its apostolic succession to *Jesus's* disciples and the early churches of Jerusalem, Antioch, Alexandria, and Constantinople.
- Differs from Roman Catholicism in rejecting papal supremacy, veneration of icons (eventually accepted after the *Iconoclastic Controversy*), and certain doctrinal emphases.
- The *Great Schism* (1054) formalized the split between Eastern (Byzantine/Orthodox) and Western (Roman Catholic) Christianity over jurisdiction and theology.
- The *Byzantine Empire* was intimately identified with Orthodoxy; the Church provided spiritual legitimacy to imperial rule and preserved Greek learning.
- Moscow became the "Third Rome" after Constantinople's fall (1453), positioning Russian Orthodoxy as the tradition's center and successor to Byzantine Christianity.
- Uses the Divine Liturgy (Eucharist) as its central practice; emphasizes mystery, image (icons), and the transfiguration of matter as sacred.
- Survived Ottoman conquest and suppression during the Ottoman centuries (1453–nineteenth century), maintaining identity under foreign rule.
- The *Iconoclastic Controversy* (eighth–ninth centuries) saw emperors suppress icon veneration; the eventual restoration of icons became a defining Orthodox doctrine distinguishing it from Western Christianity.
- Orthodox Christianity today claims approximately 300 million adherents, concentrated in Russia, Ukraine, Serbia, Greece, and the Middle East, with significant diaspora communities worldwide.
- The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople remains the symbolic leader of Orthodoxy, though Orthodox churches are autocephalous (self-governing), creating a decentralized structure contrasting sharply with papal Catholicism.
