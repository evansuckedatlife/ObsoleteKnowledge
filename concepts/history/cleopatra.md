## summary

**Cleopatra VII Philopator** (69–30 BCE) was the last active pharaoh of *Egypt*, ruling at the twilight of the *Ptolemaic Dynasty* during the era of Roman expansion. A brilliant diplomat and linguist, Cleopatra navigated the civil wars of the late Roman Republic by forming strategic alliances with *Julius Caesar* and *Mark Antony*. Her relationships with these powerful men, her political acumen, and her symbolic status as an embodiment of Hellenistic and Egyptian culture made her one of history's most famous figures, though her final defeat by *Octavian* (the future *Augustus*) ended Ptolemaic rule and Egyptian independence, ushering in Roman domination.

## you gotta know

- Cleopatra was born into the *Ptolemaic Dynasty*, a Macedonian Greek dynasty ruling *Egypt* since *Alexander the Great*'s conquests; she was the daughter of *Ptolemy XII*, and her succession involved family civil war and Egyptian factions seeking a balance between independence and Roman alliance.
- She famously seduced or allied with *Julius Caesar*, the Roman general, when he arrived in Egypt pursuing his rival *Pompey* during Rome's civil war; their relationship produced a son, *Caesarion*, and gave Cleopatra valuable Roman protection against her rivals.
- After Caesar's assassination (44 BCE), Cleopatra allied with *Mark Antony*, one of Caesar's successors in power; their relationship combined political alliance with genuine affection, and together they had three children and pursued joint political interests in the eastern Mediterranean.
- Cleopatra's intelligence, charisma, and linguistic ability (she spoke nine languages) made her more than merely a seductress; she was a shrewd negotiator who understood Roman factional politics and used her resources and charm to advance Egyptian interests.
- Her conflict with *Octavian* (later Emperor *Augustus*) in the final years of the Roman Republic proved decisive; Octavian defeated her and Mark Antony at the *Battle of Actium* (31 BCE), ensuring his sole dominance and ending Ptolemaic independence.
- According to historical tradition (though some historians debate the account), Cleopatra committed suicide by allowing an *asp* (cobra) to bite her rather than be captured and paraded in Octavian's triumph; she was about 39 years old.
- Cleopatra represented the Hellenistic world's last great power center outside of Rome; her death marked the end of Greek political independence in the eastern Mediterranean and the beginning of unambiguous Roman imperial dominance.
- Cleopatra's legend—as an exotic, seductive woman of political power—has been romanticized in literature, drama, and popular culture for two millennia, though modern historical scholarship emphasizes her political intelligence and her role as a capable ruler protecting her kingdom's interests.
