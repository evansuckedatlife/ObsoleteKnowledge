## summary

**Hippie culture**, synonymous with the **hippies** and the broader **counterculture of the 1960s**, was a transformative youth movement that emerged in the *United States* during the mid-1960s before spreading across the Western world. Rooted in pacifism, communal living, bohemian aesthetics, and an emphatic rejection of traditional bourgeois materialism, the movement developed in direct opposition to the escalating *Vietnam War*. Its values shaped modern environmentalism, popular music, and civil liberties, permanently reshaping cultural norms surrounding freedom of expression and personal identity.

## you gotta know

- Centered geographically in the *Haight-Ashbury* district of *San Francisco*, which served as the vibrant epicenter for communal gatherings, underground presses, and radical street theater.
- Reached a nationwide crescendo during the 1967 *Summer of Love*, which drew over one hundred thousand young people to California to celebrate music, psychedelic art, and communal philosophy.
- Galvanized by the widespread domestic *anti-war movement*, organizing nonviolent draft protests and sit-ins against the military draft under President *Lyndon B. Johnson* and later *Richard Nixon*.
- Championed the widespread embrace of psychedelic substances like LSD, heavily popularized by Harvard psychologist *Timothy Leary* and his famous phrase encouraging youth to turn on, tune in, drop out.
- Culminated musically and culturally at the massive 1969 *Woodstock Music and Art Fair* in *Bethel*, *New York*, featuring seminal performances by *Jimi Hendrix*, *Janis Joplin*, and *The Who*.
- Absorbed profound spiritual influences from Eastern philosophy, adopting aspects of *Buddhism* and *Hinduism*, vegetarian diets, and holistic communal farming.
- Sparked conservative political backlash that fueled the law and order campaigns of the late 1960s and early 1970s across the *United States*.
