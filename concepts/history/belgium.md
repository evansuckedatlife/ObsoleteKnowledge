## summary

**Belgium** is a nation in northwestern Europe at the crossroads of French, German, and Dutch-speaking cultures, making it a strategic flashpoint in European history. Created in 1830 as an independent state after splitting from the Netherlands, Belgium's location has made it a battleground in major European conflicts. Its colonial ventures, particularly the brutal *Belgian Congo*, left a dark historical legacy, while its post-World War II evolution made it a founding member and seat of the European Union.

## you gotta know

- Belgium was created in 1830 as an independent nation following the Belgian Revolution against Dutch rule; it had been part of the Spanish Netherlands, then the Austrian Netherlands, then annexed by France and Holland.
- The nation is divided between Flemish-speaking (Dutch-related) Flanders in the north and French-speaking Wallonia in the south; this linguistic division has driven political tension and decentralization throughout Belgian history.
- Belgium became an early industrial power in the 19th century; coal mining, textile manufacturing, and steel production fueled rapid urbanization and working-class unrest.
- King *Leopold II* (1865–1909) established the *Belgian Congo* as his personal colonial property in Central Africa, where Belgian exploitation, forced labor, and resource extraction killed an estimated 10 million Africans.
- Belgium was overrun and occupied by Germany during *World War I* (1914–1918); the Western Front's trench warfare devastated Belgian territory and killed over 100,000 Belgian soldiers.
- Germany invaded Belgium again during *World War II* (1940), leading to a four-year occupation; Belgium was liberated by Allied forces in 1944.
- After World War II, Belgium became a founding member of NATO and the European Union; Brussels became the seat of EU institutions, making Belgium central to European integration.
