## summary

**Marketing** is the comprehensive discipline and strategic process of identifying, anticipating, and satisfying consumer needs through the systematic development, pricing, promotion, and distribution of goods and services. Arising in tandem with the factory expansions of the [[industrial-revolution]], it evolved over the course of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries from simple product distribution into a sophisticated apparatus shaping modern capitalism and [[consumer-culture]]. Centered primarily in the [[united-states]], the practice fundamentally transformed how commercial enterprises generate economic demand, construct symbolic brand identities, and interact with the public through pervasive [[mass-media]].

## you gotta know

- Codified into the canonical *Four Ps* marketing mix framework—*product*, *price*, *place*, and *promotion*—articulated by theorist *E. Jerome McCarthy* in 1960 and widely popularized across global business schools by *Philip Kotler*.
- Reached the zenith of its mid-century cultural authority along *Madison Avenue* in [[new-york-city]], where elite advertising agencies engineered the post-[[world-war-ii]] consumer boom through high-concept creative campaigns and stylized lifestyle appeals.
- Expanded in lockstep with twentieth-century broadcast technologies, harnessing commercial [[radio]] networks and household [[television]] programming to establish unified national markets, pervasive corporate sponsorships, and durable brand loyalties.
- Incorporated psychological theory, Freudian psychoanalysis, and subconscious suggestion through public relations pioneer *Edward Bernays*, whose influential campaigns—such as the 1929 *Torches of Freedom* demonstration—deliberately framed commodities as emblems of social status and personal liberation.
- Shifted historically through distinct economic orientations, moving from an early production orientation focused on factory throughput during the [[industrial-revolution]] toward an aggressive sales orientation in the interwar era, and ultimately arriving at a customer-focused market orientation designed to preempt consumer desires.
- Developed rigorous empirical methodologies and audience analytics through pioneers like *Claude Hopkins*, who introduced coupon response tracking in scientific advertising, and *Arthur Nielsen*, whose retail indexes and broadcast ratings quantified market behavior.
- Defined by the persistent dilemma of advertising accountability, immortalized in the famous adage attributed to department store magnate *John Wanamaker* that half the money spent on advertising is wasted, but one never knows which half.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — created mechanized mass production capabilities and unprecedented commodity surpluses, necessitating professional systems for stimulating consumer demand and coordinating wide-area distribution.
- [[united-states]] — operated as the chief incubator for modern consumer capitalism, commercial market research, installment credit mechanisms, and national brand architecture.
- [[new-york-city]] — served as the epicenter of twentieth-century global advertising agencies, centered on *Madison Avenue* and closely linked to major broadcast headquarters.
- [[mass-media]] — provided the essential communication channels required to transmit simultaneous commercial messages and cultural archetypes to millions of households.
- [[television]] — transformed retail promotion by combining dynamic audiovisual demonstrations, sponsored soap operas, and emotional narratives directly within domestic spaces.
- [[radio]] — inaugurated commercial network broadcasting in the 1920s, pioneering national advertising jingles, prime-time program sponsorships, and scheduled brand endorsements.
- [[world-war-ii]] — accelerated logistical supply chains, industrial productivity, and governmental persuasion techniques that directly informed postwar consumer brand marketing.
