# Cuneiform

## summary

**Cuneiform** is one of the earliest known writing systems, originating in ancient *Mesopotamia* around 3400 BCE and consisting of wedge-shaped marks impressed into clay tablets. Developed by the *Sumerians*, cuneiform was adapted by *Akkadian* scribes for their *Semitic* language and spread throughout the ancient Near East, serving as the primary writing system for millennia and recording the earliest known literature.

## you gotta know

- Cuneiform originated as a system of pictographs (picture symbols) used to record inventory and transactions, then evolved into increasingly abstract wedge-shaped impressions made with a stylus on soft clay.
- The name "cuneiform" comes from Latin *cuneus* (wedge) and *forma* (shape), reflecting the characteristic wedge-shaped marks that form its letters—a consequence of using a reed stylus on clay.
- *Akkadian* scribes adapted cuneiform to write their Semitic language, replacing Sumerian as the dominant language of *Mesopotamia* and bureaucracy; cuneiform could represent both logograms (word-symbols) and phonetic signs.
- Cuneiform was the writing system for major ancient texts including the *Code of Hammurabi*, the *Epic of Gilgamesh*, and countless administrative records, contracts, and royal inscriptions.
- The wedge-shapes of cuneiform made it difficult to inscribe on surfaces other than clay, constraining its spread compared to later alphabetic systems like *Phoenician* writing.
- Cuneiform remained in use for over 3,000 years, gradually displaced by alphabetic scripts (particularly *Aramaic*) around the 1st millennium BCE, though Akkadian cuneiform persisted in *Assyria* until the destruction of *Nineveh* (612 BCE).
- Cuneiform's complexity required years of scribal training; clay tablets were fragile but also remarkably durable (especially if accidentally fired), preserving ancient texts for archaeological discovery millennia later.
