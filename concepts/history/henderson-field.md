## summary

**Henderson Field** was a crucial military airfield on Guadalcanal in the Solomon Islands that served as the focal prize of the pivotal Guadalcanal campaign during [[world-war-ii]]. Originally begun by Imperial Japanese forces, the airstrip was seized by Allied forces in August 1942 and hastily finished to shelter the legendary *Cactus Air Force*. Holding the airstrip under unrelenting aerial, naval, and land assault allowed the [[united-states]] to establish vital regional air superiority, permanently blunting the expansion of imperial [[japan]] throughout the South Pacific.

## you gotta know

- Begun by Japanese naval engineering units at *Lunga Point* in mid-1942 to establish air cover over southern sea lanes and threaten Allied communication lines between the [[united-states]] and Australia.
- Seized virtually undefended on August 7, 1942, by the 1st Marine Division under Major General *Alexander Vandegrift* during the first major amphibious Allied offensive in the Pacific Theater.
- Named by the United States Marine Corps in honor of Major *Lofton R. Henderson*, a naval aviator killed leading a dive-bomber squadron at the *Battle of Midway*.
- Served as the home base for the *Cactus Air Force*, a patchwork coalition of Marine, Navy, and Army Air Forces pilots operating under primitive conditions, severe fuel shortages, and rampant tropical disease.
- Subjected to ferocious Japanese land attacks aimed at recapturing the strip, culminating in desperate hand-to-hand combat at the *Battle of Edson's Ridge* and the *Battle for Henderson Field*.
- Endured devastating nighttime naval bombardment on October 14, 1942, when Japanese battleships *Kongo* and *Haruna* fired over nine hundred 14-inch shells directly into the airfield perimeter, destroying dozens of aircraft and igniting aviation fuel dumps.
- Operated with experimental Marston matting steel planking laid over soft volcanic mud, enabling rapid repairs by combat engineers under constant enemy fire and tropical monsoons.
- Remains active in modern history as the principal runway of *Honiara International Airport*, the chief aviation gateway for the sovereign Solomon Islands.
