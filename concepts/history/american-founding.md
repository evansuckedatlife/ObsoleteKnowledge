## summary

The **American Founding** encompasses the intellectual, political, and legal creation of the *United States* between 1776 and 1789, defined by the radical moral proposition that all human beings possess natural rights to life, liberty, and self-governance. Rooted in the political theory of the *Enlightenment* and crystallized in the 1776 *Declaration of Independence*, this founding era replaced hereditary monarchy with constitutional republicanism grounded in the consent of the governed. Its enduring historical significance lies in the moral creed articulated at its birth, which later leaders like *Abraham Lincoln* reinterpreted as an unfinished promise binding future generations to achieve universal equality.

## you gotta know

- Authored primarily by *Thomas Jefferson* in the 1776 *Declaration of Independence*, the founding was anchored by the assertion that all men are created equal with unalienable rights to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness.
- During the *American Civil War*, *Abraham Lincoln* consecrated founding principles in his 1863 *Gettysburg Address*, dating the nation's birth to 1776 and defining human equality as America's core proposition.
- Encompassed two successive governing frameworks, shifting from the decentralized *Articles of Confederation* to the 1787 *United States Constitution*, which established a federal republic with separated powers.
- Drew philosophical legitimacy directly from the European *Enlightenment*, adopting *John Locke's* natural rights and social contract theory alongside *Montesquieu's* model of divided legislative, executive, and judicial authority.
- Harbored a glaring moral contradiction by accommodating chattel slavery through the Three-Fifths Clause and Fugitive Slave Clause, conflicting directly with its professed universal rights.
- Generated the ratification debates of 1787–1788, where *Alexander Hamilton* and *James Madison* defended federal power in *The Federalist Papers*, while Anti-Federalists secured the *Bill of Rights*.
- Formed the crux of the 1858 *Lincoln-Douglas* debates, where Lincoln insisted the founders recognized slavery as an evil marked for ultimate extinction under founding ideals.
- Invoked during the *Civil Rights Movement* by *Martin Luther King Jr.*, who described the founding texts as a promissory note guaranteeing freedom and equality for all citizens.

- [[american-revolution]] — the military conflict against the *British Empire* that won independence and enabled constitutional self-governance.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — author of the *Declaration of Independence* who articulated the fundamental moral creed of human equality.
- [[george-washington]] — revolutionary commander and first president whose voluntary surrender of power established vital republican norms.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the legal framework created in 1787 to implement founding ideals through a structured federal government.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the existential war in which *Abraham Lincoln* fought to preserve a republic conceived in liberty and dedicated to equality.
- [[14th-amendment]] — reconstruction amendment that incorporated founding principles of equality and liberty directly into federal constitutional law.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — civil rights leader who held America accountable to its founding promissory note of universal human dignity.
