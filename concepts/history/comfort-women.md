## summary

**comfort women** (Japanese: *ianfu*) were women and girls forced into institutionalized sexual slavery by the Imperial Japanese military before and during *World War II*. Established extensively across occupied territories following the *Nanjing Massacre* in 1937, military brothels—known euphemistically as "comfort stations"—held an estimated tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of victims, predominantly from Korea and China, but also from the Philippines, the Dutch East Indies, and other occupied regions. The issue remains one of the most contentious diplomatic disputes in East Asian modern history and international human rights advocacy.

## you gotta know

- Institutionalized systematically across Asia after mass rapes during the 1937 *Nanjing Massacre* prompted the military high command to establish state-controlled brothels to curb sexually transmitted infections and maintain army discipline.
- Recruited largely through deception, coercive brokering, false promises of factory work, or direct abduction by colonial authorities and imperial military police.
- The vast majority of victims were drawn from colonial Korea and occupied [[china]], with substantial numbers also taken from Taiwan, the Philippines, Indonesia, and occupied European civilian populations.
- In 1991, South Korean survivor *Kim Hak-sun* became the first victim to testify publicly about her ordeal, breaking decades of silence and transforming the issue into an international human rights campaign.
- The Japanese government issued the *Kono Statement* in 1993, formally acknowledging the Imperial Japanese military's direct involvement in the coercive recruitment and management of comfort stations.
- Memorial statues depicting a seated young girl, known as the *Statue of Peace*, have been erected outside Japanese embassies and consulates globally, often sparking intense diplomatic friction.
- A landmark 2015 bilateral agreement between [[japan]] and South Korea purported to "finally and irreversibly" settle the dispute with Japanese state apologies and a fund, but faced widespread criticism from survivors and was later effectively abandoned.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict during which imperial Japanese forces operated military comfort stations across the Pacific theater.
- [[japan]] — the imperial power whose military leadership established and administered the comfort station system.
- [[china]] — territory where thousands of local women were subjected to sexual slavery following military invasions.
- [[colonialism]] — the imperial framework that enabled the systematic exploitation and coercion of subjects in colonial Korea and Taiwan.
- [[world-war-i]] — earlier global conflict whose military brothel systems served as organizational precedents studied by Japanese commanders.
