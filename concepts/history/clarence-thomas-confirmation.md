## summary

The **Clarence Thomas confirmation** was the intensely contentious October 1991 United States Senate process that resulted in the appointment of *Clarence Thomas* to the Supreme Court of the *United States*. Nominated by President George H. W. Bush to succeed civil rights icon Thurgood Marshall, Thomas faced explosive sexual harassment allegations from law professor *Anita Hill*. The televised proceedings exposed deep political fractures regarding judicial ideology, workplace misconduct, gender dynamics, and racial representation.

## you gotta know

- Centered on graphic sexual harassment allegations brought forward by *Anita Hill*, a University of Oklahoma law professor who had worked as his assistant at the EEOC and the Department of Education.
- Featured Thomas's fierce, televised rebuttal before the Senate Judiciary Committee, in which he famously denounced the televised proceedings as a "high-tech lynching for uppity blacks."
- Chaired by Delaware Senator *Joe Biden*, whose leadership of the Senate Judiciary Committee faced severe criticism for excluding additional corroborating witnesses and permitting hostile questioning of Hill.
- Narrowly concluded on October 15, 1991, with the Senate voting 52 to 48 to confirm Thomas, representing the second-narrowest Supreme Court confirmation margin in modern American history.
- Galvanized female political participation nationwide, directly precipitating the 1992 "Year of the Woman" that saw an unprecedented number of women elected to the United States Congress.
- Prompted immediate institutional reforms in federal workplace sexual harassment policies and drove a dramatic nationwide increase in harassment complaint filings with the EEOC.
