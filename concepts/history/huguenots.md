## summary

The **Huguenots** were French Protestants who emerged during the [[french-wars-of-religion|French Wars of Religion]] (1562–1598), representing the Calvinist minority in a predominantly Catholic kingdom. Persecuted through massacres and legal restrictions, they fought for religious toleration alongside Catholics in a series of brutal civil wars. Their political struggle culminated in the [[edict-of-nantes|Edict of Nantes]] (1598), which granted them limited rights and a precarious peace that lasted until its revocation a century later.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a significant religious faction in France during the Protestant Reformation, adopting Calvinist theology and worship practices.
- The name "Huguenot" likely derives from a Geneva Protestant leader, though its exact etymology remains disputed by historians.
- Faced systematic persecution, including forced conversions and brutal massacres; the [[st-bartholomews-day-massacre|St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre]] (1572) killed thousands.
- Gathered politically around noble patrons, particularly the House of Bourbon, giving the religious conflict a dynastic dimension.
- Fought three major waves of civil war (1562–1570, 1572–1573, 1585–1598) seeking both religious freedom and political safety.
- [[henry-iv|Henry IV]] (Henry of Navarre), a Huguenot who converted to Catholicism to win the throne, issued the [[edict-of-nantes|Edict of Nantes]] granting them legal toleration.
- After the Edict's revocation (1685), many Huguenots fled France, enriching Protestant nations like England, the Netherlands, and the American colonies.
