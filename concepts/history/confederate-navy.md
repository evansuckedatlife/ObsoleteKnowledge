## summary

The **Confederate Navy** was the naval force of the *Confederate States of America* during the *American Civil War* (1861–1865). Vastly outnumbered by the *Union Navy*, it could not achieve naval supremacy but pursued commerce raiding and technological innovation to break the *Union* blockade. The *CSS Virginia* (*Merrimack*), the world's first ironclad warship, epitomized this desperate effort; though tactically indecisive against the *USS Monitor*, it demonstrated that wooden sailing navies were obsolete and revolutionized naval warfare forever.

## you gotta know

- The Confederacy inherited virtually no navy at secession; the South had to build one from scratch, seizing northern ships and constructing new vessels in limited facilities with minimal industrial capacity.
- Southern strategy centered on commerce raiding and blockade-running: privateers and warships like the *CSS Alabama* attacked Union merchant shipping to cripple northern trade; blockade runners slipped through the Union cordon to bring supplies from Europe.
- The *CSS Virginia*, constructed by the Confederacy by raising the sunken *USS Merrimack* and plating her with iron armor, was launched in 1862; she was revolutionary—the first warship sheathed entirely in iron, seemingly unsinkable.
- The *Battle of Hampton Roads* (9 March 1862) saw the *CSS Virginia* clash with the *USS Monitor*, the Union's new ironclad; neither could sink the other, but the drawn battle proved that armored warships had replaced wooden vessels as the future of naval combat.
- Despite the *Virginia*'s innovation, the Confederate Navy never overcome the Union's overwhelming numerical and industrial superiority; the blockade remained effective, strangling the Southern economy and preventing essential imports.
- Innovations developed by the Confederate Navy—ironclads, semi-submersible vessels (*CSS Hunley*), naval mines—were adopted by navies worldwide; Confederate naval engineers thus influenced modern naval architecture despite their defeat.
