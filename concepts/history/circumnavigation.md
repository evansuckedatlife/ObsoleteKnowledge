## summary

**Circumnavigation** refers to the act of traveling completely around an entire planet, continent, or astronomical body by sea, air, or land. First achieved globally between 1519 and 1522 by an expedition under the crown of [[spain]] initiated by *Ferdinand Magellan* and completed by *Juan Sebastián Elcano*, the feat conclusively proved the spherical continuity of the world ocean. It fundamentally revolutionized early modern cartography, stimulated imperial competition during the [[age-of-exploration]], and became a benchmark of maritime logistics and endurance.

## you gotta know

- Initiated by the Portuguese navigator *Ferdinand Magellan*, who sailed under the Spanish flag of *Charles V* in 1519 with an armada of five ships seeking a westward route to the Moluccan spice islands.
- *Juan Sebastián Elcano* took command of the lone surviving ship, the *Victoria*, after Magellan was killed at the Battle of Mactan in the Philippines, guiding eighteen surviving crewmen back to Sanlúcar de Barrameda in 1522.
- Traversed the treacherous strait at the southern tip of South America—now known as the Strait of Magellan—which linked the Atlantic to the newly christened Pacific Ocean.
- *Sir Francis Drake* accomplished the second global circumnavigation aboard the *Golden Hind* between 1577 and 1580, claiming territory in North America for [[england]] and seizing immense Spanish treasure.
- *Jeanne Baret* became the first known woman to complete the voyage between 1766 and 1769, disguising herself as a male assistant to the naturalist *Philibert Commerçon* aboard the expedition led by *Louis Antoine de Bougainville* for [[france]].
- Modern records shifted from naval sail to aviation in 1924, when aviators of the United States Army Air Service completed the first aerial circumnavigation using modified open-cockpit biplanes over 175 days.
- Orbital circumnavigation of Earth was first achieved during the [[space-race]] in 1961 by Soviet cosmonaut *Yuri Gagarin* aboard *Vostok 1*, completing a single revolution in 108 minutes.

## connections

- [[age-of-exploration]] — maritime era whose defining commercial and nautical milestone was global transit.
- [[spain]] — funded and sponsored the historic 1519–1522 voyage to bypass Portuguese trade monopolies.
- [[colonialism]] — imperial expansion accelerated as seafaring nations mapped new territorial and trade routes.
- [[british-empire]] — capitalized on voyages like Drake's to establish global naval hegemony and maritime dominance.
- [[silk-road]] — traditional overland trade network whose disruption incentivized global maritime circumvention.
- [[circumpolar-cooperation]] — contemporary multilateral governance uniting nations across northern polar routes.
- [[space-race]] — technological rivalry that elevated circumnavigation from maritime voyages into orbital spaceflight.
