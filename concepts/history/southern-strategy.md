## summary

The **Southern Strategy** was an electoral realignment campaign pursued by the Republican Party in the [[united-states]] during the late 1960s and 1970s to attract white southern voters who were disaffected by the [[civil-rights-movement]]. Orchestrated during the presidential campaigns of *Richard Nixon* and analyzed by political strategist *Kevin Phillips*, the strategy utilized racial grievances, opposition to federally mandated school desegregation, and states' rights rhetoric to peel conservative white voters away from their historic allegiance to the Democratic Party. This political maneuver effectively dismantled the century-old "Solid South" established during the [[reconstruction-era]], permanently transforming the American electoral map and laying the structural foundation for modern conservative dominance across the Sun Belt.

## you gotta know

- Spearheaded by *Richard Nixon* in the 1968 presidential election, utilizing coded appeals to "law and order" and suburban stability to capture conservative white voters unsettled by urban riots and desegregation protests.
- Built upon the electoral breakthrough of *Barry Goldwater* in 1964, who carried five Deep South states after voting against the *Civil Rights Act of 1964*, proving that conservative constitutionalism could shatter the Democratic monopoly in the region.
- Catalyzed by third-party candidate *George Wallace* in 1968, whose fierce segregationist rhetoric won five southern states and demonstrated the electoral urgency of capturing disaffected white Democrats to secure a national majority.
- Outlined theoretically by political strategist *Kevin Phillips* in his 1969 book *The Emerging Republican Majority*, which projected that rising ethnic and racial polarization would naturally deliver the Sun Belt to the Republican Party.
- Explained bluntly in a famous 1981 interview by strategist *Lee Atwater*, who described how political rhetoric had evolved from explicit racial epithets in the 1950s into abstract, race-neutral talking points such as forced busing and states' rights.
- Coordinated in the White House by *Harry Dent Sr.*, a former aide to South Carolina Senator *Strom Thurmond*, who advised the administration to slow federal integration enforcement and appoint regional conservatives to federal posts.
- Prompted Nixon to nominate conservative southern judges *Clement Haynsworth* and *G. Harrold Carswell* to the Supreme Court as symbolic gestures to southern conservatives, though the Senate ultimately rejected both nominations over ethical concerns and civil rights records.
- Solidified the South as a dependable bastion for Republican presidential candidates, culminating in *Ronald Reagan*'s sweep of the region after opening his 1980 campaign with a speech championing states' rights near Philadelphia, Mississippi.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the landmark struggle for racial equality whose legislative victories alienated southern conservatives and precipitated the regional political realignment.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the formative historical epoch following the Civil War that created the Democratic "Solid South" in violent opposition to federal intervention.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose national two-party system and Electoral College dynamics were fundamentally redefined by the regional shift.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional provision ensuring equal protection of the laws, which served as the legal engine for integration that southern strategists sought to limit.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — the preeminent civil rights leader whose nonviolent campaigns and 1968 assassination triggered racial backlashes that conservative campaigns actively exploited.
- [[cold-war]] — the broader geopolitical standoff whose anti-communist imperatives were invoked by conservative politicians to portray civil rights activists as subversive radicals.
