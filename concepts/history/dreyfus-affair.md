## summary

The **Dreyfus Affair** was a profound political, social, and judicial crisis that polarized the Third Republic of *[[france]]* from 1894 until 1906, initiated by the wrongful treason conviction of Jewish army captain *Alfred Dreyfus*. Centered on forged military intelligence allegedly leaked to the German Empire, the ordeal unmasked widespread institutional antisemitism, judicial corruption, and authoritarianism within the French general staff. The struggle between reactionary, clerical nationalist factions and progressive, secular *Dreyfusards* transformed the nation's political landscape, cementing the separation of church and state while galvanizing modern political Zionism.

## you gotta know

- Began in autumn 1894 when French counterintelligence retrieved a torn letter, the *bordereau*, from the wastebasket of the German military attaché in Paris, detailing sensitive artillery secrets.
- Captain *Alfred Dreyfus*, a native of Alsace and one of the few prominent Jewish officers on the French general staff, was falsely accused based on flawed handwriting analysis and rampant antisemitic bias.
- Subjected on January 5, 1895, to a ritual military degradation ceremony in the courtyard of the *École Militaire*, where his sword was broken and uniform braids were severed while an enraged crowd chanted antisemitic slurs.
- Sentenced to life imprisonment and exiled to the penal colony on *Devil's Island* off the coast of French Guiana, enduring harsh solitary confinement and debilitating physical illness.
- Unraveled in 1896 when the new head of counterintelligence, Lieutenant Colonel *Georges Picquart*, discovered handwriting evidence proving that Major *Ferdinand Walsin Esterhazy* was the true culprit, only for military authorities to silence *Picquart* and transfer him to North Africa.
- Escalated into an international sensation on January 13, 1898, when novelist *Émile Zola* published *"J'Accuse...!"*, a scathing front-page open letter to President *Félix Faure* in *Georges Clemenceau's* paper *L'Aurore*, exposing the military cover-up.
- Exposed further when Major *Hubert-Joseph Henry* admitted to fabricating documents (*le faux Henry*) to reinforce the state's case against Dreyfus, leading to *Henry's* arrest and subsequent suicide in prison.
- Deeply influenced Austro-Hungarian journalist *Theodor Herzl*, who covered the public trial and anti-Jewish mob violence in Paris, concluding that assimilation could not protect Jews and accelerating his founding of modern political Zionism.
- Concluded after a contentious 1899 retrial in Rennes and an executive pardon, culminating in a July 12, 1906 Supreme Court ruling that completely exonerated Dreyfus and restored him to the army with the rank of major.

## connections

- [[france]] — the nation whose Third Republic was fractured by bitter ideological disputes over justice, secularism, and state authority.
- [[judaism]] — the religious and ethnic identity of Dreyfus that turned him into an immediate target of institutional prejudice and conspiratorial nationalism.
- [[germany]] — the imperial adversary whose embassy received leaked military secrets and whose rivalry with Paris intensified domestic espionage fears.
- [[french-revolution]] — the historical wellspring of egalitarian republican values that secular intellectuals invoked to defend Dreyfus against royalist reactionaries.
- [[realism]] — the literary and intellectual tradition embraced by *Émile Zola*, who applied rigorous social observation and polemical courage to champion justice.
- [[world-war-i]] — the subsequent European conflict in which both *Alfred Dreyfus* and his son served with distinction in the French armed forces.
- [[colonialism]] — the overseas imperial system that operated remote penal outposts like *Devil's Island* used to punish and isolate state convicts.
