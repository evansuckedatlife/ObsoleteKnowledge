## summary

**Freedom of speech** is the civil liberty to articulate opinions, ideas, and political viewpoints without fear of governmental retaliation, censorship, or legal sanction. Championed during the European *Enlightenment* by thinkers like John Locke and Voltaire, it received paramount legal codification in the First Amendment of the *United States Constitution*. Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, it evolved from a safeguard against prior restraint into a broad constitutional shield protecting political debate, artistic expression, and dissent.

## you gotta know

- Formally protected in American constitutional law via the First Amendment of the Bill of Rights, drafted under the guidance of *James Madison*.
- Heavily influenced by political philosophy from the *Enlightenment*, particularly John Milton's anti-censorship tract *Areopagitica* and John Stuart Mill's treatise *On Liberty*.
- Tested in times of national crisis, beginning with the controversial Alien and Sedition Acts of 1798 under John Adams, which drew sharp opposition from *Thomas Jefferson*.
- Limited initially during World War I in cases such as *Schenck v. United States* (1919), which established the famous "clear and present danger" standard through Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.
- Extended to protect symbolic speech in *Tinker v. Des Moines* (1969), recognizing that public school students do not shed their constitutional rights at the schoolhouse gate.
- Strengthened dramatically in *Brandenburg v. Ohio* (1969), establishing that inflammatory advocacy cannot be punished unless directed to inciting imminent lawless action.
- Served as an indispensable instrument of protest and civil disobedience during the *Civil Rights Movement*, safeguarding marches, boycotts, and political addresses.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — supreme law of the United States whose First Amendment bars Congress from abridging speech and the press.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — mass twentieth-century movement that exercised public speech, assembly, and petitioning to dismantle segregation.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical era that advanced liberal theories of individual conscience, open debate, and rational inquiry.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose judicial system developed expansive legal protections for dissident and commercial speech.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — founding statesman who fiercely opposed political censorship and championed religious and intellectual liberty.
- [[14th-amendment]] — constitutional amendment whose Due Process Clause incorporated fundamental rights against state-level interference.
