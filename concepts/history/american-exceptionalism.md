## summary

**American exceptionalism** is the foundational belief and ideological framework asserting that the *[[united-states]]* holds a distinctive, qualitative difference and moral superiority relative to other sovereign nations. Emerging from the confluence of seventeenth-century providential religious convictions and the radical republican principles of the *[[enlightenment]]* during the *[[american-revolution]]*, the thesis posits that America was uniquely founded upon universal ideas rather than common ancestry or geography. The concept has historically served as a guiding ethos for domestic identity, national integration, and international interventionism, remaining a decisive touchstone in modern political rhetoric.

## you gotta know

- The metaphorical foundation of the concept traces to *John Winthrop*'s 1630 sermon *A Model of Christian Charity*, delivered to Puritan colonists bound for Massachusetts Bay, which cited *[[jesus]]*'s Sermon on the Mount to warn that "we shall be as a city upon a hill."
- French magistrate *Alexis de Tocqueville* offered the first major sociological diagnosis in his 1835 volume *Democracy in America*, observing that Americans occupied a uniquely "exceptional" position due to their democratic equality, puritanical heritage, and absence of feudal aristocracy.
- During the 1840s, the ideology fueled the doctrine of *Manifest Destiny*, popularized by editor *John L. O'Sullivan*, which asserted a divinely mandated mission for the young republic to civilize the continent and displace European colonial powers.
- The explicit lexical formulation emerged within early twentieth-century Marxism, when Soviet premier *Joseph Stalin* condemned Communist Party USA leader *Jay Lovestone* in 1929 for promoting the "heresy" that American capitalism possessed unique strengths that exempted it from imminent collapse.
- *Ronald Reagan* canonized the modern political use of the phrase by repeatedly evoking *Winthrop*'s sermon as a "shining city upon a hill," deploying it in his 1980 election campaign and 1989 farewell address to articulate moral purpose during the *[[cold-war]]*.
- Political sociologist *Seymour Martin Lipset* formalized the academic study of the phenomenon in his 1996 text *American Exceptionalism: A Double-Edged Sword*, describing how the core creed of liberty, egalitarianism, individualism, populism, and laissez-faire simultaneously generated high social mobility and elevated rates of crime and inequality.
- Revisionist historians and critics argue that the concept functions primarily as nationalist propaganda that masks racial subjugation, imperial conquest, and *[[colonialism]]*, while scholars like *Daniel Bell* famously proclaimed an "end of American exceptionalism" in the wake of the Vietnam War and Watergate.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the sovereign republic whose founding ideals and geopolitical mission form the subject of the ideology.
- [[american-revolution]] — the transformative conflict that created a new nation explicitly grounded in anti-monarchical and republican principles.
- [[cold-war]] — the twentieth-century rivalry in which American policymakers invoked moral uniqueness to combat global communism.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical epoch whose concepts of natural law, human liberty, and popular sovereignty informed the American creed.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational legal framework venerated by exceptionalists as an unprecedented experiment in constitutional government.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition that supplied the providential themes, biblical metaphors, and moral mission animating early American identity.
- [[colonialism]] — the imperial system that critics argue was reproduced by American foreign policy despite exceptionalist rhetoric rejecting traditional empire.
