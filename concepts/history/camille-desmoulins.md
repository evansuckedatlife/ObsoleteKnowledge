## summary

**Camille Desmoulins** (formally baptized **Lucie-Simplice-Camille-Benoist Desmoulins**) was a prominent French journalist, pamphleteer, and radical republican politician whose fiery oratory helped mobilize the Parisian populace to storm the Bastille. A schoolmate of *Maximilien Robespierre* and a devoted compatriot of *Georges Danton*, he became one of the most celebrated polemical writers of the [[french-revolution]] through publications that attacked royalist tyranny. However, his horrified resistance to the mounting excesses of the *Reign of Terror* led him to urge executive mercy, prompting his trial and execution by guillotine alongside Danton.

## you gotta know

- On July 12, 1789, standing on a café table outside the Café de Foy in the gardens of the Palais-Royal, he gave an impassioned impromptu speech calling citizens to arms and plucking a green leaf as a revolutionary badge.
- His legendary exhortations at the Palais-Royal directly galvanized the popular insurrection that seized weapons from the Invalides and stormed the Bastille fortress two days later.
- He achieved widespread fame with early polemical pamphlets like *La France libre* and his celebrated newspaper *Les Révolutions de France et de Brabant*, which championed popular sovereignty and mocked counter-revolutionaries.
- While notoriously afflicted with a persistent stammer in ordinary conversation, his printed prose was universally acknowledged as exceptionally witty, eloquent, and devastatingly persuasive.
- He co-founded the radical Cordeliers Club alongside *Georges Danton* and *Jean-Paul Marat*, actively orchestrating the August 10, 1792 uprising that toppled the French monarchy.
- Serving as a deputy to the National Convention, he voted for the execution of King Louis XVI and authored *Histoire des Brissotins*, a scathing diatribe that helped orchestrate the political destruction of the moderate Girondins.
- In December 1793, appalled by the bloodletting of the Terror, he published *Le Vieux Cordelier*, which bravely championed the establishment of a "Committee of Clemency" and condemned Robespierre's Committee of Public Safety.
- Robespierre ultimately abandoned their lifelong friendship, leading to Desmoulins's arrest, his dramatic show trial before the Revolutionary Tribunal, and his execution on April 5, 1794; his young wife Lucile was guillotined shortly thereafter.

## connections

- [[french-revolution]] — the monumental political transformation that elevated his journalism to national prominence and ultimately claimed his life.
- [[france]] — the country whose ancien régime he helped dismantle in favor of a radical egalitarian republic.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual current celebrating liberty, classical republicanism, and reason that deeply informed his political philosophy.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — the rising military figure who consolidated power in France in the wake of the political exhaustion following the Terror.
- [[satire]] — the sharp literary weapon of ridicule and irony that Desmoulins wielded throughout his journalistic attacks on political adversaries.
- [[georges-danton]] — his chief political mentor, friend, and Montagnard ally with whom he shared leadership of the moderate "Indulgents" and a common fate at the scaffold.
