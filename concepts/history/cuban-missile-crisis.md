## summary

The *Cuban Missile Crisis* (October 1962) was a thirteen-day confrontation between the *United States* and the *Soviet Union* during the *Cold War* over the discovery of Soviet nuclear missiles in *Cuba*, just ninety miles from American shores. The crisis brought the world closer to nuclear war than any other Cold War moment; resolution required secret negotiations, careful brinkmanship, and American concessions regarding Jupiter missiles in Turkey that remained publicly hidden for decades.

## you gotta know

- *President John F. Kennedy* and his national security team learned on October 16, 1962, that reconnaissance photographs from U-2 spy planes had detected Soviet *intermediate-range ballistic missiles* (IRBMs) being installed in Cuba under the direction of Soviet leader *Nikita Khrushchev*.
- *Khrushchev* had secretly deployed the missiles to protect *Fidel Castro*'s revolutionary Cuban regime from American invasion (following the failed *Bay of Pigs* invasion in 1961) and to address a perceived American nuclear advantage over the Soviet Union.
- Kennedy convened the *Executive Committee* (ExComm) of senior advisors, including *Robert Kennedy* (his brother and Attorney General), *Dean Rusk*, *Robert McNamara*, and military leaders; the group debated military strikes versus naval blockade versus diplomacy.
- Kennedy announced a naval *quarantine* (blockade) of Cuba on October 22, escalating tensions as Soviet ships approached the American interdiction line; the two superpowers engaged in high-stakes brinkmanship with nuclear war seemingly imminent.
- *Nikita Khrushchev* backed down on October 28, announcing removal of the missiles from Cuba; Kennedy had secretly agreed to remove American Jupiter missiles from Turkey (kept secret for decades to avoid appearing weak and to protect American credibility).
- The crisis demonstrated both the real risk of nuclear war and the capacity of superpowers to negotiate crises; it led to the *Hot Line* agreement establishing a direct Kennedy-Khrushchev communication channel and contributed to the *Nuclear Test Ban Treaty* (1963).
