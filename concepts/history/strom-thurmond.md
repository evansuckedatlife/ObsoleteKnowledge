## summary

**Strom Thurmond** was an American politician from South Carolina who served as governor and represented his state in the United States Senate for forty-eight years between 1954 and 2003. As the presidential nominee of the segregationist *States' Rights Democratic Party*—widely known as the *Dixiecrats*—in the 1948 presidential election, he orchestrated a major regional insurrection against President *Harry S. Truman* and the national Democratic platform. His unrelenting resistance to federal integration policies and his historic 1964 party switch to the Republicans played a foundational role in realigning the political landscape of the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Mounted a major third-party presidential bid in the 1948 election as the nominee of the *Dixiecrats*, winning thirty-nine electoral votes across four southern states in protest against federal civil rights initiatives.
- Conducted the longest lone filibuster in the history of the United States Senate against the *Civil Rights Act of 1957*, speaking continuously from the floor for twenty-four hours and eighteen minutes.
- Spearheaded the drafting of the *Southern Manifesto* in 1956 alongside other regional lawmakers, condemning the Supreme Court ruling in *Brown v. Board of Education* and advocating for massive resistance.
- Entered the Senate in 1954 after winning a historic write-in campaign, making him the first person elected to the upper chamber without appearing on the printed general election ballot.
- Switched allegiance from the Democratic Party to the Republican Party in 1964 to endorse presidential nominee *Barry Goldwater*, accelerating the conservative realignment of white southern voters.
- Became the first sitting senator to reach the age of one hundred, retiring in 2003 as the longest-serving member of the body up to that point and having served as President pro tempore.
- Participated in the *D-Day* invasion during [[world-war-ii]] as an army officer attached to the 82nd Airborne Division, crash-landing in a glider behind enemy lines in Normandy.
- Secretly fathered a mixed-race daughter, *Essie Mae Washington-Williams*, in 1925 with a teenage Black domestic worker named *Carrie Butler*, an arrangement he privately supported financially until his death.

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — relentlessly organized legislative and institutional resistance against federal desegregation, voting rights acts, and public accommodation mandates.
- [[united-states]] — served as a central fixture of federal governance across six decades, profoundly shaping judicial nominations and military defense posture.
- [[world-war-ii]] — deployed with the 82nd Airborne Division into occupied France, sustaining injuries when his combat glider crashed on D-Day.
- [[cold-war]] — championed aggressive anti-communist containment policies, robust nuclear modernization, and vast expansions in federal defense budgets.
- [[14th-amendment]] — resisted federal interpretations of the Equal Protection Clause that dismantled state-mandated segregation and disenfranchisement.
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — denounced the civil rights leader as an agitator and vigorously opposed designating a federal holiday in his memory.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — invoked the Tenth Amendment to defend states' rights and limit the regulatory reach of the federal government.
