## summary

The **Congressional Black Caucus** (*CBC*) is an influential legislative organization composed of African American members of the *United States Congress*. Established in 1971 by thirteen founding representatives, the caucus was created to ensure that federal policy addressed the economic, legal, and social disparities facing Black Americans following the [[civil-rights-movement]]. Often termed the *Conscience of the Congress*, the caucus champions civil liberties, voting rights protections under the [[14th-amendment]], criminal justice reform, and urban economic development.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1971 under the primary leadership of *Charles Diggs*, who served as its first chairman, alongside twelve other Black members of the *House of Representatives*.
- Co-founded by prominent political figures including *Shirley Chisholm*, the first Black woman elected to Congress, *John Conyers*, *Charles Rangel*, *Ronald Dellums*, and *Louis Stokes*.
- Famously boycotted President *Richard Nixon*'s 1971 *State of the Union* address after he refused to meet with them, eventually securing an official meeting where they presented sixty policy recommendations.
- Spearheaded the legislative campaign led by *John Conyers* to establish a federal holiday honoring [[martin-luther-king-jr]], which was successfully signed into law in 1983.
- Drafts an annual *Alternative Federal Budget* that prioritizes public education, universal healthcare, housing access, and social safety nets over military expansion.
- Played a leading role in the passage of the *Comprehensive Anti-Apartheid Act* of 1986, organizing congressional opposition to South African apartheid and overriding *Ronald Reagan*'s presidential veto.
- Operates under the guiding motto formulated by *William L. Clay Sr.*: *Black people have no permanent friends, no permanent enemies, just permanent interests.*

## connections

- [[civil-rights-movement]] — served as the legislative continuation of the mass mobilization and voting rights victories of the 1960s.
- [[14th-amendment]] — utilizes the equal protection clause to challenge discriminatory voting maps, gerrymandering, and disenfranchisement policies in the [[united-states]].
- [[martin-luther-king-jr]] — organized the decade-long legislative effort to establish his birthday as a recognized federal holiday.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — traces its historical roots to the first Black lawmakers elected during federal reconstruction, such as *Hiram Revels* and *Joseph Rainey*.
- [[united-states]] — functions as an institutional bloc within the federal legislature shaping national domestic and foreign policies.
- [[containment]] — frequently offered progressive critiques of Cold War foreign policy, interventionism in developing nations, and military spending.
