## summary

**John Diefenbaker** was a Canadian lawyer and statesman who served as the thirteenth prime minister of Canada from 1957 to 1963. Leading the Progressive Conservative Party to a historic landslide victory in 1958, he championed a populist vision of northern development and equal citizenship known as *One Canada*. His premiership left an enduring legal and cultural legacy through the passage of the *Canadian Bill of Rights*, though his administration was marred by intense economic friction and defense disagreements with the [[united-states]] during the [[cold-war]].

## you gotta know

- Nicknamed *Dief the Chief*, he led the Progressive Conservatives to capture 208 of 265 seats in the 1958 federal election, achieving the largest parliamentary majority proportion in Canadian history up to that time.
- Enacted the landmark *Canadian Bill of Rights* in 1960, a foundational federal statute protecting fundamental freedoms, equality before the law, and individual rights.
- Extended the unconditional federal franchise to Status Indians in 1960 and appointed *James Gladstone*, a member of the Kainai Nation, as the country's first Indigenous senator.
- Abruptly cancelled the cutting-edge *Avro Arrow* supersonic interceptor aircraft program in 1959 due to ballooning costs, an event that became an enduring symbol of lost industrial ambition.
- Clashed bitterly with *James Coyne*, the [[governor-of-the-bank-of-canada]], attempting to legislate his removal during the acrimonious *Coyne Affair* over tight credit and unemployment.
- Opposed the deployment of American nuclear warheads on Canadian *Bomarc* missiles during the [[cold-war]], alienating President *John F. Kennedy* and causing his Cabinet to collapse over defense policy in 1963.
- Played a pivotal international role in the [[british-empire]] Commonwealth by condemning apartheid, prompting South Africa's withdrawal from the Commonwealth in 1961.
