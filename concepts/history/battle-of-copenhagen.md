## summary

The **Battle of Copenhagen**, also designated the **First Battle of Copenhagen**, was a ferocious naval confrontation fought on 2 April 1801 off the coast of Denmark between the Royal Navy of the [[british-empire]] and the fortified defensive line of the Dano-Norwegian fleet. Orchestrated to dismantle the anti-blockade League of Armed Neutrality sponsored by the [[russian-empire]], the engagement secured unchecked British maritime supremacy over strategic shipping corridors in the Baltic Sea during the wider conflicts of the [[french-revolution]]. The combat is universally renowned for the calculated defiance of Vice-Admiral *Horatio Nelson*, whose refusal to acknowledge an official command to withdraw transformed a perilous tactical predicament into an overwhelming victory.

## you gotta know

- *Horatio Nelson* famously raised a telescope to his blind eye when his conservative commander, Admiral Sir *Hyde Parker*, hoisted Signal 39 ordering a general retreat, quipping that he truly had a right to be blind sometimes before ordering his squadron to press the attack.
- The clash was precipitated by the Second League of Armed Neutrality, an alliance between Denmark-Norway, Sweden, Prussia, and the [[russian-empire]] intended to protect neutral merchant shipping from aggressive British inspections, which London viewed as an existential threat to its war effort against [[france]].
- Nelson chose to transfer his flag from the heavy *HMS St George* to the shallower sixty-four-gun *HMS Elephant*, specifically to maneuver across the treacherous shoals and shifting sandbanks of the Outer Deep and King's Deep (*Kongedybet*).
- Navigational hazards severely undermined the initial assault when three British ships of the line—*Agamemnon*, *Bellona*, and *Russell*—ran hard aground, leaving the remaining vessels exposed to lethal crossfire from the heavy shore batteries of the Trekroner fortress.
- Confronting heavy casualties on both sides, Nelson sent a celebrated letter under a white flag addressed "To the Brothers of Englishmen, the Danes," threatening to set alight the captured floating hulks with their wounded Danish prisoners aboard unless firing ceased, thereby initiating truce negotiations.
- Crucially, Tsar *Paul I* had been assassinated in a palace coup in Saint Petersburg just over a week before the engagement took place, an event that fatally fractured the Armed Neutrality and rendered the tactical bloodshed strategically superfluous within days.
- The 1801 engagement is distinct from the 1807 Second Battle of Copenhagen, during which British forces under Admiral *James Gambier* and *Lord Cathcart* laid siege to and bombarded the Danish capital with newly developed Congreve rockets to prevent [[napoleon-bonaparte]] from capturing the Danish fleet, giving birth to the diplomatic term "to Copenhagen."

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — asserted absolute naval hegemony to protect trade routes and enforce maritime embargoes during the global struggle.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — attempted to strangle British commerce through economic alliances, motivating preemptive Royal Navy strikes across neutral ports.
- [[russian-empire]] — organized the Second League of Armed Neutrality under Tsar *Paul I* to contest British boarding of northern maritime trade.
- [[france]] — the primary continental adversary whose ongoing military triumphs prompted Britain to isolate Europe from essential Baltic naval resources.
- [[england]] — celebrated the victory as a testament to maritime daring, cementing Nelson's stature as the foremost national hero of the age.
- [[french-revolution]] — ignited the broader global conflict that fractured European trade systems and drew neutral Baltic kingdoms into warfare.
