## summary

**Francisco Pizarro** was a Spanish conquistador who led the imperial expedition that toppled the Inca Empire in the 1530s, claiming western South America for the Crown of Castile. Operating during the wider *[[age-of-exploration]]*, he capitalized on a devastating civil war and European diseases to capture the Incan ruler *Atahualpa* at the Battle of Cajamarca. His conquest initiated centuries of Spanish imperial *[[colonialism]]* across the Andes and led to the founding of the city of Lima.

## you gotta know

- Born an illegitimate son in Trujillo, Extremadura, in the Kingdom of *[[spain]]*, rising from humble origins and illiteracy to become one of the wealthiest conquistadors in history.
- Accompanied Vasco Núñez de Balboa on his historic 16th-century expedition across the Isthmus of Panama to reach the Pacific Ocean in 1513.
- Formed a partnership with fellow conquistador Diego de Almagro and the priest Hernando de Luque to fund exploratory voyages along the Pacific coast of South America.
- Drew the famous line in the sand with his sword on the Isla del Gallo in 1527, inviting his desperate men to choose between returning to poverty in Panama or crossing the line to conquer Peru.
- Ambushed and captured the Inca Emperor *Atahualpa* at the Battle of Cajamarca in November 1532 with fewer than two hundred men.
- Executed *Atahualpa* in 1533 despite the captive emperor fulfilling his promise to fill a room once with gold and twice with silver as a ransom.
- Founded the colonial capital city of *Ciudad de los Reyes* (modern-day Lima) in 1535 as the administrative seat of the Spanish Viceroyalty of Peru.
- Assassinated in his palace in Lima in June 1541 by supporters of Diego de Almagro's son following a violent civil feud among the victorious conquistadors.

## connections

- [[spain]] — the Iberian kingdom under Emperor Charles V for which he claimed vast territories, silver mines, and wealth.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the maritime era of transatlantic navigation and conquest that brought European powers to the Americas.
- [[colonialism]] — the system of overseas political and economic subjugation established across the Andean region through his campaigns.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural and technological period in Europe that supplied the navigational and military advancements enabling his conquest.
- [[christianity]] — the religion championed by his expeditions, evidenced by the friar Vicente de Valverde presenting the Bible to *Atahualpa*.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical imperial model whose expansive provincial administration and road networks were often compared to the Inca infrastructure he subdued.
- [[napoleon-bonaparte]] — a later European conqueror whose campaigns drastically reshaped global balance and sparked the independence of Spain's American colonies.
