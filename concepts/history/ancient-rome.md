# Ancient Rome

## summary

**Ancient Rome** was a civilization and empire spanning roughly 1,200 years (c. 753 BCE–476 CE), beginning as a small settlement on the Tiber River and evolving into the ancient world's most powerful military and political force. Rome's republican institutions, legal systems, engineering achievements, and territorial expansion across the Mediterranean and beyond profoundly shaped Western civilization, while its adoption of *Christianity* transformed the religion into a global force.

## you gotta know

- Rome traditionally dates its founding to 753 BCE; early Rome was a monarchy before an aristocratic revolution (509 BCE) established the *Republic*, featuring consuls, a Senate, and eventually democratic assemblies balancing power.
- The *Republic* expanded through conquest: the *Punic Wars* (264–146 BCE) destroyed *Carthage*, the Mediterranean's rival power; Rome conquered Greece (146 BCE), Egypt (30 BCE), and vast territories across Europe, Asia, and North Africa.
- The *toga* became the quintessential symbol of Roman civic identity and status; the quality and style of one's toga indicated social rank, and the right to wear it distinguished citizens from non-citizens.
- *Julius Caesar*'s conquest of *Gaul* (58–50 BCE) and subsequent civil war ended the Republic; his adopted heir *Augustus* (63 BCE–14 CE) established the *Empire*, creating a system of autocracy veiled under republican forms.
- Roman engineering transformed the ancient world: *aqueducts* transported water to cities; *roads* facilitated trade and military movement; *concrete* enabled monumental architecture (*Colosseum*, *Pantheon*); sophisticated *plumbing* served urban centers.
- The legal system—codified Roman law into principles and procedures—became the foundation of Western legal traditions; concepts like *contracts*, *property*, and *citizenship rights* originated in Roman jurisprudence.
- Rome eventually Christianized (officially in 380 CE); the *Christian Church* became institutionally intertwined with Roman authority, and as the *Empire* fragmented (476 CE), the Church preserved Roman institutional and intellectual traditions.
