## summary

**Hernan Cortes** (1485–1547) was a Spanish conquistador who led the military expedition that conquered the [[aztec-empire]] and brought vast territories in Mexico under Spanish colonial rule. Operating with limited troops but employing superior military technology, strategic alliances with indigenous peoples hostile to Aztec rule, and ruthless psychological warfare, Cortes toppled an empire spanning millions of subjects. His conquest marked one of history's most consequential military victories by a small force and set the template for Spanish colonial dominion in the Americas.

## you gotta know

- Cortes arrived in Mexico in 1519 with approximately 600 men, horses unfamiliar to indigenous peoples, and cannons—technologies that daunted Aztec forces despite their enormous numerical advantage.
- [[montezuma-ii]], the Aztec emperor, initially greeted Cortes with hospitality, uncertain whether the Spanish represented gods or mortal enemies, a hesitation Cortes exploited.
- The Tlaxcalans and other indigenous city-states allied with Cortes against Aztec rule, providing thousands of warriors and crucial intelligence about Aztec military and political vulnerabilities.
- Cortes captured Tenochtitlan in 1521 after a devastating siege, employing brigantines (small ships) to control Lake Texcoco and cutting off Aztec supply lines and escape routes.
- [[smallpox]], introduced by Spanish forces, decimated the Aztec population during the conquest, weakening resistance and tipping the demographic balance catastrophically in Spain's favor.
- Cortes remained governor of New Spain (Mexico) for several years but was eventually investigated by the Spanish crown for potential disloyalty and self-dealing; he returned to Spain where he died in relative obscurity.
