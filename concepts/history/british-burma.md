## summary

**British Burma**, historically designated as **British Rule in Burma**, was the colonial administration established over modern Myanmar by the [[british-empire]] between 1824 and 1948. Initially annexed in successive stages by the commercial and military arms of the *British East India Company*, Burma was governed as a province of British [[india]] before being separated into a distinct crown colony in 1937. The colonial era transformed the nation into the world's leading rice exporter while violently disrupting traditional institutions, leaving deeply entrenched ethnic and political fissures that persisted long into the post-independence era.

## you gotta know

- Established through three successive imperial conflicts known as the *Anglo-Burmese Wars* (1824–1826, 1852, and 1885), which progressively dismantled the indigenous *Konbaung Dynasty*.
- Concluded militarily when British forces captured the royal capital of Mandalay in November 1885, forcing *King Thibaw* and *Queen Supayalat* into lifelong exile in India and abolishing the millennium-old Burmese monarchy.
- Governed bureaucratically as a neglected sub-province of British [[india]] from 1886 until 1937, causing widespread social resentment by staffing administrative posts and civil positions with Indian civil servants and soldiers.
- Sparked severe economic dislocation in the Irrawaddy Delta through agricultural commercialization, as indigenous peasant farmers lost ancestral lands to immigrant *Chettiar* moneylenders following global rice market collapses.
- Provoked the massive rural *Saya San Rebellion* (1930–1932), in which an anti-colonial monk mobilized peasant farmers with traditional tattoos and millennial Buddhist prophecies against colonial authorities.
- Separated administratively from India under the *Government of India Act 1935* (effective 1937), which established a local legislative council but retained British imperial control over defense and foreign policy.
- Occupied by the military forces of *Japan* during [[world-war-ii]], during which the *Burma Independence Army* under *Aung San* originally collaborated with Tokyo before switching allegiance to the Allies in 1945.
- Concluded with independence in January 1948 following the historic *Panglong Agreement*, negotiated by *Aung San* before his assassination alongside key cabinet members in July 1947.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — the global imperial sovereign that ruled the territory through military conquest and administrative centralization.
- [[colonialism]] — the geopolitical doctrine of territorial domination and economic exploitation that reoriented the Burmese agrarian economy.
- [[india]] — the neighboring imperial jewel to which Burma was administratively subordinated for more than fifty years.
- [[buddhism]] — the foundational religious tradition whose monastic institutions anchored anti-colonial resistance and nationalist identity.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict that devastated the region through fighting along the Burma Road and broke British administrative hegemony.
- [[china]] — the northern neighbor linked via crucial wartime Allied supply corridors across the rugged Burmese frontier.
- [[boston-tea-party]] — an earlier anti-imperial flashpoint caused by the commercial monopolies of the *British East India Company*, the same enterprise that launched the initial conquest of Burma.
