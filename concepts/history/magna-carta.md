# Magna Carta

## summary

**Magna Carta** ("the Great Charter") is a landmark 1215 document issued by *King John* of *England* after baronial revolt, establishing the principle that the monarch is subject to the law and guaranteeing certain legal rights and due process to *English* subjects. Though limited in immediate scope and repeatedly violated, it became a foundational symbol of constitutional governance, the rule of law, and resistance to arbitrary power across the English-speaking world.

## you gotta know

- *King John* of *England* issued the charter in June 1215 after baronial barons compelled him through military pressure; the document codified limitations on royal authority and promises of legal fairness.
- The charter guaranteed basic legal rights: freedom from arbitrary imprisonment without trial, access to swift justice, and prohibition on excessive fines or seizures of property—radical concepts in medieval feudalism.
- It established that the king was not above the law and could not govern by whim; this principle, though revolutionary for its time, became foundational to Western constitutional thought.
- The original charter was annulled by Pope *Innocent III* within months, and warfare resumed; it was repeatedly reissued, modified, and reinterpreted by later monarchs, particularly by *King Henry III*.
- Medieval reality was far more limited than later myth: the charter primarily protected baronial and clerical privileges, not common people; its provisions were routinely ignored or violated by subsequent kings.
- By the 17th century, English politicians reinterpreted Magna Carta as a proto-constitutional document limiting monarchy, weaponizing it against *Stuart* absolutism and providing rhetorical foundation for parliamentary power.
- American and later democratic movements adopted Magna Carta as an origin myth for constitutional rights and due process; it appears symbolically in the U.S. Constitution and international human-rights declarations, despite the actual 1215 document being far narrower in scope.
