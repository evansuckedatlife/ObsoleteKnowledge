## summary

The catastrophic wave of **bank failures** across the [[united-states]] between 1929 and 1933 shattered the American financial system and acted as the primary driver of the [[great-depression]]. Triggered by falling agricultural commodity prices, fragile unit-banking regulations, and depositor panic following the [[stock-market-crash]] of 1929, thousands of independent commercial lenders collapsed as runs drained cash reserves from fractional-reserve vaults. Because customer deposits lacked government insurance, these insolvencies wiped out personal savings, starved businesses of commercial credit, and caused the circulating money supply to contract by roughly one-third. The systemic collapse was arrested only in March 1933 when President [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] declared a national banking holiday, inaugurating aggressive structural stabilization and regulatory oversight under the [[new-deal]].

## you gotta know

- More than 9,000 commercial banks collapsed between 1930 and 1933, wiping out billions of dollars in depositor assets and drastically accelerating nationwide deflation.
- Newly inaugurated President [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] issued Proclamation 2039 on March 6, 1933, enforcing a four-day national banking holiday that closed all financial institutions to stem panic.
- Congress swiftly passed the *Emergency Banking Relief Act* to provide liquidity and reopen solvent lenders, accompanied by Roosevelt's reassuring first *Fireside Chat* explaining the banking mechanism to the public.
- The [[glass-steagall-act]] of 1933 established long-term structural reforms by separating commercial and investment banking and creating the *Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation* (*FDIC*).
- Federal deposit insurance effectively ended systemic retail runs by guaranteeing individual customer accounts up to $2,500, which restored public faith in depository institutions.
- The collapse of the private *Bank of United States* in [[new-york-city]] in December 1930 became the largest bank failure in American history up to that date, triggering nationwide alarm because its name misled depositors into believing it was a government institution.
- Economists *Milton Friedman* and *Anna Schwartz* famously argued in their monetary history that the [[federal-reserve]] turned a typical recession into the [[great-depression]] by failing to act as a lender of last resort.
- The first wave of systemic contagion began in November 1930 with the sudden insolvency of the prominent financial conglomerate *Caldwell and Company* in Nashville, which brought down dozens of correspondent banks across Tennessee, Kentucky, and Arkansas.

## connections

- [[stock-market-crash]] — the October 1929 market crash that weakened bank asset sheets and seeded public distrust in financial institutions.
- [[franklin-d-roosevelt]] — the president who halted the runs by declaring the 1933 national banking holiday and rebuilding public confidence.
- [[new-deal]] — the administration's legislative reform agenda that restructured commercial banking and introduced federal safety nets.
- [[glass-steagall-act]] — the landmark 1933 banking law that separated commercial deposits from speculative underwriting and created the FDIC.
- [[federal-reserve]] — the central banking authority that failed to provide emergency liquidity to solvent banks facing depositor runs.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose decentralized unit-banking laws left isolated rural lenders exceptionally vulnerable to economic downturns.
- [[new-york-city]] — the financial capital where the December 1930 failure of the Bank of United States touched off nationwide panic.
