## summary

A **hoplite** (plural: **hoplites**) was the heavily armed citizen-soldier who constituted the core of military forces across [[ancient-greece]] from the Archaic through the Classical eras. Equipped at their own personal expense with a bronze-plated wooden shield, heavy spear, and body armor, these infantrymen fought shoulder-to-shoulder in the tightly compacted tactical formation known as the phalanx. The hoplite institution embodied the civic foundation of the Greek polis, forging an indissoluble bond between middle-class property ownership, local democratic rights, and collective battlefield obligation.

## you gotta know

- Fought decisively in iconic conflicts across antiquity, routing Persian invading forces at Marathon and Plataea during the Greco-Persian Wars and clashing across decades of fratricidal warfare in the Peloponnesian War.
- Took their name from the *hoplon* (more specifically known as the *aspis*), a wide, concave wooden shield faced with bronze that featured a revolutionary double-grip system consisting of a central forearm sleeve (*porpax*) and an outer rim handle (*antilabe*).
- Arrayed in the dense phalanx formation, commonly eight ranks deep, in which every warrior's shield covered his own left flank while overlapping to protect the unprotected right flank of the comrade beside him.
- Wielded as their principal offensive armament the *dory*, an eight-foot ash-wood thrusting spear fitted with an iron leaf-shaped point and a bronze butt-spike (*sauroter*) utilized for plant-staking, counterbalancing, and secondary thrusting.
- Carried as a secondary backup blade the *xiphos*, a short iron straight sword, or the forward-curving *kopis*, drawn when spears snapped during the brutal physical collision and collective forward shoving (*othismos*) of opposing battle lines.
- Donned an elaborate bronze defensive panoply that included greaves to protect the lower legs, a linen-composite *linothorax* or bronze bell cuirass, and an iconic Corinthian helmet that offered superb head protection at the cost of peripheral sight and hearing.
- Recruited predominantly from the agrarian middle tier of society (such as the Athenian *zeugitai* class), whose status as independent, freeholders furnished both the income to purchase weapons and the civic duty to defend the city.
- Eclipsed in the fourth century BCE as flexible mercenary skirmishers, the elite Sacred Band of Thebes, and the eighteen-foot sarissa pikes of the Macedonian phalanx under *Philip II* restructured Greek military doctrine.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the Mediterranean civilization whose independent city-states established the hoplite class as the foundation of citizen warfare.
- [[rome]] — the emerging Italian city-state whose early regal and early republican levies utilized hoplite equipment and phalanxes prior to developing the flexible manipular legion.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial Mediterranean power that ultimately subjugated the Hellenistic successor states that descended from Classical Greek armies.
- [[tragedy]] — the Athenian dramatic tradition authored by veterans such as *Aeschylus*, who famously arranged for his tombstone to commemorate his combat as a hoplite at Marathon rather than his dramatic plays.
- [[poetry]] — the archaic literary genre enriched by poets like *Tyrtaeus* of Sparta, who penned elegies urging hoplite battle lines to maintain unbreakable solidarity against the enemy.
