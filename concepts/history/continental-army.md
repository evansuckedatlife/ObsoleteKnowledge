# Continental Army

## summary

The **Continental Army** was the military force raised by the thirteen colonies and maintained by the Continental Congress during the American Revolution (1775–1783). Initially a ragtag militia of volunteers and irregulars, it evolved through warfare and discipline into a professional standing army capable of defeating the British Empire and establishing the independence of the United States.

## you gotta know

- Established formally by the Continental Congress in June 1775 and commanded throughout the war by *George Washington*, the Continental Army began with fewer than 15,000 men and never exceeded 30,000 at full strength—dwarfed by British and Hessian forces.
- Recruitment relied on patriotic volunteers, state militia rotations, and later impressment; soldiers were poorly equipped, paid irregularly, and faced constant supply shortages, yet maintained cohesion through seven years of war.
- The army's victories—*Trenton* (December 1776), *Saratoga* (September–October 1777), *Yorktown* (September–October 1781)—were achieved through tactical skill, strategic positioning, and sheer endurance rather than numerical superiority.
- After the winter ordeal at *Valley Forge* (1777–78) and training reforms by *Baron von Steuben*, the Continental Army emerged as a disciplined force capable of conventional warfare against European regulars.
- The army's survival depended on French military aid (after 1778), foreign volunteers, state militias, and supply networks; internal conflicts between the Congress and Washington over resources and strategy persisted throughout the war.
- At war's end, many Continental soldiers were unpaid, mutinied for back wages (especially in Pennsylvania and New Jersey), and pressured Congress to honor promised pensions—establishing a pattern of veteran discontent after American wars.
