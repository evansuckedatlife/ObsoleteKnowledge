## summary

The **Labour Party** is one of Britain's two major political parties, representing working-class interests and center-left to leftist politics. Founded in 1900 from union and socialist movements, it emerged as the second major party after winning power under *Clement Attlee* in 1945. Labour governments created the *National Health Service*, expanded the *welfare state*, and shaped modern Britain. In recent decades, figures like *Tony Blair* rebranded Labour as a centrist force, while tensions between traditional socialism and pragmatism persist.

## you gotta know

- Emerged from trade unions and socialist societies at the turn of the 20th century, gaining strength as workers were enfranchised.
- *Clement Attlee* led Labour to a landslide victory in 1945, establishing the *NHS* and the modern welfare state, fundamentally reshaping Britain.
- Won control of Parliament multiple times but often alternated with the *Conservative Party*, reflecting deeper class and ideological divisions in British society.
- *Tony Blair* modernized Labour's image in the 1990s with "New Labour," moving toward centrist economic policies while maintaining social liberal commitments.
- Faces persistent tension between its traditional left wing (advocating socialism and workers' power) and centrist leadership seeking electoral viability.
- Remains a major force in British politics, with strong support among urban working-class and younger voters, though its union base has declined.
- Labour governments expanded public ownership of key industries, established comprehensive welfare systems, and championed the extension of voting rights and social provision.
- The party's trajectory—from working-class radicalism to mainstream centrist governance—reflects broader patterns in 20th-century European social democracy and the challenges of balancing ideological origins with electoral pragmatism.
