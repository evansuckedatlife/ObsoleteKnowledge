## summary

A **nation-state** is a political unit defined by both geographic territory and a shared cultural or ethnic identity, claiming exclusive sovereignty over its inhabitants. The nation-state emerged gradually from medieval feudalism and was formalized in the *Peace of Westphalia* (1648), becoming the dominant organizing principle of global politics. It united the concepts of nation (a people with common language, culture, and history) and state (a sovereign political apparatus), replacing empires, city-states, and religious authorities as the basic unit of international relations.

## you gotta know

- Developed through the Renaissance and early modern period as monarchs centralized power and consolidated territory under a single administrative apparatus and national identity.
- Westphalian system (1648) established the principle of territorial sovereignty and non-interference, creating a framework where sovereign nation-states recognized no higher authority.
- Enabled nationalism as both a binding force within states and a source of conflict between them, as shared national identity replaced feudal loyalty or religious orthodoxy as the primary political bond.
- Facilitated state capacity for taxation, military conscription, and bureaucratic administration on an unprecedented scale, enabling nation-states to compete globally.
- Replaced religious and imperial cosmopolitanism with a system where each nation-state pursued its own interests, creating the conditions for modern balance-of-power diplomacy and international relations.
- Remains the foundational organizational principle of global politics despite challenges from supranational organizations, multinational corporations, and transnational movements.

## connections

- [[peace-of-westphalia]] — the treaty that formalized the nation-state system and established sovereign territoriality.
- [[mustafa-kemal-ataturk]] — who exemplified nation-state creation by constructing modern Turkish national identity from Ottoman imperial rubble.
- [[nationalism]] — the ideological force that binds nation-states together and motivates their competition.
- [[diplomacy]] — the practice of relations between sovereign nation-states.
- [[sovereignty]] — the principle that nation-states possess exclusive authority within their territories.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement that rationalized nation-state theory and legitimized sovereign authority.
