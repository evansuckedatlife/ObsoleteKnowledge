## summary

**Evelyn Nesbit** was an American artists' model, chorus girl, and silent film actress who became the defining "it girl" of the early twentieth century and the central figure in what contemporaries called the "Trial of the Century." Born in Pennsylvania, she achieved national fame in [[new-york-city]] posing for prominent illustrators such as *Charles Dana Gibson*, embodying the iconic *Gibson Girl* ideal. Her life became engulfed in sensational scandal in 1906 when her jealous, eccentric millionaire husband, *Harry Kendall Thaw*, publicly murdered the celebrated architect *Stanford White* over White's earlier sexual exploitation of her.

## you gotta know

- Rose to unprecedented visual celebrity in the 1900s as a teenage artists' model, photographed by *Rudolf Eickemeyer Jr.* and inspiring *Charles Dana Gibson*'s famous pen-and-ink illustration *The Eternal Question*.
- Performed as a "Florodora girl" in the Broadway musical hit *Florodora*, joining the glamorous chorus line that captivated wealthy socialites across the Gilded Age.
- Became the adolescent mistress of prominent *Beaux-Arts* architect *Stanford White*, who famously entertained her in his lavish multi-story apartment featuring a red velvet swing.
- Married the volatile Pittsburgh coal and railroad heir *Harry Kendall Thaw* in 1905, who subjected her to domestic abuse and obsessed obsessively over her prior relationship with White.
- Watched Thaw shoot White three times in the face at point-blank range on June 25, 1906, during a rooftop performance of *Mam'zelle Champagne* at *Madison Square Garden*, a building White had designed.
- Provided explosive, unprecedentedly explicit courtroom testimony during Thaw's murder trials, detailing how White had drugged and assaulted her when she was sixteen years old.
- Her testimony helped Thaw's legal team secure a verdict of not guilty by reason of temporary insanity (formulated as "dementia americana"), leading to his commitment to the *Matteawan State Hospital*.
- Later transitioned to vaudeville, wrote autobiographies including *Prodigal Days*, and was fictionalized as a pivotal character in *E. L. Doctorow*'s celebrated novel *Ragtime*.

## connections

- [[new-york-city]] — the metropolis whose theaters, architectural monuments, and yellow journalism framed her meteoric celebrity and personal tragedy.
- [[united-states]] — the nation captivated by the tabloid coverage and class tensions laid bare by the 1907 and 1908 trials.
- [[realism]] — the aesthetic and literary movement that depicted the stark social divides and predatory urban realities of her era.
- [[silent-film]] — the emerging cinema industry in which she starred in pictures such as *Threads of Fate* and *The Unpardonable Sin*.
- [[american-literature]] — the tradition that repeatedly reinterpreted her narrative as an allegory for the gilded age, notably in *Ragtime*.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the sensational narrative mode echoed in popular accounts of White's velvet swing apartment and Thaw's madness.
