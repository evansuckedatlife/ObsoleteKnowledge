## summary

The **Roosevelt Corollary** (1904) was [[theodore-roosevelt]]'s assertion that the [[united-states]] possessed the right to intervene militarily in Latin American and Caribbean nations to suppress disorder, prevent European intervention, and enforce debt collection. Formulated as an extension of the [[monroe-doctrine]], Roosevelt justified American intervention as "international police power," positioning the U.S. as regional hegemon. The corollary represented the apex of American imperialism in the hemisphere and justified repeated U.S. military occupations and interventions in Latin America throughout the 20th century, profoundly affecting U.S.–Latin American relations.

## you gotta know

- Roosevelt articulated the corollary in 1904 as a response to instability in Dominican Republic, arguing that chronic disorder invited European intervention and American intervention was thus necessary.
- The Monroe Doctrine (1823) warned Europeans against colonizing the Western Hemisphere; Roosevelt's corollary reframed it as justification for American dominance rather than European exclusion.
- Roosevelt justified intervention through the concept of "international police power": civilized nations should regulate unstable regions, with the U.S. assuming this burden in its hemisphere.
- The U.S. intervened in Panama, Nicaragua, Haiti, and the Dominican Republic under the Roosevelt Corollary framework, often establishing protectorates and occupations lasting years or decades.
- The corollary explicitly linked intervention to debt collection and financial management, positioning U.S. intervention as ensuring hemispheric financial stability and protecting American creditors.
- Latin American nations condemned the corollary as imperialism, poisoning U.S.–Latin American relations and contributing to persistent anti-American sentiment throughout the 20th century.

## connections

- [[theodore-roosevelt]] — the president who formulated and implemented the corollary as the cornerstone of his foreign policy.
- [[monroe-doctrine]] — the 1823 doctrine that Roosevelt reinterpreted as justification for active American intervention rather than passive European exclusion.
- [[american-imperialism]] — the broader policy framework in which the Roosevelt Corollary represented American expansion of power and influence.
- [[panama-canal]] — the strategic project that Roosevelt pursued partly to strengthen American control over Caribbean and Central American affairs.
- [[progressive-era]] — the period during which Roosevelt served, combining progressive domestic reform with aggressive foreign policy.
- [[latin-america]] — the region subjected to the Roosevelt Corollary's intervention regime and most affected by American hemispheric dominance.
- [[united-states]] — the nation asserting regional hegemony through the corollary framework.
