## summary

**Cyrus the Great** (c. 600–530 BCE) founded the *Achaemenid Empire*, creating the ancient world's first truly multinational state spanning from Egypt to India. Known for his military genius and administrative tolerance, he unified the Persian kingdoms and extended Achaemenid rule through conquest and negotiation. His legacy shaped the political imagination of empires for millennia.

## you gotta know

- Born into the Anshanid dynasty, Cyrus seized power from the Median overlords and unified the Persian tribes.
- Conquered the Lydian kingdom under *Croesus* (c. 546 BCE), acquiring the wealth of Asia Minor.
- Captured Babylon in 539 BCE without destruction, positioning himself as a liberator rather than a conqueror; the *Cyrus Cylinder* records his tolerant policies.
- Established the Achaemenid Empire as a vast administrative system divided into satrapies, each governed by a satrap answerable to the king.
- Allowed conquered peoples to practice their own religions and maintain local laws, an unprecedented policy of cultural pluralism.
- Was revered in Greek historical tradition as a model ruler; *Xenophon* idealized him in the *Cyropaedia*.
- Died while campaigning on the northeastern frontier, leaving an empire that would dominate the Near East for two centuries.
- Created a royal road system connecting the empire from Sardis to Susa, facilitating communication and military movement across vast distances.
- Recruited local elites and capable administrators into his government, integrating Babylonian, Egyptian, and Median traditions into a cosmopolitan court.
- His tolerance and administrative genius created an empire that survived him for two centuries, making him one of history's most successful empire-builders by the longevity of his creation.

## connections

- [[babylonian-empire]] — whose final independent rule he ended through the conquest of Babylon.

- [[zoroastrianism]] — the Persian religion he may have practiced, though evidence is disputed.
- [[persia]] — the core territory and cultural foundation of his power.
- [[xerxes-i]] — his great-grandson, who invaded Greece in the second Persian invasion.
- [[alexander-the-great]] — the conqueror whose empire ultimately destroyed the Achaemenid legacy.
