## summary

The **19th Amendment** to the US Constitution, ratified in 1920, granted women the right to vote nationwide. Adopted after decades of suffrage activism led by figures like *Susan B. Anthony* and *Elizabeth Cady Stanton*, it marked a watershed moment in American democracy and women's rights. The amendment transformed politics by instantly expanding the electorate and laying groundwork for broader gender equality.

## you gotta know

- Ratified on August 18, 1920, after the Tennessee legislature cast the deciding vote to approve it.
- Preceded by *Susan B. Anthony*'s 1872 arrest for voting illegally, which energized the movement and kept pressure on Congress.
- Some states had already granted women's suffrage before 1920, but the amendment ended the patchwork state-by-state system.
- The amendment's simple language — "The right of citizens of the United States to vote shall not be denied on account of sex" — belied decades of struggle.
- Western states led the nation in granting women voting rights; Wyoming was the first territory to do so in 1869.
- Immediately added roughly 20 million women to the voting rolls, potentially reshaping electoral outcomes.
- Opponents warned of chaos and the collapse of traditional gender roles, yet political stability endured.
