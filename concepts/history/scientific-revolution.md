## summary

The **Scientific Revolution** was the sixteenth- to eighteenth-century intellectual movement that fundamentally transformed how humans understood the natural world, replacing medieval authority with observation, experiment, and mathematical reasoning. Figures like *Copernicus*, *Galileo*, *Descartes*, and *Newton* overthrew ancient Greek certainties and the Church's cosmology, establishing the empirical method as the foundation of natural knowledge. This epoch marked the birth of modern science and laid the groundwork for the [[Enlightenment]]'s faith in reason.

## you gotta know

- *Copernicus* displaced Earth from the center of the universe, proposing a heliocentric solar system that shattered medieval cosmology.
- *Galileo* used the telescope to observe moons orbiting Jupiter, providing direct evidence against the Earth-centered model and championing observation over received doctrine.
- *René Descartes* formalized the method of radical doubt (*Cogito, ergo sum*) and mathematics as the language of nature, bridging philosophy and physics.
- *Isaac Newton* unified the heavens and Earth with his laws of motion and universal gravitation, creating a mechanistic cosmos governed by mathematics.
- The printing press enabled rapid dissemination of new findings, creating networks of scientists across Europe who challenged authority collectively.
- Experimentation replaced mere authority: knowledge claims required testable proof, not appeal to Aristotle or Church decree.
- The distinction between empiricism (knowledge from observation) and rationalism (knowledge from reason) emerged as both were needed for discovery.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual movement that built upon and extended Scientific Revolution's faith in reason and progress.
- [[renaissance]] — the earlier humanist recovery of classical texts that provided mathematical and observational tools for scientific method.
- [[descartes]] — philosopher and mathematician who unified mind and matter and established doubt as a scientific tool.
- [[galileo-galilei]] — astronomer and physicist whose telescopic observations and mechanics pioneered the empirical approach.
- [[isaac-newton]] — synthesized planetary motion and gravity into a unified mathematical system.
- [[empiricism]] — the philosophical view that sensory experience and observation are the basis of knowledge, formalized during this era.
- [[heliocentrism]] — the sun-centered model at the revolution's core, replacing Ptolemaic Earth-centrism.
