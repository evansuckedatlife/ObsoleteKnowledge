# Jimmy Carter

## summary

**Jimmy Carter** (born 1924) was the 39th *President of the United States* (1977–1981), a *Democrat* known for his focus on human rights, diplomacy, and moral principle in foreign policy, though his presidency was marked by economic stagnation, the *Iran hostage crisis*, and Cold War tensions that he struggled to manage. After leaving office, Carter became an influential elder statesman and humanitarian.

## you gotta know

- Carter was an outsider to conventional politics: a former *nuclear engineer* and *Georgia* governor who won the presidency in 1976 on a platform of integrity and reform following *Watergate* and *Vietnam*.
- The *Camp David Accords* (1978) were his greatest diplomatic triumph: he brokered peace between *Israel* and *Egypt*, bringing *Anwar Sadat* and *Menachem Begin* to a historic accord that reshaped Middle Eastern geopolitics.
- The *Iranian Revolution* (1979) and subsequent *hostage crisis* (American embassy staff held for 444 days) was a humiliation that dominated his presidency and contributed to his electoral defeat; the crisis was resolved only after *Ronald Reagan*'s inauguration.
- Carter championed *human rights* as a cornerstone of foreign policy, criticizing Soviet violations and other authoritarian regimes, earning respect from dissidents but generating friction with strategic allies.
- The *Soviet invasion of Afghanistan* (1979) and subsequent *Cold War* escalation made detente impossible; Carter imposed grain embargoes and advocated a boycott of the 1980 *Moscow Olympics* in protest.
- His domestic presidency struggled with *stagflation* (simultaneous high inflation and unemployment), energy crises (particularly oil shocks), and the *Iran* hostage crisis; he lost re-election decisively to *Reagan* in 1980.
- After leaving office, Carter pursued *Habitat for Humanity* work, *election monitoring*, and became the longest-living former president, aging into an elder statesman role.
