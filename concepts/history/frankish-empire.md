# Frankish Empire

## summary

The **Frankish Empire** was the dominant European power from the 5th to 9th centuries, rising from a Germanic tribal kingdom to a continental empire under *Charlemagne* (r. 768–814), whose vast realm stretched from modern *France* to *Germany* to parts of *Italy*. The Frankish state was the most significant successor to the *Roman Empire* in Western Europe and laid the foundation for medieval European civilization.

## you gotta know

- The Franks were a Germanic people who established kingdoms in what is now northern *France* and the *Rhine Delta* following the collapse of the *Western Roman Empire* in the 5th century.
- The *Merovingian dynasty* (5th–8th centuries) initially ruled the Frankish realm but declined in power; the *Carolingian dynasty* under *Charlemagne* displaced them and built the empire's greatest extent.
- *Charlemagne* conquered vast territories: he subdued the *Saxons* in brutal campaigns, conquered *Italy*, crowned himself emperor in 800 (reviving imperial titles in the West), and patronized the *Carolingian Renaissance* of learning.
- The empire's administrative innovations included the *missi dominici* (royal inspectors), a powerful *Catholic Church* alliance, and a centralized bureaucracy that presaged medieval governance.
- Charlemagne's son *Louis the Pious* inherited the empire but could not hold it together; the *Treaty of Verdun* (843) partitioned the empire into three kingdoms: West *Francia* (proto-France), East *Francia* (proto-Germany), and *Lothair's middle kingdom* (Italy and the Rhine).
- The fragmentation after Charlemagne's death (814) allowed the rise of feudalism, the decentralization of power, and the emergence of separate European nations, ending the project of unified Western empire.
- Frankish cultural and religious influence was profound: they Christianized Germanic peoples, preserved Roman learning through monasteries, and shaped the Latin languages and legal traditions of medieval Europe.
