## summary

**Gaiseric** (alternatively spelled **Genseric** or **Geiseric**) was the formidable king of the Vandals and Alans who reigned from 428 to 477 CE, establishing an autonomous Germanic kingdom in North Africa. A visionary commander and diplomat, he transported his entire nation across the Strait of Gibraltar, captured the imperial metropolis of Carthage, and constructed a powerful navy that dominated Mediterranean trade routes. His most famous exploit was the thorough sack of [[rome]] in 455 CE, a catastrophe that dealt a fatal economic and psychological blow to the waning Western [[roman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Led an estimated eighty thousand Vandals and Alans from southern [[spain]] across the Strait of Gibraltar into North Africa in 429 CE, exploiting political rivalries between imperial commanders.
- Seized the fortified port city of Carthage by surprise in 439 CE without a siege, making it the thriving capital of a naval empire and cutting off Rome's primary grain supply.
- Conducted the systematic sack of [[rome]] in June 455 CE following the assassination of Emperor *Valentinian III*, spending fourteen days stripping palaces, temples, and the Capitol of their wealth.
- Honored an agreement with Pope *Leo I* to refrain from indiscriminate slaughter, torture, and burning during the occupation of Rome, though his thorough looting later gave rise to the term vandalism.
- Abducted the widowed Roman empress *Licinia Eudoxia* and her two daughters to Carthage, cementing dynastic legitimacy by wedding the princess *Eudocia* to his son *Huneric*.
- Annihilated a combined armada of the Western and Eastern Roman Empires at the Battle of Cape Bon in 468 CE by unleashing explosive fireships into the anchored Roman fleet.
- Championed Arian [[christianity]] throughout his realm, exiling and persecuting Catholic and Nicene bishops while confiscating orthodox ecclesiastical estates.
- Compelled Eastern Emperor *Zeno* in 474 CE to sign a treaty of "perpetual peace," which formally recognized Vandal sovereignty over North Africa, the Balearics, Corsica, and Sardinia until the king died peacefully in 477 CE.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — crippled Western imperial governance by stripping away the North African breadbasket and destroying multiple imperial battle fleets.
- [[rome]] — captured and systematically despoiled the imperial capital, transporting its monuments, gold, and bronze statues across the sea to Carthage.
- [[christianity]] — aggressively promoted Arian doctrine and suppressed orthodox Nicene clergy across North Africa during the fifth century.
- [[spain]] — governed the Vandal confederation in southern Iberia before orchestrating their total migration to the Maghreb.
- [[italy]] — subjected Italian coastal cities and islands to relentless amphibious raids and economic blockades throughout his fifty-year reign.
- [[gaius-cassius]] — dismantled the remnants of the Mediterranean empire that had been forged centuries earlier in the bloody civil wars waged by figures like [[gaius-cassius]].
