## summary

The **Alaska Purchase** was the 1867 treaty by which the [[united-states]] acquired Russian America from the [[russian-empire]] for 7.2 million dollars, adding over 586,000 square miles of territory. Negotiated by Secretary of State *William H. Seward* and Russian envoy *Eduard de Stoeckl*, the agreement was lampooned by political detractors as **Seward's Folly** and **Seward's Icebox**. The acquisition eliminated Russian territorial claims from North America, expanded American commercial influence across the Pacific, and later yielded immense wealth through mineral and petroleum discoveries.

## you gotta know

- *William H. Seward* concluded negotiations with Russian diplomat *Eduard de Stoeckl* on March 30, 1867, agreeing to buy roughly 375 million acres for $7.2 million, or less than two cents per acre.
- Contemporary detractors and opposition newspapers mocked the acquisition as *Seward's Folly*, *Seward's Icebox*, and President *Andrew Johnson's Polar Bear Garden*, dismissing the region as a frozen and worthless wasteland.
- Tsar *Alexander II* championed the sale after the *Crimean War* emptied imperial finances and demonstrated that Russian America could not be defended against the naval strength of the rival [[british-empire]].
- The formal transfer ceremony occurred at *Sitka* on October 18, 1867, celebrated today as *Alaska Day*, where Russian troops lowered the double-headed eagle flag and American soldiers raised the Stars and Stripes.
- Senator *Charles Sumner* rallied legislative support in the Senate with an influential three-hour speech detailing the economic potential of the Pacific fisheries and timber, officially popularizing the Aleut-derived name *Alaska*.
- Payment stalled for over a year in the House of Representatives during the [[reconstruction-era]] impeachment crisis of President *Andrew Johnson*, leading *Eduard de Stoeckl* to distribute covert funds to lobby and allegedly bribe hesitant congressmen before the appropriation passed in July 1868.
- The region initially lacked civilian governance and was administered as the military *Department of Alaska*, remaining largely neglected until the late nineteenth-century Klondike and Nome gold rushes spurred formal civil organization as the *District of Alaska*.

## connections

- [[russian-empire]] — the imperial seller seeking financial recovery after the Crimean War and wishing to offload an exposed overseas colony.
- [[united-states]] — the expanding republic that gained vast territory and strategic access to the northern Pacific Ocean and Arctic trade routes.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the turbulent postwar political atmosphere whose bitter partisan battles and presidential impeachment nearly derailed Congressional funding for the treaty.
- [[british-empire]] — the neighboring imperial rival whose naval dominance in British Columbia pressured Russian leaders to sell rather than lose the colony in war.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the recently concluded conflict that delayed earlier purchase overtures and forged goodwill between Washington and St. Petersburg due to Russian diplomatic support.
- [[cold-war]] — the twentieth-century strategic conflict in which Alaska transformed into a critical radar, aviation, and naval perimeter bordering the [[soviet-union]].
- [[colonialism]] — the geopolitical pattern of imperial powers trading territory and sovereignty over Indigenous populations without consultation or consent.
