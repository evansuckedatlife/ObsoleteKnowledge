## summary

**NAFTA** (North American Free Trade Agreement) was a trade accord signed in 1992 and implemented in 1994 that eliminated tariffs and trade barriers between the United States, Canada, and Mexico. It was intended to liberalize commerce and create economic integration across North America in the post–Cold War era, embodying neoliberal free-trade ideology. NAFTA became contentious for its effects on labor, manufacturing, and sovereignty, remaining a flashpoint in political debate for three decades.

## you gotta know

- Negotiated under President George H.W. Bush, signed under President Bill Clinton, representing an explicit embrace of economic integration with Mexico despite significant domestic opposition.
- Elimination of tariffs on agricultural, manufactured, and industrial goods; creation of a trinational commission to resolve trade disputes; special protections for intellectual property and investment.
- Supporters claimed it would lower consumer prices, expand markets for American exports, and benefit all three nations through comparative advantage and economic growth.
- Critics warned it would export manufacturing jobs to Mexico's lower-wage labor market, undermine environmental and labor standards, and subordinate national sovereignty to corporate interests.
- Post-implementation analysis showed significant job displacement in U.S. manufacturing (particularly textiles and auto parts), though offsetting job creation in trade-related sectors remained debated.
- Lasted until 2020 when it was replaced by USMCA (United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement), which maintained core free-trade principles while renegotiating labor and digital-trade provisions.
