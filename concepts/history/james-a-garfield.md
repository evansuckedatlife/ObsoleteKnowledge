## summary

**James A. Garfield** was the twentieth president of the [[united-states]], serving only two hundred days in 1881 before succumbing to an assassin's bullet and severe medical mismanagement. A distinguished Union general during the [[american-civil-war]] and a long-serving Ohio congressman during the [[reconstruction-era]], he was a gifted orator and classicist who bridged bitter party factions. His untimely death shocked the American public, effectively ending the federal spoils system and catalyzing landmark civil service reform.

## you gotta know

- Shot on July 2, 1881, at the Sixth Street Station of the *Baltimore and Potomac Railroad* in Washington, D.C., by [[charles-j-guiteau]], an unstable lawyer whose delusional applications for federal consulships had been repeatedly rejected.
- Endured eighty days of agonizing physical decline, during which his physician, *Doctor Willard Bliss*, repeatedly probed the wound with unsterilized fingers and metal instruments, causing massive systemic infections that ultimately proved far more lethal than the assassin's bullet itself.
- Treated unsuccessfully with an early electromagnetic metal detector invented by *Alexander Graham Bell* to locate the lodged projectile; the device malfunctioned and yielded confusing readings because the doctors had placed him on a bed with concealed steel wire coil springs.
- Catalyzed the passage of the *Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act* of 1883 under his vice president and successor, [[chester-arthur]], transforming the federal bureaucracy by replacing political patronage and the spoils system with competitive, merit-based examinations.
- Captured the 1880 Republican presidential nomination as a compromise dark-horse candidate on the thirty-sixth ballot, having arrived at the Chicago convention merely to deliver a nomination speech for fellow Ohioan *John Sherman* before unexpectedly halting a third-term bid by [[ulysses-s-grant]].
- Achieved the rank of major general in the Union Army during the [[american-civil-war]], leading troops to victory at the Battle of *Middle Creek* and serving as chief of staff to *William Rosecrans* at the Battle of *Chickamauga*, where he rode under heavy fire across the battlefield.
- Remains the only sitting member of the [[united-states]] House of Representatives ever elected president, as well as a polymath and former college president who published an original trapezoid-based geometric proof of the *Pythagorean theorem* in an 1876 issue of the *New England Journal of Education*.

## connections

- [[charles-j-guiteau]] — the disgruntled, mentally disturbed office-seeker who shot him at a Washington railway depot.
- [[chester-arthur]] — his vice president and Stalwart faction leader who assumed the presidency and championed civil service reform in his memory.
- [[ulysses-s-grant]] — former president whose contentious third-term campaign Garfield's dark-horse convention nomination successfully blocked in 1880.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict in which he fought as a Union major general and chief of staff at the Battle of Chickamauga.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the critical postwar period in which he served as a prominent Republican leader in the House of Representatives.
- [[united-states]] — the republic he led as its twentieth president and served as an educator, general, and legislator.
- [[14th-amendment]] — landmark constitutional provision for civil rights and equal citizenship that he vigorously defended throughout his congressional career.
