## summary

The *War of 1812* was a conflict between the *United States* and *Great Britain* lasting from 1812 to 1815, ostensibly over British impressment of American sailors and trade restrictions resulting from *Napoleon*'s *Napoleonic Wars*, though deeper causes lay in American expansionism and British support for Native American resistance to American westward expansion. The war ended in a stalemate with the Treaty of Ghent, which restored pre-war borders but established American credibility as a sovereign power willing to defend its interests against a major European empire.

## you gotta know

- *President James Madison* and Democratic-Republicans in Congress, arguing that British impressment violated American sovereignty and that Britain was arming Native Americans against American settlers, pushed for war against the reluctant *President Madison* himself—a paradox of American political history.
- The immediate causes included British impressment of American sailors (the British Navy claimed the right to stop American ships and conscript alleged British subjects), British Orders in Council restricting American trade with Europe, and British support for the *Tecumseh Confederacy* resisting American expansion into Ohio and the Mississippi Valley.
- American military campaigns focused on invading *Canada* (hoping to conquer it from Britain), while British forces, preoccupied with *Napoleon* until 1815, fought defensively; neither side achieved decisive territorial victory despite years of combat.
- The USS Constitution's victory over HMS Guerriere (1812) became an iconic American naval triumph, boosting morale and establishing the fledgling American Navy as a credible fighting force; American naval victories at Lake Erie and Lake Champlain demonstrated growing American maritime prowess.
- Native Americans, including *Tecumseh* and the Creek Nation, allied with Britain, hoping to halt American westward expansion; the war's conclusion and Tecumseh's death at the Battle of the Thames (1813) cleared Native American obstacles to American territorial expansion.
- The Treaty of Ghent (December 1814) essentially restored pre-war conditions; American negotiators, lacking leverage, accepted terms that ignored impressment and trade issues, yet the war's conclusion boosted American nationalism and confidence despite tactical military shortcomings.
