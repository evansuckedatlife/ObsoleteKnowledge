## summary

**William McKinley** (1843–1901) was the 25th President of the United States, serving from 1897 until his assassination in 1901. A Republican proponent of protective tariffs and American overseas expansion, McKinley presided over the Spanish-American War (1898), which resulted in American acquisition of the Philippines, Guam, and Puerto Rico, marking the nation's emergence as an imperial power. His assassination by an anarchist, *Leon Czolgosz*, elevated Vice President *Theodore Roosevelt* to the presidency, ushering in the Progressive era.

## you gotta know

- Born in Ohio; served as a Union soldier in the Civil War and later as a congressman known for the *McKinley Tariff* (1890), which raised import duties to protect American manufacturers.
- Elected president in 1896 on a platform of economic nationalism and the protective tariff; his presidency coincided with economic recovery from the 1890s depression, earning him credit for prosperity.
- The *Spanish-American War* (1898), ostensibly fought to liberate Cuba from Spanish rule, lasted only ten weeks and resulted in Spanish cession of the Philippines, Guam, Puerto Rico, and Guam to the United States.
- McKinley's decision to retain the Philippines rather than grant them independence triggered the *Philippine-American War* (1899–1902), a brutal counterinsurgency that killed an estimated 200,000 Filipino civilians and soldiers.
- Justified American imperial expansion through the ideology of the "white man's burden" and Christian missions; the Philippines were presented as an opportunity to "civilize" and Christianize Asian peoples.
- Re-elected in 1900 with *Theodore Roosevelt* as running mate, defeating Democrat *William Jennings Bryan*'s anti-imperialist campaign.
- Assassinated on September 6, 1901, by *Leon Czolgosz*, an anarchist, while greeting the public at the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo; died eight days later.
- His death brought *Theodore Roosevelt*, at age 42, to the presidency, transforming American politics and ushering in the Progressive era's more activist executive.
