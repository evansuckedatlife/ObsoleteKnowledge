## summary

**Jefferson Davis** was a Mississippi plantation owner, U.S. Senator, and Secretary of War who became the first and only president of the [[confederate-states-of-america]]. Leading the Confederacy through the Civil War from 1861 to 1865, Davis defended the cause of Southern secession and slavery even as Confederate military resources dwindled. His rigid leadership and inability to adapt strategy contributed to the South's defeat, and he spent years in prison after the war as a symbol of the rebellion.

## you gotta know

- Born in Kentucky in 1808; grew up in Mississippi and became a wealthy plantation owner with hundreds of enslaved laborers.
- Served as U.S. Secretary of War under President Franklin Pierce (1853–1857), advocating for Southern expansion and slavery's extension.
- Elected president of the Confederate States of America in February 1861, weeks before the fall of [[fort-sumter]].
- Ordered the bombardment of Fort Sumter in April 1861, initiating the war's opening battle and the nation's bloodiest conflict.
- Centralized Confederate power and pursued aggressive war aims, clashing with generals and state leaders over strategy and resources.
- Attempted to flee after Lee's surrender in 1865; captured by Union forces and imprisoned for two years.
