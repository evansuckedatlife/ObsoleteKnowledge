## summary

The **Farmers' Alliance** was an organized agrarian economic and political movement in the late nineteenth-century [[united-states]] that mobilized agricultural producers against monopolistic railroads, merchant crop-lien systems, and deflationary monetary policy. Originating across Texas, the rural South, and the Great Plains during the aftermath of the [[reconstruction-era]], the alliance established extensive cooperative purchasing networks and advocated for robust federal intervention in banking and commodity storage. Its program and rural grassroots networks directly fed the base of the People's Party, laying the ideological and organizational foundation for the broader Populist movement.

## you gotta know

- Formed initially in Lampasas County, Texas, during the mid-1870s to address cattle rustling and land speculation before reorganizing into the *National Farmers' Alliance and Industrial Union*, commonly referred to as the Southern Alliance under the leadership of *Charles Macune*.
- Coexisted and coordinated with parallel regional organizations, including the Northern or Northwestern Alliance organized by *Milton George*, as well as the racially segregated *Colored Farmers' Alliance* founded in Texas under *Richard M. Humphrey* to represent Black tenant farmers.
- Promoted large-scale producer cooperatives, exchange warehouses, and collective purchasing stores designed to bypass regional merchants, though financial opposition from commercial banks and severe credit shortages caused most state exchanges to fail.
- Formulated the visionary *subtreasury plan*, conceived by *Charles Macune*, which urged the [[federal-government-united-states]] to construct federal warehouses where farmers could store nonperishable staple crops while drawing low-interest treasury notes worth up to eighty percent of the commodity's value.
- Drafted the 1890 *Ocala Demands* at a national convention in Florida, calling for the abolition of national banks, the unlimited coinage of silver, a graduated federal income tax, government ownership or strict regulation of transportation networks, and the direct election of senators.
- Championed radical currency expansion and flexible credit to relieve widespread rural mortgage foreclosures and deflation, developing economic grievances that later informed the regulatory framework and credit mechanisms realized by the [[federal-reserve]].
- Shifted decisively from economic association to partisan insurgency after both major parties failed to implement its platform, culminating in the 1892 Omaha convention that founded the Populist Party and adopted the Omaha Platform.

## connections

- [[federal-government-united-states]] — target of Alliance demands for federal subtreasury warehouses, railroad regulation, and direct democratic reforms.
- [[federal-reserve]] — future national banking system whose flexible currency mandate reflected earlier agrarian protests against inelastic bank credit.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — preceding era of Southern economic dislocation, debt peonage, and tenant farming systems that sparked agrarian mobilization.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — broader period of corporate consolidation, industrialization, and railroad monopolies that organized farmers fought against.
- [[united-states]] — republic whose rural agricultural economy and electoral landscape were transformed by the Alliance's populist agitation.
