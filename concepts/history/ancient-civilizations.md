## summary

**Ancient civilizations** refers to the earliest complex, urbanized, and literate human societies that arose following the Neolithic agricultural revolution. Developing primarily within fertile river valleys across the [[middle-east]], North Africa, South Asia, and East Asia, these societies transitioned humanity from agrarian tribal villages into centralized states with stratified social hierarchies, specialized labor, and monumental architecture. Their innovations—including written language, codified legal systems, astronomical observation, and regional trade routes like the nascent [[silk-road]]—laid the institutional, intellectual, and material foundations for subsequent global history.

## you gotta know

- The earliest urban centers developed in the Fertile Crescent of the [[middle-east]], where the *Sumerians* established city-states like *Uruk* and *Ur*, invented cuneiform script on clay tablets, and built monumental stepped temples called *ziggurats*.
- Along the Nile River, ancient Egyptian civilization flourished under unified dynasties symbolized by the *Narmer Palette*, producing the monumental *Pyramids of Giza*, complex *hieroglyphic* writing, and administrative records on *papyrus*.
- The *Indus Valley Civilization* across the Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra river basins demonstrated advanced urban planning at sites like *Mohenjo-daro* and *Harappa*, noted for standardized baked bricks, grid street layouts, public sewers, and the iconic *Great Bath*.
- In East Asia, the Bronze Age cradle of [[china]] emerged along the *Yellow River*, where the *Shang Dynasty* ruled from *Anyang*, pioneered piece-mold bronze casting, and recorded ancestral divinations on pyromantic *oracle bones*.
- In ancient Mesopotamia, legal administration culminated in the *Code of Hammurabi*, a Babylonian basalt stele depicting the king receiving laws from the sun god *Shamash*, famous for establishing specific retributive penalties.
- In the Anatolian highlands, the *Hittite Empire* clashed with Egypt at the chariot-heavy *Battle of Kadesh*, pioneered early iron metallurgy, and concluded the *Treaty of Kadesh*, the earliest known surviving international peace treaty.
- Mediterranean classical antiquity traced its roots to Aegean Bronze Age predecessors, notably the maritime palace economy of the *Minoan* civilization on Crete and the fortified citadels of the *Mycenaean* Greeks who wrote in *Linear B*.
- In the Americas, primary urban civil society developed independently in Peru's coastal valleys through the *Caral-Supe* (or *Norte Chico*) culture, and in Mesoamerica through the *Olmec*, known for jade carving and colossal basalt heads.

## connections

- [[middle-east]] — cradle of ancient *Mesopotamia*, where the earliest urban settlements, wheel transport, and cuneiform writing first emerged.
- [[china]] — location of the *Yellow River* valley civilization, which developed silk production, bronze casting, and continuous literary traditions.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Mediterranean civilization whose city-states built upon Aegean Bronze Age cultures to pioneer classical philosophy, drama, and direct democracy.
- [[roman-empire]] — Mediterranean superpower that synthesized Hellenistic culture, engineering, and Roman law across Western Europe, North Africa, and the Levant.
- [[silk-road]] — trans-Eurasian trade network that linked early East Asian empires with Central Asia, South Asia, and Mediterranean states.
- [[judaism]] — ancient Levantine monotheistic faith whose prophetic writings and covenantal law emerged amidst Egyptian and Babylonian imperial hegemony.
- [[buddhism]] — philosophical and religious tradition founded in the urbanizing *Magadha* region of ancient India that spread along Asian trade routes.
