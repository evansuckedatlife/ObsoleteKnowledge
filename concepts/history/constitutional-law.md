## summary

**Constitutional law** is the body of legal principles governing how governments function, the rights of citizens, and the distribution of power among branches and levels of authority. Rooted in the *Magna Carta's* assertion that even kings are bound by law, constitutional law developed as a discipline reconciling popular sovereignty with governmental stability. In democracies, courts interpret constitutions to resolve conflicts between branches, protect individual rights, and adapt foundational documents to changing times.

## you gotta know

- The *Magna Carta* (1215) established the principle that governmental authority is limited by law and subject to legal constraints, a foundational idea for all subsequent constitutional thinking.
- Written constitutions, like the *United States Constitution* (1787), attempted to codify governmental structure and individual rights in a permanent, supreme law amendable only through extraordinary process.
- Constitutional interpretation involves courts deciding what constitutional provisions mean when applied to novel circumstances; this interpretive authority makes courts powerful arbiters of governmental legitimacy.
- The doctrine of "judicial review," firmly established in *Marbury v. Madison* (1803), empowered courts to strike down unconstitutional laws and governmental actions conflicting with constitutional provisions.
- Major constitutional struggles center on federalism (central vs. state power), separation of powers (executive, legislative, judicial), and individual rights (liberty, equality, due process).
- Landmark constitutional cases like *Brown v. Board of Education* and *Loving v. Virginia* expanded fundamental rights protections through judicial interpretation rather than requiring textual constitutional amendments.
- Constitutional amendments, like the *14th Amendment*, have explicitly expanded rights and constrained governmental power; they reflect political will to revise foundational law.
- Constitutional jurisprudence varies significantly by country: *Britain* relies on parliamentary sovereignty and convention; *Germany* emphasizes human dignity; *India* balances federalism with rights; *United States* emphasizes judicial review and protection of individual rights.
