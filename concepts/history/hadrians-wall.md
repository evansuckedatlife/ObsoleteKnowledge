## summary

**Hadrian's Wall**, or **Vallum Aelium**, is a stone and turf fortification constructed across the width of northern Britain by order of the Roman emperor *Hadrian* beginning in 122 CE. Stretching roughly seventy-three modern miles from the River Tyne near the North Sea to the Solway Firth on the Irish Sea, it formed the northern frontier of the Roman province of *Britannia*. Beyond serving as a defensive bulwark against unconquered Caledonian tribes, the wall functioned as a regulated customs border and a visible demonstration of Roman imperial administrative might.

## you gotta know

- Commissioned following Emperor *Hadrian's* visit to Britain in 122 CE to "separate the Romans from the barbarians" in accordance with his empire-wide policy of fixed consolidation rather than continuous expansion.
- Spans eighty Roman miles (approximately seventy-three modern miles) across northern [[england]], utilizing local stone in the east and originally turf and timber in the west.
- Engineered with a continuous ditch to the north, regular milecastles placed at every Roman mile, intermediate turrets for surveillance, and sixteen major garrison forts such as *Vindolanda*, *Housesteads*, and *Chesters*.
- Flanked to the south by the *Vallum*, an enormous earthwork comprising a flat-bottomed ditch flanked by two parallel mounds, which delineated a restricted military zone behind the barrier.
- Temporarily evacuated and downgraded under *Hadrian's* successor, *Antoninus Pius*, who advanced the border northward to build the turf-built *Antonine Wall* across Scotland, before troops fell back to permanently reoccupy the original stone barrier.
- Sourced the famous *Vindolanda tablets*, thin wooden postcards and documents excavated nearby that preserve rare everyday correspondence, military rosters, and mundane life along the frontier.
- Designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1987 as a premier extant exemplar of Roman linear military engineering.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the sprawling Mediterranean superpower whose frontier defense strategy shifted toward fortified limits under *Hadrian*.
- [[rome]] — the imperial capital that directed legionary movements and financed civil infrastructure projects across distant provinces.
- [[england]] — the modern country whose northern topography still bears the physical ruins and stone alignments of the barrier.
- [[british-empire]] — a later global maritime empire whose Victorian antiquarians extensively surveyed, excavated, and romanticized the wall.
- [[italy]] — homeland of the legionary commanders and senatorial governors dispatched to oversee the British frontier.
- [[ancient-greece]] — intellectual civilization whose philhellenic admirer *Hadrian* cultivated artistic and architectural traditions mirrored throughout his architectural projects.
