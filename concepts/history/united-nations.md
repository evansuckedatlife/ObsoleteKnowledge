## summary

The **United Nations** is an international organization established in 1945 after World War II to maintain international peace, security, and cooperation among nations. Founded by the major Allied powers and based on the earlier League of Nations, it embodies the principle of collective security and creates a forum for diplomacy and conflict resolution. Though limited by national sovereignty and superpower rivalry, the UN has become central to international relations, peacekeeping operations, humanitarian aid, and the codification of human rights.

## you gotta know

- Established by the United Nations Charter, signed on June 26, 1945, just after Germany's defeat, becoming effective October 24, 1945, after ratification by major powers.
- Created in response to the League of Nations' failure to prevent World War II, incorporating lessons about requiring major powers' participation (particularly the U.S.) and enforcement mechanisms.
- Organized with a General Assembly (all member nations represented equally), a Security Council (five permanent members with veto power), and specialized agencies for humanitarian and technical work.
- The five permanent Security Council members—the United States, Soviet Union, Britain, France, and China—could veto military actions, reflecting but also entrenching Cold War divisions.
- Expanded from 51 founding members to nearly 200 by the late 20th century as former colonies gained independence and joined.
- Conducted peacekeeping operations in Korea, Cyprus, the Middle East, and dozens of other conflicts, with varying degrees of success.
- Became the primary institution for international legal development, including the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, conventions on genocide, refugees, and the laws of war.
