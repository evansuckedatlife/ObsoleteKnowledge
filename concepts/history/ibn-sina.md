## summary

**Ibn Sina**, known in the Western world by his Latinized name *Avicenna*, was a Persian polymath, physician, and philosopher whose work defined the Islamic Golden Age. He synthesized classical Aristotelian thought with Islamic theology and developed an encyclopedic corpus spanning medicine, metaphysics, logic, astronomy, and pharmacology. His masterworks, particularly *The Canon of Medicine* and *The Book of Healing*, served as standard reference texts in both Middle Eastern and European universities for over five centuries.

## you gotta know

- Authored *The Canon of Medicine* (*al-Qanun fi al-Tibb*), a monumental medical compendium that organized clinical pharmacology, infectious disease etiology, and systemic anatomy.
- Composed *The Book of Healing* (*Kitab al-Shifa*), a vast philosophical and scientific encyclopedia designed to heal the soul through logic, physics, mathematics, and metaphysics.
- Formulated the famous "Floating Man" (or "Flying Man") thought experiment to argue for the immateriality of the human soul and innate self-consciousness.
- Born near Bukhara in Central Asia, he achieved mastery of the Quran, mathematics, and jurisprudence at an early age before teaching himself advanced medicine.
- Reconciled Aristotelian logic and Neoplatonic metaphysics within a monotheistic theological framework, heavily influencing later Islamic scholasticism and Christian thinkers like *Thomas Aquinas*.
- Introduced the distinction between essence (*mahiyya*) and existence (*wujud*), postulating God as the Necessary Being (*Wajib al-Wujud*) whose essence entails existence.
- Traveled widely across Persian courts, serving as court physician and vizier to various Buyid and Kakuyid rulers while surviving frequent political upheaval.

- [[middle-east]] — lived and practiced under various ruling dynasties across Persia and Central Asia.
