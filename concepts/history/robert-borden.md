## summary

**Robert Borden** (later **Sir Robert Laird Borden**) was the eighth Prime Minister of Canada, governing from 1911 to 1920 through the crucible of *[[world-war-i]]*. A Conservative lawyer from Nova Scotia, he defeated *Sir Wilfrid Laurier* in the 1911 reciprocity election and subsequently mobilized more than six hundred thousand Canadians for the overseas war effort. Borden is historically renowned for transforming Canada from a dependent dominion of the *[[british-empire]]* into a self-governing nation, securing independent Canadian representation at the Paris Peace Conference and in the League of Nations.

## you gotta know

- Swept to power in the landmark 1911 federal election by opposing *Sir Wilfrid Laurier*'s proposed free-trade reciprocity agreement with the *[[united-states]]*, rallying imperialist sentiments under the slogan "No truck or trade with the Yankees."
- Enacted the sweeping *War Measures Act* in August 1914 upon the outbreak of *[[world-war-i]]*, granting the federal cabinet wide latitude to govern by decree, censor publications, and intern thousands of Ukrainian and Austro-Hungarian immigrants classified as "enemy aliens."
- Sparked the bitter *Conscription Crisis of 1917* by introducing the *Military Service Act*, pitting predominantly anglophone provinces against French-speaking Quebec, exposing political divisions dating back to the settlement founded by [[samuel-de-champlain]].
- Engineered a landslide victory in the contentious December 1917 "Khaki Election" by assembling a coalition Union Government and altering the franchise through the *Wartime Elections Act* and *Military Voters Act*, enfranchising female relatives of overseas soldiers while disenfranchising conscientious objectors and naturalized citizens from enemy lands.
- Authored Resolution IX at the 1917 Imperial War Conference, formally demanding full recognition of Canada and other self-governing dominions as autonomous nations of an imperial commonwealth with an adequate voice in foreign policy.
- Fought persistently alongside fellow dominion prime ministers at Versailles in 1919 to obtain separate Canadian signatures on the Treaty of Versailles and distinct seats in the newly founded League of Nations and International Labour Organization.
- Nationalized financially distressed railway companies, including the Canadian Northern and Grand Trunk, to create the state-owned Canadian National Railways (CNR), and instituted Canada's first federal income tax as a temporary war measure in 1917.

## connections

- [[world-war-i]] — mobilized the Canadian Expeditionary Force and directed the home front through four years of industrialized conflict.
- [[british-empire]] — leveraged wartime contributions to dismantle imperial subservience and establish dominion sovereignty within the Commonwealth.
- [[samuel-de-champlain]] — early colonizer whose French-Canadian foundational legacy flared into fierce political opposition during Borden's 1917 conscription crisis.
- [[tecumseh]] — Indigenous warrior whose British-allied defense of Upper Canada secured the territorial border that Borden later governed.
- [[united-states]] — rejected commercial reciprocity with Washington in 1911, yet later appointed Canada's first independent diplomatic representative to the American capital.
- [[france]] — dispatched hundreds of thousands of Canadian soldiers to defend French territory in celebrated battles such as Vimy Ridge and Passchendaele.
- [[germany]] — imperial adversary whose militarism and unrestricted submarine warfare prompted Borden's commitment to total imperial mobilization.
