## summary

The **Fur Trade**, particularly the **North American fur trade**, was a vast commercial network spanning the sixteenth to the nineteenth centuries centered on the hunting, processing, and global exchange of animal pelts, most notably beaver, sea otter, and deer skins. Initiated through early maritime contacts between Indigenous peoples and European fishermen, the enterprise rapidly became an imperial obsession that stimulated interior continental exploration and dictated colonial diplomacy. It transformed Native American economies, generated corporate commercial monopolies like the *Hudson's Bay Company*, and provided the commercial imperative for European expansion across North America.

## you gotta know

- Propelled primarily by European aristocratic and bourgeois demand for waterproof beaver felt hats, which dominated continental fashion until silk emerged as a cheaper alternative in the 1830s.
- Incited bitter intertribal and imperial conflicts over hunting territories and trade routes, most notably the devastating seventeenth-century Beaver Wars fought by the Iroquois Confederacy against French-allied tribes.
- Dominated across northern regions by chartered British and Scottish corporate behemoths, especially the royal *Hudson's Bay Company* (founded in 1670) and its Montreal rival, the *North West Company*, which merged after the violent Pemmican War.
- Relied in New France on independent frontiersmen known as *coureurs des bois* and licensed canoe voyagers (*voyageurs*) who intermarried with Indigenous women, giving rise to the distinct *Métis* nation.
- Catalyzed across the Pacific Northwest by the maritime sea otter trade, which drew British, American, and Russian merchant vessels to trade with the Haida, Nuu-chah-nulth, and Tlingit peoples.
- Galvanized in the American West by the *Lewis and Clark Expedition*, dispatched by President *[[thomas-jefferson]]* partly to establish trade relations with western tribes and locate overland commercial routes.
- Pioneered in the United States by *John Jacob Astor*, whose *American Fur Company* established Fort Astoria on the Columbia River and created one of America's first great corporate monopolies.
- Replaced fixed trading forts in the Rocky Mountains with the "rendezvous system" instituted by *William Henry Ashley* in the 1820s, where solitary mountain men and Native hunters gathered annually to exchange pelts for manufactured goods.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — operated as the primary economic vehicle for European imperial encroachment and territorial claims across northern North America.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — incentivized European navigators and cartographers to map North American river networks, the Great Lakes, and the Rocky Mountains.
- [[british-empire]] — derived enormous mercantile revenue from chartered enterprises such as the *Hudson's Bay Company*, which ruled vast swaths of Canada as Rupert's Land.
- [[france]] — formed the economic foundation of New France, where royal governors built diplomatic alliances with Indigenous nations to protect fur convoys.
- [[united-states]] — spurred overland migration into the Pacific Northwest and the Great Plains, creating immense private fortunes and driving native dispossession.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — instructed the Corps of Discovery to scout fur-trading opportunities and evaluate competing British operations along the Missouri and Columbia rivers.
- [[fugitive-slave-act]] — accelerated westward settlement and the formation of new territories whose admissions constantly destabilized the congressional balance of power, resulting in the Compromise of 1850 and the [[fugitive-slave-act]].
