## summary

**Frederick the Great** (1712–1786), also known as *Frederick II*, was the King of Prussia who transformed it into a major European power through military genius and enlightened absolutism. His victories in the *War of Austrian Succession* and the *Seven Years' War* established Prussia as a rival to Austria and Russia, making him one of history's most influential military and political figures.

## you gotta know

- Born in 1712, inherited a strong Prussian military and a moderately developed state at age 28, then vastly expanded both through aggressive foreign policy and administrative reform.
- Won the *War of Austrian Succession* (1740–1748), seizing the wealthy province of *Silesia* from Austria and doubling Prussia's territory and resources, establishing Prussia as a major power.
- His invasion of Silesia (1740) launched a series of wars that reshaped European power dynamics and established Prussia as a rival to Austria's traditional dominance.
- Engaged in the costly *Seven Years' War* (1756–1763) against a coalition of Austria, Russia, and Saxony; despite near-defeat when surrounded and nearly bankrupt, he secured Prussian gains and status.
- The war's end elevated Prussia to great-power status and demonstrated Frederick's strategic brilliance in surviving near-impossible odds through mobile warfare and concentration of force.
- Embodied enlightened absolutism, reforming the Prussian bureaucracy with meritocratic principles, tolerating religious minorities including Catholics and Jews, and establishing efficient administrative practices.
- Corresponded extensively with *Voltaire* and cultivated French Enlightenment ideas, presenting himself as a philosopher-king despite his ruthless militarism and autocratic rule.
- Created a small standing army (approximately 200,000 men) of unprecedented efficiency and discipline, innovating tactics and organization that influenced European warfare for generations.
- Established Prussia as an intellectual center, founding academies and inviting scholars, while pursuing aggressive territorial expansion through military conquest and diplomacy.
- Contemporary rival to *Catherine the Great* of Russia; together they dominated Eastern European politics in the late 18th century, partitioning *Poland* between them.
