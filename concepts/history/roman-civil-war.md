## summary

The **Roman Civil War**, widely known as **Caesar's Civil War**, was a transformative military and political conflict fought across [[italy]], [[ancient-greece]], Egypt, and [[spain]] between 49 and 45 BC. Sparked when [[julius-caesar]] crossed the *Rubicon* river in direct defiance of the *Senate*, the war pitted his veteran legions against the conservative senatorial faction led by [[pompey]]. Caesar's ultimate triumph dismantled the constitutional foundations of the ailing [[roman-republic]], concentrating autocratic authority in his hands as perpetual dictator and directly establishing the conditions for the rise of the [[roman-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Crossing the *Rubicon*: In January 49 BC, [[julius-caesar]] led his *Legio XIII Gemina* across the northern boundary stream into [[italy]], uttering *alea iacta est* ("the die is cast") and committing treason to start the war.
- Decisive showdown at *Pharsalus*: In 48 BC, Caesar confronted the numerically superior senatorial host commanded by [[pompey]] in [[ancient-greece]], using a hidden fourth line of infantry armed with upward-thrusting javelins to rout Pompey's aristocratic cavalry and shatter the republican army.
- Evacuation of [[rome]] and *Brundisium*: Caught completely unprepared by Caesar's lightning march down the Italian peninsula, [[pompey]] and the majority of the *Senate* abandoned the capital without a fight and evacuated across the Adriatic Sea before Caesar could blockade the harbor of *Brundisium*.
- Alexandrian War and *Cleopatra*: Following Pompey's flight and assassination in Egypt, Caesar arrived in Alexandria, became embroiled in the royal dynastic dispute, survived a perilous urban siege, and installed *Cleopatra VII* on the throne after burning the Egyptian royal fleet.
- Suppression of Pontus at *Zela*: In 47 BC, Caesar turned northward to Anatolia to crush King *Pharnaces II* of Pontus, achieving such total victory within five hours of battle that he summarized the swift campaign to the *Senate* with the iconic words *veni, vidi, vici*.
- The African campaign and *Thapsus*: Reorganized senatorial forces under *Metellus Scipio* were destroyed by Caesar at the *Battle of Thapsus* in 46 BC, prompting *Cato the Younger* to commit suicide at Utica rather than surrender.
- Climax at *Munda*: In 45 BC in southern [[spain]], Caesar narrowly defeated Pompey's surviving sons in a savage hand-to-hand battle where he had to fight on foot alongside his frontline troops.

## connections

- [[julius-caesar]] — the charismatic general and populist statesman whose military genius and refusal to surrender his proconsular command brought down the *Senate*.
- [[pompey]] — the revered military commander of the *Optimates* who initially drove Caesar into retreat at *Dyrrhachium* before being completely undone at *Pharsalus*.
- [[roman-republic]] — the five-century-old republican system that collapsed as private warlords commanding professional armies replaced institutional consensus with armed violence.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial autocratic order inaugurated when Caesar's victory and subsequent assassination paved the way for his heir *Octavian*.
- [[rome]] — the metropolitan center of ancient power that witnessed panic among the senatorial nobility as Caesar crossed the northern frontier.
- [[italy]] — the core Italian homeland that fell swiftly into Caesar's grasp without substantial resistance during his opening winter offensive.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the eastern theater where the war's decisive engagements were contested across rugged Macedonian and Thessalian terrain.
