# League of Nations

## summary

The **League of Nations** was the first international organization designed to maintain peace and resolve disputes among member states, established in 1920 after World War I as part of the Treaty of Versailles. It represented an idealistic attempt to prevent future wars through collective diplomacy and arbitration, though its effectiveness was severely undermined by the absence of major powers (notably the United States), the rise of aggressive regimes in the 1930s, and its inability to enforce its own resolutions. It ceased to exist in 1946, superseded by the United Nations.

## you gotta know

- The League was proposed by *Woodrow Wilson* as part of his *Fourteen Points* and formalized at the *Paris Peace Conference* (1919), embedded in the Treaty of Versailles; ironically, the U.S. Senate refused to ratify membership, leaving it without its most powerful potential enforcer.
- Early membership excluded or limited the defeated powers (*Germany*, *Austria-Hungary*, *Ottoman Empire*, *Bulgaria*), the *Soviet Union* (viewed as ideologically hostile), and crucially, the *United States*; this made it a club of European powers and their allies, not a truly universal body.
- The League's *Covenant* established the principle of *collective security*: member states agreed to defend any member against aggression; it also created the *Permanent Court of International Justice* and the *International Labour Organization*.
- The League failed at its most important tests: it condemned *Japan*'s invasion of *Manchuria* (1931) but imposed no meaningful sanctions; *Italy*'s invasion of *Ethiopia* (1935) was met with weak economic restrictions that did not deter; *Germany*'s rearmament, remilitarization of the *Rhineland*, and annexation of *Austria* went unchallenged as the League had no military force.
- The League's structure gave veto power to all member states in the *Council*, making enforcement of resolutions nearly impossible; any single nation could block action, paralyzing the organization during crises.
- By the late 1930s, the League was irrelevant to the major crises precipitating World War II; its final meeting occurred in 1939, and it was formally dissolved in 1946 with its assets transferred to the newly created *United Nations*.
- The League's failure became the historical lesson for the design of the *United Nations*, which gave the five major powers (*United States*, *Soviet Union*, *United Kingdom*, *France*, *China*) permanent seats with veto power on the Security Council—a recognition that a peace organization needs the muscle of great powers.
