## summary

The **B-29 Superfortress** was an American four-engine propeller-driven heavy bomber manufactured by *Boeing* that served as the decisive strategic weapon of [[world-war-ii]] and the dawn of the [[cold-war]]. Distinguished by its state-of-the-art technological innovations, the **Superfortress** featured a pressurized crew cabin, tricycle landing gear, and remote-controlled defensive turrets coordinated by analog computers. Operating predominantly across the Asia-Pacific theater from airbases secured in the Mariana Islands, it executed the relentless incendiary bombing campaign against [[japan]] and dropped the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, forever transforming aerial warfare and international geopolitics.

## you gotta know

- Delivered the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, carried out by specialized *Silverplate* variants, including the *Enola Gay* under Colonel *Paul Tibbets*, which released *Little Boy*, and *Bockscar* under Major *Charles Sweeney*, which released *Fat Man*.
- Executed the devastating low-altitude nighttime firebombing raids across Japanese population centers orchestrated by Major General *Curtis LeMay*, most destructively demonstrated during Operation *Meetinghouse* against Tokyo in March 1945, which remains the single deadliest air attack in human history.
- Operated primarily from the Mariana Islands—specifically vast airfields constructed on Saipan, Tinian, and Guam—after earlier long-range sorties flown from bases in [[china]] and British India under Operation *Matterhorn* suffered severe logistical and maintenance bottlenecks.
- Cost more than three billion dollars to develop and manufacture, making its procurement program significantly more expensive than the contemporaneous *Manhattan Project* and representing the most capital-intensive weapons development endeavor undertaken by the [[united-states]] during the conflict.
- Featured revolutionary aviation engineering that set standards for postwar aircraft, including a fully pressurized cabin linked by a crawl tunnel over the bomb bay, aerodynamic *Boeing 117* high-lift wings, and an advanced *General Electric* analog central fire-control system.
- Provided the direct blueprint for the strategic aviation branch of the [[soviet-union]] after four aircraft made emergency landings in Siberian territory in 1944, enabling engineer *Andrei Tupolev* to thoroughly dismantle, reverse-engineer, and duplicate the airframe as the *Tupolev Tu-4*.
- Flew extensive combat sorties throughout the Korean War, where dangerous encounters with swept-wing Soviet-supplied *MiG-15* jet fighters exposed the obsolescence of piston-powered bombers, prompting a shift to nighttime operations and accelerating the transition to jet aircraft.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict in which the heavy bomber conducted the strategic air offensive that crippled industrial capacity across the Pacific.
- [[japan]] — the imperial power targeted by its extensive incendiary firebombing raids and the climactic atomic strikes that ended the Pacific War.
- [[united-states]] — the nation that designed, financed, and mass-produced the aircraft to establish absolute air supremacy over long ocean distances.
- [[soviet-union]] — the rival power that impounded four diverted airframes and copied every component to construct its own nuclear-capable bomber fleet.
- [[cold-war]] — the postwar geopolitical conflict where the bomber served as the initial long-range nuclear delivery platform for *Strategic Air Command*.
- [[china]] — the theater hosting forward airfields for early combat operations against mainland Japanese positions before Marianas bases became operational.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the culmination of industrial assembly techniques, precision machining, and mass production that enabled the manufacture of thousands of complex aircraft.
