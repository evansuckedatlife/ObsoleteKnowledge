## summary

The **Carolingian Dynasty** was a Frankish noble family that ruled large parts of Western and Central Europe during the Early Middle Ages from the eighth to the tenth century. Founded informally by *Charles Martel* and elevated to royal rank by *Pepin the Short*, the dynasty reached its zenith under *Charlemagne*, who was crowned Emperor of the Romans in 800. Their reign fostered the *Carolingian Renaissance*, an intellectual and cultural revival that reshaped European administrative structures, script, and Christian scholarship.

## you gotta know

- Rose to authority as hereditary Mayors of the Palace under the fading *Merovingian* monarchs before officially deposing them in 751.
- *Charles Martel* cemented the family's military prestige by defeating an *Umayyad Caliphate* army at the Battle of Tours in 732.
- *Pepin the Short* allied closely with the papacy, securing royal legitimacy and initiating the Donation of Pepin, which established the Papal States.
- *Charlemagne* expanded Frankish hegemony across modern [[france]], [[germany]], and northern [[italy]], receiving an imperial crown from Pope Leo III on Christmas Day in 800.
- Sparked the *Carolingian Renaissance*, which promoted classical learning, standardized liturgical texts, and created *Carolingian miniscule*, a legible script that influenced modern typography.
- The 843 Treaty of Verdun partitioned the empire among the three grandsons of Charlemagne into West Francia, Middle Francia, and East Francia, laying the territorial seeds of France and Germany.
- Collapsed during the tenth century amid localized dynastic fragmentation and recurring Viking and Magyar incursions, eventually yielding power to the Capetians in the west and Ottonians in the east.

## connections

- [[charlemagne]] — the imperial monarch who brought the family to the pinnacle of continental dominion.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical universal empire whose title and traditions the dynasty sought to revive in the West.
- [[france]] — the western kingdom that emerged directly from the dissolution of West Francia.
- [[germany]] — the eastern realm forged out of East Francia under Saxon successors.
- [[christianity]] — the unifying faith and ecclesiastical hierarchy that bolstered dynastic legitimacy and school reforms.
- [[celtic-peoples]] — the ethnic groups of Brittany, Wales, and Ireland with whom the expanding Frankish state interacted and fought.
