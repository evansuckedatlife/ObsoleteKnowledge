## summary

The **Antonine Wall**, known historically as the **Vallum Antonini**, was an expansive turf and stone fortification erected across the narrow central lowlands of Scotland by the [[roman-empire]]. Commissioned around 142 CE during the reign of Emperor *Antoninus Pius* and constructed under the supervision of governor *Quintus Lollius Urbicus*, it spanned approximately thirty-seven miles between the Firth of Forth and the Firth of Clyde. Representing the northernmost fortified frontier ever established by Roman legions in the province of *Britannia*, the wall was designed to pacify newly subjugated territory and suppress hostile Caledonian tribes. Although occupied for barely two decades before military garrisons retreated south, it remains an extraordinary testament to Roman strategic engineering, imperial frontier administration, and frontier warfare in northern [[england]] and Scotland.

## you gotta know

- Established by the [[roman-empire]] across the narrow neck of central Scotland to replace *Hadrian's Wall* as the northernmost fortified boundary of imperial rule in the province of *Britannia*.
- Commissioned by Emperor *Antoninus Pius* in 142 CE and overseen on the ground by the imperial governor *Quintus Lollius Urbicus*, who led extensive military campaigns against local tribes.
- Built using a layered superstructure of cut turf sods resting on a stone foundation roughly fourteen feet wide, flanked to the north by an enormous V-shaped ditch up to forty feet wide and twelve feet deep.
- Interspersed with sixteen major forts and several smaller fortlets, linked continuously along the southern flank by the *Military Way*, an engineered gravel road enabling rapid troop redeployment.
- Famous for its surviving *distance slabs*, highly decorated sandstone markers like the *Bridgeness Slab* that recorded exact segments of wall completed by Roman legions such as *Legio II Augusta* and *Legio XX Valeria Victrix*.
- Suffered from persistent insurgent attacks by indigenous northern tribes, leading Roman commanders to construct secondary defensive pits containing sharpened wooden stakes called *cippi* or lilia.
- Abandoned around 162 CE when imperial forces withdrew southward back to the more defensible *Hadrian's Wall*, reflecting Roman strategic retrenchment under Emperor *Marcus Aurelius*.
- Reoccupied briefly in 208 CE under the direct command of Emperor *Septimius Severus*, who repaired sections of the ramparts during his brutal northern campaigns before the frontier was permanently evacuated.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean superpower whose military frontier reached its absolute northwestern geographic limit along this fortification line.
- [[rome]] — the imperial seat of government whose strategic administrative choices dictated the commissioning, construction, and eventual abandonment of outer provincial garrisons.
- [[england]] — the core territory of Roman *Britannia* where southern legionary bases at York and Chester organized supply networks and reinforcements for the Scottish wall.
- [[hadrians-wall]] — the preceding stone barrier roughly one hundred miles south, which was temporarily evacuated in favor of the Antonine line before serving as the permanent border.
- [[italy]] — the imperial homeland where senatorial records and triumphal arches documented northern victories, including those credited to Emperor *Antoninus Pius*.
- [[british-empire]] — the modern state whose imperial antiquarians, topographical surveyors, and archaeologists extensively mapped, excavated, and published the Roman remains.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural source for classical Mediterranean military engineering traditions and geographical writings that informed Roman conquest strategies in western Europe.
