## summary

**States' rights** refers to the American political and constitutional doctrine holding that regional state governments retain sovereign political powers not expressly delegated to the federal government by the [[united-states-constitution]]. Rooted in the *Tenth Amendment* and championed early on by *James Madison* and [[thomas-jefferson]] in the *Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions*, the doctrine served as the primary counterbalancing principle limiting federal authority. Throughout nineteenth-century American history, Southern political leaders invoked state sovereignty to protect the institution of slavery, culminating in secession and the outbreak of the [[american-civil-war]]. The doctrine was subsequently revived during the [[reconstruction-era]] and the twentieth-century [[civil-rights-movement]], where Southern officials used it to justify defiance of federal court orders, integration mandates, and civil rights legislation.

## you gotta know

- Invoked by antebellum Southern statesmen, including *John C. Calhoun* and *Jefferson Davis*, to justify the preservation of chattel slavery and articulate a legal constitutional framework for secession that precipitated the [[american-civil-war]].
- Resurfaced in the mid-twentieth century as an ideological banner under the doctrine of *massive resistance*, deployed by Southern governors and legislators to obstruct racial desegregation and civil rights statutes throughout the [[civil-rights-movement]].
- Served as the core platform of the conservative *Dixiecrat* revolt in the 1948 presidential election, when *Strom Thurmond* ran as the nominee of the *States' Rights Democratic Party* to oppose federal anti-lynching and desegregation initiatives.
- Anchored textually in the *Tenth Amendment*, which reserves all sovereign powers not explicitly delegated to the national government, nor prohibited to the individual states, to the states or to the people.
- Formulated systematically in the 1798 *Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions* by *James Madison* and [[thomas-jefferson]], which protested the federal *Alien and Sedition Acts* and introduced the influential compact theory of the constitutional union.
- Precipitated the 1832 *Nullification Crisis*, during which *South Carolina* passed an ordinance declaring federal protective tariffs unconstitutional and unenforceable, prompting President *Andrew Jackson* to threaten military enforcement via the *Force Bill*.
- Transformed dramatically during the [[reconstruction-era]] by the ratification of the [[14th-amendment]], which established federal primacy by prohibiting states from denying any citizen due process, equal protection, or fundamental privileges and immunities.
- Curtailed over time by expansive judicial readings of the *Commerce Clause* and the *Necessary and Proper Clause*, beginning with Chief Justice *John Marshall*'s landmark decision in *McCulloch v. Maryland*, which affirmed the supremacy of national law over conflicting state legislation.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — establishes the American system of dual federalism and contains the *Tenth Amendment* reservation of unenumerated powers to the states.
- [[american-civil-war]] — erupted when eleven Southern states asserted their sovereign constitutional right to secede from the Union to preserve slavery.
- [[thomas-jefferson]] — drafted the 1798 *Kentucky Resolutions*, establishing the intellectual cornerstone for compact theory and state-level nullification of national statutes.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — confronted systematic resistance from segregationist governors who cited state sovereignty to oppose federal desegregation and voting rights enforcement.
- [[14th-amendment]] — established federal constitutional supremacy over state legislation by guaranteeing national citizenship, due process, and equal protection under the law.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — imposed direct federal governance, military districts, and constitutional preconditions upon former *Confederate* states, overriding local legal autonomy.
- [[united-states]] — the federal republic whose constitutional jurisprudence and domestic politics remain defined by ongoing tensions between national power and local sovereignty.
