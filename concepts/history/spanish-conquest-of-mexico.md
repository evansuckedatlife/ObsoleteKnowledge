## summary

The **Spanish Conquest of Mexico** (1519–1521) was the military campaign by *Hernán Cortés* and a relatively small force of Spanish conquistadors that overthrew the *Aztec Empire* and established Spanish colonial rule over Mexico. What began as a daring assault on one of the world's greatest cities devolved into a brutal siege as the Spanish exploited indigenous divisions, superior firearms, and—catastrophically—epidemic disease to topple an empire of millions. The conquest fundamentally reshaped the Americas, initiating three centuries of Spanish colonial dominion and triggering demographic catastrophe through introduced pathogens.

## you gotta know

- *Hernán Cortés* led a force of only about 600 Spanish soldiers, supplemented by thousands of indigenous allies, against the Aztec capital of *Tenochtitlan*.
- *Montezuma II*, the Aztec emperor, initially welcomed the Spanish, possibly viewing them as gods or supernatural beings, but conflict erupted as Spanish greed for gold became apparent.
- Cortés exploited existing resentment of Aztec imperial rule among subject peoples, recruiting indigenous warriors who resented tribute demands and sacrificial practices.
- Spanish gunpowder weapons and steel armor provided tactical advantage, though their impact was magnified by indigenous unfamiliarity with such technology.
- *Smallpox*, inadvertently introduced by the Spanish, devastated the Aztec population; the epidemic killed roughly half of the city's inhabitants during the siege, collapsing resistance.
- The siege of *Tenochtitlan* lasted nearly three months; the Spanish systematically destroyed the city while fighting through the aqueducts and causeways.
- The fall of the Aztec Empire in 1521 marked the beginning of the Spanish colonial era in Mexico, which would last until 1810.
