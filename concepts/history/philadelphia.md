## summary

**Philadelphia** was the largest and most influential city in colonial America and the crucible of the American Revolution. Founded by William Penn as a Quaker refuge in 1682, it became a thriving port and center of commerce by the 18th century. The city hosted the [[Continental Congress]], the Constitutional Convention, and George Washington's military headquarters, making it the birthplace of American democracy and independence.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1682 by William Penn as a Quaker colony based on religious tolerance and democratic principles; its name means "brotherly love" in ancient Greek.
- Grew rapidly due to its strategic position on the [[Delaware River]], becoming the second-largest British city in North America by 1776, rivaled only by London itself.
- Home to [[Benjamin Franklin]], who established a printing press, influential newspaper, and intellectual society (the Junto) that made Philadelphia a vibrant center of Enlightenment thought and scientific advancement.
- Hosted the [[Continental Congress]] (1774–1789), where delegates from the thirteen colonies gathered to coordinate revolutionary action and drafted the Declaration of Independence in summer 1776.
- Lost to British forces after the [[Battle of Brandywine]] (1777) and subsequently occupied by the British for much of the war, yet remained symbolic of revolutionary spirit and resistance.
- Served as the capital of the United States (1790–1800) after the war, hosting both Congress and President George Washington, making it the nation's first permanent capital.
- Became the site of the Constitutional Convention (1787), where delegates from the states drafted and debated the U.S. Constitution that remains the nation's founding legal document.
