## summary

**Belgian Congo** was the formal colonial state established in 1885 as the personal possession of King *Leopold II*, then administered by Belgium from 1908 until independence in 1960. The colony encompassed the vast Congo River basin in central Africa, one of the world's richest repositories of natural resources including rubber, copper, and diamonds. It became one of Africa's most exploitative colonial regimes and ultimately a flashpoint of Cold War decolonization.

## you gotta know

- Originated as the Congo Free State, King Leopold's personal fiefdom, where forced labor and violence extracted rubber for export at enormous human cost—estimates range from 1 to 10 million deaths.
- Belgium assumed direct control in 1908, nominally ending the worst abuses, but exploitation and racial segregation remained systemic throughout the colonial period.
- The colony was administered with rigid paternalism and little investment in education or native political participation, leaving it profoundly unprepared for independence.
- *Patrice Lumumba*, the first Prime Minister after independence (June 1960), symbolized nationalist hopes but was assassinated within months during the Congo Crisis, plunging the country into civil war.
- The Congo Crisis (1960–1965) drew international intervention, Cold War competition, and the emergence of *Mobutu Sese Seko*, who seized power and renamed the country Zaire.
- Belgian Congo's chaotic decolonization set a pattern for post-colonial instability and remains central to understanding African independence and Cold War geopolitics.
