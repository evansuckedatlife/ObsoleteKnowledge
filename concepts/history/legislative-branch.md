## summary

The **legislative branch** of the *United States* government, formally known as *Congress*, is the federal authority vested with the power to make laws. Established by Article I of the *Constitution*, Congress comprises the *House of Representatives* and the *Senate* and serves as the primary vehicle through which the American people, through their elected representatives, exercise sovereign power. The legislative branch embodies the principle of *separation of powers*, checking the executive and judicial branches while being checked by them in turn.

## you gotta know

- **Congress** consists of two chambers: the *House of Representatives* (435 voting members apportioned by population) and the *Senate* (100 members, two from each state); this bicameral structure was a compromise between small and large states at the Constitutional Convention.
- The legislative branch holds the "power of the purse"—authority to levy taxes, appropriate funds, and authorize government spending; this financial control is the legislature's most potent check on executive power.
- **Congress** possesses exclusive power to enact laws affecting interstate commerce, regulate currency, establish federal courts, declare war, raise armies, and regulate naturalization; these powers define the federal government's scope.
- The *House of Representatives* initiates revenue bills (bills raising taxes), reflecting the Framers' belief that this chamber, with its shorter terms and larger, more numerous membership, best represents popular will on fiscal matters.
- Members of the House serve two-year terms and stand for election frequently, making them responsive to current public opinion; Senators serve six-year staggered terms, insulating the Senate from sudden electoral swings.
- The legislative process requires bills to pass both chambers and be signed by the President; Congress may override a presidential veto with a two-thirds supermajority in both chambers, demonstrating legislative independence.
- The legislature cannot unilaterally amend the Constitution; such changes require proposal by two-thirds of both chambers or by a constitutional convention, then ratification by three-fourths of state legislatures, a deliberately difficult threshold.
- Congress operates through a committee system where detailed legislative work occurs; specialization and compromise happen in committee, allowing the full chambers to vote on negotiated final versions.
