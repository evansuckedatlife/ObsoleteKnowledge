## summary

**Humanisme**, or **Renaissance Humanism**, was the transformative intellectual, literary, and pedagogical movement that emerged in fourteenth-century Italy and revolutionized European thought through the passionate revival of classical antiquity. Built upon the curriculum of the **Studia Humanitatis**—encompassing grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry, and moral philosophy—it rejected the abstract scholasticism of medieval universities in favor of human agency, eloquent self-expression, and civic engagement. By unearthing neglected classical manuscripts and applying critical philology, this intellectual program established the foundational tenets of modern liberal arts, historical criticism, and secular civic philosophy.

## you gotta know

- Pioneered in fourteenth-century Italy by the Tuscan scholar and lyric poet *Petrarch*, traditionally celebrated as the father of humanism for his tireless searches across monastic libraries to recover forgotten Latin texts, including the lost correspondence of *Cicero*.
- Institutionalized through the educational curriculum known as the **Studia Humanitatis**, which prioritized secular eloquence, ethical behavior, and historical discernment over scholastic Aristotelian logic and speculative theology.
- Benefited from the financial patronage and political protection of wealthy merchant oligarchies and ruling dynasties, most prominently the *Medici family* in Renaissance *Florence*.
- Fostered the civic humanism championed by Florentine chancellors like *Leonardo Bruni* and *Coluccio Salutati*, who argued that classical erudition must be applied directly to active political governance and the defense of republican liberty.
- Articulated its quintessential philosophical manifesto in *Giovanni Pico della Mirandola*'s 1486 oration *On the Dignity of Man*, which celebrated humanity's divine gift of free will to shape its own place in the cosmic hierarchy.
- Revolutionized historical scholarship through the philological methods of *Lorenzo Valla*, whose critical linguistic scrutiny demonstrated that the *Donation of Constantine* was an eighth-century clerical forgery rather than an authentic imperial charter.
- Accelerated dramatically following the fall of Constantinople in 1453, which prompted an influx of Byzantine scholars who brought crucial manuscripts of *Plato* and other Hellenic thinkers to Western Europe.
- Expanded beyond the Alps into Northern Europe, evolving into Christian humanism through figures like *Desiderius Erasmus* and *Thomas More*, who utilized classical textual criticism to reform scriptural studies and satirize ecclesiastical abuses.

## connections

- [[renaissance]] — the broad cultural, artistic, and scientific revitalization of Western civilization that was intellectually driven by this classical resurgence.
- [[italy]] — the urbanized peninsula whose direct architectural ruins, commercial wealth, and political rivalries sparked the initial recovery of classical learning.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the ancient civilization whose original philosophical doctrines, dramatic literature, and linguistic heritage were systematically reclaimed by humanist scholars.
- [[rome]] — the classical civilization whose Latin prose, republican history, and rhetorical models provided the primary standard of excellence for humanist education.
- [[petrarch]] — the pioneering scholar whose discovery of classical manuscripts and celebration of literary selfhood inaugurated the movement.
- [[florence]] — the Tuscan republic that served as the preeminent center of early humanist scholarship, civic philosophy, and artistic patron networks.
- [[medici-family]] — the preeminent banking house whose generous cultural investments founded the Laurentian Library and supported humanist scholars.
