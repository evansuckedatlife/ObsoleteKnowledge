## summary

The **Reconstruction Acts**, also known as the **Military Reconstruction Acts** or the **Reconstruction Act of 1867**, were four pivotal statutes passed by the 40th *United States Congress* between March 1867 and March 1868 that outlined the terms for readmitting ten unreconstructed Confederate states into the Union during the [[reconstruction-era]]. Spearheaded by the [[radical-republicans]] over the vehement vetoes of President [[andrew-johnson]], the legislation declared existing Southern civil regimes provisional and divided the region into five military districts commanded by federal army officers. The statutes required each state to draft a new constitution enfranchising Black male citizens, ensure ratification by registered voters, and ratify the [[14th-amendment]] as non-negotiable prerequisites for regaining representation in Congress.

## you gotta know

- Divided ten former Confederate states into five military districts headed by Union Army officers—including *Philip Sheridan* and *Daniel Sickles*—who possessed sweeping authority to suppress civil disorder, remove hostile officials, and protect freedpeople.
- Required unreconstructed Southern states to convene constitutional conventions, extend universal suffrage to adult Black men, and formally ratify the [[14th-amendment]] prior to readmission to the Union and congressional seating.
- Enacted over the repeated executive vetoes of President [[andrew-johnson]], whose insistence that Southern states had never legally left the Union and already possessed legitimate civil governments cemented his breach with Congress.
- Exempted Tennessee entirely from military rule, as the state had already ratified the Fourteenth Amendment in July 1866 and had its congressional delegation seated prior to the acts' passage.
- Expanded through three subsequent supplementary statutes in 1867 and 1868 to manage administrative impasses, empowering military commanders to initiate voter registration and purge uncooperative civilian officeholders.
- Neutralized an organized white conservative electoral boycott in Alabama through the Fourth Reconstruction Act, which amended the rules so that proposed constitutions could be ratified by a simple majority of votes cast rather than a majority of registered voters.
- Triggered a major constitutional confrontation in *Ex parte McCardle* (1869), causing the Republican-led Congress to revoke the Supreme Court's appellate jurisdiction under the 1867 Habeas Corpus Act to prevent conservative justices from reviewing the statutes' constitutionality.
- Passed alongside the *Command of the Army Act* and the *Tenure of Office Act* to prevent presidential interference with military commanders, establishing the statutory conflict that culminated in the [[impeachment-of-andrew-johnson]].

## connections

- [[reconstruction-era]] — the broader postwar historical era defined by federal efforts to reintegrate the South and protect freedpeople.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the constitutional guarantee of birthright citizenship and equal protection whose state-level ratification was mandated by the statutes.
- [[andrew-johnson]] — the seventeenth president whose obstinate resistance to congressional reconstruction policies and vetoes of these statutes led to his political isolation.
- [[radical-republicans]] — the congressional faction led by *Thaddeus Stevens* and *Charles Sumner* that formulated and passed the legislative package.
- [[impeachment-of-andrew-johnson]] — the historic impeachment trial provoked when the president dismissed Secretary of War *Edwin Stanton* over the enforcement of military reconstruction.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the armed conflict that abolished slavery but left open the constitutional terms under which rebellious states would be restored to the Union.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational document expanded by postwar civil rights amendments and tested by military jurisdiction.
