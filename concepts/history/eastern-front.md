## summary

The **Eastern Front** was the primary theater of *World War II*, where Nazi Germany and its allies clashed with the Soviet Union from 1941 to 1945. It witnessed the war's most brutal combat, technological innovation, and strategic turning points, ultimately claiming over 26 million lives. The front's outcome reshaped European geopolitics and sealed Germany's defeat.

## you gotta know

- Opened by *Operation Barbarossa*, Germany's massive 1941 invasion—the largest military operation in history in terms of scale and casualties.
- The *Siege of Stalingrad* (1942–1943) became the symbolic and strategic pivot: the Soviet Union's costly but decisive victory halted German eastward expansion.
- The *Battle of Kursk* (1943) was the largest tank engagement in history; the Soviet victory there ended German hopes to regain initiative in the east.
- The Soviets employed "scorched earth" tactics, destroying infrastructure as they retreated to deny resources to the advancing Wehrmacht.
- Soviet General *Georgy Zhukov* emerged as a mastermind of coordinated large-scale operations, enabling the eventual Soviet offensives westward.
- The war on the Eastern Front produced technological leaps in tank design, combined-arms tactics, and logistics that influenced military doctrine for decades.
- Soviet casualties dwarfed those elsewhere; the Red Army suffered over 10 million dead but proved it could outlast and eventually overwhelm German forces.
