## summary

The **federal government of the United States** is the national governing authority of the American constitutional republic, established in 1789 under the supreme framework of the [[united-states-constitution]]. Composed of three distinct branches—legislative, executive, and judicial—operating under institutional checks and balances, it shares sovereign authority with individual state governments in a federalist system. From its foundational administration under *George Washington* through civil conflict and modern regulatory expansion, the national government has continually broadened its sovereign authority over domestic welfare, foreign affairs, and economic policy.

## you gotta know

- Formulated at the 1787 Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia to replace the ineffective *Articles of Confederation*, creating a national government with direct powers of taxation, commerce regulation, and military defense.
- Inaugurated in 1789 with *George Washington* serving as the first president, establishing executive precedents, initial cabinet departments, and the federal judiciary under the *Judiciary Act of 1789*.
- Structured according to the doctrine of separation of powers, distributing constitutional responsibilities across the bicameral Congress, the unitary Presidency, and the Supreme Court.
- Affirmed federal legal supremacy and expansive implied powers in landmark early Supreme Court decisions by Chief Justice *John Marshall*, notably *McCulloch v. Maryland*.
- Radically reshaped during the [[american-civil-war]] and [[reconstruction-era]], as the Union victory and the [[14th-amendment]] subordinated state sovereignty to federal constitutional guarantees of due process and equal protection.
- Extended its administrative reach across the national economy through the broad interpretation of the [[federal-commerce-power]], creating regulatory bodies to govern interstate industry and transport.
- Established central monetary institutions, most notably chartering the [[federal-reserve]] in 1913 to manage national currency elasticity, banking stability, and monetary policy.
- Responded to populist pressures from organized civic and rural movements, including the [[farmers-alliance]], which pressured federal officials for graduated income taxes, direct senator elections, and antitrust measures.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — foundational legal document that establishes, defines, and limits the three branches of the national government.
- [[george-washington]] — revolutionary commander who presided over the constitutional convention and defined the initial office of the federal presidency.
- [[federal-commerce-power]] — primary constitutional clause utilized by Congress and the federal courts to justify extensive economic legislation.
- [[federal-reserve]] — central banking authority created by federal statute to regulate national monetary policy and financial institutions.
- [[farmers-alliance]] — nineteenth-century agrarian coalition that successfully pressured the national government for progressive regulatory and democratic reforms.
- [[14th-amendment]] — landmark Reconstruction amendment that empowered federal courts and Congress to enforce civil rights against state violation.
- [[american-civil-war]] — existential armed conflict that preserved the federal union and permanently established federal supremacy over state secession.
