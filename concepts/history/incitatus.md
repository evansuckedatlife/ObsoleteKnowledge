## summary

**Incitatus** was the favorite racehorse of the Roman emperor *Caligula*, who governed the [[roman-empire]] between 37 and 41 AD. Chronicled by ancient biographers including *Suetonius* and *Cassius Dio*, the steed was showered with extravagant imperial honors, including an ivory manger, a marble stable, purple blankets, and an entourage of domestic servants. While traditionally cited as dramatic proof of the emperor's descent into erratic madness, modern historians often interpret the episode as an intentional, cutting [[satire]] engineered by *Caligula* to mock the subservience and sycophancy of the Roman Senate.

## you gotta know

- Named from the Latin adjective meaning "swift" or "spurred on", having previously competed under the name *Porcellus* ("little pig") for the Green faction (*Prasina*) in the chariot races of the Circus Maximus.
- Provided with an elaborate royal lifestyle described by *Suetonius* in *The Twelve Caesars*, which included a stall carved entirely of marble, a manger fashioned from elephant ivory, purple blankets, and a jeweled collar.
- Protected on the night preceding major races by a cordon of Roman soldiers, who patrolled the surrounding neighborhood to enforce absolute silence so that the animal would not suffer broken rest.
- Endowed with his own private palace complete with a full staff of domestic slaves and luxury furniture, intended to entertain guests invited in the horse's personal name.
- Alleged by ancient sources to have been prospective candidate for the Roman consulship, the most prestigious magistracy in the empire, an intention *Caligula* supposedly voiced before his assassination.
- Served banquet meals consisting of high-grade barley mixed with gold leaf, alongside vintage wine poured in heavy golden chalices to drink toasts to the emperor's health.
- Represented by revisionist historians not as the delusion of a deranged tyrant, but as a deliberate insult signaling to the elite senators that their official political duties were so trivial that an animal could discharge them equally well.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — the autocratic imperial realm whose Julio-Claudian administration was dominated by *Caligula's* turbulent four-year reign.
- [[rome]] — the ancient capital city where the imperial palace and the Circus Maximus hosted grand spectacles of equestrian competition.
- [[tiberius]] — the dour grand-uncle whose death at Capreae elevated the young *Caligula* to supreme imperial leadership.
- [[claudius]] — the scholarly uncle chosen by the Praetorian Guard to rule after *Caligula* was murdered by conspirators in an underground palace corridor.
- [[satire]] — the comedic and political weapon that modern scholars believe was Caligula's true motivation when mocking his senatorial courtiers.
