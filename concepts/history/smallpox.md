## summary

**Smallpox**, caused by the **Variola** virus, was a lethal and disfiguring infectious disease characterized by severe fevers and distinctive maculopapular skin eruptions that plagued human populations worldwide for thousands of years. Carried across oceans during the [[age-of-exploration]], the pathogen wiped out immense portions of indigenous populations in the Americas, fundamentally shaping the course of European [[colonialism]]. The disease served as the crucible for modern immunology when *Edward Jenner* developed the world's first successful vaccine in 1796. A historic international public health campaign culminating during the [[cold-war]] achieved complete global eradication in 1980, making it humanity's single greatest triumph over infectious illness.

## you gotta know

- Global eradication was officially certified by the *World Health Organization* in 1980 following a relentless ring-vaccination strategy, marking the first time human collective action eradicated an infectious disease from the planet.
- English physician *Edward Jenner* developed the first smallpox vaccine in 1796 after noting milkmaids exposed to milder *cowpox* were immune, famously testing his hypothesis by inoculating eight-year-old *James Phipps*.
- During the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire, an outbreak killed Emperor *Cuitláhuac* and decimated the defenders of *Tenochtitlan*, devastating indigenous societies and shifting the balance of power decisively toward [[spain]].
- Centuries prior to Jennerian vaccination, healers throughout [[china]], the [[ottoman-empire]], and West Africa practiced *variolation*, deliberately transferring infectious scabs or pustular fluid into healthy individuals to stimulate protective immunity.
- General [[george-washington]] mandated compulsory variolation for Continental Army soldiers wintering at Valley Forge during the American Revolutionary War, preventing catastrophic troop losses from rampant military epidemics.
- The virus belongs to the genus *Orthopoxvirus* and manifested in two primary clinical forms: virulent *Variola major*, which carried a devastating thirty percent fatality rate, and the milder *Variola minor*.
- Official stocks of live virus are restricted to two heavily secured high-containment laboratories: the *CDC* in the [[united-states]] and the *VECTOR Institute* in Koltsovo, inherited from the [[soviet-union]].
- Physical records of the pathogen reach deep into antiquity, evidenced by characteristic pustular lesions preserved upon the face and limbs of the mummified Egyptian pharaoh *Ramesses V*.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — decimated vulnerable indigenous populations across the Americas and Pacific, shattering native political structures and clearing demographic space for European imperial expansion.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — maritime trade networks and exploratory voyages unintentionally introduced virulent Eurasian pathogens to isolated, immunologically naive civilizations around the globe.
- [[spain]] — military forces under *Hernán Cortés* exploited the societal chaos caused by smallpox epidemics that paralyzed Aztec and Inca leadership.
- [[george-washington]] — protected the Continental Army from disastrous military outbreaks through a far-sighted decision to mandate army-wide inoculation.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical rivals set aside diplomatic hostilities to supply millions of vaccine doses and specialized bifurcated needles for global eradication.
- [[enlightenment]] — empirical scientific inquiry and controlled clinical experimentation in [[england]] catalyzed the shift from hazardous variolation to safe Jennerian *cowpox* vaccination.
- [[dna]] — genomic sequencing of viral fragments extracted from ancient archaeological mummies and historical medical collections has mapped the evolutionary origins of orthopoxviruses.
