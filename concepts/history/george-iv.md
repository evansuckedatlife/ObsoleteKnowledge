## summary

**George IV** was King of the United Kingdom from 1820 to 1830, having previously served as **Prince Regent** during his father *George III's* mental incapacity. A man of refined cultural taste but volatile temperament and questionable judgment, his reign was marked by political instability, Catholic emancipation struggles, and scandal surrounding his troubled marriage to *Caroline of Brunswick*.

## you gotta know

- Served as **Prince Regent** from 1811 to 1820 during his father *George III's* descent into madness, giving his name to the elegant "Regency era" of British culture and architecture.
- The Regency period (1811–1820) saw a flowering of Romantic literature, neoclassical design, and fashionable society centered on the prince's patronage of the arts.
- Became king at age 57 but faced immediate scandal when he attempted to divorce his estranged queen, *Caroline*, leading to her public trial on charges of adultery and eventual death in 1821.
- The Caroline scandal deeply damaged his public reputation; London crowds supported Caroline and jeered the king, seeing the divorce as a betrayal of a long-suffering queen.
- Profoundly influenced by his relationship with *Maria Fitzherbert*, a Catholic widow whom he married secretly in 1785 (before his formal marriage to Caroline).
- His relationship with Fitzherbert created constitutional and religious complications for an Anglican monarch; it challenged the Royal Marriage Act and established church supremacy.
- As king, faced the lingering challenge of Catholic emancipation, which he initially opposed (fearing loss of Protestant dominance) before reluctantly supporting *Wellington's* Catholic Relief Bill of 1829.
- The Catholic emancipation compromise cost him the loyalty of his Tory supporters but was necessary to prevent widespread sectarian violence in Ireland.
- Commissioned major construction projects including the renovation of *Windsor Castle* and development of Regent Street in London, patronizing architecture and the arts.
- His cultural patronage resulted in some of the finest Regency buildings and interiors in Britain; his aesthetic legacy outlasted his political one.
- Known for lavish spending, heavy drinking, and dissolute personal behavior that earned him the nickname "Prinny" among the public and sympathy he rarely enjoyed.
- Left the throne deeply unpopular, with his final years shadowed by illness and the weight of political conflicts unresolved; his death in 1830 was met with widespread indifference.
