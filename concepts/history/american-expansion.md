## summary

**American expansion** refers to the rapid territorial growth of the United States from independence through the late 19th century, driven by ideology, economics, and demographics. The nation's expansion westward—from the *Atlantic* to the *Pacific*—displaced Native Americans, defined *Manifest Destiny*, and shaped American identity. This process reshaped the continent's political boundaries, intensified sectional tensions over slavery, and established the continental United States.

## you gotta know

- The *Louisiana Purchase* of 1803 doubled the nation's size, acquisitions from France for $15 million, opening the interior to American settlement.
- The *Lewis and Clark Expedition* (1804–1806) explored the new western territories, gathering geographic and anthropological knowledge that facilitated subsequent settlement.
- *Manifest Destiny*, the ideology that American expansion to the Pacific was inevitable and righteous, became the dominant narrative justifying westward pressure.
- Indian Removal policies, culminating in the *Indian Removal Act* (1830) and the *Trail of Tears*, forcibly relocated tens of thousands of Native Americans.
- The *Mexican-American War* (1846–1848) brought vast southwestern territories into the US, including present-day California, Nevada, Utah, and Arizona.
- The *Oregon Territory* settlement with Britain (1846) extended American sovereignty to the Pacific Northwest, completing continental expansion.
- Settlement accelerated via the *Homestead Act* (1862), which offered free land to settlers, and the transcontinental railroad (completed 1869).
