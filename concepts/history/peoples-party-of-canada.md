## summary

The **People's Party of Canada** is a federal political party in Canada founded in September 2018 by former cabinet minister *Maxime Bernier*. Formed following Bernier's narrow defeat in the 2017 *Conservative Party of Canada* leadership race, the party advocates classical liberalism, right-wing populism, and strict fiscal conservatism. It has distinguished itself by opposing official multiculturalism, calling for substantial reductions in immigration, and mounting fierce resistance to public health mandates imposed during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## you gotta know

- Established by *Maxime Bernier* after he resigned from the *Conservative Party*, accusing his former party of abandoning true conservative principles and catering to special interests.
- Campaigned vigorously against Canada's system of agricultural supply management, particularly for dairy and poultry, which Bernier labeled a government-backed cartel.
- Advocated ending federal funding for official multiculturalism, arguing instead for policies that encourage rapid integration into Canadian civic and cultural life.
- Positioned itself as the principal federal political vehicle opposing COVID-19 lockdowns, vaccine passports, and federal travel mandates during the pandemic, drawing strong support from the 2022 *Freedom Convoy* movement that confronted [[justin-trudeau]].
- Captured nearly five percent of the national popular vote in the 2021 federal election, more than tripling its 2019 share, though it failed to win any seats in the House of Commons.
- Calls for substantial reductions in annual immigration levels, the repeal of the federal *Multiculturalism Act*, and the abolition of federal carbon-pricing initiatives.
- Draws ideological inspiration from right-wing populist and libertarian movements in the [[united-states]] and Europe, while emphasizing Canadian national sovereignty against globalist institutions.

## connections

- [[justin-trudeau]] — Liberal prime minister whose policies on immigration, carbon pricing, and pandemic measures served as the primary target of PPC campaigning.
- [[pierre-elliott-trudeau]] — prime minister who originally instituted Canada's official multiculturalism policy and constitutional framework, which the PPC seeks to scale back.
- [[lester-b.-pearson]] — Liberal leader who laid the foundations of modern Canadian social welfare programs and state institutions that the PPC critiques from a free-market perspective.
- [[united-states]] — country whose populist and libertarian political rhetoric heavily informs the rhetoric, media strategy, and messaging of the PPC.
- [[culture]] — central battleground for the PPC, which criticizes modern identity politics, state-funded diversity initiatives, and cultural globalism.
