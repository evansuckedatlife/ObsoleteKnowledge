## summary

A **class system** is a hierarchical model of social stratification in which individuals and groups are organized based on economic standing, inherited wealth, educational pedigree, and social influence. While theoretically allowing greater mobility than rigid caste systems or feudal estates, it heavily preserves elite privilege through cultural codes, exclusive institutions, and institutional patronage. Understanding class structures is essential for analyzing European political revolutions, the expansion of the *British Empire*, and modern ideological conflicts.

## you gotta know

- Provided the foundational social trust in elite British institutions that enabled the *Cambridge Five* spy ring to penetrate British intelligence unhindered on behalf of the *Soviet Union* during the *Cold War*.
- Underpinned the traditional social order of *England*, where attendance at public schools such as Eton and universities like Oxford and Cambridge forged an insular ruling class dominating politics and diplomacy.
- Served as the primary target of Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels in their materialist critique of capitalism, which framed all human history as the struggle between opposing socioeconomic classes.
- Accelerated dramatically following the *Industrial Revolution*, which created a vast urban industrial proletariat while consolidating the wealth and political dominance of the industrial bourgeoisie.
- Catalyzed the *French Revolution* of 1789, as the disenfranchised Third Estate revolted against the entrenched tax exemptions and hereditary privileges of the nobility and clergy.
- Influenced prominent social realists in nineteenth-century *realism*, including authors like *Charles Dickens* who systematically exposed class disparities in urban legal, workhouse, and educational institutions.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — economic transformation that reshaped Western class structures into modern capitalist divisions of labor.
- [[british-empire]] — global imperial entity administered through elite bureaucratic networks rooted in aristocratic privilege.
- [[england]] — nation whose entrenched aristocracy and public-school traditions produced an enduring cultural model of class stratification.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical ideological contest between capitalist democracies and Marxist-Leninist states centered on class conflict.
- [[soviet-union]] — socialist superpower whose intelligence services exploited British class deference to recruit elite Cambridge defectors.
- [[french-revolution]] — radical late-eighteenth-century upheaval that overthrew feudal class estates in favor of legal equality.
- [[clarence-darrow]] — legal crusader who frequently defended working-class labor unions against the power of entrenched industrial monopolies.
- [[claude-mckay]] — radical poet whose international socialist writings analyzed the intersection of racial oppression and capitalist labor exploitation.
