# Spanish Armada

## summary

The **Spanish Armada** (1588) was a massive naval invasion fleet sent by King *Philip II of Spain* against *Elizabeth I's* England, intended to conquer the island and overthrow the Protestant queen. Though assembled with superior numbers and galleons, the Spanish fleet was defeated by English tactics, superior seamanship, and fortuitous storms, marking a turning point in naval power and securing England's independence from Spanish intervention.

## you gotta know

- *Philip II*, Catholicism's champion, saw Protestant England as a heretical threat and pirate base; decades of English privateers attacking Spanish treasure fleets, plus English support for Spanish rebels in the Netherlands, motivated the invasion.
- The Spanish Armada consisted of 130 ships carrying roughly 30,000 men; it was assembled with meticulous planning and considerable expense, representing the greatest naval power then afloat and the most advanced warship designs of the era.
- English tactics proved decisive: rather than boarding actions (Spanish specialty), English captains maintained distance and used superior gunfire and maneuverability; English cannon fire damaged Spanish ships more effectively than Spanish attempts to return fire.
- A combination of English naval tactics, Spanish organizational problems (the Armada's original commander died before departure), and fortunate weather—including an Atlantic storm dubbed the "Spanish Armada's" final adversary—scattered the Spanish fleet.
- After two weeks of skirmishes, the Armada withdrew northward and attempted to return to Spain by sailing around Scotland and Ireland; rough seas, inadequate supplies, and lack of shelter destroyed many ships; roughly 50 percent of the fleet was lost.
- The victory established England as a genuine naval power, secured Elizabeth's reign against foreign conquest, and marked the beginning of English naval supremacy that would persist for three centuries; it became a cultural touchstone of English identity and Protestant victory.
