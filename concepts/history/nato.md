## summary

The **North Atlantic Treaty Organization** was a military and political alliance formed in 1949 to unite Western democracies against Soviet expansion during the *Cold War*. Founded as a counterweight to *Soviet* sphere of influence in Eastern Europe, NATO established the principle of collective security—an attack on one member would be treated as an attack on all. It became the defining institutional framework of Western strategy for the next seven decades.

## you gotta know

- Established by the North Atlantic Treaty (signed April 1949) among twelve original members: Belgium, Canada, Denmark, France, Iceland, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, the United Kingdom, and the United States.
- Created in direct response to Soviet blockade of Berlin (1948–49) and the communist takeover of Czechoslovakia, demonstrating Western concern about Soviet intentions.
- Article 5, the mutual defense clause, has only been invoked once—after the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks on the United States.
- NATO's command structure, led by a Supreme Commander, was modeled on unified military organization and included integrated planning across member nations.
- Expanded repeatedly during the *Cold War* and especially after its collapse, incorporating former *Soviet* satellite states and Warsaw Pact members.
- Featured nuclear deterrence as a cornerstone of strategy, with U.S. weapons stationed throughout Western Europe.
- The alliance persisted beyond the Soviet collapse, redefining its mission from containing communism to managing regional conflicts and emerging security threats.
