## summary

The **Brooklyn Navy Yard**, formally known as the *New York Naval Shipyard*, was a premier shipbuilding and drydock facility situated along the *East River* in *Kings County*. Active as a federal naval shipyard from 1801 until 1966, the complex served as the manufacturing backbone for the American fleet across major conflicts including the American Civil War and both world wars. At its peak during the Second World War, the yard employed over seventy thousand civilian workers and earned widespread renown as the birthplace of legendary battleships like the *USS Missouri* and *USS Iowa*.

## you gotta know

- Launched the famed *USS Missouri* (BB-63), the historic *Iowa-class* battleship upon whose surrender deck representatives of Imperial *Japan* signed the final documents ending *World War II* in *Tokyo Bay*.
- Constructed the *USS Maine* (ACR-1), the armored cruiser whose catastrophic explosion in the harbor of *Havana*, *Cuba*, precipitated the *Spanish-American War*.
- Mobilized massive industrial output nicknamed the "Can-Do Shipyard" during the global fight against the *Axis powers*, outfitting hundreds of front-line combat vessels and repairing thousands of Allied ships.
- Built the steam frigate *USS Fulton*, designed by *Robert Fulton*, widely recognized as the world's first steam-powered warship commissioned by any sovereign navy.
- Outfitted the ironclad *USS Monitor* with specialized armaments and mechanical components before its clash with the Confederate ironclad *CSS Virginia* during the *Battle of Hampton Roads*.
- Constructed the *USS Arizona* (BB-39), the *Pennsylvania-class* battleship that was destroyed during the 1941 attack on *Pearl Harbor*.
- Decommissioned in 1966 by Secretary of Defense *Robert McNamara*, after which the historic 300-acre waterfront complex was converted into an urban industrial park and media production center.
