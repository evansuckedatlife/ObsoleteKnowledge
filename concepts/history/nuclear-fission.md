## summary

**Nuclear fission** is the physical process by which a heavy atomic nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei, releasing enormous amounts of energy in the form of heat, radiation, and kinetic energy. Discovered experimentally in 1938 and explained theoretically in 1939, it became the foundation for both atomic weapons and nuclear power generation. The recognition that fission could be sustained in a chain reaction transformed physics from an esoteric discipline into a geopolitical force, triggering the Manhattan Project and ushering in the nuclear age.

## you gotta know

- Discovered by German scientists Otto Hahn and Fritz Strassmann through experiments bombarding uranium with neutrons; the process released far more energy than chemical reactions.
- Lise Meitner and Otto Frisch provided the theoretical explanation: when uranium-235 splits, the sum of product masses is slightly less than the original nucleus, with the lost mass converted to energy via Einstein's E=mc².
- Chain reaction possibility emerged from the observation that each fission releases neutrons capable of triggering additional fissions, creating potentially runaway energy release.
- Enrico Fermi demonstrated the first controlled chain reaction in 1942, proving that fission could be sustained and directed—the scientific breakthrough that made atomic weapons feasible.
- The Manhattan Project weaponized fission by enriching uranium-235 and producing plutonium-239, creating sufficient fissile material for sustained chain reactions in explosive devices.
- Post-war applications expanded to nuclear power generation for electricity, nuclear propulsion for submarines and carriers, and nuclear medicine—beneficial and destructive uses of the same physics.
