## summary

**Canonization** is the solemn, juridical procedure by which the Roman Catholic Church inscribes an individual into the official liturgical catalogue of saints, declaring with infallibility that the deceased resides in heavenly glory. Originally arising from communal veneration of early Christian martyrs, the practice was systematically centralized under the authority of the bishop of *Rome* throughout the Middle Ages. The development of this adjudicative institution exemplifies the bureaucratization of church law, ecclesiastical scrutiny of miraculous evidence, and the codification of heroic virtue.

## you gotta know

- Early Christian sanctity was conferred through local consensus and episcopal recognition, until Bishop *Ulrich of Augsburg* became the first person officially canonized by a pope in 993 under *John XV*.
- Pope *Alexander III* issued letters in the late twelfth century sharply curbing regional bishops from permitting public veneration of deceased individuals without papal permission.
- The universal centralization of the process was firmly codified into canon law in 1234 by Pope *Gregory IX* in his influential decretals *Rex pacificus*.
- In 1587, Pope *Sixtus V* established the *Promoter of the Faith*, popularly remembered as the *Devil's Advocate*, tasked with hunting for flaws in candidates' lives and debunking purported miraculous occurrences.
- Medieval monarchs frequently utilized the procedure for dynastic legitimacy, notably when King *Louis IX* of *medieval-france* was canonized as St. Louis in 1297 following his death during the *Crusades*.
- The classical post-Tridentine pathway requires four formal stages: declaration as a *Servant of God*, proclamation of *Venerable* status for heroic virtue, *Beatification* as Blessed, and final universal inscription.
- Pope *John Paul II* modernized and accelerated the tribunal procedures in 1983 through the apostolic constitution *Divinus Perfectionis Magister*, significantly curtailing the adversarial role of the Devil's Advocate.

## connections

- [[crusades]] — holy wars whose aristocratic fallen commanders, including crusader monarchs, were frequently proposed for veneration.
- [[medieval-france]] — realm whose Capetian royalty achieved immense sacral prestige through the swift canonization of King Louis IX.
- [[papal-authority]] — centralizing power of the papacy that gradually claimed exclusive jurisdiction over declarations of holiness.
- [[christianity]] — world religion whose belief in the communion of saints and intercessory prayer underpins the entire institution.
- [[rome]] — administrative seat of the Holy See and the specialized Congregation for the Causes of Saints.
- [[france]] — kingdom intimately intertwined with medieval piety, scholastic theology, and prominent canonized figures.
