## summary

The **Electoral Commission** (formally the **Electoral Commission of 1877**) was a temporary fifteen-member tribunal established by the *United States Congress* to resolve twenty disputed electoral votes from the presidential [[election-of-1876]]. Composed of five senators, five representatives, and five Supreme Court justices, the panel voted strictly along an 8–7 partisan divide to award all contested returns to Republican *[[rutherford-hayes]]*. Its controversial rulings averted constitutional paralysis, but directly precipitated the [[compromise-of-1877]], which ended military occupation in the American South and concluded the [[reconstruction-era]].

## you gotta know

- Created by the *Electoral Commission Act of 1877* to break a constitutional impasse over competing electoral slates submitted by Florida, Louisiana, South Carolina, and Oregon in the race between *[[rutherford-hayes]]* and *[[samuel-tilden]]*.
- Structured to maintain partisan balance, assigning five seats to the Senate (three Republicans, two Democrats), five to the House (three Democrats, two Republicans), and five to the Supreme Court.
- Conceived with the expectation that independent Supreme Court Justice *David Davis* would provide the deciding neutral vote, but he disqualified himself after the Illinois legislature abruptly elected him to the *United States Senate*.
- Forced the appointment of Republican Justice *Joseph P. Bradley* as the decisive fifteenth member because every other remaining justice on the Supreme Court bench was affiliated with the Republican Party.
- Ruled in favor of the Republicans by identical 8–7 votes on every procedural and substantive challenge, awarding all twenty disputed electoral votes to Hayes and granting him a 185–184 victory.
- Established the legal doctrine that federal authorities could not "go behind the returns," holding that the tribunal could not investigate allegations of voter intimidation or fraud certified by official state returning boards.
- Incensed Democrats, who decried the decision as a fraudulent usurpation and threatened a crippling filibuster to halt the official tally before Inauguration Day.
- Spurred the backroom negotiations that produced the [[compromise-of-1877]], withdrawing federal troops from the former Confederacy and leaving Southern governance in the hands of the Democratic "Redeemers."

## connections

- [[election-of-1876]] — the disputed presidential race whose rival returns necessitated the creation of the tribunal.
- [[rutherford-hayes]] — the Republican nominee awarded all twenty disputed electoral votes to claim the presidency.
- [[samuel-tilden]] — the Democratic nominee who led the popular vote and needed only one disputed elector to win.
- [[compromise-of-1877]] — the political pact struck to accept the commission's findings in exchange for federal troop withdrawal.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the post-Civil War period effectively ended by the political resolution of the dispute.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational document whose ambiguous Twelfth Amendment counting procedures caused the stalemate.
- [[14th-amendment]] — the Reconstruction amendment whose protections were hollowed out as federal enforcement receded following the crisis.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict whose bitter regional animosities threatened to re-erupt during the disputed proceedings.
