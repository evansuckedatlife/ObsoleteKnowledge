## summary

**Burgundy** (*Bourgogne*) is a historical region and former sovereign duchy in east-central *France*, celebrated for its strategic geography, monastic reforms, and late-medieval geopolitical preeminence. Originating with the East Germanic *Kingdom of the Burgundians* who settled within the *Roman Empire* during the fifth century, the territory developed into a formidable territorial power under four successive Valois dukes. Reaching its zenith in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, the *Duchy of Burgundy* rivaled the French monarchy and the Holy Roman Empire before its sudden collapse at the 1477 Battle of Nancy, precipitating a lasting partition between France and the Habsburgs.

## you gotta know

- Ruled by four great Valois dukes—Philip the Bold, John the Fearless, Philip the Good, and Charles the Bold—who forged a vast realm spanning the Rhône to the Low Countries.
- Emerged from the Germanic Burgundians who established a fifth-century kingdom along the Saône and Rhône, providing the historical basis for the epic *Nibelungenlied*.
- Allied with *England* during the Hundred Years' War following the 1419 assassination of John the Fearless, culminating in Burgundian forces capturing Joan of Arc at Compiègne in 1430.
- Saw its great power status abruptly shattered when Duke Charles the Bold was killed fighting Swiss and Lorrainer forces at the Battle of Nancy in 1477.
- Dismembered in the War of the Burgundian Succession, with Louis XI absorbing the duchy proper into *France* while Mary of Burgundy's marriage transferred the Low Countries to the Habsburgs.
- Led the Northern *Renaissance* through flamboyant courtly patronage, bankrolling masters such as painter Jan van Eyck, sculptor Claus Sluter, and composer Guillaume Du Fay.
- Fought an ill-fated crusade against the Ottomans in [[bulgaria]] at the 1396 Battle of Nicopolis, where the future duke John the Fearless was captured and held for an astronomical ransom.
- Anchored Western monastic reform during the High Middle Ages as the birthplace of the influential Benedictine abbey of Cluny and the Cistercian mother abbey of Cîteaux.

## connections

- [[france]] — kingdom with which the Valois dukes waged bitter dynastic and military struggles for control over Western European hegemony.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical realm that accepted early Burgundian tribes as imperial federates (*foederati*) along the Rhine and Savoyard frontiers.
- [[england]] — crucial diplomatic and military partner of Burgundy against King Charles VII of France during the late stages of the Hundred Years' War.
- [[bulgaria]] — eastern land where Burgundian crusading forces met disaster against Ottoman troops at the 1396 Battle of Nicopolis.
- [[renaissance]] — period of profound cultural renewal enriched by the lavish artistic and musical patronage of the ducal court at Dijon.
- [[christianity]] — faith profoundly reshaped by the Burgundian monastic networks of Cluny and Cîteaux that governed hundreds of European priories.
- [[germany]] — imperial territory whose Habsburg emperors inherited substantial Burgundian lands following the death of Charles the Bold.
