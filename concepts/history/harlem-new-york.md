## summary

**Harlem** is the neighborhood in upper Manhattan that emerged as the cultural and political capital of African American life in the twentieth century. Home to the [[harlem-renaissance|Harlem Renaissance]] of the 1920s and a crucial base for *Civil Rights* leadership, Harlem produced generations of musicians, writers, activists, and elected officials who shaped American culture. As a densely concentrated center of Black American political power, it became the electoral district for major figures like [[adam-clayton-powell-jr|Adam Clayton Powell Jr.]] and [[charles-rangel|Charles Rangel]].

## you gotta know

- Rapidly transformed from a middle-class German and Jewish neighborhood into a predominantly African American enclave after 1910 as racial restrictive covenants pushed Black migration northward.
- Became the epicenter of the [[harlem-renaissance|Harlem Renaissance]] (1920s–1930s), a flourishing of jazz, blues, literature, and visual arts that redefined African American cultural identity.
- Hosted vibrant nightclubs like the Cotton Club (ironically segregated despite hosting Black performers) and Apollo Theater, centers of artistic innovation and audience gathering.
- Served as the political base for [[adam-clayton-powell-jr|Powell]], who represented Harlem in Congress and wielded his electoral mandate to pressure national civil rights agendas.
- Experienced severe economic decline and disinvestment from the 1960s onward, with redlining and suburbanization draining wealth and opportunity.
- Remains symbolically central to African American identity and continues to be a site of cultural production and historical memory.
- Boundaries roughly defined as 110th to 155th Street, from the Hudson River to the East River.
