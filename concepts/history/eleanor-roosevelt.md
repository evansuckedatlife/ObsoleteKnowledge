## summary

**Eleanor Roosevelt** (1884–1962), born **Anna Eleanor Roosevelt**, was an American political leader, humanitarian, and diplomat who served as First Lady of the *United States* from 1933 to 1945 during the presidency of *Franklin D. Roosevelt*. She fundamentally redefined the role of presidential spouse, transforming it into an influential platform for civil rights, women's equality, and progressive labor policy. Following *World War II*, she served as an American delegate to the *United Nations*, where she chaired the committee that drafted the *Universal Declaration of Human Rights*.

## you gotta know

- Longest-serving First Lady in United States history, occupying the role across four terms through the *Great Depression* and *World War II*.
- Chaired the *United Nations Human Rights Commission* and led the international effort to draft the foundational 1948 *Universal Declaration of Human Rights*, earning the moniker "First Lady of the World."
- Resigned from the *Daughters of the American Revolution* in 1939 after they refused to permit Black singer *Marian Anderson* to perform at Constitution Hall, subsequently helping arrange Anderson's landmark concert at the Lincoln Memorial.
- Authored *My Day*, a syndicated daily newspaper column that ran from 1935 until 1962, discussing human rights, domestic policy, and family life with millions of American readers.
- Conducted regular, women-only White House press conferences, forcing news agencies across the nation to employ female journalists to cover executive events.
- Served as the active "eyes and legs" of her husband after polio compromised his mobility, touring coal mines, New Deal project sites, military hospitals, and Pacific battle zones.
- Born into a prominent patrician family as the favorite niece of President *Theodore Roosevelt*, who walked her down the aisle at her wedding to *Franklin D. Roosevelt*.
- Advocated aggressively for racial justice, lobbying the White House to support anti-lynching legislation and eliminate discriminatory practices within federal relief programs.
