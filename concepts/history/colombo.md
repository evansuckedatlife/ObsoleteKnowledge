## summary

**Colombo** is the executive and judicial capital, largest city, and commercial epicenter of Sri Lanka. Situated on the southwestern coast of the island along the Indian Ocean, the city grew from an ancient maritime trading post into a fortified colonial stronghold successively occupied by Portuguese, Dutch, and British powers. Renowned for its natural and man-made port facilities, it served as the imperial administrative hub of Ceylon under the [[british-empire]] and remains a crossroads of multicultural heritage.

## you gotta know

- Functioned for centuries as an Indian Ocean trading center known to Arab, Persian, and Chinese merchants, who traded cinnamon, gems, and spices at its natural harbour along the maritime [[silk-road]].
- Visited in 1505 by Portuguese explorer *Lourenço de Almeida*, who negotiated trading rights with the Kingdom of *Kotte* and subsequently established a fortified outpost known as the *Fort*.
- Besieged and captured in 1656 by the Dutch East India Company (*VOC*), who rebuilt its administrative quarters and laid out the nearby residential neighborhood of the *Pettah*.
- Ceded to Great Britain in 1796 during the Napoleonic wars, becoming the official colonial capital of British Ceylon following the annexation of the Kingdom of *Kandy* in 1815.
- Transformed by imperial engineers through the late nineteenth-century construction of its massive artificial deep-water breakwater, making it one of the busiest maritime coaling ports under [[colonialism]].
- Preserves a layered built landscape featuring the neoclassical *Old Parliament Building*, the *National Museum of Colombo*, and landmark Buddhist sites such as the *Gangaramaya Temple*.
- Witnessed the historic 1950 meeting of Commonwealth foreign ministers that established the *Colombo Plan*, an influential international initiative designed to bolster economic and social cooperation in the Asia-Pacific.
- Handed legislative responsibilities to the nearby planned administrative capital of *Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte* in 1982, while remaining the financial and executive nerve center of the island nation.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power that unified Ceylon and developed the city as its primary naval, economic, and administrative capital.
- [[colonialism]] — the centuries of European maritime expansion through Portuguese, Dutch, and British rule that shaped urban topography and commerce.
- [[buddhism]] — the predominant religious tradition of Sri Lanka, represented within the city through major cultural institutions and historic temples.
- [[silk-road]] — the historic maritime trade network through which spices and precious gems were shipped across the Indian Ocean.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the era of maritime navigation during which Portuguese navigators first established a fort on the southwestern coast.
