## summary

The **temperance movement** (or **American temperance movement**) was an organized social crusade against the consumption of alcoholic beverages that arose during the early nineteenth century, primarily across the [[united-states]], [[england]], and northern Europe. Driven heavily by evangelical [[christianity]] and social reformers responding to the disruptive urbanization of the [[industrial-revolution]], the crusade framed liquor as the chief catalyst of poverty, domestic violence, and moral corruption. It provided an unprecedented avenue for female political mobilization, directly stimulating early campaigns for [[womens-rights]] and [[suffrage]] before culminating in constitutional prohibition.

## you gotta know

- Culminated nationally in the [[united-states]] with the ratification of the *Eighteenth Amendment* in 1919 and the enactment of the *Volstead Act*, establishing a federal ban on the manufacture, sale, and transportation of intoxicating liquors until its repeal by the *Twenty-First Amendment* in 1933.
- *Carrie Nation* became the movement's most infamous radical, capturing international attention by entering saloons across Kansas and other dry territories brandishing a hatchet and Bible to smash liquor bottles, kegs, and barroom mirrors in violent assaults she termed "hatchetations."
- The *Woman's Christian Temperance Union* (*WCTU*), organized in 1874 and directed for two decades by *Frances Willard*, grew into the largest women's association in the world under an expansive "Do Everything" agenda that linked temperance to labor rights, prison reform, and female enfranchisement.
- Pioneering women's rights advocates including *Susan B. Anthony* and *[[elizabeth-cady-stanton]]* began their public activism within temperance organizations, arguing that disenfranchised women urgently needed legal [[womens-rights]] and political [[suffrage]] to protect their families from the catastrophic domestic consequences of spousal alcoholism.
- The *Anti-Saloon League*, formed in 1893 and driven by legislative strategist *Wayne Wheeler*, revolutionized modern interest-group politics by marshaling evangelical Protestant congregations and executing ruthless single-issue pressure politics against politicians regardless of partisan affiliation.
- Antebellum agitation originated within the religious fervor of the Second Great Awakening, giving rise to the *American Temperance Society* in 1826 and the *Washingtonian movement* in 1840, a working-class fraternal society of reformed drunkards that championed emotional public confession and voluntary pledges of total abstinence.
- *Neal Dow*, a Quaker reformer and Portland mayor celebrated as the "Father of Prohibition," secured the landmark 1851 *Maine Law*, the first statewide statutory prohibition against the sale and production of alcohol, which served as the legislative blueprint for similar dry laws across the northern states.

## connections

- [[suffrage]] — the political crusade that gained massive momentum from temperance networks seeking the ballot to outlaw the saloon trade.
- [[womens-rights]] — the broader legal movement catalyzed by women seeking legal autonomy and protection against alcohol-fueled domestic abuse and poverty.
- [[elizabeth-cady-stanton]] — early feminist strategist whose initial reform work in temperance convinced her of the fundamental necessity of female empowerment.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational governing charter amended by the Eighteenth Amendment to prohibit alcohol and the Twenty-First Amendment to repeal it.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the economic transition that demanded sober factory labor, accelerating employer and civic backing for legal sobriety measures.
- [[christianity]] — the evangelical revivalism and Social Gospel theology that supplied the moral authority, grassroots networks, and female leadership for the crusade.
- [[world-war-i]] — the military conflict that fostered food-conservation imperatives and anti-German hostility toward immigrant brewers, accelerating nationwide prohibition.
