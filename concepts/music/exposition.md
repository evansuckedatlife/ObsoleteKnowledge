## summary

An **exposition** is the initial large-scale structural section of a movement composed in *[[sonata-form]]*, responsible for introducing the principal thematic material and establishing the primary tonal tension of the work. Emerging in eighteenth-century Europe during the *[[enlightenment]]*, the form became the central organizing architecture of instrumental genres across the *[[classical-music]]* era, including the *[[symphony]]*, string quartet, and sonata. Its essential harmonic mechanism involves moving the listener away from the home key toward a contrasting key area, thereby providing the dramatic and intellectual impetus that propels the subsequent sections of the piece.

## you gotta know

- Presents the primary thematic material of a movement before any significant thematic fragmentation or transformation takes place.
- Articulates a critical harmonic journey from the tonic key to a secondary key, typically modulating to the dominant in major-key movements and to the relative major in minor-key movements.
- Divided traditionally into four successive structural units: the primary theme group (*P*), the transition (*TR*), the secondary theme group (*S*), and the closing theme group (*C*).
- Features the medial caesura (*MC*), a dramatic mid-exposition pause or rhetorical break identified in modern sonata theory that punctuates the transition and heralds the entrance of the secondary theme.
- Concludes historically with an essential expositional closure (*EEC*), marked by the first satisfactory perfect authentic cadence (*PAC*) in the new key.
- Customarily terminated by a double repeat bar in classical works by *Joseph Haydn* and *Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart*, instructing performers to repeat the entire section from the beginning.
- Differs functionally in concertos, where a double-exposition design traditionally features an initial orchestral statement in the tonic followed by a solo exposition that executes the structural modulation.
- Expanded in nineteenth-century *romanticism* and twentieth-century *[[modernism]]* to encompass three-key expositions, pioneered prominently by *Ludwig van Beethoven* and *Franz Schubert*.

## connections

- [[sonata-form]] — the tripartite structural design whose opening section is the exposition.
- [[development]] — the subsequent unstable section that fragments and elaborates the motifs introduced here.
- [[recapitulation]] — the resolving final section that restates the primary and secondary themes entirely in the home key.
- [[tonality]] — the hierarchical pitch system that creates the harmonic tension underlying the movement.
- [[classical-music]] — the historical idiom in which this compositional technique was codified.
- [[symphony]] — the orchestral genre whose opening movements almost universally employ this section.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical epoch whose ideals of balance, balance, and rational discourse shaped this musical rhetoric.
