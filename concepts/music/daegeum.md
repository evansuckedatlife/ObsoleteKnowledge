## summary

The **daegeum** (also transliterated as **taegum**) is a prominent transverse bamboo flute native to [[korea]], celebrated for its wide dynamic range and distinctive buzzing timbre. Emerging as early as the Three Kingdoms period and formalized during the Unified Silla dynasty, it serves both as the principal melodic voice and standard tuning reference in traditional [[gugak]]. Its singular acoustic character derives from a vibrating inner reed membrane known as the *cheong*, making it one of the most recognizable and versatile wind instruments in East Asian musical history.

## you gotta know

- Distinctive buzzing membrane (*cheong*): The instrument features an extra aperture situated between the mouth hole and finger holes called the *cheonggong*, which is covered by a delicate reed membrane that vibrates with an intense, reedy resonance when blown with force.
- Legendary origin (*Manpasikjeok*): The thirteenth-century historical chronicle *Samguk Yusa* recounts that *King Sinmun* of Unified Silla received a mystical bamboo stalk from the Dragon King of the East Sea; when fashioned into a flute, its music miraculously repelled invaders, cured sicknesses, and calmed turbulent seas.
- Two divergent modern forms: The instrument exists primarily as the longer, lower-pitched *jeongak daegeum* used in solemn court rituals and stately chamber suites, and the shorter, agile *sanjo daegeum* developed in the late nineteenth century for rapid folk solos.
- Master ensemble tuning standard: In court orchestra performances such as the Royal Ancestral Shrine ritual (*Jongmyo Jeryeak*), the instrument sounds the fundamental reference pitch *hwangjong*, establishing the exact benchmark against which all accompanying strings and winds calibrate.
- Rare bamboo construction: Master craftsmen preferentially construct concert instruments from *ssangdongjuk*, a rare, thick-walled bamboo anomaly where two stems grow from a single root node, providing the dense interior walls essential for powerful projection.
- Expressive ornamentations (*nongum*): Performers achieve characteristic wide microtonal shifts, weeping glissandi, and dramatic vibrato by rolling the tube inward or outward against the chin while manipulating breath pressure across the embouchure.
- Non-fingered tuning apertures: At the distal end of the instrument, makers drill several open, non-fingered holes traditionally termed *chilseonggong* ("seven star holes"), which regulate the overall acoustic column length and stabilize low-register intonation.

## connections

- [[korea]] — the East Asian peninsula where the flute developed across multiple dynasties into a premier cultural symbol.
- [[gugak]] — the overarching tradition of Korean music in which the flute anchors both courtly and popular repertoires.
- [[janggu]] — the hourglass-shaped drum whose rhythmic cycles (*jangdan*) provide foundational accompaniment for flute suites.
- [[gayageum]] — the twelve-string plucked zither that frequently shares thematic lines and responsive dialogues with the flute.
- [[geomungo]] — the scholar's six-string fretted zither associated with the same Confucian court aesthetics as the aristocratic flute.
- [[pansori]] — dramatic vocal storytelling whose expressive vocal tears, rasp, and modal inflections deeply shaped instrumental folk *sanjo*.
- [[classical-music]] — court and ceremonial traditions worldwide that mirror the institutional prestige and formal codification of royal *jeongak*.
- [[improvisation]] — spontaneous modal exploration that defines both shamanic *sinawi* ensembles and virtuosic solo flute performance.
