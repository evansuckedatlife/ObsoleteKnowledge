## summary

**Bessie Smith**, universally acclaimed as the **Empress of the Blues**, was the foremost American blues singer of the 1920s and early 1930s. Emerging from poverty and itinerant Southern tent shows, she rose to national prominence as the defining artist of classic vaudeville blues and early commercial [[jazz]]. Her booming, unamplified contralto projection, subtle pitch inflection, and unflinching lyrical depictions of working-class female autonomy set lasting benchmarks for twentieth-century vocalists. Her recorded legacy established the standard for early race records and profoundly impacted the trajectory of American popular music across the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Reigned as the supreme recording star of the 1920s classic blues era under the title *Empress of the Blues*, commanding unprecedented performance fees and becoming the highest-paid Black entertainer in the world.
- Rescued the struggling race records division of *Columbia Records* in 1923 with her debut 78-rpm release of *Downhearted Blues* backed with *Gulf Coast Blues*, which sold roughly 780,000 copies in six months.
- Partnered with cornetist [[louis-armstrong]] on historic 1925 recording sessions, producing the definitive call-and-response arrangement of *W. C. Handy*'s *St. Louis Blues* accompanied by reed organist *Fred Longshaw*.
- Composed and recorded the landmark 1927 topical standard *Backwater Blues*, capturing the devastation, displacement, and racial hardship wrought by the catastrophic *Great Mississippi Flood*.
- Starred in *St. Louis Blues* (1929), a two-reel musical dramatization directed by *Dudley Murphy* that remains her only surviving visual film appearance, featuring stride pianist *James P. Johnson* and the *Hall Johnson Choir*.
- Scored a poignant hit with *Nobody Knows You When You're Down and Out* in 1929, an autobiographical-sounding lament cut shortly before the onset of the *Great Depression* undermined her touring revenue and luxury lifestyle.
- Developed her immense vocal projection and theatrical stagecraft touring the *Theater Owners Booking Association* (TOBA) circuit after receiving early guidance from blues matriarch *Ma Rainey* in the *Rabbit Foot Minstrels*.
- Lost her life following an automobile crash along Mississippi Route 61 in 1937, generating a long-standing myth about hospital segregation that inspired *Edward Albee*'s play *The Death of Bessie Smith* and prompted *Janis Joplin* to buy her headstone in 1970.

## connections

- [[louis-armstrong]] — virtuosic cornet partner whose emotive obbligato lines elevated her classic 1925 tracks like *St. Louis Blues* and *Careless Love Blues*.
- [[jazz]] — the evolving American musical genre she helped crystallize through rhythmic swing, expressive blue notes, and high-profile collaborations with instrumental innovators.
- [[united-states]] — the country where her nationwide TOBA circuit tours and best-selling phonograph records shaped modern African American cultural identity.
- [[new-york-city]] — recording capital where she cut her historic master discs for *Columbia Records* and headlined top Harlem theatrical revues.
- [[duke-ellington]] — contemporary bandleader whose sophisticated big-band orchestrations developed parallel to her blues mastery during the Harlem Renaissance.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — historical era whose Jim Crow aftermath, disenfranchisement, and racial violence formed the societal backdrop of her Southern childhood and touring career.
- [[improvisation]] — defining musical technique embodied in her subtle melodic variations, microtonal blue notes, and flexible, conversational vocal delivery.
