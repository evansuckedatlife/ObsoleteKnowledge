## summary

***Gianni Schicchi*** is a celebrated one-act comic *[[opera]]* composed by *Giacomo Puccini* to an Italian libretto by *Giovacchino Forzano*, premiering at the Metropolitan Opera in *[[new-york-city]]* in 1918. Set in thirteenth-century *[[florence]]*, the work serves as the humorous concluding panel of Puccini's famous triptych of short operas known as *Il trittico*. Based on an episode in the thirtieth canto of *Dante Alighieri's* *Inferno*, it remains a staple of the international operatic repertoire, prized for its rapid-fire wit, ensemble writing, and lyricism.

## you gotta know

- Premiered as the third and final installment of *Il trittico*, following the verismo tragedy *Il tabarro* and the convent drama *Suor Angelica*.
- Contains the ubiquitous *[[soprano]]* aria *O mio babbino caro* ("Oh my dear papa"), sung by Lauretta as she pleads with her father to help her marry Rinuccio.
- Derived directly from a brief mention in *Dante's* *Inferno*, where the historical title rogue is condemned to the eighth circle of Hell for falsifying another man's person.
- Revolves around the death of the wealthy Florentine patriarch Buoso Donati, whose greedy relatives are furious to discover he has bequeathed his vast fortune to a monastery.
- Features the clever peasant protagonist disguising himself as the deceased Buoso in a darkened bedroom to dictate a fraudulent new will to a legal notary.
- Culminates in a comedic double-cross where the protagonist leaves Buoso's prize possessions—the mule, the house in Florence, and the mills at Signa—to none other than himself.
- Ends with the title character stepping forward to deliver a spoken address to the audience, pleading for an extenuating verdict and asking for their generous applause.
- Composed alongside Puccini's other late masterworks during the final years of *[[world-war-i]]*, showcasing intricate orchestration and mordant comedic timing.
