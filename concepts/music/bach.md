## summary

**Johann Sebastian Bach** (1685–1750) was a German Baroque composer and organist whose mastery of counterpoint and fugal form stands unmatched in Western music history. Working throughout his life in Germany—first as a church organist, later as Kapellmeister (court music director) at the court of Köthen and in Leipzig—Bach synthesized contrapuntal complexity with harmonic richness and spiritual depth. His vast output of sacred and instrumental works established principles of composition that remain foundational to music theory and practice.

## you gotta know

- *The Art of Fugue* (incomplete, published posthumously) is his monumental exploration of fugal technique, featuring 18 fugues and canons of increasing complexity built on a single subject.
- *The Well-Tempered Clavier* (two volumes, 24 preludes and fugues each in all major and minor keys) is a pedagogical masterpiece demonstrating the viability of equal temperament tuning and remains essential to keyboard musician training.
- *The Musical Offering* is a multi-movement work for various ensembles based on a theme submitted by King Frederick the Great of Prussia; it showcases Bach's ability to transform a simple melody into intricate contrapuntal variations.
- As an organist, Bach pioneered the expansion of organ music, composing numerous toccatas, fugues, and works for organ solo, which established the organ as a major concert and compositional instrument.
- Bach had at least 20 children, several of whom became important composers in their own right: *Wilhelm Friedemann*, *Carl Philipp Emanuel*, *Johann Christian*, and *Johann Christoph Friedrich* shaped the Classical period's emergence.
- He held positions in Weimar, Köthen, and Leipzig, where he served as Thomas Cantor (director of music) at the Thomas Church for nearly 27 years, composing hundreds of sacred cantatas for weekly performance.

## connections

- [[counterpoint]] — Bach is its supreme master; his works are the canonical study texts.
- [[fugue]] — The form Bach perfected; "fugue" and "Bach" are nearly synonymous in music education.
- [[baroque-music]] — The culminating figure of the Baroque era's harmonic and contrapuntal sophistication.
- [[cantata]] — Bach composed over 200 sacred cantatas for liturgical use in Leipzig churches.
- [[organist]] — Bach's keyboard works (especially organ works) redefined the instrument's technical and expressive possibilities.
- [[polyphony]] — Bach's mastery of multiple simultaneous melodic lines shaped all subsequent understanding of polyphonic structure.
