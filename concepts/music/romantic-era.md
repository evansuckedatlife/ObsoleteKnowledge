## summary

The **Romantic era** (roughly 1820–1900) in music and dance was a period of revolutionary expansion: composers and choreographers pursued emotional intensity, exotic subject matter, and unprecedented technical display. Symphonies grew vast; operas became theatrical spectacles; ballet emerged as a dominant art form with works like *Swan Lake* and *The Nutcracker* anchoring the repertoire. The Romantic aesthetic valued personal expression, nature, nationalism, and the supernatural—a dramatic shift from Classical restraint and formality.

## you gotta know

- Romanticism arose as a reaction to Classical order, embracing emotion, subjectivity, and the irrational.
- The orchestra expanded dramatically: more brass, woodwinds, and percussion created richer textures and louder crescendos than ever before.
- Program music—instrumental works depicting explicit narratives—rose in prestige; Berlioz's *Symphonie fantastique* is the quintessential example.
- Composers like Liszt and Chopin elevated the solo piano; the concert etude became both virtuosic display and emotional poetry.
- Ballet achieved canonical status through the Russian Imperial tradition: Marius Petipa choreographed *Swan Lake* and *The Nutcracker*, with scores by Tchaikovsky.
- Verdi and Wagner transformed opera: Italian opera grew more dramatically intense, while Wagner developed the leitmotif and Gesamtkunstwerk (total art work).
- National schools emerged: Russian, Czech, Scandinavian, Spanish composers drew on folk traditions and regional identity, moving beyond Italian/German dominance.

## connections

- [[classical-music]] — the immediate predecessor that Romantics rejected and built upon.
- [[ballet]] — a performance art that achieved maturity during the Romantic era, especially in Russia.
- [[pyotr-ilyich-tchaikovsky]] — the Russian master whose symphonies, operas, and ballets epitomize Romantic grandeur.
- [[igor-stravinsky]] — a composer who bridged Romanticism and Modernism, born in Russia during the Romantic height.
- [[giuseppe-verdi]] — Italian opera composer who brought Romanticism to its peak in works like *La Traviata* and *Rigoletto*.
- [[music-and-dance]] — the symbiotic relationship between composed music and choreography flourishing in the Romantic era.
