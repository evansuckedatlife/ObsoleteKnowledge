## summary

An **ensemble** is any collective group of two or more musicians who perform vocal or instrumental music together in coordinated synchronization. Ranging in size from intimate duos and chamber string quartets to massive symphonic orchestras and big bands, ensembles exist across virtually every culture and historical era. Studying the ensemble is central to musicology because it governs the dynamics of blend, intonation, collective texture, orchestration, and communication among performing artists.

## you gotta know

- Denotes both the physical personnel of a musical group and the quality of collective balance, unity, and cohesion displayed during performance.
- Ranges in scale from small chamber combinations—such as trios, quartets, and quintets—up to large symphonic orchestras and wind bands requiring an independent conductor.
- Relies on shared standards of intonation, frequently governed in Western traditions by *[[equal-temperament-tuning]]* to maintain pitch coherence across diverse instruments.
- Served in the jazz tradition as a flexible vehicle for collective dialogue, from the polyphonic counterpoint of New Orleans styles to the big bands of *[[duke-ellington]]*.
- Fostered revolutionary group improvisation in the 1960s modal post-bop quartet of *[[john-coltrane]]*, propelled by the polyrhythms of drummer *[[elvin-jones]]*.
- Provided an experimental laboratory for avant-garde chamber textures, as demonstrated by the idiosyncratic woodwind lineups constructed by *[[eric-dolphy]]*.
- Uses established structural archetypes in Western art music, such as the classical string quartet pioneered by Joseph Haydn and Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart.

## connections

- [[elvin-jones]] — the master drummer whose dynamic interactions redefined the role of rhythm within the jazz ensemble.
- [[eric-dolphy]] — avant-garde soloist and composer who pioneered unique chamber ensemble configurations.
- [[equal-temperament-tuning]] — the universal tuning compromise enabling distinct instruments in an ensemble to play together in harmony.
- [[classical-music]] — the long-standing tradition that formalized standard ensembles like the orchestra and string quartet.
- [[jazz]] — the idiom that revolutionized ensemble performance through flexible improvisation, swinging time, and big band arranging.
- [[polyphony]] — the multi-part musical texture that requires independent lines to function together within an ensemble.
- [[counterpoint]] — the technique of combining distinct melodic voices, forming the structural core of chamber ensemble music.
- [[improvisation]] — the practice of spontaneous creation that requires acute real-time communication among ensemble members.
