## summary

The **gayageum** (also spelled **kayagum**) is a traditional Korean plucked zither characterized by silk strings stretched over movable bridges across an elongated paulownia soundboard. First developed in the sixth century within the ancient *Gaya* confederacy, the instrument became central to both refined court orchestral suites and vibrant folk music traditions across [[korea]]. In the late nineteenth century, the creation of a smaller, more nimble variant revolutionized the tradition by giving birth to the virtuoso solo genre known as *sanjo*. Today, the instrument stands as the quintessential chordophone of [[gugak]], celebrated for its expressive microtonal bending, dramatic dynamic range, and seamless integration into contemporary [[classical-music]].

## you gotta know

- Constructed from a hollowed soundboard of paulownia wood over which twelve silk strings pass, each elevated and tuned by an individual movable wooden bridge known as an *anjok*, or "wild goose foot."
- Mythological and historical chronicles in the *Samguk sagi* attribute its design to King *Gasil* of the *Gaya* confederacy, who commissioned the royal musician *Ureuk* to write twelve compositions to unify the state's cultural identity before the kingdom fell to *Silla*.
- Split into two primary structural forms: the larger *pungnyu* (or *jeongak*) style played slowly in aristocratic court settings, and the smaller *sanjo* style built with closer string spacing to facilitate rapid finger articulation in folk music.
- Right-hand technique employs the thumb, index, and middle fingers to pluck, flick, and pull the strings without artificial picks, while the left hand presses the strings behind the *anjok* to generate wide pitch glides, microtonal inflections, and the deep, stylized vibrato known as *nonghyeon*.
- In the 1890s, master musician *Kim Chang-jo* organized the foundational repertoire of *sanjo*, an extended solo form that cycles through progressively accelerating rhythmic patterns (*jangdan*) provided by an accompanying [[janggu]] drum.
- An essential vehicle for *gayageum byeongchang*, an exacting performance tradition where a single artist accompanies their own dramatic singing of narrative episodes drawn from [[pansori]] literature or regional folk tunes.
- Modern twentieth- and twenty-first-century adaptations expanded beyond the traditional twelve silk courses, introducing 18-, 21-, and 25-string models strung with nylon or metal wound strings to perform diatonic scores, cross-cultural collaborations, and experimental pieces shaped by musical [[modernism]].

## connections

- [[gugak]] — the overarching corpus of traditional Korean court and folk music in which the zither occupies the central place.
- [[geomungo]] — the fretted six-stringed Korean zither struck with a bamboo rod, historically favored by Confucian scholars as a philosophical counterpart.
- [[janggu]] — the hourglass-shaped double-headed drum whose rhythmic patterns provide essential accompaniment for traditional solo suites.
- [[pansori]] — the theatrical narrative vocal tradition that directly fed melodic themes and emotional modes into solo instrumental suites.
- [[improvisation]] — the historical engine of the solo repertoire, allowing performers to elaborate standard motifs before compositions solidified into codified schools.
- [[classical-music]] — the global and domestic concert art tradition in which modern multi-string variants perform newly commissioned concertos.
- [[japan]] — East Asian neighbor whose thirteen-string *koto* shares common ancestral roots with continental long-zither traditions.
