## summary

**George Frideric Handel** (1685–1759) was a German-born Baroque composer who spent most of his career in London, becoming the towering musical figure of 18th-century England. Master of both operatic drama and monumental choral works, he composed operas, oratorios, concertos, and orchestral suites of unsurpassed craftsmanship and immediate emotional appeal. His works defined the Baroque aesthetic in England and influenced generations of composers.

## you gotta know

- Born in Halle, Germany; trained in Italy before settling in London, where he became the dominant musical force from the 1720s onward.
- Composed approximately 40 operas in the Italian style, featuring elaborate coloratura roles for castrati singers and heroic narratives from classical antiquity and mythology.
- Created monumental choral works, particularly oratorios, which became his primary focus after the operatic market declined; the most famous is *Messiah* (1742), containing the beloved *Hallelujah* chorus.
- Wrote *Water Music* and *Music for the Royal Fireworks*, two celebrated instrumental suites composed for public court occasions, showcasing brilliant orchestration and popular appeal.
- Was master of the concerto grosso form, alternating between a small group of soloists and the full orchestra, creating dynamic textural contrast.
- Received royal patronage from Kings George I and George II, and his music became central to English civic, religious, and courtly life.
- Maintained a mastery of counterpoint rivaling Bach's, combined with an incomparable gift for immediate, singable melody—making his works accessible yet sophisticated.
