## summary

**Roman numeral analysis** is the standard notational system for identifying chords and their harmonic functions within a tonal key. Using Roman numerals (I, IV, V, etc.) to denote chord scale degrees and uppercase/lowercase to indicate major/minor quality, it provides an elegant shorthand for understanding harmonic structure. Developed over centuries and codified in nineteenth-century theory pedagogy, roman numeral analysis remains the primary tool for musicians to comprehend how Classical, Baroque, and Romantic composers achieved coherence through harmonic logic.

## you gotta know

- Capital Roman numerals (I, IV, V) denote major triads; lowercase (i, iv, v) denote minor triads in the same key position.
- In C major: I = C major, IV = F major, V = G major; in C minor: i = C minor, iv = F minor, v = G minor.
- The tonic chord (I) is the resting point; the dominant (V) creates tension; the subdominant (IV) bridges them—this hierarchy organizes entire pieces.
- Seventh chords add complexity: V7 (dominant seventh) is especially important, creating maximum tension resolved back to I.
- Figured bass (numbers beneath bass notes) is the historical precursor; roman numerals evolved as a more explicit system for analysis.
- A progression like I-IV-V-I exemplifies the fundamental harmonic motion from stability through tension back to resolution.
- Extensions and modifications: secondary dominants (V/V), borrowed chords (iv in major key), and chromatic alterations complicate the basic system.
