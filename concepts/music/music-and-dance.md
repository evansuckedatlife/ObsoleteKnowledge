## summary

**Music and dance** is the symbiotic creative partnership between composed music and choreography that flourished most fully during the Romantic era (roughly 1820–1900) and remains central to classical ballet. The partnership elevated both arts: composers created unprecedented musical richness and complexity for the demands of stage movement, while choreographers designed movement to embody musical structure, emotion, and narrative. This fusion produced some of Western culture's most beloved and enduring works.

## you gotta know

- Romantic-era composers including *Tchaikovsky*, *Delibes*, *Adam*, and *Stravinsky* elevated ballet music from accompaniment to art-form status, creating expansive scores with full symphonic resources.
- *Tchaikovsky's Swan Lake* (1876) and *The Nutcracker* (1892) are among the most performed musical compositions worldwide; their music transcends the dance context and stands as masterworks of symphonic composition.
- *Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring* (1913) revolutionized both music and dance simultaneously, combining dissonant, polyrhythmic orchestration with primitive, angular choreography that scandalized and transformed the arts.
- Legendary choreographers including *Marius Petipa*, *George Balanchine*, and *Michel Fokine* developed the technical vocabulary and narrative structures of classical and contemporary ballet in collaboration with composers.
- The relationship works bidirectionally: composers must understand the physical and temporal constraints of dance movement, while choreographers must visualize and interpret musical structure, emotion, and form through movement.
- Famous pieces such as *Giselle*, *La Sylphide*, *The Firebird*, and *Cinderella* demonstrate how music and choreography together create unified artistic statements unachievable by either art alone.
- In the 20th century, composers and choreographers expanded the partnership beyond Romantic narrative ballet into abstract movement-music relationships and postmodern experimentation.
