## summary

**A symphony** is an extended orchestral work, typically in three or four movements, that showcases the full resources of an ensemble through elaborate formal structures and thematic development. Emerging as a distinct genre in the mid-18th century from lighter Italian opera overtures, the symphony became the flagship form of Classical music and remains a central genre in orchestral repertoire. The symphony's archetypal structure—fast-slow-minuet-fast—provided composers with a flexible framework for both intellectual rigor and emotional expression.

## you gotta know

- The symphony evolved from the Italian *sinfonia* (an overture to operas), which gradually expanded from a brief three-movement form into a substantial, independent concert work.
- The typical four-movement structure consists of: Allegro (fast, in sonata form), Andante (slow), Minuet and Trio (or Scherzo in later symphonies), and Presto or Allegro (fast rondo or sonata form).
- *Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart* wrote 41 numbered symphonies, establishing the Classical symphonic ideal: elegant proportion, memorable themes, and virtuosic orchestration while maintaining formal clarity.
- *Ludwig van Beethoven* transformed the symphony into a vehicle for profound philosophical and emotional statement, expanding it beyond Classical conventions; his nine symphonies are regarded as the pinnacle of the form.
- The first movement typically employs *sonata form*, presenting two contrasting themes, developing them through harmonic and rhythmic variation, and recapitulating them in a final resolution.
- Romantic-era composers including *Hector Berlioz*, *Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky*, and *Anton Bruckner* expanded the symphony in scope, duration, and orchestral resources, exploring program music and unprecedented harmonic complexity.
- The symphony declined somewhat in the early 20th century as other forms gained prominence, but composers including *Dmitri Shostakovich*, *Aaron Copland*, and *Leonard Bernstein* revitalized the form with new expressive possibilities.
