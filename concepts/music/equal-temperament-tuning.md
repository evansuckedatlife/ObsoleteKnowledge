## summary

**Equal temperament tuning** is an acoustic tuning system that divides an octave into twelve logarithmically equal semitones, each corresponding to a frequency ratio of precisely the twelfth root of two. Developed mathematically across the sixteenth through eighteenth centuries in Europe and China, it superseded older historical temperaments such as Pythagorean tuning, just intonation, and meantone temperaments. It is fundamental to modern music because it permits unlimited harmonic modulation between keys on fixed-pitch instruments without causing dissonant wolf intervals.

## you gotta know

- Divides each octave into twelve equal intervals of one hundred cents, ensuring that every chromatic key sounds acoustically equivalent in relative intervallic structure.
- Solves the ancient problem of the Pythagorean comma, the slight acoustic discrepancy that accumulates when stacking twelve pure fifths against seven pure octaves.
- Eliminates the harsh "wolf intervals" that rendered distant or heavily sharped and flatted keys unusable in rigid meantone temperaments.
- Developed independently in late sixteenth-century China by scholar Zhu Zaiyu and in Europe by mathematicians Simon Stevin and Vincenzo Galilei.
- Paved the way for complex harmonic modulation across all twenty-four major and minor keys, establishing the foundation of Western tonal harmony.
- Johann Sebastian Bach championed flexible well-temperament rather than strict equal temperament in *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, though popular history frequently conflates the two.
- Sacrifices the acoustic purity of natural intervals, leaving equal-tempered major thirds noticeably sharper and minor thirds flatter than their natural harmonic overtones.
