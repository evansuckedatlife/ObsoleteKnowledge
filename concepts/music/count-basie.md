## summary

**Count Basie** (1904–1984), born William James Basie, was an American pianist and bandleader whose orchestra became one of the defining engines of the swing era. With his impeccable sense of rhythm and minimalist piano touch, he led one of jazz's greatest ensembles, featuring legendary soloists and the riffing big-band sound that made swing accessible to millions. He remained an active force in jazz for five decades, cementing his status as a peer to *Duke Ellington* and *Louis Armstrong*.

## you gotta know

- Started as a vaudeville performer and pianist in the 1920s; formed his famous orchestra in 1935 in Kansas City.
- Famous for his economy on the piano—playing spare, perfectly-timed chords rather than elaborate passages; rhythm was his instrument.
- Led one of the greatest big bands: the "All-American Rhythm Section" and soloists like *Lester Young* (tenor saxophone) and *Buck Clayton* (trumpet).
- Recordings like "One O'Clock Jump" and "April in Paris" became jazz standards; his blues feel was instantly recognizable.
- Competed directly with *Duke Ellington* for prestige, but offered a leaner, swinging alternative to Ellington's lush orchestration.
- Toured extensively, bringing swing to millions; more commercially successful than many contemporaries, yet never losing critical respect.
- The band continued performing into the 1980s; Basie was one of jazz's most durable and consistent figures whose influence on rhythm-section playing remains unmatched in jazz history.
