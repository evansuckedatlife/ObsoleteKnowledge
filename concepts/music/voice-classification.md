## summary

**Voice classification** is the categorical system for organizing human singing voices according to their natural range, timbre, and placement of resonance. The standard Western classical taxonomy divides voices into six primary categories—soprano, mezzo-soprano, and alto for females; tenor, baritone, and bass for males—each with distinct acoustic properties and typical roles in opera, choral, and art-song repertoire. This system enables composers to write for specific voices and singers to understand their vocal identity.

## you gotta know

- Voice classification is based on three criteria: *range* (the span of pitches a voice can sing), *timbre* (the quality or color of the voice), and *fach* (German term for "type," describing the category of roles a voice is suited for).
- *Soprano* is the highest female voice, characterized by brightness and agility; *mezzo-soprano* is the middle female voice, darker and warmer; *alto* (or contralto) is the lowest female voice, with a rich, deep tone.
- *Tenor* is the highest male voice, capable of lyrical sweetness or dramatic power; *baritone* is the male middle voice, combining warmth with dramatic flexibility; *bass* is the lowest male voice, with profound resonance.
- In operatic tradition, a singer's fach determines the roles they perform; for example, a "lyric soprano" sings different repertoire from a "dramatic soprano" or "coloratura soprano," each with distinct technical demands.
- Historical practices significantly altered voice classification: *castrato* singers (males castrated before puberty) occupied soprano and alto ranges and were prized for their power and agility in Baroque opera.
- The classification system varies across musical traditions; non-Western classical music systems may organize voices differently based on their specific melodic, harmonic, and performance practices.
- Composers from Mozart to Verdi to Wagner carefully specified vocal types for each operatic role, understanding that a voice's natural category affects both its technical possibilities and its expressive character.
