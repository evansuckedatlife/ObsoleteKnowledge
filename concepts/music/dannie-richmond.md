## summary

**Dannie Richmond** (born **Charles Daniel Richmond**) was an American jazz drummer celebrated for his extraordinary, decades-long musical partnership with the visionary bassist and composer *Charles Mingus*. Emerging onto the mid-century *[[new-york-city]]* post-bop scene, he served as Mingus's indispensable rhythmic counterpart, dynamic confidant, and stylistic anchor from 1956 until Mingus's death in 1979. His uncanny capacity to steer abrupt tempo shifts, metric modulations, and fiery polyrhythmic turbulence cemented his reputation as one of the most versatile and expressive percussionists in modern American music.

## you gotta know

- Began his musical career not as a drummer but as a rhythm-and-blues tenor saxophonist, switching to the drum kit in his early twenties only six months before his fateful audition for *Charles Mingus*.
- Mingus famously hired him after recognizing his innate rhythmic intuition and swing, forging an intuitive, telepathic connection that anchored landmark albums like *Mingus Ah Um*, *The Black Saint and the Sinner Lady*, and *Blues & Roots*.
- Acted as the essential stabilizing conductor for Mingus's sprawling extended forms, guiding ensembles through sudden accelerandos, decelerandos, and radical rubato passages without ever losing pulse or momentum.
- Featured prominently during Mingus's triumphant 1964 European tour alongside woodwind innovator *[[eric-dolphy]]*, creating intense, boundary-pushing concert performances that challenged the line between hard bop and the avant-garde.
- Provided explosive rhythmic commentary on politically charged civil rights anthems, adding biting accents to satire pieces like *Fables of Faubus* that directly protested racial segregation and state violence in the *[[united-states]]*.
- Performed and recorded broadly outside the Mingus circle, collaborating with soul-jazz saxophonist *Joe Henderson*, trumpet icon *Chet Baker*, and pop-rock artists like *Mark-Almond* and *Elton John*.
- Served as the musical director of the *Mingus Dynasty* and the *Mingus Big Band* repertory ensembles following Mingus's passing, preserving his mentor's complex scores and mentoring younger generations of jazz musicians.
