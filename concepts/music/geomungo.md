## summary

The **geomungo** (also called the **hyeongeum**) is a traditional Korean six-string plucked zither featuring both raised wooden frets and movable bridges. Originating in the ancient kingdom of Goguryeo, the instrument is played by striking and plucking twisted silk strings with a slender bamboo rod called a *suldae*. Revered by Confucian literati as the premier vehicle for spiritual self-cultivation, it forms an essential pillar of court and folk traditions in [[korea]], prized for its austere, percussive timbre.

## you gotta know

- Unique playing technique using a *suldae*: Unlike the finger-plucked [[gayageum]], the performer strikes and plucks the heavy silk strings using a pencil-sized bamboo plectrum held between the index and middle fingers of the right hand.
- Legendary Goguryeo origin story: According to the twelfth-century chronicle *Samguk sagi*, prime minister *Wang San-ak* invented the instrument by redesigning a Chinese [[guqin]]; when he played it, black cranes reputedly descended to dance, inspiring the name *hyeongeum* ("black crane zither").
- Hybrid fret and bridge anatomy: The body of aged paulownia and chestnut wood features sixteen tall, fixed wooden frets (*gwae*) under three melody strings alongside three movable bridges (*anjok*) shaped like wild geese that support the drone and bass strings.
- Distinct percussive slap dynamic: Striking down across the strings with the *suldae* causes the bamboo stick to strike the leather protective pad (*daejeon*) affixed to the soundboard, producing a crisp percussive crack integral to each phrase.
- Pitch bending and microtonal nuance: Performers use the fingertips of the left hand to press, drag, and push the thick twisted silk strings across the wide frets, generating wide vibrato and expressive microtonal shifts known as *nonghyeon*.
- Aristocratic prestige in Joseon society: Confucian scholars (*seonbi*) regarded the instrument as the noblest musical apparatus, playing it in private retreats for meditation and moral refinement rather than public performance.
- Radical folk adaptation through [[sanjo]]: In the late nineteenth century, musician *Baek Nak-jun* adapted the austere scholar instrument into the emotionally charged, virtuosic folk solo genre known as [[sanjo]], defying conservative aristocratic conventions.
- Evolution in contemporary composition: While traditional [[gugak]] ensembles preserve the historic six-string design, modern composers create pieces for modified variants with up to sixteen strings to expand chromatic range for intercultural orchestral collaboration.

## connections

- [[gayageum]] — the brighter, twelve-string zither plucked with bare fingers that serves as the feminine counterpart in traditional string duos.
- [[gugak]] — the overarching tradition of Korean music encompassing both the aristocratic court suites and virtuosic folk styles played on the zither.
- [[sanjo]] — the dramatic, multi-movement solo instrumental form pioneered on the instrument by *Baek Nak-jun* during the late nineteenth century.
- [[janggu]] — the double-headed hourglass drum whose shifting rhythmic cycles provide essential accompaniment for solo suites and ensemble performances.
- [[daegeum]] — the large transverse bamboo flute whose buzzing membrane and sustained tones regularly blend with the zither in chamber music.
- [[korea]] — the homeland where the instrument developed over more than fifteen centuries as a central symbol of indigenous musical heritage.
- [[improvisation]] — the core musical practice foundational to the development of solo folk suites before the spread of Western notation.
- [[classical-music]] — the broader art music tradition that frames both historic East Asian court repertoires and contemporary avant-garde compositions.
