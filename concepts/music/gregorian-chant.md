## summary

**Gregorian chant** (also *plainsong* or *plainchant*) is the monophonic liturgical singing tradition of the Roman Catholic Church, codified in the 6th–8th centuries and traditionally attributed to *Pope Gregory I*, though modern scholarship shows it developed gradually. Sung in unison without instrumental accompaniment, Gregorian chant uses Latin texts, modal melodies, and a free, speech-like rhythm aligned to liturgical needs rather than metrical regularity.

## you gotta know

- Gregorian chant emerged from earlier Christian liturgical traditions and Jewish cantorial practice; it became the standardized music for the Mass and Office (daily prayers) throughout the medieval Catholic Church.
- The tradition was *monophonic*—a single melodic line sung by all voices in unison—as opposed to the later development of polyphonic (multi-voiced) church music; this allowed congregational participation and clarity of the sacred text.
- Notation evolved from early neumes (symbolic marks indicating pitch direction without specifying absolute pitch) to the four-line staff, allowing exact transcription and transmission of melodies across centuries.
- Gregorian chant uses the eight *church modes* (also called Gregorian modes), each with a different final note and range, somewhat analogous to minor and major keys but with distinct characteristics; these modes carry liturgical significance.
- The rhythm of Gregorian chant is *free* and *prose-like*, not metrically regular; singers follow the natural phrasing of Latin text and liturgical function, allowing for rubato and expressive freedom within the fixed melodic skeleton.
- Some composers working in the plainsong tradition expanded its expressive range through subtle ornamentation and elaborate melismatic passages (multiple notes on a single syllable), while maintaining the unaccompanied, monophonic character.
- Gregorian chant had declined by the 19th century but was revived in the late 1800s as the Church sought to restore liturgical authenticity; modern Catholic Mass still uses simplified Gregorian melodies.
