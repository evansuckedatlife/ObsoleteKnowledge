# Baroque Music

## summary

**Baroque music** is the Western classical music style spanning roughly 1600–1750, emerging from the Renaissance and preceding the Classical era. Characterized by ornate melody, complex counterpoint, dramatic contrast, and the emergence of instrumental music alongside vocal forms, Baroque music represents a shift toward emotional expressivity and formal innovation.

## you gotta know

- The term "Baroque" (from Portuguese *barroco*, meaning "irregular pearl") was applied retrospectively in the 20th century; the period encompasses *Monteverdi*, *Handel*, *Bach*, *Vivaldi*, and many others.
- Baroque music emphasized *affections*—the expression of distinct emotional states through melody, harmony, and rhythm; a single movement might express one dominant emotion rather than multiple moods.
- *Opera* emerged as a major art form in the Baroque, pioneered by *Monteverdi*; works like *L'Orfeo* (1607) combined dramatic text, singing, instrumental accompaniment, and spectacle.
- The *basso continuo* (figured bass) became standard: a keyboard player and bass instrument (cello or bassoon) provided harmonic foundation, allowing composers to notate only the melody line and harmonic figures.
- *Counterpoint*—the art of combining independent melodic lines—reached its apex in Baroque music; *Bach*'s fugues are the supreme examples, with multiple voices weaving together in strict mathematical complexity.
- Instrumental music gained independence from vocal music; the concerto, suite, and sonata forms developed; composers wrote specifically for violin, harpsichord, and organ rather than adapting vocal music to instruments.
- Ornamental embellishment was prized; melodies featured trills, turns, and elaborate runs; performers were expected to improvise ornamentation, making notation a skeleton rather than a fixed score.
- Major Baroque composers include *Johann Sebastian Bach* (fugues, cantatas, *The Art of Fugue*), *George Frideric Handel* (*Water Music*, *Messiah*), *Antonio Vivaldi* (*The Four Seasons*), and *Henry Purcell*.
