## summary

**Sacred music** encompasses all compositions created for worship, prayer, and spiritual contemplation across religions and cultures. From Gregorian chant to polyphonic masses to contemporary spiritual compositions, sacred music serves the highest human longings: to transcend the material world, to express communal faith, and to honor the divine. Western classical music was born largely from sacred music; the greatest composers from Bach to Mozart invested enormous genius in works created for church use.

## you gotta know

- Sacred music predates written notation; chant traditions (Gregorian, Byzantine, Jewish cantorial) developed orally over centuries.
- The Catholic mass became the primary large form in Western sacred music, standardizing texts and structure across centuries.
- Polyphonic sacred music reached supreme heights in the Renaissance with composers like Palestrina and Josquin des Prez, balancing multiple independent vocal lines.
- The Reformation created new sacred traditions: Lutheran chorales, English anthems, and Protestant hymn singing became vehicles for theological instruction.
- The Requiem (Mass for the Dead) became a major compositional project: Mozart, Verdi, and Brahms created monumental requiems blending liturgy with concert ambition.
- Sacred music extended beyond Christianity: Jewish liturgical music, Islamic quranic recitation, and Hindu devotional music represent independent sacred traditions.
- The rise of secular concert halls in the nineteenth century separated sacred music from its liturgical context, yet composers continued writing for worship.

## connections

- [[liturgical-music]] — music explicitly composed for church services and ritual use.
- [[mass-form]] — the largest and most formal sacred genre in the Catholic tradition.
- [[motet]] — shorter polyphonic sacred works, often for choir or small ensemble.
- [[gregorian-chant]] — the foundational monophonic sacred tradition of Western Christianity.
- [[requiem]] — the Mass for the Dead, a major sacred form that attracted great composers.
- [[hymn]] — congregational sacred song, central to Protestant and other traditions.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition that generated most Western sacred music.
