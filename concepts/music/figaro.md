## summary

**Figaro** is an iconic fictional valet and barber created in eighteenth-century *[[france]]* by the dramatist *Pierre Beaumarchais*, who became one of the most celebrated figures in world literature and *[[opera]]*. Originating in a theatrical trilogy set in Seville, *[[spain]]*, the character embodies the quintessential wily trickster whose resourcefulness consistently outwits his aristocratic superiors. As an emblem of the social ferment of the *[[enlightenment]]*, his comedic resistance against feudal privilege made him an enduring symbol in both dramatic history and *[[classical-music]]*.

## you gotta know

- Created by French playwright *Pierre Beaumarchais* across a renowned theatrical trilogy: *Le Barbier de Séville* (1775), *Le Mariage de Figaro* (1784), and *La Mère coupable* (1792).
- Serves as the title protagonist of *Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart's* opera buffa *Le nozze di Figaro* (1786), with a renowned libretto written by *Lorenzo Da Ponte*.
- Features as the quick-witted barber of Seville in *Gioachino Rossini's* beloved comic masterpiece *Il barbiere di Siviglia* (1816), introduced by the celebrated patter aria *Largo al factotum*.
- Outwits the aristocrat Count Almaviva in *The Marriage of Figaro*, successfully defending his betrothed, the maid Susanna, from the Count's efforts to invoke the archaic *droit du seigneur*.
- Delivers a famous fourth-act monologue in the original Beaumarchais play attacking hereditary nobility, declaring that the aristocracy merely took the trouble to be born.
- Uncovers the surprising secret of his parentage during a courtroom trial, revealing that his true parents are the housekeeper Marcellina and the elderly doctor Bartolo.
- Inspired multiple musical treatments in the tradition of *[[french-opera]]* and European theater, including an earlier 1782 setting by *Giovanni Paisiello*.
- Emphasized Enlightenment ideals of merit, cunning, and human dignity over feudal hierarchy and arbitrary class privilege on the eve of the French Revolution.
