## summary

**Gioachino Rossini** was an Italian composer who, in the early nineteenth century, became the defining voice of comic opera and a founder of bel canto, the "beautiful singing" aesthetic. Brilliant at orchestration and witty melody, he composed nearly forty operas before retiring at thirty-seven, his career cut short by theatrical fashion and his own temperament. *The Barber of Seville* remains one of the world's most performed operas, its charm and virtuosity undiminished after two centuries.

## you gotta know

- Born in Pesaro in 1792; rapidly became the most celebrated opera composer of his generation across Europe.
- Mastered both comic opera (*buffa*) and serious opera (*seria*), but achieved immortal fame through comedy.
- *The Barber of Seville* (1816) premiered to mixed reviews but became his greatest triumph, now the cornerstone of the operatic repertoire.
- Created the Rossini crescendo: a simple musical phrase repeated and layered with increasing instrumentation, building enormous theatrical momentum.
- Wrote for brilliant soprano voices, creating vocal lines of extraordinary agility demanding precision, breath control, and ornamental flourish.
- Composed the opera *Guillaume Tell* (William Tell) in 1829, whose overture became the most famous operatic prelude of the era.
- Retired from opera in 1829 at the height of fame, living quietly in Paris and writing church music; his reasons remain partly mysterious.
