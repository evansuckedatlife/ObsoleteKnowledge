## summary

**Ed Sheeran** is an English singer-songwriter and acoustic pop musician whose melodic sensibility and rhythmic loop-pedal performances elevated him into one of the best-selling recording artists of the twenty-first century. Rising from independent busking and London club circuits in [[england]], he achieved international dominance through mathematically titled albums such as *+* (*Plus*), *×* (*Multiply*), and *÷* (*Divide*). His mastery of folk balladry, contemporary urban production, and arena-filling stadium arrangements has made his catalogue a perennial staple of modern competitive music trivia and record-breaking streaming charts.

## you gotta know

- Broke global commercial records with the quadruple-meter pop single *Shape of You*, which became one of the most-streamed individual tracks in Spotify history alongside milestones by [[drake]].
- Built his signature live performance aesthetic around the solo use of acoustic guitars and sophisticated multi-channel loop stations rather than a conventional backing band.
- Earned worldwide acclaim for acoustic wedding anthems such as *Thinking Out Loud* and *Perfect*, the latter featuring lush orchestral arrangements that incorporate the soaring countermelodies of the [[cello]].
- Gained early critical recognition after independently releasing *The Orange Room* and *No. 5 Collaborations Project*, leading to mentorship from Jamie Foxx and commercial signing in the [[united-states]].
- Triumphed in high-profile copyright trials in New York federal court, successfully defending the fundamental four-chord progression and syncopated rhythm of *Thinking Out Loud* against claims of infringing Marvin Gaye's *Let's Get It On*.
- Co-wrote major international chart-toppers for prominent industry peers, penning Justin Bieber's acoustic ballad *Love Yourself* and collaborating extensively across hip-hop, grime, and folk genres.
- Released the introspective 2023 studio album *-* (*Subtract*), recorded with producer Aaron Dessner, which departed from stadium-oriented pop in favor of intimate acoustic arrangements and somber cello accompaniments.
