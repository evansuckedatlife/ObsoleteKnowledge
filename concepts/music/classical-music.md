## summary

**Classical music** refers to the musical period and aesthetic that emerged in the mid-18th century as a reaction against the complexity and ornamentation of Baroque styles. Centered on clarity, symmetry, and emotional restraint, it dominated European concert life from roughly 1750 to 1820, establishing the symphony, concerto, and sonata as the defining large-scale forms. The era produced some of history's most enduring masterpieces and established many conventions of harmony and structure that persist in concert music today.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as the "galant" style in mid-18th-century Europe, prioritizing melodic clarity over Baroque polyphonic density.
- Built on the harmonic vocabulary of tonality but refined it through strict formal designs: sonata form, rondo, theme-and-variations.
- The symphony and solo concerto became the prestige genres, performed in public concert halls rather than aristocratic chambers.
- *Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart*, *Ludwig van Beethoven*, and *Joseph Haydn* were the towering figures; each expanded the form in different directions.
- "Classical" balance meant a four-square, memorable melody supported by simple harmonic progressions—the opposite of Baroque's learned counterpoint.
- The period ended around 1820 as Romantic composers like *Schubert* and *Beethoven's* late works began abandoning strict formality for emotional depth and orchestral color.
- Major centers: Vienna (Austria), Mannheim (Germany), and Paris, where court orchestras championed the new style.
- Classical composers elevated the piano from accompaniment to solo instrument, creating a new market for domestic music-making among the educated middle class.
- The form stripped away the elaborate ornamentation of the Baroque and prioritized the direct, unadorned expression of a single melodic idea, making music more accessible to wider audiences.
