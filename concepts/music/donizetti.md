## summary

**Gaetano Donizetti** (1797–1848) was an Italian composer and master of the *bel canto* vocal tradition, one of the most prolific opera composers of the 19th century. Working primarily in Italy and across Europe, Donizetti composed over 60 operas—a staggering output balancing comedic brilliance with genuine emotional depth. His career peaked in the 1830s and 1840s, though illness and personal tragedy cut his life short at 50, during a period of creative decline that troubles many accounts of his legacy.

## you gotta know

- *L'elisir d'amore* (1832; *The Elixir of Love*) is his comic masterpiece, a *buffa* opera blending romance, humor, and sentiment, famous for the tenor aria *Una furtiva lagrima* (a tear that steals) and its effortless melody that defines operatic lyricism; audiences and singers adore its accessibility and emotional warmth.
- *Lucia di Lammermoor* (1835) is his masterwork in the tragic genre, featuring one of opera's most demanding soprano roles and the celebrated *mad scene* where Lucia's mental collapse is depicted through virtuosic vocal display; it inspired countless 19th-century imitations and remains a soprano showcase.
- *La favola* (1830) and *The Daughter of the Regiment* (1840) showcase his gift for comic, rapid-paced ensemble writing and brilliant buffo (comic bass) roles, establishing Donizetti as a master of both tragic depth and comedic brilliance.
- Donizetti had an enormous Italian fanbase and was celebrated across Europe; he served briefly as master of the royal chapel in Naples and earned international commissions from Paris, London, and Vienna, though he remained based primarily in Italy and France throughout his career.
- His prolific output included not only operas but also chamber music, songs, and sacred works; the sheer volume of composition—over 60 operas in fewer than 30 years—was considered remarkable even by 19th-century standards of productivity.
- His operas bridged the earlier Classical *bel canto* aesthetics of Rossini and Bellini with the emerging *Romantic* era's emphasis on emotional intensity and orchestral depth, making him a transitional figure who influenced Verdi's development of opera.
- By the 1840s, Donizetti suffered from syphilis, which caused progressive paralysis and mental deterioration; he spent his final years in a psychiatric hospital in Bergamo, dying in mental and physical decline, a tragic end that has shadowed posthumous assessments of his genius.
