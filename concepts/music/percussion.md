## summary

**Percussion** encompasses the family of musical instruments sounded by striking, shaking, rubbing, or scraping, forming the oldest and most geographically pervasive instrument class in human history. From prehistoric ritual rattles to the specialized batteries of European [[classical-music]] and the polyrhythmic traditions of [[west-africa]], these instruments establish the primary framework for musical meter and [[rhythm]]. In both traditional art forms and modern popular genres, percussion provides an indispensable foundation of timing while serving as an expressive vehicle for dynamic color, timbre, and spontaneous [[improvisation]].

## you gotta know

- Organologically classified within the *Hornbostel-Sachs* system into membranophones, which produce sound via a stretched membrane over a resonating shell, and idiophones, which produce sound through the vibration of the instrument's primary material without requiring strings or membranes.
- Timpani became the foundational orchestral percussion instruments in European [[classical-music]] during the era of [[baroque-music]], valued for their ability to project precise pitches tuned to the tonic and dominant notes of a composition.
- The standard drum kit evolved in the early twentieth-century [[united-states]] as dance bands and early [[jazz]] ensembles combined bass drums, snare drums, and cymbals into a unified apparatus operated by a single player using drumsticks, wire brushes, and foot pedals.
- In [[hindustani-classical-music]], rhythmic cycles known as [[tala]] are maintained by drums such as the two-piece *tabla* and the double-headed *pakhawaj*, which require intricate finger techniques and vocalized phonetic patterns called *bols* to articulate complex temporal divisions.
- Ensemble traditions in [[west-african-drumming]] organize multiple performers into interlocking polyrhythms guided by a steady iron bell pattern, utilizing instruments such as the *djembe* and variable-tension talking drums preserved by hereditary musical storytellers known as the [[griot]].
- Romantic and modern orchestral composers substantially broadened the percussion section, with [[pyotr-ilyich-tchaikovsky]] introducing the bell-like celesta into ballet scoring and [[igor-stravinsky]] deploying asymmetric meters, relentless timpani attacks, and primitive percussive textures in *The Rite of Spring* during the rise of musical [[modernism]].
- The twentieth-century avant-garde established percussion as an autonomous concert medium, marked by *Edgard Varèse*'s pioneering 1931 piece *Ionisation* for thirteen percussionists playing thirty-seven instruments, which eliminated melodic development in favor of pure rhythm and acoustic texture.

## connections

- [[rhythm]] — the foundational temporal element of music structured through pulse, meter, and syncopated accents articulated by percussion.
- [[classical-music]] — the European art tradition that developed sophisticated orchestral percussion sections from Baroque timpani to massive modernist batteries.
- [[jazz]] — the twentieth-century American genre that revolutionized drum kit playing through swing phrasing, ride cymbal patterns, and polyrhythmic interplay.
- [[tala]] — the cyclical metric system in Indian music that guides drum accompaniment and rapid rhythmic calculations.
- [[hindustani-classical-music]] — the northern Indian tradition whose classical performances balance melodic elaboration with virtuosic *tabla* accompaniment.
- [[west-african-drumming]] — communal musical traditions built upon layered polyrhythms, call-and-response dynamics, and talking drum communication.
- [[igor-stravinsky]] — composer whose ground-breaking ballet scores placed percussive dissonance and jagged rhythmic accents at the forefront of orchestral composition.
- [[improvisation]] — the spontaneous creation of rhythmic variations, cadenzas, and drum solos across global and jazz performance traditions.
