## summary

An **accidental** is a musical notation symbol—principally the sharp, flat, or natural—placed immediately before a notehead to temporarily raise, lower, or restore its pitch outside the established key signature. Originating in medieval music notation to resolve melodic tritones and soften hexachords, the system expanded alongside Western [[polyphony]] and early [[tonality]]. Mastering the function of an **accidental** is fundamental to understanding chromaticism, modulation, and pitch architecture across European art music and global traditions.

## you gotta know

- The three primary standard symbols are the *sharp* (which raises a pitch by one half step), the *flat* (which lowers a pitch by one half step), and the *natural* (which cancels any preceding sharp or flat).
- Under modern conventions established in the common practice era of [[classical-music]], an accidental applies to all subsequent occurrences of that specific pitch within the same measure, unless explicitly cancelled or altered by a new symbol.
- *Double sharps* (notated as a stylized cross) raise a pitch by two half steps, whereas *double flats* (notated as two conjoined flat signs) lower a pitch by two half steps, preserving proper diatonic spelling during harmonic transitions.
- The historical root of all modern accidentals lies in medieval [[gregorian-chant]], where scribes employed two forms of the letter *B*—the square *b durum* and the rounded *b molle*—to avoid the dissonant tritone known as the *diabolus in musica*.
- The graphic design of modern symbols evolved directly from these medieval script forms: the natural sign and sharp sign derived from *b durum*, while the modern flat sign evolved directly from *b molle*.
- In the vocal polyphony of the [[renaissance]], unnotated chromatic alterations were often applied implicitly by singers according to unwritten conventions, a practice known as *musica ficta*.
- The advent of twentieth-century [[modernism]] and microtonal composition spurred the creation of extended accidentals, such as quarter-tone sharps and flats, used by avant-garde composers like *[[igor-stravinsky]]*.
- A *courtesy accidental* (or *cautionary accidental*) is placed in parentheses to remind performers of a pitch's true status after a barline or following an ambiguous chromatic shift, preventing performance errors in complex scores.

## connections

- [[tonality]] — provides the diatonic harmonic framework where accidentals function as departures, chromatic inflections, or pivot points for modulation.
- [[classical-music]] — codified the strict measure-based conventions and resolution expectations governing accidentals in orchestral and chamber scores.
- [[polyphony]] — necessitated early chromatic adjustments to prevent harsh voice-leading clashes and parallel dissonances between counter-melodies.
- [[counterpoint]] — governs the precise linear rules, such as raising leading tones at cadence points, that demand chromatic accidentals.
- [[gregorian-chant]] — birthed the earliest proto-accidentals through the distinction between hard and soft variants of the pitch *B*.
- [[baroque-music]] — expanded equal temperament and figured bass notation, leading to ubiquitous accidental usage in dramatic recitatives.
- [[igor-stravinsky]] — pushed harmonic language beyond traditional systems, employing dense clusters of conflicting accidentals in landmark modernist works.
- [[renaissance]] — relied heavily on implied accidentals through the customary performance traditions of *musica ficta*.
