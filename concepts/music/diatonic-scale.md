## summary

A **diatonic scale** is a fundamental seven-pitch musical scale constructed from five whole steps and two half steps within an octave, where the two half steps are separated from each other by at least two whole steps. Rooted in the theoretical traditions of [[ancient-greece]] and codified through medieval [[gregorian-chant]], it forms the foundational pitch collection of Western art music, folk idioms, and modern popular song. Its intrinsic asymmetrical arrangement of intervals provides the perceptual hierarchy necessary for functional [[tonality]] and modal organization.

## you gotta know

- Consists of seven distinct pitch classes per octave arranged in a pattern of five whole tones (T) and two semitones (S), such as the major scale sequence T-T-S-T-T-T-S.
- Forms the white keys of a modern piano keyboard when played starting on the note C, representing the natural major scale without sharps or flats.
- Serves as the basis for the eight traditional church modes in medieval Europe, which derived distinct melodic flavors simply by rotating the starting pitch along the same diatonic collection.
- Can be generated by stacking six consecutive perfect fifths (for example, F-C-G-D-A-E-B) and collapsing the resulting pitches into a single octave.
- Possesses the property of unique interval vector distribution, meaning that every interval size from minor second to tritone occurs a distinct number of times, establishing an unmistakable tonal center.
- Contrasts directly with the chromatic scale, which divides the octave into twelve equal semitones, and the whole-tone scale, which lacks the semitonal tension that drives harmonic resolution.
- Underpins the harmonic syntax of common-practice Western [[classical-music]], enabling functional progressions based on tonic, subdominant, and dominant chords.

## connections

- [[tonality]] — the hierarchical harmonic system that relies directly on diatonic asymmetry to establish a home key.
- [[gregorian-chant]] — the liturgical monophonic tradition whose modal system was derived entirely from diatonic pitch sets.
- [[classical-music]] — the broader tradition whose melodic, contrapuntal, and harmonic languages are organized around diatonic scales.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the civilization whose theorists, notably *Pythagoras* and *Aristoxenus*, first systematized diatonic genera.
- [[polyphony]] — multi-voiced vocal and instrumental writing governed by diatonic intervals and voice-leading rules.
- [[counterpoint]] — the art of combining distinct melodic lines guided by diatonic consonance and dissonance.
