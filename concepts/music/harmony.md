## summary

**Harmony** refers to the combination of simultaneous notes and the system governing which note combinations create coherence, tension, and resolution in music. From the perspective of tonality, harmony organizes pitch relationships through chords, progressions, and functional relationships that create musical architecture and emotional effect. Harmony is foundational to Western Classical music, jazz, and most popular music traditions, providing the vertical dimension that complements melody's horizontal unfolding.

## you gotta know

- Harmony operates through *chords*—simultaneous pitch combinations (typically three or more notes); a triad (three notes spaced in thirds) is the basic harmonic building block in Western music.
- *Harmonic function* describes the role a chord plays in a progression: the tonic (home chord, providing stability), subdominant (creating tension or modulation), and dominant (creating tension that demands resolution back to tonic).
- A *chord progression* is a sequence of chords; the most common progression in Western music is the *I-IV-V-I* (tonic-subdominant-dominant-tonic), which underlies countless folk songs, pop songs, and classical works.
- *Harmonic rhythm* (the speed at which harmonies change) is independent of melodic rhythm; a slowly changing harmony beneath a fast melody creates textural complexity; rapid harmonic change beneath a simple melody creates urgency.
- *Consonance* (intervals and chords that sound stable and resolved) and *dissonance* (intervals that sound tense and demand resolution) are relative concepts that shifted historically; major and minor thirds were dissonant in medieval music but consonant by the Renaissance.
- In jazz and blues, harmony is often understood through *chord changes*—abstract patterns (not rooted in functional tonality) that musicians improvise over; the harmonic structure is the skeleton for improvisation.
- *Parallel harmony* (all voices moving together in the same direction) contrasts with *contrary harmony* (voices moving in opposite directions), affecting the perceptual clarity and independence of melodic lines.

- [[polyphony]] — the broader context in which harmonic relationships unfold across multiple voices.
