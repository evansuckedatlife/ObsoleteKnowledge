## summary

**Modal music** is a system of melody and improvisation organized around *modes*—recurring melodic and harmonic frameworks distinct from Western major and minor tonality. Prevalent in Indian classical music (ragas), Persian/Arabic music (dastgah, maqam), and many non-Western traditions, modal systems offer a fundamentally different approach to pitch organization. Rather than functional harmony progressing toward tonal centers, modal music explores the subtle emotional and coloristic possibilities of a single pitch framework.

## you gotta know

- In Western medieval and Renaissance music, modes (Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, etc.) preceded the major-minor tonality system; some musicologists use "modal" to describe pre-tonal European music.
- Non-Western modal systems are independent of Western tonality and reflect different aesthetic priorities: color, ornamentation, and spiritual resonance over harmonic progression.
- *Ragas* in Hindustani classical music are intricate melodic frameworks with prescribed ascending/descending patterns, emotional associations, and time-of-day usage.
- *Dastgah* in Persian music and *maqam* in Arabic music similarly organize melody around a tonal center, embellished with microtonal inflections (quarter-tones) unavailable in Western tuning.
- Modal improvisation often involves exploring a single pitch framework deeply rather than modulating through multiple keys as in Western jazz or classical forms.
- Western jazz musicians, especially after the 1950s, adopted modal thinking from Indian and African music, creating a synthesis (e.g., *Miles Davis*' *Kind of Blue*).
- The distinction between modal and tonal music is philosophical as much as technical: modal systems prioritize *color* and *emotion* within a framework; tonal systems prioritize *functional harmony*.

## connections

- [[tonality]] — the Western system from which modal music fundamentally differs.
- [[raga]] — the Indian classical modal framework, the most documented non-Western mode system.

- [[tala]] — the rhythmic cycle that accompanies raga improvisation.
