## summary

**Mezzo-soprano** (literally "half-soprano" in Italian) is the female voice type between soprano and alto, commanding a warm, robust middle-to-lower range ideal for heroic and character roles. Though ranked as secondary to the soprano in Classical opera hierarchies, mezzo-sopranos have created some of opera's most memorable performances: as witches, warriors, seductresses, and complex protagonists. The voice type rose in prestige through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries as composers recognized its unique dramatic power.

## you gotta know

- The mezzo-soprano range typically spans from around A3 to E6, lying lower than soprano but higher than alto.
- Naturally produced chest resonance gives mezzo-sopranos a distinctive warm, penetrating timbre suited to dramatic intensity.
- Mezzo roles in Classical opera were often secondary—nurses, confidantes—but the Romantic era elevated them to leads.
- Carmen in Bizet's *Carmen* remains the iconic mezzo role: a free-spirited, dangerous woman whose low range carries seductive power.
- The *Habanera*, Carmen's most famous aria, sits in the mezzo's sweet spot and requires both lyrical beauty and fierce personality.
- Composers from Rossini to Verdi to Wagner wrote substantial mezzo roles, particularly for male-to-female vocal dynamics and dramatic variety.
- Modern opera houses feature prominent mezzo-sopranos performing roles traditionally sung by both female and male singers (trouser roles).
