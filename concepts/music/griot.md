## summary

**Griot** (also *grotte* or *griotte* for female performers) is a West African musician-historian and professional storyteller, traditionally functioning as a keeper and transmitter of oral history, genealogy, and cultural memory. Griots combine musical performance with narrative artistry, playing instruments like the kora (harp-lute) or djembe while singing or reciting histories, praise-songs, and moral tales. In West African societies, griots hold an elevated social status as cultural custodians and advisors to nobility, making them essential to the preservation and transmission of pre-literate history.

## you gotta know

- The griot tradition is central to West African cultures, particularly in Mali, Senegal, Guinea, and neighboring regions.
- Griots are trained from childhood in musicianship, languages, historical narratives, and genealogies, forming apprenticeships that pass knowledge across generations.
- A griot's repertoire includes praise-songs for patrons, historical accounts of royal lineages, moral tales, and commentary on contemporary events.
- The kora (a 21-string harp-lute) and ngoni (a smaller harp) are the primary instruments of griot tradition, though some griots play djembe or other drums.
- In traditional West African courts, griots served as advisors, poets, and memory-keepers, their words carrying legal and political weight.
- The term *griot* likely derives from French colonial pronunciation of the Mandinka *guiriot* or Wolof *gewar*, reflecting the trade's international reach.
- Modern griots maintain traditional repertoires while adapting to contemporary contexts, including world music performance and cultural education.
- Griots held a complex social position: honored for their knowledge and performance, yet sometimes considered outsiders due to their hereditary occupation and association with patronage networks.
- The griot's memory is trained to extraordinary capacity; some griots can recite genealogies spanning centuries with precise detail, functioning as living historical records.
- Female griots (griottes or griots) play equally important roles as male griots, though sometimes with distinct performance contexts; some specialize in particular genres or patron relationships.
- Griots use formulaic patterns and improvisation together: memorized traditional narratives are embellished with contemporary commentary, allowing narratives to remain living, evolving traditions.
- The griot tradition is recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage, protecting the transmission of oral traditions in a world increasingly dominated by written records and digital media.
- Modern griots like *Ali Farka Touré* and *Oumou Sangaré* have brought traditional repertoires to global audiences through recordings and performances, creating cross-cultural dialogue.
