## summary

A **castrato** (plural: castrati) is a male singer castrated before puberty to preserve a high, soprano or alto register while retaining the physical strength and vocal power of an adult male. Castration was performed in Italy from roughly the 16th century onward, reaching its peak in the 17th and 18th centuries, when castrati dominated operatic leading roles and became celebrities commanding enormous fees. The practice declined sharply in the 19th century as social attitudes shifted, though a handful of castrati survived into the early 20th century, creating the only audio recordings we have of the sound.

## you gotta know

- Castration was performed on boys (typically between ages 6 and 12) with the goal of preserving their soprano or alto voice while their bodies developed into manhood, creating a unique vocal timbre: high, agile, and resonant with adult power.
- In the *bel canto* era (18th and early 19th centuries), castrati were the *primo uomo* (first man) of opera, singing the hero roles in heroic and romantic operas; composers wrote virtuosic soprano and alto arias specifically for them.
- Famous castrati include *Farinelli* (Carlo Broschi), *Senesino*, and *Caffarelli*, who achieved international fame, commanded astronomical fees, and were treated as celebrities and objects of fascination.
- The castrato sound is described as ethereal, penetrating, and able to sustain notes with extraordinary breath control; the combination of soprano range with male vocal power created an effect unattainable by female singers or natural male voices.
- Castration was justified ostensibly as a path to musical greatness and financial success for poor families, though it also served the needs of religious institutions (convents and all-male choirs) and the musical theater.
- The practice became increasingly controversial by the 19th century as Romanticism emphasized the "natural" and "authentic"; replacement of castrati by female sopranos and tenors diminished their operatic roles dramatically.
- Early recordings of *Alessandro Moreschi*, a papal castrato recorded in 1904, provide the only direct audio evidence of castrato vocal technique, though debates about its authenticity and acoustic fidelity persist.
