## summary

An **interval** is the distance in pitch between two musical sounds, experienced either successively in melody or simultaneously in harmony. Formulated theoretically in *Ancient Greece* through the mathematical acoustic proportions of *Pythagoras*, intervals constitute the foundational atomic units of melody, scales, and chords across global musical traditions. Mastery of interval quality, inversion, and consonance remains vital to Western counterpoint, tonal harmony, and ear training.

## you gotta know

- Measured numerically by counting staff letter names inclusive of both pitches (e.g., from C to G is a fifth).
- Classified into five primary qualities: *perfect* (unisons, fourths, fifths, octaves), *major*, *minor*, *diminished*, and *augmented*.
- Distinguished into *harmonic intervals* (sounded simultaneously, establishing harmony or dissonance) and *melodic intervals* (sounded successively, generating contour and tunes).
- Inverts symmetrically across an octave, where the sum of an interval and its inversion equals nine (seconds invert to sevenths, thirds to sixths, fourths to fifths), swapping major with minor and augmented with diminished.
- Categorized historically into *consonances* (stable, restful intervals like major and minor thirds, sixths, and perfect fifths) and *dissonances* (unstable intervals requiring resolution, such as seconds, sevenths, and the tritone).
- Contains the famous *tritone* (an augmented fourth or diminished fifth spanning three whole tones), traditionally dubbed *diabolus in musica* ("the devil in music") and strictly restricted in medieval polyphony.
- Forms the acoustic basis of temperament systems, contrasting pure Pythagorean or just intervals with modern equal temperament.

## connections

- [[tonality]] — the tonal framework structured entirely by scales, chord qualities, and pitch intervals.
- [[counterpoint]] — the strict voice-leading system governed by precise treatment of consonant and dissonant intervals.
- [[homophony]] — the texture built on vertical chordal intervals supporting a linear melody.
- [[gregorian-chant]] — early medieval chant repertoire whose step-wise melodic intervals defined the church modes.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the civilization whose theorists first derived musical interval ratios via the monochord.
- [[classical-music]] — the tradition relying on functional intervallic relationships to drive formal development.
