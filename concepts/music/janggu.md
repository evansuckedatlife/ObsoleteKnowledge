## summary

The **janggu** (also romanised as **janggo**) is the hourglass-shaped double-headed drum of traditional Korean music ([[gugak]]), serving as the foundational rhythmic anchor across court and folk traditions. Originating by the Three Kingdoms period and flourishing through the Joseon dynasty, the instrument provides metric structure and dynamic color to virtually every major Korean ensemble. Its dual-headed construction produces complementary acoustic timbres—a deep resonant bass and a crisp piercing treble—enabling a single performer to govern complex rhythmic cycles and animate ensemble performance.

## you gotta know

- Features an hourglass-shaped wooden resonator (*bodong*) carved from paulownia wood, flanked by two disparate animal-skin heads laced together across a narrow central waist with thick cords.
- Produces two contrasting tonal colors through unequal animal skins: the thicker left head (*gungpyeon*), made of cowhide or doghide, emits a low resonant bass, while the thinner right head (*chaepyeon*), fashioned from horsehide, yields a sharp, ringing treble.
- Played using two distinct striking implements: the left hand wields the *gunggulchae*, a mallet ending in a rounded wooden knob or curved crook, while the right hand maneuvers the *yeolchae*, a slender and supple bamboo whip.
- Regulates pitch and head tension via movable leather sleeves called *bugol* (or *julsaegim*), which can be slid along the V-shaped tension cords toward the waist to tighten the skins or outward to loosen them.
- Anchors the cyclic rhythmic patterns known as *jangdan*, which structure traditional compositions through recurring metric frameworks ranging from the stately *jinyangjo* and rolling *gutgeori* to the rapid *hwimori*.
- Accompanies classical court orchestral suites (*jeongak*), solo instrumental suites (*sanjo*) featuring the [[gayageum]] or [[geomungo]], and dramatic vocal storytelling in [[pansori]], where the drummer also interjects vocal shouts of encouragement (*chuimsae*).
- Serves as the principal rhythmic engine in modern *samulnori* percussion quartets, driving fast-paced dialogue alongside the *kkwaenggwari* brass gong, *jing* large gong, and *buk* barrel drum.
- Documented in ancient Goguryeo tomb murals, Silla terracotta figurines, and the seminal Joseon musical treatise *Akhak Gwebeom* (1493), which cataloged its construction, cord configurations, and ritual court functions.

## connections

- [[gugak]] — the overarching tradition of Korean music in which the drum acts as the indispensable rhythmic timekeeper.
- [[pansori]] — the dramatic narrative singing genre wherein a solo vocalist is accompanied by the drummer providing metric punctuation and vocal encouragement.
- [[gayageum]] — the twelve-string plucked zither that relies on the drum's metric patterns during virtuosic *sanjo* suites.
- [[geomungo]] — the six-string fretted zither whose percussive bamboo plectrum interlocks with the drum's low-pitched strokes.
- [[daegeum]] — the large bamboo transverse flute whose expressive vibrato unfolds over the drum's foundational metric cycles.
- [[improvisation]] — the core musical practice animating folk genres like *sinawi*, where the drummer guides spontaneous melodic flights.
- [[classical-music]] — the formal court tradition (*jeongak*) that codified refined drum notations, ceremonial tempos, and banquet suites.
