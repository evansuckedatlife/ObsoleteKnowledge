## summary

A **hymn** is a formal religious song of praise, devotion, or adoration addressed to a deity, most prominently cultivated within Christian worship traditions. Developing from early scriptural canticles, Greek poetry, and Latin medieval plainsong, the genre underwent a massive populist transformation during the Protestant Reformation. Through metrical verse and strophic form, hymnody democratized sacred music by placing theological expression and singing directly into the voices of the congregation.

## you gotta know

- Structured typically in strophic form, using regular poetic meter where multiple verses of text are sung to an identical repeated melody.
- Originated in Western Christian practice through early figures like *Saint Ambrose* of Milan, who popularized metrical Latin devotional songs to combat Arianism.
- Revolutionized during the Protestant Reformation in *Germany* by *Martin Luther*, who championed congregational singing in the vernacular language over Latin-only clerical chanting.
- Propagated Luther's famed chorale *"A Mighty Fortress Is Our God"* (*"Ein feste Burg ist unser Gott"*), which became an anthem of the Reformation.
- Flourished in the English-speaking world through the prolific texts of *Isaac Watts* ("the father of English hymnody") and *Charles Wesley*, whose works shaped Methodism.
- Harmonized standardly in four-part chordal *homophony* (soprano, alto, tenor, bass), making it an enduring textbook model for learning functional tonal harmony.
- Evolved into distinct African American spiritual and gospel hymnody traditions, which profoundly influenced American sacred and popular music.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition whose liturgy, devotion, and theology are voiced through hymnody.
- [[homophony]] — the primary four-part chordal musical texture used in congregational hymn books.
- [[gregorian-chant]] — the earlier monophonic liturgical chant tradition from which office hymns emerged.
- [[motet]] — the polyphonic choral genre that frequently took hymn melodies and texts as cantus firmus material.
- [[mass-form]] — the central liturgical service alongside which hymns are frequently sung.
- [[germany]] — the homeland of the Lutheran chorale, the pivotal German-language model of hymnody.
- [[baroque-music]] — the musical period in which composers like *Johann Sebastian Bach* harmonized Lutheran hymn chorales.
