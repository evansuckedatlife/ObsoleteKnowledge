## summary

In music, a **canon** is a polyphonic compositional form in which two or more voices enter successively, each singing or playing the same melodic line at different times, creating complex harmonic relationships through layering. The voices typically sing the identical melody but staggered by a fixed interval (measured in beats or measures), generating counterpoint through self-imitation. Canons range from simple, folk-like rounds (like "Row, Row, Row Your Boat") to complex intellectual exercises by composers like Bach, where strict mathematical rules govern the voice entries and the resulting harmony.

## you gotta know

- A *round* is the simplest canon: all voices sing the same complete melody in sequence, each voice cycling back to the beginning after reaching the end, creating an infinite loop.
- A *canon at the unison* means each voice enters on the same note; a *canon at the fifth* means each succeeding voice enters five scale degrees higher, transposing the melody.
- *Retrograde canons* invert the melodic direction (the second voice sings the melody backward); *inversion canons* flip the intervals (ascending intervals become descending); *crab canons* combine both.
- *Bach's Art of Fugue* includes multiple canons of increasing complexity, including canons where different voices read the score in different directions—a theoretical marvel and a monument to compositional ingenuity.
- Canons were beloved pedagogical tools and intellectual puzzles during the Renaissance and Baroque periods; composers composed canons to demonstrate mastery and to entertain educated audiences who could parse the complex voice relationships.
- The meditative, hypnotic effect of canons comes from the layering of identical material at different time points; each voice provides harmonic color and rhythmic complexity to its companions.
- The *canon principle* influences modern musical composition, minimalist music (where repeating patterns emerge from staggered entry), and even electronic/algorithmic music where layered loops create complex textures.

## connections

- [[counterpoint]] — canons are exercises in strict contrapuntal technique.
- [[fugue]] — the fugue shares canon's principle of imitative polyphony but with more complex voice interaction and development.
- [[bach]] — Bach composed some of history's most intellectually sophisticated and beautiful canons.
- [[round]] — the simplest, most folk-like manifestation of canon.
- [[polyphony]] — canons are polyphonic by definition, combining multiple simultaneous melodic lines.
- [[minimalism]] — later composers drew inspiration from canon's hypnotic, layered effect.
