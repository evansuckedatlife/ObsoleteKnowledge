## summary

**Ruggero Leoncavallo** was an Italian composer central to the verismo movement, which brought stark realism to the opera stage. He is remembered chiefly for *Pagliacci* (1897), a one-act tragedy of jealousy and murder among traveling players that climaxes with one of opera's most shattering arias. Though he composed throughout his long career, Leoncavallo remained defined by a single masterwork that captures the raw emotion verismo sought.

## you gotta know

- Born in Naples in 1858; began as a pianist and organist before pursuing opera composition.
- *Pagliacci* (The Clowns) premiered in 1897 and quickly became a repertory standard worldwide.
- The opera's narrative centers on a traveling commedia dell'arte troupe; the protagonist must perform while emotionally destroyed.
- Created one of opera's most devastating monologues: the prologue where the character warns that actors are human beings with real suffering.
- A rival claimant to verismo alongside Puccini; the two competed for similar dramatic, contemporary stories set outside royal courts.
- Composed numerous other operas (*La Bohème*, *Zaza*) but none achieved *Pagliacci*'s staying power.
