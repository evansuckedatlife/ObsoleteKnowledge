## summary

**East Asian music** encompasses the deeply interconnected musical traditions of [[korea]], [[japan]], and China, unified by shared aesthetic and cosmological principles rooted in *Confucianism*, *Daoism*, and *Buddhism*. Developed over millennia through royal court rituals, theatrical spectacles, and folk festivities, this regional idiom emphasizes timbre, microtonal inflection, and pentatonic pitch organization rather than Western harmonic [[tonality]] or [[polyphony]]. Central traditions such as Chinese *yayue*, Korean [[gugak]], and Japanese *gagaku* revere natural materials, acoustic nuance, and pregnant silence, forming one of the world's most sophisticated musical systems.

## you gotta know

- Pentatonic scale systems: Traditional melodies across the region are predominantly founded on anhemitonic five-tone scales—known in Chinese theory as *wusheng* (*gong*, *shang*, *jiao*, *zhi*, and *yu*)—which govern melodic motion without relying on chord progressions or functional [[tonality]].
- Long zither organology: Movable-bridge plucked zithers define the regional soundscape, tracing a common lineage from the Chinese *guzheng* to the Korean [[gayageum]] and fretted [[geomungo]] and the Japanese *koto*, all celebrated for pitch-bending and wide vibrato produced by left-hand string pressure.
- Tang court music transmission: Court traditions achieved widespread international exchange during the Chinese Tang dynasty, establishing the foundational *tangak* repertoire in [[korea]] and the imperial *tōgaku* tradition within *gagaku* in [[japan]], which preserved ancient continental suites that later disappeared in China.
- Theatrical and narrative vocal arts: Dramatic vocal genres prioritize nasal resonance, stylized declamation, and percussive punctuation over Western [[opera]] conventions, seen in Chinese regional drama, the restrained chanting of Japanese *noh*, and the raspy emotional delivery of Korean [[pansori]].
- Heterophony and cyclical rhythm: Ensembles perform predominantly in heterophony, elaborating a single melodic outline simultaneously through idiomatic [[improvisation]] over recurring metric cycles (*changdan* in [[korea]]) anchored by double-headed hourglass drums like the [[janggu]] and Japanese *tsuzumi*.
- Aesthetics of timbre, noise, and silence: Classical theory treats timbre and unpitched noise—such as the buzzing membrane of the Korean [[daegeum]] or the plectrum scrape of the Japanese *biwa*—as central musical elements, balanced against the pregnant silence known in Japanese aesthetics as *ma*.
- The eight materials classification: Instruments were traditionally organized into the *bayin* ("eight sounds") system by resonant substance—silk, bamboo, gourd, metal, stone, clay, leather, and wood—a cosmological scheme governing court orchestra arrangements throughout the Sinosphere.

## connections

- [[gugak]] — the indigenous Korean traditional music system encompassing both court (*jeongak*) and folk (*minsogak*) repertoires.
- [[japan]] — neighboring archipelago whose court *gagaku* and theatrical *noh* and *kabuki* preserved and transformed continental musical forms.
- [[gayageum]] — twelve-string plucked zither whose flexible silk strings exemplify the expressive pitch-bending techniques of regional zithers.
- [[pansori]] — Korean dramatic storytelling genre pairing a solo singer and barrel drummer, illustrating regional vocal stamina and narrative pathos.
- [[janggu]] — double-headed hourglass drum that articulates the foundational metric cycles (*changdan*) governing traditional Korean ensemble music.
- [[opera]] — Western theatrical singing tradition whose harmonic architecture and staging contrast with East Asian stylized music-drama traditions.
- [[improvisation]] — spontaneous melodic elaboration and microtonal ornamentation cultivated extensively in solo instrumental suites like Korean *sanjo*.
- [[tonality]] — Western harmonic system organized around tonic-dominant hierarchies, contrasting with the modal, pentatonic, and heterophonic structures of East Asia.
