## summary

**Rhythm** is the organization of musical time through patterns of duration, pulse, and accent—the element that governs how long notes sound and how they relate to one another and to a steady pulse. Every musical tradition, from Western classical to Indian classical to jazz, has a sophisticated system for organizing rhythm; rhythm serves as the temporal skeleton on which melody and harmony are built, creating momentum, structure, and expressive power.

## you gotta know

- Rhythm is distinct from *beat* (the underlying regular pulse) and *meter* (the recurrent pattern of accented and unaccented beats); rhythm describes the actual durations of sounding notes against that framework.
- In Western classical music, rhythm is notated through note values (whole notes, half notes, quarter notes, etc.) and rests, organized into measures by a *time signature* (e.g., 4/4, 3/4).
- *Syncopation* is a disruption of expected rhythmic accent, emphasizing weaker beats or creating patterns that conflict with the underlying meter—a hallmark of jazz, ragtime, and African-influenced musics.
- In Indian classical music, rhythm is organized by *tala*, a cyclic rhythmic pattern of fixed duration containing specific beat divisions; the drummer (tabla player) and melodic soloist must coordinate within the tala framework.
- *Polyrhythm*—the simultaneous use of contrasting rhythmic patterns—appears prominently in African and Afro-diasporic musics and has been systematically explored by 20th-century classical composers.
- The concept of rhythmic "feel" or "groove"—the particular way a rhythm is performed with slight variations in timing and emphasis—is crucial to performance practice in jazz, funk, and contemporary music.
- Composers such as Igor Stravinsky revolutionized rhythmic complexity in the 20th century by shifting accents, changing meters abruptly, and creating elaborate polyrhythmic textures.

## connections

- [[beat]] — the underlying regular pulse that rhythm patterns relate to and organize.
- [[meter]] — the recurring pattern of strong and weak beats that gives rhythm structure and regularity.
- [[syncopation]] — rhythmic displacement and accent that disrupts expected metrical patterns.
- [[tala]] — the Indian classical rhythmic cycle system that organizes rhythm in Hindustani and Carnatic music.
- [[tempo]] — the speed at which rhythm unfolds, measured in beats per minute.
- [[musical-form]] — the larger structural organization within which rhythmic patterns create sections and movements.
- [[jazz]] — a tradition where rhythm, especially syncopation and polyrhythm, is central to the aesthetic.
