## summary

**The sitar** is a plucked string instrument fundamental to North Indian classical (*Hindustani*) music, consisting of a resonating wooden body with a long neck, multiple sympathetic strings, and high-fretted main strings. Played with finger plucks and hand techniques that create elaborate slides and ornamentations, the sitar's expressive range and tonal complexity made it the iconic voice of Indian classical music—famously popularized globally by *Ravi Shankar* in the 20th century.

## you gotta know

- The sitar typically has six or seven main playable strings and an additional 10–20 sympathetic strings (running below the frets) that resonate with played notes, creating its distinctive shimmering timbre.
- Unlike fretted Western instruments, the sitar's frets are moveable, allowing precise tuning to the specific pitches required by each *raga* (melodic mode).
- The instrument demands virtuosic finger and hand technique: players execute rapid passages with a single finger, create complex glissandos and microtonal bends, and control the instrument's percussive attack.
- The sitar player performs within the framework of a *raga*, improvising elaborate melodic explorations that must follow the *tala* (rhythmic cycle) provided by the *tabla* drummer.
- *Ravi Shankar* (1920–2012) became the first Indian classical musician to achieve worldwide fame, introducing the sitar to Western audiences through concerts and collaborations with Western artists, including *The Beatles*.
- The sitar's resonance and flexibility allow it to express the microtonal inflections, bending, and ornamentation central to the Hindustani aesthetic, distinguishing it from *Carnatic* plucked instruments.
- Modern sitar compositions preserve ancient repertoire while allowing for individual artistic innovation within the raga and tala framework.
