## summary

**Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart** was an Austrian composer who, in his tragically short life of thirty-five years, created a body of work unmatched in its scope, elegance, and emotional depth. Born in Salzburg, he was a child prodigy who mastered keyboard and violin before his adolescence and spent his career producing symphonies, concertos, operas, and chamber works that defined the Classical era. His music balances perfect formal construction with inexplicable grace, making him perhaps the most universally admired composer in the Western canon.

## you gotta know

- Born in Salzburg in 1756; began composing and performing publicly by age five, traveling Europe as a child prodigy.
- Wrote his first opera at age twelve and composed continuously across all genres: 41 symphonies, 27 piano concertos, 5 violin concertos, 22 operas.
- His three final symphonies (Nos. 39, 40, 41 "Jupiter," all in 1788) represent the apex of symphonic achievement in the Classical form.
- *The Magic Flute*, *The Marriage of Figaro*, and *Don Giovanni* stand among the greatest operas ever written, each showcasing different operatic modes.
- Combined the galant elegance of the Italian style with the rigor of Germanic counterpoint and form—a uniquely balanced achievement.
- Struggled financially despite his genius; lived an unstable life moving between Salzburg and Vienna, dependent on patrons and concert income.
- Died suddenly in Vienna in 1791, possibly of rheumatic fever, leaving the *Requiem* unfinished—completed by Franz Xaver Süssmayr.
