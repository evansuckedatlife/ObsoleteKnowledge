## summary

A **chord** is a simultaneous sounding of two or more distinct musical pitches, traditionally serving as the fundamental building block of vertical harmony in Western music. Systematized with the historical rise of [[tonality]] in Europe, chords supply vertical foundation and acoustic weight to melodic lines, establishing emotional tension, structural stability, and forward motion toward cadential resolution. From the strict triads of [[classical-music]] to the altered, extended harmonies of [[jazz]], chords form the primary theoretical framework through which musical progression is conceived and understood.

## you gotta know

- The fundamental chord structure in Western harmony is the triad, which consists of three notes stacked in intervals of thirds: a root, a major or minor third, and a perfect fifth.
- Inversion refers to the arrangement of a chord's pitch classes where a note other than the root acts as the lowest bass note, creating first inversion with the third in the bass or second inversion with the fifth in the bass.
- Seventh chords incorporate an additional pitch a third above the fifth (a seventh above the root), generating intrinsic harmonic dissonance that demands resolution, most famously in dominant seventh chords that propel tonal cadences.
- French music theorist *Jean-Philippe Rameau* transformed harmonic understanding in his landmark 1722 treatise *Traité de l'harmonie*, codifying the concept of the root and demonstrating that inverted chords maintain their core functional identity.
- Chromatic altered chords, including Neapolitan chords and augmented sixth chords (conventionally designated Italian, French, and German), were developed by classical composers to dramatically intensify voice leading toward the dominant.
- Renaissance mannerist composer [[carlo-gesualdo]] challenged early triadic voice leading by stringing together unexpected, chromatic chords, foreshadowing the freer harmonic explorations of later centuries.
- In twentieth-century [[jazz]], chord construction broadened to incorporate rich extensions such as ninths, elevenths, and thirteenths, forming the complex harmonic changes navigated by improvisers like [[chet-baker]].
- Atonal modernist composers of the Second Viennese School largely abandoned traditional triadic functional chords, organizing vertical sonorities instead through pitch-class sets and twelve-tone rows.

## connections

- [[tonality]] — depends on the hierarchical relationships and functional syntax that organize chords around a central tonic pitch.
- [[counterpoint]] — represents the vertical harmonic sonorities that historically coalesced from overlapping, independent horizontal voice lines.
- [[polyphony]] — generated complex vertical pitch combinations through simultaneous vocal lines prior to the codification of functional harmony.
- [[classical-music]] — formalized common-practice harmonic theory and Roman numeral analysis around functional diatonic chord progressions.
- [[jazz]] — utilizes sophisticated chord substitutions, altered extensions, and lead sheets as the harmonic springboard for spontaneous improvisation.
- [[carlo-gesualdo]] — daring late Renaissance composer who juxtaposed distant, chromatic chords to achieve raw emotional shock.
- [[chet-baker]] — cool jazz instrumentalist celebrated for his lyrical, intuitive voice leading across intricate harmonic chord changes.
