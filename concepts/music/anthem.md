## summary

An **anthem** is an English-language choral composition designed for liturgical performance within the Anglican tradition, particularly associated with the *Chapel Royal* and cathedral choirs throughout [[england]]. Emerging during the Protestant Reformation as the vernacular counterpart to the Latin [[motet]], it evolved from concise, homophonic choral prayers into expansive sacred masterworks featuring elaborate [[polyphony]] and independent instrumental accompaniment. The genre fundamentally defined English [[sacred-music]] from the Tudor [[renaissance]] through the [[baroque-music]] era and beyond, establishing an enduring ceremonial tradition that united church liturgy with national identity and royal state pageantry.

## you gotta know

- *George Frideric Handel* composed four Coronation Anthems for the 1727 coronation of King George II, most famously *Zadok the Priest*, whose arpeggiated string introduction and explosive choral entrance have sounded at the anointing of every British monarch since.
- Historical practice divides the genre into two distinct structural formats: the **full anthem**, sung throughout by the complete choir in dense imitative [[counterpoint]], and the **verse anthem**, which alternates between solo voices ("verse") and choral responses ("chorus").
- During the Restoration, *Henry Purcell* brought the verse anthem to its artistic summit at the *Chapel Royal*, integrating French overtures, adventurous chromatic harmonies, and dramatic declamatory word-painting into expressive masterpieces such as *Hear My Prayer, O Lord* and *Remember Not, Lord, Our Offences*.
- The genre arose directly from Edward VI's liturgical mandates requiring worship in the vernacular tongue, prompting *Thomas Tallis* to compose foundational, strictly syllabic homophonic settings like *If Ye Love Me* and *Hear the Voice and Prayer* to accompany the newly instituted *Book of Common Prayer*.
- In the late Elizabethan and Jacobean eras, *William Byrd* expanded the choral texture with radiant polyphonic anthems such as *Sing Joyfully*, while *Orlando Gibbons* established the preeminent early model of the verse anthem with *This Is the Record of John*, scoring a solo [[tenor]] against an accompaniment of viols.
- Prescribed as an essential musical component of Anglican Morning and Evening Prayer (Mattins and Evensong), the form is positioned immediately following the third collect, drawing its text primarily from the Coverdale Psalter or prophetic biblical scriptures.
- Following the Restoration, King Charles II demanded lively string accompaniment in imitation of the court of [[france]], prompting *Pelham Humfrey* and *Matthew Locke* to introduce twenty-four violins, operatic recitatives, and dance-like rhythms to *Chapel Royal* services.

## connections

- [[motet]] — the Latin polyphonic counterpart that served as the primary liturgical model for Tudor composers adapting music to the English vernacular.
- [[polyphony]] — the multi-voiced compositional texture that flourished in cathedral full choir settings throughout the Renaissance.
- [[baroque-music]] — the stylistic era that brought orchestral suites, basso continuo, and dramatic concertante vocal solos into English sacred music.
- [[counterpoint]] — the foundational technique of interwoven melodic lines perfected by English cathedral organists and choral directors.
- [[england]] — the nation where the Henrician Reformation and the Chapel Royal cultivated the genre as a cornerstone of national worship.
- [[renaissance]] — the historical era during which vernacular liturgy transformed Tudor and Jacobean church composition.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition whose liturgy, liturgical hours, and scripture provide the foundational texts and ritual setting for the genre.
