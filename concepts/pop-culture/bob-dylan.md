## summary

**Bob Dylan**, born *Robert Allen Zimmerman*, is an American singer-songwriter, poet, and cultural figure who revolutionized popular music beginning in the early 1960s in [[new-york-city]]. Emerging from the Greenwich Village folk revival scene, he transformed songwriting by combining acoustic folk idioms with literary, surrealist imagery and searing social commentary before notoriously pivoting to electric rock instrumentation. His extraordinary catalog spans six decades, earning him the 2016 Nobel Prize in Literature for creating new poetic expressions within the great American song tradition.

## you gotta know

- Rose to international fame as an acoustic folk balladeer with his 1963 breakthrough album *The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan*, which contained generational anthems such as *Blowin' in the Wind* and *A Hard Rain's a-Gonna Fall*.
- Outraged acoustic folk purists by notoriously going electric at the 1965 Newport Folk Festival backed by the *Paul Butterfield Blues Band*, marking a seminal watershed in rock history.
- Released the landmark 1965 single *Like a Rolling Stone*, which shattered commercial radio standards with its six-minute runtime and biting lyrical confrontation, later anchoring the album *Highway 61 Revisited*.
- Completed a celebrated mid-1960s triumvirate of innovative rock albums with *Bringing It All Back Home*, *Highway 61 Revisited*, and the double album *Blonde on Blonde*, deeply shaping lyrical ambitions in popular music including peers who created works like [[born-to-run]].
- Traveled through the American South and embraced prominent political advocacy during the [[civil-rights-movement]], singing at the 1963 March on Washington alongside prominent civil rights leaders.
- Earned the 2016 Nobel Prize in Literature, becoming the first songwriter to win the honor, which sparked extensive worldwide debate over the boundaries of literary poetry and musical performance.
- Retreated to Woodstock following a 1966 motorcycle crash, where he collaborated with his backing group *The Band* on home recordings in a pink house later released as *The Basement Tapes*, before recording rootsy country-folk records like *John Wesley Harding* and *Nashville Skyline*.
- Mounted multiple dramatic stylistic reinventions across later decades, including his acclaimed 1975 relationship-autopsy album *Blood on the Tracks*, an evangelical Christian gospel period beginning with *Slow Train Coming*, and an acclaimed late-career resurgence with *Time Out of Mind*.

## connections

- [[born-to-run]] — *Bruce Springsteen* was hailed early in his career as a new Dylan before adopting his own operatic Jersey shore narrative rock style.
- [[bohemian-rhapsody]] — fellow landmark classic-rock composition that challenged standard pop song structure and radio length limitations.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — his early 1960s protest topical songs became core anthems for activists marching across the [[united-states]].
- [[american-literature]] — recognized by the Swedish Academy as an enduring American literary voice alongside *Walt Whitman* and *Herman Melville*.
- [[new-york-city]] — Greenwich Village coffeehouses served as the incubator where he adopted his stage pseudonym and launched his career.
- [[cold-war]] — early masterpieces like *Masters of War* and *A Hard Rain's a-Gonna Fall* directly reflected nuclear anxiety during the missile crises.
- [[modernism]] — his mid-sixties lyrics incorporated Symbolist and Beat poetry influences, drawing comparison to *Arthur Rimbaud* and *T. S. Eliot*.
