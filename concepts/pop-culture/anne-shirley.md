## summary

**Anne Shirley** is the spirited, imaginative orphan protagonist created by Canadian author *Lucy Maud Montgomery* in the classic 1908 novel *Anne of Green Gables*. Mistakenly sent to live with the elderly, unmarried siblings *Matthew Cuthbert* and *Marilla Cuthbert* on Prince Edward Island, she gradually wins over her adoptive family and the conservative rural community through her fierce integrity, quick wit, and romantic worldview. As an enduring icon of children's literature, her coming-of-age journey explores female intellectual ambition, the restorative power of imagination, and domestic kinship in the late Victorian era of the *[[british-empire]]*.

## you gotta know

- Arrives at the fictional village of *Avonlea* after an administrative mix-up at the orphanage, which was asked to supply a boy to help with heavy chores on the family farm.
- Insists vehemently on her preferred spelling, declaring that "Anne" looks distinguished and elegant whereas "Ann" without an "e" looks dreadful, plain, and commonplace.
- Known throughout town and literature as **Anne of Green Gables**, a moniker honoring the picturesque gabled homestead where she finds a permanent sense of belonging.
- Engages in an academic and personal rivalry with classmate *Gilbert Blythe*, which ignites dramatically when he mocks her red hair by calling her "Carrots" and she shatters a slate over his head.
- Accidentally intoxicates her dearest friend, *Diana Barry*, by serving her three large glasses of homemade red currant wine under the mistaken belief that it is non-alcoholic raspberry cordial.
- Falls prey to a fraudulent traveling peddler who sells her defective black hair dye, turning her auburn hair a grotesque shade of bottle-green that forces *Marilla* to crop it completely short.
- Wins the prestigious Avery Scholarship to Redmond College after graduating at the top of Queen's Academy in Charlottetown, though she puts her immediate academic ambitions on hold to support *Marilla* following *Matthew*'s sudden death.
- Synthesizes aspects of regional romanticism with domestic *[[realism]]*, reflecting *Montgomery*'s own upbringing in rural Canada and contemporary debates on female higher education.

- [[realism]] — the literary tradition whose focus on everyday social customs and rural life grounds Anne's flights of poetic fancy.
- [[american-literature]] — the closely linked North American tradition of domestic orphan fiction that includes works like *Little Women*.
- [[england]] — the ancestral culture whose classic romantic poetry, ballads, and historical dramas inspire Anne's dramatic soliloquies.
- [[tragedy]] — the narrative mode evoked by the sudden, fatal heart attack suffered by her beloved guardian Matthew.
