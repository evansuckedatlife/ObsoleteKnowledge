## summary

**Like a Rolling Stone** is a landmark 1965 folk-rock single written and recorded by American singer-songwriter *Bob Dylan*, appearing as the opening track on his sixth studio album, *Highway 61 Revisited*. Recorded over two days in [[new-york-city]], the six-minute composition shattered contemporary commercial radio conventions that strictly limited pop singles to three minutes. Its cynical lyrical confrontation, dense imagery, and sweeping electric instrumentation fundamentally transformed the boundaries of popular songwriting in the [[united-states]], marking Dylan's historic metamorphosis from acoustic folk icon into rock revolutionary.

## you gotta know

- Opens with a thunderous snare crack delivered by session drummer *Bobby Gregg*, an explosive opening note that rock musician *Bruce Springsteen* later described as sounding as though "somebody'd kicked open the door to your mind."
- Anchored by its famous sneering chorus asking, "How does it feel? / How does it feel? / To be on your own / With no direction home / Like a complete unknown / Like a rolling stone?", directly addressing a fallen high-society figure named *Miss Lonely*.
- Features an iconic, improvised Hammond organ riff played by *Al Kooper*, an invited studio guest and guitarist who sneaked onto the organ bench without producer *Tom Wilson*'s knowledge and created the song's signature sound.
- Driven by blistering electric blues lead guitar lines contributed by *Mike Bloomfield*, who was recruited by Dylan specifically to bring an aggressive Chicago blues edge to the tracking session.
- Originated as an unwieldy, ten-to-twenty-page piece of vindictive and cathartic rhythmic prose drafted by Dylan after returning exhausted and disillusioned from a grueling 1965 concert tour of [[england]].
- Columbia Records marketing executives initially refused to release the track due to its unprecedented six-minute running time, but a promotional vinyl acetate leaked to DJ *Murray the K* and the popular Manhattan nightclub *Arthur* sparked overwhelming public demand that forced its commercial release.
- Reached number two on the *Billboard* Hot 100 chart, kept out of the top spot only by *The Beatles*' song *Help!*, and was later ranked number one on *Rolling Stone* magazine's definitive list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time.
