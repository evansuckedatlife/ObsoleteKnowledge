## summary

**Documentary cinema** is filmmaking that purports to record reality rather than fiction—to document actual events, people, and places. Unlike narrative cinema, documentary claims a relationship to truth; its ethics revolve around the question of what it means to represent reality on film. Documentary has been used for propaganda, journalism, artistic expression, and historical record, often simultaneously, making it one of cinema's most morally complex forms.

## you gotta know

- *The Lumière brothers* (*Louis Lumière* and *Auguste Lumière*) pioneered documentary by filming everyday scenes (workers leaving a factory, a train arriving) in the 1890s; they established the observational mode.
- Documentary's founding claim is *indexicality*: film captures light reflected from the actual world, creating a mechanical trace of reality; this distinguishes it from all other arts, but does not make it objective.
- *Editing* is documentary's invisible hand; selection, sequencing, and framing determine what is emphasized, omitted, and suggested; no documentary is unmediated reality.
- *Propaganda documentaries* have been influential; [[frank-capra]] directed U.S. Army films during World War II that shaped American understanding of the war; fascist regimes used documentary to promote ideology.
- *Observational cinema* (or *cinéma vérité*) emerged mid-20th century; it attempted to document without intervention, letting events unfold before the camera, though the presence of the camera always affects what is filmed.
- Documentary encompasses diverse forms: *newsreels* (brief topical films shown in theaters), *educational films*, *personal essay films*, *investigative journalism*, and artistic/experimental work.
- The question of *truth in documentary* remains unsettled; film can lie through selection, framing, and juxtaposition even when each image is factually accurate.

## connections

- [[frank-capra]] — his wartime propaganda documentaries showed cinema's power to shape collective understanding of history and ideology.
- [[louis-lumiere]] — pioneered the documentary impulse; his films established that cinema could record reality.
- [[photojournalism]] — a parallel art form committed to visual truth; documentary cinema inherited both its ethics and its ambiguities.
- [[film-editing]] — the tool through which documentary's apparent objectivity is constructed; editing determines meaning.
- [[propaganda]] — documentary and propaganda are historically intertwined; the same techniques can serve truth-seeking or manipulation.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — even documentary uses visual juxtaposition to create meaning beyond the literal image.
