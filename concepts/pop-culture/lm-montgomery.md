## summary

**L. M. Montgomery** (born **Lucy Maud Montgomery**) was a celebrated Canadian novelist best known for creating the spirited, imaginative orphan *Anne Shirley* in her 1908 classic *Anne of Green Gables*. Raised on Prince Edward Island, her pastoral romances, humorous domestic sketches, and coming-of-age novels achieved immense international popularity during the twilight of the *British Empire*. Montgomery published twenty novels, hundreds of short stories, and extensive personal journals, leaving an indelible imprint on children's literature, feminist criticism, and global popular culture.

## you gotta know

- Published her breakout debut novel *Anne of Green Gables* in 1908, which became an immediate global bestseller and prompted iconic author *Mark Twain* to declare Anne "the dearest and most moving and delightful child since the immortal Alice."
- Set the majority of her novels in Prince Edward Island, immortalizing the real town of Cavendish and converting the island's red soil, rural homesteads, and apple orchards into a global pilgrimage destination.
- Penned seven sequels following *Anne Shirley* from girlhood into motherhood, including *Anne of Avonlea* and *Anne of the Island*, alongside other popular series such as *Emily of New Moon* and *The Story Girl*.
- Achieved extraordinary posthumous celebrity in *Japan*, where translator *Hanako Muraoka* secretly translated *Anne of Green Gables* during *World War II*, sparking a nationwide cultural affection, school curriculum adoptions, and a famed 1979 anime series directed by *Isao Takahata*.
- Worked as one of the few female newspaper journalists of her era at the Halifax *Chronicle-Herald*, writing under pen names and fighting rigorously to control her own literary copyrights against predatory publishers like *L. C. Page & Company*.
- Documented her private battles with clinical depression, her husband Reverend Ewan Macdonald's religious melancholia, and grief over *World War I* in exhaustive, candid personal journals published decades after her death.
- Appointed an Officer of the Order of the British Empire (OBE) in 1935 in recognition of her worldwide literary contributions.

## connections

- [[realism]] — literary movement whose conventions she blended with regionalist romanticism to portray rural maritime life.

- [[japan]] — nation where her work found massive, enduring cultural resonance following *World War II*.
- [[american-literature]] — neighboring literary tradition influenced by her domestic fiction and contemporaries like *Louisa May Alcott*.
- [[modernism]] — literary period contemporary with her later career, contrasting sharply with her traditional sentimental narrative forms.
