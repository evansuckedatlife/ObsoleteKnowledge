## summary

**Fleetwood Mac** is an influential British-American rock band formed in London in 1967 by guitarist *Peter Green*, drummer *Mick Fleetwood*, and bassist *John McVie*. Initially rising to prominence as leaders of the British blues boom, the group underwent multiple stylistic reinventions before relocating to the [[united-states]] and achieving legendary status with the 1974 addition of *Lindsey Buckingham* and *Stevie Nicks*. Their 1977 masterpiece *Rumours* became one of the best-selling records of all time, turning raw personal heartbreak and bitter interpersonal fractures into lush, enduring pop-rock harmonies.

## you gotta know

- Achieved global commercial superstardom with the 1977 release of *Rumours*, which spent thirty-one non-consecutive weeks at number one on the Billboard 200 and produced defining tracks like "Dreams", "Go Your Own Way", and "The Chain".
- The definitive and most famous five-piece lineup featured the rhythm section of *Mick Fleetwood* and *John McVie*, keyboardist and songwriter *Christine McVie*, guitarist *Lindsey Buckingham*, and singer *Stevie Nicks*.
- Stevie Nicks's ethereal ballad "Dreams" earned the group their solitary number-one single on the US Billboard Hot 100, celebrated for its hypnotic bassline and deeply introspective lyrics.
- Formed originally in [[england]] during 1967 as Peter Green's Fleetwood Mac, establishing their reputation as a premier blues outfit responsible for classic compositions including "Black Magic Woman", "Albatross", and "Man of the World".
- Followed the unprecedented success of *Rumours* with the experimental and costly double album *Tusk* (1979), driven by Buckingham's fascination with new wave production and featuring the USC Trojan Marching Band.
- The 1975 self-titled record *Fleetwood Mac* established their signature three-part vocal arrangements and introduced timeless staples such as "Rhiannon", "Landslide", and "Say You Love Me".
- "Don't Stop", written by Christine McVie, was adopted as the official campaign anthem for Bill Clinton during the 1992 [[united-states]] presidential election and reunited the classic lineup for his 1993 inaugural celebration.
- Their signature breakup anthem "Go Your Own Way" features on the multi-platinum soundtrack to the film [[forrest-gump]], underscoring the central character's multi-year running trek across the continent.

## connections

- [[forrest-gump]] — contributed the classic anthem "Go Your Own Way" to the Academy Award-winning film's chart-topping soundtrack.
- [[england]] — the country where the ensemble originated in 1967 under the leadership of blues guitarist Peter Green.
- [[united-states]] — the nation where the band relocated during the 1970s, establishing their Los Angeles creative nexus and superstar commercial identity.
- [[cold-war]] — the historic geopolitical era throughout which the band rose from underground clubs to international arena dominance.
- [[modernism]] — aesthetic current of emotional fracture, fragmented perspectives, and sonic experimentation that shaped late-twentieth-century rock songwriting.
- [[realism]] — artistic sensibility mirrored in the band's unflinching lyrical honesty regarding divorce, romantic betrayal, and addiction.
- [[forgiveness-rock-record]] — modern indie-rock release by Broken Social Scene, whose large collective format and interwoven romantic tensions often prompted critical comparisons to the band.
