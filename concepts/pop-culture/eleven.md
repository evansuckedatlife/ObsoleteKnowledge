## summary

**Eleven**, born *Jane Ives* and later known as *Jane Hopper*, is the central fictional protagonist of the *Netflix* science fiction drama series *Stranger Things*, created by the *Duffer Brothers*. Portrayed by actress *Millie Bobby Brown*, she is a telekinetic and telepathic young girl raised in a clandestine government laboratory during the peak tensions of the *Cold War*. The character became a defining global pop-culture phenomenon of late-2010s television, celebrated for her emotional vulnerability, fierce loyalty, and stark visual iconography.

## you gotta know

- Possesses formidable psychokinetic and telepathic abilities that typically manifest with physical strain, marked distinctively by bleeding from her left nostril.
- Grew up subjected to sensory deprivation and abusive psychological experimentation under *Dr. Martin Brenner*, whom she was conditioned to refer to as "Papa," at the *Hawkins National Laboratory*.
- Escaped the research facility in November 1983, befriending a trio of local middle school boys—*Mike Wheeler*, *Dustin Henderson*, and *Lucas Sinclair*—who hid her in a suburban basement.
- Developed an intense affection for frozen *Eggo* waffles, which served as a recurring motif and humorous narrative bridge to her gradual socialization.
- Accidentally opened the interdimensional rift known as "the Gate," releasing predatory entities from an alternate nightmare dimension dubbed the *Upside Down*, including the *Demogorgon* and the *Mind Flayer*.
- Adopted by Hawkins Police Chief *Jim Hopper*, who legally took her in under the name *Jane Hopper* to shield her from continued pursuit by American military and intelligence authorities.
- Loses her powers temporarily following an agonizing encounter with the *Mind Flayer* at the *Starcourt Mall*, forcing her to rely on inner resilience and personal identity rather than supernatural force.
- Investigated by secret government projects designed to counter espionage by the *Soviet Union*, reflecting historical *Project MKUltra* experiments carried out by American intelligence agencies.
