## summary

**Brown Sugar** is the celebrated yet deeply provocative 1971 rock single by the British band *The Rolling Stones*, serving as the opening track of their acclaimed album *Sticky Fingers*. Driven by an indelible open-G guitar riff from *Keith Richards* and a roaring vocal delivery from *Mick Jagger*, the song became a global chart-topping hit and a staple of stadium rock. It remains a focal point of intense critical discussion due to its controversial lyrics confronting transatlantic slavery, racial violence, and drug addiction.

## you gotta know

- Recorded in December 1969 at the legendary *Muscle Shoals Sound Studio* in Sheffield, Alabama, during the same sessions that produced *"Wild Horses"*.
- Served as the inaugural single released on the band's independent imprint *Rolling Stones Records*, famously introducing the iconic tongue-and-lips logo designed by *John Pasche*.
- Built around *Keith Richards*' signature five-string open-G tuning riff and propelled by a blistering tenor saxophone solo performed by longtime sideman *Bobby Keys*.
- Features taboo-laden lyrics that deliberately intertwine chattel slavery, plantation exploitation, heroin abuse, and interracial sexuality in the historic American South.
- Prompted enduring debate over whether its scandalous lyrical pastiche satirized historical atrocities or perpetuated harmful, racist exploitation for shock value.
- Reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in the [[united-states]] and number two on the Official Singles Chart in [[england]].
- Retired by the band from their live concert setlists in 2021 due to heightened modern sensitivities surrounding its depictions of the slave trade.
