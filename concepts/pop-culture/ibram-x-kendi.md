## summary

**Ibram X. Kendi** (born **Ibram Henry Rogers**) is an American author, historian, and scholar of race and public policy in the [[united-states]]. He rose to international prominence as a leading public intellectual and as the founding director of the Center for Antiracist Research at Boston University. Best known for his theoretical division of racial ideas into segregationist, assimilationist, and antiracist categories, his bestselling historical works reshaped modern discourse surrounding structural inequity and institutional racism in twenty-first-century American culture.

## you gotta know

- Authored the landmark 2016 historical survey *Stamped from the Beginning: The Definitive History of Racist Ideas in America*, which won the National Book Award for Nonfiction and established his academic reputation.
- Published the widely read 2019 book *How to Be an Antiracist*, which combines memoir and political theory to argue that actions and policies are never race-neutral: an individual or institution is either upholding racism or actively dismantling it.
- Developed a foundational framework tracing the intellectual history of American racism through five historical figures: Puritan minister *Cotton Mather*, Founding Father *Thomas Jefferson*, abolitionist *William Lloyd Garrison*, scholar *W. E. B. Du Bois*, and activist *Angela Davis*.
- Collaborated with young adult novelist *Jason Reynolds* to publish *Stamped: Racism, Antiracism, and You* in 2020, bringing his historical analysis to adolescent readers and subsequently becoming a major focus of national school curriculum debates.
- Selected the middle name *Xolani* (a Zulu word meaning peace) and the surname *Kendi* (a Meru word meaning the loved one) alongside his wife *Sangeeta Pawar* during their 2013 wedding ceremony, intentionally replacing his birth surname.
- Established the Antiracist Research and Policy Center at American University in Washington, D.C., before moving his research team to Boston University in 2020 amid historic national protests.
- Chronicled his personal battle with stage IV colon cancer during the late 2010s, drawing an extended parallel between the aggressive medical interventions needed to eradicate malignant tumors and the systemic interventions required to eliminate societal racism.
