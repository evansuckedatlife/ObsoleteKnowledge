## summary

**Starbucks**, officially incorporated as the **Starbucks Coffee Company**, is a multinational chain of coffeehouses and roastery reserves headquartered in Seattle, Washington. Founded in 1971, the enterprise revolutionized modern consumer beverage habits by introducing artisanal dark-roast coffee, European espresso culture, and the sociological idea of the cafe as a community hub to the global mass market. As the world's largest coffeehouse chain, it stands as an enduring emblem of late-twentieth-century American commercial expansion, corporate globalization, and retail branding.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1971 by English teacher *Jerry Baldwin*, history teacher *Zev Siegl*, and writer *Gordon Bowker* at Seattle's historic *Pike Place Market*, initially selling premium whole roasted coffee beans and equipment rather than prepared drinks.
- Took its distinctive corporate name from *Starbuck*, the thoughtful, coffee-loving first mate of Captain Ahab aboard the whaling ship *Pequod* in Herman Melville's landmark nineteenth-century novel *Moby-Dick*.
- Revolutionized into a modern beverage powerhouse under executive *Howard Schultz*, who acquired the company in 1987 after being captivated by the vibrant community atmosphere and barista craft of Milanese espresso bars during an Italian business trip.
- Famous for its green circular emblem showcasing a twin-tailed siren or mermaid derived from a sixteenth-century Nordic woodcut, evoking the historic seafaring roots of the Pacific Northwest and maritime spice trade.
- Popularized a distinct Italian-inspired sizing taxonomy—most notably *tall*, *grande*, and *venti*—which fundamentally altered mainstream American vernacular and retail ordering conventions.
- Pioneered the commercial adoption of the sociological concept of the "third place"—a welcoming communal refuge separate from home and workplace where individuals can linger, converse, or study.
- Created cultural consumer phenomena through seasonal specialty beverages, particularly the *Pumpkin Spice Latte* (introduced in 2003), which ignited widespread autumn marketing trends across the commercial food and beverage industry.
- Sparked enduring corporate controversies and public debates surrounding rapid urban expansion, predatory saturation clustering that squeezed out independent local cafes, and recent high-profile labor unionization campaigns across hundreds of American stores.
