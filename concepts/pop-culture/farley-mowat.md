## summary

**Farley Mowat** was one of Canada's most widely read, passionate, and controversial environmental writers and naturalists, whose books have sold millions of copies worldwide and been translated into dozens of languages. Born in Ontario, he used vivid narrative accounts to champion northern conservation, highlight the plight of Indigenous peoples, and challenge established wildlife management practices. His works, notably *Never Cry Wolf* and *People of the Deer*, combined gripping storytelling with polemical advocacy to reshape popular perceptions of the Arctic wilderness.

## you gotta know

- Served as an infantry officer in the Canadian Army during *World War II*, fighting in the Italian Campaign and northwestern Europe, experiences that informed his anti-war memoir *And No Birds Sang*.
- Published *People of the Deer* in 1952, a searing indictment of the Canadian government's neglect of the Ihalmiut Inuit of the Keewatin Region that provoked fierce official pushback.
- Gained international celebrity with his 1963 book *Never Cry Wolf*, an autobiographical account detailing his field study of Arctic wolves in subarctic Canada, later adapted into a 1983 Disney feature film.
- *Never Cry Wolf* popularized the thesis that wolves are gentle predators subsisting primarily on small rodents like mice rather than decimating caribou herds, which revolutionized environmental public opinion despite scientific criticism of his methodology.
- Authored beloved humorous memoirs about his childhood and pets, most notably *The Dog Who Wouldn't Be* (1957) and *Owls in the Family* (1961).
- Denied entry into the *United States* in 1985 under the McCarran-Walter Act due to his environmental activism and left-wing political views, prompting international diplomatic embarrassment.
- Donated over 200 acres of land along the Northumberland Strait to the Nature Conservancy of Canada and had a Sea Shepherd Conservation Society anti-poaching ship named in his honor.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — military service in Sicily and Italy that profoundly shaped his skepticism of authority and trauma portrayed in *And No Birds Sang*.
- [[cold-war]] — political climate and paranoia that led to his barring from entering the American border under anti-subversion statutes.
- [[realism]] — literary and naturalistic movement contrasted with his controversial semi-fictionalized approach to advocacy journalism.
- [[colonialism]] — bureaucratic and paternalistic administrative practices he furiously attacked in his chronicles of Arctic Indigenous communities.
- [[american-literature]] — North American literary context in which his environmental memoirs and natural histories achieved widespread mainstream acclaim.
