## summary

**Ed Sullivan** was an influential American entertainment journalist and television host whose long-running variety program, *The Ed Sullivan Show*, anchored Sunday night viewing on CBS from 1948 to 1971. Known for his stiff posture, idiosyncratic speaking mannerisms, and keen eye for commercial talent, Sullivan curated a wide variety of acts encompassing rock and roll, Broadway, opera, and circus arts. His broadcast served as a preeminent cultural barometer of post-war America, introducing monumental musical acts to tens of millions of living rooms.

## you gotta know

- Hosted CBS's premier Sunday night variety program, originally titled *Toast of the Town* before being officially renamed *The Ed Sullivan Show* in 1955.
- Broadcast the epochal American television debut of *The Beatles* on February 9, 1964, drawing an estimated record-breaking audience of over seventy-three million viewers.
- Popularized his signature promotional catchphrase promising viewers a "really big shew," which became a staple of impressions and popular culture parody.
- Welcomed *Elvis Presley* for several historic 1956 and 1957 appearances, where censors famously mandated that cameramen shoot Presley strictly from the waist up to conceal his gyrating hips.
- Maintained a steadfast commitment to racial integration on television by regularly booking African American performers such as *The Supremes*, *Stevie Wonder*, *Louis Armstrong*, and *Harry Belafonte* despite pushback from Southern affiliate sponsors.
- Began his professional media career as a sportswriter and Broadway gossip columnist in *New York City* before entering radio and television broadcasting.
- Broadcast his show from CBS Studio 50 in Manhattan, which CBS officially renamed *The Ed Sullivan Theater* in his honor, later serving as the studio for late-night programs hosted by *David Letterman* and *Stephen Colbert*.
