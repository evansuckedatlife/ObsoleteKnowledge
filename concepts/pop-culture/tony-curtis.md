## summary

**Tony Curtis**, born **Bernard Schwartz**, was an acclaimed American film actor of Hollywood's Golden Age whose prolific career spanned more than six decades and over one hundred motion pictures. Emerging from a difficult childhood in working-class New York City into studio stardom during the 1950s, he successfully transformed himself from a dashing teen idol and swashbuckler into a versatile leading man capable of intense dramatic pathos and brilliant comic timing. He is best remembered for his indelible performances in the scathing journalism noir *Sweet Smell of Success*, the pioneering civil rights drama *The Defiant Ones*, and the beloved classic farce *Some Like It Hot*.

## you gotta know

- Disguised himself in female drag alongside *Jack Lemmon* in *Billy Wilder*'s landmark 1959 comedy *Some Like It Hot*, playing an on-the-run jazz musician who poses as *Josephine* and adopts an exaggerated *Cary Grant* impression to woo Sugar Kane, played by *Marilyn Monroe*.
- Earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Actor for his role in *The Defiant Ones* (1958) as an escaped racist convict chained to *Sidney Poitier*, famously insisting that Poitier receive equal top billing above the title.
- Delivered a career-defining dramatic performance as the sleazy, desperate press agent Sidney Falco opposite *Burt Lancaster*'s tyrannical columnist J.J. Hunsecker in Alexander Mackendrick's cynical 1957 noir *Sweet Smell of Success*.
- Portrayed Antoninus, the gentle singing slave and companion opposite *Kirk Douglas* and *Laurence Olivier* in *Stanley Kubrick*'s 1960 epic *Spartacus*, featuring in the famous censored "snails and oysters" philosophical bath sequence.
- Shed his glamorous heartthrob image to depict real-life serial killer Albert DeSalvo in Richard Fleischer's 1968 true-crime film *The Boston Strangler*, earning critical acclaim and a Golden Globe nomination for the chilling transformation.
- Formed one of the defining celebrity power couples of mid-century Hollywood through his 1951 marriage to actress *Janet Leigh*, producing two daughters including future scream queen and Oscar-winner *Jamie Lee Curtis*.
- Enlisted in the United States Navy during World War II aboard the submarine tender USS Proteus, where he was wounded in Guam and personally witnessed the formal surrender of Japan in Tokyo Bay from the ship's signal bridge.
- Grew up in severe poverty in the Bronx as the son of Hungarian Jewish immigrants, speaking only Hungarian until age five and enduring anti-Semitic street violence before discovering acting via the G.I. Bill alongside *Marlon Brando*.

## connections

- [[billy-wilder]] — directed his standout comedic performance in *Some Like It Hot*, brilliantly utilizing his deadpan delivery and split-second shifts between multiple disguise personas.
- [[marilyn-monroe]] — his iconic co-star in *Some Like It Hot*, with whom he shared electric on-screen romance alongside notorious off-screen tension during production.
- [[jack-lemmon]] — his beloved comedic partner in *Some Like It Hot* and *The Great Race*, complementing his suave persona with manic physical energy.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — performance tradition that influenced his broad farcical roles, enabling him to blend sophisticated wit with high-energy physical humor.
- [[new-york-city]] — his birthplace in the Bronx, where early hardships instilled a resilient drive and a sharp urban cadence that defined his screen characters.
- [[world-war-ii]] — naval military service in the Pacific theater that interrupted his youth but granted him G.I. Bill funding to pursue professional dramatic training.
- [[judaism]] — cultural and religious identity that grounded his personal life, leading him in later decades to finance the restoration of Budapest's Great Synagogue.
