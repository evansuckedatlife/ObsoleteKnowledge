## summary

**Early Morning Rain** (also known as **Early Mornin' Rain**) is a landmark 1964 folk song composed by celebrated Canadian singer-songwriter *Gordon Lightfoot*. Written from the perspective of an impoverished, homesick traveler stranded on a rainy airport runway watching a luxurious Boeing 707 roar into the sky, the piece captured the quiet despair of transient life. It emerged as an enduring standard of the 1960s folk revival, earning widespread acclaim through influential interpretations across the folk, pop, and country genres.

## you gotta know

- Composed by *Gordon Lightfoot* in 1964 while living in Los Angeles, inspired by his own experiences of homesickness as he watched commercial passenger planes take flight through overcast skies.
- Popularized internationally in 1965 by the prominent American folk trio *Peter, Paul and Mary*, who took the song into the Billboard Hot 100 and established Lightfoot's reputation in the [[united-states]].
- First recorded in 1965 by the influential Canadian folk duo *Ian & Sylvia*, whose enthusiastic advocacy led legendary manager *Albert Grossman* to sign Lightfoot to his roster.
- Uses the contrast between the freedom of travelers inside the "big silver bird" and the grounded, shivering narrator with "no place to go" to critique class alienation and geographic displacement.
- Covered by *Elvis Presley* on his 1972 studio album *Elvis Now*, and subsequently performed before an estimated global audience of over one billion people during the 1973 satellite telecast *Aloha from Hawaii*.
- Included by *Bob Dylan* on his eclectic 1970 double album *Self Portrait*, cementing Lightfoot's standing among the preeminent narrative lyricists of the North American folk tradition.
- Served as the opening track of Lightfoot's 1966 debut album *Lightfoot!*, establishing his signature combination of acoustic fingerpicking, baritone delivery, and vivid northern imagery.
- Recorded across decades by diverse music luminaries including *Judy Collins*, *Neil Young*, *Paul Weller*, and bluegrass guitar virtuoso *Tony Rice*, who made it a staple of his live repertoire.

## connections

- [[eagles]] — premier American country-rock group whose acoustic instrumentation, three-part vocal arrangements, and themes of western rootlessness directly descended from Lightfoot's folk innovations.
- [[united-states]] — country where the song was originally drafted at Los Angeles International Airport and where its major commercial cover versions became radio hits.
- [[industrialization]] — technological shift highlighted in the lyrical contrast between modern commercial jet aviation and the impoverished laborer stranded on the gravel tarmac below.
- [[modernism]] — cultural era reflecting themes of psychological estrangement, transit, loneliness, and the emotional desolation wrought by mechanized urban progress.
- [[american-literature]] — storytelling heritage sharing the song's thematic focus on nomadic drifters, cross-country transit, economic hardship, and yearning for home.
- [[cold-war]] — historical era whose mid-century economic expansion and burgeoning commercial aviation industry formed the backdrop for Lightfoot's portrait of modern travel.
- [[western-film]] — cinematic genre whose archetypal drifters, open horizons, and melancholic wanderers parallel the wandering traveler depicted in the song's verses.
