## summary

**Galaga** is a seminal fixed-shooter arcade video game developed and published by Namco in 1981 in *Japan* and North America. Serving as the direct commercial sequel to 1979's *Galaxian*, the title revolutionized the golden age of arcade video games through its dynamic enemy flight patterns, dual-fighter mechanic, and bonus stages. It remains one of the most widely recognized, ported, and enduring arcade works in gaming history, establishing core design tropes for competitive score-chasing and shoot-'em-up titles worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Developed by Namco designer *Shigeru Yokoyama* and released in September 1981, building directly on the hardware and foundations of *Galaxian*.
- Introduced the iconic tractor-beam gameplay mechanic, wherein enemy "Boss Galaga" craft can capture the player's starfighter from the bottom of the screen.
- Players can deliberately sacrifice a ship to the tractor beam and subsequently destroy the capturing alien to retrieve it, forming a powerful side-by-side "Dual Fighter" that doubles offensive firepower.
- Featured structured "Challenging Stages" every few rounds where alien waves fly through choreographed aerial formations without firing, rewarding players with massive bonus points for achieving a perfect hit rate.
- Distinguished itself from predecessors like *Space Invaders* by having enemies swoop onto the screen in cinematic loops and dive-bomb maneuvers rather than moving in rigid lateral rows.
- Achieved immense arcade ubiquity across North America through its distribution deal with Midway, making it a foundational staple of early competitive video gaming and competitive arcade culture alongside *Pac-Man* and *Donkey Kong*.
- A prominent Easter egg or minigame variant was later famously incorporated into the loading screen of Namco's 1995 home console release of *Ridge Racer* on the PlayStation.

## connections

- [[japan]] — country where Namco originated, engineered, and debuted the original arcade cabinet.

- [[first-person-shooter]] — genre of action gaming that eventually supplanted fixed overhead and scrolling shooters in mainstream popularity.
- [[minecraft]] — modern gaming phenomenon that frequently pays homage to foundational arcade milestones.
