## summary

**Frozen** is a 2013 American computer-animated musical fantasy film produced by *Walt Disney Animation Studios* and directed by *Chris Buck* and *Jennifer Lee*. Loosely inspired by *Hans Christian Andersen*'s 1844 fairy tale *The Snow Queen*, it chronicles the perilous journey of Princess *Anna*, who sets off alongside an eccentric iceman, his reindeer, and a magical snowman to locate her estranged sister, Queen *Elsa*, whose cryokinetic sorcery has inadvertently trapped their homeland of *Arendelle* in eternal winter. Generating unprecedented cultural resonance across the [[united-states]] and the world, it won two Academy Awards and redefined contemporary animated cinema by centering fraternal solidarity over conventional romance.

## you gotta know

- Famously subverts classical fairy-tale storytelling when Prince *Hans* of the Southern Isles betrays Anna, revealing that the requisite act of true love needed to thaw her frozen heart is not romantic devotion, but Anna's selfless sacrifice to save Elsa.
- The musical centerpiece *Let It Go*, composed by *Kristen Anderson-Lopez* and *Robert Lopez* and sung by Broadway star *Idina Menzel*, became a massive global sensation, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Song and reaching the top five on the *Billboard Hot 100*.
- The immense emotional power of *Let It Go* fundamentally altered the film's production, convincing the screenwriters to discard their original plan of depicting Elsa as an irredeemable villain in favor of making her a sympathetic, misunderstood figure.
- Features a celebrated comedic performance by *Josh Gad* as the magical, naive snowman *Olaf*, whose jaunty Broadway-style musical number *In Summer* humorously contrasts his oblivious love of heat with his frozen anatomy.
- Reached nearly 1.3 billion dollars in global box-office receipts, becoming the highest-grossing animated feature film of its era and launching a multi-billion-dollar franchise that culminated in the 2019 sequel [[frozen-2]].
- The visual architecture and costumes of *Arendelle* were deeply influenced by Norwegian folk traditions, incorporating wooden stave churches, traditional *rosemåling* floral painting, and regional *bunad* garments.
- Walt Disney had personally attempted to adapt *The Snow Queen* beginning in the late 1930s, but the production stalled for over seventy years in development hell because screenwriters struggled to make the cold titular protagonist relatable.

- [[united-states]] — country where Disney artists, animators, and songwriters developed this landmark entry of the *Disney Revival* era.
- [[film-editing]] — editorial craft highlighted during the musical montage of *Do You Want to Build a Snowman?*, spanning years of childhood alienation in minutes.
- [[camera-movement]] — sweeping virtual cinematography showcased in the dynamic swooping camera work that builds Elsa's crystalline ice palace.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — visual composition displayed in the warm, cluttered castle interiors contrasted against the vast, desolate blue-and-white mountain landscapes.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — symbolic design wherein Elsa's protective gloves and frozen coronation accessories physically embody the repression of her innate identity.
- [[tragedy]] — narrative element framing the sudden death of the sisters' parents at sea and the profound psychological trauma of Elsa's isolated upbringing.
