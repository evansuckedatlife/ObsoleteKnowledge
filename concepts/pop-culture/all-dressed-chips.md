## summary

**All-Dressed Chips** are a beloved Canadian potato chip flavor created in the province of Quebec during the 1970s, characterized by an all-in-one blend of barbecue, ketchup, sour cream and onion, and salt and vinegar seasonings. Known in Francophone markets as *chips assaisonnées*, the snack combines sweet, salty, savory, and sharply acidic notes into a distinctively complex taste profile. Ubiquitous across Canada, the variety has developed an international cult following as an iconic culinary symbol of Canadian snacking identity.

## you gotta know

- Introduced to the consumer market in 1978 by the Quebec snack food manufacturer *Yum Yum*, where the seasoning was officially named *assaisonnées* before becoming widely translated as *all-dressed*.
- Blends four standard potato chip flavorings—tangy barbecue, vinegary tomato ketchup, creamy sour cream and onion, and pungent salt and vinegar—to produce a robust multi-layered glaze.
- Achieved nationwide ubiquity under the *Ruffles* brand produced by Frito-Lay Canada, whose thick ridge-cut design held the heavy coating of reddish spice powder particularly well.
- Held long-standing status as a regional Canadian exclusivity, prompting cross-border snacking tourism and international snack-box ordering among displaced Canadians living abroad.
- Debuted across the American retail market in late 2015 as a limited-time promotional flavor by *Ruffles*, which proved so exceptionally popular that Frito-Lay made it a permanent national product for several years.
- Employs the Canadian colloquialism *all-dressed*, an English translation of the Quebec French phrasing *toute garnie*, traditionally used when requesting hamburgers, hot dogs, or pizzas with every available topping.
- Represents a major fixture in Canadian food identity alongside other distinctive national culinary favorites such as poutine, ketchup chips, butter tarts, and Halifax donairs.
- Features prominent references across popular television shows, including the mockumentary series *Trailer Park Boys* and the sitcom *Letterkenny*, cementing its status as an everyday cultural touchstone.
