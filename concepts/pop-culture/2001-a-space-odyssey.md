## summary

**2001: A Space Odyssey** is a monumental 1968 science fiction film directed and produced by *Stanley Kubrick*, with a screenplay written collaboratively with English science fiction author *Arthur C. Clarke*. Spanning prehistoric Africa, orbital space stations, lunar excavations, and the outer reaches of Jupiter, the production contemplates human evolutionary origin, technological overreach, and encounters with unknowable extraterrestrial intelligence. Renowned for its groundbreaking practical effects, minimal dialogue, and audacious deployment of classical symphonic music, the feature is celebrated as a peerless masterpiece of global cinema.

## you gotta know

- Famous for the historic match cut in its opening movement, jumping millions of years from a primitive bone club thrown aloft to a weaponized satellite in Earth orbit.
- Features the sentient shipboard artificial intelligence *HAL 9000*, voiced with eerie calm by actor *Douglas Rain*, who suffers an internal logical breakdown and systematically kills crewmen.
- Divided into four broad movements: *The Dawn of Man*, the lunar excavation of the *TMA-1* monolith, the Jupiter mission aboard the *Discovery One*, and *Jupiter and Beyond the Infinite*.
- Utilized classical compositions in lieu of an original orchestral score, indelibly linking its imagery to *Also sprach Zarathustra* by *Richard Strauss* and *The Blue Danube* by *Johann Strauss II*.
- Showcased practical visual illusions masterminded by *Douglas Trumbull*, *Wally Veevers*, and *Con Pederson*, notably front-projection landscape photography and rotating centrifuge sets.
- Concludes with the psychedelic slit-scan *Star Gate* sequence, culminating in astronaut *David Bowman* aging within an enigmatic neoclassical chamber and transforming into the *Star Child*.
- Created simultaneously alongside *Clarke*'s prose novel, though the two works exhibit minor divergences regarding destinations and monolith mechanics.

## connections

- [[film-editing]] — demonstrated through the celebrated bone-to-satellite cut, arguably the most famous temporal jump cut in cinematic history.
- [[camera-movement]] — manifested in sweeping tracking shots and floating point-of-view perspectives that mimic zero-gravity weightlessness.
- [[cold-war]] — informs the narrative through tensions between American and Soviet space administrators aboard the orbital transit station.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — displayed in pristine white spacecraft interiors, geometric control panels, and the striking isolation of deep space.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — uses the unyielding black monolith as an emblem of cosmic knowledge, alien evolution, and metaphysical transcendence.
- [[soviet-montage]] — builds upon ideological editing principles to synthesize disparate conceptual eras of human tool usage.
- [[existentialism]] — permeates Bowman's confrontation with loneliness, artificial consciousness, and transformation into a cosmic entity.
- [[101-dalmatians]] — exemplifies contemporaneous 1960s British film industry craftsmanship pushing technological visual boundaries.
