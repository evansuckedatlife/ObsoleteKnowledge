## summary

**Smarties** is the commercial brand name shared by two iconic, completely distinct confectionery products popular across opposite sides of the Atlantic. In the Commonwealth, the name designates vibrant, sugar-coated, oblate spheroidal chocolate dragées created in York, *England*, by confectionery pioneer *Rowntree & Co.* in 1937 and later manufactured globally by *Nestlé*. In the *United States*, the brand designates stacked, chalky, disc-shaped tablet candies made of compressed dextrose, invented in 1949 by *Edward Dee* of *Ce De Candy* and celebrated as an indelible staple of Halloween trick-or-treating.

## you gotta know

- Introduced originally to the British public in 1937 under the product name "Chocolate Beans" by confectionery giant *Rowntree & Co.* in York, *England*, before being rebranded under the catchy trade name that achieved global distribution.
- Distinguished in the Commonwealth by their traditional eight vibrant candy shell colors: red, orange, blue, green, yellow, pink, purple, and brown, with the orange dragée historically flavored with natural orange oil in its chocolate core.
- Packaged for over six decades inside a beloved cylindrical cardboard tube featuring an iconic, resealable plastic pop-off cap stamped with an embossed letter of the alphabet to promote childhood spelling games.
- Became the subject of widespread public attention in 2006 when *Nestlé* eliminated synthetic artificial food colorings from British production, temporarily retiring the beloved blue variety until a natural spirulina algae pigment was successfully engineered.
- Produced independently in North America since 1949 as dry, tart, pastel-colored compressed dextrose wafer rolls by the *Dee* family through *Smarties Candy Company* (originally *Ce De Candy*).
- Known exclusively under the alternate commercial trade name *Rockets* within Canada to prevent legal trademark confusion with the pre-existing British chocolate product.
- Embedded deeply in global consumer lore through the memorable British advertising slogan: "Do you eat the red ones last?", establishing an enduring psychological eating ritual celebrated across generations.
