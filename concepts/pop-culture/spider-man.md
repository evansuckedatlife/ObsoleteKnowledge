## summary

**Spider-Man**, the superhero alter ego of science prodigy **Peter Parker**, is a flagship cultural icon created by writer *Stan Lee* and artist *Steve Ditko* for *Marvel Comics*. Debuting in August 1962 in the [[united-states]], the character fundamentally revolutionized serialized narrative by introducing a flawed, self-doubting, and financially strained adolescent who functioned as an independent hero rather than an established hero's teenage sidekick. Rooted in the bustling urban landscape of [[new-york-city]], his enduring appeal stems from the central moral burden that personal ability entails unavoidable social duty.

## you gotta know

- Debuted in *Amazing Fantasy* #15 (1962), a twilight anthology issue that became one of the most commercially successful and valuable issues in the history of graphic narrative in the [[united-states]].
- Adopted his guiding philosophical creed, "With great power there must also come great responsibility," after his selfish failure to halt a fleeing burglar directly caused the shooting death of his beloved surrogate father, *Uncle Ben*.
- Operates primarily across the high-rises and boroughs of [[new-york-city]], employing custom-engineered wrist-mounted mechanical web-shooters, an precognitive danger alert known as "spider-sense," and the ability to cling electrostatically to solid surfaces.
- Maintained a precarious livelihood in classic stories by selling freelance action photographs of himself to *The Daily Bugle*, a sensationalist newspaper run by the belligerent editor *J. Jonah Jameson*, who routinely demonized him as a masked menace.
- Possesses one of modern fiction's most acclaimed rogues galleries, featuring chemically altered or technologically enhanced foes such as the *Green Goblin* (*Norman Osborn*), *Doctor Octopus* (*Otto Octavius*), *Venom*, and *Mysterio*.
- Suffered a defining emotional and narrative catastrophe in 1973 when the *Green Goblin* threw *Gwen Stacy* from the George Washington Bridge, culminating in her fatal neck snap and marking the transition of the comic medium into darker, more realistic thematic territory.
- Starred in multiple blockbuster cinematic adaptations that dominated global box offices, culminating in multiverse crossovers such as [[spider-man-no-way-home]].
