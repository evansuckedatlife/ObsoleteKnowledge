## summary

**Emily of New Moon** is a classic 1923 Canadian coming-of-age novel written by *Lucy Maud Montgomery*, introducing the passionate young orphan *Emily Byrd Starr*. Set on Prince Edward Island within the wider cultural sphere of the *[[british-empire]]*, the book explores the artistic struggles, fierce independence, and vivid inner life of an aspiring child writer. Considered more autobiographical and psychologically complex than Montgomery's earlier *Anne of Green Gables*, it spawned two sequels and remains a staple of twentieth-century children's literature.

## you gotta know

- Features the orphaned protagonist *Emily Byrd Starr*, who is sent to live at New Moon farm with her stern maternal relatives, Aunt Elizabeth, Aunt Laura, and Cousin Jimmy Murray.
- Written by *Lucy Maud Montgomery* in 1923 as the first entry in a trilogy that includes *Emily Climbs* and *Emily's Quest*.
- Emphasizes the heroine's intense dedication to becoming a professional author, frequently depicting her writing private letters to her deceased father on discarded bill paper.
- Portrays Emily experiencing mystic trances and moments of acute aesthetic ecstasy that she terms "the flash," reflecting an innate connection to poetic wonder.
- Introduces memorable lifelong companions, including the fierce and fiery *Ilse Burnley*, the artistic prodigy *Teddy Kent*, and the loyal friend *Perry Miller*.
- Contrasts with the lighter, sunnier tone of *Anne of Green Gables* by incorporating darker themes of family grief, psychic intuition, and emotional suppression.
- Adapted into a popular Canadian television series produced by Salter Street Films that aired in the late 1990s.

## connections
