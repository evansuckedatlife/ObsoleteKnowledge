# Commedia dell'arte

## summary

**Commedia dell'arte** was a form of Italian improvisational theater that flourished from the 16th to 18th centuries, featuring masked stock characters (like Arlecchino, Pantalone, and the innamorati) performing scenarios with recurring plots and physical gags—acrobatic slapstick, pratfalls, and wit. The tradition originated from Italian street theater and deeply influenced European drama, opera, and comedy; performers relied on improvisation within established character types and plot structures, creating a democratic, itinerant theater form accessible to common people and beloved across social classes.

## you gotta know

- *Stock characters* defined Commedia: *Arlecchino* (Harlequin) was a clever servant in a diamond-patterned costume; *Pantalone*, an elderly merchant, was often outwitted and ridiculed; *Il Capitano*, a blustering soldier, was mocked for cowardice and vanity; lovers (*innamorati*) were usually unmasked and spoke in elevated language, providing contrast.
- *Masks* were iconic: all characters except lovers wore exaggerated masks revealing type and personality at a glance; the mask allowed actors to push physicality and caricature without restraint, enabling the ensemble speed and clarity that made improvisation possible.
- *Scenarios* (plot outlines) were prepared in advance, but dialogue and business (physical comedy) were *improvised*; actors had stock speeches (lazzi) and gags they deployed, allowing ensemble cohesion despite spontaneity.
- *Physical comedy* and *slapstick* were central: actors trained as acrobats and dancers; pratfalls, chases, sword play, and absurd situations provided humor accessible to illiterate and literate audiences alike, transcending language barriers.
- Commedia emerged in the 16th century from Italian street entertainers and evolved into professional troupes that toured Europe; troupes like *Gelosi* achieved celebrity status and performed for royalty.
- *Molière* (Jean-Baptiste Poquelin) was heavily influenced by Commedia; his comedies incorporate stock characters, scenarios, and slapstick while introducing more sophisticated dialogue and character depth.
- Commedia directly influenced *opera*: characters, plot types, and comic situations from Commedia appear in operas by *Mozart* (Marriage of Figaro) and *Rossini*; the tradition provides the comedic foundation for comic opera (*opera buffa*).
