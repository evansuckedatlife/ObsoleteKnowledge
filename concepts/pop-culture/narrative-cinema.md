## summary

**Narrative cinema** is filmmaking that tells a story through visual and temporal sequencing—the techniques by which cinema makes meaning from shots, scenes, and scenes arranged in time. Unlike literature's narrative, which unfolds through words, cinema's narrative unfolds through images, editing, and structure. Understanding how cinema tells stories requires understanding how shots combine, how time is manipulated, and how the viewer's attention is guided.

## you gotta know

- *Editing* is cinema's primary narrative tool; it creates cause, effect, simultaneity, and contrast by juxtaposing shots; editing is what transforms raw footage into *story*.
- *Montage* is the principle that meaning emerges from shot combination, not from the shots themselves; *Eisenstein* showed that two shot images placed together create a third meaning, unique to their juxtaposition.
- *Shot scale and angle* carry narrative meaning: close-ups isolate emotion or importance, wide shots establish space and context, high angles diminish characters, low angles empower them.
- *Point of view* in cinema can be literal (subjective camera showing what a character sees) or implicit (how the editing and framing align the viewer with a character's perspective).
- *Non-linear structure* becomes possible in cinema in ways that differ from literature; flashbacks, parallel action, and time-jumps can be instantaneous through editing (unlike prose, which must narrate them).
- *Intertitles* (text cards in silent film) were cinema's first explicit narrative device; they announced causality, time, and dialogue, establishing conventions adopted even in modern films (title sequences, captions).
- Cinema can show simultaneous action across multiple locations in ways literature cannot, creating a unique form of narrative simultaneity and cross-cutting.

## connections

- [[film-editing]] — the primary means by which cinema constructs narrative; editing *is* narrative cinema's central technology.
- [[citizen-kane-twist]] — an exemplar of non-linear narrative cinema; its multiple-narrator structure revolutionized how cinema could tell stories.
- [[soviet-montage]] — the theoretical framework that explains how shot combination creates meaning; foundational to narrative cinema.
- [[point-of-view]] — how cinema guides viewer alignment with characters; essential to understanding narrative cinema's emotional register.
- [[unreliable-narrator]] — a literary concept that cinema adapted; *Rashomon* (which is not in your hub_menu but a foundational work) demonstrates cinema's capacity for narratorial unreliability.
