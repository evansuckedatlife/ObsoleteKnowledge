## summary

**Gloria Swanson** was an American actress and producer who rose to become one of the reigning superstars of the *[[silent-film]]* era during the 1920s in the *[[united-states]]*. After her mainstream career faded with the dawn of talking pictures, she staged one of cinema's most famous comebacks by starring as the forgotten silent star *Norma Desmond* in *[[billy-wilder]]*'s 1950 film noir masterpiece *Sunset Boulevard*. Her searing portrayal of tragic vanity and faded Hollywood glamour cemented her legendary status and earned her a third Academy Award nomination for Best Actress.

## you gotta know

- Began her screen journey at the Essanay Studios in Chicago before joining Mack Sennett's Keystone Studios, where she appeared in slapstick comedy shorts alongside cross-eyed comedian Chester Conklin.
- Achieved meteoric fame through a series of romantic dramas directed by Cecil B. DeMille, including *Don't Change Your Husband* (1919) and *Male and Female* (1919), establishing her as Hollywood's supreme fashion icon and trendsetter.
- Formed her own independent production company through United Artists with financing from her lover Joseph P. Kennedy, producing and starring in the controversial melodrama *Sadie Thompson* (1928), for which she received her first Academy Award nomination.
- Starred in the troubled, unfinished silent epic *Queen Kelly* (1929), directed by the tyrannical Erich von Stroheim, whose lavish eccentricities and sexually daring themes caused Kennedy to halt production, a disaster later excerpted within *Sunset Boulevard*.
- Played *Norma Desmond* in *Sunset Boulevard* opposite *[[william-holden]]*, delivering iconic lines such as "I *am* big! It's the *pictures* that got small!" and concluding with the famous descent toward the newsreel cameras: "All right, Mr. DeMille, I'm ready for my close-up."
- Real-life silent film icons Buster Keaton, H. B. Warner, and Anna Q. Nilsson appeared alongside her in *Sunset Boulevard* as Norma's bridge companions, whom William Holden's character cynically dubs "the waxworks."
- Successfully transitioned to television, radio, and Broadway later in life, earning a Tony nomination in 1971 for *Butterflies Are Free* and advocating passionately for vegetarianism and organic health foods.

## connections

- [[billy-wilder]] — the Austrian-born auteur who directed and co-wrote *Sunset Boulevard*, resurrecting Swanson's career with the role of a lifetime.
- [[william-holden]] — co-star who played the desperate, cynical screenwriter Joe Gillis murdered in Norma Desmond's swimming pool.
- [[silent-film]] — the revolutionary artistic era of pantomime and visual storytelling in which Swanson first achieved global stardom.
- [[charlie-chaplin]] — fellow United Artists founding associate and pioneer of physical comedy whose silent dominance mirrored Swanson's dramatic reign.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose booming 20th-century studio system created both Swanson's superstardom and the dark satire of her comeback.
- [[gillian-anderson]] — fellow celebrated dramatic actress who similarly navigated the perils of industry typecasting after achieving iconic status in television.
