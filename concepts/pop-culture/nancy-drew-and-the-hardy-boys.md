## summary

**Nancy Drew and the Hardy Boys** are landmark American juvenile detective series created by publisher **Edward Stratemeyer** through the Stratemeyer Syndicate beginning in the late 1920s. Starring teen sleuth **Nancy Drew** alongside brothers Frank and Joe Hardy (**The Hardy Boys**), these long-running mystery franchises were produced by rotating stables of ghostwriters under the collective pen names *Carolyn Keene* and *Franklin W. Dixon*. Selling hundreds of millions of copies worldwide, they defined the adolescent mystery genre and pioneered modern corporate syndicate publishing in children's fiction.

## you gotta know

- Created by book packaging pioneer *Edward Stratemeyer*, who designed detailed plot outlines before hiring freelance ghostwriters to write the prose under fixed-fee work-for-hire contracts.
- The Hardy Boys debuted first in 1927 with *The Tower Treasure*, ghostwritten primarily by Canadian author Leslie McFarlane under the corporate pseudonym *Franklin W. Dixon*.
- Nancy Drew debuted in 1930 with *The Secret of the Old Clock*, largely penned by journalist Mildred Wirt Benson under the house pseudonym *Carolyn Keene*.
- The Hardy brothers operate out of the fictional coastal town of Bayport with their detective father Fenton Hardy, driving their sleek sedan and solving coastal smuggling and theft rings.
- Nancy Drew resides in fictional River Heights with her attorney father Carson Drew, solving local mysteries with best friends Bess Marvin and George Fayne while driving her signature blue roadster.
- In 1959, the Stratemeyer Syndicate began an extensive multi-year revision program to shorten texts, modernize pacing, and systematically remove racial and ethnic stereotypes from the early installments.
- The two franchises officially merged for shared adventures in the 1970s television adaptation and collaborative crossover book lines like the *SuperMystery* series.
