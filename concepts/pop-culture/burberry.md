## summary

**Burberry**, incorporated as the **Burberry Group**, is an iconic British luxury fashion house established in 1856 by former draper's apprentice **Thomas Burberry** in Basingstoke, [[england]]. World-renowned for inventing weatherproof outerwear textiles, the brand fundamentally transformed military and civilian dress through its functional trench coat and ubiquitous checked pattern. Its transition from a nineteenth-century utilitarian field outfitter into an elite luxury staple illustrates the intersection of industrial manufacturing, imperial utility, and global fashion prestige.

## you gotta know

- Invented *gabardine* in 1879, a revolutionary, tightly woven, breathable, and water-repellent twill textile that eliminated the stifling rubberized fabrics traditionally used for wet-weather garments.
- Designed the standard modern *trench coat* for British officers serving on the Western Front during [[world-war-i]], outfitting garments with utilitarian shoulder epaulettes, protective storm flaps, and metallic D-rings for carrying field gear.
- Created the world-famous *Burberry check* (first known as the Haymarket check)—a camel, black, red, and ivory tartan design introduced in the 1920s as an interior lining for trench coats.
- Outfitted celebrated polar expeditions throughout the heroic age of exploration, providing durable weatherproof suits for *Roald Amundsen* during his 1911 journey to the South Pole and *Ernest Shackleton* on his 1914 *Endurance* mission.
- Earned prestigious Royal Warrants of Appointment as official weather-proofers to the British Royal Family, granted successively by *Queen Elizabeth II* and *King Charles III*.
- Executed an ambitious brand reinvention in the early 2000s under the leadership of chief executive *Rose Marie Bravo* and creative director *Christopher Bailey*, repositioning the heritage brand at the forefront of runway luxury.
- Confronted a severe reputational crisis in the late 1990s and early 2000s when counterfeit check goods became heavily associated with British football terrace firms and working-class "chav" subcultures, prompting the company to temporarily halt production of check baseball caps.
- Led the traditional luxury sector in digital integration, pioneering live-streamed runway shows, immediate consumer purchase options, and curated acoustic music performances.

## connections

- [[england]] — historic home and manufacturing base where the company was founded and whose national aesthetic the house came to symbolize.
- [[world-war-i]] — defining conflict that catalyzed the mass adoption of military trench coats and cemented the company's civilian utility.
- [[british-empire]] — worldwide colonial apparatus that distributed durable British outdoor textiles and uniforms across global garrisons and trade outposts.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of mechanized spinning and fabric engineering that made the large-scale industrial manufacture of gabardine possible.
- [[modernism]] — early twentieth-century design movement whose focus on sleek functionalism and industrial utility matched the utilitarian ethos of the trench coat.
- [[france]] — European center of haute couture with which the British fashion house competed while building flagship boutiques along the Parisian avenues.
- [[japan]] — vital international market where the fashion house formed successful licensed domestic lines known as *Burberry Blue Label* and *Burberry Black Label*.
