## summary

**David Cronenberg** is an influential Canadian film director, screenwriter, and actor celebrated as the primary architect of the body horror cinematic subgenre. Emerging during the 1970s, his films examine the psychological disintegration of identity through visceral mutations, biological infections, technological interfaces, and surgical transformations. Widely recognized with major international festival accolades, his provocative filmography bridges subterranean cult cinema and prestigious psychological drama.

## you gotta know

- Established his reputation as a pioneering master of biological horror through transgressive Canadian genre pictures such as *Shivers*, *Rabid*, and *The Brood*.
- Directed the 1986 science fiction classic *The Fly*, starring *Jeff Goldblum* as the hubristic scientist *Seth Brundle*, whose telepod fusion with an insect serves as an agonizing physical allegory for terminal illness.
- Helmed the 1983 cyberpunk masterwork *Videodrome*, starring *James Woods*, which famously critiques television consumption, media indoctrination, and sensory hallucination with the phrase "Long live the New Flesh."
- Adapted notoriously demanding literary texts, including *William S. Burroughs*'s *Naked Lunch* using drug-fueled bio-typewriters and *J.G. Ballard*'s *Crash*, which investigates erotic obsession tied to catastrophic automobile accidents.
- Formed a celebrated creative partnership with actor *Viggo Mortensen*, yielding psychological thrillers such as *A History of Violence* and *Eastern Promises*, the latter remembered for a brutal naked bathhouse knife duel.
- Directed *Dead Ringers* in 1988, which featured *Jeremy Irons* playing twin gynecologists sliding into drug dependency, shared psychological collapse, and surgical obsession.
- Returned to his quintessential biomechanical themes with the 2022 speculative feature *Crimes of the Future*, presenting a dark near-future where bodily surgery and accelerated organ evolution are embraced as avant-garde performance art.

- [[realism]] — stark, objective camera style that treats extraordinary bodily horrors with clinical and dispassionate photographic detachment.
- [[modernism]] — avant-garde engagement with post-human identity, narrative fragmentation, and intellectual transgressions against bodily boundaries.
- [[tragedy]] — narrative structure tracing ambitious scientists and intellectual figures whose rational experiments unleash self-destructive physical undoing.
- [[dark]] — thematic counterpart exploring the biological degradation, physical mutations, and existential despair of individuals trapped in inexorable torment.
