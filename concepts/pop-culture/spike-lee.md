## summary

**Spike Lee**, born **Shelton Jackson Lee**, is an acclaimed American filmmaker, screenwriter, producer, and actor whose production company *40 Acres and a Mule Filmworks* has generated some of the most influential cinematic works of modern cinema. Emerging out of [[new-york-city]] in the mid-1980s, Lee revitalized independent filmmaking in the [[united-states]] with provocative examinations of systemic racism, urban politics, police brutality, and Black cultural heritage. His signature aesthetic merges daring technical bravura, kinetic cutting, and theatrical direct address to confront audiences with the complex sociopolitical realities of contemporary America.

## you gotta know

- Rose to international prominence with his breakout low-budget independent comedy *She's Gotta Have It* (1986), in which he introduced the fast-talking sneaker enthusiast character *Mars Blackmon*, whom he later reprised in iconic commercial campaigns with *Michael Jordan*.
- Directed the masterwork *Do the Right Thing* (1989), chronicling rising racial hostilities on a scorching summer day in the Bedford-Stuyvesant neighborhood of Brooklyn, culminating in the killing of *Radio Raheem* by police officers and the destruction of *Sal's Pizzeria*.
- Employs a recognizable formal trademark known as the "double dolly" shot, in which both the camera and the actor are pulled forward together on a synchronized dolly track, making the subject appear to float through the environment detached from ground movement.
- Directed the monumental biographical drama *Malcolm X* (1992), featuring an Oscar-nominated performance by *Denzel Washington* and a climactic address filmed on location at historical pilgrimage sites in Mecca.
- Earned his first competitive Academy Award for Best Adapted Screenplay for *BlacKkKlansman* (2018), which detailed the true account of *Ron Stallworth*, a Black police detective who successfully infiltrated the Colorado Springs chapter of the Ku Klux Klan.
- Chronicled the human devastation and governmental negligence surrounding Hurricane Katrina in his monumental four-part documentary series *When the Levees Broke: A Requiem in Four Acts* (2006).
- Branded his major directorial releases with the proprietary stylistic signature "A Spike Lee Joint," affirming an auteurist authority across studio and independent productions alike.
