## summary

**Erich von Stroheim** was an Austrian-American film director, screenwriter, and actor whose uncompromising artistry shaped the golden age of [[silent-film]]. Known in Hollywood promotional circles as "The Man You Love to Hate," he revolutionized cinematic naturalism and lavish production design before being sidelined by studios for his obsessive perfectionism and budget overruns. In his later acting career, he achieved enduring fame by portraying the tragic butler *Max von Mayerling* in *Sunset Boulevard*, bringing real-world Hollywood tragedy directly into the film's narrative.

## you gotta know

- Immigrated to the [[united-states]] from Vienna in 1909 and fabricated an aristocratic Prussian military heritage, adopting the aristocratic "von" to secure studio prestige.
- Began working in cinema under [[d-w-griffith]], serving as an assistant director and costume consultant on landmark historical epics.
- Directed his masterwork *Greed* in 1924, an uncompromising adaptation of *Frank Norris's* novel *McTeague* shot on location in San Francisco and Death Valley.
- *Greed* originally ran over nine hours before *Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer* forcibly cut it down to around two hours, discarding the excised footage to the director's perpetual heartbreak.
- Clashed repeatedly with legendary production heads like *Irving Thalberg* over exorbitant expenditures, authentic silk underwear on extras, and meticulous [[mise-en-scene]].
- Starred memorably in [[france]] in *Jean Renoir's* anti-war classic *La Grande Illusion* as the aristocratic German prison commandant *Captain von Rauffenstein*.
- Earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actor in [[billy-wilder]]'s *Sunset Boulevard*, playing a former silent director turned devoted servant to *Norma Desmond*.

## connections

- [[billy-wilder]] — the master filmmaker who cast him as the devoted butler and former director *Max* in *Sunset Boulevard*.
- [[silent-film]] — the expressive cinematic movement in which he produced his most ambitious and lavish auteur masterworks.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — the meticulous visual staging and authentic material detailing that defined his uncompromising directorial philosophy.
- [[d-w-griffith]] — his early mentor and employer on groundbreaking silent productions like *Intolerance*.
- [[film-editing]] — the crucial post-production craft that became the primary battleground between his sprawling artistic visions and studio accountants.
- [[realism]] — the artistic movement advocating unflinching psychological and environmental truth that inspired his location shooting in *Greed*.
- [[world-war-i]] — the global conflict during which his caricatured portrayals of brutal Prussian officers established his signature screen persona.
