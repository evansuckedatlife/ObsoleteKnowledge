## summary

**Lost in the Barrens** is an adventure novel by Canadian author and environmentalist *Farley Mowat*, published in 1956 and later issued under the title *Two Against the North*. The story recounts the perilous survival ordeal of two teenage boys—one white and one Cree—stranded across the desolate subarctic tundra of Northern Canada. Celebrated as a classic of wilderness survival literature, the book was awarded the Governor General's Award for Juvenile Fiction in 1956.

## you gotta know

- Written by prominent Canadian naturalist *Farley Mowat*, celebrated for his wildlife advocacy and controversial nonfiction classic *Never Cry Wolf*.
- Centers on the friendship between *Jamie Macnair*, an orphaned white teenager sent to live with his uncle in Manitoba, and *Awasin*, a skilled Cree youth and son of a local band chief.
- Follows the boys as they accompany an Indigenous hunting expedition north into the Barren Lands to aid a starving group of *Chipewyan* hunters.
- Chronicles their accidental separation from the main party after pursuing a canoe trip through uncharted northern waterways, leaving them stranded without supplies.
- Depicts their cooperative struggle to build a stone-and-moss cabin, hunt caribou, craft winter garments, and survive the brutal subarctic winter before returning south.
- Won the prestigious *Governor General's Award* for English-language children's literature in 1956 and the *Canadian Library Association Book of the Year for Children Award* in 1958.
- Inspired a 1961 sequel novel by Mowat titled *The Curse of the Viking Grave*, as well as a 1990 television film adaptation starring *Nicholas Shields* and *Michael Mahonen*.
