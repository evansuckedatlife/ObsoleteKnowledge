## summary

**Fat Lip** is a 2001 breakout punk rock and rap-rock anthem by Canadian band *Sum 41*, released as the lead single from their debut studio album, *All Killer No Filler*. Written by frontman *Deryck Whibley* and drummer *Steve Jocz*, the song fuses aggressive skate punk guitar riffs with rapid, trade-off hip-hop vocal cadences. The track reached number one on the *Billboard* Modern Rock Tracks chart, achieving massive rotation on *MTV* and establishing itself as a generational staple of early-2000s youth culture.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the signature breakthrough single for Ajax, Ontario band *Sum 41*, consisting of *Deryck Whibley*, *Dave Baksh*, *Jason McCaslin*, and *Steve Jocz*.
- Features unique alternating vocal duties, trading verses among *Whibley*, *Jocz*, and *Baksh* to emulate dynamic rap groups like the *Beastie Boys* alongside heavy punk riffs.
- Opens with its instantly recognizable spoken-word refrain: "Storming through the party like my name was El Niño."
- Centers lyrically on adolescent rebellion, suburban boredom, social nonconformity, and resisting societal pressures to assimilate into adulthood.
- Catapulted to number one on the *Billboard* Modern Rock Tracks chart in the *[[united-states]]*, driving its parent album *All Killer No Filler* to multi-platinum sales.
- Accompanied by an iconic music video directed by *Marc Klasfeld*, filmed at a liquor store in Pomona, California, featuring local suburban youth performing impromptu rap verses.
- Widely licensed across prominent action sports media, appearing in video games such as *Guitar Hero* and EA Sports titles, cementing its legacy in early-2000s pop-punk.
