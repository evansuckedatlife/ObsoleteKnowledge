## summary

**Dolly Parton**, born **Dolly Rebecca Parton**, is a celebrated American singer-songwriter, multi-instrumentalist, actress, and philanthropist revered as one of the most significant figures in country music history. Born into rural poverty in the Great Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee, she achieved national fame alongside *Porter Wagoner* before building a legendary solo career characterized by narrative songwriting and crossover pop success. Renowned for evergreen compositions like *Jolene*, *9 to 5*, and *I Will Always Love You*, she is equally celebrated for global humanitarian work, including founding the childhood literacy initiative known as the *Imagination Library*.

## you gotta know

- Composed her famous goodbye tribute to Porter Wagoner, *I Will Always Love You*, on the very same day she penned the iconic jealousy anthem *Jolene*.
- Wrote her signature autobiographical song *Coat of Many Colors* on the back of a dry-cleaning receipt while on a tour bus, recalling the patchwork garment sewn by her mother while telling the biblical story of [[joseph]].
- Starred in the 1980 workplace feminist comedy *9 to 5* alongside *Jane Fonda* and *Lily Tomlin*, earning an Academy Award nomination for its title track, whose rhythmic backing was produced by clicking her acrylic nails.
- Her ballad *I Will Always Love You* achieved worldwide pop immortality when *Whitney Houston* recorded a powerhouse cover for the 1992 film *The Bodyguard*.
- Opened the *Dollywood* theme park in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, in 1986, transforming the economic landscape of her native Appalachian region through tourism and job creation.
- Founded *Dolly Parton's Imagination Library* in 1995, an international literacy program that mails free, age-appropriate books every month to registered children from birth to age five.
- Recorded the acclaimed collaborative country-folk album *Trio* in 1987 with fellow luminaries *Emmylou Harris* and *Linda Ronstadt*.
- Donated one million dollars to the Vanderbilt University Medical Center in 2020, which helped fund crucial research for the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine.
