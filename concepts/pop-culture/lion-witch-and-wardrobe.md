## summary

**The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe** is a 1950 high fantasy novel written by British scholar *C. S. Lewis* and illustrated by *Pauline Baynes*, serving as the inaugural published volume of *The Chronicles of Narnia*. Set against the backdrop of [[world-war-ii]], the story follows four young British siblings who are evacuated from London to the countryside and discover a magical kingdom trapped in perpetual winter. Renowned for its rich imaginative scope and Christian theological allegory, the work stands as one of the most celebrated masterworks in twentieth-century children's literature across [[england]] and the world.

## you gotta know

- Follows the four *Pevensie* children—*Peter*, *Susan*, *Edmund*, and *Lucy*—who enter the magical land of *Narnia* through the back of an enchanted wardrobe located in the country manor of *Professor Kirke*.
- Depicts *Narnia* cursed under a hundred-year winter where it is "always winter and never Christmas," enforced through the tyrannical sorcery of the *White Witch*, also known as *Jadis*.
- Edmund is enticed into betraying his brother and sisters after the *White Witch* tempts him with enchanted *Turkish Delight* and promises of royal dominion over his siblings.
- The great lion *Aslan*, rightful king and sovereign creator of *Narnia*, willingly offers his own life at the *Stone Table* to satisfy the *Deep Magic from the Dawn of Time*, sparing the traitor Edmund from execution.
- Bound, shaved, and humiliated by the Witch's monstrous army, Aslan is slain on the *Stone Table*, but resurrects at sunrise through the *Deeper Magic from Before the Dawn of Time*, which dictates that death reverses when a willing, innocent victim is killed in a traitor's stead.
- Concludes with the four siblings reigning for decades as benevolent monarchs from *Cair Paravel* until they pursue the mythical *White Stag* through the woods, tumbling back through the wardrobe to discover that not a single moment has elapsed in [[england]].
- Conceived by Lewis after sheltering three young evacuee girls at his Oxford home, *The Kilns*, during the German aerial Blitz bombings of London, and dedicated to his goddaughter *Lucy Barfield*.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — forms the primary thematic framework, with Aslan's sacrificial death and resurrection mirroring the Passion of Christ.
- [[world-war-ii]] — provides the historical catalyst for the Pevensie siblings' wartime evacuation from urban London under Operation Pied Piper.
- [[england]] — home of C. S. Lewis, Oxford University, and the English pastoral landscapes that inspired the Professor's country estate.
- [[british-empire]] — reflects the mid-twentieth-century ethos, classic schoolchild archetypes, and patriotic ideals of British literature.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — shares mythic elements of folklore, fauns, talking beasts, and pastoral romance common in English Renaissance drama.
