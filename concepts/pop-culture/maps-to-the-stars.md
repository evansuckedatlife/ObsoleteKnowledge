## summary

**Maps to the Stars** is a 2014 satirical psychological drama film directed by Canadian auteur *David Cronenberg* from a screenplay written by *Bruce Wagner*. Set against the parasitic, hallucination-fueled backdrop of Hollywood celebrity culture in the [[united-states]], the film charts the toxic entanglements of the dysfunctional *Weiss* family and their desperate, aging industry associates. Premiering in competition at the Cannes Film Festival in [[france]], the film offers an unrelenting critique of narcissism, trauma, and commercial artifice.

## you gotta know

- Directed by body-horror and psychological master *David Cronenberg*, marking his first feature film shot partly on location in the [[united-states]].
- Earned *Julianne Moore* the prestigious *Best Actress Award* at the 2014 Cannes Film Festival for her scathing performance as the fading, neurotic Hollywood star *Havana Segrand*.
- Screenplay written by novelist *Bruce Wagner*, known for his biting, satirical portrayals of the entertainment industry's moral vacuity.
- Stars an ensemble cast including *Mia Wasikowska* as the disfigured pyromaniac daughter *Agatha*, *John Cusack* as a predatory TV therapist, *Olivia Williams*, and *Robert Pattinson* as a limousine chauffeur and aspiring screenwriter.
- Interweaves genuine supernatural apparitions and psychological delusions as characters are haunted by literal ghosts of abused children and deceased movie stars.
- Concludes in an unsettling modern adaptation of classical [[tragedy]], involving incestuous sibling reunions, murder, and ritualistic suicide under the Hollywood night sky.

## connections

- [[france]] — where the film held its world premiere at the Cannes Film Festival and won top honors for lead acting.
- [[united-states]] — the primary setting of Los Angeles, lampooning the consumerism and predatory dynamics of the Hollywood film industry.
- [[tragedy]] — structurally mimics classic Greek dramatic arcs of hubris, ancestral curses, child murder, and inescapable familial ruin.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — deployed through sterile modernist mansions, sleek limousines, and claustrophobic soundstages reflecting emotional emptiness.
- [[existentialism]] — engages directly with themes of fleeting fame, bodily decay, and the desperate search for meaning in an artificial world.
- [[film-editing]] — utilizes disorienting, sharp cuts to weave seamlessly between waking reality and guilt-ridden hallucinations.
- [[camera-movement]] — uses cold, creeping pans and deliberate tracking shots to create an atmosphere of dread and voyeurism.
