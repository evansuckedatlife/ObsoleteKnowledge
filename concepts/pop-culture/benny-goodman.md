## summary

**Benny Goodman**, born **Benjamin David Goodman**, was an American clarinetist, bandleader, and composer heralded as the **King of Swing**. Emerging from working-class immigrant origins in Chicago during the nineteen-twenties, he led one of the most commercially successful and critically acclaimed big bands of the Great Depression era. His landmark nineteen-thirty-eight concert at Carnegie Hall legitimized jazz as serious American concert music, while his hiring of Black musicians established him as an early champion of racial integration in popular entertainment.

## you gotta know

- Crowned the public emblem of the big band era after his ensemble ignited a national sensation during their nineteen-thirty-five residency at the *Palomar Ballroom* in Los Angeles.
- Revolutionized the institutional status of popular dance music on January sixteenth, nineteen-thirty-eight, by headlining a historic, sold-out jazz concert at *New York City's* prestigious *Carnegie Hall*.
- Struck a historic blow against institutional segregation in the American entertainment industry by publicly hiring pianist *Teddy Wilson*, vibraphonist *Lionel Hampton*, and guitarist *Charlie Christian* to perform in his small combos.
- Trained as a classical virtuoso under teachers like *Franz Schoepp*, later commissioning and premiering major twentieth-century chamber compositions from classical masters such as *Béla Bartók*, *Aaron Copland*, and *Paul Hindemith*.
- Popularized electrifying, driving arrangements written by African American arranger and bandleader *Fletcher Henderson*, which defined the signature hard-driving swing sound.
- Achieved early national radio fame on the NBC program *Let's Dance*, where his late-night broadcasts reached young West Coast listeners who propelled his band to superstardom.
- Famously recorded swing anthems such as *Sing, Sing, Sing (With a Swing)*, featuring thunderous floor-tom drum solos played by long-time collaborator *Gene Krupa*.
- Known for an exacting, relentless perfectionism as a bandleader, earning a notorious reputation among musicians for directing a cold, silent glare dubbed *The Ray* at anyone who committed a performance mistake.
