## summary

**Hey Jude** is an iconic 1968 rock ballad by the English rock band *The Beatles*, written by *Paul McCartney* and credited to the Lennon–McCartney songwriting partnership. Composed to comfort *Julian Lennon* during the painful divorce of his parents, *John Lennon* and *Cynthia Powell*, the song was released as the debut single on the band's Apple Records label. Clocking in at over seven minutes, its soaring optimism and monumental orchestral singalong coda propelled it to nine weeks at number one on the American charts, establishing it as one of the most successful pop singles of all time.

## you gotta know

- Originally titled *"Hey Jules"* when *Paul McCartney* began singing it in his car while driving to visit Cynthia and Julian Lennon in Surrey.
- Features a legendary four-minute extended outro consisting of a 36-piece orchestra, massed vocal harmonies, and the euphoric repeating chant of *"Na-na-na na"*.
- Recorded in July and August 1968 at Trident Studios in London, chosen because it possessed an advanced eight-track tape recorder that EMI's Abbey Road Studios lacked at the time.
- Sparked a minor studio argument between McCartney and lead guitarist *George Harrison* over whether guitar fills should echo every vocal phrase in the verses.
- Contains an accidental expletive uttered by *John Lennon* around the three-minute mark that was famously left buried low in the final audio mix.
- Released as a non-album single backed by John Lennon's hard-rocking political anthem *"Revolution"*, showcasing the band's stylistic duality.
- Spent nine weeks atop the Billboard Hot 100 in the [[united-states]], setting a record as the longest-running American number one single by *The Beatles*.
