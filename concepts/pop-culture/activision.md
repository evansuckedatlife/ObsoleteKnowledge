## summary

**Activision** is an American video game publisher founded in 1979 in Sunnyvale, California, recognized as the video game industry's very first independent, third-party software developer and distributor. Emerging from a dispute over creator recognition at *Atari*, the studio achieved foundational critical and commercial success on early home consoles with landmark cartridges such as *Pitfall!*. Over subsequent decades, it expanded across the [[united-states]] into a multi-billion-dollar publishing titan. Today, it is best known for driving the monumental [[first-person-shooter]] franchise *Call of Duty*, pioneering serialized annual blockbuster game releases, and shaping contemporary competitive gaming worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Publishes *Call of Duty*, a monumental military [[first-person-shooter]] franchise that debuted in 2003 depicting infantry skirmishes, later revolutionizing global multiplayer through cinematic modern combat and maintaining an unprecedented annual release schedule steered by rotating lead studios *Infinity Ward*, *Treyarch*, and *Sledgehammer Games*.
- Released *Pitfall!* in 1982 for the Atari 2600, a groundbreaking jungle-exploring platformer designed by *David Crane* that sold over four million cartridges and demonstrated that independent software houses could outshine first-party hardware manufacturers.
- Combined operations with *Vivendi Games* in 2008 to create **Activision Blizzard**, uniting premier console properties with the massively multiplayer online worlds of *Blizzard Entertainment* and later mobile developer *King*.
- Achieved massive commercial ubiquity during the late 1990s and 2000s by acquiring and serializing pop-culture lifestyle franchises, notably *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater*, *Spyro the Dragon*, *Crash Bandicoot*, and the peripheral-driven sensation *Guitar Hero*.
- Founded in December 1979 by disgruntled *Atari* game designers *David Crane*, *Larry Kaplan*, *Alan Miller*, and *Bob Whitehead*, who partnered with music-industry manager *Jim Levy* after *Atari* management refused to credit programmers on game packaging or share cartridge profits.
- Purchased by technology giant *Microsoft* in October 2023 for approximately sixty-nine billion dollars, concluding an extensive regulatory battle across multiple continents to mark the most expensive acquisition in the history of video games.
- Weathered the catastrophic North American video game crash of 1983 by diversifying into business software and rebranding as *Mediagenic*, an era of near-bankruptcy that ended when an investment group led by *Bobby Kotick* purchased the company in 1991 and restored its iconic original name.

## connections

- [[first-person-shooter]] — the flagship genre that forms the foundation of its contemporary revenue, player base, and cultural imprint through *Call of Duty*.
- [[esports]] — organized professional tournament scene where the company established franchised city-based circuits such as the *Call of Duty League*.
- [[world-war-ii]] — seminal twentieth-century military conflict that provided the historical narrative theater for the original 2003 game and numerous subsequent entries.
- [[cold-war]] — era of global political intrigue and covert operations featured prominently across the narrative arcs of the *Black Ops* series.
- [[united-states]] — nation where the company was established in Silicon Valley and built its long-standing corporate headquarters in Santa Monica, California.
- [[counter-strike]] — foundational competitive tactical shooter whose esports longevity and weapon mechanics served as an influential peer in the genre.
- [[minecraft]] — ubiquitous sandbox game representing the rare contemporary digital franchise to rival the overall lifetime commercial sales figures of its top properties.
