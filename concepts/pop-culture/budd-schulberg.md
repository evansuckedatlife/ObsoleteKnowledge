## summary

**Budd Schulberg** was an acclaimed American novelist, screenwriter, and sports journalist best known for exposing ruthless ambition in Hollywood and institutional corruption on the waterfront. Rising to fame with his scathing industry satire *What Makes Sammy Run?*, he cemented his historical reputation by winning an Academy Award for the screenplay of *On the Waterfront*. His life and career remained deeply entangled with the political tensions of twentieth-century America, defining both literary *realism* and postwar cinema.

## you gotta know

- Won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for *On the Waterfront*, collaborating closely with director *Elia Kazan* to depict racketeering among Hoboken longshoremen.
- Authored the classic 1941 Hollywood novel *What Makes Sammy Run?*, creating the iconic, ruthlessly opportunistic character *Sammy Glick*.
- Appeared as a friendly witness before the House Un-American Activities Committee in 1951, testifying about Communist Party influence in Hollywood during the height of the *Cold War*.
- Wrote the hard-hitting boxing novel *The Harder They Fall*, exposing fight-fixing and mob exploitation in professional prizefighting.
- Scripted the landmark satirical drama *A Face in the Crowd*, which starred *Andy Griffith* as the corrupt, media-manipulating populist *Lonesome Rhodes*.
- Founded the Watts Writers Workshop in Los Angeles following the 1965 Watts riots to support emerging African American voices during the *civil rights movement*.
- Served in the Office of Strategic Services during *World War II*, compiling photographic and film evidence used as prosecution material in the Nuremberg trials.
