## summary

**Al Jolson** was a Lithuanian-born American singer and comedian billed as *The World's Greatest Entertainer*, whose magnetic performance style dominated Broadway and [[vaudeville]] during the early twentieth century. He achieved watershed cultural significance by starring in *The Jazz Singer* (1927), the commercial milestone whose synchronized sound sequences decisively triggered the transition away from the [[silent-film]] era across the [[united-states]]. His brassy vocal delivery, sentimental showmanship, and use of recording technology transformed popular culture, though his signature reliance on blackface minstrel performance remains a defining and controversial aspect of his legacy.

## you gotta know

- Starred as *Jakie Rabinowitz* (stage name *Jack Robin*) in *The Jazz Singer* (1927), electrifying audiences with the ad-libbed proclamation, "Wait a minute, wait a minute, you ain't heard nothin' yet!" which launched the talkie era.
- Followed his breakthrough with *The Singing Fool* (1928), featuring *Sonny Boy*, the first movie soundtrack record to sell over one million copies in the [[united-states]].
- Reached mass fame performing in blackface minstrel makeup, singing standards such as *Swanee*, *My Mammy*, and *California, Here I Come* while dropping to one knee with outstretched arms.
- Developed an energetic stage presence in [[new-york-city]] Broadway revues and the circuit of [[vaudeville]], where he broke the fourth wall, addressed audiences intimately, and ran along runways built into the orchestra seats.
- Maintained a complex racial legacy; while he relied on stereotyped minstrel conventions later condemned during the [[civil-rights-movement]], he also championed Black songwriters and fought for desegregated Broadway casting.
- Reinvigorated his career late in life after Columbia Pictures released *The Jolson Story* (1946) and *Jolson Sings Again* (1949), where he dubbed the vocal tracks for actor *Larry Parks*.
- Became one of the most dedicated celebrity performers for Allied armed forces during [[world-war-ii]], traveling relentlessly across combat zones with the USO to entertain troops.
- Born **Asa Yoelson** to a Jewish family in Lithuania, emigrating to Washington, D.C., where his father worked as a synagogue cantor, establishing personal parallels to themes within [[judaism]].

## connections

- [[silent-film]] — the dominant cinematic medium whose commercial reign Jolson ended through his synchronized singing sequences.
- [[vaudeville]] — the competitive stage variety circuit where he perfected his boisterous, boundary-breaking performance style.
- [[charlie-chaplin]] — the silent cinema icon who initially resisted the talking-picture revolution ignited by Jolson's talkies.
- [[united-states]] — the nation where Jolson reigned as the highest-paid and most celebrated entertainer of the 1920s.
- [[new-york-city]] — the capital of American theater where his Winter Garden Theatre extravaganzas established his Broadway stardom.
- [[judaism]] — the religious heritage of his cantor father that informed the semi-autobiographical storyline of *The Jazz Singer*.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict during which he traveled to active overseas fronts to stage shows for servicemen.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the historical era whose ascendance prompted critical reevaluation of Jolson's ubiquitous use of blackface minstrelsy.
