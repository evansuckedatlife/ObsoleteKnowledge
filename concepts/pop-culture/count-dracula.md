## summary

**Count Dracula** is the titular aristocratic vampire antagonist of *Bram Stoker*'s 1897 Gothic horror novel *Dracula*. Originating from Transylvania and seeking to establish a dominion of the undead in Victorian London, the character codified the modern mythological archetype of the vampire in global popular culture. Through countless theatrical adaptations and landmark film portrayals, Dracula became one of the most adapted fictional figures in literary and cinematic history.

## you gotta know

- Created by Irish author *Bram Stoker* in his 1897 epistolary novel, drawing partial inspiration from Transylvanian folklore and fifteenth-century Wallachian prince *Vlad III Dracula* (*Vlad the Impaler*).
- Possesses supernatural abilities including superhuman strength, shapeshifting into a wolf, bat, or mist, weather control, and telepathic command over nocturnal creatures like rats and wolves.
- Constrained by classical Gothic vulnerabilities, such as an inability to enter a residence uninvited, loss of powers in daylight, revulsion toward crucifixes and garlic, and destruction via a stake through the heart.
- Embodies late-Victorian anxieties regarding reverse colonization, sexual decadence, the decline of the nobility, and the invasion of modern Britain by archaic continental perils.
- Portrayed in an unauthorized German silent screen adaptation as Count Orlok in *F. W. Murnau*'s 1922 masterpiece *Nosferatu*, which established the cinematic trope that sunlight kills vampires.
- Defined visually in twentieth-century pop culture by Hungarian actor *Bela Lugosi* in the 1931 Universal Pictures adaptation, creating the image of the caped, aristocratic predator with a formal accent.
- Reimagined with ferocious, aristocratic menace by *Christopher Lee* across Hammer Film Productions' long-running horror cycle starting in 1958.

## connections

- [[f-w-murnau]] — German director who mounted the seminal unauthorized 1922 screen adaptation *Nosferatu*.
- [[silent-film]] — medium that birthed the earliest screen representations of the Transylvanian count.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — artistic device through which Dracula's shadow and fangs represent repressed Victorian sexual desire and disease.
- [[tragedy]] — dramatic genre informing Gothic literature and the doomed fates of Dracula's afflicted victims like Lucy Westenra.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — dramatist whose theatrical traditions deeply influenced nineteenth-century Victorian staging of Gothic villains.
- [[criterion-collection]] — boutique film label whose catalog preserves early horror masterworks including restorations of *Nosferatu* and *Vampyr*.
- [[england]] — sovereign country where Dracula travels by sea aboard the *Demeter* to stalk Victorian London.
