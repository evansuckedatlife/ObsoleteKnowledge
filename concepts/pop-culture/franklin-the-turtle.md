## summary

**Franklin the Turtle**, commonly known simply as **Franklin**, is a celebrated Canadian children's literature franchise and animated television property created by author Paulette Bourgeois and illustrator Brenda Clark. Debuting in the 1986 picture book *Franklin in the Dark*, the franchise chronicles the gentle developmental milestones and moral learning experiences of an anthropomorphic young turtle and his woodland companions in the village of Woodland. Adapted into an acclaimed international animated series by studio Nelvana, the property remains a beloved touchstone of early-childhood pedagogy and North American public television.

## you gotta know

- Created by writer Paulette Bourgeois and illustrator Brenda Clark, beginning with the 1986 Canadian picture book *Franklin in the Dark*, inspired by an episode of the television series *M*A*S*H* in which character Hawkeye Pierce confesses claustrophobia.
- Characterized by the opening narrative formula used across dozens of original Scholastic and Kids Can Press storybooks: "Franklin could count by twos and tie his shoes. He could unzip zippers and button buttons. But Franklin had a problem."
- Inhabits the tranquil fictional community of Woodland with animal friends named simply by species and gender, notably his best friend *Bear*, alongside *Beaver*, *Snail*, *Fox*, *Rabbit*, *Goose*, and *Badger*.
- Adapted into an animated television series produced by Toronto-based animation studio Nelvana, running for six seasons and sixty-six episodes between 1997 and 2004, followed by several feature-length specials.
- Broadcast widely across North American networks including Family Channel and Treehouse TV in Canada, as well as Nickelodeon, Noggin, and CBS in the *[[united-states]]*, becoming a ubiquitous morning staple for preschool audiences.
- Emphasizes relatable psychosocial dilemmas over fantastic adventure, addressing universal childhood experiences such as fear of the dark, telling lies, schoolyard jealousy, riding a bicycle without training wheels, and welcoming a newborn sibling named *Harriet*.
- Spawned a computer-animated reboot titled *Franklin and Friends* in 2011, incorporating updated three-dimensional rendering while retaining the core gentle themes of cooperation, honesty, and emotional vulnerability.
- Achieved massive commercial success with over sixty-five million books sold worldwide in more than thirty languages, cementing the green-shelled protagonist as an international emblem of character education.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — aligns closely with classic North American children's didactic literature traditions emphasizing gentle realism and empathetic social development.

- [[realism]] — artistic and narrative impulse grounding Franklin's domestic conflicts in genuine emotional truths rather than whimsical fantasy.
