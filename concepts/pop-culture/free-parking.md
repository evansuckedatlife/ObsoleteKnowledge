## summary

**Free Parking** (also called the **Free Parking space**) is one of the four corner spaces on the standard *Monopoly* board, situated between the orange *New York Avenue* and the red *Kentucky Avenue*. Originating in the classic American board game published by *Parker Brothers* in the [[united-states]], it was officially designed as a completely neutral resting space where a token lands without incurring fines, paying rent, drawing cards, or receiving rewards. Despite the official rules explicitly designating it as an idle resting spot, it achieved legendary status throughout global gaming culture for an almost ubiquitous informal house rule that awards accumulated tax and penalty fees to players who land on it, fundamentally altering the intended economic simulation.

## you gotta know

- In the official rulebook designed by *Charles Darrow* and *Parker Brothers*, the space offers no financial payoff whatsoever, serving solely as an uneventful sanctuary where players neither collect money nor face property hazards.
- The widespread house rule dictates that all cash paid to the bank from *Income Tax*, *Luxury Tax*, *Chance*, and *Community Chest* fines is pooled in the center of the board and awarded to whoever lands on this space.
- Competitive tournament circuits and the *Monopoly World Championship* strictly forbid the jackpot house rule because injecting windfall cash into circulation prevents bankruptcies and drags games out for hours.
- The space is situated at square twenty on the standard forty-space board layout, directly opposite the *Go* square and buffering the high-traffic orange and red property groups.
- Probabilistic analyses show it is visited less often than *Jail* or *Illinois Avenue*, yet landing on it remains highly desirable in the late game to safely leapfrog lethal hotel developments.
- In 1988, *Parker Brothers* capitalized on the square's legendary cultural identity by publishing an official spin-off card game bearing the same title, centered on taxi driving and parking meters.
- The underlying board game evolved from *Elizabeth Magie*'s 1904 anti-monopolist patent *The Landlord's Game*, which featured a public park space to illustrate how non-privatized municipal land benefits citizens without generating capitalist rent.
