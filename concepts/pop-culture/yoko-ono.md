## summary

**Yoko Ono** is an influential Japanese multimedia artist, singer, songwriter, and peace activist whose radical conceptual works reshaped twentieth-century avant-garde art and music. Emerging during the late 1950s and 1960s within the experimental *Fluxus* movement across *Tokyo* and [[new-york-city]], she created pioneering participatory installations, instructional texts, and performance pieces before achieving worldwide fame alongside her husband, *John Lennon*. Her daring public demonstrations for pacifism and boundary-pushing recordings challenged conventional aesthetics and established her as an enduring, fiercely debated icon of global popular culture.

## you gotta know

- Collaborated extensively with *John Lennon* after their 1969 marriage, staging iconic media actions such as the *Bed-Ins for Peace* in *Amsterdam* and *Montreal* to oppose the Vietnam War while releasing anthemic protest music including *Give Peace a Chance*.
- Long endured sexist and xenophobic media narratives that falsely blamed her for the 1970 breakup of the *Beatles*, a persistent pop-culture myth repeatedly refuted by *Paul McCartney* and other members of the group.
- Created the landmark 1964 performance art work *Cut Piece*, staged at the *Sogetsu Art Center* in *Tokyo* and later in *Carnegie Recital Hall*, in which she sat motionless on stage while audience members stepped forward with shears to cut away pieces of her clothing.
- Published *Grapefruit* in 1964, a famous artist book composed of conceptual "event scores" offering poetic instructions for readers to perform in their minds or daily lives, such as *Painting to Be Stepped On* and *Cloud Piece*.
- Fronted the *Plastic Ono Band* alongside *Lennon*, pioneering raw vocal improvisation, primal screams, and avant-garde rock textures on albums like *Yoko Ono/Plastic Ono Band* (1970) and *Fly* (1971), heavily influencing subsequent punk, new wave, and riot grrrl musicians.
- Met *Lennon* in November 1966 at London's *Indica Gallery* during an exhibition of her conceptual art, where he famously climbed a ladder to view her magnifying-glass piece *Ceiling Painting* and read the tiny, affirmative word *YES*.
- Endured the devastating air raids on *Tokyo* during [[world-war-ii]] as a displaced child from an aristocratic banking family, foraging for food in the countryside, a formative trauma that catalyzed her lifelong pacifist activism, later commemorated by her *Imagine Peace Tower* in *Reykjavik*.

## connections

- [[japan]] — her birthplace and early cultural milieu where she initiated avant-garde happenings and conceptual exhibitions.
- [[new-york-city]] — central staging ground for her loft series on Chambers Street and key developments with the *Fluxus* movement.
- [[modernism]] — radical twentieth-century artistic framework that inspired her departure from traditional painting toward conceptual performance.
- [[world-war-ii]] — catastrophic childhood experience of military devastation that fueled her dedication to international pacifist movements.
- [[england]] — nation where she exhibited at the *Indica Gallery* and formed her creative partnership with *John Lennon*.
- [[youtube]] — premier online video archive preserving historic broadcasts of her interviews, *Bed-In* recordings, and *Cut Piece* performances.
