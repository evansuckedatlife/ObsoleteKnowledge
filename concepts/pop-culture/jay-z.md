## summary

**Jay-Z** (*Shawn Carter*, also widely known as **Hova** or **J-Hova**) is an American rapper, songwriter, record executive, and entrepreneur from [[new-york-city]] who ranks among the most influential and commercially successful figures in hip-hop history. Rising from Brooklyn's Marcy Projects, he co-founded Roc-A-Fella Records and released a string of critically acclaimed albums that defined the sound of East Coast rap across multiple decades. Beyond his musical legacy, he became hip-hop's first billionaire through investments spanning streaming media, fashion, sports management, and fine beverages.

## you gotta know

- Released his landmark debut album *Reasonable Doubt* in 1996 on his independent imprint Roc-A-Fella Records, featuring intricate street lyricism and classic collaborations with *The Notorious B.I.G.* and *Mary J. Blige*.
- Achieved mainstream commercial superstardom with *Vol. 2... Hard Knock Life* (1998), propelled by the hit title track that famously sampled the Broadway musical *Annie*.
- Released the critically revered album *The Blueprint* on September 11, 2001, which helped pioneer the soul-sampling production style of a young *Kanye West* and *Just Blaze*.
- Engaged in one of hip-hop's most celebrated lyrical rivalries with Queens rapper *Nas*, trading scathing diss tracks in "Takeover" and *Nas*'s responding "Ether" before publicly reconciling years later.
- Announced a temporary retirement with the star-studded *The Black Album* (2003), which featured the *Rick Rubin*-produced rock-rap anthem "99 Problems" and celebrated his farewell at Madison Square Garden.
- Served as the president of Def Jam Recordings from 2004 to 2007, where he signed global superstars including *Rihanna* and oversaw the breakthrough careers of *Ne-Yo* and *Young Jeezy*.
- Collaborated with his wife *Beyoncé* across numerous joint tracks and the collaborative project *The Carters*, alongside monumental collaborative albums such as *Watch the Throne* with *Kanye West*.
- Reflected on marital infidelity, legacy, and Black financial empowerment on his deeply personal 2017 studio album *4:44*, produced entirely by *No I.D.*.
