## summary

**Bad** is the seventh solo studio album by American singer-songwriter *Michael Jackson*, released on August 31, 1987, by *Epic Records*, as well as the title of its iconic lead track and short film. Conceived as the formidable follow-up to *Thriller*, the work presented an edgy, aggressive evolution of Jackson's artistry that blended funk, synth-pop, hard rock, and dance music. It established unprecedented pop records by generating five consecutive number-one singles on the US *Billboard* Hot 100, while its titular short film directed by *Martin Scorsese* transformed modern music video production.

## you gotta know

- Became the first album in recording history to yield five consecutive number-one singles on the *Billboard* Hot 100: "I Just Can't Stop Loving You," "Bad," "The Way You Make Me Feel," "Man in the Mirror," and "Dirty Diana."
- The eighteen-minute short film for the title song was directed by *Martin Scorsese* and featured an early breakout screen role for actor *Wesley Snipes* as Mini Max, a neighborhood antagonist confronting Jackson's character, Darryl.
- The title track was parodied by comedy musician *Weird Al Yankovic* in his 1988 hit song and video "Fat," an affectionate homage that Jackson personally permitted and supported by granting access to the authentic subway set.
- Legendary producer *Quincy Jones* co-produced the record alongside Jackson, marking the final studio album collaboration of their historic partnership following *Off the Wall* and *Thriller*.
- The album cut "Smooth Criminal" premiered Jackson's famous anti-gravity forward lean, executed in live concerts through specialized shoes engineered to latch into hidden floor pegs.
- Jackson initially envisioned the song "Bad" as a powerhouse vocal duet with fellow pop titan *Prince*, but Prince declined the offer, reportedly expressing reservations about the opening line "your butt is mine."
- The accompanying *Bad World Tour*, running from 1987 to 1989, represented Jackson's inaugural solo international stadium tour, setting world attendance records and grossing over $125 million across 123 concerts.

- [[new-york-city]] — metropolitan urban setting of the Hoyt-Schermerhorn subway station in Brooklyn where Scorsese filmed the dramatic dance showdown for the title track.
- [[united-states]] — nation where the album was recorded at Westlake Audio in Los Angeles, fundamentally reshaping American commercial sound engineering.
- [[film-editing]] — vital technique employed across Jackson's music videos to coordinate intricate group choreography with fast-paced rhythmic storytelling.
- [[camera-movement]] — sweeping crane movements and fluid tracking shots captured by cinematographer *Michael Chapman* to highlight Jackson's footwork and spatial command.
