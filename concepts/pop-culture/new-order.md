## summary

**New Order** is a pioneering English rock band formed in Salford in 1980 by the surviving members of the seminal post-punk group *Joy Division*. Consisting of vocalist/guitarist *Bernard Sumner*, bassist *Peter Hook*, and drummer *Stephen Morris*, joined soon after by keyboardist *Gillian Gilbert*, the group arose in the immediate aftermath of frontman *Ian Curtis*'s tragic death. By seamlessly merging the melancholic intensity of post-punk with the pulsating rhythms of New York club culture, electro, and synth-pop, they became one of the most influential and innovative alternative acts in modern music history.

## you gotta know

- Emerged directly from the tragedy of *Joy Division* following the suicide of singer *Ian Curtis* in May 1980, adopting a strict pact to rename the band and start anew.
- Released the revolutionary 1983 standalone single *Blue Monday*, which became the best-selling 12-inch vinyl single of all time worldwide.
- Famously lost money on initial sales of *Blue Monday* due to *Peter Saville*'s elaborate, die-cut sleeve designed to resemble a 5¼-inch floppy disk.
- Served as the flagship artistic act for *Tony Wilson*'s legendary Manchester independent label *Factory Records* and heavily financed the famed nightclub *The Haçienda*.
- Characterized sonically by *Peter Hook*'s melodic, high-register bass playing intertwined with electronic sequencers, drum machines, and *Bernard Sumner*'s understated vocals.
- Achieved widespread commercial and critical triumph with studio albums like *Power, Corruption & Lies* (1983), *Low-Life* (1985), and *Technique* (1989), the latter recorded in Ibiza during the rise of acid house.
- Composed the official anthem for the English national football team at the 1990 FIFA World Cup, *World in Motion*, which featured a memorable rap by footballer *John Barnes*.
