## summary

**Mad Men** is an acclaimed American period drama television series created by *Matthew Weiner*, running for seven seasons on AMC from 2007 to 2015. Centered on the Madison Avenue advertising agency Sterling Cooper in [[new-york-city]] during the 1960s, the narrative tracks the enigmatic creative director *Don Draper*. The show is widely regarded as one of the greatest television dramas of all time, winning sixteen Emmy Awards and four consecutive Golden Globes while dissecting the social upheaval, existential alienation, and consumerist mythology of the post-war *[[united-states]]*.

## you gotta know

- The protagonist *Don Draper*, portrayed by *Jon Hamm*, is secretly a farm boy named *Dick Whitman* who assumed his dead commanding officer's identity during the Korean War.
- The pilot episode, *Smoke Gets in Your Eyes*, establishes Draper's marketing genius when he crafts the slogan *It's Toasted* to salvage cigarette manufacturer Lucky Strike.
- *Peggy Olson*, played by *Elisabeth Moss*, begins as Draper's timid secretary before rising through raw talent to become a groundbreaking female copywriter.
- Major ensemble partners at the firm include the hedonistic silver-haired scion *Roger Sterling*, British financial wizard *Lane Pryce*, and conniving account executive *Pete Campbell*.
- The stylish office manager *Joan Holloway*, portrayed by *Christina Hendricks*, deftly navigates blatant workplace sexism to eventually secure a partnership and launch her own production firm.
- The show's famous season one finale, *The Wheel*, features Draper delivering an iconic pitch for the Kodak slide projector, renaming the device the *Carousel* around memories of family nostalgia.
- The series meticulously incorporates real historical flashpoints of the 1960s, including the Cuban Missile Crisis, the assassination of John F. Kennedy, the Civil Rights Movement, and the 1969 Apollo 11 moon landing.
- The series concludes in 1970 with Draper meditating at a California spiritual retreat, smiling serenely just before the cut to the real-world 1971 Coca-Cola commercial *I'd Like to Buy the World a Coke*.
