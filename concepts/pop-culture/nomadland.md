## summary

**Nomadland** is a 2020 American neo-Western drama film directed, written, edited, and produced by *Chloé Zhao*, based on *Jessica Bruder*'s 2017 non-fiction book *Nomadland: Surviving America in the Twenty-First Century*. Starring *Frances McDormand*, the film chronicles the solitary journey of a widowed woman who leaves her economically devastated Nevada company town to live as a modern vehicular nomad across the American West. Revered for its lyrical docudrama style and empathetic portrait of post-recession resilience, the film won three Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, and Best Actress.

## you gotta know

- Directed by *Chloé Zhao* and starring *Frances McDormand* as *Fern*, winning the Academy Award for Best Picture at the 93rd Academy Awards.
- Adapted from the 2017 investigative book *Nomadland: Surviving America in the Twenty-First Century* by journalist *Jessica Bruder*.
- Follows *Fern*, who loses her livelihood and home after the US Gypsum plant in Empire, Nevada, permanently shuts down and its ZIP code is discontinued.
- Incorporates real-life vehicular nomads—most notably *Linda May*, *Swankie*, and van-dwelling mentor *Bob Wells*—playing fictionalized versions of themselves.
- Explores seasonal itinerant labor across the [[united-states]], notably featuring *Fern* working grueling shifts at an Amazon fulfillment center and a South Dakota campground.
- Features an evocative, minimalist piano score composed by Italian minimalist composer *Ludovico Einaudi*.
- Showcases sweeping cinematography of the American West by *Joshua James Richards*, utilizing natural magic-hour light reminiscent of modern [[western-film]] aesthetics.
- Secured *Chloé Zhao* historical acclaim as the second woman, and the first woman of color, to win the Academy Award for Best Director.

- [[realism]] — relies on natural light, unvarnished location shooting, and real working-class people portraying themselves in documentary fashion.
- [[united-states]] — critiques the fraying economic safety net and hollowed-out corporate towns of twenty-first-century America.
- [[existentialism]] — examines solitary grief, deliberate detachment from consumerist society, and transient purpose on the open road.
- [[american-literature]] — traces its narrative roots to classic American literary traditions of wanderlust and journeying found in work by writers from *Walt Whitman* to *John Steinbeck*.
- [[industrialization]] — explores the fallout of corporate plant closures and post-industrial abandonment in the rural interior.
