## summary

The **Eagles** are an influential American rock band formed in Los Angeles in 1971 by *Glenn Frey*, *Don Henley*, *Bernie Leadon*, and *Randy Meisner*. Celebrated for their melodic songwriting, tight multi-part vocal harmonies, and masterful synthesis of country, folk, and hard rock, they became one of the defining cultural and commercial forces of the 1970s. Their catalog produced some of the highest-selling albums in global recording history, capturing both the sunny optimism and the dark disillusionment of the post-counterculture American West.

## you gotta know

- Released *Hotel California* in 1976, a critically acclaimed concept album exploring excess, greed, and spiritual exhaustion, anchored by the iconic title track featuring a climactic dual guitar solo by *Don Felder* and *Joe Walsh*.
- Their 1976 compilation *Their Greatest Hits (1971–1975)* was certified 38-times platinum by the RIAA, standing as one of the two best-selling albums of all time in the [[united-states]] alongside *Michael Jackson*'s *Thriller*.
- Scored their first breakthrough single with *"Take It Easy"* in 1972, a breezy country-rock anthem co-written by *Glenn Frey* and singer-songwriter *Jackson Browne*.
- Formed after the four original members were recruited by producer *John Boylan* to serve as the touring and studio backing band for singer *Linda Ronstadt* on her 1972 self-titled record.
- Added guitarist *Joe Walsh* in 1975 following the departure of founding banjoist and country purist *Bernie Leadon*, transforming the ensemble from an acoustic-tinged country outfit into an arena-filling hard rock powerhouse.
- Broke up acrimoniously in 1980 following onstage hostility between Frey and Felder at a Long Beach benefit concert, leading Henley to famously state that the band would reunite only "when hell freezes over."
- Reunited in 1994 to release the smash live album *Hell Freezes Over*, embarking on one of the highest-grossing concert tours in music history and setting industry precedents for premium ticket pricing.
- Bassist *Randy Meisner* performed lead vocals and hit the soaring high notes on the 1975 hit waltz *"Take It to the Limit"* before departing due to stage fright, physical exhaustion, and intra-band arguments.

## connections

- [[early-morning-rain]] — early folk-revival standard whose acoustic textures and country-influenced storytelling laid groundwork for the California country-rock explosion that this band commercialized.
- [[united-states]] — home country whose shifting post-Vietnam cultural landscape and booming recording industries shaped the group's themes of suburban ambition and western mythmaking.
- [[western-film]] — cinematic tradition that directly inspired the outlaw mythology, frontier imagery, and Old West motifs across their 1973 concept record *Desperado*.
- [[american-literature]] — creative lineage sharing the group's recurring literary themes of the corrupted American Dream, Hollywood decadence, and existential desert journeys.
- [[new-york-city]] — major media capital housing the dominant radio networks, music critics, and Atlantic Records executives who chronicled their fierce chart rivalries and 1980 dissolution.
- [[realism]] — narrative approach informing their mature songwriting, which stripped away 1960s hippie idealism in favor of cynical, clear-eyed observations on wealth and heartbreak.
- [[modernism]] — aesthetic current reflected in the fragmented disillusionment, surreal hotel allegories, and psychological unease running through their late-1970s masterpiece *Hotel California*.
