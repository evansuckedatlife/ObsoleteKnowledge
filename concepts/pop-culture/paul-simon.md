## summary

**Paul Simon**, born **Paul Frederic Simon**, is an acclaimed American singer-songwriter and guitarist whose career spans more than six decades of popular music history. First rising to international prominence in the 1960s alongside *Art Garfunkel* in the celebrated folk-rock duo *Simon & Garfunkel*, he later forged an extraordinarily influential solo career characterized by sophisticated lyrical poetry and world music fusion. Renowned for bridging American folk traditions with global rhythms, he stands as one of the most decorated songwriters in the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Formed the seminal folk-rock partnership *Simon & Garfunkel* in [[new-york-city]], scoring enduring hits such as "The Sound of Silence," "The Boxer," and the Grammy-winning classic "Bridge over Troubled Water."
- Composed the celebrated soundtrack for *Mike Nichols*'s 1967 film *The Graduate*, which yielded the iconic chart-topping single "Mrs. Robinson."
- Traveled to Johannesburg during the apartheid era to record the landmark 1986 album *Graceland*, collaborating with South African musicians such as *Ladysmith Black Mambazo* to popularize *mbaqanga* and township jive globally.
- Scored solo masterworks and hit singles including "Me and Julio Down by the Schoolyard," "Kodachrome," and the polyrhythmic smash "You Can Call Me Al."
- Achieved a number-one hit with "50 Ways to Leave Your Lover" in 1975, renowned among musicians for drummer *Steve Gadd*'s intricate military-style snare groove.
- Followed the success of *Graceland* with the 1990 album *The Rhythm of the Saints*, expanding his exploration of non-Western musical traditions by incorporating Afro-Brazilian percussion recorded with the Salvador-based drumming troupe *Olodum*.
- Wrote the book and score for the 1998 Broadway musical *The Capeman* in collaboration with Nobel laureate *Derek Walcott*, chronicling the life of Puerto Rican gang member *Salvador Agron*.
- Inducted twice into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, receiving honors both as a member of *Simon & Garfunkel* in 1990 and as a distinguished solo artist in 2001.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the nation whose folk, rock, and pop vernaculars the songwriter shaped across seven decades.
- [[new-york-city]] — the urban center where he grew up in Queens, attended Queens College, and staged the historic 1981 *The Concert in Central Park*.
- [[pentatonix]] — the contemporary a cappella quintet that popularized his songwriting to twenty-first-century listeners through their cover of "The Sound of Silence."
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the socio-political backdrop of the 1960s that informed the socially conscious lyricism of his early folk repertoire.
- [[colonialism]] — the complex historical framework surrounding the international debate and United Nations cultural boycott during the recording of *Graceland* in South Africa.
- [[realism]] — the songwriting ethos reflected in his narrative vignettes documenting working-class urban lives and domestic struggles.
- [[modernism]] — the twentieth-century aesthetic movement that influenced his impressionistic lyrics, stream-of-consciousness phrasing, and collage-like studio arrangements.
