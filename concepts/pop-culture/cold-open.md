## summary

A **cold open** is a narrative technique in television, radio, and cinema where a program jumps straight into the story or action before displaying the title sequence or opening credits. Emerging extensively in mid-twentieth-century broadcasting, the device hooks viewers immediately to deter them from changing the channel during commercial breaks between programs. It has evolved into a celebrated creative canvas, ranging from dramatic thrillers launching mystery plots to sketch comedy shows staging topical political satire.

## you gotta know

- Jumps directly into dramatic action, a comedic sketch, or an unfolding plot prior to the broadcast of the opening title credits.
- Popularized in mid-century American broadcasting to capture audience attention instantly and prevent channel surfing between scheduled shows.
- Famously institutionalized by *Saturday Night Live*, where every comedic opening sketch traditionally concludes with the iconic catchphrase: "Live from New York, it's Saturday Night!"
- Widely employed across serialization dramas, procedural crime programs, and espionage thrillers to depict an inciting crime or mysterious catastrophe.
- Utilized in classic sitcoms and workplace comedies to stage self-contained, high-energy mini-jokes completely detached from the episode's main narrative arc.
- Traces conceptual lineage to classical literary devices like *in medias res*, which throws the audience directly into the midst of dramatic events.
- Often referred to interchangeably in industry production scripts and network broadcasting schedules as a *teaser sequence*.
