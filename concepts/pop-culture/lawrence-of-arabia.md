## summary

**Lawrence of Arabia** is a monumental 1962 historical epic film directed by David Lean and produced by Sam Spiegel, depicting British officer T. E. Lawrence during the Arab Revolt against the Ottoman Empire. Starring Peter O'Toole in his star-making screen role alongside Omar Sharif, Alec Guinness, and Anthony Quinn, the film is widely considered one of the greatest masterpieces in world cinema. Renowned for its breathtaking Super Panavision 70 cinematography and sweeping score by Maurice Jarre, it won seven Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Director.

## you gotta know

- Features one of cinema's most celebrated match cuts, transitioning from a close-up of T. E. Lawrence blowing out a lit match directly to a scorching sunrise over the Arabian desert.
- Introduces *Sherif Ali*, played by Omar Sharif in his breakout international role, via an iconic telephoto mirage shot across the shimmering, heat-distorted desert sands.
- Depicts the audacious overland capture of the port city of *Aqaba* by crossing the supposedly impassable *Nefud Desert* to attack its coastal artillery batteries from the unprotected rear.
- Centers on the internal psychological disintegration and divided loyalties of Lawrence as he realizes the Arab tribes are pawned within imperial diplomatic schemes like the Sykes-Picot Agreement.
- Adapted by screenwriters Robert Bolt and Michael Wilson from Lawrence's autobiographical war memoir *Seven Pillars of Wisdom*.
- Features no speaking roles for female characters, a rare distinction for a major commercial epic that won the Academy Award for Best Picture.
- Edited by Anne V. Coates, whose bold and rhythmic pacing influenced generations of New Hollywood directors including Steven Spielberg and George Lucas.
