## summary

**Raichu** is an Electric-type creature in the *Pokémon* media franchise created by *Satoshi Tajiri* and *Ken Sugimori* for Nintendo and Game Freak in *[[japan]]*. Introduced in the 1996 video games *Pokémon Red* and *Green*, it is the final natural evolution of the global franchise mascot *Pikachu*, achieved by exposing *Pikachu* to a Thunder Stone. Despite its significantly superior base combat statistics, the creature is famously overshadowed in branding and pop culture by its unevolved pre-evolution, embodying a recurring thematic tension between competitive combat efficacy and visual commercial appeal.

## you gotta know

- Evolves directly from *Pikachu* through the application of an evolutionary item called the Thunder Stone, permanently sacrificing its ability to learn several subsequent attacks through standard leveling in early game generations.
- Gained an alternate dual Electric and Psychic regional variant known as **Alolan Raichu** in the seventh-generation games *Sun* and *Moon*, which levitates off the ground by surfing atop its broad, paddle-shaped tail via telekinesis.
- In the animated television series, the Gym Leader of Vermilion City, *Lt. Surge*, commands an intimidating specimen that easily overpowers *Ash Ketchum*'s *Pikachu* in their first match before succumbing to unevolved agility and speed in the rematch.
- Long before its public release, early design drafts by game artist *Atsuko Nishida* originally planned a third evolutionary stage named *Gorochu*, which featured prominent fangs and a pair of horns before being cut due to Game Boy memory limitations.
- Possesses an elongated tail terminating in a sharp lightning-bolt shape that functions as an electrical ground, protecting the rodent from discharging accumulated high-voltage currents into its own body.
- Frequently appears in competitive doubles play within *[[esports]]* tournament circuits, where its *Lightning Rod* ability and supportive utility attacks like *Fake Out* grant it indispensable strategic value alongside offensive sweepers.
- Lore entries in early Pokédex versions contained uncharacteristic references to real-world biology and geography, noting that its electrical discharges could reach ten thousand volts and comfortably knock out an Indian elephant.
