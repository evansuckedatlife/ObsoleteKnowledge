## summary

**Black Christmas** is a landmark 1974 psychological slasher film directed by *Bob Clark* and written by *Roy Moore*, starring *Olivia Hussey*, *Keir Dullea*, *Margot Kidder*, and *John Saxon*. Produced in Canada under the alternate release titles **Silent Night, Evil Night** and **Stranger in the House**, the film depicts a sorority house besieged by an unseen madman during the winter holidays. Widely celebrated as one of the earliest and most influential entries in the slasher subgenre, it established foundational tropes including subjective camera work, anonymous harassing telephone calls, and holiday-themed terror.

## you gotta know

- Directed by *Bob Clark*, who later directed the beloved holiday comedy *A Christmas Story*, establishing the macabre holiday tone four years before *John Carpenter*'s *Halloween*.
- Features pioneering subjective point-of-view tracking shots created through fluid [[camera-movement]], placing the audience in the vantage point of the prowler scaling the trellis and infiltrating the attic.
- Popularized the terrifying urban legend trope where police trace harassing calls and inform the protagonist that the intruder is already inside the residence.
- Centers on *Jess Bradford* (played by *Olivia Hussey*), a collegiate protagonist who subverted contemporary horror expectations by navigating an unwanted pregnancy and steadfastly defending her bodily autonomy against her unstable pianist boyfriend *Peter Smythe*.
- The demented antagonist, known only by the aliases *Billy* and *Agnes*, is never clearly seen or unmasked, maintaining a psychological ambiguity rare in subsequent slasher cinema.
- Notable for its chilling final shot framed by atmospheric [[mise-en-scene]], panning slowly away from the attic door as an unanswered telephone rings into the credits while police guard the wrong room downstairs.
- Features *Margot Kidder* in an acclaimed performance as the sardonic, heavy-drinking sorority sister *Barb Coard*, who is murdered in her bedroom with a glass unicorn figurine.
- Inspired loosely by the real-life *Westmount* murders in Montreal and classic urban folklore concerning imperiled babysitters.
