## summary

**Christian Dior** was a pioneering French fashion couturier who founded the eponymous luxury house **Dior**, often referred to internationally as the **House of Dior**, in Paris in 1946. He revolutionized haute couture and the global textile market in 1947 by introducing his celebrated *New Look*, characterized by rounded shoulders, cinched waists, and billowing ankle-length skirts. His romantic, lavish aesthetic swept away the somber, fabric-rationed silhouette imposed by [[world-war-ii]], re-establishing Paris as the undisputed epicenter of international fashion.

## you gotta know

- He was born in the seaside town of Granville in northern [[france]], originally operating an avant-garde art gallery in Paris that exhibited works by modernist painters before entering high fashion design.
- His debut spring-summer 1947 collection, officially named *Corolle*, was famously christened the *New Look* by *Carmel Snow*, the influential editor-in-chief of *Harper's Bazaar*.
- The signature ensemble of his initial collection was the *Bar suit*, composed of an ivory silk shantung jacket with padded basques tailored tightly above an extravagantly pleated black wool skirt.
- His extravagant consumption of high-grade textiles sparked fierce public protests on both sides of the Atlantic among working-class communities still subject to wartime textile austerity.
- He revolutionized the business architecture of haute couture by aggressively licensing his brand name across perfumes, hosiery, ties, and furs, beginning with the evergreen fragrance *Miss Dior*.
- Following his sudden death from a heart attack in Italy in 1957, his twenty-one-year-old prodigy assistant *Yves Saint Laurent* was promoted to head the fashion empire.
- The company later became the crowning jewel of the modern multinational luxury conglomerate *LVMH*, led by French business magnate *Bernard Arnault*.

## connections

- [[france]] — the home nation where he established his legendary couture atelier on *Avenue Montaigne* in Paris.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the catastrophic conflict whose severe material rationing was decisively supplanted by his generous fashion lines.
- [[modernism]] — the artistic movement he championed as an early Parisian gallery owner displaying contemporary painters.
- [[industrialization]] — the global economic process that facilitated the mass manufacture and distribution of his licensed fashion merchandise.
- [[renaissance]] — the classical European rebirth of ornamentation and elegance that profoundly inspired his architectural tailoring.
- [[realism]] — the aesthetic philosophy contrasted by his unabashed pursuit of escapist fantasy and hyper-feminine opulence.
