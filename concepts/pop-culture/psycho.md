## summary

**Psycho** is a 1960 psychological horror film directed by *Alfred Hitchcock* and released in the *[[united-states]]*. Based on the novel by *Robert Bloch*, the film broke longstanding Hollywood censorship taboos and established fundamental tropes of modern cinematic suspense and the proto-slasher genre. It remains one of the most commercially and critically influential American movies ever produced, celebrated for its disorienting narrative structure, groundbreaking montage, and iconic score by *Bernard Herrmann*.

## you gotta know

- Features the immortal shower murder scene in room one of the *Bates Motel*, featuring *Janet Leigh* as *Marion Crane* stabbed to death accompanied by screeching string glissandos composed by *Bernard Herrmann*.
- Stars *Anthony Perkins* as the quiet taxidermy enthusiast *Norman Bates*, whose severe psychological fracturing and matricidal identity theft drive the narrative twist.
- Revolutionized mainstream Hollywood screenwriting by abruptly killing off its apparent protagonist forty minutes into the film, completely upending traditional character identification and pacing.
- Shot rapidly in high-contrast black-and-white using the television production crew of *Alfred Hitchcock Presents* to minimize costs following resistance from *Paramount Pictures*.
- Pioneered legendary techniques in *[[film-editing]]*, utilizing roughly seventy-eight camera setups and fifty-two rapid cuts within a forty-five-second sequence to simulate explicit violence without showing graphic penetration.
- Pushed the boundaries of the *Motion Picture Production Code* by displaying the first flushing toilet depicted in a mainstream American motion picture.
- Concludes with the psychological explanation delivered by *Simon Oakland* and the haunting final shot of *Norman Bates* smiling beneath a subliminal skeletal overlay.

- [[visual-metaphor]] — uses stuffed predatory birds, fractured mirrors, and Gothic architecture perched above modern motels to externalize mental decay.
- [[quentin-tarantino]] — influenced the director's penchant for shocking mid-story protagonist deaths and nonlinear tension building.

- [[james-stewart]] — Hitchcock's frequent leading man whose complex neuroses in *Vertigo* directly paved the way for Norman Bates.
- [[united-states]] — transformed post-war American popular cinema, box-office distribution policies, and modern horror expectations.
