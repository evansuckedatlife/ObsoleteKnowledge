## summary

**Lana Del Rey**, born **Elizabeth Woolridge Grant** and early in her career performing as **Lizzy Grant**, is an influential American singer-songwriter whose work is characterized by cinematic melancholy, explorations of tragic romance, and references to mid-century Americana. Rising to international fame with her viral 2011 single *Video Games*, she pioneered an alternative pop aesthetic that fundamentally reshaped 2010s mainstream music away from dance-pop toward moody, downtempo balladry. Her critically acclaimed albums, particularly *Born to Die* and *Norman Fucking Rockwell!*, cemented her status as one of the essential American songwriters of the twenty-first century.

## you gotta know

- Broke into mainstream consciousness with the self-directed home-video montage for *Video Games*, featuring vintage clips, paparazzi footage, and faded California imagery.
- Released the landmark 2012 major-label debut *Born to Die*, an album that spent hundreds of weeks on the Billboard 200 and spawned enduring hits like *Summertime Sadness* and *Blue Jeans*.
- Collaborated with Dan Auerbach of The Black Keys on the psychedelic-rock influenced 2014 record *Ultraviolence*, establishing her creative departure from polished radio production.
- Earned career-defining critical acclaim with the Jack Antonoff-produced 2019 LP *Norman Fucking Rockwell!*, which received a Grammy nomination for Album of the Year and topped numerous decade-end critical lists.
- Wrote the 2020 poetry collection *Violet Bent Backwards over the Grass*, which was simultaneously issued as a spoken-word album set to music by Antonoff.
- Drew frequent comparisons to contemporary pop star [[lady-gaga]], alongside whom she emerged in the New York indie circuit before forging a starkly different aesthetic path.
- Explored expansive, folk-influenced confessionals on subsequent albums including *Chemtrails over the Country Club* and *Did You Know That There's a Tunnel Under Ocean Blvd*.

## connections

- [[lady-gaga]] — peer New York pop innovator whose theatrical maximalism contrasts with Del Rey's melancholic mid-century nostalgia.
- [[united-states]] — the primary source of cultural myths, iconography, and historical nostalgia saturating her lyrical world.
- [[new-york-city]] — the early artistic crucible where she attended Fordham University and began singing under her birth name in Lower East Side clubs.
- [[american-literature]] — a continuous lyrical reference point through overt homages to Walt Whitman, F. Scott Fitzgerald, and Sylvia Plath.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical current echoing throughout her recurring lyrical themes of mortality, romance, and spiritual alienation.
- [[modernism]] — early twentieth-century aesthetic sensibility informing her fractured historical allusions and pastiche.
