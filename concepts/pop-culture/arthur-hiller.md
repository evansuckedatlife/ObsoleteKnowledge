## summary

**Arthur Hiller** was a prominent Canadian-American film and television director who helped shape mainstream Hollywood storytelling across five decades. He achieved worldwide renown for directing the cultural phenomenon *Love Story* (1970), one of the highest-grossing romantic dramas in cinematic history, while maintaining equal fluency in biting political satires and beloved screwball comedies. In addition to his prolific artistic output in the [[united-states]], Hiller served as an influential industry statesman through his leadership of the Directors Guild of America and the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences.

## you gotta know

- Directed the 1970 romantic melodrama *Love Story*, which earned seven Academy Award nominations, rescued Paramount Pictures from financial ruin, and coined the famous catchphrase "Love means never having to say you're sorry."
- Born in Edmonton, Alberta, and served in the Royal Canadian Air Force as a navigator flying bombing missions over Europe during [[world-war-ii]] before transitioning into broadcasting.
- Directed two landmark satirical screenplays by Paddy Chayefsky: the anti-war comedy *The Americanization of Emily* (1964) and the scathing medical black comedy *The Hospital* (1971), which won Chayefsky an Oscar.
- Helmed *Silver Streak* in 1976, an action comedy that marked the historic first on-screen pairing of comedic icons Gene Wilder and Richard Pryor.
- Directed the acclaimed 1979 screwball comedy *The In-Laws*, showcasing chaotic banter and comic timing between Peter Falk and Alan Arkin.
- Tackled grave moral themes in *The Man in the Glass Booth* (1975), an adaptation of Robert Shaw's play concerning Holocaust guilt, identity, and Nazi war crime trials.
- Served as president of the Directors Guild of America from 1989 to 1993, and president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences from 1993 to 1997, later receiving the Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award.
