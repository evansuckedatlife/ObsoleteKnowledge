## summary

**Goodfellas** is a landmark 1990 American biographical crime film directed by Martin Scorsese and adapted from Nicholas Pileggi's 1985 non-fiction book *Wiseguy*. Chronicling the rise and fall of Lucchese crime family associate Henry Hill from the 1950s through 1980, the film stars Ray Liotta, Robert De Niro, and Joe Pesci. Widely regarded as one of the greatest cinematic masterworks ever produced, it redefined the gangster genre through propulsive voiceover narration, freeze frames, frantic pop-soundtrack integration, and hyper-kinetic camerawork.

## you gotta know

- Directed by Martin Scorsese and co-written with Nicholas Pileggi, adapting the true-crime investigative bestseller *Wiseguy* detailing the criminal career of mob informant Henry Hill.
- Opens with the indelible line delivered by Ray Liotta as Henry Hill: "As far back as I can remember, I always wanted to be a gangster," immediately following the brutal roadside stabbing of Billy Batts.
- Features a famous three-minute unbroken Steadicam tracking shot following Henry and Karen through the service entrance, kitchen, and bustling floor of the Copacabana nightclub to the song "Then He Kissed Me".
- Earned Joe Pesci an Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his volatile portrayal of Tommy DeVito, highlighted by the largely improvised, tension-filled "Funny how? Do I amuse you?" restaurant confrontation.
- Revolutionized cinematic soundtrack usage by employing contemporary rock and pop classics, including Eric Clapton's piano coda to Derek and the Dominos' "Layla" over a montage of uncovered dead bodies.
- Concludes the gang's active criminal ascent with the chaotic, frantic May 11, 1980 sequence depicting a paranoid, drug-addled Henry Hill coordinating cocaine drops while constantly scanning the sky for police helicopters.
- Depicts the real-life 1978 Lufthansa heist at John F. Kennedy International Airport, which yielded millions in cash and jewelry and precipitated Jimmy Burke's murderous paranoia.

## connections

- [[new-york-city]] — central geographic and cultural setting of Brooklyn, Queens, and Idlewild/JFK Airport where the Lucchese crew operated.
- [[camera-movement]] — technical virtuosity illustrated by the iconic Copacabana long take and dynamic whip-pans throughout key scenes.
- [[film-editing]] — visionary pacing and rhythmic montage crafted by legendary editor Thelma Schoonmaker, utilizing rapid cuts and freeze frames.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — meticulous period set design, lavish food preparation, and sharp Italian tailoring reflecting street-level mob hierarchy.
- [[tragedy]] — narrative arc tracing Henry Hill's seductive ascent, moral decay, betrayal of his comrades, and hollow survival in suburban anonymity.
- [[realism]] — gritty, unromanticized depiction of petty greed, everyday violence, and mundane domestic domesticity within organized crime syndicates.
- [[united-states]] — social backdrop examining post-war consumerism, suburban sprawl, ethnic enclaves, and subversions of the American Dream.
