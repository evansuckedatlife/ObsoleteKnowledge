## summary

**Dav Pilkey** is a bestselling American author and illustrator renowned for creating transformative children's book franchises, including *Captain Underpants* and *Dog Man*. Diagnosed with ADHD and dyslexia during his childhood, he transformed early experiences of academic alienation and disciplinary isolation into an empathetic, highly visual storytelling style. His graphic novels have sold hundreds of millions of copies worldwide, reshaping modern juvenile literacy through accessible humor and exuberant cartooning.

## you gotta know

- Created the smash-hit *Captain Underpants* chapter book series in 1997, following prank-loving fourth graders *George Beard* and *Harold Hutchins*, who hypnotize their cruel principal *Mr. Krupp* into a ridiculous superhero.
- Invented the interactive *Flip-O-Rama* animation technique featured in his books, allowing young readers to rapidly flip two pages back and forth to simulate kinetic slapstick action sequences.
- Launched the massive graphic novel spin-off series *Dog Man* in 2016, chronicling a half-canine, half-human police officer who battles crime alongside the reformed feline villain *Petey* and kitten clone *Li'l Petey*.
- Created the follow-up series *Cat Kid Comic Club*, an instructional narrative that teaches children about diverse artistic mediums, creative writing, and resilience when facing artistic failure.
- Wrote and illustrated the acclaimed 1996 picture book *The Paperboy*, which earned a Caldecott Honor for its lyrical, quiet, and tender exploration of early-morning responsibility.
- Regularly appeared on the American Library Association's most banned and challenged books lists due to parental complaints regarding bathroom humor, antiauthoritarian attitudes, and perceived rowdiness in *Captain Underpants*.
- Developed his signature characters while seated at isolated hallway desks in elementary school in Ohio, where teachers sent him as punishment for disrupting class with impromptu doodles.

- [[physical-comedy]] — kinetic visual humor captured on the page through animated movement, rubbery anatomy, and dynamic *Flip-O-Rama* fight scenes.
