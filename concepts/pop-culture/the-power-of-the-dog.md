## summary

**The Power of the Dog** is a 2021 psychological drama Western film written and directed by *Jane Campion*, based on the 1967 novel of the same name by *Thomas Savage*. Set across the vast cattle ranches of 1925 Montana, the narrative dissects performative frontier masculinity, repressed homoerotic longing, and insidious domestic cruelty within an aristocratic ranching family. Starring *Benedict Cumberbatch*, *Kirsten Dunst*, *Jesse Plemons*, and *Kodi Smit-McPhee*, the critically lauded production earned twelve nominations at the 94th Academy Awards, winning Best Director for Campion and revitalizing the revisionist Western genre.

## you gotta know

- Centers on *Phil Burbank*, a volatile Montana rancher who graduated Phi Beta Kappa in classics from an Eastern university but embraces crude frontier savagery while venerating the memory of his deceased mentor, *Bronco Henry*.
- Contrasts Phil with his stolid brother *George Burbank*, who unexpectedly marries *Rose Gordon*, a vulnerable widow whose delicate son *Peter* arrives at the ranch during medical school vacation to study anatomy and botany.
- Depicts Phil launching a relentless psychological campaign of intimidation against Rose, whistling menacing melodies and driving her into severe, isolated alcoholism while openly mocking Peter's fastidious manners and handmade paper flowers.
- Shifts abruptly when Phil takes a sudden, seductive interest in mentoring Peter in cowboy crafts, guiding him on horseback and weaving him a custom rawhide lasso, unaware of Peter's icy calculation.
- Reveals Peter secretly harvesting deadly anthrax spores from a diseased cattle carcass, which he weaponizes after Rose innocently barters away Phil's spare hides to local Native American traders.
- Culminates in Phil immersing his cut, bare hands into a bucket of water containing Peter's infected hides to complete the braided rope, leading to Phil's rapid death from fatal anthrax poisoning.
- Resolves with Peter attending Phil's funeral clad in clean clothing, clutching the completed lasso with gloved hands, and quietly reading Psalm 22:20: "Deliver my soul from the sword; my darling from the power of the dog."
- Features a haunting, dissonant score composed by *Jonny Greenwood*, recorded with mechanical cello and detuned saloon piano, paired with sweeping cinematography captured in New Zealand's remote Otago region.
