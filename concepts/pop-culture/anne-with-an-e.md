## summary

**Anne with an E** is a Canadian television drama series created by *Moira Walley-Beckett*, adapted from *Lucy Maud Montgomery*'s 1908 novel *Anne of Green Gables*. Premiering on CBC in 2017 and distributed globally on Netflix, the series stars *Amybeth McNulty* as the imaginative orphan *[[anne-shirley]]* as she navigates adolescent hardship on Prince Edward Island. By shifting away from purely nostalgic pastoral tropes, the production infuses nineteenth-century Canadian domestic storytelling with psychological grit, historical *[[realism]]*, and frank explorations of childhood trauma, class prejudice, and structural oppression in the Edwardian era.

## you gotta know

- Created and showrun by *Moira Walley-Beckett*, an Emmy-winning former writer on *Breaking Bad* who brought an intense psychological realism and cinematic maturity to the adaptation.
- Casts Irish-Canadian actress *Amybeth McNulty* in the central role, earning critical praise for balancing the protagonist's soaring poetic imagination with genuine manifestations of post-traumatic stress.
- Departs from traditional adaptations by incorporating visceral, harrowing flashbacks that reveal the severe neglect and physical abuse Anne experienced within institutional orphanages and foster households.
- Expands beyond Montgomery's original texts to incorporate historically marginalized populations, introducing the Mi'kmaq residential school tragedy through the character *Ka'kwet* and Prince Edward Island's Black community through *Bash Lacroix*.
- Selects its episode titles throughout the first season from *Charlotte Brontë*'s novel *Jane Eyre*, underlining thematic parallels between Victorian orphan literature and institutional female resilience.
- Faced sudden cancellation in 2019 following its third season due to a distribution dispute between the CBC and Netflix, prompting an unprecedented international fan campaign featuring billboards in Times Square and digital petitions.
- Embraces a naturalistic aesthetic marked by hand-held camerawork, moody atmospheric lighting, and weathered production design that grounds Avonlea in authentic period grime rather than romanticized sentimentality.

## connections
