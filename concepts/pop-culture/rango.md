## summary

**Rango** is a 2011 American computer-animated Western comedy film directed by *Gore Verbinski* and produced by *Nickelodeon Movies* and *Blind Wink Productions*, featuring landmark visual effects by *Industrial Light & Magic*. The story chronicles an eccentric, sheltered pet chameleon who is abruptly ejected from his terrarium into the harsh Mojave Desert, stumbles into the drought-stricken settlement of *Dirt*, and fabricates a fearsome gunslinger persona to become the outpost's town sheriff. Praised for its gritty visual photorealism, sophisticated adult humor, and cinephilic deconstructions, the project achieved commercial success and won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

## you gotta know

- Stars *Johnny Depp* as the identity-obsessed green chameleon who invents his courageous hero persona on the spot in a dusty saloon after inadvertently killing an apex red-tailed hawk with a falling water tower.
- Employed an innovative live production method dubbed emotion capture, staging full rehearsals with the ensemble cast in costume and on practical sets to capture physical spontaneity and comedic timing instead of isolated voice-booth tracks.
- Features the sinister antagonist *Rattlesnake Jake*, a predatory Western gunfighter with a multi-barreled Gatling gun embedded in place of his rattle, voiced with venomous menace by *Bill Nighy*.
- Stands as a dense, affectionate cinematic homage to the *[[western-film]]* tradition, quoting visual framing from *Sergio Leone* spaghetti westerns, the water intrigue of *Chinatown*, and the psychedelic imagery of *Hunter S. Thompson*.
- Captured the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature at the 84th Academy Awards, becoming the first non-Disney, non-Pixar animated film to claim the prize in five years.
- Portrays an underlying fable of corrupt resource monopolies headed by the *Mayor of Dirt*, an elderly desert tortoise modeled both visually and rhetorically on *John Huston*'s character *Noah Cross*.
- Culminates in a surreal spiritual encounter with the *Spirit of the West*, an ethereal, golf-cart-driving apparition modeled on screen icon *Clint Eastwood*, who imparts philosophical wisdom on destiny and communal duty.
