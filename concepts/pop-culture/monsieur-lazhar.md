## summary

**Monsieur Lazhar** is a 2011 Canadian drama film written and directed by *Philippe Falardeau*, adapted from *Évelyne de la Chenelière*'s one-character stage play *Bashir Lazhar*. Set in a Montreal elementary school, the film follows an Algerian political refugee who is hastily hired as a substitute teacher following the sudden suicide of a beloved middle-school instructor. Through its restrained, compassionate storytelling, the film explores the intersections of collective childhood trauma, pedagogical detachment, immigration bureaucracy, and personal grief, earning widespread international acclaim and a nomination for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film.

## you gotta know

- Directed by Quebec filmmaker *Philippe Falardeau*, who adapted the script from *Évelyne de la Chenelière*'s monodrama while expanding the narrative to include a full classroom of children, school administrators, and immigration officials.
- Stars Algerian actor, writer, and humorist *Mohamed Fellag* as *Bachir Lazhar*, an asylum seeker whose gentle, traditional classroom presence conceals profound domestic loss.
- Opens with the shocking suicide of teacher *Martine Lachance*, who hangs herself inside her classroom during recess, a tragic scene inadvertently discovered by students *Simon* (*Émilien Néron*) and *Alice* (*Sophie Nélisse*).
- Depicts the clash between Bachir's traditional European didactic pedagogical style—such as dictation from classic literature like *Honoré de Balzac* and arranging desks in rows—and the school's sanitized, modern bureaucratic curriculum.
- Explores Bachir's precarious political status as he seeks political asylum in Canada after his wife, a feminist teacher and author, was murdered alongside their children in an arson attack by religious extremists in Algeria.
- Highlights the institutional taboos against physical affection, grief counseling, and genuine emotional confrontation between educators and grieving children in contemporary public schools.
- Dominated the 32nd Genie Awards in Canada, sweeping six major categories including Best Motion Picture, Best Director, Best Actor for *Mohamed Fellag*, and Best Supporting Actress for young breakout *Sophie Nélisse*.
- Earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Foreign Language Film at the 84th Academy Awards, representing Canada alongside contemporary international nominees such as *A Separation*.
