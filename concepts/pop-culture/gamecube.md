## summary

**GameCube** (officially the Nintendo GameCube) is a sixth-generation home video game console developed and released by Nintendo in 2001 in *Japan* and North America. Developed under the codename *Dolphin*, the system served as the successor to the Nintendo 64, marking Nintendo's initial transition from silicon ROM cartridges to optical disc media. Despite placing third in sales during its contemporary generation behind Sony's PlayStation 2 and Microsoft's Xbox, the platform garnered critical acclaim for its ergonomic controller, technological architecture, and celebrated first-party software library.

## you gotta know

- Released in late 2001 featuring a distinctive compact, cube-shaped exterior casing designed with an integrated carrying handle on its rear panel.
- Used proprietary miniDVD-based optical discs capable of holding roughly 1.5 gigabytes of data, designed deliberately to mitigate software piracy and avoid paying DVD Video licensing fees.
- Engineered with a specialized PowerPC-based central processing unit known as *Gekko* and an advanced graphics processing unit developed with ArtX dubbed *Flipper*.
- Launched without an original *Mario* platformer for the first time on a Nintendo home console, instead debuting alongside *Luigi's Mansion* as its premier flagship title.
- Served as the original platform for *Super Smash Bros. Melee* (2001), which became the console's best-selling software title and evolved into a premier enduring pillar of competitive fighting game tournaments worldwide.
- Famously pushed the artistic boundaries of long-running franchises through polarizing and influential releases, such as the cel-shaded visual design of *The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker* (2002) and *Super Mario Sunshine* (2002).
- Established pioneering hardware inter-connectivity via the GameCube-Game Boy Advance cable, allowing handheld consoles to function as secondary auxiliary screens and controllers for games like *The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures*.
- Succeeded in 2006 by the wildly popular Nintendo Wii, which maintained direct backward compatibility with its game discs and controller ports.

## connections

- [[galaga]] — classic arcade shooter made playable on the console through various retro compilation discs and classic anthologies.
- [[japan]] — home country of Nintendo's Kyoto headquarters where the system was engineered and manufactured.
- [[esports]] — competitive gaming scene where the platform's premier title, *Super Smash Bros. Melee*, became an iconic, long-lived tournament fixture.
- [[counter-strike]] — contemporary early-2000s competitive game that flourished on personal computers while this system competed on home televisions.
- [[first-person-shooter]] — genre represented on the console by landmark titles like Retro Studios' critically acclaimed *Metroid Prime* (2002).
- [[minecraft]] — modern global sandbox phenomenon that later arrived on subsequent generations of Nintendo console hardware.
