## summary

**Lin-Manuel Miranda** is an acclaimed American composer, lyricist, playwright, and actor who transformed modern musical theater by infusing classic Broadway songwriting with hip-hop, salsa, and pop idioms. Rising to prominence with his vibrant Manhattan-set debut *In the Heights*, he achieved global cultural phenomenon status by writing and starring in the Pulitzer-winning biographical musical *Hamilton*. His polyrhythmic wordplay, diverse casting philosophy, and cross-medium storytelling have earned him MacArthur fellowships, Tony Awards, and major film scoring triumphs.

## you gotta know

- Created and starred as the titular *Alexander Hamilton* in the monumental 2015 Broadway musical *Hamilton*, inspired by Ron Chernow's historical biography, which framed the *[[american-revolution]]* through modern rap battles and color-conscious casting.
- Wrote the music and lyrics for and starred as Usnavi de la Vega in *In the Heights*, a semi-autobiographical musical set over three blistering summer days in *[[new-york-city]]*'s Upper Manhattan neighborhood of Washington Heights that won the 2008 Tony Award for Best Musical.
- Composed original songs for Walt Disney Animation Studios blockbusters, earning an Academy Award nomination for "How Far I'll Go" from *Moana* and topping the Billboard charts with the viral smash "We Don't Talk About Bruno" from *Encanto*.
- Co-founded the improvisational hip-hop comedy troupe *Freestyle Love Supreme* alongside Thomas Kail and Anthony Veneziale, showcasing extemporaneous rapping that laid the groundwork for his theatrical cadence and lyrical density.
- Earned the 2016 Pulitzer Prize for Drama, three Tony Awards, three Grammy Awards, two Emmy Awards, and a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship, leaving him just an Oscar shy of achieving the competitive EGOT status.
- Made his feature directorial debut with the 2021 film adaptation of Jonathan Larson's autobiographical musical *Tick, Tick... Boom!*, starring Andrew Garfield as the struggling *Rent* composer.
- Wrote the short one-act musical *21 Chump Street* for a 2014 episode of *This American Life*, based on an undercover high school drug bust reported by Robbie Brown.
- Collaborated with Tom Kitt and Amanda Green to compose the music for the high-energy athletic stage adaptation *Bring It On: The Musical*, which incorporated competitive cheerleading stunts into Broadway choreography.

## connections

- [[new-york-city]] — the urban incubator where he was raised, which serves as the vibrant physical setting for *In the Heights* and the theatrical home of his Broadway successes.
- [[american-revolution]] — the historical crucible dramatized in *Hamilton*, reimagined through cabinet debates staged as competitive hip-hop rap battles.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the foundational document debated and authored in part by his signature subject, who famously wrote fifty-one of the Federalist Papers.
- [[vaudeville]] — the fast-paced, multi-act American performance heritage whose lyrical dexterity, variety stylings, and theatrical patter echo through modern musical comedy.
- [[modernism]] — the artistic movement emphasizing formal experimentation, self-conscious reinvention, and genre hybridization that opened the door for hip-hop on the theatrical stage.
- [[the-backyardigans]] — a fellow landmark of twenty-first-century American musical storytelling that introduced sophisticated, genre-hopping theatrical choreography to younger generations.
- [[united-states]] — the broader cultural and historical canvas whose immigrant narratives, political struggles, and musical evolutions anchor his body of work.
