## summary

**Rick Mercer**, born **Richard Vincent Mercer**, is a Canadian comedian, television personality, political satirist, and author from Newfoundland whose sharp national commentary shaped Canadian broadcast humor for three decades. Renowned for his rapid-fire monologues delivered while pacing graffiti-lined alleyways, he dissected national identity and government scandals with warmth and biting wit. Through groundbreaking series such as *This Hour Has 22 Minutes*, the television industry satire *Made in Canada*, and the multi-award-winning *The Rick Mercer Report*, he stood as one of Canada's most celebrated and recognizable cultural figures.

## you gotta know

- Created the internationally famous satirical segment *Talking to Americans*, traveling across the [[united-states]] to persuade citizens, academics, and politicians—including then-governor *George W. Bush*—to accept ludicrous fabricated claims about Canadian geography and government.
- Hosted *The Rick Mercer Report* on CBC Television from 2004 to 2018, opening each weekly broadcast with his trademark two-minute political rant filmed in one continuous kinetic walk through Toronto back alleys.
- Co-founded the landmark CBC news parody series *This Hour Has 22 Minutes* in 1993 alongside fellow Newfoundland comedians *Mary Walsh*, *Cathy Jones*, and *Greg Thomey*.
- Traveled to every Canadian province and territory for *The Rick Mercer Report*, partaking in strenuous physical activities, emergency rescue drills, and military exercises alongside everyday workers and public officials.
- Created and starred in the dark workplace comedy series *Made in Canada*, portraying a ruthless entertainment executive while lampooning television production bureaucracy.
- Invested as an Officer of the Order of Canada in 2014 in recognition of his contributions to Canadian comedic arts and his extensive philanthropic work, notably in youth mental health and anti-bullying advocacy.
- Began his theatrical career with acclaimed solo stage productions in the early 1990s, including *Show Me the Button, I'll Push It, or Charles Lynch Must Die*, reflecting on Canadian constitutional tensions surrounding the Meech Lake Accord.
