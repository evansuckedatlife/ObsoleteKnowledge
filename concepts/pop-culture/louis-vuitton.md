## summary

**Louis Vuitton** is a French luxury fashion house and leather goods company founded in 1854 by trunkmaker and malletier *Louis Vuitton*. Renowned worldwide for pioneering stackable flat-topped canvas trunks, the brand expanded into a global symbol of prestige and high fashion, anchored by its iconic *Monogram* canvas pattern. Today operating as the flagship brand of the multinational conglomerate *LVMH*, it represents a cornerstone of modern luxury goods and haute couture.

## you gotta know

- Founded in Paris in 1854 after its namesake craftsman served as trunkmaker and packer to the French Empress *Eugénie de Montijo*.
- Revolutionized luggage design by creating rectangular, flat-topped trunks wrapped in waterproof gray *Trianon* canvas that could be stacked in modern cargo holds.
- Introduced the *Damier* checkerboard pattern in 1888 and the worldwide recognizable interlocking *LV* floral monogram in 1896, originally designed to deter counterfeiting.
- Patented an unpickable tumbler lock system in 1886 alongside his son *Georges Vuitton*, famously challenging escape artist *Harry Houdini* to break out of a locked box.
- Merged with champagne producer *Moët Hennessy* in 1987 to form the luxury conglomerate *LVMH*, led by chief executive *Bernard Arnault*.
- Revolutionized luxury fashion under creative director *Marc Jacobs* from 1997 to 2013, who launched ready-to-wear collections and artist collaborations with *Takashi Murakami* and *Yayoi Kusama*.
- Appointed prominent creative directors for menswear including British designer *Kim Jones*, off-white visionary *Virgil Abloh*, and musician *Pharrell Williams*.

## connections

- [[france]] — established in Paris and remains one of the ultimate cultural and commercial emblems of the French nation.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — expanded precisely due to the rise of steam locomotive and transatlantic steamship travel demanding stackable, durable luggage.
- [[japan]] — heavily influenced by Japanese mon crest aesthetics during the creation of the 1896 monogram, and later partnered with artists like *Takashi Murakami*.
- [[new-york-city]] — major luxury hub hosting flagship boutiques along Fifth Avenue and staging milestone high-fashion runway spectacles.
- [[colonialism]] — prospered during nineteenth-century imperial travel and global luxury expansion across international trade circuits.
- [[modernism]] — transformed utilitarian baggage design into abstract luxury fashion, marrying functional craft with artistic avant-garde movements.
