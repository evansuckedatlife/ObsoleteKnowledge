## summary

**Hotel California** is the landmark title track and fifth studio album by the American rock band the *Eagles*, released in late 1976 on Asylum Records. Composed by *Don Felder*, *Don Henley*, and *Glenn Frey*, the song is world-renowned for its surreal allegorical lyrics, intricate dual-guitar harmonization, and biting critique of hedonistic excess in Southern California. The record captured the Grammy Award for Record of the Year in 1978, sold tens of millions of copies worldwide, and endures as one of the defining masterpieces of 1970s classic rock.

## you gotta know

- Climaxed by an extended electric guitar duel between *Don Felder* on a Gibson double-neck and *Joe Walsh* on a Fender Telecaster, culminating in their famous harmonized arpeggiated coda.
- Narrates a weary traveler's arrival at a luxurious, eerie desert mission where enchanting figures indulge in narcotic escapism, immortalized by the line "you can check out any time you like, but you can never leave."
- Conceived by *Don Henley* and *Glenn Frey* as a socio-cultural allegory reflecting on the loss of American innocence, the corruption of artistic ideals, and the decadent decay of the 1970s music industry.
- The album cover showcases an iconic twilight photograph of the *Beverly Hills Hotel* captured by photographer *David Alexander* with graphic design by *John Kosh*.
- Marked the dramatic stylistic transition of the *Eagles* from soft country-rock toward hard-edged rock following the recruitment of guitar virtuoso *Joe Walsh* to replace founding member *Bernie Leadon*.
- Recorded at Criteria Studios in Miami and the Record Plant in Los Angeles by producer *Bill Szymczyk*, who insisted Felder replicate his original cassette demo note-for-note.
- Famously inspired widespread urban legends alleging connections to Anton LaVey and the Church of Satan, assertions which the band repeatedly dismissed as ridiculous misinterpretations of their metaphors.
- Incorporates the memorable phrase "steely knives," which served as an affectionate musical nod to jazz-rock group *Steely Dan*, who had referenced the Eagles in their song "Everything You Did."
