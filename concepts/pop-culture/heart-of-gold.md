## summary

**Heart of Gold** is the celebrated 1972 folk-rock ballad by Canadian singer-songwriter *Neil Young*, released as the lead single from his fourth studio album, *Harvest*. Featuring Young's plaintive vocal delivery, acoustic guitar, and iconic harmonica melodies, the track became his only number-one single on the US Billboard Hot 100. Beyond the musical sphere, the title represents a ubiquitous cultural idiom denoting innate moral kindness beneath a flawed or hardened exterior, and famously names the flagship starship in *Douglas Adams*'s classic comedic science-fiction universe *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*.

## you gotta know

- Composed by *Neil Young* following a severe spinal injury that rendered him temporarily unable to stand and play his heavy electric guitar, compelling him to compose gentle acoustic material while seated.
- Recorded during impromptu sessions in Nashville, Tennessee, featuring backing vocal harmonies by prominent country and folk luminaries *Linda Ronstadt* and *James Taylor*, alongside *Ben Keith*'s evocative pedal steel guitar.
- Became a massive commercial breakthrough that propelled *Harvest* to the top of the album charts, though Young famously reflected in his retrospective liner notes for *Decade* that the song *"put me in the middle of the road. Traveling there soon became a bore, so I headed for the ditch."*
- Provoked an infamous reaction from *Bob Dylan*, who initially resented hearing the song on the radio because its acoustic guitar, harmonica hook, and vocal timbre sounded so strikingly reminiscent of his own folk style.
- Serves as the name of the revolutionary starship in *Douglas Adams*'s *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*, powered by the groundbreaking *Infinite Improbability Drive* and stolen by two-headed Galactic President *Zaphod Beeblebrox*.
- Embodies a classic character trope across [[western-film]] and [[american-literature]], frequently manifested as the rough-hewn bandit, grizzled prospector, or saloon dancer whose outward cynicism conceals selfless altruism.
- Ranked by *Rolling Stone* on its prestigious list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time, recognized as one of the definitive acoustic anthems of the 1970s singer-songwriter boom.
