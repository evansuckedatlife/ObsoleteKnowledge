## summary

**broadcast journalism** is the profession, practice, and electronic dissemination of non-fiction news reporting, investigative documentaries, and public affairs programming via radio, terrestrial television, and cable networks. Developing across the twentieth century in the *[[united-states]]* and Europe, it fundamentally transformed modern civic engagement by transmitting synchronous audiovisual accounts of breaking historical events directly into private households. From battlefield radio dispatches to authoritative nightly news broadcasts, electronic news reporting established the definitive visual and rhetorical vocabulary for modern political accountability, emergency communications, and mass democratic culture.

## you gotta know

- Pioneered in radio by foreign correspondents led by *Edward R. Murrow*, whose atmospheric live rooftop broadcasts for CBS during the London Blitz brought the visceral realities of *[[world-war-ii]]* directly into civilian living rooms.
- Produced the watershed television documentary *See It Now* in 1954, in which *Edward R. Murrow* used Senator *Joseph McCarthy*'s own recorded words to dismantle his anti-communist crusade during the height of the *[[cold-war]]*.
- Influenced executive policymaking and public consensus during the *Vietnam War*, epitomized by CBS anchor *Walter Cronkite*'s historic 1968 post-Tet Offensive special report that prompted President *Lyndon B. Johnson* to reassess his military strategy.
- Captured nationwide attention during the *[[civil-rights-movement]]*, when televised footage of police dogs in *Birmingham* and police brutality against peaceful marchers in *Selma* galvanized public demand for federal legislative reform.
- Expanded from concise fifteen-minute evening roundups into flagship thirty-minute broadcasts anchored by trusted cultural figures such as *Walter Cronkite*, *Chet Huntley*, *David Brinkley*, *Peter Jennings*, and *Dan Rather*.
- Disrupted the traditional morning and evening broadcast schedules in 1980 through *Ted Turner*'s Cable News Network (*CNN*), which inaugurated continuous live twenty-four-hour crisis coverage during the 1991 Persian Gulf War.
- Addressed journalistic ethics through extensive investigative reporting on systemic abuses within entertainment, politics, and sports, notably covering the legal proceedings, media reckonings, and convictions of cultural figures such as *[[bill-cosby]]*.
- Governed for decades in the American broadcasting market by the *Fairness Doctrine*, an FCC policy requiring licensed stations to present contrasting viewpoints on controversial public issues until its repeal in 1987.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — defined the geopolitical reporting, nuclear anxieties, and ideological framing of international news desks for over four decades.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — leveraged televised press conferences and camera coverage to expose racial violence to a horrified national electorate.
- [[united-states]] — provided the regulatory and commercial framework that nurtured powerful network news divisions and cable news channels.
- [[world-war-ii]] — stimulated rapid technological innovations in field recording, shortwave transmission, and remote foreign correspondence.
- [[bill-cosby]] — prompted extensive investigative reports, retrospective documentaries, and courtroom updates examining institutional complicity in the media industry.
- [[silent-film]] — pioneered cinematic newsreels shown in theaters, laying the stylistic groundwork for later televised visual news.
