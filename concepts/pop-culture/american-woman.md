## summary

**American Woman** is a hard-rock protest song written and performed by the Canadian rock band *The Guess Who*, released in January 1970 as the title track of their sixth studio album. Produced by *Jack Richardson*, the song quickly ascended to the top of the charts in both Canada and the *United States*, famously reaching number one on the Billboard Hot 100. It is renowned for its iconic, bluesy opening guitar riff and its satirical lyrics addressing contemporary military and social politics during the height of the Vietnam conflict.

## you gotta know

- Recorded by *The Guess Who* after originating as an impromptu, live stage improvisation by guitarist *Randy Bachman* and lead singer *Burton Cummings*.
- Topped the Billboard Hot 100 for three consecutive weeks in 1970, marking the first time a Canadian rock band achieved a number-one hit on that American chart.
- The lyrics, frequently misinterpreted as a straightforward anti-female polemic, serve primarily as an explicit political metaphor critiquing *United States* militarism, the draft, and urban turmoil during the *Cold War* era.
- Famous for warning "I don't need your war machines / I don't need your ghetto scenes," directly repudiating the geopolitical tensions of its neighboring superpower.
- Invited to perform at the White House for *Richard Nixon*, the band was requested by first lady *Pat Nixon* to omit the track due to its controversial anti-war sentiments.
- Covered prominently in 1999 by *Lenny Kravitz* for the soundtrack to *Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me*, earning a Grammy Award for Best Male Rock Vocal Performance.
- Anchored by *Randy Bachman*'s heavily distorted, fuzz-driven Les Paul guitar tone, which remains one of the defining riffs of early 1970s classic rock.
