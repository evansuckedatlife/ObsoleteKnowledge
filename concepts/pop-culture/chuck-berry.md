## summary

**Chuck Berry**, born **Charles Edward Anderson Berry**, was an American singer, songwriter, and guitarist universally recognized as one of the essential architects of rock and roll music. Emerging from St. Louis during the mid-1950s, he translated rhythm and blues into a revolutionary musical style that defined the nascent youth culture of postwar America. His inventive guitar solos, rhythmic double-stops, and poetic lyricism about automobiles, teenage freedom, and consumer life fundamentally shaped the course of modern popular music.

## you gotta know

- Defined rock and roll guitar vocabulary through his percussive rhythm licks, double-stop string bends, and opening guitar introductions, directly influencing guitarists like *Keith Richards*, *Jimi Hendrix*, and *George Harrison*.
- Composed and recorded his signature anthem *Johnny B. Goode* in 1958, an autobiographical celebration of a country boy achieving guitar stardom that was included on the *Voyager Golden Record* as the singular representation of rock music sent into deep space.
- Popularized his trademark stage stunt known as the *duck walk*, where he hopped across the performance stage on one leg while kicking the other forward and swinging his guitar.
- Scored his first major breakthrough hit with *Maybellene* in 1955 on *Chess Records*, which adapted the traditional Western swing tune *Ida Red* into a driving rhythm-and-blues crossover triumph that bridged racially segregated radio audiences in the [[united-states]].
- Penned a prolific catalogue of timeless singles celebrating postwar youth culture and rebellious optimism, including *Roll Over Beethoven*, *Rock and Roll Music*, *Sweet Little Sixteen*, *School Day*, and *Brown Eyed Handsome Man*.
- Achieved his only number-one single on the Billboard Hot 100 chart late in his career with the suggestive 1972 novelty recording *My Ding-a-Ling*, captured live during a concert in England.
- Faced legal battles and racial prejudice during the era of the [[civil-rights-movement]], including a controversial 1959 arrest and subsequent federal imprisonment under the *Mann Act*, which temporarily halted his commercial momentum at his artistic height.
- Honored as an inaugural inductee into the *Rock and Roll Hall of Fame* in 1986 and celebrated in the 1987 documentary film *Hail! Hail! Rock 'n' Roll*, which chronicled his 60th birthday concerts organized by *Keith Richards*.
