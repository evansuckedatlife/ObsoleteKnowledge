## summary

**Duran Duran** is a pioneering English new wave and synth-pop band formed in *Birmingham* in 1978 by keyboardist *Nick Rhodes* and bassist *John Taylor*. Rising to international dominance as spearheads of the Second British Invasion during the early 1980s, the group featured the classic lineup of Rhodes, Taylor, charismatic lead singer *Simon Le Bon*, guitarist *Andy Taylor*, and drummer *Roger Taylor*. By fusing post-punk energy, disco dance beats, high-fashion New Romantic style, and groundbreaking music video cinematography, they scored over twenty UK top-ten hits, sold over one hundred million records worldwide, and were inducted into the *Rock and Roll Hall of Fame* in 2022.

## you gotta know

- Captured global stardom during the dawn of the MTV era with a string of multi-platinum pop anthems, including "Hungry Like the Wolf," "Rio," "Save a Prayer," and their first US Billboard Hot 100 number one, "The Reflex."
- Derived their band name from *Dr. Durand Durand*, the eccentric villain portrayed by *Milo O'Shea* in the 1968 psychedelic French science-fiction film *Barbarella*, directed by *Roger Vadim* and starring *Jane Fonda*.
- Revolutionized the music video medium by collaborating with Australian director *Russell Mulcahy*, filming on glossy 35mm film stock in glamorous locales like *Sri Lanka* and *Antigua* to create cinematic short narratives.
- Recorded "A View to a Kill," the theme song for the 1985 *James Bond* film starring *Roger Moore*, which remains the only Bond theme in recording history to reach number one on the US Billboard Hot 100 chart.
- Dubbed "The Fab Five" by the British press after *Princess Diana* declared them her favorite band, triggering a level of intense teen pop hysteria comparable to Beatlemania throughout Europe and North America.
- Sparked fierce censorship debates in 1981 with the sexually charged music video for "Girls on Film," directed by *Godley & Creme*, which was promptly banned by the *BBC* and heavily censored by MTV for its depiction of scantily clad models.
- Fractured temporarily in 1985 into two distinct chart-topping side projects: the hard-rock supergroup *The Power Station* (with *Robert Palmer* and Chic drummer *Tony Thompson*) and the atmospheric art-pop trio *Arcadia*.
- Engineered an unexpected, critically lauded commercial comeback in 1993 with their self-titled release commonly known as *The Wedding Album*, propelled by international acoustic hit singles "Ordinary World" and "Come Undone."
