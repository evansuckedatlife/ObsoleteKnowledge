## summary

**Forgiveness Rock Record** is the fourth studio album by the celebrated Canadian indie-rock collective *Broken Social Scene*, released in May 2010 on Arts & Crafts. Produced by *John McEntire* of the post-rock band *Tortoise*, the album introduced streamlined arrangements, rhythmic precision, and accessible pop sensibilities to the group's signature orchestral sprawl. Featuring contributions from an array of prominent indie musicians, the record earned widespread critical acclaim, a nomination for Alternative Album of the Year at the 2011 Juno Awards, and a place on the 2010 Polaris Music Prize shortlist.

## you gotta know

- Represents the fourth full-length studio effort by Toronto-based collective Broken Social Scene, anchored by core co-founders *Kevin Drew* and *Brendan Canning*.
- Helmed by celebrated producer and multi-instrumentalist *John McEntire*, whose Chicago-based post-rock pedigree brought crisp drum production and electronic clarity to the band's dense instrumental layering.
- Yielded prominent indie singles such as the urgent lead track "World Sick", the exuberant groove of "Forced to Love", and the textured synthesizer ballad "All to All".
- Reunited the band's celebrated extended musical family, showcasing lead and backing vocals from *Leslie Feist*, *Emily Haines* and *James Shaw* of Metric, and *Amy Millan* and *Evan Cranley* of Stars.
- Welcomed notable guest appearances from international alternative rock icons, including *Spiral Stairs* (Scott Kannberg) of Pavement on the tongue-in-cheek track "Texaco Bitches" and *Sebastien Grainger* of Death from Above 1979.
- Critically praised for trading the chaotic, ambient jam-session aesthetic of their 2005 self-titled predecessor for melodic discipline, taut song structures, and danceable grooves.
- The band's shifting interpersonal dynamics, revolving vocal pairings, and history of internal romances drew frequent comparisons from music critics to classic 1970s soft-rock legends [[fleetwood-mac]].
- Recorded primarily at McEntire's celebrated Soma Electronic Music Studios in Chicago, anchoring its creation within the midwestern [[united-states]] indie scene alongside sessions in Toronto.
