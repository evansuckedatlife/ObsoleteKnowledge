## summary

**American cinema** refers to the body of films produced in the United States, and especially the industrial and artistic dominance of Hollywood from the 1920s through the present. American films shaped global visual language, narrative conventions, and entertainment itself; they exported American values, optimism, and mythology to the world. Hollywood became synonymous with cinema as an art form and an industry, creating distinctive genres and star systems that defined popular culture.

## you gotta know

- The *studio system* (roughly 1920s–1950s) consolidated production under a few major studios (MGM, Paramount, Warner Bros., Fox, RKO); they controlled actors, directors, screenwriters, and distribution, creating a vertically integrated industrial machine.
- *Golden Age cinema* (1930s–1940s) produced classics of [[screwball-comedy]], [[detective-fiction]], westerns, and melodrama; these films established the narrative and visual conventions still taught and imitated.
- American cinema developed a distinctive optimism: the [[american-dream]], the everyman hero, belief in individual agency and democracy (especially in films by [[frank-capra]]).
- *Genre* became central to American filmmaking: westerns, musicals, film noir, comedy, war films—each with recognizable visual and narrative codes that audiences came to expect.
- *Star system*: American cinema created a culture of celebrity; actors like [[james-stewart]], *Humphrey Bogart*, *Audrey Hepburn* became mythic figures, their personas shaping the roles they played.
- The influence of American cinema is global; it set technical standards, narrative structures, and thematic preoccupations that other film industries adopted, adapted, or rebelled against.
- Post-war shifts (1950s onward) saw the rise of television, the decline of the studio system, and the emergence of director-driven, more adult-oriented cinema and "New Hollywood."

## connections

- [[frank-capra]] — a defining figure whose populist humanism shaped American cinema's moral voice.
- [[james-stewart]] — his career embodies the American actor and the democratic everyman in American film.
- [[screwball-comedy]] — a genre that proved entertainment and social critique were not opposed.
- [[western-film]] — a uniquely American genre that exports frontier mythology.
- [[american-dream]] — the thematic core of much American cinema; the belief that hard work and virtue bring success.
- [[silent-film]] — the foundation of all American cinema; American directors pioneered editing and visual language.
