## summary

**Film editing** is the art of assembling shot sequences into a coherent narrative and emotional experience. From silent cinema's discovery that *juxtaposition creates meaning* to modern digital workflows, editing has been recognized as cinema's primary creative tool—more than cinematography or performance, editing is where cinema becomes cinema. The editor controls time, pacing, emotion, and the viewer's attention itself.

## you gotta know

- Soviet filmmakers (Eisenstein, Pudovkin) theorized montage as cinema's essence: the meaning emerges not from individual shots but from their *collision*—a close-up of eyes, then a meal, then a child generates hunger and parental love, not literal sequence.
- Continuity editing developed in Hollywood to make cuts *invisible*, allowing audiences to follow geography and action seamlessly; a cut on movement hides the discontinuity and maintains immersion.
- Kuleshov Effect (1920s): viewers assign emotional meaning to neutral shots based on surrounding context—a blank face next to food reads as hunger, next to a coffin reads as grief. Editing manufactures emotion through juxtaposition.
- D.W. Griffith pioneered the vocabulary: close-ups for emotion, long shots for geography, rapid intercutting between parallel actions to create suspense; his 1916 *Intolerance* is a masterclass in editing-driven storytelling.
- Silent cinema *required* editing to communicate—no dialogue meant every pause, expression, and spatial relationship depended on shot selection and pacing to land meaning and emotion.
- Pacing is the editor's primary instrument: fast cutting during action or emotional intensity, slow burns during suspense or grief, each controlling how time *feels* rather than its literal duration.
- Modern digital editing (non-linear, software-based) replaced mechanical film splicing in the 1990s, making revision seamless and fundamentally changing how editors conceptualize rhythm and structure.

## connections

- [[soviet-montage]] — the theory and practice that editing *creates* meaning through collision rather than mere arrangement; the essential counterpoint to Hollywood's continuity paradigm.
- [[camera-movement]] — the complementary technique; camera movement within a shot and editing between shots are two dimensions of the same spatial and temporal grammar.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — editing interacts with what's *in* the frame; the composition of a shot constrains its cutting possibilities and emotional weight.
- [[alfred-hitchcock]] — master of editing-driven suspense; his control over pacing and reveal transformed editing from an invisible craft into a narrational instrument.
- [[intertitles]] — text cards in silent cinema that editing placed for maximum narrative and emotional effect; their timing and placement were editing decisions.
- [[cinematography]] — the images the editor assembles; strong cinematography gives editors more options, but weak cinematography constrains editing possibilities.
- [[narrative-cinema]] — editing is what transforms a sequence of images into a *story* with cause, effect, and emotional trajectory.
