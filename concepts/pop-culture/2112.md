## summary

**2112** is the breakthrough 1976 concept album and title epic by the Canadian progressive rock power trio *Rush*. Released by *Mercury Records* after the commercial failure of *Caress of Steel*, the record features a side-long, twenty-minute science fiction suite depicting a dystopian future where music and individualism are outlawed by a totalitarian technocracy. It solidified *Rush's* critical and commercial standing in the [[united-states]] and internationally, establishing a foundational landmark in modern progressive metal and conceptual rock storytelling.

## you gotta know

- Side one is occupied by the seven-part title suite, which chronicles an anonymous protagonist discovering an antique acoustic guitar in a cave and defying the ruling technocracy.
- The overarching narrative portrays the *Solar Federation*, an interstellar regime governed by the authoritarian *Priests of the Temples of Syrinx*, who enforce absolute conformity via giant computers.
- The album was written as a defiant artistic gamble when *Rush* refused label pressure to produce radio-friendly pop singles following disappointing sales of their previous record.
- Drummer and primary lyricist *Neil Peart* credited the individualist philosophy of novelist *Ayn Rand* in the liner notes, acknowledging thematic parallels to her novella *Anthem*.
- The suite concludes with the protagonist committing suicide after realizing freedom cannot coexist with the regime, followed by a spoken proclamation: *"Attention all planets of the Solar Federation: We have assumed control."*
- Side two features standalone tracks reflecting eclectic hard rock styles, including *A Passage to Bangkok*, *The Twilight Zone*, and the introspective ballad *Tears*.
- The album introduced the iconic *Starman* emblem created by graphic designer *Hugh Syme*, depicting a naked man defying a red star, which became *Rush's* enduring mascot.
