## summary

**Parasite**, known in Korean as **Gisaengchung**, is a 2019 South Korean black comedy thriller film directed by *Bong Joon-ho*, who co-wrote the screenplay with *Han Jin-won*. Centering on the destitute Kim family as they systematically infiltrate the household of the wealthy Park family, the film delivers a scathing indictment of modern capitalist inequality and social class division. It achieved unprecedented global acclaim, becoming the first non-English-language film in history to win the Academy Award for Best Picture and cementing South Korean cinema's monumental impact on international pop culture.

## you gotta know

- Became the first South Korean film to win the prestigious Palme d'Or at the Cannes Film Festival, receiving a unanimous vote from the jury headed by *Alejandro González Iñárritu*.
- Made history at the 92nd Academy Awards by winning four major Oscars, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best International Feature Film.
- Follows the impoverished Kim family, led by patriarch *Ki-taek* (played by regular Bong collaborator *Song Kang-ho*), who reside in a cramped semi-basement apartment (*banjiha*) folding pizza boxes to survive.
- Features the famous "Jessica Jingle," a mnemonic tune sung by daughter *Ki-jung* (played by *Park So-dam*) to remember her fabricated identity as an art therapist, set to the melody of the Korean children's song "Dokdo is Our Land."
- Employs elaborate schemes to oust the Parks' existing domestic staff, including planting underwear in a chauffeured car and exploiting the longtime housekeeper *Moon-gwang*'s severe allergy to peaches.
- Pivots dramatically halfway through the narrative when the Kims discover that *Moon-gwang*'s husband, *Geun-sae*, has been secretly living in a subterranean concrete bunker beneath the Park estate for years to evade loan sharks.
- Uses stark visual verticality and architecture as a [[visual-metaphor]], contrasting the elevated, sun-drenched minimalist mansion designed by architect *Namgoong Hyeonja* with the subterranean depths flooded by city wastewater during a torrential rainstorm.
- Features the recurring motif of scent, particularly the distinctive "subway smell" noticed by the wealthy *Mr. Park*, which ultimately triggers the climactic violence during a lavish outdoor birthday party.

## connections

- [[mise-en-scene]] — the precise spatial composition and architectural staging used to visually emphasize class disparity across staircases and glass walls.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — the symbolic use of scholar's stones, basement stairs, and descending rainwater to represent socioeconomic stratification.
- [[film-editing]] — the rhythmic cross-cutting mastered by editor *Yang Jin-mo*, especially during the bravura six-minute montage detailing the peach-orchestrated dismissal of the housekeeper.
- [[camera-movement]] — the calculated vertical tracking and downward pans that physically map the characters' descent through the socioeconomic hierarchy.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — the chaotic, physical domestic deception that characterizes the first half of the film before its shift into horrific violence.
- [[tragedy]] — the narrative trajectory of inevitable fatal consequence stemming from systemic division, culminating in familial ruin and violent rupture.
- [[realism]] — the social verisimilitude depicting actual economic anxiety, youth unemployment, and semi-basement housing conditions in modern Seoul.
- [[industrialization]] — the rapid urban and economic development that created extreme wealth polarization in post-war South Korea.
