## summary

**Apostle of Hustle** was a Canadian indie rock band formed in Toronto in 2001 by singer, songwriter, and multi-instrumentalist *Andrew Whiteman*, best known as a core guitarist for the indie collective *Broken Social Scene*. Conceived following Whiteman's extended travels in Spain studying flamenco guitar, the band fused Latin American folk traditions, post-rock arrangements, and quirky indie pop. Released on the seminal Canadian label *Arts & Crafts*, their discography helped define the sonic diversity of the mid-2000s Canadian independent music explosion.

## you gotta know

- Founded as a creative vehicle for *Andrew Whiteman*, who enlisted bassist *Julian Brown* and drummer *Dean Stone* to complete the initial core trio.
- Sparked by Whiteman's two-month stay in the Andalusian region of Spain, where he immersed himself in traditional flamenco styles and rhythms.
- Released their acclaimed debut LP *Folkloric Feel* in 2004 on the *Arts & Crafts* imprint, earning widespread critical praise for its acoustic hybridity.
- Followed with the 2007 album *National Anthem of Nowhere*, a politically charged concept album exploring social alienation, economic disparity, and urban decay.
- Released their third and final full-length record, *Eats Darkness*, in 2009, which integrated heavy electronic textures and post-punk aggression.
- Closely tied to *Broken Social Scene*, sharing collaborative members, touring lineups, and the vibrant early-2000s Queen West musical community in Toronto.
- Featured eclectic instrumentation across their records, incorporating Cuban tres, melodica, glockenspiel, horns, and complex polyrhythmic percussion.
