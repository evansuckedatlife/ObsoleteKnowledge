## summary

**Deborah Ellis** is a Canadian author, peace activist, and philanthropist best known for writing socially conscious young-adult novels that depict children persevering through war, poverty, and political oppression. Her internationally celebrated *Breadwinner Series* brought global awareness to the plight of Afghan women and children living under repressive fundamentalist rule. Combining immersive documentary-style [[realism]] with deep humanitarian activism, Ellis has donated millions of dollars in book royalties directly to charitable relief organizations in the [[middle-east]] and across the globe.

## you gotta know

- Authored *The Breadwinner* (2000), which centers on *Parvana*, an eleven-year-old girl in Kabul who disguises herself as a boy to work and support her family after her father is unjustly arrested by the Taliban.
- Expanded the Afghan narrative into *The Breadwinner Series* through subsequent novels including *Parvana's Journey* (2002), *Mud City* (2003), and *My Name Is Parvana* (2012).
- Traveled directly to refugee camps in Pakistan in 1996 to interview exiled Afghan women, using their firsthand testimonials as the primary basis for Parvana's experiences.
- Saw *The Breadwinner* adapted into a critically acclaimed 2017 animated feature film directed by *Nora Twomey* and executive produced by *Angelina Jolie*, which received an Academy Award nomination.
- Examined the Israeli-Palestinian conflict through a multi-perspective narrative in *The Heaven Shop* authorial successor *A Company of Fools*, as well as *Three Wishes: Palestinian and Israeli Children Speak* (2004), which sparked school censorship debates over its candid war testimonies.
- Portrayed the devastating impact of the HIV/AIDS pandemic on orphaned children in southern Africa through her 2004 novel *The Heaven Shop*.
- Donated almost all royalties from *The Breadwinner* books to *Canadian Women for Women in Afghanistan* and the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees, financing schools and literacy programs for girls.
