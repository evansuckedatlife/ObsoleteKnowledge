## summary

**E. B. White** (*Elwyn Brooks White*) was a distinguished American essayist, author, and prose stylist whose concise, humorous, and humane writing defined twentieth-century literary journalism. As a pivotal contributor to *The New Yorker* for more than five decades, he established the publication's signature urbane tone and thoughtful editorial conscience. Beyond his essays, he achieved worldwide immortality through beloved children's classics such as *Charlotte's Web* and his timeless revision of the grammar guide *The Elements of Style*.

## you gotta know

- Authored the landmark 1952 children's novel *Charlotte's Web*, chronicling the barnyard friendship between *Wilbur* the pig and *Charlotte A. Cavatica*, a wise gray spider who saves him from slaughter by weaving praise into her web.
- Published *Stuart Little* in 1945, his first work for young readers, recounting the whimsical and episodic adventures of an elegant, mouse-sized boy born into an ordinary Manhattan family.
- Revised and expanded *The Elements of Style* in 1959, modernizing the classic writing manual originally written by his former Cornell University English professor *William Strunk Jr.*
- Penned the classic 1949 essay *"Here Is New York"*, a reflective, poetic meditation dividing the city into the commuter's, the worker's, and the outsider's metropolis while presciently warning of urban vulnerability.
- Joined the fledgling editorial staff of *The New Yorker* in 1927, where he penned the "Notes and Comment" opening pages, wrote humorous captions, and formed an influential creative partnership with his editor and wife, *Katharine Sergeant Angell White*.
- Wrote the 1970 children's book *The Trumpet of the Swan*, which follows *Louis*, a trumpeter swan born without a voice who learns to play a brass trumpet to win the affection of his love, *Serena*.
- Received a special Pulitzer Prize in 1978 honoring his letters, essays, and lifetime contribution to American children's literature and the craft of English letters.
- Lived on a quiet saltwater farm in North Brooklin, Maine, where his personal routines caring for geese, sheep, pigs, and arachnids provided the direct rustic inspiration for his pastoral masterpieces.

- [[new-york-city]] — cosmopolitan metropolis that nurtured his journalism at *The New Yorker*, formed the backdrop of *Stuart Little*, and inspired his classic essay *"Here Is New York"*.
- [[modernism]] — contemporary literary movement whose complex abstractions and deliberate obscurity he countered with clarity, directness, and democratic accessibility.
- [[realism]] — narrative framework grounding his animal fables, wherein life, biological reproduction, and mortality are portrayed without mawkish sentimentality.
- [[linguistics]] — field engaging with the syntactic rules, grammatical conventions, and rhetorical prescriptivism codified in his co-authored guide *The Elements of Style*.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical heritage reflected in his rational skepticism, defense of democratic freedoms, and devotion to intellectual decency during the mid-century.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global cataclysm that shadowed his 1940s essays, prompting his eloquent public defenses of international cooperation and individual liberties.
