## summary

**Both Sides, Now** is a landmark folk-pop philosophical song written by Canadian singer-songwriter *Joni Mitchell* in March 1967. First popularized as a major hit single by *Judy Collins* in 1968, Mitchell later recorded it for her 1969 album *Clouds* and revisited it as an orchestrated torch song in 2000. It remains one of the most recorded and analyzed compositions in North American popular music, famous for its meditative exploration of disillusionment, shifting perspectives, and the elusive nature of life and love.

## you gotta know

- Written by *Joni Mitchell* after reading Saul Bellow's 1959 novel *Henderson the Rain King*, specifically inspired by a passage where the protagonist views clouds from an airplane.
- First recorded and released as a hit single by *Judy Collins* on her 1967 album *Wildflowers*, winning a Grammy Award for Best Folk Performance in 1969.
- Mitchell released her own definitive acoustic guitar rendition on her second studio album, *Clouds* (1969), having previously performed it live on television and in folk coffeehouses.
- The lyrics are structured across three distinct life domains—clouds, love, and life itself—contrasting naive, romanticized childhood impressions ("ice cream castles in the air") with mature, painful reality ("they only block the sun").
- Each verse culminates in the refrain acknowledging cognitive limitation and epistemic humility: "I've looked at [clouds / love / life] from both sides now... and still somehow, it's [clouds' / love's / life's] illusions I recall; I really don't know [clouds / love / life] at all."
- Mitchell re-recorded the song in 2000 with a lush orchestral arrangement arranged by Vince Mendoza on her concept album *Both Sides Now*, showcasing a deeper, weathered vocal register.
- The 2000 orchestral recording was prominently featured in a pivotal, heartbreaking emotional scene in the 2003 romantic comedy film *Love Actually*, underscoring Emma Thompson's silent grief.
