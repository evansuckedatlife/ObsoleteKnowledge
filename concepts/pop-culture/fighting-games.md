## summary

The **fighting games** category, universally recognized as the **fighting game genre**, represents a prominent video game discipline centered on close-quarters, one-on-one martial combat between two on-screen combatants across timed rounds. Developed primarily in [[japan]] and popularized in arcades across the globe during the early 1990s, the genre pioneered competitive multiplayer dynamics through asymmetric character design, intricate input execution, and deep spatial tactics. Its technical complexity and passionate grassroots culture established foundational conventions for modern competitive gaming and international digital tournaments.

## you gotta know

- Codified into its definitive modern competitive architecture by *Capcom's* seminal arcade smash *Street Fighter II: The World Warrior* in 1991, which established the six-button control layout, directional command specials like the *Hadouken*, and dynamic character matchups.
- Discovered competitive combo mechanics almost entirely by accident when *Street Fighter II* lead designer *Noritaka Funamizu* noticed that precise attack inputs could cancel recovery animation frames, a bug intentionally preserved that became the bedrock of combat execution.
- Sparked major moral panics and congressional hearings in the [[united-states]] due to the extreme digitized violence, gore, and decapitation fatalities of *Midway's* *Mortal Kombat* in 1992, directly spurring the establishment of the Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB).
- Expanded into three-dimensional movement and polygonal rendering through groundbreaking releases from [[japan]], notably *Sega's* *Virtua Fighter* and *Namco's* *Tekken* and *Soulcalibur*, introducing sidestepping, ring-outs, and grappling systems.
- Cultivated the dedicated Fighting Game Community (FGC), a unique competitive subculture tracing its lineage to crowded, smoky physical arcades rather than corporate leagues, preserving a passionate open-bracket tournament ethos distinct from other competitive scenes.
- Produced legendary competitive folklore such as *"Evo Moment #37"* (the *"Daigo Parry"*), where Japanese player *Daigo Umehara* executed fifteen consecutive frame-perfect parries against *Justin Wong's* *Chun-Li* super move in *Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike* at *Evo 2004*.
- Relies heavily on mathematical frame data principles, where competitive mastery requires understanding startup frames, active hitboxes, hurtboxes, hitstun, block advantage, and the high-stakes mind games of wake-up pressure known as *okizeme*.
- Thrived alongside broader [[esports]] developments, serving as a pillar of international spectator entertainment while inspiring competitive crossover titles like the platform-brawler *Super Smash Bros.* series.

## connections

- [[esports]] — the competitive multiplayer gaming ecosystem in which fighting game tournaments like *EVO* represent the oldest continuous grassroots tradition.
- [[japan]] — the geographic and industrial cradle where pioneering studios like *Capcom*, *SNK*, *Namco*, and *Sega* forged the genre's mechanics.
- [[united-states]] — home to major arcade scenes, competitive communities, and rival development powerhouses like *Midway Games*.
- [[first-person-shooter]] — parallel competitive video game genre that emerged alongside fighting games to define late-twentieth-century multiplayer rivalry.
- [[counter-strike]] — PC-based competitive staple sharing the high-pressure tournament culture and precision motor demands seen across the fighting community.
