## summary

**The Incredibles** is a celebrated 2004 computer-animated superhero film written and directed by Brad Bird and produced by Pixar Animation Studios for Walt Disney Pictures. Set in an alternate mid-century retro-futuristic world where public backlash forces superheroes into mundane suburban hiding, the narrative follows Bob and Helen Parr as they balance domestic crises with covert vigilantism against an embittered former fan. Revered for its mature thematic depth, dynamic pacing, and mid-century modern aesthetic, it elevated animation by deconstructing nuclear family dynamics and heroic responsibility.

## you gotta know

- Directed by Brad Bird, who was recruited from traditional hand-drawn animation after helming *The Iron Giant*, making it the first Pixar feature developed with an entirely external auteur and human-only primary cast.
- Centers on Bob Parr, formerly Mr. Incredible, who struggles with a soul-crushing white-collar insurance job under an oppressive boss before covertly accepting missions on the volcanic island of Nomanisan.
- Features Helen Parr, known as Elastigirl, who discovers her husband's secret missions, commandeers a jet with callsign India Golf Niner-Niner, and adapts her body into a parachute and life raft to protect her children.
- Antagonist Buddy Pine adopts the supervillain persona Syndrome, constructing deadly Omnidroid war machines and declaring his goal to sell superhuman inventions so that "when everyone's super, no one will be."
- Iconic fashion designer Edna Mode, voiced by director Brad Bird himself, refuses to design cloaks for vigilantes in a memorable sequence citing the fatal cape-snag accidents of past superheroes like Stratogale and Dynaguy.
- Features Samuel L. Jackson as Lucius Best, alias Frozone, whose desperate search for his super suit while his apartment building is besieged became one of the most widely quoted comedic sequences in animation.
- Scored by composer Michael Giacchino with a bombastic, brass-heavy jazz orchestral score inspired by John Barry's 1960s *James Bond* soundtracks, replacing original collaborator John Barry when musical visions diverged.
- Won two Academy Awards at the 77th ceremony for Best Animated Feature and Best Sound Editing, beating out competitors and establishing a darker, action-driven benchmark for digital cinematic storytelling.
