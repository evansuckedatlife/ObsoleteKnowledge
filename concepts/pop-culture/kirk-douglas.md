## summary

**Kirk Douglas**, born **Issur Danielovitch Demsky**, was an iconic American stage and film actor, producer, and director whose intense, explosive performances defined the Golden Age of Hollywood in the *[[united-states]]*. Renowned for his chiselled jaw, distinctive cleft chin, and relentless physical charisma, he rose from impoverished immigrant beginnings in upstate New York to become one of cinema's most formidable leading men. Douglas achieved historic cinematic significance not only through classic roles in masterpieces like *Spartacus* and *Paths of Glory*, but also by courageously defying the anti-communist Hollywood blacklist to openly credit screenwriter *Dalton Trumbo*.

## you gotta know

- Born to illiterate Jewish immigrant parents from the Russian Empire in Amsterdam, New York, he served in the United States Navy during *[[world-war-ii]]* before transforming himself into a major Broadway and Hollywood star.
- Garnered three Academy Award nominations for Best Actor for his riveting, dynamic performances as an unscrupulous prizefighter in *Champion* (1949), a ruthless Hollywood producer in *The Bad and the Beautiful* (1952), and tortured painter *Vincent van Gogh* in *Lust for Life* (1956).
- Formed his own independent production banner, *Bryna Productions* (named after his mother), gaining creative autonomy and spearheading challenging projects that bypassed traditional studio conservatism.
- Collaborated closely with director *Stanley Kubrick* on the seminal anti-war masterpiece *Paths of Glory* (1957), portraying the principled French officer *Colonel Dax*, and on the historic historical epic *Spartacus* (1960).
- Played a decisive role in shattering the Hollywood blacklist during the *[[cold-war]]* by insisting that blacklisted screenwriter *Dalton Trumbo* receive official on-screen credit for *Spartacus*, publicly defying industry ostracism alongside President *John F. Kennedy*'s public attendance of the film.
- Starred in a wide range of rugged genre classics, including celebrated entries in the *[[western-film]]* canon such as *Gunfight at the O.K. Corral* (1957) as *Doc Holliday* opposite *Burt Lancaster*, with whom he forged a famous seven-film screen partnership.
- Earned critical acclaim under director *[[billy-wilder]]* in the biting film noir *Ace in the Hole* (1951), playing the cynical, manipulative newspaper journalist *Chuck Tatum*.
- Received an Academy Honorary Award in 1996 for fifty years of creative and moral force in the motion picture community, living to the age of 103 as the patriarch of an influential Hollywood dynasty that includes his son, Oscar-winning actor *Michael Douglas*.
