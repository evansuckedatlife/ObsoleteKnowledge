## summary

**Hollywoodland**, known historically through its monumental hillside billboard the **Hollywoodland Sign**, was a prestigious 1920s residential housing development in the Hollywood Hills of Los Angeles, California. Built in 1923 by real estate developers to promote an upscale enclave above the booming movie capital, the colossal electric sign originally spelled out all thirteen letters across Mount Lee. Although intended as a temporary commercial advertisement, the sign was eventually truncated and adopted as the world's most enduring physical icon of American cinema, ambition, and celebrity culture.

## you gotta know

- Erected in 1923 at a cost of roughly twenty-one thousand dollars by a syndicate that included Los Angeles Times publisher *Harry Chandler* and developer *Hobart Johnstone Whitley*.
- Originally illuminated by four thousand light bulbs that flashed in three successive sequences—*HOLLY*, then *WOOD*, then *LAND*—to draw motorists up into the newly developed hills.
- Became the site of a tragic Hollywood legend in September 1932 when despondent twenty-four-year-old Welsh-born actress *Peg Entwistle* leaped to her death from atop the letter *H*.
- Deteriorated severely over the 1930s and 1940s after the real estate firm went bankrupt, leading the *Hollywood Chamber of Commerce* to assume ownership in 1949 and strip off the final four letters *LAND*.
- Rebuilt completely from scratch in 1978 as a permanent steel structure following a high-profile public fundraising campaign spearheaded by shock-rock musician *Alice Cooper* and *Hugh Hefner*.
- Featured prominently as the title and central setting of the 2006 neo-noir mystery drama film *Hollywoodland*, which investigates the mysterious 1959 death of *Adventures of Superman* star *George Reeves*.
- Protected today as an officially designated Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument, secured against trespassers behind electronic perimeter fences and security sensors.
