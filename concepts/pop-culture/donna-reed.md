## summary

**Donna Reed**, born **Donna Belle Mullenger**, was an American screen and television actress whose warmth, emotional authenticity, and technical range made her one of mid-twentieth-century Hollywood's most enduring figures. Best remembered for her portrayal of Mary Hatch Bailey in *It's a Wonderful Life*, she brought profound heart and resilience to classic American cinema opposite leading men of the Golden Age. Her unexpected dramatic departure in *From Here to Eternity* earned her an Academy Award, and she later pioneered female creative agency by producing and starring in her own landmark television sitcom.

## you gotta know

- Portrayed *Mary Hatch Bailey* in *Frank Capra*'s holiday classic *It's a Wonderful Life* (1946), anchoring the emotional core of the film opposite [[james-stewart]] with radiant optimism and steadfast devotion.
- Won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for her subversive, against-type performance as the disillusioned club hostess *Lorene* (Alma Burke) in *Fred Zinnemann*'s 1953 wartime drama *From Here to Eternity*.
- Proved her genuine athletic capability on the set of *It's a Wonderful Life* by personally breaking the window of the old Granville house with a single baseball throw, startling director [[frank-capra]] who had hired a stunt marksman.
- Headlined the long-running television sitcom *The Donna Reed Show* from 1958 to 1966, portraying pediatrician's wife *Donna Stone* while exerting significant off-screen creative control through her production company, Todon Productions.
- Maintained an immense fan base among American servicemen during [[world-war-ii]], regularly answering thousands of letters from soldiers overseas who regarded her as the quintessential hometown sweetheart.
- Co-founded the anti-war advocacy group *Another Mother for Peace* during the late 1960s, actively opposing the Vietnam War and speaking out against nuclear proliferation.
- Replaced *Barbara Bel Geddes* as family matriarch *Miss Ellie Ewing* on the primetime television drama *Dallas* during the 1984–1985 season, demonstrating her enduring stature across five decades of screen entertainment.
