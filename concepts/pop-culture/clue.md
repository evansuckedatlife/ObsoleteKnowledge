## summary

**Clue** is a renowned murder-mystery franchise originating from the British deduction board game devised by *Anthony E. Pratt* under the title **Cluedo**, later adapted into a 1985 American ensemble mystery comedy film directed by *Jonathan Lynn*. Set within a secluded New England gothic mansion during the height of the 1950s Red Scare, the film follows six blackmailed dinner guests who must deduce which of them murdered their extortionist host before the police arrive. Celebrated for its breathless pacing, brilliant comic ensemble, and pioneering theatrical release with multiple distinct endings, the work stands as a paramount cult classic that elevated tabletop conventions into celebrated cinematic farce.

## you gotta know

- Features the canonical roster of color-coded murder suspects consisting of *Colonel Mustard*, *Miss Scarlet*, *Mrs. White*, *Mrs. Peacock*, *Professor Plum*, and *Mr. Green*, investigating lethal weapons like the candlestick, lead pipe, wrench, rope, revolver, and knife.
- The 1985 film adaptation directed by *Jonathan Lynn* and co-written with *John Landis* stars *Tim Curry* in a tour-de-force comic performance as *Wadsworth* the butler, who frantically reenacts every murder across the mansion halls in the third act.
- Released theatrically with three distinct endings labeled A, B, and C, leading different theaters to screen different culprits until home video consolidated the endings sequentially with the title card "Here's what really happened."
- *Madeline Kahn* delivered one of the most famous unscripted moments in cinematic comedy as *Mrs. White*, improvising her furious diatribe about "flames, flames on the side of my face" when describing her burning resentment toward the murdered singing telegram girl.
- Grounds its murder plot directly in the political paranoia of the *Cold War*, using *McCarthyism*, congressional espionage investigations, and nuclear secrets as the secret leverage with which *Mr. Boddy* blackmails every guest.
- The underlying board game was created during *World War II* air raids in Birmingham, *England*, patented by Pratt in 1947, and subsequently published by Waddingtons in the United Kingdom and Parker Brothers in the *United States*.
- *Michael McKean* portrays the ostensibly closeted and bumbling *Mr. Green*, who is revealed in the canonical final ending to be an undercover FBI agent who eliminates the real villain and declares that he is going home to sleep with his wife.
