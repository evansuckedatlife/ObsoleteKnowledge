## summary

**Visual metaphor** in cinema uses image, composition, and camera movement to express abstract concepts, emotional states, and thematic meanings without dialogue or exposition. Unlike literary metaphors that compare through language, cinematic visual metaphors embody meaning through what audiences *see and feel*—shadows that externalize guilt, architecture that expresses entrapment, camera angles that literalize emotional power relations. Visual metaphor is cinema's primary mechanism for transmuting psychological and philosophical abstraction into sensory experience.

## you gotta know

- Dutch angles (tilted frames) suggest moral corruption, disorientation, or psychological instability without stating it; the viewer *feels* something is wrong before consciously recognizing the tilted horizon.
- Shadows and chiaroscuro lighting externalize invisible psychological states: a character's guilt might manifest as shadows that seem to hunt them, their inner darkness made visible as environmental darkness.
- Vertical positioning encodes power: characters placed high in frame suggest authority or spiritual elevation, while characters placed low suggest vulnerability or moral degradation; no dialogue needed, the composition *shows* hierarchy.
- Mirrors, reflections, and symmetrical composition can express fragmentation, doubling, or psychological splitting; a character who sees themselves multiply or inverted becomes visually alienated from themselves.
- Camera movement through space can *embody* a character's emotional journey: a rising camera might suggest hope or realization, a descending camera suggests defeat or moral corruption.
- Recurring visual motifs (doors, windows, staircases) accumulate symbolic weight across a film; what begins as literal set dressing becomes thematic through repetition and compositional emphasis.
- Color symbolism in color cinema extends visual metaphor's range: reds express passion or danger, blues suggest melancholy or detachment, greens suggest illness or corruption; production designers and cinematographers strategically deploy color.

## connections

- [[camera-movement]] — visual metaphor often emerges through camera motion; a tilted camera tilt or crane movement can express emotional or moral shift without explanation.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — the spatial arrangement of set elements, lighting, and actors creates visual metaphor; a character physically trapped by set design becomes symbolically trapped psychologically.
- [[f-w-murnau]] — master of visual metaphor whose *Sunrise* and *Nosferatu* use shadows, composition, and camera movement to externalize psychological truths and inner turmoil.
- [[german-expressionism]] — the movement that prioritized visual metaphor over realistic representation; Expressionists distorted space and lighting to express psychological and spiritual states.
- [[psychological-drama]] — visual metaphor's essential partner; when internal psychological states are the subject, visual metaphor is the primary means of externalization and expression.
- [[symbolism]] — visual metaphor is cinema's form of literary symbolism; what literature conveys through recurring words and images, cinema achieves through visual composition and camera placement.
- [[cinematography]] — the technical craft that enables visual metaphor; cinematographers' choices in lighting, depth of field, and color temperature determine the metaphorical register.
