## summary

**Gandalf** is a fictional wizard and central protagonist created by English author and philologist J. R. R. Tolkien for his legendarium, appearing prominently in *The Hobbit* (1937) and *The Lord of the Rings* (1954–1955). Within Middle-earth, he is not a mortal human but an angelic spirit known as an *Ainu* (specifically a *Maia*) sent by the divine Valar in the guise of an old man to counsel, inspire, and unite the Free Peoples against the Dark Lord Sauron. His mythic archetypal presence stands as one of the most celebrated and influential figures in modern English fantasy literature and world popular culture.

## you gotta know

- Introduced in *The Hobbit* as an eccentric, wandering conjurer who recruits the hobbit Bilbo Baggins to assist Thorin Oakenshield's dwarven company in reclaiming Erebor.
- Known originally as *Olórin* in the Undying Lands of Valinor, he was chosen as one of the five *Istari* (wizards) sent across the sea to Middle-earth around the year 1000 of the Third Age.
- Bears the Elven Ring of Power called *Narya*, the Ring of Fire, given to him in secret upon his arrival at the Grey Havens by Círdan the Shipwright to kindle hope in people's hearts.
- Named differently across various cultures in Tolkien's world: known as *Mithrandir* ("Grey Pilgrim") among the Elves, *Tharkûn* among the Dwarves, and *Incánus* in the South.
- Confronts and falls fighting a demonic Balrog of Morgoth in the subterranean depths of the Mines of Moria beneath Khazad-dûm, declaring the famous line: "You cannot pass!" (rendered as "You shall not pass!" in film adaptations).
- Undergoes a mythic death and divine resurrection, returning from beyond mortal boundaries as *Gandalf the White* to replace the corrupted wizard Saruman as the leader of the White Council.
- Serves as the principal geopolitical strategist and moral catalyst during the War of the Ring, rallying the kingdom of Rohan and defending Minas Tirith in Gondor.
- Depicted in Peter Jackson's Oscar-winning cinematic trilogies by Sir Ian McKellen, cementing the character's visual legacy in modern global cinema.

## connections

- [[england]] — home nation and intellectual cradle of creator J. R. R. Tolkien, whose landscape and philological history shaped Middle-earth.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — foremost dramatist of English literature whose theatrical and linguistic traditions laid the groundwork for modern English epic storytelling.
- [[christianity]] — theological worldview that heavily informed Tolkien's conceptualization of grace, sacrifice, and resurrection in his character's rebirth.
- [[tragedy]] — classical literary form whose motifs of hubris and ruin Gandalf actively works to avert across the mortal realms.
- [[ancient-greece]] — foundational mythological heritage whose legendary wanderers and divine messengers prefigure archetypal guides like the Istari.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical civilization whose decline and expansive imperial architecture heavily inspired Tolkien's depiction of ancient Gondor.
