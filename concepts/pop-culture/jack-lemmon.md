## summary

**Jack Lemmon**, born **John Uhler Lemmon III**, was an acclaimed [[united-states]] actor whose versatile career spanned five decades across cinema, television, and theatre. Renowned for portraying neurotic, beleaguered everymen caught in the gears of modern life, he combined manic [[physical-comedy]] with profound emotional vulnerability. His celebrated partnerships with director [[billy-wilder]] and comic foil [[walter-matthau]] produced enduring American screen classics, establishing him as the first male performer to win Academy Awards in both the supporting and leading acting categories.

## you gotta know

- Disguised himself as female double-bass player *Daphne* alongside [[tony-curtis]] and [[marilyn-monroe]] in the landmark [[billy-wilder]] farce *Some Like It Hot* (1959), earning an Oscar nomination for his manic timing and energetic physical performance.
- Formed an iconic, decades-long on-screen partnership with [[walter-matthau]], famously starring as the fastidious, obsessive-compulsive neurotic *Felix Ungar* opposite Matthau's slovenly *Oscar Madison* in Neil Simon's hit comedy *The Odd Couple* (1968).
- Achieved a historic Academy Award milestone by becoming the first male performer to win Oscars in both major acting categories, winning Best Supporting Actor as Ensign Pulver in *Mister Roberts* (1955) and Best Actor as troubled businessman Harry Stoner in *Save the Tiger* (1973).
- Starred as the ambitious, morally conflicted corporate clerk C.C. Baxter in [[billy-wilder]]'s bittersweet classic *The Apartment* (1960), straining spaghetti with a tennis racket in a defining blend of melancholy and [[slapstick-comedy]] set in [[new-york-city]].
- Demonstrated remarkable dramatic range beyond comedic roles, garnering Academy Award nominations as a desperate alcoholic in Blake Edwards' *Days of Wine and Roses* (1962) and as aging, desperate salesman Shelley Levene in James Foley's film adaptation of David Mamet's *Glengarry Glen Ross* (1992).
- Won the Best Actor award at the Cannes Film Festival twice—one of only a handful of performers in history to achieve this feat—for his tense, politically charged roles in *The China Syndrome* (1979) and Costa-Gavras's *Missing* (1982).
- Developed his stagecraft and musical abilities as president of the Hasty Pudding Theatricals at Harvard University, served as a communications officer during [[world-war-ii]], and worked as a piano player and radio actor in [[new-york-city]] before breaking into Hollywood.

## connections

- [[billy-wilder]] — legendary director who directed him in seven features, refining his anxious screen persona into an iconic comic archetype.
- [[walter-matthau]] — frequent comedic foil and close lifelong friend across ten films, epitomised by their contrasting dynamic in *The Odd Couple*.
- [[marilyn-monroe]] — celebrated co-star in *Some Like It Hot*, playing singer Sugar "Kane" Kowalczyk opposite his hilarious female undercover persona.
- [[tony-curtis]] — screen partner on the run from the mob in *Some Like It Hot*, pairing with him as fellow undercover musicians.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — classic visual and farcical performance tradition that directly informed his agile movements, frantic gestures, and expressive double takes.
- [[physical-comedy]] — foundational discipline he mastered to ground chaotic, high-concept situations in believable bodily exhaustion and nervous ticks.
- [[james-stewart]] — fellow screen legend who defined an earlier generation's moral, stuttering, and relatable American everyman.
- [[world-war-ii]] — military conflict during which he served as an ensign in the [[united-states]] Navy before launching his professional acting career.
