## summary

**Like a Virgin** is a 1984 dance-pop song recorded by American singer *Madonna*, released as the lead single and title track of her breakthrough second studio album. Produced by *Nile Rodgers* in [[new-york-city]], the track pairs a driving post-disco bassline with provocative lyrics exploring romantic renewal and emotional vulnerability. The song transformed Madonna into an international cultural phenomenon and catalyzed intense debates across the [[united-states]] regarding female sexuality, youth culture, and visual performance in the television era.

## you gotta know

- Written by the songwriting partnership of *Billy Steinberg* and *Tom Kelly*, who originally conceived the track as a sensitive, autobiographical ballad based on Steinberg's emotional relief upon entering a fulfilling new relationship after a devastating breakup.
- Produced by *Chic* co-founder and rhythm guitarist *Nile Rodgers*, who initially disliked the demo's hook but recorded it at the *Power Station* in [[new-york-city]] with his *Chic* rhythm section partners *Bernard Edwards* on bass and *Tony Thompson* on drums.
- Ignited nationwide controversy during the first annual *MTV Video Music Awards* in September 1984, where Madonna performed the song after emerging from atop a giant wedding cake in a bustier, veil, and a belt buckle reading "Boy Toy," writhing suggestively on the floor.
- Accompanied by a widely circulated music video directed by *Mary Lambert* and shot in Venice, [[italy]], featuring Madonna dancing provocatively aboard a gondola in wedding attire juxtaposed with scenes of a real male lion roaming the Venetian streets.
- Reached number one on the *Billboard* Hot 100 in December 1984 and stayed at the top position for six consecutive weeks, becoming Madonna's first number-one single in the [[united-states]].
- Sparked vocal protests and moral panic from religious organizations and conservative watchdog groups, who contended that the song's suggestive wordplay and imagery promoted premarital promiscuity to teenage girls.
- Memorably analyzed during the opening diner scene of *Quentin Tarantino*'s 1992 debut film *Reservoir Dogs*, where the character *Mr. Brown* delivers an explicit monologue dissecting the song's sexual meaning.
