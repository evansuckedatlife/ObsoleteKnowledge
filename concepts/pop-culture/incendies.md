## summary

**Incendies** is a 2010 Canadian psychological drama film directed by *Denis Villeneuve*, adapted from *Wajdi Mouawad*'s acclaimed stage play of the same name, also known in English as **Scorched**. The story follows adult fraternal twins from Quebec who journey to their mother's war-torn ancestral homeland in the Levant to fulfill her dying wishes by locating their long-lost father and brother. Renowned for its devastating climatic revelations and classical dramatic structure, the film earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Foreign Language Film and catapulted Villeneuve into global cinematic prominence.

## you gotta know

- Begins with the reading of the will of *Nawal Marwan*, an immigrant mother whose death prompts notary *Jean Lebel* to deliver two sealed envelopes to her twin children, *Jeanne* and *Simon*, tasking them with tracking down a father they believed dead and a brother they never knew existed.
- Features an iconic, haunting opening sequence set to the song "You and Whose Army?" by the English rock band *Radiohead*, showing young child soldiers having their heads shaved inside a dusty military encampment.
- Adapts the narrative architecture of Greek [[tragedy]], specifically *Sophocles*'s *Oedipus Rex*, culminating in the horrific realization that the twins' missing father and brother are the exact same person: their mother's rapist and torturer, *Abou Tarek*, originally named *Nihad*.
- Employs chapter headings named after central people and places—including *Nihad*, *Deressa*, and *Kfar Ryat*—to alternate seamlessly between the twins' contemporary investigation and Nawal's past during a brutal sectarian conflict modeled on the Lebanese Civil War.
- Depicts Nawal's harrowing survival of a bus massacre perpetrated by Christian nationalist paramilitaries, during which she disguises herself with a crucifix to avoid execution before witnessing the slaughter and immolation of Muslim refugees.
- Portrays Nawal's incarceration inside the southern prison of *Kfar Ryat*, where she becomes known to guards and fellow inmates as the "Woman Who Sings" because she defiantly chants melodies to retain her humanity and comfort others.
- Traces the mystery's unraveling to an incident at a public swimming pool in Montreal, where Nawal suddenly suffers a debilitating catatonic stroke after spotting a distinctive tattoo of three dots on the heel of a passerby, identifying him as both her estranged son and her prison torturer.

## connections

- [[inception]] — released the same year in 2010, sharing a preoccupation with buried trauma, non-linear narrative cross-cutting, and labyrinthine subconscious investigations.
- [[tragedy]] — provides the narrative backbone for the film, modernizing the ancient Greek fatalism and incestuous catharsis of *Oedipus Rex*.
- [[middle-east]] — serves as the historical and geographical setting for Nawal's youth, capturing the factional warfare and religious displacement of 1970s conflicts.
- [[film-editing]] — used extensively to weave dual timelines together through parallel action, graphic matches, and rhythmic temporal shifts.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — utilized by Villeneuve to juxtapose the stark, cold modernism of Quebec with the scorched, sun-drenched landscapes of the Levant.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — evident in recurring motifs such as the three-dot tattoo, childhood swimming pools, and mirrored family photographs that symbolize fractured identities.
- [[camera-movement]] — characterized by deliberate slow push-ins and sweeping panoramic tracking shots that emphasize emotional paralysis and desolate environments.
