## summary

**Escape**, commercially known as **Escape (The Piña Colada Song)** or simply **The Piña Colada Song**, is a 1979 soft rock single written and performed by British-American singer-songwriter *Rupert Holmes*. Released as the lead single from his studio album *Partners in Crime*, the track surged to the top of the Billboard Hot 100, achieving the unique historical distinction of becoming the final number-one hit of the 1970s and the initial chart-topper of the 1980s. Celebrated for its narrative storytelling, ironic romantic twist, and ubiquitous tropical refrain, the song stands as a timeless cornerstone of yacht rock and late-twentieth-century American pop culture.

## you gotta know

- Reached the summit of the Billboard Hot 100 in late December 1979, was briefly displaced for one week, and reclaimed the number-one spot in January 1980, making it the only recording in Billboard history to hold the top spot across two distinct decades.
- The narrative lyrics detail a man bored with his current lover who answers a newspaper personal advertisement seeking someone who enjoys tropical beverages and walking in the rain, only to discover at the rendezvous bar that the advertiser is his existing partner.
- The song was originally conceived with the working title *People Need Other People* and featured lyrics about personal connections before Holmes completely revised the lyrics into a tongue-in-cheek narrative scenario.
- In his original draft, Holmes penned the famous line as "If you like Humphrey Bogart", but replaced the film icon with "piña colada" just prior to the recording session to invoke a refreshing tropical vacation fantasy.
- The famous parenthetical title was added retroactively after radio stations across the country were inundated with phone calls from listeners requesting "the piña colada song" because they did not recognize the official single title.
- Composer *Rupert Holmes* was a remarkably accomplished dramatist and writer beyond pop music, going on to win two Tony Awards in 1986 for writing the book, music, and lyrics for the Broadway musical *The Mystery of Edwin Drood*.
- The song enjoyed massive twenty-first-century cultural resurgences through memorable soundtrack placements in major motion pictures, notably *Shrek* (2001) and Marvel Studios' *Guardians of the Galaxy* (2014) as a staple of the *Awesome Mix Vol. 1*.
