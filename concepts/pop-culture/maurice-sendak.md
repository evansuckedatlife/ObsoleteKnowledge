## summary

**Maurice Sendak** was an acclaimed American author and illustrator whose groundbreaking children's books revolutionized youth literature in the twentieth century. Born in Brooklyn to Jewish-Polish immigrant parents, Sendak infused his fantasy narratives with emotional honesty, childhood vulnerability, and psychological complexity rather than sentimental moralizing. His 1963 masterpiece *Where the Wild Things Are* earned the Caldecott Medal and cemented his position as one of the most influential picture book creators in literary history.

## you gotta know

- Wrote and illustrated *Where the Wild Things Are* in 1963, depicting young *Max* sailing across an ocean to tame ferocious horned monsters through a staring contest.
- Published *In the Night Kitchen* in 1970, which sparked controversy and frequent censorship over the casual nudity of its wandering child protagonist, *Mickey*.
- Created the beloved *Little Bear* book series in collaboration with author Else Holmelund Minarik, establishing classic mid-century early reader illustration.
- Illustrated *The Nutshell Library* in 1962, a set of four miniature volumes that included the whimsical alphabet rhyme *Alligators All Around* and the cautionary verse *Pierre*.
- Addressed the trauma of the Holocaust directly through his dark work, reflecting on extended family members murdered in Europe during the war.
- Collaborated with playwright Tony Kushner on an illustrated adaptation of the Czech children's opera *Brundibár*, originally performed by children in the Theresienstadt ghetto.
- Received the Hans Christian Andersen Award in 1970 and the inaugural Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award in 2003 for his life's contribution to international children's literature.
