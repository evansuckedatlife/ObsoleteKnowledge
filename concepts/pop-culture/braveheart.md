## summary

**Braveheart** is a 1995 epic historical drama film directed by and starring *Mel Gibson*, depicting the life of late-13th-century Scottish warrior *William Wallace*. Written by *Randall Wallace*, the film chronicles Wallace leading the Scots in the First War of Scottish Independence against King *Edward I* of England ("Longshanks") following the execution of his secret bride. The production was a major box-office success and won five Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director, becoming an enduring pop-culture touchstone for cinematic battlefield choreography, rousing battle oratory, and Scottish national romanticism.

## you gotta know

- Directed, produced by, and starring *Mel Gibson* as Scottish rebel leader *William Wallace*, opposite *Patrick McGoohan* as the ruthless King *Edward I*.
- Inspired by Blind Harry's 15th-century epic poem *The Actes and Deidis of the Illustre and Vallyeant Campioun Schir William Wallace*.
- Features famous, impassioned battle speeches, most notably Wallace's rallying cry at the Battle of Stirling Bridge: "They may take our lives, but they'll never take our freedom!"
- Received widespread criticism from historians for extensive historical inaccuracies, including depicting kilts and woad blue face-paint centuries out of era, and fabricating a romance between Wallace and *Isabella of France*.
- Renowned for its groundbreaking, visceral medieval combat sequences, utilizing hundreds of members of the Irish Army Reserve as extras on locations in Ireland and Scotland.
- Clinched five Oscars at the 68th Academy Awards: Best Picture, Best Director for Gibson, Best Cinematography, Best Sound Effects Editing, and Best Makeup.
- The iconic climax depicts Wallace's public torture and execution in London, refusing to submit to the English crown and crying out "Freedom!" with his dying breath.
