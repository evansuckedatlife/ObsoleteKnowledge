## summary

*Ernst* **Ingmar Bergman** was an internationally celebrated Swedish film and theatre director whose career spanned more than five decades, cementing him as one of cinema's greatest auteurs. Renowned for stark psychological depth, spiritual doubt, and probing explorations of mortality and human cruelty, his works defined European art cinema. Masterpieces like *The Seventh Seal*, *Wild Strawberries*, and *Persona* revolutionized visual storytelling through intimate close-ups and intense philosophical introspection.

## you gotta know

- Swedish auteur regarded alongside *Federico Fellini* and *Akira Kurosawa* as a defining giant of postwar modernist cinema.
- Raised in a strict Lutheran household by a father who was chaplain to the royal family, an upbringing that infused his work with recurring crises of religious faith and divine silence.
- Achieved international renown with the medieval allegory *The Seventh Seal* (1957), featuring the iconic scene of a returned crusader playing chess with *Death*.
- Released *Wild Strawberries* (1957) in the same breakthrough year, tracking an aging professor's nostalgic and surreal reverie on mortality and regret.
- Directed the experimental psychological drama *Persona* (1966), celebrated for its radical depiction of merged female identities and avant-garde cinematic ruptures.
- Maintained enduring collaborations with cinematographer *Sven Nykvist*, master of natural lighting, and an ensemble cast including *Liv Ullmann*, *Max von Sydow*, *Bibi Andersson*, and *Erland Josephson*.
- Explored family trauma and marital disillusionment in late masterpieces like *Cries and Whispers* (1972), *Scenes from a Marriage* (1973), and the semi-autobiographical epic *Fanny and Alexander* (1982).

## connections

- [[existentialism]] — philosophical current central to his meditations on mortality, loneliness, and the search for purpose in an indifferent universe.
- [[christianity]] — religious tradition that shaped his austere upbringing and fueled his cinematic trilogy on the silence of God.
- [[modernism]] — 20th-century artistic movement characterized by self-reflexive narrative ruptures and psychological interiority, epitomized by *Persona*.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — stage and screen composition perfected through extreme close-ups, two-shots, and stark Scandinavian landscapes on the island of *Fårö*.
- [[camera-movement]] — cinematic technique developed with collaborator *Sven Nykvist* to capture minute shifts in human emotion.
- [[film-editing]] — editorial craft used prominently in his avant-garde transitions and iconic face-merging sequences.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — artistic device exemplified by the knight's fateful chess match against personified death.
- [[tragedy]] — dramatic mode informing his harrowing studies of spiritual isolation, terminal illness, and domestic anguish.
