## summary

**One Hundred and One Dalmatians** (frequently titled **101 Dalmatians**) is a 1961 animated adventure film produced by *Walt Disney Productions* and adapted from the 1956 British novel *The Hundred and One Dalmatians* by *Dodie Smith*. Set across London and rural [[england]], the story depicts the desperate quest of two parents to rescue their stolen litter from the clutches of an aristocratic fur enthusiast who intends to slaughter them for a spotted coat. Acclaimed for introducing xerographic production methods that streamlined animation drawing, the work remains a high point of silver-age American studio animation, lauded for its mid-century graphic modernism and unforgettable villainy.

## you gotta know

- Centered on the villainous schemes of *Cruella de Vil*, an eccentric, chain-smoking heiress whose plot to kidnap puppies for a spotted coat provides the narrative engine of the story.
- Adapted from the 1956 children's novel by English author *Dodie Smith*, who based the canine characters on her own real pet dalmatian named *Pongo*.
- Marked the initial feature-length deployment of xerography in animation, a technological breakthrough spearheaded by *Ub Iwerks* that eliminated manual hand-inking and transferred animators' pencil sketches directly onto cels.
- Introduced the *Twilight Bark*, an expansive telegraphic canine communication network across the English countryside used by dogs to relay intelligence about missing puppies.
- Featured the iconic song *Cruella De Vil*, improvised on piano by songwriter *Roger Radcliffe* with singing vocals performed by *Bill Lee*.
- Climaxed with a daring winter escape from *Hell Hall* in Suffolk, wherein the puppies roll in chimney soot to disguise their white coats from their pursuers.
- Relies extensively on [[slapstick-comedy]] and [[physical-comedy]] during the bumbling exploits of Cruella's hired henchmen, *Jasper* and *Horace Badun*.

## connections

- [[england]] — serves as the atmospheric mid-century setting, ranging from Regent's Park townhouses to the snowy countryside of Suffolk.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — employs jagged line art and sickly green smoke as visual cues signaling the corruption and vanity of its antagonist.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — animates the clumsy failures of the henchmen who are repeatedly thwarted by farm animals.
- [[physical-comedy]] — structures the humorous choreography of the opening montage matching dog breeds with their human owners.
- [[film-editing]] — utilizes rapid rhythmic cutting during the canine rescue raids and the climactic vehicular pursuit through the snow.
- [[i-love-lucy]] — mirrors the era's domestic television boom, visible when the puppies gather around a screen to watch the canine hero *Thunderbolt*.
- [[2001-a-space-odyssey]] — shares a 1960s British production milieu where bold new visual technologies transformed traditional cinematic rendering.
