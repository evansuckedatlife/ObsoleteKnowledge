## summary

**Beyblades** are customizable spinning-top toys originally developed and manufactured by the Japanese company *Takara* in 1999, later popularized internationally by *Hasbro*. Players launch their tops into an enclosed plastic arena called a *Beystadium* using mechanical ripcord launchers, competing to outlast or knock out opposing tops. Accompanied by a long-running manga and anime franchise, the game became a global commercial phenomenon and a competitive gaming staple among children in the early 2000s and beyond.

## you gotta know

- Launched with the iconic English catchphrase *"Let it rip!"*, which players shout when pulling the ripcord launcher to fire their tops into play.
- Divided into four primary performance types that follow a rock-paper-scissors dynamic: *Attack*, *Defense*, *Stamina*, and *Balance*.
- Composed of modular interchangeable components, traditionally including a *Bit Chip*, *Attack Ring*, *Weight Disk*, and *Blade Base*, allowing players to customize physics attributes.
- Matches are won through three primary victory conditions: a *Survivor Finish* (spinning longer than the opponent), a *Ring Out Finish* (knocking the opponent out of the stadium), or a *Burst Finish* (causing the opponent's top to physically disassemble upon impact, introduced in the *Beyblade Burst* generation).
- Supported by the original anime television series following the protagonist *Tyson Granger* (*Takao Kinomiya*) and his team, the *Bladebreakers*, who summon mystical *Bit-Beasts* like *Dragoon*, *Dranzer*, *Driger*, and *Draciel*.
- Evolved through distinct generational system iterations, transitioning from plastic tops to the die-cast metal parts of the *Metal Fight Beyblade* era, followed by spring-loaded burst mechanics and gear-rail acceleration systems.
