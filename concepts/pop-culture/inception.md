## summary

**Inception** is a 2010 science fiction heist film written and directed by *Christopher Nolan*. The story follows *Dom Cobb*, an industrial espionage specialist who infiltrates the subconscious minds of corporate targets through experimental dream-sharing technology, who is hired to plant an idea into a corporate heir's mind rather than steal one. Famed for its ambitious cross-cut dream architecture, gravity-defying practical effects, and philosophical ambiguity, the film grossed over 800 million dollars worldwide and secured four Academy Awards.

## you gotta know

- Stars *Leonardo DiCaprio* as Dom Cobb, an "extractor" unable to return to his children in the United States due to murder charges stemming from the suicide of his wife, *Mal*, played by *Marion Cotillard*.
- Follows an audacious assignment funded by energy magnate *Mr. Saito* to perform the nearly impossible inverse of extraction—"inception"—on rival heir *Robert Fischer* to compel him into dismantling his late father's industrial empire.
- Gathers a specialized crew consisting of architect *Ariadne*, who designs dream mazes; point man *Arthur*, who manages logistics; master forger *Eames*, who impersonates key figures; and chemist *Yusuf*, who concocts powerful sedatives.
- Introduces the concept of "totems"—small personal items with secret physical peculiarities, such as Cobb's spinning brass top and Arthur's weighted die, designed to let a dreamer determine whether they are in someone else's subconscious.
- Uses synchronized "kicks"—sudden physical sensations of falling or impact—to awaken dreamers across three nested dream tiers: a rain-slicked city, a zero-gravity luxury hotel, and a fortified alpine snow fortress.
- Coordinates the timing of dream collapses using the slowed-down tempo of *Édith Piaf*'s song "Non, je ne regrette rien," which directly inspired composer *Hans Zimmer*'s signature booming brass score.
- Ends on one of modern cinema's most famous cliffhangers, as Cobb reunites with his children after spinning his totem on a dining table, with the camera cutting to black just as the spinning top exhibits a faint, inconclusive wobble.
