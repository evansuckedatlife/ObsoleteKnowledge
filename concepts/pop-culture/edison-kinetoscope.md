## summary

The **Edison Kinetoscope** was an early motion picture exhibition device invented in the early 1890s in the [[united-states]] by *William Kennedy Dickson* under the direction of *Thomas Edison*. It introduced the concept of **peephole cinema**, where an individual viewer watched continuous celluloid film spooled rapidly past an illuminated shutter through an eyepiece. As a vital bridge between still photography and modern projection, it laid the technical and commercial foundation for the entire [[silent-film]] industry.

## you gotta know

- Designed primarily by *Edison's* Scottish assistant *William Kennedy Dickson* between 1889 and 1892 in West Orange, New Jersey.
- Relied on a continuous loop of perforated 35mm celluloid film developed with *George Eastman*, establishing the standard gauge for global cinema.
- Functioned as a personal viewing cabinet rather than an auditorium projector because *Edison* believed individual coin-operated units were far more profitable.
- Debuted commercially in April 1894 with the opening of the first public parlor in [[new-york-city]], sparking nationwide amusement parlor fads.
- Films for the machine were shot inside the *Black Maria*, a dark, tarpaper-covered revolving studio built on a turntable to track natural sunlight.
- Early subjects included short variety attractions such as boxer bouts, dancers, acrobats, and *Fred Ott's Sneeze*, recorded on strips lasting only tens of seconds.
- Rapidly superseded by the *Lumière brothers'* *Cinématographe* in [[france]], which projected motion pictures onto large screens for communal audiences.
