## summary

**Whiplash** is a 2014 American psychological drama film written and directed by Damien Chazelle. The film depicts the ferocious battle of wills between an ambitious jazz drumming conservatory student, Andrew Neiman, and his abusive studio band instructor, Terence Fletcher. Celebrated for its breathless rhythm, visceral audio design, and tour-de-force performances, the film claimed three Academy Awards and cemented Chazelle as a major voice in contemporary cinema.

## you gotta know

- Stars Miles Teller as ambitious jazz drummer Andrew Neiman and J. K. Simmons as the tyrannical instructor Terence Fletcher at the fictional Shaffer Conservatory in *New York City*.
- J. K. Simmons won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for his terrifying performance, famously hurling a chair at Neiman's head and demanding perfection with the line "Not quite my tempo."
- Damien Chazelle based the script on his own agonizing experiences in a highly competitive high school jazz studio band, originally producing an eighteen-minute short film to secure financing.
- The movie takes its title from Hank Levy's complex 1973 big-band jazz composition written in 7/4 time, which serves as a central rehearsal piece alongside Juan Tizol and Duke Ellington's *Caravan*.
- Editor Tom Cross earned the Academy Award for Best Film Editing by cutting rehearsal and performance sequences with the rapid, percussive intensity of an action film, syncing cuts to cymbal crashes, snare strikes, and splashing sweat.
- Neiman practices until his hands bleed profusely, submerging raw blisters into pitchers of ice water to endure Fletcher's relentless psychological and physical humiliation.
- The climactic ten-minute sequence at Carnegie Hall features Neiman hijacking Fletcher's performance with an unprompted, transcendent drum solo of *Caravan*, forcing Fletcher into a grudging, symbiotic conducting partnership.
