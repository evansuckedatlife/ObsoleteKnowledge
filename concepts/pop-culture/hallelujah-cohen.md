## summary

**Hallelujah** is a celebrated folk-rock hymn written and originally recorded by Canadian singer-songwriter *Leonard Cohen* for his 1984 album *Various Positions*. Characterized by its haunting melody, melancholic tone, and intricate juxtaposition of sacred scripture with romantic heartbreak, it achieved global acclaim largely through iconic covers by *John Cale* and *Jeff Buckley*. Today, it stands as one of the most widely performed and culturally ubiquitous secular anthems in modern popular music.

## you gotta know

- Originally released on *Leonard Cohen*'s 1984 studio record *Various Positions*, initially rejected for American distribution by Columbia Records executive *Walter Yetnikoff*.
- Famously references biblical narratives in its opening stanzas, including King David playing a secret chord to please the Lord and Samson being shorn by Delilah.
- Cohen reportedly drafted as many as eighty distinct verses across several notebooks before paring down the recorded version.
- *John Cale* reworked the song in 1991 for a tribute album, selecting more secular verses and arranging the distinctive arpeggiated piano accompaniment.
- *Jeff Buckley* based his critically revered 1994 acoustic guitar cover on Cale's arrangement, propelling the track to legendary status on his album *Grace*.
- Featured prominently in the 2001 animated feature film *Shrek*, which popularized *Rufus Wainwright*'s rendition and introduced the composition to younger generations.
- Self-referentially explains its own harmonic progression in the first verse through the lyrical breakdown: "the fourth, the fifth, the minor fall, the major lift."
