## summary

**The Lone Ranger** is an enduring American multimedia franchise centered on a masked former Texas Ranger who rides through the Old West fighting outlaw injustice alongside his Native American partner, *Tonto*. Created by George W. Trendle and writer Fran Striker, the hero debuted on Detroit radio station WXYZ in 1933 before expanding into wildly popular pulp novels, comic strips, movie serials, and a definitive television series starring Clayton Moore and Jay Silverheels. With his white stallion *Silver*, signature silver bullets, and the thunderous strains of Rossini's *William Tell Overture*, the character became a quintessential archetype of American frontier mythology and popular morality.

## you gotta know

- Follows *John Reid*, the sole survivor of a brutal ambush by the Cavendish outlaw gang on a detachment of six Texas Rangers, who dons a black domino mask made from his fallen brother's vest to conceal his identity while bringing criminals to justice.
- Accompanied by his loyal Native American companion *Tonto*, famously portrayed on television by Mohawk actor Jay Silverheels, who nurses Reid back to health, addresses him with the famous phrase "kemosabe," and shares his moral crusade.
- Mounted upon his majestic white stallion *Silver*, heralded by the character's ubiquitous battle cry: "Hi-yo, Silver, away!"
- Adopted the breakneck brass finale of Gioachino Rossini's 1829 opera overture to *William Tell* as its universally recognized musical theme across radio, television, and film.
- Bound by an uncompromising, self-imposed moral code authored by creator Fran Striker, which dictated that the hero must never drink, smoke, use profanity, or shoot to kill when shooting to disarm was possible.
- Used distinctive silver bullets not as an indulgence of vanity, but as a grave reminder of the heavy cost and sanctity of human life, leaving behind a single cartridge as his calling card of justice.
