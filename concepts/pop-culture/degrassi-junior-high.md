## summary

**Degrassi Junior High** is a pioneering Canadian teen drama television series created by *Linda Schuyler* and *Kit Hood*, which originally aired on CBC from 1987 to 1989. Set in an authentic east-end Toronto school, the production set a global standard for adolescent television through its raw commitment to social [[realism]], age-appropriate non-professional actors, and unvarnished narrative arcs. The series remains a cultural landmark in youth programming for confronting difficult topics like teenage pregnancy, substance abuse, and eating disorders without sensationalism or easy moralizing.

## you gotta know

- Premiered in 1987 on CBC and PBS, tracking an ensemble cast of suburban Canadian seventh and eighth graders as they navigated real-world adolescent pressures.
- Grounded its visual and dramatic style in gritty social [[realism]], casting untrained child actors who wore their own street clothes—frequently an oversized [[denim-jacket]]—and applied their own makeup to preserve verisimilitude.
- Featured the groundbreaking character *Joey Jeremiah* (*Pat Mastroianni*), whose iconic fedora, mischievous antics, and personal schemes defined the show's comedic energy and earned him a Gemini Award.
- Broadcast the critically acclaimed, controversial two-part episode *"It's Late"*, depicting the teenage pregnancy of eighth-grader *Christine "Spike" Nelson* (*Amanda Stepto*), which won an International Emmy Award.
- Addressed intense adolescent crises directly, including *Wheels's* (*Neil Hope*) tragic loss of his parents to a drunk driver and *Stephanie Kaye's* (*Nicole Stoffman*) struggles with school politics and body image.
- Avoided moralizing after-school-special tropes by allowing storylines to carry lasting emotional consequences across episodes, a stylistic choice that directly informed its sequel [[degrassi-the-next-generation]].
- Concluded after three seasons when the students graduated to *Degrassi High* (1989–1991), culminating in the television film *School's Out!* (1992).

## connections

- [[denim-jacket]] — staple everyday attire worn by adolescent cast members to emphasize working-class Canadian authenticity.
- [[realism]] — the foundational storytelling aesthetic prioritizing naturalistic dialogue, unglamorous aesthetics, and genuine adolescent trauma.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — the deliberate staging of modest Toronto public school corridors, cramped bedrooms, and natural lighting.
- [[camera-movement]] — mobile handheld cinematography utilized across school hallways to capture spontaneous peer interactions.
- [[film-editing]] — the cross-cutting structural editing between parallel classroom subplots that framed ensemble storytelling.
- [[i-love-lucy]] — contrasting historical touchstone of traditional studio multi-camera television formats superseded by naturalistic location shooting.
