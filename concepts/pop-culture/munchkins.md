## summary

**Munchkins** are a fictional diminutive people inhabiting **Munchkin Country** in the Land of Oz, first introduced in L. Frank Baum's 1900 children's novel *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*. They achieved immortal status in twentieth-century popular culture through Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer's 1939 musical fantasy film *The Wizard of Oz*, where they welcome Dorothy Gale following her house's fatal landing on the Wicked Witch of the East. Their musical sequences and unique costuming established an indelible cinematic trope and a standard reference in American fantasy.

## you gotta know

- First appeared in print in L. Frank Baum's 1900 novel *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*, where they inhabit the eastern quadrant of Oz and are identified by their fondness for the color blue.
- In the 1939 MGM movie classic *The Wizard of Oz*, they inhabit Munchkinland and celebrate their liberation from tyranny by singing *"Ding-Dong! The Witch Is Dead"*.
- The film production cast more than one hundred actors with dwarfism, recruited largely through Leo Singer's European vaudeville troupe known as Singer's Midgets.
- Represented during the musical greeting by civic and professional factions such as the Mayor of Munchkin City, the Coroner, the *Lollipop Guild*, and the *Lullaby League*.
- The distinctive high-pitched voices heard in the 1939 film were created by recording vocalists at normal speed and then accelerating the audio track in post-production.
- The term entered popular lexicon beyond Oz lore, used informally to describe young children, small physical stature, or in gaming jargon to denote a min-maxing rules abuser.
- In November 2007, the surviving actors who portrayed the characters in the 1939 film were collectively honored with a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — foundational tradition of classic American fairy tales initiated by L. Frank Baum's Oz series at the turn of the twentieth century.
- [[united-states]] — nation where the original literature was authored and where the 1939 MGM cinematic adaptation became a permanent broadcast cultural institution.
- [[vaudeville]] — theatrical variety circuit that trained and employed many of the performers who made up Singer's Midgets prior to the film's production.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — theatrical performance mode sharing physical humor and timing methods with early musical fantasy cinema.
- [[silent-film]] — preceding era of early cinematic adaptations that laid the technical groundwork for MGM's pioneering three-strip Technicolor spectacle.
