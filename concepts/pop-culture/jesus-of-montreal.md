## summary

**Jesus of Montreal**, known in French as **Jésus de Montréal**, is a critically acclaimed 1989 satirical drama film written and directed by French-Canadian filmmaker *Denys Arcand*. The story follows a troupe of bohemian actors commissioned by a Roman Catholic basilica to modernize its annual Passion Play atop Mount Royal, only for their lives to progressively mirror the biblical events they dramatize. Renowned for its biting critique of consumerism, institutional religion, and artistic commodification, the film won the Jury Prize at the Cannes Film Festival and earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Foreign Language Film.

## you gotta know

- Revolves around protagonist *Daniel Colombe*, portrayed by *Lothaire Bluteau*, an idealistic actor who conducts extensive archaeological research to craft a radical, historical interpretation of the life of Christ.
- Reinterprets Jesus of Nazareth as an itinerant first-century teacher and philosopher influenced by the Essenes, rejecting traditional ecclesiastical orthodoxy in favor of historical-critical scholarship.
- Mirrors the New Testament narrative structure through modern allegories, including an iconic sequence where Daniel furiously destroys broadcast equipment during an exploitative advertising audition, recreating the cleansing of the Temple.
- Critiques contemporary media culture, corporate marketing, and institutional hypocrisy as the Catholic authorities move to shut down the production despite ecstatic public reception.
- Culminates in a tragic climax when police interrupt the performance on Mount Royal, inadvertently causing the heavy wooden cross to collapse upon Daniel, inflicting fatal head trauma.
- Concludes with a secular resurrection allegory where Daniel's internal organs are transplanted to save others, granting sight to a blind woman and extending the life of a cardiac patient.
- Swept the 11th Genie Awards in Canada, capturing twelve trophies including Best Motion Picture, Best Director, and Best Actor.

- [[tragedy]] — narrative framework depicting an ethical individual destroyed by unyielding institutional and legal machinery.
- [[modernism]] — self-reflexive metacinema utilizing a play-within-a-film to dismantle historical narrative and conventional belief.
- [[existentialism]] — central philosophical focus on individual authenticity, artistic conviction, and moral choice in a commercialized world.
- [[france]] — key co-producing nation and cultural touchstone for francophone Quebecois cinema and Cannes festival recognition.
- [[realism]] — gritty, unvarnished depiction of Montreal's underfunded healthcare system and precarious bohemian artistic circles.
- [[jim-carrey]] — prominent Canadian screen figure whose work in *The Truman Show* shares the film's suspicion of mediated reality.
- [[jessie-reyez]] — Canadian musical artist whose autobiographical critiques of industry gatekeepers mirror the film's anti-exploitation message.
