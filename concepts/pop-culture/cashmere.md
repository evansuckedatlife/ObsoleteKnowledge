## summary

**Cashmere**, also designated as **cashmere wool** or **pashmina**, is a rare and luxurious animal textile fiber obtained from the fine insulating undercoat of cashmere goats. Originating across the high-altitude plateaus of Central and South Asia, the material is celebrated for its extraordinary softness, lightness, and superior thermal insulation compared to standard sheep wool. Cultivated through labor-intensive harvesting and traditional artisan weaving, it has served as an international status symbol in both aristocratic heritage and modern designer apparel.

## you gotta know

- Harvested from the delicate underdown of the cashmere goat (*Capra hircus laniger*), predominantly bred across the windswept highlands of Mongolia, northern China, Tibet, and Kashmir.
- Collected during the springtime molting season through painstaking manual combing rather than mechanical shearing, requiring the annual yield of multiple goats to produce a single pullover sweater.
- Demonstrates exceptional thermal efficiency, possessing natural crimped fibers that trap insulating air pockets to deliver nearly three times the warmth of standard ovine wool at a fraction of the weight.
- Flourished as an imperial luxury commodity when intricately patterned Kashmiri shawls bearing the iconic tear-drop *boteh* or paisley motif captured the fascination of European high society.
- Popularized among European aristocrats by figures such as Empress Joséphine of *France*, whose extensive collection of imported shawls sparked a widespread nineteenth-century fashion craze.
- Catalyzed industrial textile innovations in Scotland and *Italy*, where heritage processing mills utilized soft local river water to wash, spin, and finish premium yarn.
- Maintained a lasting cultural status as a luxury staple of modern urban fashion, widely worn by trendsetters and fictional style icons such as *Carrie Bradshaw*.
