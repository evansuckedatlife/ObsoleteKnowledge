## summary

**James Bond**, codenamed **Agent 007** or simply **007**, is a fictional British Secret Intelligence Service agent created in 1953 by author *Ian Fleming*. Operating under a legendary license to kill, the suave operative became a defining figure of modern espionage fiction and the protagonist of one of history's most durable cinematic franchises. His exploits across print and cinema reflect shifting geopolitical anxieties from the peak of the *[[cold-war]]* to contemporary global counterterrorism.

## you gotta know

- Created by British naval intelligence veteran *Ian Fleming*, who introduced the character in the 1953 novel *Casino Royale* and named him after the American ornithologist *James Bond*.
- Serves as an operative in Britain's *MI6* under the secret moniker *007*, where the double-zero prefix denotes discretionary authorization to kill in the line of duty.
- Adapted into an iconic film series produced by *EON Productions*, beginning with the 1962 feature *Dr. No* starring Scottish actor *Sean Connery*.
- Portrayed in official cinematic installments by six principal actors across six decades: *Sean Connery*, *George Lazenby*, *Roger Moore*, *Timothy Dalton*, *Pierce Brosnan*, and *Daniel Craig*.
- Routinely aided by recurring departmental allies, including intelligence chief *M*, quartermaster gadget-inventor *Q*, administrative assistant *Miss Moneypenny*, and CIA ally *Felix Leiter*.
- Battles global criminal syndicates and megalomaniacal masterminds, most notably the transnational terrorist network *SPECTRE* headed by the recurring archenemy *Ernst Stavro Blofeld*.
- Characterized by enduring cultural tropes including a bespoke *Aston Martin DB5*, sophisticated wardrobe, lethal gadgets, and a signature vodka martini ordered shaken, not stirred.
- Confronted *SMERSH*, an operational Soviet counterintelligence bureau modeled directly on historical agencies of the *[[soviet-union]]*, across Fleming's earliest literary adventures.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — the ideological conflict that established the geopolitical backdrop, adversarial tensions, and espionage tactics of his earliest literary and cinematic missions.
- [[england]] — the sovereign home nation and headquarters of *MI6*, whose global intelligence interests and imperial prestige he defends.
- [[british-empire]] — the declining geopolitical apparatus whose post-imperial anxieties and global influence his international exploits romanticized.
- [[soviet-union]] — the primary state adversary opposing his agency throughout the formative espionage narratives of the twentieth century.
- [[france]] — the frequent continental European setting for Fleming's casino escapades, glamorous liaisons, and early mission assignments.
- [[film-editing]] — the technical cinematic craft utilized to pioneer dynamic pacing, action choreography, and suspense sequences across the long-running film franchise.
- [[camera-movement]] — the cinematic technique employed across iconic pre-title sequences, sweeping gun-barrel openings, and exotic vehicular pursuits.
