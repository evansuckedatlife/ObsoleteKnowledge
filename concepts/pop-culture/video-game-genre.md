## summary

**Video game genre** is the classification system for games based on gameplay mechanics, player objectives, and experiential focus. Like film genres (westerns, comedies, dramas), game genres organize player expectations and define what skills, strategies, and emotional responses a game will demand. Understanding genre is essential to understanding game culture, esports, and how games evolved as a medium.

## you gotta know

- *FPS (First-Person Shooter)* games prioritize combat, rapid reflexes, and aiming; they define a large portion of esports and gaming culture; [[counter-strike]], [[call-of-duty]], and [[halo]] are genre-defining titles that established the competitive standard for the medium.
- *RPGs (Role-Playing Games)* emphasize character progression, dialogue, narrative choice, and statistical systems inherited from tabletop games; they can be single-player epics ([[skyrim]]) or massive multiplayer worlds spanning years of play.
- *Strategy games* (turn-based or real-time) require resource management, long-term planning, and tactical thinking; they range from competitive esports titles like *StarCraft* to board-game adaptations that challenge players intellectually.
- *Action-adventure* games blend exploration, combat, puzzle-solving, and narrative; they are often single-player focused and emphasize immersion and cinematic presentation, creating seamless worlds.
- *Board game adaptations* in digital form ([[settlers-of-catan]], [[ticket-to-ride]]) translate physical game mechanics into digital spaces, preserving core rules while adding convenience and reaching new audiences.
- Genre boundaries are *porous*: games often blend genres (action-RPGs, tactical shooters); genre conventions shift as technology and player expectations evolve over time.
- *Esports* have formalized certain genres (FPS, MOBA, strategy) as competitive disciplines; genre choice affects whether a game can support competitive play at professional levels.

## connections

- [[first-person-shooter]] — a genre defined by perspective and mechanics; one of gaming's primary competitive forms.
- [[esports]] — competitive gaming relies on genre conventions; FPS and strategy games dominate the esports ecosystem.
- [[rpg]] — a genre emphasizing narrative and character progression; major driver of gaming's cultural significance and narrative complexity.
- [[game-design]] — genre conventions shape design choices; designers work within, modify, or subvert genre expectations.
- [[gameplay-mechanics]] — the foundation of genre; different genres prioritize different mechanics (aim in FPS, resource management in strategy).
- [[video-game-industry]] — industry success often depends on identifying genre trends and player preferences.
