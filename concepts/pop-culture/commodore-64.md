## summary

The **Commodore 64** is an iconic 8-bit home computer introduced by Commodore International in January 1982 at the Consumer Electronics Show. Celebrated by the Guinness World Records as the single highest-selling desktop computer model of all time, it democratized personal computing across the globe throughout the 1980s. Its remarkable graphical and musical capabilities—particularly driven by its revolutionary sound synthesizer chip—fostered an explosive grassroots subculture of bedroom programmers, digital musicians, and video game developers.

## you gotta know

- Introduced in January 1982 by *Jack Tramiel*, dominating the home computing market through aggressive pricing and sales in discount department stores.
- Holds the Guinness World Record for the single best-selling desktop computer model, with total sales estimated between twelve and seventeen million units.
- Powered by the *MOS Technology 6510* microprocessor running at approximately one megahertz and equipped with a standard sixty-four kilobytes of RAM.
- Revolutionized electronic music via the *MOS 6581/8580 Sound Interface Device* (SID chip), enabling sophisticated three-channel synthesized audio.
- Enabled vibrant hardware sprite graphics and scrolling backgrounds via the *VIC-II* display chip, popularizing high-speed domestic action gaming.
- Boasted an extensive software library exceeding ten thousand commercial titles, cementing its status as an early gaming and demoscene juggernaut.
- Sparked early software piracy and hacking subcultures through ubiquitous cassette tape and *Commodore 1541* 5.25-inch floppy disk drives.
