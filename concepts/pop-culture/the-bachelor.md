## summary

**The Bachelor** is an American reality television dating game show that debuted in March 2002 on the ABC network, created by television producer *Mike Fleiss*. The program centers on a single eligible bachelor who considers a pool of romantic interests, eliminating candidates over the course of a season through a structured series of group dates, one-on-one outings, and ceremonial eliminations. As a cornerstone of modern broadcast reality programming in the [[united-states]], the show established genre conventions that reshaped competitive romance television and spawned an expansive media franchise.

## you gotta know

- Features a single male lead presented with roughly twenty-five to thirty female contestants competing for his affection, culminating in a prospective marriage proposal.
- Uses the iconic "rose ceremony" as its primary elimination mechanism, wherein contestants accepted by the lead receive a red rose while unselected suitors are sent home.
- Concludes key intermediate stages with "hometown dates," where the lead travels across the country to meet the families of the final four contestants.
- Introduces overnight "fantasy suites" during the penultimate round in exotic resort destinations, offering unfilmed private time for the final three contestants.
- Hosted for its first twenty-five seasons by *Chris Harrison*, whose signature sign-off declared each finale "the most dramatic rose ceremony ever."
- Sparked numerous successful spin-offs including *The Bachelorette*, *Bachelor in Paradise*, and *The Golden Bachelor*.
- Faced frequent cultural criticism regarding formulaic emotional manipulation, staged drama, and long-standing controversies surrounding ethnic diversity and racial representation.
- Premiered with businessman *Alex Michel* as its inaugural bachelor in 2002, who chose *Amanda Marsh* but did not propose.
