## summary

**Back to the Future** is a landmark 1985 American science-fiction comedy film directed by *Robert Zemeckis* and co-written with *Bob Gale*. Starring *Michael J. Fox* as teenager *Marty McFly* and *Christopher Lloyd* as eccentric scientist *Doc Brown*, the narrative chronicles Marty's accidental journey thirty years into the past via a plutonium-powered DeLorean sports car. Its ingenious screenplay, kinetic pacing, and resonant celebration of mid-century Americana established it as the highest-grossing film of 1985 and an enduring touchstone of modern cinema.

## you gotta know

- Marty McFly travels from 1985 back to 1955 in a modified *DeLorean DMC-12* sports car that requires 1.21 gigawatts of electrical power and a velocity of 88 miles per hour to engage the flux capacitor.
- Marty must engineer the romance of his teenage parents, *George McFly* (*Crispin Glover*) and *Lorraine Baines* (*Lea Thompson*), at the Enchantment Under the Sea dance after inadvertently disrupting their original meeting.
- *Eric Stoltz* was originally cast as Marty McFly and filmed for roughly five weeks before *Robert Zemeckis* and executive producer *Steven Spielberg* chose to recast the role with *Michael J. Fox*, reshooting his scenes at night while Fox filmed the sitcom *Family Ties*.
- The film produced the chart-topping hit single "The Power of Love" by *Huey Lewis and the News*, who makes a humorous cameo appearance as a high school band audition judge declaring Marty's band "too darn loud."
- The narrative climax hinges on channeling a lightning strike from the Hill Valley Courthouse clock tower at precisely 10:04 p.m. on November 12, 1955, back into the DeLorean.
- Marty introduces modern rock music to a bewildered 1955 prom audience by performing *Chuck Berry*'s classic "Johnny B. Goode," prompting bandleader *Marvin Berry* to place an urgent long-distance phone call to his cousin Chuck.
- Early versions of the screenplay depicted the time machine as a converted household refrigerator, an idea discarded over safety concerns that children might imitate the film by trapping themselves inside appliances.
