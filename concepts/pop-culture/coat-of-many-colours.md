## summary

The **Coat of Many Colours** is a legendary garment originating in the biblical narrative of Genesis, given by the patriarch Jacob to his favored son Joseph as a symbol of paternal devotion. Inspiring millennia of adaptation, it became the centerpiece of fraternal betrayal when Joseph's jealous brothers stripped him of the tunic, sold him into slavery, and dipped it in goat's blood. In modern popular culture, the garment gave rise both to Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice's hit musical *Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat* and to Dolly Parton's autobiographical country classic.

## you gotta know

- Originates in the *Book of Genesis* as an ornate tunic given to *Joseph* by his father *Jacob*, signifying his status as the preferred and favored heir.
- Provokes intense jealousy among Joseph's eleven brothers, who conspire against him, cast him into a pit, and sell him into Egyptian slavery.
- Stained with goat's blood by Joseph's brothers to deceive their grieving father into believing a wild beast had devoured his favorite child.
- Translated historically from the Hebrew term *ketonet passim*, which scholars debate as signifying either multicolored fabric, long sleeves, or ornate embroidery.
- Inspired the wildly popular 1968 stage musical *Joseph and the Amazing Technicolor Dreamcoat*, composed by *Andrew Lloyd Webber* with lyrics by *Tim Rice*.
- Adapted into *Dolly Parton*'s celebrated 1971 autobiographical country song recounting a patchwork coat sewn from rags by her mother in the Smoky Mountains.
- Functions broadly across art and literature as an enduring cultural archetype for parental love, unearned sibling rivalry, and personal resilience.
