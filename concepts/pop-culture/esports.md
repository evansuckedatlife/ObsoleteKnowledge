## summary

**Esports** encompasses competitive video gaming organized at professional, televised, and international scales. Emerging from arcade tournaments and LAN parties in the 1990s, esports evolved from underground subculture into a billion-dollar industry with franchised teams, stadium events, and global audiences. The legitimacy and infrastructure of esports now rivals traditional sports in organizational sophistication while retaining gaming's accessibility and digital-native communities.

## you gotta know

- Origins trace to early 1980s arcade competitions (*Donkey Kong*, *Pac-Man*), but modern esports infrastructure crystallized with *StarCraft* in South Korea during the early 2000s, where pro gamers became televised celebrities.
- *Counter-Strike* became foundational to Western esports legitimacy; its tactical depth, accessibility, and competitive purity made it the first game to sustain a truly global pro circuit across decades.
- Multiplayer Online Battle Arena (MOBA) genre—particularly *League of Legends* and *Dota 2*—scaled esports into stadiums; the annual *League of Legends World Championship* rivals traditional sports events in viewership and prize pools.
- Franchise model (borrowed from traditional sports) replaced open-circuit tournaments; teams like T1, Fnatic, and Team Liquid command sponsorship, salaries, and broadcast rights rivaling major sports leagues.
- Twitch (founded 2011) and YouTube Gaming transformed esports infrastructure by making live viewership and community interaction central to the medium itself, not ancillary to competition.
- Prize pools have grown exponentially: *Dota 2*'s International ($30M+ annually) and *Fortnite* tournaments demonstrate esports' capacity to attract venture-scale investment and mainstream corporate sponsorship.
- Youth participation and skill ceiling remain esports' defining feature; unlike traditional sports, reaction time and mechanical precision peak in the late teens and early twenties, creating shorter professional careers but lower barriers to entry.

## connections

- [[first-person-shooter]] — the genre that proved esports viability through *Counter-Strike*'s decade-spanning competitive dominance.
- [[counter-strike]] — foundational esports title whose competitive purity and tactical depth established the genre's standards.
- [[call-of-duty]] — mainstream console shooter that brought esports into franchised, televised formats via the Call of Duty League.
- [[streaming-culture]] — esports and streaming are interdependent; Twitch monetization and parasocial communities between viewers and pro players drove esports growth.
- [[korea]] — *StarCraft* and *League of Legends* made South Korea the esports superpower; Korean teams and players dominate most major international competitions.
- [[video-game-industry]] — esports represents a new revenue stream (media rights, sponsorship, streaming) beyond game sales, reshaping industry business models.
- [[skill-based-competition]] — esports legitimacy depends on demonstrable, reproducible skill; the medium's competitive purity attracts athletes and meritocratic viewers.
