## summary

**Clark Gable** was a legendary American film actor widely crowned as the "King of Hollywood" during the height of the studio era. Possessing rugged masculinity, effortless charisma, and a rakish nonchalance, Gable redefined romantic leading men from polished aristocrats into pragmatic, virile common men. He achieved enduring cinematic immortality through his Oscar-winning performance in *It Happened One Night* and his iconic portrayal of the cynical blockade-runner Rhett Butler in *Gone with the Wind*.

## you gotta know

- Earned the Academy Award for Best Actor for director [[frank-capra]]'s 1934 masterpiece *It Happened One Night*, establishing the template for the fast-talking screwball leading man.
- Popularized a vivid on-screen dynamic rooted in [[class-conflict]] alongside Claudette Colbert, famously triggering an urban legend that his bare chest in the film caused undershirt sales to plummet.
- Immortally embodied the roguish, pragmatic smuggler Rhett Butler in the 1939 epic *Gone with the Wind*, delivering cinema's most famous parting retort opposite Vivien Leigh.
- Defined Hollywood's depiction of the tumultuous [[american-civil-war]] and subsequent [[reconstruction-era]], portraying Southern society's collapse through cynical detachment and romantic fervor.
- Began his theatrical career in stage productions and uncredited roles in the [[silent-film]] era before rising to contract stardom at Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer.
- Enlisted in the U.S. Army Air Forces during [[world-war-ii]] following the tragic death of his wife Carole Lombard, flying combat missions over Europe as an aerial gunner.
- Concluded his storied screen career opposite Marilyn Monroe and Montgomery Clift in the 1961 western drama *The Misfits*, directed by John Huston.

## connections

- [[class-conflict]] — the overarching social dynamic Gable mastered on screen, frequently portraying rugged working men who puncture aristocratic pretension.
- [[frank-capra]] — the director who steered Gable to his sole Academy Award and unlocked his signature blend of swagger and comedic vulnerability.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the historical conflict serving as the sweeping canvas for Gable's defining performance in *Gone with the Wind*.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — the postwar historical backdrop against which Rhett Butler navigates social upheaval, political opportunism, and personal tragedy.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict during which Gable interrupted his peak Hollywood stardom to fly active combat missions in Europe.
- [[silent-film]] — the early medium in which Gable labored as an extra and bit player before sound technology elevated his distinctive, rugged baritone.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose mid-century masculine ideals, populist impulses, and cinematic dominance Gable came to embody.
