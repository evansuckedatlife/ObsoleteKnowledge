## summary

**Avonlea** is the fictional rural community on Prince Edward Island, Canada, that serves as the primary setting for *Lucy Maud Montgomery*'s beloved 1908 novel *Anne of Green Gables* and several of its sequels. Based heavily on the real agricultural hamlet of Cavendish, where Montgomery spent her childhood, the village embodies an idealized pastoral idyll of close-knit maritime village life at the turn of the twentieth century. It remains one of the most famous literary locations in North American fiction, inspiring tourist destinations, stage productions, and international television adaptations.

## you gotta know

- Created by author *Lucy Maud Montgomery* as the home of the imaginative, red-headed orphan *Anne Shirley*, who is adopted by the aging siblings *Matthew* and *Marilla Cuthbert*.
- Modeled directly on the real-world settlement of *Cavendish, Prince Edward Island*, incorporating authentic Prince Edward Island topography such as red dirt roads, sandstone cliffs, and apple orchards.
- Features landmark locations within the story including *Green Gables* farmstead, the *Haunted Wood*, *Lover's Lane*, and the *Lake of Shining Waters* (named by Anne in place of Barry's Pond).
- Serves as the central setting for several Montgomery works beyond the debut novel, including *Anne of Avonlea* (1909), *Chronicles of Avonlea* (1912), and *Further Chronicles of Avonlea* (1920).
- Served as the focal world for Kevin Sullivan's internationally syndicated television series *Road to Avonlea* (titled *Avonlea* in Canada), which ran from 1990 to 1996.
- Preserved today in Parks Canada's *Green Gables Heritage Place*, attracting hundreds of thousands of literary pilgrims worldwide, particularly from Japan and North America.
