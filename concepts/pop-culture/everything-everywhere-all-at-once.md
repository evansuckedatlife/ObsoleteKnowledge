## summary

***Everything Everywhere All at Once*** (commonly abbreviated as **EEAAO**) is a 2022 absurdist science-fiction comedy-drama written and directed by the filmmaking duo *Daniel Kwan* and *Daniel Scheinert*, known as the *Daniels*. The narrative follows an overwhelmed laundromat owner who must traverse parallel dimensions and tap into alternate versions of herself to halt a reality-destroying entity. Combining kinetic martial arts, family melodrama, and philosophical depth, the film was a massive critical and box-office triumph that captured seven Academy Awards including Best Picture.

## you gotta know

- Dominated the 95th Academy Awards by winning seven Oscars, sweeping Best Picture, Best Director, Best Original Screenplay, Best Film Editing for *Paul Rogers*, Best Supporting Actor for *Ke Huy Quan*, Best Supporting Actress for *Jamie Lee Curtis*, and Best Actress for *Michelle Yeoh*.
- Made cinema history with *Michelle Yeoh*'s win as the first openly Asian performer to receive the Academy Award for Best Actress, honoring her multidimensional performance as the stressed immigrant protagonist *Evelyn Wang*.
- Orchestrated a celebrated Hollywood return for *Ke Huy Quan*, the former child star of *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom*, who won Best Supporting Actor for portraying the gentle, fanny-pack-fighting husband *Waymond Wang*.
- Centers on an existential battle against *Jobu Tupaki*, the nihilistic omniverse persona of Evelyn's daughter *Joy Wang* (played by *Stephanie Hsu*), who creates an "Everything Bagel" vortex designed to extinguish all existence.
- Relies on lightning-fast [[film-editing]] by *Paul Rogers*, who juxtaposed disparate timelines and parallel lives in microsecond bursts, evoking the fragmented, sensory-overloaded feeling of the modern internet.
- Features celebrated surreal gags that double as emotional climaxes, such as a universe where humans have hot dogs for fingers and a dialogue between two silent, sentient rocks perched above a desert canyon.
- Spotlights avant-garde costume styling by *Shirley Kurata*, whose runway-inspired ensembles for Jobu Tupaki satirize high-fashion aesthetics and subvert the traditional [[evening-gown]] through anarchic, surreal couture.

## connections

- [[film-editing]] — the kinetic montage technique assembled by editor Paul Rogers to convey simultaneous parallel lives without losing emotional clarity.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — the deliberate visual composition across cramped laundromats, bureaucratic tax offices, and stylized multiverse tableaus.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — the use of concrete symbols like the dark Everything Bagel and self-adhesive googly eyes to embody existential void versus compassionate presence.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical framework that drives the characters from nihilistic apathy toward an affirmative embrace of mundane human connection.
- [[evening-gown]] — the formal runway couture transformed into absurd villain outfits in the film and worn by the cast across their historic award-season victories.
- [[falling-into-you]] — a fellow cultural phenomenon that swept industry awards through unapologetic emotional vulnerability and maximalist execution.
- [[exotica]] — an artistic ancestor in its pastiche methodology, interweaving disparate cultural fragments into an overwhelming sensory experience.
- [[united-states]] — the country where the immigrant generational narrative is set, reflecting the Chinese-American family dynamic.
