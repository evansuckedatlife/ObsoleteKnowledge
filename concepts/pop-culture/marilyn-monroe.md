## summary

**Marilyn Monroe**, born **Norma Jeane Mortenson** (later baptized **Norma Jeane Baker**), was a mid-twentieth-century actress, model, and singer who became the quintessential blonde bombshell of Golden Age Hollywood and a defining cultural icon of the post-war [[united-states]]. Renowned for her radiant screen presence and comedic timing in landmark films such as *Gentlemen Prefer Blondes* and *Some Like It Hot*, she transcended cinema to embody the anxieties, glamour, and contradictions of the [[cold-war]] era. Her tragic death in 1962 crystallized her image into an enduring emblem of celebrity, ultimately serving as the central muse for [[pop-art]] and the mass-media critique of high [[modernism]].

## you gotta know

- Achieved global renown as Hollywood's archetypal blonde bombshell, immortalized by her billowing white dress over a subway grate in *The Seven Year Itch* and her breathy performance of *Happy Birthday, Mr. President* for *John F. Kennedy* at Madison Square Garden.
- Won a Golden Globe Award for her celebrated performance as Sugar Kane Kowalczyk in [[billy-wilder|Billy Wilder's]] comedy *Some Like It Hot*, demonstrating mastery of [[slapstick-comedy]] and tender vulnerability alongside [[tony-curtis|Tony Curtis]] and [[jack-lemmon|Jack Lemmon]].
- Served as the definitive subject for *Andy Warhol*, whose silkscreened *Marilyn Diptych* transformed her publicity portrait from *Niagara* into a landmark of [[pop-art]] interrogating mass [[consumerism]], reproducibility, and mortality.
- Captured worldwide media fascination through high-profile marriages to baseball legend *Joe DiMaggio* and celebrated playwright *Arthur Miller*, fusing athletic celebrity and intellectual prestige under intense public scrutiny.
- Rebelled against studio contract constraints by co-founding Marilyn Monroe Productions in 1954 to gain artistic control, while simultaneously studying Method acting under *Lee Strasberg* at the Actors Studio in New York.
- Undertook a historic 1954 USO tour in Korea to perform for more than one hundred thousand American soldiers, underscoring the vital soft-power role of Hollywood celebrity during the [[cold-war]].
- Showcased dramatic depth within the [[western-film]] genre, co-starring in *River of No Return* and giving her final completed screen performance opposite *Clark Gable* in *John Huston's* revisionist drama *The Misfits*.
- Began her career after being discovered by photographer *David Conover* while spraying airplane parts and inspecting parachutes at the Radioplane Company munitions factory during [[world-war-ii]].

## connections

- [[pop-art]] — immortalized her image through silkscreen printing to critique mass production and celebrity culture.
- [[billy-wilder]] — directed two of her most celebrated comedic achievements, *The Seven Year Itch* and *Some Like It Hot*.
- [[cold-war]] — the historical era whose societal tensions, consumer expansion, and military conflicts shaped her career.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose mid-century studio system and mass media propelled her to global stardom.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — the genre whose timing, physical precision, and comedic tropes elevated her finest screen roles.
- [[western-film]] — the traditional cinematic genre she subverted in *River of No Return* and her final film, *The Misfits*.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — the deliberate aesthetic orchestration of lighting, makeup, and framing that established her screen persona.
- [[modernism]] — the broader cultural and artistic movement that engaged with her commodification and mythic status.
