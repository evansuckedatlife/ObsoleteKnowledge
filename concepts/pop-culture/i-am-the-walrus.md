## summary

**I Am the Walrus** is an avant-garde psychedelic rock song recorded by the English rock band *The Beatles*, written primarily by *John Lennon* and credited to the Lennon–McCartney songwriting partnership. Released in November 1967 as the B-side to the chart-topping single *Hello, Goodbye* and featured in the television film and soundtrack album *Magical Mystery Tour*, the track is celebrated for its dense, surrealist wordplay, unorthodox studio production, and innovative orchestral accompaniment arranged by *George Martin*. It remains an enduring touchstone of 1960s countercultural art and modern experimental popular music.

## you gotta know

- Composed by *John Lennon* after learning that an English teacher at his alma mater, *Quarry Bank High School*, had assigned students to analyze Beatles song lyrics in class.
- Deliberately constructed using nonsense rhymes, absurd juxtaposition, and surreal imagery to baffle overzealous literary critics and academic commentators.
- Takes its eponymous marine mammal title from *Lewis Carroll*'s poem *The Walrus and the Carpenter*, featured in *Through the Looking-Glass*, though Lennon later noted with irony that the walrus was the villain of the poem.
- Arranged and produced by *George Martin*, featuring an audacious score for sixteen orchestral strings and brass alongside guest choral chanting from the *Mike Sammes Singers*.
- Incorporates a live BBC Third Programme radio broadcast of *The Tragedy of King Lear* by [[william-shakespeare]] directly into the final mix, capturing lines spoken by *Oswald* and *Edgar*.
- Opening with a distinctive descending electric piano riff, the song opens with the iconic enigmatic declaration: *"I am he as you are he as you are me and we are all together."*
- Banned from British radio broadcast by the BBC due to the inclusion of the playful, cheeky line *"semolina pilchard climbing up the Eiffel Tower"* and the colloquial mention of *"knickers"*.

## connections

- [[england]] — the nation where the track was written, recorded at London's *Abbey Road Studios*, and originally broadcast.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the English playwright whose tragedy *King Lear* was captured over the airwaves and collaged into the song's outro.
- [[modernism]] — the twentieth-century artistic and literary movement whose techniques of pastiche, montage, and surrealist poetry heavily influenced Lennon's lyricism.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical framework whose themes of absurdity and meaninglessness resonated throughout late-1960s countercultural art and lyrics.
- [[british-empire]] — the historic global order that laid the cultural and linguistic foundation for the international explosion of the *British Invasion*.
- [[cold-war]] — the tense mid-twentieth-century geopolitical era that provided the societal backdrop for the rise of 1960s psychedelic youth counterculture.
