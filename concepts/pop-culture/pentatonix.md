## summary

**Pentatonix**, frequently abbreviated as **PTX**, is an American a cappella vocal ensemble formed in Arlington, Texas, in 2011. Comprising five vocalists known for intricate harmonies, rhythmic vocal percussion, and deep basslines, the group revolutionized twenty-first-century vocal music by propelling contemporary a cappella into mainstream commercial pop in the [[united-states]]. Renowned for their viral online arrangements, multi-platinum holiday albums, and multiple Grammy Awards, the quintet demonstrated that unaccompanied human voices could rival modern synthesized production.

## you gotta know

- Rose to national prominence after winning the third season of the NBC vocal competition series *The Sing-Off* in 2011, performing an arrangement of "Eye of the Tiger" in the finale.
- Derived their ensemble name from the pentatonic scale, a musical scale containing five distinct pitches per octave, reflecting the five founding vocalists.
- Originally formed by high school friends *Scott Hoying*, *Mitch Grassi*, and *Kirstin Maldonado*, who recruited bass vocalist *Avi Kaplan* and beatboxer *Kevin Olusola* shortly before auditioning for television.
- Gained immense international popularity via YouTube after being dropped by their initial record label, producing viral sensation medleys such as their tribute to electronic duo *Daft Punk*.
- Won three consecutive Grammy Awards between 2015 and 2017, becoming the first predominantly a cappella act to win in categories beyond religious or specialized choral music.
- Collaborated with country music icon *Dolly Parton* on a re-imagined recording of her signature song "Jolene," earning the 2017 Grammy Award for Best Country Duo/Group Performance.
- Underwent their first major personnel change in 2017 when founding bass *Avi Kaplan* departed due to the demands of touring, subsequently replaced by *Matt Sallee*.
- Produced an acclaimed a cappella rendition of "The Sound of Silence," composed by American singer-songwriter [[paul-simon]], showcasing their dynamic vocal range and orchestral vocal layering.
