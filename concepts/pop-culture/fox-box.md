## summary

**Fox Box**, originally styled as **The Fox Box** and later rebranded as **4Kids TV**, was an influential American terrestrial Saturday morning children's programming block that broadcast on the Fox Broadcasting Company from September 2002 until December 2008. Programmed and leased by 4Kids Entertainment to succeed the long-running *Fox Kids* block, it became a cultural hub for early-2000s youth entertainment through its distinct lineup of imported anime, licensed video game adaptations, and original domestic animation. The block is remembered for shaping millennial and Generation Z pop culture with iconic theme songs, aggressive promotional branding, and controversial localization practices.

## you gotta know

- Debuted on September 14, 2002, replacing *Fox Kids* after 4Kids Entertainment, led by executive *Alfred R. Kahn*, secured a multi-year time-lease agreement with the Fox network to control Saturday morning programming.
- Promoted an energetic identity centered on the Fox Box mascot, a mischievous talking yellow cube named *Boxy*, accompanied by rapid-fire interstitials, interactive online contests, and edgy youth branding.
- Served as the primary American broadcast home for landmark Japanese anime series imported from *[[japan]]*, notably *Sonic X*, *Kirby: Right Back at Ya!*, *Shaman King*, *Fighting Foodons*, *Tokyo Mew Mew* (localized as *Mew Mew Power*), and *One Piece*.
- Produced and launched the critically acclaimed 2003 reboot of *Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles*, which returned to the gritty, serialized tone of the original Mirage Comics while retaining kid-friendly appeal.
- Gained notoriety for heavy 4Kids localization practices and broadcast standards censorship, such as digitally editing out firearms, cigarette smoking (famously replacing Sanji's cigarette with a lollipop in *One Piece*), religious iconography, and Japanese text.
- Rebranded in January 2005 as *4Kids TV* to strengthen brand synergy with the company's licensed toy lines, trading cards, and video game merchandise.
- Coexisted in the fiercely competitive Saturday morning broadcast environment alongside contemporary children's properties like *[[franklin-the-turtle]]*, which aired in syndication on rival public and commercial airwaves.
- Ended operations on December 27, 2008, following contractual payment disputes between 4Kids Entertainment and Fox, marking one of the final chapters in the historic era of broadcast network Saturday morning cartoons.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — country of broadcast where Fox affiliates delivered the morning block into millions of terrestrial television households.
- [[japan]] — source country for the block's most successful animated imports, which introduced a generation of young viewers to Japanese animation and gaming franchises.
- [[franklin-the-turtle]] — fellow cornerstone of 2000s North American children's television that offered a calm, moralizing alternative to Fox Box's action-heavy commercial block.
- [[esports]] — modern gaming landscape heavily populated by competitive titles starring franchises popularized across Fox Box weekend broadcasts, including Sonic the Hedgehog.
- [[first-person-shooter]] — adult gaming genre that television broadcast censors actively contrasted against by strictly policing simulated gun violence and ballistic weapons within morning animations.
