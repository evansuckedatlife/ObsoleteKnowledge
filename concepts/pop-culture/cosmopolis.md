## summary

**Cosmopolis** is a 2003 postmodern novel by acclaimed American author *Don DeLillo*, published by Scribner. Unfolding across a single April day at the dawn of the new millennium, the story tracks twenty-eight-year-old multi-billionaire asset manager *Eric Packer* as he crawls across Manhattan in a custom, soundproofed stretch limousine to get a haircut. Through sharp, stylized dialogue and apocalyptic imagery, the book delivers a prophetic examination of cyber-capitalism, high-frequency currency trading, and psychological alienation in contemporary [[american-literature]].

## you gotta know

- The protagonist, *Eric Packer*, conducts his business, medical checkups, and philosophical debates almost entirely inside a heavily customized white limousine lined with cork and packed with glowing surveillance monitors.
- Packer's slow journey across 47th Street in [[new-york-city]] is continually paralyzed by gridlock caused by a presidential motorcade, an anarchic anti-globalization protest, and the public funeral of a Sufi rap star.
- As the day advances, Packer wagers his vast personal fortune and clients' assets against the value of the Japanese yen, obstinately defying market realities because the currency's rise violates his theoretical models.
- The journey includes intermittent, detached romantic meetings with his aristocratic poet wife, *Elise Shifrin*, whom he encounters by chance at bookstores and diners along his route.
- Packer's chief of theory, *Vija Kinski*, delivers lengthy disquisitions on how cyber-capitalism has dematerialized time, turning finance into an abstract force disconnected from physical reality.
- Desensitized by wealth and absolute digital control, Packer pursues escalating physical risks, ultimately murdering his own bodyguard and deliberately seeking out a mortal adversary.
- The narrative ends in an abandoned warehouse where Packer confronts *Benno Levin*, a destitute and resentful former employee who has stalked him with the intent to kill him.
- Adapted into a 2012 feature film directed by Canadian auteur *David Cronenberg*, starring *Robert Pattinson* as Packer, which faithfully preserves DeLillo's austere, aphoristic dialogue.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — forms a key work in DeLillo's celebrated bibliography of late-capitalist and media-saturated American fiction.
- [[new-york-city]] — provides the dense, claustrophobic urban grid that imprisons the protagonist's luxury vehicle.
- [[modernism]] — adopts the modernist single-day urban odyssey structure, echoing *James Joyce's* *Ulysses*.
- [[existentialism]] — permeates Packer's search for physical sensation, self-destruction, and meaning amidst electronic sterility.
- [[cold-war]] — represents the historical transition from geopolitical standoff to the frictionless unipolar era of global speculative finance.
- [[corduroy]] — presents a stark thematic contrast between innocent mid-century urban consumerism and predatory twenty-first-century financial abstractions in [[new-york-city]].
- [[court-and-spark]] — shares a preoccupation with the isolating, hollow effects of immense financial privilege and artistic detachment.
