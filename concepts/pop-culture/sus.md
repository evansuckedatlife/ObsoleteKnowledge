## summary

**sus** is an informal slang clipping and colloquial adjective derived from the word *suspicious*, used to describe someone or something that appears untrustworthy, deceptive, or dubious. While originating decades earlier in regional dialects across the English-speaking world, the term achieved explosive global adoption during 2020 through internet culture and online multiplayer gaming. It stands as a prime modern case study in internet [[linguistics]], demonstrating how digital subcultures accelerate lexical diffusion into the international vernacular.

## you gotta know

- Surged to ubiquitous global popularity in mid-2020 due to the immense viral success of the social deduction video game *Among Us*, where players used it in in-game text chat to accuse potential impostors of covert sabotage.
- Derived historically as a colloquial clipping of *suspicious* or *suspect*, with documented usage in mid-twentieth-century British, Australian, and American police and underworld parlance (such as the contentious *sus laws* in [[england]]).
- Incorporated into African American Vernacular English (AAVE) in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries before becoming staple youth slang throughout the [[united-states]].
- Spawned the phrase *when the imposter is sus*, an inescapable internet meme frequently paired with grotesque photomontages and distorted soundbites across social media platforms like TikTok, Reddit, and Twitch.
- Functions flexibly within internet grammar as an adjective ("that play looks sus"), a noun phrase designating suspicion, or an active verb signifying accusation during group play in [[esports]] and streaming contexts.
- Named by multiple lexicographical bodies and dictionaries, including Merriam-Webster and Oxford, as one of the definitive words capturing youth digital communication during the COVID-19 pandemic era.
