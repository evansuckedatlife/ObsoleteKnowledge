## summary

**board game inclusivity** is the deliberate cultural and industrial movement within tabletop game design, manufacturing, and community organizing to make hobby gaming welcoming and accessible to people of all backgrounds, identities, and physical abilities. Originating as a grassroots reaction against the historical dominance of niche hobbyist spaces by male players in *[[germany]]* and the *[[united-states]]*, the movement seeks to dismantle systemic barriers across both game mechanics and industry gatekeeping. By prioritizing accessible rule design, diverse artistic representation, colorblind-friendly visual components, and welcoming non-combative themes, it has helped broaden modern tabletop gaming into a global mainstream hobby.

## you gotta know

- Epitomized by the critical and commercial triumph of *Wingspan*, designed by *Elizabeth Hargrave*, which demonstrated massive market demand for peaceful, nature-focused, and educational themes rather than traditional combat, fantasy violence, or aggressive domination.
- Prompted critical re-evaluations of beloved Eurogame classics such as *[[settlers-of-catan]]* and *Puerto Rico* for uncritically celebrating colonialism, land seizure, and historical resource exploitation while erasing indigenous populations.
- Catalyzed the design of anti-colonial gameplay experiences, notably *Spirit Island* by *R. Eric Reuss*, which intentionally subverts European expansionist tropes by casting players as elemental spirits defending an island against colonial invaders.
- Established essential component accessibility standards for color vision deficiency (*CVD*), requiring publishers to utilize unique silhouettes, iconography, and distinct patterns alongside color palettes so colorblind players can easily identify pieces.
- Promoted inclusive language standards in rulebook writing, moving away from the default generic masculine pronoun "he" in favor of the singular "they" or alternating gendered pronouns across gameplay examples.
- Expanded the demographic reach of hobby gaming through welcoming "gateway games" like *[[ticket-to-ride]]* and modular card engines like *[[dominion]]*, which emphasize low barrier-to-entry mechanics over intimidating complexity.
- Spurred the creation of industry mentorship initiatives like the *Tabletop Mentorship Program* and dedicated diversity conventions aimed at elevating underrepresented women, BIPOC, LGBTQ+, and disabled game designers and artists.
- Challenged exclusionary behavior in local game stores and board game conventions by establishing explicit codes of conduct and fostering welcoming community spaces for marginalized tabletop players.

## connections

- [[settlers-of-catan]] — represented the foundational modern German board game whose colonial expansionist theme became a key focal point of thematic critique.
- [[agricola]] — illustrated the Eurogame genre's tradition of non-violent, agricultural worker placement that paved the way for diverse engine-building titles.
- [[ticket-to-ride]] — provided a gentle, universally accessible route-building game that welcomed cross-generational families and diverse casual players.
- [[dominion]] — popularized deck-building card games with modular mechanics that lowered intimidation factors for novice tabletop hobbyists.
- [[germany]] — served as the cradle of the Eurogame design philosophy, which historically prioritized family-friendly play and hidden scoring over player elimination.
- [[united-states]] — serves as the primary epicenter for modern crowdfunding campaigns, gaming conventions, and diversity advocacy organizations within the tabletop industry.
