## summary

**Populism** is a political and cultural impulse built on faith in "the people" and distrust of elites—institutions, experts, and the powerful. In pop-culture, populism appears as a recurring narrative and ideological force: the ordinary person against the corrupt powerful, the outsider against the establishment. Populist themes run through American cinema, television, and literature, shaping heroes, villains, and the fundamental structure of dramatic conflict.

## you gotta know

- Historical populism emerged in late 19th-century America as a political movement against industrial elites, big banks, and monopolies; *William Jennings Bryan's* "Cross of Gold" speech (1896) became its iconic moment.
- In pop-culture, populism is a *narrative energy*: the underdog, the small-town hero, the working person triumphing through merit and honesty against corruption and aristocratic pretension.
- *Frank Capra* became populism's primary filmmaker; his films *consistently* dramatize the conflict between ordinary democratic virtue and institutional evil; the people are good, the powerful are corrupt.
- The *everyman-hero* is populism's protagonist: not aristocratic or exceptional, but noble through integrity and common sense; *James Stewart* embodied this archetype in Capra's hands.
- Populism's *anti-establishment* thrust manifests as skepticism of government, large institutions, and expert authority; democratic faith is vested in the masses, not in governance.
- The paradox of populism: it claims to speak for "the people" but often requires a charismatic leader or artist (Capra, Bryan) to articulate what "the people" think.
- Populism in pop-culture often conflates wealth with moral corruption and poverty with virtue; class antagonism is the deep plot.

## connections

- [[frank-capra]] — the filmmaker most committed to populist themes; his entire oeuvre dramatizes the triumph of ordinary virtue over institutional corruption.
- [[cross-of-gold]] — the historical moment that defined American populism; Bryan's speech shaped the rhetoric and imagery of populist movements.
- [[everyman-hero]] — populism's narrative protagonist; the ordinary person revealed to be morally superior to the powerful.
- [[william-jennings-bryan]] — the political figure most associated with 19th-century American populism; his rhetoric and ideology influenced Capra's films.
- [[american-cinema]] — populist themes are foundational to American film's narrative imagination and moral structure.
- [[democracy]] — populism claims to speak for democratic values against anti-democratic elites.
