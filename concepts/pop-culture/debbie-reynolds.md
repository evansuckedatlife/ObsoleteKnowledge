## summary

**Debbie Reynolds** was an American actress, singer, dancer, and film historian whose effervescent charm made her one of the definitive musical ingénues of Hollywood's Golden Age. She achieved immediate international stardom at age nineteen playing *Kathy Selden* opposite *Gene Kelly* in the landmark musical *Singin' in the Rain* (1952). Beyond her screen work, she was renowned for her energetic cabaret performances, an Academy Award-nominated turn in *The Unsinkable Molly Brown* (1964), and her tireless dedication to preserving Hollywood memorabilia.

## you gotta know

- Breakthrough role came as *Kathy Selden* in the 1952 musical classic *Singin' in the Rain*, where she famously held her own against demanding co-stars *Gene Kelly* and *Donald O'Connor* despite having no prior dance training.
- Earned her sole Academy Award nomination for Best Actress portraying the eponymous survivor of the *Titanic* in *The Unsinkable Molly Brown* (1964).
- Scored a number-one pop hit in 1957 with the ballad *Tammy* from the romantic comedy *Tammy and the Bachelor*, which remained a signature song throughout her musical career.
- The mother of actress and author *Carrie Fisher* and producer *Todd Fisher*, Reynolds died of a stroke in December 2016 just one day after Fisher's death, an event chronicled in the documentary *Bright Lights: Starring Carrie Fisher and Debbie Reynolds*.
- A passionate film preservationist, she amassed one of the world's largest private collections of classic Hollywood costumes and props, including *Marilyn Monroe*'s white subway dress and *Judy Garland*'s ruby slippers from *The Wizard of Oz*, before auctioning the collection in 2011.
- Won the Miss Burbank beauty contest in 1948 at age sixteen, which directly led to her talent contract with Warner Bros. and later Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer.
- Voiced the title spider in the beloved 1973 animated musical adaptation of *Charlotte's Web* and starred in Disney Channel's *Halloweentown* franchise as the witch *Aggie Cromwell*.
- Received the Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences in 2015 for her decades of charitable leadership with *The Thalians*, a mental health organization.
