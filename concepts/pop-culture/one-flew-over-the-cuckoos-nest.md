## summary

**One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest** is a landmark 1962 countercultural novel by American author *Ken Kesey*, famously adapted into an acclaimed 1975 feature film directed by *Miloš Forman*. Set inside an Oregon psychiatric hospital, the narrative explores an escalating battle of wills between the rebellious anti-authoritarian convict *Randle Patrick McMurphy* and the tyrannical, emasculating ward supervisor *Nurse Ratched*. Renowned as a profound parable of institutional conformity versus personal freedom, both the book and its cinematic adaptation remain major cornerstones of modern American culture.

## you gotta know

- Conceived while Kesey worked the night shift as an orderly at the Menlo Park Veterans Hospital in California, where he also participated in government-sponsored clinical trials of psychoactive substances including LSD.
- Narrated in the novel by *Chief Bromden*, a towering Native American patient who feigns muteness and perceives society as an oppressive, all-controlling mechanized apparatus dubbed "The Combine".
- Centers on *Randle McMurphy*, a charismatic criminal who fakes insanity to evade penal labor on a prison work farm, expecting a relaxed stay in the psychiatric institution.
- Features *Nurse Ratched*, commonly called "Big Nurse", who rules the ward through psychological manipulation, shame, and calculated group therapy interrogations.
- Reaches a climax when McMurphy smuggles alcohol and women into the ward for a late-night party, encouraging the stammering patient *Billy Bibbit* to gain confidence before Billy's subsequent suicide upon Ratched's discovery.
- Concludes tragically when McMurphy assaults Ratched and is subjected to a punitive frontal lobotomy, prompting Bromden to perform a mercy killing with a pillow before throwing a heavy hydrotherapy console through a window to escape.
- The 1975 screen adaptation, starring *Jack Nicholson* and *Louise Fletcher*, swept the "Big Five" Academy Awards (Best Picture, Director, Actor, Actress, Screenplay), a historic feat accomplished by only three films.
