## summary

**Bohemian Rhapsody** is a groundbreaking 1975 progressive-rock suite composed by *Freddie Mercury* and performed by the British rock band *Queen* for their fourth studio album, *A Night at the Opera*. Defying orthodox commercial music conventions, the six-minute track contains no refrains or recurring choruses, instead sequencing through an a cappella introduction, a tragic piano ballad, an elaborate multi-tracked mock-operatic interlude, a hard rock explosion, and an introspective coda. It became an international chart-topping phenomenon and remains one of the most famous and influential recordings in rock history.

## you gotta know

- Composed entirely by *Freddie Mercury*, who famously kept the complex musical arrangement in his head and directed bandmates using scraps of telephone-directory paper.
- Structured across distinct theatrical movements without a recurring chorus, shifting from a choral intro to a confessional murder ballad (*Mama, just killed a man*), an opera parody, hard rock, and a reflective outro.
- Made pioneering use of 24-track analog tape technology, painstakingly bouncing and overdubbing up to 180 vocal tracks until the magnetic tape turned nearly transparent from repeated physical passes.
- Features operatic characters, Italian commedia dell'arte archetypes, and historical references in its operatic section, shouting names such as *Scaramouche*, *Galileo*, *Figaro*, and *Bismillah*.
- Promoted with an iconic, budget-conscious promotional music video directed by *Bruce Gowers* featuring four faces dramatically lit in a diamond formation, widely credited with popularizing the music video format.
- Topped the UK Singles Chart for nine weeks upon release in late 1975, and hit number one again in 1991 following Mercury's death from AIDS-related complications.
- Famously regained massive popularity in the [[united-states]] in 1992 after being featured in an iconic headbanging car scene in the comedy film *Wayne's World*.
- Faced initial resistance from record executives and management who insisted its six-minute runtime made it unplayable on commercial radio, until DJ *Kenny Everett* played it fourteen times over a single weekend.
