## summary

**The Fault in Our Stars** is a 2012 young adult novel written by American author *John Green*, chronicling the romance between two teenage cancer patients who meet in a community support group. Its title derives from an ironic inversion of a famous line in *William Shakespeare*'s play *Julius Caesar*, confronting questions of mortality, oblivion, and the human search for enduring meaning. The book spent consecutive weeks atop *The New York Times* Best Seller list and was adapted into a smash-hit 2014 feature film starring *Shailene Woodley* and *Ansel Elgort*.

## you gotta know

- Narrated by sixteen-year-old *Hazel Grace Lancaster*, a thyroid cancer patient whose lungs require her to carry a portable oxygen tank named *Philip*.
- Focuses on Hazel's romantic relationship with *Augustus "Gus" Waters*, a charming former basketball player who lost his leg to osteosarcoma and remains obsessed with leaving a lasting mark on the world.
- Takes its title from a line spoken by *Cassius* to *Brutus* in *Julius Caesar* by *[[william-shakespeare]]*: "The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars, / But in ourselves, that we are underlings."
- Centers a major plot arc on Hazel's favorite novel, the fictitious *An Imperial Affliction*, which cuts off mid-sentence to reflect the abrupt nature of death.
- Features a pivotal journey by Hazel and Gus to Amsterdam to confront *Peter Van Houten*, the reclusive, alcoholic author of *An Imperial Affliction*, who shatters their illusions.
- Culminates in Gus's sudden relapse and heartbreaking decline, leading to a "pre-funeral" where Hazel delivers a poignant eulogy celebrating their small infinity within numbered days.
- Adapted into a 2014 box-office hit directed by *Josh Boone*, grossing over $300 million worldwide and cementing *John Green* as a dominant literary voice of the 2010s.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — provides the thematic foundation and title through *Cassius*'s fatalistic astrological speech in *Julius Caesar*.
- [[american-literature]] — stands as one of the defining commercial and critical triumphs in 21st-century American adolescent fiction.
- [[tragedy]] — engages classical dramatic conventions by leading doomed youthful lovers toward an inevitable, devastating catastrophe.
- [[existentialism]] — examines the philosophical terror of oblivion and the deliberate construction of purpose amidst mortal finitude.
- [[realism]] — portrays the clinical realities, physical indignities, and everyday logistics of chronic illness without sentimental sanitization.
- [[united-states]] — set primarily in suburban Indianapolis, reflecting middle-class American youth subculture and community institutions.
