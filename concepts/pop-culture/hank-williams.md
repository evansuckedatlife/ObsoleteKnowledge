## summary

**Hank Williams** was an American singer-songwriter and musician widely regarded as one of the most influential and foundational figures in country music history. Rising from rural Alabama during the Great Depression, he pioneered the honky-tonk sound with his emotionally raw singing, charismatic stage presence, and timeless, plainspoken songwriting. Despite dying at the age of twenty-nine, his vast catalog established conventions that shaped modern American popular, country, and rock music.

## you gotta know

- Recorded numerous foundational country classics, including *I'm So Lonesome I Could Cry*, *Your Cheatin' Heart*, *Cold, Cold Heart*, and *Hey, Good Lookin'*.
- Emerged from rural Alabama, learning guitar and rhythm techniques as a youth from African American street musician *Rufus "Tee-Tot" Payne*.
- Backed on his most famous studio recordings and barnstorming tours by his renowned ensemble, the *Drifting Cowboys*.
- Reached national stardom following triumphant appearances on the *Louisiana Hayride* radio program and the *Grand Ole Opry* in Nashville.
- Recorded several gospel and moralistic spoken-word songs under the religious alter ego *Luke the Drifter*.
- Suffered lifelong chronic back pain from spina bifida occulta, leading to severe alcohol and prescription drug dependence.
- Died suddenly of heart failure in the back seat of his Cadillac on New Year's Day 1953 while en route to a concert in Canton, Ohio.
