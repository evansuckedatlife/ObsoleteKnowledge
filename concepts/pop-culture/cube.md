## summary

**Cube** is a 1997 Canadian science-fiction psychological horror film directed by *Vincenzo Natali* and co-written with *André Bijelic* and *Graeme Manson*. Produced by the *Canadian Film Centre*, the minimalist feature follows six strangers who awaken inside an industrial, labyrinthine structure composed of thousands of booby-trapped cubic rooms with no memory of how they arrived. The film became an enduring international cult classic and a foundational milestone of the trapped-in-a-deadly-game cinematic subgenre, directly influencing later horror and thriller franchises.

## you gotta know

- The central premise involves six characters trapped inside a massive tessellated maze of brightly colored cubic chambers, many of which conceal lethal mechanical traps including flamethrowers, razor-wire grids, and acid sprays.
- Each character is named after an infamous real-world penal institution: *Quentin* after San Quentin State Prison, *Holloway* after HM Prison Holloway, *Kazan* after Kazan psychiatric prison, *Rennes* after Centre pénitentiaire de Rennes, *Alderson* after Federal Prison Camp, Alderson, and *Worth* after Leavenworth federal penitentiary.
- *Joan Leaven*, a mathematics student played by *Nicole de Boer*, discovers that identification numbers stamped on the hatches between chambers indicate whether a room is booby-trapped based on whether the coordinates contain prime numbers or prime powers.
- *Worth*, an architect played by *David Hewlett*, confesses to the group that he unknowingly designed the exterior shell of the structure for an anonymous corporate bureaucracy, admitting that no single person or entity understands the facility's overall purpose.
- *Rennes*, an aging escape artist known as *the Wren*, teaches the survivors to test prospective chambers by throwing boots to trigger motion and pressure sensors, only to be executed when an unprimed chamber douses him with corrosive acid.
- *Kazan*, an autistic man with remarkable savant mathematical calculation abilities, ultimately calculates complex prime factors and permutations in his head, enabling the final surviving member to navigate to the single bridge room that shifts to the outside world.
- Shot entirely on a single constructed cubic set measuring fourteen by fourteen feet in Toronto, the production changed the perceived color of different chambers using sliding gel lighting panels, demonstrating innovative low-budget *mise-en-scène*.
- The film launched a cult franchise including the 2002 sequel *Cube 2: Hypercube*, directed by *Andrzej Sekuła*, the 2004 prequel *Cube Zero*, and a 2021 Japanese remake directed by *Yasuhiko Shimizu*.

## connections

- [[mise-en-scene]] — utilized a single constructed physical room with interchangeable gel lighting panels to simulate hundreds of distinct modular cubic environments.
- [[film-editing]] — relied on precise cutting and claustrophobic pacing to sustain psychological tension and spatial disorientation across identical geometric sets.
- [[camera-movement]] — used dynamic wide-angle lenses and sweeping pans to emphasize the oppressive geometry and mechanical menace of the hatches.
- [[existentialism]] — embodies classic absurdist themes where individuals are trapped in a meaningless, mechanized bureaucracy without grand design or identifiable purpose.
- [[dan-levy]] — shares roots in the vibrant Toronto and Canadian independent screen production community that developed international cult followings.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — employs the shifting, lethal architectural puzzle box as an allegory for societal alienation, corporate disassociation, and human paranoia.
- [[tragedy]] — depicts the catastrophic psychological unraveling of the survivors, whose paranoia and internal violence destroy them before the maze itself does.
