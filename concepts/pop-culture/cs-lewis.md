## summary

**C. S. Lewis** (*Clive Staples Lewis*) was an eminent British scholar, novelist, literary critic, and lay theologian who held prestigious academic chairs at both *Oxford University* and *Cambridge University*. He is internationally revered as the author of *The Chronicles of Narnia*, a seven-volume fantasy saga that became one of the most beloved and commercially successful classics of children's literature in the English language. Beyond fantasy fiction, his incisive wartime BBC broadcasts defending religious belief and imaginative satires like *The Screwtape Letters* cemented his reputation as one of the most influential cultural apologists of the modern era.

## you gotta know

- Created the iconic fantasy world of *Narnia*, introduced in the 1950 novel *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe*, revolving around the great lion *Aslan* as an allegorical Christ figure who sacrifices himself to redeem a traitor.
- Was a core leading member of the *Inklings*, the legendary Oxford informal literary discussion society that met at *The Eagle and Child* pub and included *The Lord of the Rings* author *J. R. R. Tolkien*.
- Abandoned his childhood faith as an adolescent atheist before undergoing a profound intellectual reconversion to the *Anglican* faith in 1931, sparked by late-night philosophical conversations with *Tolkien* and *Hugo Dyson*.
- Delivered a celebrated series of live radio addresses over the *BBC* during the height of the Blitz in *World War II*, which were later compiled into the foundational apologetic volume *Mere Christianity*.
- Penned the brilliant epistolary satire *The Screwtape Letters*, which depicts a senior bureaucratic demon named *Screwtape* advising his novice nephew *Wormwood* on how to subvert a human soul away from virtue.
- Served in the British Army during *World War I* and was wounded by an exploding shell at the *Battle of Arras* in 1918, an experience that deeply informed his later writings on suffering and human pain.
- Produced major scholarly works on medieval and Renaissance literature, most notably his masterwork *The Allegory of Love*, which revolutionized academic study of courtly love traditions.
- Married the American poet and writer *Joy Davidman*; her agonizing death from cancer inspired his raw, poignant philosophical examination of loss, *A Grief Observed*, published under the pseudonym *N. W. Clerk*.
- Passed away on November 22, 1963, an event largely obscured in global news coverage due to the simultaneous assassination of American President *John F. Kennedy* and the death of novelist *Aldous Huxley*.

- [[england]] — the adoptive country where he lived, served during wartime, and completed his academic tenures at *Magdalen College, Oxford* and *Magdalene College, Cambridge*.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflict in which he fought in the trenches of the Western Front, suffering combat wounds that shaped his moral philosophy.
- [[world-war-ii]] — wartime backdrop during which his inspiring national *BBC* radio lectures laid the groundwork for *Mere Christianity*.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — foundational figure of English letters whose dramatic legacy Lewis examined extensively throughout his academic career.
- [[renaissance]] — historical and literary era that formed the primary focus of his landmark volume in the *Oxford History of English Literature*.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical movement whose rationalist and secular assumptions Lewis frequently critiqued in his apologetics.
