## summary

**George Harrison** was an English musician, singer-songwriter, and film producer who achieved worldwide renown as the lead guitarist of *The Beatles*. Often referred to as "the Quiet Beatle," he developed a singular melodic slide guitar style and introduced Indian instrumentation and Hindu spirituality into Western popular music during the 1960s. Following the group's dissolution, he forged a triumphant solo career with the landmark triple album *All Things Must Pass* and pioneered major humanitarian rock philanthropy.

## you gotta know

- Joined John Lennon's skiffle group *The Quarrymen* in Liverpool in 1958 at age fourteen, evolving into the lead guitarist of *The Beatles* throughout their recording career.
- Authored some of the band's most celebrated later works, including "Here Comes the Sun," "Something," and "While My Guitar Gently Weeps," despite frequently struggling for album space against the dominant Lennon–McCartney partnership.
- Introduced the sitar to pop music on the 1965 track "Norwegian Wood (This Bird Has Flown)" after studying under classical Indian virtuoso Ravi Shankar, precipitating widespread Eastern philosophical influences across Western counterculture.
- Released the acclaimed triple LP *All Things Must Pass* in 1970, which produced the chart-topping international hit "My Sweet Lord" and established his distinct slide guitar technique.
- Organized the pioneering 1971 *Concert for Bangladesh* at Madison Square Garden with Ravi Shankar, establishing the foundational modern model for large-scale charity rock benefits.
- Founded the independent film production company *HandMade Films* in 1978 to finance Monty Python's satirical religious comedy *Life of Brian*, later backing classic British independent cinema.
- Co-founded the 1980s supergroup *The Traveling Wilburys* alongside Bob Dylan, Tom Petty, Roy Orbison, and Jeff Lynne, performing under the pseudonym Nelson Wilbury.
