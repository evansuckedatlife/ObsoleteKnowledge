## summary

**Leonard Cohen** (born **Leonard Norman Cohen**) was an iconic Canadian singer-songwriter, poet, and novelist whose career spanned six decades. Emerging from the vibrant Montreal literary community before establishing himself in New York's folk and bohemian scenes, he achieved international acclaim for his baritone voice, intricate lyricism, and profound explorations of spirituality, romance, and grief. His timeless compositions, most notably the universally recognized hymn *Hallelujah*, established him as one of the most influential and revered figures in popular music.

## you gotta know

- Composed the global standard *Hallelujah*, first released on his 1984 studio album *Various Positions*; originally met with indifference by Columbia Records executive Walter Yetnikoff, the song later achieved universal renown through iconic covers by John Cale and Jeff Buckley.
- Established his early musical career with the debut album *Songs of Leonard Cohen* (1967), which featured enduring folk masterpieces such as *Suzanne*, *Sisters of Mercy*, and *Hey, That's No Way to Say Goodbye*.
- Commenced his artistic journey as an acclaimed modernist poet and novelist in Canada, publishing celebrated poetry collections like *Let Us Compare Mythologies* and *The Spice-Box of Earth*, alongside the experimental novels *The Favourite Game* and *Beautiful Losers*.
- Lived extensively during the 1960s on the Greek island of Hydra, where he met his romantic partner and artistic muse Marianne Ihlen, inspiring classic folk anthems like *So Long, Marianne* and *Bird on the Wire*.
- Reinvented his musical persona in the late 1980s by adopting synthesizers, drum machines, and a world-weary bass-baritone register on seminal albums like *I'm Your Man* (1988), which included the hits *First We Take Manhattan* and *Tower of Song*.
- Lived for several years during the 1990s as an ordained Zen Buddhist monk at the Mount Baldy Zen Center under master Kyozan Joshu Sasaki, receiving the Dharma name Jikan, meaning silence.
- Undertook extensive, critically praised worldwide concert tours in his seventies and eighties after discovering his longtime business manager had embezzled his retirement savings and publishing assets.
- Concluded his artistic career with the acclaimed 2016 album *You Want It Darker*, collaborating with his son Adam Cohen and the choir of his childhood Montreal synagogue, Shaar Hashomayim, shortly before his death.

- [[new-york-city]] — vital artistic hub where he resided at the Chelsea Hotel and immersed himself in the Greenwich Village folk and avant-garde music circuits.
- [[tragedy]] — recurring thematic current running through his poetic meditations on human frailty, romantic disillusionment, and mortal loss.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical outlook underpinning his persistent inquiries into personal liberty, despair, faith, and the search for spiritual meaning.
