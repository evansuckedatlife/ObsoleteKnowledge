## summary

The **video game industry** is the global ecosystem of companies, creators, and business models that produce, distribute, and monetize games. From arcade beginnings to console dominance to mobile and PC gaming, the industry has evolved through hardware generations, business model innovations, and shifting player demographics. It is now one of the world's largest entertainment industries, rivaling film and music in cultural and economic impact.

## you gotta know

- *Nintendo* revolutionized the industry after the 1983 market crash; the NES (Nintendo Entertainment System) established console dominance and the publishing model that continues today (hardware, exclusive titles, licensing control).
- *Console wars* (Nintendo vs. Sega, PlayStation vs. Xbox) drive technological advancement and exclusive game libraries; each generation of hardware brings new technical possibilities and industry consolidation.
- *Publishing models* have evolved from arcade (pay-per-play) to home console (buy the game) to digital distribution (downloadable games, subscriptions) to *free-to-play* (monetization through cosmetics and battle passes).
- *Esports* opened a new revenue stream: media rights, sponsorships, and streaming generate billions; esports reshapes how games are designed (competitive balance, spectator experience).
- *Streaming culture* (Twitch, YouTube Gaming) created new audience relationships; games are consumed both as products and as watched entertainment, affecting game marketing and longevity.
- *Indie games* emerged as a distinct category with digital storefronts (Steam, Epic Games Store); small teams can now reach global audiences, challenging traditional publishing gatekeeping.
- *Mobile gaming* (smartphones, tablets) created a new mass market; it is now the largest revenue segment globally, reaching audiences beyond traditional gamers.

## connections

- [[nintendo]] — a foundational industry force that shaped console design, publishing models, and competitive dynamics.
- [[esports]] — transformed games into spectator sport; creates new revenue and shapes game design priorities.
- [[game-development]] — the creative and technical labor underlying the industry; evolving tools and platforms shape what games are possible.
- [[streaming-culture]] — second-order industry effect; watching games is now as culturally significant as playing them.
- [[indie-games]] — a counter-force to corporate consolidation; democratized game creation and distribution.
- [[monetization-models]] — the business logic underlying industry structure; from arcade coins to subscription services.
