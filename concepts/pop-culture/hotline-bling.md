## summary

**Hotline Bling** is a massive pop and R&B single by Canadian recording artist *Drake*, originally premiered in July 2015 via OVO Sound and Republic Records before being appended to his 2016 studio album *Views*. Built around an evocative organ sample from *Timmy Thomas*'s 1972 soul classic "Why Can't We Live Together", the track pairs a breezy, cha-cha-inflected rhythm with wistful lyrics lamenting an estranged lover's newfound social independence. Propelled by an instantly iconic music video directed by *Director X*, the song became a generation-defining internet sensation, earned two Grammy Awards, and solidified Drake's status as a dominant pop icon.

## you gotta know

- Anchored by an immensely popular music video that featured Drake executing playful, self-deprecating dance movements inside minimalist, color-saturated geometric chambers.
- The visual presentation and luminous chromatic backdrops crafted by *Director X* drew extensive aesthetic inspiration from the light-and-space architectural installations of artist *James Turrell*.
- Birthed the legendary "Drakeposting" internet meme format, a universally recognized two-panel reaction template contrasting Drake's gestures of aversion and approval.
- Secured Grammy Awards for Best Rap Song and Best Rap/Sung Performance at the 59th Annual Ceremony, prompting Drake to publicly criticize the Recording Academy for categorizing an overt pop record as rap.
- Debuted during the second episode of Drake's OVO Sound Radio broadcast on Apple Music's Beats 1, premiering simultaneously alongside his high-profile Meek Mill response record "Charged Up."
- Evolved out of an artistic reimagining of Virginia musician D.R.A.M.'s viral single "Cha Cha", which sparked minor discussions regarding creative borrowing and credit within the hip-hop community.
- Climbed to number two on the US Billboard Hot 100 chart, where its ascent to the top spot was blocked by the concurrent dominance of *The Weeknd*'s "The Hills" and Adele's "Hello."
- Achieved diamond certification from the Recording Industry Association of America, reflecting more than ten million equivalent units sold in the United States alone.
