## summary

**Walmart** (historically styled as **Wal-Mart** and **Wal-Mart Stores**) is an American multinational retail corporation that operates the world's largest chain of discount supercenters, grocery stores, and wholesale clubs. Founded in 1962 by businessman *Sam Walton* in Rogers, Arkansas, the enterprise pioneered algorithmic inventory logistics, universal barcodes, and rural discount retailing to expand across North America and international markets. The company grew into the largest private employer on Earth and the quintessential symbol of modern big-box consumer capitalism in the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1962 by *Sam Walton*, who previously ran a franchised *Ben Franklin* five-and-dime store in Newport, Arkansas, before opening the first official discount store in Rogers.
- Headquartered in Bentonville, Arkansas, where the Walton family maintains corporate control and where the company developed its centralized satellite communication and supply-chain logistics.
- Operates *Sam's Club*, a warehouse membership club named after the founder, established in 1983 to rival competitor retail clubs across suburban America.
- Adopted the ubiquitous retail pricing strategy known as *Every Day Low Prices* (*EDLP*), eschewing short-term promotional sales in favor of permanent discount pricing supported by extreme vendor bargaining leverage.
- Championed the mass commercial implementation of the *Universal Product Code* (*UPC*) barcode system and *Retail Link*, a pioneering proprietary database network connecting suppliers directly to real-time store inventory data.
- Introduced the *Supercenter* format in 1988 in Washington, Missouri, combining a traditional discount department store with a full-scale supermarket, pharmacy, and automotive center under a single roof.
- Faced prominent labor criticism and landmark legal battles, most notably the gender-discrimination class-action suit *Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Dukes*, decided by the Supreme Court of the [[united-states]] in 2011.
- Acquired e-commerce marketplace *Jet.com* and major Indian online retailer *Flipkart* in the 2010s to counter digital retail dominance during modern shifts in commercial infrastructure.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — home country where the company transformed post-war retail geography, consumer habits, and rural supply economies.
- [[industrialization]] — foundational economic process whose manufacturing and freight advances enabled the company's high-volume global distribution network.
- [[cold-war]] — historical era whose late-twentieth-century globalization and supply deregulation allowed the enterprise to expand overseas into Latin America, Europe, and Asia.
- [[new-york-city]] — major financial center whose stock exchange listed the company in 1972 and whose municipal ordinances long resisted big-box store zoning.
- [[modernism]] — aesthetic and infrastructural movement whose rationalized distribution and modular suburban architectural design influenced big-box supercenters.
- [[realism]] — artistic and literary tradition whose documentary focus on working-class American life frequently intersects with sociological depictions of retail labor.
