## summary

**Asteroids** is a landmark vector graphics space arcade game released in November 1979 by Atari, Inc. Designed by *Lyle Rains* and *Ed Logg*, the game places players in command of a solitary triangular spaceship navigating a lethal field of drifting celestial rocks and hostile flying saucers in a wraparound two-dimensional coordinate space. As one of the definitive blockbusters of the golden age of arcade video games, it sold more than 70,000 arcade cabinets, pioneered high-score leaderboard tables using player initials, and established foundational physics-driven action mechanics in the [[united-states]] and across the globe.

## you gotta know

- Designed by *Ed Logg* and *Lyle Rains*, who adapted vector display hardware from Atari's earlier title *Lunar Lander* to generate crisp, glowing monochrome vector lines through the proprietary *QuadraScan* rendering system.
- Features continuous wraparound screen physics on both the horizontal and vertical axes, where shooting large rocks breaks them into two medium fragments, which subsequently fracture into two small targets before disappearing.
- Popularized the worldwide arcade convention of recording the top ten players' high scores using three-letter initials entered via directional controls, sparking competitive arcade culture across the [[united-states]].
- Threatens the player with two distinct enemy saucers: a large, inaccurate saucer that shoots indiscriminately, and a smaller, lethal craft whose shots calculate trajectories directly toward the player's current position.
- Provides a risky emergency hyperspace button that teleports the ship to an unpredictable random coordinate on the screen, carrying the ever-present danger of materializing directly inside an oncoming boulder.
- Inspired the notorious high-scoring arcade exploit known as "lurking," in which skilled players intentionally eliminated all but one small asteroid to safely farm endless waves of incoming saucers for points.
- Sold millions of home cartridges when ported to the Atari 2600 and Atari 7800, cementing Atari's commercial footprint in home gaming against rivals from [[japan]].

## connections

- [[united-states]] — country of origin where Atari engineers designed the original coin-operated cabinet and established early commercial video game dominance.
- [[esports]] — early competitive high-score chasing in this title helped establish score verification bodies like Twin Galaxies and organized arcade tournaments.
- [[first-person-shooter]] — early action forebear whose fast-paced projectile management and evasive maneuvers influenced later action combat design.
- [[counter-strike]] — modern competitive shooter whose emphasis on fine motor twitch reflexes and precision shooting inherits the competitive spirit of arcade action.
- [[minecraft]] — enduring block-based sandbox title whose early survival gameplay loops share basic mechanical roots in rock fragmentation and spatial navigation.
- [[cold-war]] — historical era whose technological arms race and space exploration rivalry inspired the late-1970s pop-cultural obsession with orbital combat.
- [[japan]] — rival arcade gaming powerhouse whose blockbuster release of *Space Invaders* directly challenged Atari to produce a multidirectional response.
