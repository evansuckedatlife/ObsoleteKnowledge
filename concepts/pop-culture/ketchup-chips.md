## summary

**Ketchup chips** are a distinctly Canadian variety of seasoned potato chip characterized by their sweet, tangy, and intensely red tomato-based powder seasoning. Developed during the rapid mid-twentieth-century expansion of commercial snack manufacturing, they have evolved into an iconic cultural staple in Canada that is notoriously uncommon south of the border in the *[[united-states]]*. In pop culture and trivia, the snack frequently serves as an affectionate shorthand for Canadian identity and culinary distinctiveness.

## you gotta know

- A beloved cultural touchstone and regional snack throughout Canada, known for leaving deep reddish-pink powder stains on fingers.
- Typically seasoned with a blend of tomato powder, garlic powder, onion powder, sugar, salt, and vinegar or malic acid, producing a sweet and savory tang rather than just tomato paste flavor.
- Commercialized in Canada during the 1970s, with major potato chip brands like *Hostess* (now under *Frito-Lay*) and *Humpty Dumpty* pioneering the mass market distribution of the flavor.
- Although standard ketchup originated elsewhere, ketchup-flavored chips remain widely unavailable in the *[[united-states]]*, turning them into a popular tourist novelty and border-crossing souvenir.
- Often referenced in Canadian media, including television sitcoms like *[[kims-convenience]]* and sketch comedy like *[[kids-in-the-hall]]*, alongside other national snack traditions like all-dressed chips and poutine.
- International manufacturers occasionally test limited runs in foreign markets, though none maintain the ubiquitous consumer loyalty found across Canadian grocery shelves and convenience stores.
