## summary

**Barbie Girl** is a 1997 Europop and bubblegum dance anthem by the Danish-Norwegian quartet *Aqua*, released as the standout third single from their debut studio album, *Aquarium*. Featuring high-pitched theatrical vocals from *Lene Nystrøm* and deep spoken counterpoints by *René Dif*, the song affectionately parodies consumerist lifestyles and gender tropes through the stylized personas of the iconic fashion doll *Barbie* and her boyfriend *Ken*. It became an overwhelming international number-one smash hit that sparked an influential First Amendment legal battle with toy conglomerate *Mattel*.

## you gotta know

- Reached number one on singles charts in more than fifteen countries, topping the UK Singles Chart for four straight weeks and climbing into the top ten on the American *Billboard* Hot 100.
- Toy manufacturer *Mattel* initiated a high-profile federal trademark infringement lawsuit against *MCA Records*, alleging the suggestive lyrics infringed on the company's trademark and tarnished the doll's wholesome reputation.
- Judge *Alex Kozinski* of the US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit ruled decisively in favor of the record label, establishing the track as protected parody under the First Amendment and concluding his legal opinion with the famous phrase: "The parties are advised to chill."
- Lead vocalist *Lene Nystrøm* assumed the role of the synthetic doll proclaiming "I'm a Barbie girl, in the Barbie world / Life in plastic, it's fantastic," while co-vocalist *René Dif* delivered the chorus refrain "Come on, Barbie, let's go party!"
- Songwriter and keyboardist *Søren Rasted* was inspired to write the track after attending a kitsch art exhibition in Copenhagen showcasing plastic toys, which led him to draft the lyric "life in plastic, it's fantastic."
- *Mattel* eventually softened its hostility toward the song decades later, sanctioning an interpolated sample for the collaborative track "Barbie World" by *Nicki Minaj* and *Ice Spice* on the soundtrack to the 2023 blockbuster film *Barbie*.
- Conservative media monitors and parental advocacy associations voiced strong outrage upon release over double-entendre phrases such as "undress me everywhere" and "kiss me here, touch me there, hanky panky."
