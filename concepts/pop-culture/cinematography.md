## summary

**Cinematography** is the art and craft of making movies—the technical and creative choices that define what the camera captures. It encompasses camera placement, movement, lens selection, lighting design, and color grading, all working together to create the visual language of a film. Cinematography transforms a script into a visible world and shapes how viewers perceive narrative, character, and emotion.

## you gotta know

- The cinematographer (director of photography) collaborates with the director to determine the visual style, choosing lenses, angles, lighting, and color palettes that serve the story and brand.
- Lighting design is foundational: it reveals or obscures, suggests mood, and directs the viewer's eye; high-key lighting creates clarity and optimism, while low-key creates mystery and shadow and psychological depth.
- Depth of field—the range of distance in focus—controls visual storytelling; shallow depth isolates a character emotionally, while deep focus grounds them in their environment and social world.
- Camera movement (pans, tilts, tracking shots, cranes) is a tool of meaning-making; static frames create stability, while movement can convey urgency, discovery, or unease and disorientation.
- Color temperature and grading shape emotional register: warm tones suggest nostalgia or comfort, cool tones suggest alienation or dread; color grading is a postproduction craft.
- Composition and framing guide viewer attention; the rule of thirds and other compositional principles organize the visual space; breaking them creates discomfort or emphasis.
- Different film stocks (or digital sensors) have distinct visual properties; black-and-white emphasizes texture and form, while color stocks vary in saturation and grain.
