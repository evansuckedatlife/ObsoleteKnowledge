## summary

**You've Got a Friend in Me** is an American pop song written and recorded by singer-songwriter *Randy Newman* as the central theme for the groundbreaking 1995 computer-animated film *Toy Story*. Rooted in traditional ragtime, blues, and Americana styles, the song captures the affectionate, enduring bond between the pull-string cowboy doll *Woody* and his young human owner *Andy*. Widely celebrated across contemporary cinema, the track earned nominations for an Academy Award and a Golden Globe, eventually becoming the signature musical anthem of the entire *Pixar* franchise.

## you gotta know

- Written and performed by *Randy Newman* for *Toy Story* (1995), serving as the opening title song that establishes the warm childhood companionship between *Woody* and *Andy* before the introduction of *Buzz Lightyear*.
- Garnered nominations for Best Original Song at both the Academy Awards and the Golden Globe Awards in 1996, cementing *Pixar Animation Studios*' critical and commercial ascendancy.
- Features *Newman*'s distinctive gravelly vocal delivery, warm piano chording, and nostalgic brass instrumentation evoking New Orleans jazz, Dixieland rhythms, and vintage early twentieth-century American parlor songs.
- Performed as a heartfelt duet with country music superstar *Lyle Lovett* for the film's closing credits, contrasting *Lovett*'s smooth Texas drawl with *Newman*'s bluesy, idiosyncratic cadence.
- Adapted across multiple subsequent franchise installments, notably including a triumphant big-band swing version belted by the squeaky toy penguin *Wheezy* (voiced by *Robert Goulet*) in *Toy Story 2*.
- Reimagined in *Toy Story 3* (2010) as a dramatic, passionate flamenco rendition recorded by the *Gipsy Kings* to accompany a sequence featuring an accidentally reset, romance-minded Spanish-mode *Buzz Lightyear*.
- Commissioned specifically by director *John Lasseter*, who explicitly rejected the traditional musical theater model where characters break into song, opting instead for a conversational narrative voiceover reflecting the protagonist's emotional loyalty.
