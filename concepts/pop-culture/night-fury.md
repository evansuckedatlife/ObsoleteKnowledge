## summary

The **Night Fury** is a fictional species of dragon originating in *DreamWorks Animation*'s animated cinematic franchise *How to Train Your Dragon*, loosely based on the book series by *Cressida Cowell*. Initially described as the unholy offspring of lightning and death itself, the species is distinguished by its jet-black scales, retractable teeth, supersonic dive speeds, and explosive plasma blasts. The central protagonist dragon, named **Toothless**, forms an empathetic bond with the young Viking protagonist *Hiccup Horrendous Haddock III*, fundamentally dismantling generations of prejudice and war between humanity and dragons.

## you gotta know

- Debuted in the 2010 animated film *How to Train Your Dragon*, directed by *Chris Sanders* and *Dean DeBlois*.
- Feared by the Viking village of Berk as the fastest, smartest, and rarest dragon species, noted in the Dragon Book for never missing and never showing itself.
- Shot down by *Hiccup* using a mechanical bola launcher, which severs half of its caudal tail fin and leaves it unable to achieve stable flight without a human prosthetic rider.
- Designed visually by combining behavioral and aesthetic traits of black leopards, panthers, salamanders, and domestic cats, emphasizing both lethal grace and expressive playfulness.
- Fires localized concussive bursts of acetylene and oxygen plasma that detonate on impact rather than standard streams of sustained fire.
- Employs biological echolocation in enclosed spaces and dark caves by emitting focused sonic plasma pulses.
- Encounters a white female counterpart designated a *Light Fury* in the 2019 concluding installment *How to Train Your Dragon: The Hidden World*, revealing the hidden ancestral realm of dragons.

## connections

- [[visual-metaphor]] — employed through the dragon's prosthetic tail fin and Hiccup's prosthetic leg to symbolize shared vulnerability and mutual reliance.
- [[tragedy]] — the narrative framing of the species' near-extinction caused by the relentless big-game dragon hunter *Grimmel the Grisly*.
- [[film-editing]] — used extensively to craft the visceral pacing and kinetic aerial choreography during flight sequences.
- [[western-film]] — echoed in the trope of an untamed frontier creature tamed through patience rather than violent subjugation.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the mythological tradition of chimerae and winged mythical beasts that inspired modern dragon iconography.
- [[japan]] — the aesthetic influence of anime flight sequences, specifically the aerial animations of *Hayao Miyazaki*, on the dragon's soaring mechanics.
