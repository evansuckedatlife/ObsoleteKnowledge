## summary

**Adia** is an alternative pop ballad recorded by Canadian singer-songwriter *Sarah McLachlan* and released in 1998 as the second single from her breakthrough fourth studio album, *Surfacing* (1997). Co-written with longtime producer *Pierre Marchand*, the song became an international smash hit, reaching number three on the Billboard Hot 100 in the [[united-states]] and earning a Grammy Award nomination. Driven by plaintive piano chords, swelling vocal harmonies, and introspective themes of guilt, redemption, and forgiveness, the track stands as a definitive anthem of late-1990s adult alternative music.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the emotional centerpiece of *Surfacing*, written as an apology and plea for forgiveness to McLachlan's best friend after McLachlan began dating—and eventually married—her friend's ex-husband, drummer *Ashwin Sood*.
- Climbed to number three on the US Billboard Hot 100 in 1998, becoming McLachlan's highest-charting single in the [[united-states]] alongside "Angel" and establishing her as a core fixture of commercial radio.
- Earned a Grammy Award nomination for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance in 1999, cementing McLachlan's critical prestige during the height of the Lilith Fair era.
- Featured an acclaimed music video directed by *Javier Aguilera*, shot in gritty black-and-white and muted color on the streets of [[new-york-city]], showing McLachlan singing to indifferent urban commuters.
- Anchored the promotional cycle for the groundbreaking *Lilith Fair* festival tour, which McLachlan founded to showcase female solo artists and female-fronted bands across North America.
- Showcases the distinctive production style of *Pierre Marchand*, blending acoustic grand piano, ambient electric guitars, and layered ethereal background vocals characteristic of contemporary [[realism]] in singer-songwriter production.
- Refrains from traditional verse-chorus resolution, utilizing an iconic opening line ("Adia, I do believe I failed you") that immediately frames the composition as an unsparing confessional letter.
