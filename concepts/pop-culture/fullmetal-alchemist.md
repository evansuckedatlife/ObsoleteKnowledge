## summary

**Fullmetal Alchemist** (*Hagane no Renkinjutsushi*, commonly abbreviated as **FMA**) is an acclaimed Japanese dark fantasy manga series written and illustrated by *Hiromu Arakawa*, serialized in *Monthly Shonen Gangan* from 2001 to 2010. Set in the steampunk-inspired nation of *Amestris*, it chronicles the perilous journey of brothers *Edward Elric* and *Alphonse Elric*, who seek the mythical *Philosopher's Stone* to restore their bodies after a catastrophic, forbidden attempt at human transmutation. Renowned for its intricate moral philosophy, geopolitical intrigue, and critique of state-sanctioned militarism, it remains one of the most celebrated and commercially successful multimedia franchises originating from [[japan]].

## you gotta know

- The narrative revolves around the foundational law of Equivalent Exchange, an alchemical doctrine dictating that to obtain something of value, something of equal value must be surrendered, a principle that the protagonists continually interrogate and deconstruct.
- After their mother *Trisha Elric* dies, the brothers attempt forbidden human alchemy; the ritual backfires, causing Edward to lose his left leg and Alphonse his entire body, leading Edward to sacrifice his right arm to tether Alphonse's soul to an iron suit of armor.
- Edward becomes the youngest certified State Alchemist in history at age twelve, receiving his military rank and title from Fuhrer *King Bradley*, while outfitting his missing limbs with high-tech mechanical prosthetics known as *automail*.
- Two distinct television anime adaptations exist: the 2003 series diverged from the unfinished manga to forge an original storyline linking to an alternate version of [[world-war-i]] Europe, whereas the 2009 series, *Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood*, faithfully adapted the entire manga canon.
- The backstory is defined by the *Ishvalan Civil War*, a genocidal military campaign that traumatized characters such as *Roy Mustang* and *Riza Hawkeye*, serving as a profound thematic examination of wartime atrocities and the complicity of scientists in state violence.
- The overarching antagonists are *Father*, an ancient homunculus born from a flask in the lost kingdom of *Xerxes*, and his seven artificial children named after the seven deadly sins, who orchestrate nationwide massacres to carve a massive transmutation circle.
- At the story's philosophical climax, Edward willingly surrenders his *Gate of Truth* and forfeits his ability to ever perform alchemy again in order to restore his brother's mortal body, choosing human bonds over transcendent power.

## connections

- [[japan]] — country of origin for the original manga and its two celebrated anime adaptations produced by studio *Bones*.
- [[tragedy]] — core dramatic mode underpinning the brothers' devastating bodily loss, the fate of *Nina Tucker*, and the horrors of the Ishvalan genocide.
- [[world-war-i]] — historical era whose industrial trench warfare, military uniforms, and geopolitical tensions inspired the 2003 anime's alternate-reality finale.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical framework reflected in Alphonse's existential dread regarding artificial identity and Edward's ultimate rejection of metaphysical authority.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical transformation reflected in the steampunk aesthetic of Amestris, from locomotive networks to mechanized *automail* prosthetics.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — narrative technique employed throughout the series, notably the stark white expanse of the *Gate of Truth* representing cosmic knowledge.
