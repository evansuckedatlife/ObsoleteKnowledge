## summary

**Christian Dior** was a seminal French couturier who revolutionized women's fashion in the immediate aftermath of the Second World War. Founding his eponymous Parisian fashion house in 1946 with the backing of textile magnate *Marcel Boussac*, he rose to worldwide fame in 1947 with his debut collection, christened the "New Look." His structured, voluptuous silhouettes revived the luxury economy of Paris and redefined mid-century feminine elegance.

## you gotta know

- Debuted the spring-summer 1947 *Corolle* collection, famously dubbed the "New Look" by *Harper's Bazaar* editor *Carmel Snow*.
- Characterized the New Look by tiny, cinched waists, structured boning, and voluminous, calf-length skirts made with lavish yardage of fabric.
- Directly countered wartime fabric rationing and utilitarian fashion mandates enforced during the occupation of Europe.
- Established the *Bar suit*, composed of an ivory silk shantung jacket with padded peplums and an accordion-pleated black wool skirt.
- Launched the iconic fragrance *Miss Dior* in 1947, naming the scent in honor of his sister *Catherine Dior*, a French Resistance survivor.
- Mentored a young *Yves Saint Laurent*, who took over artistic direction of the fashion house following Dior's sudden death in 1957.
- Pioneered luxury licensing agreements across stockings, furs, and cosmetics, establishing the structural blueprint for modern luxury fashion conglomerates.

## connections

- [[france]] — nation where he lived, founded his couture house, and revitalized national artistic prestige.
- [[world-war-ii]] — conflict whose rationing and austerity directly shaped the rebellious luxury of his post-war designs.

- [[new-york-city]] — major transatlantic fashion capital that embraced and institutionalized his commercial success.
