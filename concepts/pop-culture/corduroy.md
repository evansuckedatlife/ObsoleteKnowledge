## summary

**Corduroy** is a classic 1968 children's picture book written and illustrated by American author *Don Freeman*, published by Viking Press. Set inside a multi-story department store in mid-century [[new-york-city]], the story follows a small stuffed bear dressed in green corduroy overalls who searches the darkened building at night for his missing button so someone will finally buy him. Celebrated for its emotional gentleness and its pioneering depiction of an African American child protagonist, the book remains a beloved foundational text of modern [[american-literature]].

## you gotta know

- The title character, a small teddy bear named *Corduroy*, sits patiently on a toy shelf in a grand downtown department store, waiting day after day for a customer to take him home.
- The narrative begins when a young girl named *Lisa* spots the bear and asks her mother to buy him, but her mother refuses because she has already spent enough money and notes that the bear is missing a button from one of his shoulder straps.
- After the department store closes and the crowds leave, the bear climbs down from his shelf and wanders into the dark store to find his lost button, marvelling at the escalators as if climbing a moving mountain.
- Exploring the palace of the furniture department on an upper floor, he mistakes a mattress button on a display bed for his own and tugs on it so hard that he topples over, knocking down a tall floor lamp with an enormous crash.
- The commotion draws a night watchman patrolling the store with a flashlight, who finds the bear tucked under the bedcovers and gently carries him back downstairs to his spot in the toy department.
- The next morning, *Lisa* returns to the store alone, using coins saved in her personal piggy bank to purchase the bear despite his missing button.
- Bringing him to her family's apartment bedroom, *Lisa* lovingly stitches a replacement button onto his overalls, and the two embrace as true friends, demonstrating unconditional acceptance and care.
- Published at the height of the [[civil-rights-movement]] in the [[united-states]], the book made history by casually centering an African American girl and her mother in everyday urban life without making race a didactic issue.
