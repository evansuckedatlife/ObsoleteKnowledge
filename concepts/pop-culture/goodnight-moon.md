## summary

**Goodnight Moon** is an American children's picture book written by *Margaret Wise Brown* and illustrated by *Clement Hurd*, published in 1947 by *Harper & Brothers*. Set entirely within a vibrant green bedroom, the text follows an anthropomorphic bunny ritualistically bidding goodnight to every object, creature, and unseen presence in the room as darkness falls. Renowned for its hypnotic cadence, alternating monochrome and saturated color spreads, and subtle atmospheric shifts, it stands as a cornerstone of twentieth-century bedtime literature in the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Published in 1947, the book features a gentle rhyming poem tracking a young bunny in striped pajamas who systematically says goodnight to toys, furniture, and surroundings in "the great green room."
- Illustrated by *Clement Hurd*, whose wife *Edith Thacher Hurd* helped mix the vibrant color palette, including the striking green walls and orange-red floorboards.
- Alternates between full-color two-page spreads and black-and-white spot drawings, with the room subtly dimming and the hands of two clocks advancing over the course of an hour and ten minutes from 7:00 to 8:10 PM.
- Famous for its comforting yet surreal domestic catalog, wishing farewell to "a telephone", "a red balloon", "two little kittens", "a pair of mittens", "a quiet old lady whispering 'hush'", and eventually abstract concepts like "noises everywhere" and "air".
- Features interior visual easter eggs, notably a framed painting of *The Runaway Bunny*—an earlier 1942 collaboration between *Brown* and *Hurd*—hung above the fireplace mantel.
- Initially rejected and boycotted for decades by influential *New York Public Library* children's librarian *Anne Carroll Moore*, who disliked its departure from traditional fairy tales and pedagogical moralizing; the library did not acquire copies until 1972.
- Employs a calming sensory arc that relies on rhythm, repetition, and spatial permanence to alleviate bedtime anxiety and ease young children into sleep.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — landmark classic of twentieth-century American children's literature and modernist nursery verse.
- [[new-york-city]] — created within the vibrant mid-century publishing and progressive education circles of Greenwich Village.
- [[modernism]] — influenced by the *Bank Street College of Education* philosophy focusing on the here-and-now world of child perception rather than traditional fairy tale fantasy.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — uses advancing clock hands, fading light, and shifting moon positions to depict time passing and the arrival of slumber.
- [[realism]] — grounds fantasy in concrete domestic rituals, everyday household objects, and tactile room furnishings.
- [[united-states]] — enduring cultural touchstone, widely cited, parodied, and read to generations of American infants and toddlers.
