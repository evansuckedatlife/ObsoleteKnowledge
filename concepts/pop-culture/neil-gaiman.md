## summary

**Neil Gaiman**, born **Neil Richard MacKinnon Gaiman** in 1960, is an English author, graphic novelist, audio dramatist, and screenwriter celebrated for his mythic fantasy, dark urban fairy tales, and inventive folklore. He achieved international literary prominence by writing the seminal *DC Comics* / *Vertigo* monthly series *The Sandman* before publishing widely read novels such as *American Gods*, *Coraline*, *Neverwhere*, and *Stardust*. Over a career spanning more than four decades, his distinctive narrative voice has garnered prestigious literary honors across comics, speculative fiction, and children's literature, including the *Hugo*, *Nebula*, *Bram Stoker*, *Eisner*, *Newbery*, and *Carnegie* medals.

## you gotta know

- Wrote the foundational dark-fantasy comic book series *The Sandman* (1989–1996) for *DC Comics*, revolving around *Dream* (also known as *Morpheus*), the brooding personification of dreaming and lord of the *Endless* alongside siblings *Death*, *Destiny*, *Destruction*, *Desire*, *Despair*, and *Delirium*.
- Achieved historic literary recognition when issue #19 of *The Sandman*, titled "A Midsummer Night's Dream" and illustrated by *Charles Vess*, won the 1991 *World Fantasy Award* for Short Fiction, marking the only comic book in history to claim the prize before rules were amended.
- Co-wrote the comedic apocalyptic fantasy novel *Good Omens* (1990) with English author *Terry Pratchett*, chronicling the misadventures of the fastidious angel *Aziraphale* and the rock-and-roll demon *Crowley* as they conspire to sabotage the coming of the Antichrist.
- Published the acclaimed urban-fantasy novel *American Gods* (2001), depicting an ex-convict named *Shadow Moon* recruited into an escalating subterranean conflict between traditional mythological gods brought by historical immigrants and modern technological avatars representing media, internet, and globalization.
- Penned the gothic children's novella *Coraline* (2002), in which an inquisitive girl enters a hidden passage behind a bricked doorway in her flat to encounter an eerie alternate household governed by the sinister, button-eyed *Other Mother*.
- Captured both the American *Newbery Medal* and the British *Carnegie Medal* for his 2008 youth novel *The Graveyard Book*, an inventive dark fantasy inspired by *Rudyard Kipling*'s *The Jungle Book* following an orphaned infant named *Nobody Owens* raised by ghosts, ghouls, and a vampiric guardian named *Silas*.
- Reinterpreted classical Scandinavian and Germanic oral sagas in his 2017 collection *Norse Mythology*, reconstructing the cosmogony from *Yggdrasil* to the cataclysm of *Ragnarök*.
- Mentored and inspired generations of contemporary comic artists and queer fantasy showrunners, including graphic novelist [[nd-stevenson]], while overseeing screen adaptations of his own works for television networks and streaming platforms.

## connections

- [[nd-stevenson]] — fellow graphic novelist, comic creator, and fantasy writer who shares his focus on mythic deconstruction and graphic narrative craft.
- [[england]] — home nation where he was born in Portchester, began his literary journalism career, and cultivated his dark folkloric imagination.
- [[american-literature]] — national literary tradition within which his novels, dark fantasies, and comic scripts gained major canonical standing.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — Elizabethan playwright who appears as an important character in *The Sandman* and whose works form the narrative fabric of Gaiman's award-winning comic issues.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Mediterranean classical civilization whose mythic figures, underworld geography, and pantheon appear regularly throughout Gaiman's mythological stories.
- [[modernism]] — literary and cultural movement whose thematic disillusionment, urban alienation, and psychological fragmentation heavily inform Gaiman's dark urban fantasy.
