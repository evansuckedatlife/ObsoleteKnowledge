## summary

**Ararat** is a 2002 Canadian-French historical drama film written and directed by Atom Egoyan that examines the enduring trauma and legacy of the 1915 Armenian Genocide. Structured as a layered metafictional story, it revolves around the production of an epic historical film in Toronto, following diverse characters grappling with heritage, memory, truth, and generational grief. The work is widely celebrated in international cinema for its sophisticated narrative nesting, philosophical inquiry into representation, and intense examination of how collective catastrophes reverberate through modern diasporic identities.

## you gotta know

- Directed by Armenian-Canadian auteur Atom Egoyan, who created a complex film-within-a-film narrative about an aging director named Edward Saroyan, played by real-life singer *Charles Aznavour*, shooting a movie about the 1915 siege of Van.
- Features *David Alpay* in his film debut as Raffi, an Armenian youth working on the production crew who travels to Turkey to shoot footage of Mount Ararat and faces interrogation upon returning to Canadian customs.
- Co-stars *Christopher Plummer* as David, a retiring Canadian customs inspector whose tense interrogation of Raffi at the border serves as the primary framing device and philosophical crucible for discussing truth, art, and belief.
- Stars Egoyan's wife and frequent collaborator *Arsinée Khanjian* as Ani, an art historian delivering lectures on Armenian-American painter *Arshile Gorky*, linking modern art history with historical suffering.
- Explores *Arshile Gorky*'s famous painting *The Artist and His Mother*, using the altered portrait and the painter's troubled life to symbolize fractured memory and survivor guilt.
- Swept the 23rd Genie Awards in Canada, winning Best Motion Picture, Best Actress for Khanjian, Best Supporting Actor for *Elias Koteas*, Best Costume Design, and Best Original Score for composer *Mychael Danna*.
- Screened out of competition at the 2002 Cannes Film Festival, sparking substantial geopolitical dialogue and controversy surrounding historical denialism and official recognition.
