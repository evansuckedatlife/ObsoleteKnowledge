## summary

**Total Eclipse of the Heart** is a 1983 epic power ballad recorded by Welsh singer *Bonnie Tyler* and written and produced by American composer *Jim Steinman*. Released as the lead single from Tyler's fifth studio album, *Faster Than the Speed of Night*, the multi-platinum track reached number one across international record charts, including the Billboard Hot 100 in the *[[united-states]]* and the UK Singles Chart in *[[england]]*. The song stands as the quintessential pop-rock melodrama of the 1980s, renowned for its Wagnerian rock instrumentation, operatic vocal intensity, and enduring cultural life during actual astronomical eclipses.

## you gotta know

- Ranked as one of the best-selling singles of all time, holding the number-one spot on the Billboard Hot 100 for four consecutive weeks and earning Grammy nominations for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance and Best Producer.
- Features the haunting counter-melody vocals of Canadian singer *Rory Dodd*, who repeatedly sings the famous refrain "Turn around, bright eyes" throughout the track's dramatic crescendos.
- Accompanied by a gothic music video directed by *Russell Mulcahy* and shot at the Victorian gothic Holloway Sanatorium in *[[england]]*, featuring glowing-eyed choirboys, leather-clad dancers, levitating gymnasts, and striking *[[visual-metaphor]]*.
- Originally developed from a melody *Jim Steinman* conceived for a musical adaptation of the vampire story *Nosferatu*, the track was later repurposed by Steinman into his European stage musical *Dance of the Vampires* under the title "Totale Finsternis".
- Regularly experiences staggering spikes in global digital streaming and sales during real-world solar eclipses, serving as an unofficial anthem for celestial events worldwide.
- Meat Loaf claimed for decades that Steinman originally wrote the composition for his own follow-up album, though Steinman granted it to Tyler after their production partnership formed.
- The complete, uncut album version extends over seven minutes, showcasing wall-of-sound production, thunderous piano playing by *Roy Bittan*, and blistering guitar work by *Rick Derringer*.
