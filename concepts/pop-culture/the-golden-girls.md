## summary

**The Golden Girls** is a celebrated *United States* television sitcom created by *Susan Harris* that aired on NBC for seven seasons from 1985 to 1992. Set in Miami, Florida, the series chronicles the lives of four older, single women who share a suburban ranch-style home and navigate romance, family conflict, health crises, and retirement. Acclaimed for its sharp writing, progressive social commentary, and iconic ensemble cast, the program remains a touchstone in television history and one of only a handful of shows in which all primary lead performers won Primetime Emmy Awards.

## you gotta know

- The core ensemble consisted of four veteran comedic actresses: *Bea Arthur* as the sarcastic substitute teacher *Dorothy Zbornak*, *Betty White* as the naive Minnesotan *Rose Nylund*, *Rue McClanahan* as the libidinous Southern belle *Blanche Devereaux*, and *Estelle Getty* as Dorothy's sharp-tongued Sicilian mother, *Sophia Petrillo*.
- Every one of the four principal leads won an Emmy Award for their performance on the show, cementing it alongside *All in the Family* and *Will & Grace* in this rare television achievement.
- The framing device for late-night bonding sessions throughout the show's run was the kitchen table, where the quartet regularly dissected emotional problems and unresolved grievances over slices of cheesecake.
- *Sophia Petrillo* consistently prefaced her illustrative childhood fables and Sicilian moral lessons with the nostalgic catchphrase, *Picture it: Sicily...*, recounting elaborate and often dubious historical anecdotes.
- *Rose Nylund* frequently exasperated her roommates with rambling, bizarre folktales about her eccentric Scandinavian-American hometown of *St. Olaf, Minnesota*, featuring characters like *the Great Herring War* participants.
- The show broke ground on network television by tackling topical and controversial issues with warmth and humor, addressing the *HIV/AIDS* epidemic, elder care, chronic fatigue syndrome, assisted suicide, age discrimination, and gay marriage long before such themes were common in prime-time broadcasting.
- Originally, *Rue McClanahan* was slated to play Rose and *Betty White* was cast as Blanche, but director *Jay Sandrich* suggested switching their roles to avoid repeating character archetypes White had played on *The Mary Tyler Moore Show* and McClanahan on *Maude*.
- After *Bea Arthur* decided to depart the series at the end of the seventh season in 1992, the remaining three actresses starred in a short-lived CBS spin-off entitled *The Golden Palace*, in which the trio bought and managed a boutique Miami hotel.
