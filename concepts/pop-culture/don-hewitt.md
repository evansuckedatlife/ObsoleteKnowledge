## summary

**Don Hewitt** was an influential American television news producer and broadcast executive best known as the creator and long-time executive producer of the landmark CBS newsmagazine *60 Minutes*. Over a career spanning more than five decades, he pioneered television journalism standards by treating news reporting with the narrative drive, pacing, and visual storytelling of personal human drama. His innovative television direction shaped foundational political events, including the first televised presidential debates in the *United States*, revolutionizing how national audiences consumed investigative reporting.

## you gotta know

- Created the iconic CBS newsmagazine *60 Minutes* in 1968, serving as its executive producer and driving editorial force for thirty-six years until stepping down in 2004.
- Formulated the fundamental editorial philosophy of *60 Minutes* around the mantra "tell me a story," framing complex investigative investigations through dramatic character-driven narratives rather than dry recitations of policy.
- Directed the historic first televised debate between presidential candidates *John F. Kennedy* and *Richard Nixon* in 1960, establishing the paramount influence of visual aesthetics and broadcast posture in national politics.
- Produced the *CBS Evening News with Walter Cronkite* during pivotal moments of the [[cold-war]], the [[civil-rights-movement]], and the assassination of *John F. Kennedy*.
- Pioneered technical broadcast conventions in news television, including split-screen interviews, the use of teleprompters, and tight, confrontational camera close-ups during high-stakes interviews.
- Championed aggressive investigative methods, sending star reporters like *Mike Wallace* and *Morley Safer* on confrontational "ambush" interviews that came to define modern broadcast reporting.
- Began his journalistic career in print as a copy boy for the *New York Herald Tribune* and served as an officer and war correspondent during [[world-war-ii]] before transitioning into early television.
