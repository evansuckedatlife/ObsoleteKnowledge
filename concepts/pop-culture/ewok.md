## summary

The **Ewok** (plural **Ewoks**) is a fictional species of diminutive, fur-covered, bipedal forest dwellers appearing within the *Star Wars* media franchise created by American filmmaker *George Lucas*. Introduced in the 1983 cinematic release *Return of the Jedi*, these tree-dwelling hunter-gatherers inhabit the temperate Forest Moon of Endor and prove instrumental in dismantling the Galactic Empire's planetary shield generator. Despite their primitive material culture and teddy-bear-like physical appearance, they are skilled guerilla fighters whose climactic triumph over a technologically advanced military force remains one of the most famous and debated narrative choices in modern science-fiction cinema.

## you gotta know

- Made their worldwide debut in *Star Wars: Episode VI – Return of the Jedi* (1983), where they worship the golden protocol droid *C-3PO* as a divine figure before allying with the Rebel Alliance.
- The word itself is never actually spoken aloud in the dialogue of *Return of the Jedi*, appearing only in the motion picture's end credits, promotional marketing campaigns, and tie-in publications.
- The species was conceived by *George Lucas* as an anagrammatic and phonetic derivative of the *Wookiee* species, whom Lucas had originally planned to feature in the climactic ground battle before deciding *Chewbacca*'s technical competence contradicted the primitive-versus-industrial dynamic.
- British actor *Warwick Davis* achieved his breakout Hollywood role at age eleven playing the inquisitive Ewok scout *Wicket W. Warrick*, taking over the central costume when senior actor *Kenny Baker* fell ill.
- The woodland combat sequences in which the tribe defeats heavily armored stormtroopers and towering *AT-ST* walkers using logs, swinging pendulums, gliders, and stone catapults were deliberately staged as an allegory for the Vietnam War.
- Sound designer *Ben Burtt* created the fictional Ewokese language by recording and manipulating phonetic segments from the Kalmyk Oirat language spoken by an elderly Russian refugee, augmented by Tibetan, Nepali, and Tagalog phrases.
- The immense popularity of the characters among younger audiences led *Lucasfilm* to produce two live-action made-for-television films, *The Ewok Adventure* (1984) and *Ewoks: The Battle for Endor* (1985), as well as an animated Saturday-morning television series.
