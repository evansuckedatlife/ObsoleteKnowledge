## summary

**Aslan** is the central divine figure and titular lion of *The Chronicles of Narnia*, a seven-book fantasy series published between 1950 and 1956 by British scholar and author C. S. Lewis. Conceived as an explicit theological representation of Jesus Christ in an alternate world, he serves as the noble, wise, and awe-inspiring King of Narnia and son of the Emperor-beyond-the-Sea. Renowned for embodying both profound grace and wild, untamable majesty, Aslan anchors the mythological, moral, and allegorical underpinnings of twentieth-century children's fantasy literature.

## you gotta know

- Stands as the only recurring character to appear in all seven volumes of C. S. Lewis's *The Chronicles of Narnia*, beginning with *The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe*.
- Functions as an imaginative incarnation of Christ, famously designed by Lewis not as an allegorical disguise but as an exploration of what Christ might be like if incarnated into a talking animal world.
- Sacrifices his life willingly on the Stone Table to atone for the treachery of Edmund Pevensie, enduring humiliation and death at the hands of the White Witch.
- Resurrects the following morning through the "Deeper Magic from Before the Dawn of Time," breaking the Stone Table and overturning the Witch's claim on Narnia.
- Is repeatedly characterized by Mr. Beaver with the famous proverb that he is "not a tame lion," yet is undeniably "good."
- Creates the world of Narnia through his celestial song in the prequel *The Magician's Nephew*, commanding life and speech into being.
- Brings about the end of the world in *The Last Battle*, presiding over the final judgment and guiding faithful souls upward and onward into his eternal country.
