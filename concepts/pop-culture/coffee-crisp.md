## summary

**Coffee Crisp** is a popular Canadian confectionery bar composed of alternating crispy vanilla wafer layers and coffee-flavored soft candy cream, enrobed in a smooth milk chocolate coating. Originally introduced in 1938 by the British confectioner *Rowntree's* through its Toronto manufacturing subsidiary, the brand became an enduring cultural touchstone and an iconic staple of northern retail shelves. Now produced globally by *Nestlé*, the treat remains famously celebrated for its subtle mocha profile and its classic advertising slogan declaring how coffee is best enjoyed.

## you gotta know

- Introduced to the Canadian market in 1938 by the York, [[england]]-based firm *Rowntree's*, adapting an earlier British product known as the *Rowntree's Wafer Crisp*.
- Distinguished structurally by its stacked interior wafers interspersed with aerated coffee-infused praline cream, which produces an airy, delicate texture compared to dense nougat candy bars.
- Promoted for decades with the ubiquitous catchphrase: *"How do you like your coffee? / I like my coffee crisp!"*, which became deeply ingrained in North American commercial lore.
- Survived the wartime commodity quotas of [[world-war-ii]], when cocoa and coffee beans were subject to rationing throughout [[british-empire]] merchant shipping routes.
- Acquired by the Swiss multinational food conglomerate *Nestlé* in 1988 during its high-profile acquisition of *Rowntree's*, preserving domestic production at a historic Toronto facility on Sterling Road.
- Formed the subject of repeated cross-border consumer petitions when displaced expatriates in the [[united-states]] successfully lobbied *Nestlé* for limited specialty import campaigns during the early 2000s.
- Spawned numerous seasonal and limited-edition variations, including French Vanilla, Raspberry Mocha, Triple Mocha, and ice cream adaptations that capitalized on its distinct flavor profile.

## connections

- [[england]] — home of the historic confectionery enterprise *Rowntree's*, which originally engineered and patented the wafer bar formula.
- [[british-empire]] — established the transoceanic trade conduits and dominion retail markets that brought English chocolate makers to Canadian factories.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — introduced automated steam confectionery machinery, pneumatic aeration, and modern packaging technologies essential for wafer production.
- [[industrialization]] — transformed artisanal chocolate crafting into high-volume, standardized packaged goods marketed to industrial urban laborers.
- [[world-war-ii]] — impacted the Atlantic ingredient supply chains, testing the resilience of domestic confectionery production across allied Commonwealth territories.
- [[united-states]] — represented an elusive cross-border retail frontier where enthusiastic devotees created a thriving gray market for imported Canadian bars.
- [[cold-war]] — spanned the mid-century consumer boom that transformed packaged snacks into ubiquitous symbols of Western domestic prosperity.
