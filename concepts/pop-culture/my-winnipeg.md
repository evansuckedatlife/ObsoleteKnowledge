## summary

**My Winnipeg** is a 2007 avant-garde documentary film directed by Canadian auteur *Guy Maddin*, who described the work as a "docu-fantasia." Blending genuine civic history, dreamlike mythology, and staged childhood autobiography, the film presents a deeply idiosyncratic portrait of his snowy, isolated hometown of Winnipeg, Manitoba. Celebrated for its unique fusion of archival techniques, surreal dark humor, and evocative melodramatic pastiche, it is widely considered one of the defining masterpieces of contemporary Canadian cinema.

## you gotta know

- Directed, co-written, and narrated by Canadian independent filmmaker *Guy Maddin*, framing the narrative as a sleepwalking traveler trying to escape his hometown.
- Coined by Maddin as a "docu-fantasia" because it boldly mixes authentic municipal chronicles with complete surrealist fabrications.
- Features iconic, macabre imagery such as frozen horse heads protruding from the icy Red River following a racetrack fire.
- Employs casting interventions in personal memoir, having legendary noir actress *Ann Savage* portray Maddin's domineering mother.
- Laments the physical and spiritual erasure of civic landmarks, centering on the demolition of the historic *Winnipeg Arena*.
- Earned the Best Canadian Feature Film award at the 2007 Toronto International Film Festival and received widespread critical acclaim internationally.
- Stylistically evokes vintage black-and-white expressionist forms, employing layered optical printing, intertitles, and rapid montage cutting.

- [[silent-film]] — aesthetic lineage directly channeled through intertitles, iris shots, and heightened theatrical acting styles.
- [[visual-metaphor]] — central artistic device turning frozen rivers, hidden railways, and urban snowscapes into psychological mirrors.
- [[soviet-montage]] — formal editing theory echoed in the collision of historic urban footage and personal reenactments.
- [[f-w-murnau]] — master of expressionist cinema whose atmospheric shadowplay heavily inspires Maddin's photographic palette.
- [[modernism]] — early twentieth-century artistic movement that questioned objective memory and linear documentary reality.
- [[mise-en-scene]] — atmospheric, dream-like staging within vintage train compartments and cramped family domestic sets.
