## summary

**The Game of Life**, commonly known simply as *Life*, is a popular American board game originally devised in 1860 by *Milton Bradley* as *The Checkered Game of Life*. Modernized in 1960 by toy designer *Reuben Klamer* to celebrate the company's centennial, the game simulates an individual's journey from early adulthood through education, career paths, marriage, homeownership, and retirement. Recognized by its iconic three-dimensional plastic board, colorful dial spinner, and tiny plastic peg passengers, it stands as one of the most commercially successful family parlor games of the modern era.

## you gotta know

- Invented originally in 1860 by lithographer *Milton Bradley* as a morality game where players advanced across a checkered board by landing on virtues like *Bravery* and avoided vices like *Ruin*.
- Redesigned in 1960 with the assistance of television personality *Art Linkletter*, shifting the objective from Victorian moral purity to postwar suburban prosperity and wealth accumulation.
- Players navigate plastic station wagons along a winding track, adding blue and pink pegs to represent a spouse and children acquired along the journey.
- Features early, consequential choices between attending college—which requires tuition debt but unlocks higher-paying white-collar professions—or entering the workforce immediately.
- Replaced traditional six-sided dice with a centerpiece numbered wheel spinner that clicks loudly as players spin for movement and payout values.
- Culminates at the retirement phase, where players calculate their assets to retire comfortably at either *Millionaire Acres* or the less affluent *Countryside Acres*.
- Inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame in 2003 in recognition of its profound influence on American leisure and consumer culture.
