## summary

**Half-Life** is a legendary 1998 science-fiction *first-person shooter* video game developed by *Valve* and published by *Sierra Studios*, which revolutionized interactive storytelling by dispensing with pre-rendered cutscenes in favor of unbroken, first-person gameplay. The title simultaneously references the physical concept of **half-life**, the characteristic decay rate at which radioactive atomic nuclei disintegrate over time, adopting the Greek letter lambda as its iconic emblem. Following theoretical physicist *Gordon Freeman* as he battles an alien invasion at a secret research facility, the game set a new benchmark for narrative immersion and gave rise to celebrated multiplayer modifications.

## you gotta know

- Revolutionized the [[first-person-shooter]] genre by keeping the player entirely in control through scripted real-time sequences, beginning with an unforgettable introductory tram ride through the sprawling *Black Mesa Research Facility*.
- Casts the silent protagonist *Gordon Freeman*, an MIT-educated physicist equipped with a protective *HEV Suit* and an iconic red crowbar, who accidentally triggers an interdimensional catastrophe known as the *Resonance Cascade*.
- Takes its name directly from the nuclear physics metric describing exponential radioactive decay, using the Greek symbol lambda, which represents the nuclear decay constant, across its branding and hazard suits.
- Spawned the legendary multiplayer tactical shooter [[counter-strike]], which originated as a community modification developed by *Minh Le* and *Jess Cliffe* before being acquired and expanded by Valve.
- Introduced groundbreaking enemy artificial intelligence, pitting the player against coordinated *HECU* military commandos who flanked corridors, threw grenades to flush out cover, and communicated tactical positions.
- Developed on the *GoldSrc* engine, a heavily modified version of the *Quake* engine created by Valve co-founders *Gabe Newell* and *Mike Harrington*.
- Followed by the critically acclaimed *Half-Life 2* in 2004, which introduced the revolutionary *Source* engine, realistic physics simulation with the *Gravity Gun*, and the dystopian alien empire known as the *Combine*.
- Concluded with enduring cliffhangers across episodic installments, leaving the long-awaited sequel *Half-Life 3* as one of the most famous pieces of vaporware folklore in modern gaming culture.
