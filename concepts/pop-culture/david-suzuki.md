## summary

**David Suzuki** is a prominent Canadian geneticist, broadcaster, and environmental activist best known for bringing ecological science into mainstream popular culture. For over four decades, he reached global audiences as the charismatic host of the television documentary series *The Nature of Things*. Recognized internationally for his warnings against biodiversity loss and unchecked climate degradation, he has been a transformative voice for science communication and environmental sustainability.

## you gotta know

- Hosted the long-running scientific educational television series *The Nature of Things* from 1979 until his retirement in 2023, translating complex ecological topics for everyday viewers.
- Endured internment alongside his Japanese-Canadian family in British Columbia during *World War II*, an experience that profoundly shaped his commitment to human rights and civil liberties.
- Earned a doctorate in zoology from the *United States* at the University of Chicago, initially conducting influential laboratory research on the genetics of the fruit fly *Drosophila melanogaster*.
- Co-founded the non-profit *David Suzuki Foundation* in 1990 to advocate for environmental preservation, public policy reform, and sustainable economic systems.
- Authored more than fifty books spanning academic genetics, children's literature, and environmental treatises such as *The Sacred Balance*.
- Awarded the prestigious *Right Livelihood Award* in 2009 for his lifelong campaign to awaken the world to the perils of climate change.
- Criticized the ecological toll of modern global *industrialization*, warning that economic models prioritizing endless extraction conflict with natural planetary limits.
