## summary

**Lights** is an electropop and synth-pop song recorded by English singer-songwriter *Ellie Goulding*, released as the sixth single from her 2010 debut album of the same name and the lead track from its reissue, *Bright Lights*. Co-written with producers *Richard Stannard* and *Ash Howes*, the track contrasts shimmering, danceable electronic production with Goulding's delicate, folk-rooted vibrato. It achieved extraordinary commercial longevity in North America, becoming a celebrated sleeper hit that helped define the early-2010s mainstream assimilation of British electronic pop.

## you gotta know

- Written as a direct personal reflection on Goulding's severe childhood *nyctophobia*, an intense fear of the dark that forced her to sleep with ambient lamps and illumination left on.
- Transformed from an earlier, slower acoustic folk ballad into an up-tempo electropop composition featuring sparkling synthesizers, a pulsing four-on-the-floor beat, and multi-tracked vocal layers.
- Achieved one of the slowest, most persistent chart ascents in American music history, taking thirty-three weeks to enter the top forty of the Billboard Hot 100 before finally peaking at number two in August 2012.
- Spent fifty-seven total weeks on the Billboard Hot 100, ranking among the longest-charting female singles in Billboard history and finishing as one of the top five performing songs of the entire year in 2012.
- Accompanied by a minimalist music video directed by *Sophie Muller*, highlighting Goulding dancing in an ethereal black void surrounded by handheld flashlights, laser beams, and kaleidoscopic prism effects.
- Gained immense traction across clubs and alternative radio stations through numerous high-profile remixes, most notably an explosive dubstep version by American electronic producer *Bassnectar*.
- Earned critical acclaim for bridging the divide between delicate British indie-folk singer-songwriter traditions and radio-friendly electronic dance music, propelling Goulding to international stardom.
- Certified multiple times platinum by the Recording Industry Association of America, selling over five million digital copies in the United States alone.
