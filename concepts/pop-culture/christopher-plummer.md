## summary

**Christopher Plummer** was a revered Canadian stage, television, and motion picture actor whose prolific career spanned seven decades of classical theater and Hollywood cinema. Widely celebrated as one of the preeminent English-speaking classical actors of the post-war era, he gained universal mainstream recognition as Captain *Georg von Trapp* in the 1965 film classic *The Sound of Music*. He earned the Triple Crown of Acting by winning an Academy Award, two Tony Awards, and two Primetime Emmy Awards, remaining an energetic and commanding performer until his death in 2021.

## you gotta know

- Famously portrayed the austere Austro-Hungarian naval officer Captain *Georg von Trapp* opposite *Julie Andrews* in *The Sound of Music* (1965), a role he initially disdained as overly sentimental before embracing its cultural legacy.
- Won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor at age eighty-two for his performance in *Beginners* (2010), playing an elderly father who comes out as gay in his final years, making him the oldest actor to win a competitive Oscar.
- Replaced *Kevin Spacey* as oil tycoon *J. Paul Getty* in Ridley Scott's *All the Money in the World* (2017) via historic last-minute reshoots just weeks before theatrical release, earning an Oscar nomination at age eighty-eight.
- Reigned for decades as a premier interpreter of *William Shakespeare*, headlining legendary productions of *Hamlet*, *King Lear*, *Macbeth*, *Othello*, and *Richard III* at the *Stratford Festival* in Ontario and *Broadway*.
- Portrayed veteran television journalist *Mike Wallace* opposite *Al Pacino* and *Russell Crowe* in Michael Mann's acclaimed corporate drama *The Insider* (1999).
- Voiced the villainous South American explorer *Charles Muntz* in the celebrated Pixar animated feature *Up* (2009).
- Quoted Shakespearean passages while playing the eyepatch-wearing Klingon commander General *Chang* in *Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country* (1991).
- Portrayed wealthy mystery novelist *Harlan Thrombey*, whose suspicious demise drives the plot of Rian Johnson's ensemble whodunit *Knives Out* (2019).
