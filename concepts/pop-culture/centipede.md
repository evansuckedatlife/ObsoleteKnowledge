## summary

**Centipede** is a landmark vertically oriented arcade shoot 'em up designed and published by *Atari* in 1981 during the golden age of arcade video games. Conceived by *Ed Logg* and programmed alongside *Dona Bailey*—one of the few female game developers in the early arcade industry—the game tasks players with piloting the "Bug Blaster" to eliminate an arthropod descending through a colorful mushroom field. With its intuitive trackball controls, fast-paced action, and vibrant visual design, it achieved massive commercial success and broke demographic barriers by attracting an exceptionally large female player base.

## you gotta know

- Programmed and co-designed by *Dona Bailey*, who was the only female engineer in *Atari*'s coin-operated division at the time, alongside veteran *Asteroids* co-creator *Ed Logg*.
- Utilized an analog *trackball* controller and a single firing button, giving players fluid two-dimensional analog movement across the bottom quadrant of the screen instead of rigid joystick constraints.
- Featured a multi-segmented titular target that winds downward through a grid of mushrooms, breaking into two independently crawling, autonomous creatures whenever a middle segment is shot.
- Populated the playfield with destructible *mushrooms*, each absorbing four shots to destroy, which dynamically altered the trajectory and descent speed of the insect segments.
- Introduced erratic secondary enemy threats, including vertically dropping *fleas* that replant mushrooms, bouncing *spiders* that eat mushrooms and threaten the player, and poisonous *scorpions* that cause segments to plunge straight down.
- Employed an eye-catching pastel color palette that shifted dramatically with each successive level wave, distinguishing its artistic design from the stark black-and-white or neon schemes of contemporary shooters.
- Inspired the successful 1982 arcade sequel *Millipede*, which expanded the formula with destructive DDT poison bombs, swarming bees, and new enemy insect behaviors.
