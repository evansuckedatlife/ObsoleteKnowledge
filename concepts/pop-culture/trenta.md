## summary

**Trenta**, widely recognized in commercial retail as the **Trenta size**, is an extra-large beverage cup volume introduced in January 2011 by the multinational coffeehouse corporation *Starbucks*. Holding thirty-one fluid ounces, or roughly nine hundred sixteen millilitres, the cup was introduced across retail stores in the *[[united-states]]* strictly for cold, iced beverages. It represents a landmark milestone in the commercial expansion of fast-casual refreshments, exemplifying twenty-first-century American portion scaling, beverage container engineering, and mass-market consumer culture.

## you gotta know

- Holds thirty-one fluid ounces (916 mL), giving the cup a liquid capacity larger than a standard seven hundred fifty millilitre bottle of wine and exceeding the average anatomical volume of the adult human stomach.
- Restricted exclusively to cold beverages such as iced coffee, cold brew, Iced Tea, and Starbucks Refreshers, with corporate policy prohibiting hot drinks or espresso-dense specialty lattes due to safety and health parameters.
- Derives its name from the Italian cardinal numeral for "thirty", extending the pseudo-Italian naming conventions popularized by former Starbucks executive *Howard Schultz* alongside Short, Tall, Grande, and Venti.
- Sparked widespread public commentary, national nutritional debates, and late-night television satire upon release, famously inspiring viral infographics comparing its physical volume to consumer items.
- Debuted across southern regional test markets in Georgia, Virginia, and Florida in early 2011 before rolling out to thousands of retail stores nationwide by mid-year.
- Engineered with a specialized wide base, reinforced translucent plastic, and thick sidewall thermoforming to maintain structural balance when loaded with substantial quantities of ice and liquid.
- Prompted rival fast-food and beverage franchises to introduce thirty-ounce and forty-ounce iced containers to compete for prolonged daytime customer consumption.
