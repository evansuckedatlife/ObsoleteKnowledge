## summary

**Hugh Laurie**, born **James Hugh Calum Laurie**, is an award-winning English actor, comedian, writer, and musician celebrated for his remarkable creative breadth across British television comedy and American network drama. He first rose to national prominence as half of the celebrated comedy double act *Fry and Laurie* alongside *Stephen Fry*, as well as playing foppish aristocrats in the classic historical sitcom *Blackadder*. He subsequently attained massive worldwide stardom and critical acclaim by portraying the misanthropic, diagnostic savant Dr. Gregory House on the long-running American television procedural *House*.

## you gotta know

- Educated at *Eton College* and *Selwyn College, Cambridge*, where he served as president of the renowned *Cambridge Footlights* theatrical club alongside *Emma Thompson* and *Stephen Fry*.
- Formed the wildly successful sketch comedy duo *A Bit of Fry & Laurie* with *Stephen Fry*, creating four series of literate, musically inventive, and absurdist British satire.
- Memorably portrayed various incarnations of dim-witted aristocrats, notably *George, Prince of Wales* and Lieutenant the Honourable George Colthurst St. John, in the television comedy series *Blackadder*.
- Masterfully depicted the well-meaning but utterly featherbrained gentleman *Bertie Wooster* in the beloved ITV period comedy series *Jeeves and Wooster*, adapted from the comic masterpieces of *P. G. Wodehouse*.
- Starred as the brilliant, limping, Vicodin-dependent medical genius Dr. Gregory House on *House*, winning two *Golden Globe Awards* and receiving six consecutive *Emmy Award* nominations.
- Achieved his career-defining American dramatic role after director *Bryan Singer* famously remarked that Laurie's audition tape was exactly the kind of *compelling American actor* he had been seeking, unaware Laurie was actually British.
- Published a best-selling spoof thriller novel entitled *The Gun Seller* in 1996 and released two successful blues music albums, *Let Them Talk* and *Didn't It Rain*.

## connections

- [[england]] — the nation where he was raised, educated at Cambridge, and spent decades anchoring the pinnacle of the domestic comedic pantheon.
- [[slapstick-comedy]] — the theatrical genre of broad physical humor that informed his energetic performances as foppish buffoons in British television.
- [[physical-comedy]] — the performance art form he honed during the *Footlights* era and famously deployed in his elastic portrayals of *Bertie Wooster*.
- [[vaudeville]] — the historic light-entertainment variety tradition whose song-and-dance sketches and musical repartee strongly influenced *A Bit of Fry & Laurie*.
- [[hollywoodland]] — the emblematic American entertainment metropolis where he reinvented himself from an English comedic icon into a decorated dramatic television leading man.
- [[humpty-dumpty]] — the quintessential English nursery character whose themes of fragile ego and linguistic absurdity mirror the high-society buffoons Laurie famously played.
- [[hogsmeade]] — the fictional Scottish village from the fantasy franchise heavily populated by his close real-world friends, university colleagues, and British dramatic co-stars.
