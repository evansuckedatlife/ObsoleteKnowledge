## summary

**Peter Mansbridge** is a British-born Canadian journalist and news anchor who served as the chief correspondent for *CBC News* and sole anchor of its flagship nightly newscast, *The National*, from 1988 until his retirement in 2017. Widely regarded as the authoritative voice of Canadian broadcast journalism across three decades, he guided viewers through historic international and domestic crises ranging from the fall of the Berlin Wall to the 9/11 attacks. His calm cadence, dignified delivery, and extensive live field reporting earned him numerous Gemini Awards and an enduring stature in North American broadcast media.

## you gotta know

- Anchored CBC's flagship nightly broadcast *The National* for nearly thirty years between 1988 and 2017, succeeding veteran newsman *Knowlton Nash*.
- Began his broadcasting career in 1968 after being discovered by a local station manager while working as a ticket agent announcing flight departures at Churchill Airport in northern Manitoba.
- Provided career-defining live coverage during major global geopolitical milestones, notably reporting directly from the collapse of the Berlin Wall during the climax of the [[cold-war]].
- Covered historic domestic turning points including the 1995 Quebec referendum, the Meech Lake Accord debates, and the 2014 shooting on Parliament Hill in Ottawa.
- Conducted extensive sit-down interviews with international leaders, including several American presidents from the [[united-states]], British prime ministers from [[england]], and Russian figures following the dissolution of the [[soviet-union]].
- Retired from *The National* on Canada Day in 2017, having earned twelve Gemini Awards and appointment as an Officer of the Order of Canada.
