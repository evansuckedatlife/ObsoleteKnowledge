## summary

**Cards Against Humanity** is an adult party card game developed by a group of Highland Park High School alumni in Chicago and financed through Kickstarter in 2011. Self-styled as "a party game for horrible people," the game adapts the core fill-in-the-blank mechanics of traditional party games like *Apples to Apples* while incorporating risqué, satirical, and dark humor. It became a viral phenomenon of tabletop gaming in the 2010s, renowned both for its subversive card combinations and its elaborate, absurdist anti-consumerist marketing campaigns.

## you gotta know

- Designed by eight friends led by *Max Temkin*, who funded its initial print run using a viral Kickstarter campaign that surpassed its funding goal by nearly three hundred percent.
- Gameplay consists of a rotating judge, termed the *Card Czar*, reading a black question or prompt card, while other players submit their funniest white answer cards face-down.
- Released under a Creative Commons license (*CC BY-NC-SA*), allowing players to download, print, and fabricate the complete game for free or modify it with custom expansions.
- Became infamous for headline-grabbing Black Friday stunts, such as selling actual pasteurized bull feces, digging a pointless hole in the ground funded by live donations, and buying a plot of land on the US-Mexico border.
- Spearheaded the proliferation of adult tabletop card games throughout the 2010s, spawning dozens of themed official expansion packs and independent clones.
- Introduced diverse micro-expansions designed by guest writers, donating millions of dollars in proceeds to educational charities and progressive non-profit foundations.
- Mechanically adapted the foundational framework of family-friendly games like *Apples to Apples*, transforming the genre into an edgy social staple of college dormitories and casual gatherings.
