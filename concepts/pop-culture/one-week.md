## summary

**One Week** is a seminal 1920 American two-reel silent comedy short written and directed by *Buster Keaton* and *Edward F. Cline*, starring Keaton alongside actress *Sybil Seely*. The film depicts a pair of newlyweds who attempt to assemble a prefabricated build-it-yourself kit house over the course of seven days, only for a jealous romantic rival to secretly alter the numbered crates. Renowned for its breathtaking mechanical stunts, geometric chaos, and iconic train collision climax, it marked Keaton's debut as an independent filmmaker and director of his own studio production unit.

## you gotta know

- Marked Keaton's first independent release after departing his apprenticeship under fellow comedy icon *Roscoe "Fatty" Arbuckle*.
- Parodied *The Home Builders* (1919), a promotional industrial film produced by the Ford Motor Company demonstrating the easy construction of portable catalog homes.
- Features a pivotal plot device in which a spurned suitor, "Handy Hank", maliciously renumbers the packing crates of the prefabricated kit home, resulting in a surreal, twisted, and asymmetrical architectural monstrosity.
- Employs an ingenious fourth-wall break where a hand reaches into the cinematic frame to cover the lens when *Sybil Seely* drops her soap while bathing.
- Showcases Keaton's legendary physical agility when a ferocious windstorm spins the lopsided home like a carousel while the inhabitants try to host a housewarming party.
- Culminates in a celebrated visual gag where the couple tows the house across railroad tracks, narrowly watching an oncoming locomotive swerve onto an adjacent spur line, only for a second train coming from the opposite direction to instantly obliterate the house.
- Added to the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress in 2008 for its cultural, historical, and aesthetic significance.
