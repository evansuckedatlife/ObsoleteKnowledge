## summary

**Philadelphia and Forrest Gump** denotes the historic mid-1990s cinematic pairing that resulted in actor *Tom Hanks* winning consecutive Academy Awards for Best Actor in 1993 and 1994. In *Jonathan Demme's* legal drama *Philadelphia*, Hanks played *Andrew Beckett*, an attorney dying of AIDS fighting unlawful workplace termination, while in *Robert Zemeckis's* sweeping epic *Forrest Gump*, he portrayed an innocent, kind-hearted Alabamian who inadvertently experiences landmark twentieth-century American milestones. This extraordinary critical and popular dual triumph cemented Hanks as one of the preeminent dramatic actors of Hollywood's modern era.

## you gotta know

- Marked only the second time in Oscar history that an actor won back-to-back Best Actor awards, matching the landmark feat first achieved by *Spencer Tracy* in 1937 and 1938.
- *Philadelphia* (1993) was one of the first mainstream Hollywood studio pictures to confront the HIV/AIDS epidemic and homophobic workplace discrimination directly, earning widespread civic praise.
- Hanks underwent an intense physical transformation for *Philadelphia*, losing over thirty pounds to realistically depict the devastating physical deterioration caused by AIDS complications.
- *Forrest Gump* (1994), adapted from the novel by *Winston Groom*, became an immense international box-office juggernaut that captured six Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director.
- The title character of *Forrest Gump* traverses pivotal events in the [[united-states]], crossing paths with historical figures during the Vietnam War, the counterculture movement, and the [[civil-rights-movement]].
- Both films generated legendary soundtrack accompaniments, notably *Bruce Springsteen's* Oscar-winning track *Streets of Philadelphia* and the multi-platinum classic-rock score of *Forrest Gump*.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — both films examine fundamental moral, social, and political transformations across twentieth-century American history.
- [[realism]] — applied in *Philadelphia* to present an unflinching, compassionate exploration of illness, legal prejudice, and human dignity.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — explicitly depicted in *Forrest Gump* during scenes recreating the desegregation of the University of Alabama.
- [[film-editing]] — *Forrest Gump* made groundbreaking use of optical digital compositing and archival montage to insert its protagonist into historical newsreels.
- [[tragedy]] — forms the poignant dramatic core of *Andrew Beckett's* battle against medical decline and social stigmatization in *Philadelphia*.
- [[american-literature]] — *Forrest Gump* originated from the picaresque American satirical tradition through *Winston Groom's* 1986 novel.
