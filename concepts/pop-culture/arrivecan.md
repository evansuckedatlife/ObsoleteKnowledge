## summary

**ArriveCAN** is a Canadian digital border and travel customs application created by the Canada Border Services Agency in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. Rolled out in April 2020 to collect mandatory health declarations, quarantine itineraries, and vaccination records from travelers entering Canada from the [[united-states]] and abroad, the platform became an obligatory gateway for cross-border transit. It subsequently became the focal point of a massive Canadian federal political scandal involving catastrophic procurement oversight, tens of millions in cost overruns, and questionable technology subcontracting.

## you gotta know

- Introduced in April 2020 as a mobile phone application and online portal to digitize customs declarations and enforce federal pandemic quarantine mandates for international travelers.
- Required travelers crossing land borders from the [[united-states]] or arriving at Canadian international airports to upload proof of vaccination and negative test results under threat of heavy fines.
- Became the subject of intense public outrage when a July 2022 software glitch erroneously issued automated quarantine orders to over ten thousand vaccinated travelers, trapping them at home.
- Sparked parliamentary hearings and a scathing 2024 report by Auditor General Karen Hogan, which determined that the project ballooned from an initial budget of eighty thousand dollars to an estimated fifty-nine million dollars.
- Relied heavily on GCstrategies, a two-person consulting firm operating out of a rural Ontario home that received millions in government commissions to subcontract IT tasks without doing actual software development.
- Prompted investigations by the Royal Canadian Mounted Police and parliamentary committees into allegations of forged resumes, rigged procurement criteria, and improper relationships between public officials and private vendors.
- Phased out its mandatory status in October 2022, after which it was transitioned into an optional border pre-clearance tool for submitting advance customs forms at major airports.
