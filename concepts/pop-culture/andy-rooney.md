## summary

**Andy Rooney** (born **Andrew Aitken Rooney**) was an American television writer, journalist, and cultural essayist best remembered for his weekly commentary segment, *A Few Minutes with Andy Rooney*, on the CBS news program *60 Minutes*. Broadcasting from his cluttered office in *New York City* to millions of households across the *United States*, he became a fixture of Sunday evening television from 1978 until his retirement in 2011. Known for his rumpled demeanor, famously bushy eyebrows, and dry, skeptical delivery, he transformed complaints about everyday domestic annoyances into an enduring form of broadcast commentary.

## you gotta know

- Anchored the closing segment of *60 Minutes* for more than three decades with *A Few Minutes with Andy Rooney*, delivering roughly 1,097 television essays between 1978 and 2011.
- Cultivated an iconic, curmudgeonly public persona characterized by wry rhetorical questions, a distinctive nasal drawl, and skeptical monologues about everyday items such as soap packaging, airline travel, neckties, and kitchen gadgets.
- Began his journalistic career during *World War II* as an infantryman and staff correspondent for the military newspaper *Stars and Stripes*, writing dispatches from combat zones across Europe.
- Documented pivotal military campaigns during *World War II*, flying on Eighth Air Force bombing runs over *Germany*, reporting from the Normandy beachhead, and being among the first journalists to witness the liberation of Nazi concentration camps.
- Worked extensively as a behind-the-scenes television and radio writer in *New York City* during the golden age of broadcasting, penning scripts for television personalities such as *Arthur Godfrey* and *Harry Reasoner*.
- Became a frequent target of affectionate parody throughout American popular culture, most famously on *Saturday Night Live*, where comedians satirized his complaints, eccentric cadence, and preoccupation with trivial matters.
- Authored more than a dozen bestselling books compiling his syndicated newspaper columns and television scripts, including *A Few Minutes with Andy Rooney*, *Pieces of My Mind*, and *Common Nonsense*.
