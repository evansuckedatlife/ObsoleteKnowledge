## summary

**Gord Downie** was the charismatic frontman, lyricist, and lead singer of the iconic Canadian rock band *The Tragically Hip*, as well as a celebrated poet and Indigenous rights activist. Born in Amherstview, Ontario, he led the band to unprecedented domestic adoration with enigmatic, fiercely literate rock anthems exploring obscure historical events, local geographies, and emotional alienation. Following a terminal diagnosis of glioblastoma, he spent his final months rallying the Canadian public toward reconciliation with Indigenous peoples before his passing in 2017.

## you gotta know

- Fronted *The Tragically Hip* from their formation in Kingston, Ontario in 1984 through albums such as *Up to Here*, *Road Apples*, *Fully Completely*, and *Day for Night*.
- Renowned for an electrifying, erratic stage presence marked by stream-of-consciousness soliloquies, frantic dancing, handkerchief tossing, and megaphone rants during live concerts.
- Wove historical, cultural, and geographic specifics into rock anthems, such as the wrongful conviction of *David Milgaard* (*Wheat Kings*), the disappearance of hockey player *Bill Barilko* (*Fifty-Mission Cap*), and the prison break of the *Kingston Penitentiary* (*38 Years Old*).
- Diagnosed with terminal brain cancer (glioblastoma) in late 2015, prompting the band to embark on the emotional *Man Machine Poem* summer tour across Canada in 2016.
- The band's final concert in their hometown of Kingston on August 20, 2016, was broadcast commercial-free by the CBC, drawing an estimated 11.7 million viewers—nearly a third of Canada's population.
- Dedicated his remaining time to *Secret Path*, a solo multimedia concept album, graphic novel, and animated film detailing the tragic life and death of *Chanie Wenjack*, a twelve-year-old Ojibwe boy who died fleeing an Indian residential school.
- Named to the Order of Canada and honored by the Assembly of First Nations with the Lakota name *Wicapi Omani* ("Man Who Walks Among the Stars") for his reconciliation work.
