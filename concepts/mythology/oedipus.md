## summary

**Oedipus** was a legendary king of *Thebes*, whose life embodies the tragedy of human struggle against an inescapable fate. Born into a curse through no fault of his own, he unknowingly fulfilled a terrible prophecy by murdering his father and marrying his mother, then drove himself into exile upon learning the horrifying truth. His story stands as ancient literature's supreme meditation on the limits of human agency—no matter how cleverly one resists destiny, the very efforts to escape it often hasten its fulfillment.

## you gotta know

- According to the prophecy given to his father King Laius, Oedipus was fated to murder his father and wed his mother; his parents abandoned him as an infant to escape this doom.
- Raised in ignorance by a shepherd, Oedipus grew up believing himself the son of King Polybus of Corinth, never suspecting his true parentage.
- Confronted by a drunk who called him a bastard, Oedipus sought the Oracle at Delphi and learned he was fated to slay his father and marry his mother.
- Fleeing what he thought was his home in Corinth, Oedipus encountered a man at a crossroads (his true father Laius), quarreled with him, and killed him in the struggle.
- Arriving at *Thebes*, he encountered the *Sphinx*, a monster that posed a riddle to all travelers and devoured those who failed; Oedipus alone solved it, destroying the beast and saving the city.
- As reward for his heroism, the grateful Thebans made him king and gave him the hand of the widowed Queen Jocasta in marriage—not knowing she was his biological mother.
- Years later, plague and pestilence descended on *Thebes*; investigations revealed that King Laius's murder remained unavenged, bringing divine punishment upon the land.
- Recognizing the horrifying truth—that he had murdered his father and fathered children with his mother—Oedipus blinded himself in anguish and went into exile as a broken outcast.
