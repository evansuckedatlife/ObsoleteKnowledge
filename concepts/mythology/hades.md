## summary

**Hades** is the Olympian god of the underworld, death, and the dead in Greek and Roman mythology, ruling over the souls of the deceased from his shadowy subterranean palace. One of the three paramount gods (alongside *Zeus* and *Poseidon*), Hades is often portrayed as less capricious and more austere than his brothers—less interested in human affairs than in maintaining the boundaries between the living and the dead. He is wealthy in precious minerals and is associated with fertility as well as death through his abduction of *Persephone*.

## you gotta know

- One of the three cosmic rulers: Zeus governs the sky, Poseidon the sea, Hades the underworld—each supreme in their realm, though Zeus holds ultimate authority.
- Rules over all the dead without exception; souls of the deceased descend to his domain regardless of their virtue or vice, making him the most impartial of gods—death, for him, is no respecter of persons.
- Married *Persephone*, daughter of *Demeter*, whom he abducted into the underworld; the myth explains the seasons: Persephone spends half the year with Hades, causing Demeter's grief and winter's dormancy.
- Guarded by *Cerberus*, a three-headed dog, who prevents the living from entering and the dead from leaving; Cerberus is the most famous gatekeeper in mythology and embodies Hades' function as boundary-keeper.
- The name "Hades" refers both to the god and the place (the underworld itself); Roman equivalents include *Pluto* and *Dis*, used interchangeably in later literature.
- Generally portrayed as stern, grim, and not easily moved by prayer; unlike Zeus and other gods, he does not favor mortals with gifts or alter his judgments; his realm is governed by inexorable law.
- Associated with wealth and precious metals mined from the earth, giving him a darker economic aspect; in some fertility cults, he is a chthonic (earth-dwelling) god of grain and agricultural bounty beneath the surface.

## connections

- [[zeus]] — his elder brother, the supreme god; Hades submits to Zeus' authority but rules independently in his own realm.
- [[poseidon]] — the third brother; where Poseidon is volatile and vengeful, Hades is distant and implacable.
- [[persephone]] — his wife, abducted into the underworld; their marriage links him to seasonal cycles and fertility despite his domain of death.
- [[cerberus]] — the guardian beast; Cerberus serves Hades and embodies his role as keeper of boundaries between living and dead.
- [[the-aeneid]] — *Aeneas* descends to Hades' underworld in Book VI, encountering the god's realm and its denizens; Hades looms as the ultimate destination.
- [[underworld]] — Hades' domain; the realm of the dead in Greek imagination, a place of shade, memory, and immutable law.
- [[death]] — Hades represents the personification and deification of death itself; he is death's sovereign lord.
