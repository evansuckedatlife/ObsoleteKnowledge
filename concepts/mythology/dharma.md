## summary

**Dharma** is a foundational Sanskrit concept in both Hinduism and Buddhism referring to cosmic law, moral duty, and the righteous order of the universe. In Hindu mythology, *Dharma* is not merely an abstract principle but the very force that *Vishnu* embodies and actively sustains through his incarnations. It represents the ethical and social obligations that bind all beings to their proper roles in the cosmic order.

## you gotta know

- A Sanskrit term with multiple overlapping meanings: cosmic law, righteousness, duty, moral obligation, and religious practice central to all Indian philosophy.
- In Hindu philosophy, *Dharma* is one of the four aims of life (*Purusharthas*), alongside wealth, desire, and liberation (*Artha*, *Kama*, and *Moksha*).
- *Vishnu* is directly associated with upholding and restoring *Dharma* whenever cosmic chaos threatens to overtake it, making this his fundamental role in the universe.
- Each individual is believed to have their own *Dharma* based on their caste, age, stage of life, and social role—called *Svadharma* (one's own specific duty).
- *Sita* in the *Ramayana* is portrayed as the supreme embodiment of *Dharma*, especially feminine duty, virtue, and unwavering loyalty despite suffering.
- *Rama's* entire heroic mission in the *Ramayana* is the restoration of *Dharma* against the chaos of demonic rule, establishing the precedent for righteous action.
- In Buddhism, *Dharma* refers to the Buddha's teachings and the universal law of causality that governs all existence, though with slightly different emphasis than Hinduism.
- Failure to follow one's *Dharma* is believed to generate negative *Karma* and perpetuate the cycle of rebirth, making adherence to duty cosmically significant.

## connections

- [[vishnu]] — the deity whose primary cosmic function is the protection and restoration of *Dharma*.
- [[rama]] — *Vishnu's* incarnation whose central narrative is the restoration of *Dharma* against evil.
- [[sita]] — the character who embodies *Dharma* through perfect adherence to duty and righteousness.
- [[buddhism]] — the religion that adopted and reinterpreted *Dharma* as the Buddha's teachings and universal law.
- [[ramayana]] — the epic poem centered on the restoration of *Dharma* through *Rama's* victory.
- [[karma]] — the cosmic principle of cause and effect that operates within the framework of *Dharma*.
- [[hinduism]] — the religious framework in which *Dharma* functions as a foundational ethical and metaphysical principle.
