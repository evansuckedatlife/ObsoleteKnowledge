## summary

**Horses** occupy an extraordinary and sacred status across world mythology and folklore, embodying speed, nobility, divine power, and liminal travel between cosmic realms. From chariot steeds carrying solar deities across the heavens to supernatural winged beasts and monstrous water spirits, equines serve as indispensable companions to gods and epic heroes alike. Their symbolic roles reflect both the historical impact of equestrian culture and spiritual beliefs regarding war, destiny, and psychopompic journeys.

## you gotta know

- *Pegasus*, the winged stallion foaled from the severed neck of *Medusa*, carried thunderbolts for *Zeus* and helped heroes like *Bellerophon* defeat monsters.
- *Sleipnir*, the eight-legged steed sired by *Loki* and ridden by *Odin*, could gallop across air, sea, and directly into the underworld realm of *Hel*.
- In Greek myth, *Poseidon* served as the creator and tamer of equines, bearing the cult title *Poseidon Hippios* and fathering immortal steeds like *Arion*.
- *Chariot of the Sun* myths across diverse cultures, such as the Greek god *Apollo* or the Norse goddess *Sol*, depict celestial steeds pulling the sun disk through the heavens every day.
- *Balius* and *Xanthus*, immortal talking steeds gifted to *Peleus* and driven by *Achilles* in the Trojan War, prophesied *Achilles*' impending doom in *The Iliad*.
- In Hindu myth, the seven-headed flying horse *Uchchaihshravas* emerged during the churning of the milk ocean (*Samudra Manthan*) and became the mount of *Indra*.
- The *Ashvamedha*, or horse sacrifice, stood as the supreme royal ritual in Vedic tradition, affirming a monarch's undisputed sovereign supremacy over neighboring realms.
- Celtic traditions revere *Epona*, a protector goddess of equines and cavalry, while terrifying folkloric water spirits like the *kelpie* lured travelers to drown in Scottish lochs.
