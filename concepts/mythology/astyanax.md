## summary

**Astyanax** was the infant son of *Hector* and *Andromache*, prince of Troy who never reached manhood. His brief appearance in the *Iliad* is profoundly tragic: a child caught in war he cannot comprehend, whose fate symbolizes Troy's ultimate destruction. After the city falls, his death becomes emblematic of the war's cruelty and the erasure of Troy's future.

## you gotta know

- His name *Astyanax* means "lord of the city" in Greek, also called *Scamandrius* after the river near Troy—titles of hope and promise that his short life would never fulfill.
- Born into the royal Trojan family during the decade-long war, he appears in the *Iliad* only briefly, most memorably in the scene where his father *Hector* says goodbye to his family before battle.
- Famously frightened by his father's war helmet and its Gorgon plume, crying in his nurse's arms—a poignant image showing the human cost of war and innocence confronted with symbols of violence.
- His fate was sealed when Troy fell after the wooden horse strategy; accounts vary on his death: some say *Achilles's* son *Pyrrhus* threw him from the walls, others name *Achilles* directly or *Menelaus*.
- His death was considered particularly abhorrent even by ancient standards, representing the Greeks' ruthless and deliberate elimination of Troy's entire royal lineage to prevent future revenge or challenges to Greek dominance.
- His mother *Andromache*, left without husband or son, becomes a captive and slave after his death, forced to serve *Pyrrhus* and later pass to *Helenus*—symbolizing the total destruction of her family.
- Later Roman tradition, particularly in *Virgil's Aeneid*, posthumously gives Astyanax a son named *Ascanius* who escapes Troy with *Aeneas* and becomes the progenitor of Rome—a mythological bridge linking Trojan destiny to Roman greatness.

## connections

- [[hector]] — his father, the greatest Trojan warrior, whose death in combat precedes and leaves *Astyanax* defenseless against the victorious Greeks.
- [[andromache]] — his mother, whose grief and mourning of both *Hector* and *Astyanax* defines her character and becomes emblematic of war's cost on women.
- [[the-iliad]] — the epic poem in which his brief, poignant life and the farewell scene with *Hector* appears, marking his innocence against warfare.
- [[the-trojan-war]] — the decade-long conflict that defined and ultimately ended his short existence before he could come of age.
- [[troy]] — the city-state whose future, lineage, and hope for continuation died with his murder by the Greeks.
- [[achilles]] — the greatest Greek warrior whose son *Pyrrhus* (also called *Neoptolemus*) threw *Astyanax* from the walls in different accounts of his death.
