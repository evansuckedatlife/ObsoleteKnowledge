## summary

**Ares** is the Olympian god of war, bloodlust, and violent chaos in Greek mythology. Unlike *Athena*, who represents strategic wisdom in combat, *Ares* embodies raw aggression, brutality, and the destructive aspects of warfare. He is portrayed as cowardly despite his strength, often bested by heroes and even by other gods, making him a figure of contempt as much as fear in the Greek pantheon.

## you gotta know

- Son of *Zeus* and *Hera*, making him a full Olympian, yet despised by his own father for his viciousness and lack of martial wisdom.
- Represents the bloodlust, chaos, and slaughter of battle—the carnage and panic of warfare—distinct from *Athena*, who embodies tactical military strategy and righteous victory.
- Appears repeatedly in the *Iliad* as both a fierce combatant and a buffoonish, whining figure who complains of wounds and runs from the battlefield when injured.
- Famously wounded in combat by the mortal hero *Diomedes*, who was aided by *Athena* to pierce his divine body—a humiliation highlighting his inferiority to the war goddess.
- Had a renowned affair with *Aphrodite*, goddess of love, discovered when her husband *Hephaestus* trapped them in a magic net; their union produced the fearful deities *Phobos* (fear) and *Deimos* (panic).
- Typically depicted as hot-tempered, brutal, aggressive, and rash—lacking the dignity, wisdom, and restraint valued by Greek culture, making him a figure of ridicule despite his power.
- Identified with the Roman god *Mars*, though significantly Mars was treated with far greater respect as a symbol of disciplined military valor and civic duty rather than mere brutality.
- Sacred animals included the serpent and the vulture; the color red, symbolizing blood, was closely associated with his violent and destructive nature throughout mythology.

## connections

- [[zeus]] — his father, the king of the gods, who frequently expresses disdain and disgust for him despite his divine status and power.
- [[athena]] — the rival goddess of war, representing strategic wisdom and disciplined victory versus *Ares's* brutal bloodlust and chaotic destruction.
- [[aphrodite]] — his forbidden divine lover, with whom he fathered *Phobos* (fear) and *Deimos* (panic), two powerful aspects of warfare.
- [[diomedes]] — the mortal hero who wounded *Ares* in the *Iliad*, with direct aid from *Athena*, proving a mere human could overcome him.
- [[the-iliad]] — the epic poem of the Trojan War where *Ares* plays a prominent role alongside other deities but is portrayed as shameful and ineffectual.
- [[hephaestus]] — the craftsman god who publicly trapped *Ares* and *Aphrodite* in an unbreakable bronze net, exposing them to the mockery of all Olympians.
