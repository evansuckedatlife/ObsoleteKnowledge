# Artemis

## summary

In *Greek mythology*, **Artemis** (called *Diana* by the Romans) is the goddess of the hunt, wild animals, and the moon, and the twin sister of *Apollo*, god of music and the sun. Born on the island of *Delos* alongside her brother, Artemis is one of the twelve Olympian deities, venerated as a virgin goddess who fiercely protects her independence and that of her followers.

## you gotta know

- Artemis and *Apollo* are fraternal twins born to *Zeus* and *Leto* on the island of *Delos*; this sacred isle became a major religious center honoring both deities.
- She is typically depicted as a young woman with a bow and arrow, accompanied by a deer or hounds; her domain is wilderness, hunting, and the protection of wild animals, making her a paradoxical figure of both violence (the hunt) and nurture.
- Artemis is the sworn virgin goddess; she famously rejected all suitors and requests for marriage, asking her father Zeus to grant her eternal virginity and a band of virgin attendants (the Amazons or nymphs).
- She serves as protector of young women and childbirth; girls entering adulthood were initiated into her cult, and she was invoked during labor, despite being herself always virginal.
- Artemis was venerated across Greece but had particularly important sanctuaries at Delos (her birthplace) and at Ephesus in *Anatolia*, where the Temple of Artemis at Ephesus was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.
- In mythology, she is fiercely protective of her virginity and those of her followers; she punishes those who transgress against her, most famously transforming the hunter *Actaeon* into a stag after he glimpsed her bathing.
- The moon is her celestial realm; she drives her silver chariot across the night sky, and she is often conflated with *Selene*, the personified moon goddess, and later with *Hecate*, goddess of magic and the crossroads.
