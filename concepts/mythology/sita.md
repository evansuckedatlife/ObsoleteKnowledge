## summary

In *Hindu mythology*, particularly the *Ramayana* epic, **Sita** is the virtuous princess and wife of *Rama*, whose abduction by the demon-king *Ravana* becomes the central plot driving the entire narrative. Her rescue and restoration becomes *Hanuman*'s defining heroic quest and greatest service, and her steadfast devotion amid trials makes her an icon of feminine virtue and loyalty in Hindu culture.

## you gotta know

- Sita is born from the earth itself (her name means "furrow"), a divine gift to King Janaka; she is renowned for her beauty, intelligence, and unwavering virtue, establishing her as an ideal of womanhood in Hindu thought.
- She marries *Rama* in a contest (*swayamvara*), choosing him from among assembled princes; their union is idealized as a perfect partnership, though tested by separation and scandal.
- When *Rama*, his brother *Lakshman*, and Sita are exiled to the forest for fourteen years, *Ravana* (the ten-headed demon-king of Lanka) sees Sita and abducts her, taking her across the ocean to his island fortress.
- *Hanuman*, the devoted follower of Rama, undertakes a heroic journey to Lanka to find Sita; he crosses the ocean, infiltrates Ravana's palace, discovers Sita in captivity, and reports back to Rama—his quest becomes the pivot of the narrative.
- Sita endures captivity and refuses Ravana's advances, maintaining her fidelity to Rama despite being alone and far from home; her steadfast virtue during this trial is central to her characterization.
- After Rama defeats and kills Ravana in battle, he rescues Sita, but questions her fidelity after her long captivity; Sita must undergo an *agni pariksha* (ordeal by fire) to prove her purity—a troubling moment in the text that has generated centuries of interpretation.
- Sita represents dharma (righteousness) and feminine virtue in Hindu ethics; her story has been performed, recited, and celebrated across India for thousands of years, influencing ideals of motherhood, wifehood, and devotion.
