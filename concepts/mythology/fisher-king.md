## summary

The **Fisher King**, also known as the **Wounded King** or **Roi Pêcheur**, is a legendary monarch in Arthurian romance who serves as the keeper of the Holy Grail. Afflicted by an agonizing, incurable wound to his groin or thighs, his physical suffering mystically incapacitates his realm, transforming it into a barren *Wasteland*. His plight represents the ultimate spiritual test for chivalric questers of the [[round-table]], whose compassion and sacred questioning are required to restore life to the king and his devastated lands.

## you gotta know

- Suffers from an unhealing wound in his thighs or groin, traditionally inflicted by a holy weapon such as the bleeding Lance of Longinus or the *Dolorous Stroke* delivered by *Sir Balin*.
- His bodily impairment magically blights his kingdom into the *Terre Gaste* or *Wasteland*, where crops fail to grow, livestock perish, and women are rendered infertile until his restoration.
- Earned his title because his infirmity prevents him from riding horses, leaving quiet angling from a small boat near his hidden castle of *Corbenic* as his only physical solace.
- First introduced in *Chrétien de Troyes*'s 12th-century Old French romance *Perceval, the Story of the Grail*, where *Perceval* visits the castle but fails to ask the requisite question: "Whom does one serve with the Grail?"
- Rendered as **Anfortas** in *Wolfram von Eschenbach*'s epic *Parzival*, which reinterprets the Grail as a celestial stone (*lapsit exillis*) brought to earth by angels and guarded by the sovereign.
- Integrated into the Christianized Grail cycle of the *Vulgate* and *Sir Thomas Malory*'s *Le Morte d'Arthur* as *King Pelles*, the descendant of *Joseph of Arimathea* and grandfather of *Sir Galahad*.
- Healed only when the purest knight—variously *Perceval* in early traditions or *Galahad* in later Vulgate romances—successfully achieves the Grail and applies the holy lance's blood to the wound.
- Provided the thematic foundation for 20th-century literature through *Jessie Weston*'s study *From Ritual to Romance*, directly inspiring *T. S. Eliot*'s landmark poem *The Waste Land*.

## connections

- [[king-arthur]] — sovereign whose knights depart Camelot on the catastrophic spiritual quest to locate the king's Grail castle.
- [[round-table]] — chivalric fellowship whose members are tested and spiritually judged against the mystery of the wounded monarch.
- [[lancelot]] — Arthur's champion who visits Corbenic in a state of enchantment and fathers Galahad upon the king's daughter *Elaine*.
- [[merlin]] — prophet and architect of Arthur's court whose foreknowledge anticipates the lineage of the Grail-bearer and the healer of the king.
- [[christianity]] — religious foundation that infused the Fisher King's court with Eucharistic mysticism, the Holy Chalice, and the Longinus relic.
- [[france]] — medieval territory where courtly poets first adapted Celtic cauldron folklore into the canonical romance of the wounded ruler.
- [[modernism]] — 20th-century literary movement that appropriated the myth of the wounded sovereign to symbolize cultural decay after warfare.
