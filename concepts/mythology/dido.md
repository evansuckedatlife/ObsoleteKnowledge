## summary

**Dido**, also called **Elissa**, was a legendary queen and founder of Carthage who appears in *Virgil's Aeneid* as a tragic figure at the intersection of love and duty. Fleeing her brother's tyranny in Phoenicia, she established Carthage as a mighty city-state, only to fall in love with the wandering hero *Aeneas*. His departure to fulfill his destiny in Italy leads to her suicide, a moment that crystallizes the conflict between personal happiness and historical necessity.

## you gotta know

- Originally a Phoenician princess, sister to King *Pygmalion* of Tyre, whom she fled after he murdered her husband *Acerbas* (or *Sychaeus*) to seize his wealth and lands.
- Founded Carthage through strategic ingenuity: she negotiated with the local Libyan king for "as much land as a bull's hide could cover," then cut the hide into narrow strips and enclosed a vast territory.
- Met *Aeneas* when his shipwrecked fleet was blown off course to her port; the two became passionate lovers, and Dido believed *Aeneas* would marry her, stay, and help defend her city.
- *Aeneas*, reminded by *Mercury* (*Hermes*) of his divinely ordained destiny to reach Italy and found Rome, reluctantly abandoned *Dido* to continue his voyage—prioritizing fate over love.
- Her suicide—depicted variously as self-immolation on a funeral pyre or death by her own sword—became one of Western literature's defining tragic moments and the climax of the *Aeneid*.
- Represents the fatal incompatibility between romantic love and heroic duty, exploring how destiny demands sacrifice; *Aeneas's* choice honors historical greatness but destroys personal happiness.
- Later historical tradition sometimes conflates her legendary biography with the actual archaeological evidence of Carthage's founding (9th century BCE), blending mythology with ancient Near Eastern history.
- Inspired centuries of later retellings: Ovid's *Heroides*, opera by Purcell and Berlioz, and modern adaptations that often give her more agency and allow her to survive or flourish.

## connections

- [[aeneas]] — the hero whose romantic love for her and subsequent abandonment define her tragic arc and moral transformation.
- [[the-aeneid]] — the Roman epic poem in which her story achieves its most famous literary expression and becomes the subject of intense emotional conflict.
- [[carthage]] — the powerful Mediterranean city-state whose founding, growth, and destiny she single-handedly accomplished through ingenuity and determination.
- [[phoenicia]] — her ancient homeland and cultural origin in the eastern Mediterranean, from which she fled tyranny to establish new greatness.
- [[tragic-love]] — the thematic center of her narrative, exploring how personal romantic desires conflict with larger historical forces and responsibilities.
- [[fate-and-destiny]] — the inexorable forces that tear her and *Aeneas* apart, requiring sacrifice and determining historical outcomes over individual happiness.
