## summary

A **creation myth** (or **cosmogony**) is a symbolic narrative explaining how the universe, Earth, life, and humankind began within a given tradition. Found in virtually every culture across human history, these foundational accounts address universal existential questions concerning divine order, cosmic purpose, and humanity's relationship with the sacred. In comparative mythology and quiz bowl, understanding creation accounts—from primordial waters and cosmic eggs to generational conflicts and primeval voids—provides essential context for the hierarchies, genealogies, and cultural themes of world mythologies.

## you gotta know

- *Ex nihilo* ("out of nothing") accounts, central to [[judaism]] and [[christianity]], depict a supreme deity willing the universe into existence through divine speech, contrasting with mythic traditions that require pre-existing matter.
- Primordial waters and chaotic voids represent the most common starting conditions globally, seen in the Egyptian emergence of the sun god [[ra]] from *Nun* and the Babylonian *Enuma Elish*, where *Marduk* slays the saltwater serpent *Tiamat* to fashion heaven and earth.
- In [[ancient-greece]], *Hesiod*'s *Theogony* traces the cosmos from an initial yawning abyss called *Chaos*, which generated primeval beings such as *Gaia* (Earth), *Tartarus*, and *Eros*, beginning a succession of generational conflicts culminating in the rule of [[zeus]].
- Norse cosmogony describes the collision of icy *Niflheim* and fiery *Muspelheim* in the great void *Ginnungagap*, yielding the primeval frost giant *Ymir*; [[odin]] and his brothers subsequently dismembered *Ymir* to fashion the earth, oceans, and dome of the sky.
- Cosmic egg and anatomical dismemberment motifs appear across Asian and Indo-European cosmologies, including the Chinese narrative of *Pangu* emerging from an egg and the Vedic *Purusha Sukta*, where deities associated with [[vishnu]] sacrifice the primeval giant *Purusha* to construct the cosmos.
- Earth-diver myths, widespread across North American Indigenous and Siberian folklore, depict a creator deity sending animals—frequently a muskrat, turtle, or duck—into endless primeval waters to retrieve a speck of mud that expands into the habitable continent.
- Emergence myths, particularly prominent among Southwestern Native nations such as the *Hopi* and *Navajo*, describe humanity and spirit beings ascending sequentially through stratified underground worlds before emerging into the present fourth or fifth world.
- World-parent separation myths portray primordial earth and sky deities locked in an embrace until separated by their divine children, exemplified by the Egyptian air god *Shu* separating *Geb* and *Nut*, and the Maori narrative of *Tāne* cleaving *Ranginui* from *Papatūānuku*.

## connections

- [[ra]] — Egyptian solar deity who arose upon the primordial *benben* mound to initiate cosmic creation and fashion the Ennead.
- [[odin]] — Norse Allfather who slew the frost giant *Ymir* to construct Midgard and breathed life into the first humans, *Ask* and *Embla*.
- [[zeus]] — Olympian thunder god whose victory over the Titans established permanent cosmic order and stabilized the Greek mythological universe.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Mediterranean culture whose primary cosmogonic framework was codified in the foundational epic poetry of *Hesiod*.
- [[vishnu]] — Hindu preserver deity who slumbers upon the serpent *Shesha* between cosmic cycles before *Brahma* emerges to manifest existence.
- [[judaism]] — ancient religious tradition featuring the influential *Genesis* account of a six-day cosmic creation ordered by divine command.
- [[middle-east]] — ancient region whose mythic traditions produced the Babylonian *Enuma Elish* and other foundational chaoskampf narratives.
