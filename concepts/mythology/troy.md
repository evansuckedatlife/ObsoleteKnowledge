## summary

**Troy** was an ancient city in what is now northwest Turkey, the legendary center of the Trojan War immortalized in *Homer's* *Iliad*. It stands as the boundary between myth and archaeology—a site whose prominence in Greek legend has been verified by modern excavation. The city represents the archetypal tragic battlefield: a place where heroes clash, cities fall, and empires are redrawn.

## you gotta know

- Home to King *Priam*, his son *Hector*, and the royal family during the Bronze Age collapse.
- Besieged for ten years by a Greek coalition over the abduction of *Helen* by the Trojan prince *Paris*.
- *Hector* served as Troy's greatest defender and the symbol of its doomed nobility, commanding respect even from his enemies.
- The city was ultimately destroyed not by military defeat but by the deception of the Trojan Horse—a massive wooden structure crafted by *Odysseus* and the Greeks, which the Trojans brought inside their walls, allowing soldiers to emerge and slay the city's defenders.
- *Achilles*, the invincible Greek warrior, was killed near Troy's walls by *Paris* with an arrow to the heel, the one vulnerable spot on his body.
- Archaeological evidence confirms multiple settlements at the site in modern-day Hisarlik, Turkey, making Troy one of history's most excavated cities and validating what scholars long believed was only legend.
- The fall of Troy marked the end of the Late Bronze Age and the decline of Mycenaean civilization, representing a real historical turning point in Mediterranean history.
- The Trojan War involved the mobilization of nearly all Greek city-states, demonstrating the scale and significance of the conflict in ancient mythology and likely historical memory.
