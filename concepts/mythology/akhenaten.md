## summary

**Akhenaten**, born *Amenhotep IV*, was an 18th Dynasty Egyptian pharaoh (r. 1353–1336 BCE) who fundamentally disrupted Egyptian religion by elevating *Aten*, the sun disk, to supreme and exclusive worship. He abandoned the polytheistic tradition of the priesthood and established a new capital city dedicated to his revolutionary theology. His radical religious reform failed after his death, but he remains a pivotal figure in the history of religious thought and one of history's most controversial reformers.

## you gotta know

- Changed his birth name from *Amenhotep IV* to *Akhenaten* ("beneficial to the Aten") around year 5 of his reign to reflect his complete religious transformation and identification with his god.
- Elevated the *Aten* (sun disk) from a minor deity to the sole god of Egypt, replacing the traditional polytheistic system, creating what many scholars consider the world's first coherent monotheistic religion.
- Moved the royal capital from Thebes to *Akhetaten* (modern-day Amarna), a purpose-built city constructed in less than a decade and dedicated entirely to the worship and artistic celebration of the Aten.
- Broke radically with the powerful priesthood of *Amun* at Thebes, stripping them of vast wealth, temple lands, and religious authority—a direct threat to their institutional power and a source of enduring opposition.
- His reign lasted only about 17 years (c. 1353–1336 BCE); after his death, the priesthood systematically erased records of him, defaced his images, and restored traditional polytheism with deliberate finality.
- Married the renowned *Nefertiti*, whose iconic limestone bust became one of the most recognizable artifacts of ancient Egypt; he portrayed their family intimately in revolutionary relief art showing paternal affection.
- His artistic legacy revolutionized Egyptian portraiture and representation, introducing unprecedented naturalism and domestic scenes that departed radically from centuries of formal, hieratic tradition.
- His successor *Tutankhamun* (initially *Tutankhaten*) reversed his reforms, returning Egypt to traditional gods; later kings attempted to erase Akhenaten from history, viewing him as a heretic and religious criminal.
