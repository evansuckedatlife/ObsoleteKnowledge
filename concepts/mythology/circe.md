## summary

**Circe** is a divine enchantress in Homer's *Odyssey*, a goddess or semi-divine being who inhabits an isolated island where she transforms men into animals through magic and sorcery. When *Odysseus's* men land on her island, she enchants and metamorphoses them into swine. *Circe* becomes both obstacle and ally: she initially traps the hero, but ultimately becomes his lover, advisor, and guide, providing crucial prophecies for his journey home.

## you gotta know

- Daughter of *Helios* (the sun god) and the *Oceanid* *Persei*, born of pure divine lineage yet dwelling in complete isolation on the mythical island of *Aeaea* in the Western Ocean.
- Famously transforms *Odysseus's* men into swine by mixing enchantments into wine and food—a classical symbol of dehumanization through vice and loss of civilized humanity.
- Defeated when *Odysseus*, warned by *Hermes* with the protective herb *moly*, confronts her with his sword, forcing her to reverse the spell and recognize him as her equal.
- After her defeat, she becomes *Odysseus's* lover, and the two cohabitate for a full year while she helps him recover physically and emotionally from his arduous ten-year journey.
- Provides crucial prophecies about the dangers ahead: the *Sirens* (dangerous singers), *Scylla* and *Charybdis* (sea monsters), and the *Cattle of the Sun* (sacred cattle whose theft brings divine wrath).
- Her name has entered Western literature and language as a metaphor for a temptress or enchantress who lures men through seduction to their destruction and moral degradation.
- Later tradition, contrary to Homer, makes her the mother of *Telemachus* (by *Odysseus*) and the mother of *Latinus* (by *Aeneas*)—expansions that complicate her genealogy.
- Represents a feminine power that is simultaneously erotic, magical, domesticated, and dangerous—a figure embodying transformation and transgression that fascinates as much as it threatens.

## connections

- [[odysseus]] — the hero who initially resists her magic but then partners with her as lover and advisor for a year on her island.
- [[the-odyssey]] — the epic poem in which her enchanted island of *Aeaea* forms one of the journey's most famous and sexually charged episodes.
- [[hermes]] — the messenger god who warns *Odysseus* in advance with the protective herb *moly* and aids his resistance against her sorcery.
- [[sirens]] — fellow supernatural women whose deadly songs and seduction threaten *Odysseus* after he leaves *Circe's* island, tested by her prophecies.
- [[magic-and-transformation]] — the thematic power she embodies through metamorphosis and enchantment in the mythological world's understanding of feminine power.
- [[penelope]] — *Odysseus's* mortal wife and true queen, displaced and betrayed by *Circe* in his affections during his long journey home to *Ithaca*.
