## summary

**Telemachus** is the son of *Odysseus* and *Penelope*, introduced in Homer's *Odyssey* as a young man on the threshold of adulthood. While his father endures a decade-long journey home from Troy, *Telemachus* grows up in his absence, harassed by suitors consuming his inheritance and pressuring his mother to remarry. His own journey of self-discovery—seeking news of his father and reclaiming his inheritance—parallels *Odysseus's* voyage and emphasizes the costs of war on the next generation.

## you gotta know

- An infant (mere weeks or months old) when *Odysseus* left for the Trojan War, and a young man of approximately twenty when the *Odyssey* begins, knowing his father only through stories and legend.
- Tormented by his mother's increasingly aggressive suitors, who occupy his father's palace, consume his family's livestock and resources, and publicly demand she choose one of them to marry.
- Lacks the authority to expel the suitors despite being the nominal prince; his youth and uncertain paternity (some suitors claim *Odysseus* died at Troy) undermine his legitimacy.
- Inspired by the goddess *Athena* (disguised as Mentes), he departs *Ithaca* to seek news of his father, traveling first to *Pylos* to consult the elderly king *Nestor*, then to *Sparta* to visit *Menelaus*.
- Learns from these encounters that *Odysseus* is alive and that the goddess *Calypso* holds him captive on an island—crucial knowledge that transforms his understanding of his duty and heritage.
- Returns to Ithaca just as *Odysseus* arrives in disguise, and together they orchestrate the slaughter of the suitors in a bloody climax, with *Telemachus* fighting alongside his father as a man come of age.
- Participates actively in the massacre of the suitors, asserting his rightful claim to his father's throne, inheritance, and dignity, and finally becoming the adult prince he could not be in his father's absence.
- In some later accounts (post-Homeric), he becomes a founder of new cities or marries *Circe's* daughter *Cassiope*, extending the *Odyssey's* narrative beyond Homer's text.

- [[ithaca]] — his home and the inheritance he fights to reclaim.
- [[athena]] — the goddess who guides his journey and supports his maturation.
