## summary

**Excalibur** is the legendary magical sword of *King Arthur*, granted to him by the mysterious *Lady of the Lake* and symbolic of Arthur's right to rule. The sword's origins, powers, and ultimate fate are central to *Arthurian legend*, representing not merely a weapon but a sign of divine favor and legitimate kingship.

## you gotta know

- Excalibur is sometimes conflated with the *Sword in the Stone*—the blade that establishes Arthur's birthright as king—but in most versions they are distinct swords, with Excalibur being the greater prize granted later by supernatural means.
- The *Lady of the Lake*, a mysterious magical being dwelling in an enchanted lake, presents Excalibur to Arthur alongside a magical scabbard that prevents wounds; the scabbard is said to be worth more than the sword itself because it grants invulnerability.
- The sword is described as having no equal in sharpness, durability, and magical properties; no armor can withstand its blade, and it never becomes dull or breaks, making it an instrument of divine will.
- Arthur wears Excalibur in all his great battles against the *Saxons* and in defense of *Britain*; the sword becomes so associated with his authority that losing it would mean losing his power to rule.
- In *Malory*'s telling and other Arthurian romances, when Arthur falls mortally wounded at the Battle of Camlann, he commands *Bedivere*, his last remaining knight, to throw Excalibur back into the lake from which it came.
- Bedivere twice fails to obey, pocketing the sword because it seems too precious to surrender; only on the third attempt does he finally hurl it into the water, where the *Lady's hand* emerges to catch it and withdraw it beneath the waves.
- Excalibur's return to the lake is interpreted as the sword's restoration to its divine source and a symbol of Arthur's passing from the mortal realm; the act is both an ending and a restoration of cosmic order.
