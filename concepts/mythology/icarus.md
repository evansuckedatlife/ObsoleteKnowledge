## summary

**Icarus** was the son of the master craftsman *Daedalus* in Greek mythology, famous for his fatal attempt to escape from *Crete* by flying on wax wings. Given explicit warnings by his father not to fly too close to the sun, Icarus ignored the caution, and the wax melted, causing him to plummet into the sea. His story became mythology's most enduring cautionary tale about youthful arrogance, disobedience, and the consequences of ignoring wise counsel from those who came before.

## you gotta know

- Son of [[daedalus|Daedalus]], the ingenious craftsman imprisoned by [[minos|King Minos]] on [[crete|Crete]] in the labyrinth prison.
- His father crafted wax wings held together with feathers as the means of escape from their island confinement.
- Daedalus warned Icarus not to fly too high (where the sun's heat would melt the wax) or too low (where sea spray would dampen the wings).
- Emboldened by the thrill of flight, Icarus disregarded his father's wisdom and soared upward toward the sun.
- The wax wings melted; Icarus fell into the sea (the Icarian Sea, as it was named in his memory) and drowned.
- Represents the archetype of youthful hubris—confidence without wisdom, ambition untempered by experience or caution.
- His fall symbolizes the gap between desire and capability, the dangers of defying natural limits, and the cost of ignoring parental guidance.

## connections
