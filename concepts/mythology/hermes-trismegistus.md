## summary

**Hermes Trismegistus**, or *Thrice-Great Hermes*, is a legendary syncretic figure combining the Greek messenger god *Hermes* and the Egyptian deity *Thoth*, patron of wisdom, magic, and writing. Emerging in Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt, he was revered as the author of the *Hermetica*, a foundational body of texts exploring theology, alchemy, astrology, and natural philosophy. His teachings profoundly shaped Western esotericism, Islamic philosophy, and the intellectual revival of the European *Renaissance*.

## you gotta know

- Revered as a primordial sage and prophet who harmonized Hellenistic philosophy with ancient Egyptian priestly theology in Hellenistic Alexandria.
- Purported author of the *Corpus Hermeticum*, a collection of philosophical dialogues including the famous *Poimandres*, which describes the creation of the cosmos and the ascent of the soul.
- Credited with originating the *Emerald Tablet* (*Tabula Smaragdina*), a cryptic alchemical text containing the famous occult maxim *As above, so below*.
- Given the epithet *Trismegistus* (*Thrice-Greatest*) because he was believed to hold mastery over the three parts of universal wisdom: alchemy, astrology, and theurgy.
- Translated into Latin by *Marsilio Ficino* under the patronage of *Cosimo de' Medici*, sparking widespread interest in Hermetic philosophy during the Italian *Renaissance*.
- In late antiquity and medieval European folklore, he was often regarded not as a god, but as an ancient mortal philosopher who lived contemporary with or prior to *Moses*.
- Severely demystified in the early seventeenth century by scholar *Isaac Casaubon*, who proved through philological dating that the *Corpus Hermeticum* was composed in late Roman antiquity rather than ancient Egyptian antiquity.

## connections

- [[thoth]] — the Egyptian god of writing and wisdom with whom *Hermes* was syncretized.
- [[osiris]] — the central Egyptian lord of the underworld frequently invoked alongside *Thoth* in Greco-Egyptian mysteries.
- [[isis]] — sister-wife of *Osiris* who features prominently in Egyptian esoteric dialogues and Hermetic fragments.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural and philosophical civilization whose pantheon provided *Hermes*.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural movement whose humanists revived and translated the *Hermetica*.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual era that moved away from Hermetic alchemy toward modern empirical sciences.
- [[hindu-mythology]] — comparative esoteric tradition featuring deep philosophical inquiries into divine unity and cosmic order.
