## summary

The **Battle of Camlann** (also known simply as **Camlann**) was the legendary final conflict of Arthurian myth in which the golden age of Camelot was shattered and the fellowship of the [[round-table]] was destroyed. Fought across the windswept plains of early medieval [[england]] between the loyal royal host of [[king-arthur]] and the traitorous rebel army of *Mordred*, the cataclysmic engagement resulted in the near-total annihilation of both forces. The battle holds profound cultural and literary significance as the tragic culmination of the Matter of Britain, marking the mortal wounding of the king and his mystical passage across the waters to the sacred isle of *Avalon*.

## you gotta know

- Culminated in a fatal single combat between [[king-arthur]] and *Mordred*, where Arthur ran the usurper through with his spear and Mordred struck Arthur a mortal blow to the head before dying.
- Precipitated according to Sir *Thomas Malory* by a freak accident during peace negotiations, when an unnamed knight drew his sword to kill an adder that had stung his foot, sparking panic and a general charge.
- Concluded with the grief-stricken king ordering Sir *Bedivere* to cast the enchanted sword *Excalibur* back into the lake, where a mystical arm rose from the surface to catch the blade and brandish it three times.
- Arthur's dying body was placed upon a black funeral barge by sorrowful queens, including his reconciled half-sister [[morgan-le-fay]], to be transported to the magical island of *Avalon* to heal until his prophesied return.
- Triggered by the political and martial fracture of the realm following the exposed romance between Queen *Guinevere* and Sir [[lancelot]], which lured Arthur away on a campaign in [[france]] and allowed Mordred to seize the crown.
- Geoffrey of Monmouth provided the first expansive narrative of the battle in his 12th-century *Historia Regum Britanniae*, identifying the setting as the river *Camblan* and portraying Arthur's victory as a Pyrrhic catastrophe.
- Recorded in historical annals as early as the 10th-century Welsh text *Annales Cambriae*, whose entry for the year 537 mentions the strife of *Camlann* where Arthur and *Medraut* fell, without explicitly stating they fought on opposing sides.
- Welsh folklore preserved in *Culhwch and Olwen* and the Welsh Triads claims only three warriors survived the carnage, including *Morfran*, who was shunned because men feared his extreme ugliness came from a devil, and *Sandde Bryd Angel*, whose ethereal beauty led foes to mistake him for an angel.

## connections

- [[king-arthur]] — the legendary high king whose reign and chivalric kingdom perished upon the blood-soaked field.
- [[round-table]] — the celebrated order of knights whose brotherhood was permanently extinguished during the catastrophic clash.
- [[lancelot]] — Arthur's supreme champion whose forbidden love affair fractured the court and made civil war inevitable.
- [[merlin]] — the prophetic court wizard who foretold the downfall of the kingdom and the doom stemming from Mordred's birth.
- [[morgan-le-fay]] — the enigmatic sorceress who tended to her mortally wounded brother and guided his barge to Avalon.
- [[england]] — the historical realm whose mythic heroic age was brought to an apocalyptic close at Camlann.
- [[france]] — the continental territory Arthur was besieging when news arrived of Mordred's treacherous domestic usurpation.
