## summary

**Apophis** (known to the ancient Egyptians as **Apep**) was the primordial giant serpent embodying chaos, darkness, and nonexistence (*isfet*), who stood as the eternal arch-nemesis of the sun god *Ra*. Originating in Egyptian religious thought during the Middle Kingdom and rising to prominence in New Kingdom funerary texts, he dwelled in the underworld (*Duat*) where he sought each night to devour the solar barque and submerge creation into eternal darkness. Because his threat was perpetual, the daily repelling of the serpent was considered vital to cosmic renewal, requiring ongoing magical rituals performed by priests and the *pharaoh* to maintain divine order (*maat*). His mythology represents the quintessential embodiment of absolute disorder and uncreation, making him one of the most enduring symbols of cosmic evil in world mythology.

## you gotta know

- Battles the sun god *Ra* each night as the solar barque travels through the underworld (*Duat*), seeking to consume the vessel and plunge the cosmos into eternal darkness.
- Defended against by the god *Set*, who stood bravely at the prow of the sun barge to spear the monster, aided by the protective coiled serpent deity *Mehen*.
- Used a hypnotic, paralyzing gaze to stun the celestial defenders and attempted to halt the sun ship by swallowing the waters of the subterranean river.
- Sapped and decapitated by the *Great Cat of Heliopolis*—a feline manifestation of *Ra* or *Bastet*—beneath the sacred persea (*ished*) tree, a famous scene preserved in Spell 17 of the *Book of the Dead*.
- Served as the mythological explanation for frightening natural phenomena such as solar eclipses, dark dust storms, and violent earthquakes, which were viewed as momentary assaults on the sun.
- Banished through daily temple execration rituals outlined in the *Book of Overthrowing Apep* (recorded in the *Bremner-Rhind Papyrus*), wherein priests cursed, trampled, and burned wax figurines of the beast.
- Possessed no temples, priesthood, or offerings, having arisen unnaturally from the primordial waters of *Nun* (in some traditions from the spit of the goddess *Neith*), representing absolute uncreation rather than a legitimate divine power.
- Endured cyclical dismemberment in underworld compositions like the *Book of Gates* and the *Amduat*, where he was bound in chains, carved into pieces, and consumed by fire, only to regenerate each evening.

## connections

- [[ra]] — the supreme sun god whose nightly subterranean journey Apophis relentlessly sought to disrupt.
- [[set]] — the contentious storm god who served as the primary defender at the prow of the solar barque, spearing the serpent.
- [[osiris]] — lord of the underworld whose realm of regenerative death Apophis threatened to annihilate.
- [[isis]] — goddess of magic whose powerful spells and protective enchantments aided in disorienting and subduing the beast.
- [[thoth]] — god of wisdom and sacred magic who recited incantations to counteract the serpent's hypnotic enchantments.
- [[pharaoh]] — earthly ruler tasked with upholding cosmic order (*maat*) through state rituals that ritually defeated the chaos serpent.
- [[horus]] — divine champion and protector of kingship, whose triumphant cosmic order mirrored the daily destruction of chaotic forces.
