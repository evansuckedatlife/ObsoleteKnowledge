## summary

**The Fates**, known in *Hellas* as the **Moirai** and in *Rome* as the **Parcae**, are the three sister goddesses who personify the inexorable, preordained destiny of every mortal and deity in classical mythology. Emerging from early Greek epic and archaic cosmological poetry, the sisters govern human life through the metaphor of spinning, measuring, and cutting an individual's fateful thread. They represent the supreme principle that even the Olympians cannot alter the fundamental boundaries of mortality, establishing fate as the ultimate cosmic boundary in the ancient imagination.

## you gotta know

- The triad consists of three distinct sisters: *Clotho* ("the spinner"), who spins the thread of life from her distaff; *Lachesis* ("the allotter"), who measures its assigned length with her rod; and *Atropos* ("the unturnable"), who snips the thread with her shears to end a mortal life.
- Their authority over destiny is sovereign and absolute, presenting a central theological paradox in classical literature where even *Zeus*, supreme ruler of Mount Olympus, is powerless to override their decrees without unravelling the universal order.
- In *Homer's* *The Iliad*, the inescapable decrees of fate take center stage when *Zeus* contemplates saving his beloved son *Sarpedon* from death at Troy, only to be admonished by *Hera* that no god may release a mortal long destined to die.
- Classical sources offer competing genealogies for the sisters: in *Hesiod's* *Theogony*, they are first described as ancient daughters of the primordial night goddess *Nyx*, but are later identified as the offspring of *Zeus* and the Titaness *Themis*, making them sisters to the *Horae*.
- In *Plato's* *Republic*, the *Myth of Er* depicts the sisters seated on thrones in white robes, singing in harmony with the Sirens around the cosmic spindle of *Ananke* (Necessity), with *Lachesis* singing of the past, *Clotho* of the present, and *Atropos* of the future.
- During the infancy of the hero *Meleager*, the sisters visited his mother *Althaea* to declare that the child would live only until a burning log currently on the hearth was consumed by fire, prompting his mother to extinguish and hide the brand.
- In Roman religion and literature, they were identified with native birth goddesses known as the *Parcae*—specifically *Nona*, *Decima*, and *Morta*—who originally oversaw nine-month gestation and delivery before taking on the fatalistic spinning roles of Greek tradition.

## connections

- [[zeus]] — the king of the gods whose supreme authority is ultimately circumscribed by their unyielding decrees.
- [[the-iliad]] — the Homeric epic depicting the tragic tension between divine will and the fixed doom apportioned to heroes.
- [[achilles]] — the greatest Greek champion at Troy whose choice between a long life and glorious early death reflects their dispensation.
- [[apollo]] — the god of prophecy who transmits glimpses of their predetermined designs to mortals through oracles.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural world whose literature and drama were fundamentally shaped by the terror of inevitable destiny.
- [[roman-empire]] — the civilisation that syncretised the Greek trio with native birth spirits into the fateful *Parcae*.
- [[theseus]] — the legendary king of Athens whose heroic dynasty was repeatedly ensnared by inescapable familial curses.
