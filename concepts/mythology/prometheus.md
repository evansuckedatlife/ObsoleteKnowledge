## summary

**Prometheus** is a Titan in Greek mythology who defied *Zeus* by stealing fire from the gods and giving it to humans, enabling civilization itself. As punishment, Zeus condemned him to eternal torment: chained to a rock in the Caucasus, his liver eaten daily by an eagle only to regenerate each night. Prometheus embodies human aspiration, transgression against divine authority, and the tragic hero who sacrifices himself for others' benefit. His myth has inspired philosophers, artists, and revolutionaries across millennia.

## you gotta know

- A Titan, not an Olympian; though his family sided with the Olympians against the Titans, Prometheus maintained independence and antagonism toward Zeus' rule.
- Stole *fire* from the gods—representing not merely the physical flame but technology, knowledge, arts, and civilization itself—and gave it to humans; this gift is the foundation of human progress.
- Zeus punished him by chaining him to a rock in the Caucasus mountains; an eagle sent by Zeus ate his liver each day, which regenerated overnight, making his suffering eternal and cyclical.
- *Hercules* eventually freed him after many centuries of torment, shooting the eagle and breaking his chains; but some versions claim Prometheus remains bound even after liberation, symbolizing the permanence of his defiance.
- Served as a *culture-hero*: he taught humans practical arts, medicine, metalworking, and prophecy; he is thus a god-like benefactor to humanity, making him sympathetic despite his transgression.
- *Aeschylus'* *Prometheus Bound* dramatizes him as a rebel against tyranny; the play transforms him from mythological figure into philosophical icon representing resistance to unjust authority.
- His name means "forethought" or "foresight"; some versions claim he could see the future and knew his punishment in advance, making his choice to aid humanity an act of conscious sacrifice.

## connections

- [[zeus]] — his nemesis and punisher; their antagonism frames the myth as a conflict over divine justice, authority, and the proper relationship between gods and humans.
- [[prometheus-bound]] — *Aeschylus'* masterwork dramatizing Prometheus' defiance; the play makes Prometheus a philosophical hero questioning authority and justice.
- [[fire]] — his gift to humanity; fire represents knowledge, technology, civilization, and the means by which humans transcend animal nature.
- [[transgression]] — Prometheus' act defines transgression: violation of divine law motivated by compassion for humans, raising the question of whether such transgression is moral.
- [[the-oresteia]] — *Aeschylus* also made Prometheus a character in the broader mythological system; his story intersects with other divine conflicts and human history.
- [[titans]] — Prometheus is a Titan, linking him to the elder generation of gods; his alliance with the Olympians against the other Titans complicates his identity.
- [[aeschylus]] — the playwright who made Prometheus a central figure; Aeschylus' version emphasizes the philosophical and political dimensions of Prometheus' defiance.
