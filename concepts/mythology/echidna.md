# Echidna

## summary

In *Greek mythology*, **Echidna** is a monstrous creature—part woman, part serpent—who serves as the progenitrix of numerous classical monsters. Paired with the titan *Typhon*, Echidna bore the most fearsome beasts of Greek myth, making her a symbolic source of chaos and danger in the mythological cosmos.

## you gotta know

- Echidna is depicted as a woman from the waist up with a serpent's tail below; some sources describe her as more fully serpentine with only human features.
- Her mother was *Medusa*, the Gorgon slain by *Perseus*; this genealogy connects Echidna to ancient serpent-deities and chthonic (underworld) powers.
- Echidna's union with *Typhon*, a primordial storm giant, produced the most famous monsters of Greek myth: *Cerberus* (the three-headed hound guarding the underworld), the *Hydra of Lerna* (nine-headed serpent slain by *Heracles*), and the *Chimera* (lion-goat-serpent hybrid).
- Her offspring also included *Orthus* (a two-headed dog) and *Ladon* (the hundred-headed dragon guarding the golden apples of the Hesperides), and *Pegasus* (the divine horse, though some accounts vary).
- Echidna herself lived in a cave, sometimes said to be in the underworld or at the edge of the known world, representing the borderland between civilization and primal chaos.
- She is less frequently featured as a character in myths than her offspring; her primary role is genealogical—explaining the origin of monsters and connecting them to each other and to older divine/chthonic powers.
- In *Hesiod*'s *Theogony*, which provides the most detailed account of her, Echidna represents a bridge between the Titans and the monsters that challenge Olympian gods.
