## summary

**Hermes** is the Olympian god of messages, boundaries, commerce, thieves, and the dead in Greek mythology. Born of *Zeus* and the nymph *Maia*, *Hermes* is characteristically clever, quick-witted, and amoral—equally comfortable aiding heroes as deceiving them. As divine messenger (*psychopompos*), he guides souls to the Underworld; as protector of travelers and merchants, he represents boundaries, transitions, and the movement of goods and information. His attribute is the caduceus, the staff entwined with serpents.

## you gotta know

- Son of *Zeus* and the Pleiad nymph *Maia*, born in a secret cave on Mount Cyllene in Arcadia; known for precocious cleverness from birth, stealing cattle from *Apollo* on his first day alive.
- Invented the lyre on the day of his birth from a tortoise shell, creating music so enchanting that *Apollo* was moved to forgive the cattle theft and trade him the caduceus for the instrument.
- Serves as the divine messenger (*angelos*) between gods and mortals, traveling between Olympus and the mortal realm; also functions as *psychopompos*, the guide of souls to the Underworld and *Hades*.
- Wears the petasos (a winged hat) and the winged sandals (*talaria*), symbols of his speed and mobility; carries the caduceus, the herald's staff around which two serpents are entwined—later a medical symbol.
- Delivered the sealed jar containing human sorrows to *Pandora* before her marriage to *Epimetheus*, making him instrumental in humanity's suffering and the fall of the Golden Age.
- Provided crucial aid to *Perseus*, gifting him the adamantine (*unbreakable*) sickle and winged sandals essential for beheading *Medusa* while invisible via *Athena's* helm of darkness.
- Characterized as both helpful and mischievous, serving as patron of thieves, merchants, travelers, speakers, and heralds—blurring the line between trickery and legitimate commerce, theft and cleverness.
- Presides over boundaries, transitions, commerce, language, persuasion, and journeys; his domain encompasses all crossroads (where herms—stone pillars—marked his presence) and all liminal spaces.

## connections

- [[zeus]] — his father and the king of the gods who sends him on missions.
- [[hades]] — god of the Underworld whom Hermes frequently encounters as a psychopomp.
- [[pandora]] — the figure whose deception he facilitated by delivering her to humanity.
- [[perseus]] — the hero whom he assisted in his quest against Medusa.
- [[apollo]] — his rival and eventual ally, with whom he had conflict over the lyre.
