## summary

**Olympus**, the sacred mountain dwelling of the twelve Olympian gods, stands as the cosmological center of Greek mythology. Located in northern Greece, this divine realm serves as the administrative and social heart of the pantheon, where gods feast, debate, and scheme over mortal affairs. For ancient Greeks, Olympus was both a physical place and a transcendent space beyond mortal reach.

## you gotta know

- The twelve major Olympian gods—including *Zeus*, *Hera*, *Poseidon*, *Aphrodite*, *Apollo*, and *Athena*—reside on Olympus as a hierarchical community, with *Zeus*'s supremacy established through his victory over the Titans.
- *Zeus* rules as king of the gods from his throne atop the mountain, making executive decisions that bind all divine and mortal realms; his word is law even in disputes between other deities.
- Olympus is shrouded in perpetual cloud and mist, making it invisible to mortals; the gods feast on ambrosia and nectar, immortal foods that sustain their eternal existence and preserve their divine power.
- Divine politics play out in Olympian assemblies, where gods propose quarrels, form alliances, and distribute divine favor to chosen heroes and champions on Earth; petty squabbles between gods often cascade into mortal consequences.
- The mountain's location shifts across different mythological traditions—sometimes placed in northern Greece near Mount Pieria, sometimes imagined as a celestial realm beyond geographic space accessible only to divine or specially favored beings.
- Not all gods reside on Olympus; *Hades* rules the underworld from his own realm, *Poseidon* dominates the sea from aquatic palaces, and minor deities occupy other sacred sites, creating a distributed divine hierarchy.
- Mortal heroes who earned divine favor—like *Heracles* or *Achilles*—were sometimes granted immortality and a place in Olympus after death, blurring the boundary between divine and human realms and rewarding exceptional mortals with eternal status.
- The twelve Olympians are distinct from older chthonic deities and nature spirits; Olympus itself becomes a symbol of order, civilization, and hierarchical power in contrast to the chaos of the pre-Olympian cosmos.

- [[hermes]] — the divine messenger who travels between Olympus and the mortal world.
