## summary

**Poseidon** is the Olympian god of the sea, earthquakes, and horses in Greek mythology, ruling from his underwater palace with his trident-bearing authority. One of the twelve Olympians and brother to *Zeus* and *Hades*, Poseidon appears throughout Greek epic and drama as a volatile, passionate, vengeful deity whose wrath shipwrecks heroes and whose favor grants safe passage. He embodies the sea's dual nature: life-giving and catastrophic, fertile and destructive.

## you gotta know

- One of the three paramount gods (alongside Zeus and Hades), each ruling a cosmic domain: Zeus the sky, Hades the underworld, Poseidon the sea—a tripartite sovereignty of the universe.
- Armed with a *trident*, which he uses to shake the earth, cause earthquakes, and churn the sea; the trident is his symbol of power and appears in art as his defining attribute.
- Rules the ocean realm from a golden palace in the sea depths; controls storms, currents, tides, and the fate of sailors; sailors prayed to him before voyages and made offerings for safe passage.
- Father of *Medusa* (in some versions) and thus linked to her monstrous transformation; also fathered numerous sea-nymphs (*Nereids*) and monsters like *Polyphemus* the Cyclops.
- Harbors implacable enmity toward *Odysseus* in *The Odyssey*, repeatedly delaying his homecoming and destroying ships; his decade-long vendetta against Odysseus is the epic's central antagonistic force.
- Competed with *Athena* for patronage of Athens; she won by offering the olive tree, while Poseidon's gift of a salt-water spring was deemed less valuable—a myth explaining Athena's pre-eminence in the city.
- Linked to horses through origin myths: some accounts claim he created horses by striking the earth with his trident; he appears as a horse-god in pre-Olympian cults, suggesting an older fertility deity absorbed into the Olympian pantheon.

## connections

- [[zeus]] — his elder brother; together they form the top tier of Olympian authority, though Zeus ranks above all.
- [[hades]] — the third brother; Poseidon is less austere and more volatile than his underworld sibling.
- [[athena]] — rival goddess; their contest for Athens and their opposing gifts (olive vs. salt spring) encapsulate their different domains and values.
- [[the-odyssey]] — Poseidon's vendetta against Odysseus is the epic's central divine obstacle; his wrath is why Odysseus takes ten years to reach home.
- [[the-trojan-war]] — a Poseidon-favored location; he had early ties to Troy and appears as a minor but present force in the war.
- [[medusa]] — his victim (or in some versions, his child); the Gorgon embodies his capacity for rage and violation.
- [[the-sea]] — his domain; all maritime imagery in Greek literature carries Poseidon's presence.
