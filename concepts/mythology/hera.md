## summary

**Hera** is the queen of the Olympian gods and goddess of marriage, family, and women in Greek mythology. As the wife of *Zeus*, she is perpetually wronged by his countless infidelities and extra-marital offspring, making her a figure both powerful and consumed by jealousy. **Hera's** vindictive pursuit of *Zeus's* illegitimate children—particularly the hero *Heracles*—reveals her complexity: a wronged spouse whose wrath becomes legendary, yet also a protector of matrimonial bonds and legitimate heirs.

## you gotta know

- Queen of Olympus and one of the twelve major Olympian deities, presiding over marriage, family, women, and the bonds of matrimonial fidelity and legitimate heirs.
- Married to *Zeus*, the king of the gods, whose notorious infidelities and countless affairs with goddesses, nymphs, and mortals make her marriage eternally tempestuous and jealousy her defining emotion.
- Mother of *Ares* (war god), *Hephaestus* (craftsman god), and *Hebe* (goddess of youth), though notably she bears *Zeus* fewer children than he has with others, reinforcing her sense of betrayal.
- Sometimes described as the sole creator of *Typhon*, the most terrible monster in Greek mythology, born from her anger at Zeus—a being capable of threatening even the Olympian order.
- Her persecution of *Heracles*, *Zeus's* illegitimate son by the mortal *Alcmene*, is legendary: she drove him to murderous madness, inflicted the Nemean Lion and other monsters upon him, and hounded him throughout his Twelve Labors.
- Worshipped throughout Greece with special prominence at Argos, and associated with the peacock (her sacred bird), the pomegranate (symbol of fertility and marriage), and the color gold (reflecting queenly status).
- Known for her dignity, formality, and strict adherence to proper hierarchy, protocol, and ritual observance—qualities making her both deeply revered and genuinely feared by gods and mortals alike.
- Identified with the Roman goddess *Juno*, who paradoxically became even more prominent in Roman religious practice than Hera was in Greek religion, reflecting Roman emphasis on civic order and marital law.
