## summary

**Karma** is the Hindu and Buddhist principle of cosmic cause and effect, where every action in one life generates consequences that ripple across lifetimes. The Sanskrit term literally means "action," but it encompasses the moral weight of all deeds—virtuous or vicious. *Karma* operates as an impersonal law of universal justice: deeds accumulate moral debt or credit, determining the circumstances of future rebirths. This concept transforms the nature of divine justice from personal punishment to mechanical consequence, making the individual responsible for their own destiny.

## you gotta know

- Core principle established in the *Vedas* and later elaborated in the *Upanishads*, the *Bhagavad Gita*, and canonical Buddhist texts like the *Pali Canon* and *Mahayana* sutras.
- Functions as the mechanism of *samsara* (the cycle of rebirth): actions in one life determine one's station, social class, and degree of suffering in subsequent lifetimes.
- Operates according to *dharma* (duty, virtue, and cosmic order); fulfilling dharma creates positive *karma*, while violating it generates negative *karma* that binds the soul to the cycle of rebirth.
- A person's *karma* is not punishment inflicted by a god or judge, but the natural unfolding of consequences—impersonal, inevitable, and inescapable, governed by universal law rather than divine will.
- Can accumulate over multiple lifetimes; a person born into suffering or hardship may be experiencing consequences of past misdeeds or failures to observe *dharma*, not arbitrary divine wrath.
- Provides a theodicy (explanation for suffering and injustice) that differs radically from Western religious traditions: suffering is not mysterious or unjust, but earned and deserved through past actions.
- Differs between Hindu and Buddhist interpretations: Hindu *karma* relates closely to caste assignment and social hierarchy as natural consequences, while Buddhist *karma* is more individualistic and emphasizes moral agency.
- Becomes the basis for ethical systems in both religions: actions matter because they shape one's destiny; no external savior can erase *karma*, only right action can counteract or purify it.

## connections

- [[dharma]] — the ethical framework within which karma operates; fulfilling dharma generates positive karma.
- [[vedas]] — the ancient texts establishing karma as a core cosmic principle.
- [[vishnu]] — the deity who upholds dharma and the cosmic order within which karma unfolds.
- [[buddhism]] — the religious tradition that adapted and universalized the concept of karma.
- [[hinduism]] — the tradition in which karma is foundational to understanding life and rebirth.
- [[reincarnation]] — the cyclical process whose trajectory is determined by accumulated karma.
- [[fate-and-destiny]] — the Western philosophical theme closest to karma's deterministic logic.
