## summary

A **fairy tale** is a narrative genre featuring magical elements, archetypal characters (princes, witches, enchanted forests), and moral lessons wrapped in wonder. Originating in European folklore and codified through literary collections by figures like the Brothers Grimm and Hans Christian Andersen, fairy tales have become foundational to theatrical tradition—from classical ballet *Sleeping Beauty* to contemporary musicals like *Into the Woods*. They remain the skeleton upon which storytellers drape questions about desire, transformation, and the price of getting what you wish for.

## you gotta know

- Oral traditions across Europe—French, German, Italian—were collected and standardized in the 17th–19th centuries, creating a canonical repertoire (Cinderella, Sleeping Beauty, Little Red Riding Hood, Jack and the Beanstalk).
- *The Brothers Grimm* (early 1800s) and *Hans Christian Andersen* (mid-1800s) published influential collections that shaped how fairy tales are understood and retold.
- Fairy tales employ *archetypal narrative structures*: a protagonist in an impossible situation, magical aid or test, and resolution through transformation or revelation—patterns that recur across cultures.
- Classical ballet embraced fairy tales as ideal libretti: *Sleeping Beauty* (Tchaikovsky/Petipa), *Giselle*, and *Cinderella* use the genre's supernatural logic to justify virtuosic spectacle.
- The genre traditionally ends in "happily ever after," a narrative closure that later writers (Sondheim, de Mille, modern retellings) explicitly interrogate and subvert.
- Fairy-tale tropes—the wish, the curse, the transformation, the test—persist in contemporary literature, musical theatre, and film, showing the genre's archetypal power.
- Thematically, fairy tales explore moral ambiguity, desire and its consequences, the coming-of-age journey, and the tension between individual will and fate.
- The genre's appeal lies in its *suspension of disbelief*: magic is presented as normal and explicable within the story world, allowing listeners to engage with impossible scenarios as plausible ethical dilemmas.
- Fairy-tale logic is transactional: wishes have prices, curses follow rules, and victory requires cleverness or sacrifice—this moral economy differs sharply from modern narrative realism, making the genre instantly recognizable across retellings.
