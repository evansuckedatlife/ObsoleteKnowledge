## summary

**The Sleeping Beauty** is a grand three-act [[ballet]] with a prologue composed by *Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky* and choreographed by *Marius Petipa*, which premiered in 1890 at the Imperial Mariinsky Theatre in *Saint Petersburg*. Commissioned by Imperial Theatres director *Ivan Vsevolozhsky*, the work adapted the classic [[fairy-tale]] by *Charles Perrault* into a visual and symphonic apotheosis of the Russian Imperial court. It represents the pinnacle of nineteenth-century classical dance, seamlessly wedding symphonic structure to rigorous choreographic architecture and revitalizing theatrical dance across Europe.

## you gotta know

- Features the famous *Rose Adagio* in Act I, in which *Princess Aurora* balances en pointe on one leg while greeting four royal suitors in turn, universally regarded as one of the supreme technical and artistic tests in all of [[ballet]].
- Centers on the christening of *Princess Aurora*, where the malevolent fairy *Carabosse* curses the infant to prick her finger on a spindle and die, a fate softened by the *Lilac Fairy* into a century-long slumber broken by true love's kiss.
- Depicts the rescue of the sleeping kingdom in Act II, where the *Lilac Fairy* summons a vision of *Princess Aurora* for *Prince Désiré*, guiding his boat through an enchanted forest to awaken her with a kiss and shatter the spell.
- Concludes in Act III with a lavish court wedding incorporating virtuoso divertissements danced by iconic [[fairy-tale]] figures from [[france]], including *Puss in Boots*, *Little Red Riding Hood*, and the celebrated *Bluebird pas de deux*.
- Staged through an extraordinarily detailed collaboration between choreographer *Marius Petipa*, who provided precise tempo and measure specifications, and *Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky*, whose rich, leitmotif-driven symphonic score elevated theatrical music above mere accompaniment.
- Conceived by theatre director *Ivan Vsevolozhsky* as an homage to the Sun King *Louis XIV* and French courtly spectacle, serving as a political and artistic assertion of Imperial Russian splendor at the close of the nineteenth century.
- Received an initially muted, polite reaction from Tsar *Alexander III* during its 1890 dress rehearsal—who simply remarked "Very nice"—disappointing *Tchaikovsky*, yet the production rapidly became an enduring triumph that directly inspired subsequent masterworks like *[[the-nutcracker]]*.

## connections

- [[ballet]] — the classical art form elevated to its academic zenith through Petipa's choreography and Petipa-Tchaikovsky structural synthesis.
- [[swan-lake]] — *Tchaikovsky*'s earlier choreographic work, which was drastically revised and recognized as a masterpiece only after the triumph of *The Sleeping Beauty*.
- [[the-nutcracker]] — *Tchaikovsky*'s final ballet, commissioned as a direct result of the immense success of *The Sleeping Beauty*.
- [[fairy-tale]] — the folkloric genre popularized by *Charles Perrault* that provided the narrative framework and divertissement characters for the libretto.
- [[giselle]] — the quintessential Romantic predecessor whose dual acts and supernatural maiden traditions Petipa transformed into classical Imperial grandeur.
- [[france]] — the cultural and literary home of the seventeenth-century court of *Louis XIV*, whose regal splendor directly inspired the staging and costumes.
- [[george-balanchine]] — legendary choreographer whose childhood performances in this Imperial production deeply shaped his neoclassical aesthetic in the [[united-states]].
