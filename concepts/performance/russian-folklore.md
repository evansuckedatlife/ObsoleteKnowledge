## summary

**Russian folklore** encompasses the mythology, fairy tales, folk customs, and carnival traditions of Russian culture stretching back centuries. Rich with magical creatures, transformations, and archetypal heroes, these narratives drew on Slavic paganism, Byzantine influences, and the experiences of Russian peasant life. The tradition became a vital source for modernist artists, particularly composers and choreographers seeking authentic, emotionally resonant material.

## you gotta know

- Central to Russian folklore are magical creatures including *Baba Yaga* (a fearsome witch), firebirds, and mythological beings drawn from pre-Christian Slavic belief systems.
- Russian carnival traditions, particularly the *Maslenitsa* (butter festival), featured puppet theater, music, and masked performance that influenced later ballet narratives.
- The *Firebird* (*Zhar-ptica*) appears in numerous Russian fairy tales as a magical bird of gold or fire, embodying transformation and transcendence.
- *Petrushka* (Punch) originates in Russian carnival puppet theater, representing the clever fool figure who subverts authority through wit and mischief.
- Russian fairy tales (*skazki*) typically follow archetypal patterns: a humble hero, magical helpers, impossible tasks, and triumph through cunning or virtue.
- The tradition draws significantly from both Slavic pagan mythology and Orthodox Christian values, creating a unique synthesis.
- Early-20th-century Russian artists including *Stravinsky* and *Fokine* revived folklore as a source of authentic modernity, rejecting western European refinement.
