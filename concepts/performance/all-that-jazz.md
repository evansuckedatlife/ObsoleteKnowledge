## summary

**All That Jazz** is a 1979 American musical drama film directed and co-written by *Bob Fosse*, offering an unflinching, semi-autobiographical portrait of his own manic workaholism, self-destruction, and creative genius. Starring *Roy Scheider* as choreographer-director *Joe Gideon*, the film dramatizes Fosse's real-life physical collapse while simultaneously editing his film *Lenny* and staging the original Broadway production of *[[chicago-musical]]* in the *[[united-states]]*. Celebrated for its revolutionary editing, dark existentialism, and surreal musical sequences, it shared the prestigious *Palme d'Or* at Cannes and stands as one of the most innovative and introspective masterworks of American cinema and *[[musical-theatre]]*.

## you gotta know

- *Joe Gideon*, portrayed by *Roy Scheider* in an acclaimed Oscar-nominated performance, serves as the thinly veiled surrogate for *[[bob-fosse]]*, capturing the director's relentless chain-smoking, pill addiction, womanizing, and perfectionism.
- Features an iconic morning ritual set to Antonio Vivaldi's *Cello Concerto*, where Gideon takes Alka-Seltzer and Dexedrine, uses eye drops, lights a cigarette, and smiles into his bathroom mirror while saying, *"It's showtime, folks!"*
- Interspersed with conversations between Gideon and *Angelique*, a mysterious, veil-draped personification of Death portrayed by *Jessica Lange*, who listens to his confessions in a backstage dressing-room purgatory.
- Concludes with a hallucinatory, tour-de-force television spectacle where Gideon sings *"Bye Bye Life"*—a morbid rework of the Everly Brothers' *"Bye Bye Love"*—staged alongside *Ben Vereen* as doctors attempt a failed open-heart surgery, ending with Gideon zipped inside a black body bag to *"There's No Business Like Show Business"*.
- The film's legendary opening sequence captures a grueling, high-pressure Broadway audition set to George Benson's rendition of *"On Broadway"*, showcasing rapid-fire editing and Fosse's signature angular, isolated choreography.
- Features the provocative musical number *"Take Off with Us"*, which starts as a breezy airline dance routine for commercial producers before transforming into an overtly sensual, androgynous exploration of dance titled *"Airotica"*.
- Cast members drew heavily from Fosse's intimate personal life, including *Ann Reinking* portraying Gideon's mistress *Katie Jagger* (a version of herself) and *Leland Palmer* as Gideon's ex-wife *Audrey Paris* (modeled on Fosse's real-life collaborator and spouse *Gwen Verdon*).
- Tied with Akira Kurosawa's historical epic *Kagemusha* for the *Palme d'Or* at the 1980 Cannes Film Festival, and earned nine nominations at the Academy Awards, winning four for its art direction, costume design, film editing, and original song score.

## connections

- [[bob-fosse]] — director, co-writer, and choreographer whose career, heart attack, and personal vices directly inspired the film.
- [[chicago-musical]] — landmark Broadway production whose staging caused Fosse's medical emergency and which lent its opening anthem *"And All That Jazz"* to the film's title.
- [[musical-theatre]] — dramatic discipline deconstructed by the film, exposing the physical toll, commercial compromises, and psychological demands placed on performers.
- [[modern-dance]] — choreography tradition interwoven with jazz dance idioms throughout the stylized rehearsal sequences.
- [[new-york-city]] — setting of Broadway stages, rehearsal lofts, and hospitals where the film's events unfold.
- [[united-states]] — national culture of commercial entertainment, show business, and postwar cinema that framed Fosse's career.
- [[modernism]] — twentieth-century artistic movement whose non-linear narrative techniques, reflexivity, and surrealist motifs deeply influenced the film's structure.
