## summary

**Musical theatre** is a theatrical form that synthesizes dramatic narrative, music, choreography, and elaborate spectacle into a live performance. Emerging in the late 19th century from operetta and vaudeville traditions, it matured into a distinctly American art form through the innovations of composer-lyricist teams like *Rodgers and Hammerstein*, who proved that songs could advance character and plot rather than interrupt it. Musical theatre became one of the defining cultural expressions of the 20th century, blending the precision of ballet with the immediacy of contemporary music and human vulnerability.

## you gotta know

- Musical theatre descends from European operetta and 19th-century vaudeville and revue traditions, but crystallized as a distinct American form in the early 20th century.
- *Rodgers and Hammerstein revolutionized the form* by integrating songs meaningfully into narrative, making them inseparable from character development and plot progression rather than ornamental interludes (*Oklahoma*, 1943).
- The form combines *four core elements*: spoken dramatic dialogue, orchestrated musical numbers, choreography and dance, and visual spectacle—each reinforcing character and story.
- *American Golden Age* (1940s–1960s): works like *The Sound of Music*, *West Side Story*, and *Carousel* established the form's emotional and artistic credibility, attracting serious composers and storytellers.
- *Stephen Sondheim* (1960s onward) elevated musical theatre's lyrical sophistication, using complex rhyme schemes and internal wordplay to mirror psychological complexity in characters like *Sweeney Todd* and *Into the Woods*.
- *Andrew Lloyd Webber* dominated the 1980s with mega-musicals like *Cats* and *The Phantom of the Opera*, proving that spectacle and melody alone could sustain multi-year runs and international casts.
- *Choreographers as co-creators*: figures like *Bob Fosse*, *Jerome Robbins*, and *Agnes de Mille* elevated dance from ornamentation to narrative force, making movement as important as song.
- Musical theatre reaches audiences through both *Broadway institutions* (in New York) and touring productions; it remains a gateway to theatre for casual audiences while attracting experimental artists (*Rent*, *Hamilton*).
