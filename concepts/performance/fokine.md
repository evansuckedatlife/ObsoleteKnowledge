## summary

**Michel Fokine** was a Russian-born choreographer and dancer who revolutionized ballet in the early 20th century by rejecting rigid classical conventions. He pioneered dramatic, narrative-driven ballets grounded in pantomime and emotional authenticity, transforming dance into a cohesive theatrical art form. His innovations under the *Ballets Russes* made him a defining figure in modern dance history and forever changed the relationship between movement and dramatic expression.

## you gotta know

- Served as principal dancer and choreographer at the Imperial Ballet in St. Petersburg before joining Diaghilev's company.
- Created *The Firebird* (1910) to *Stravinsky's* revolutionary score, establishing a powerful template for modernist ballet collaborations.
- Choreographed *Petrushka* (1911), fusing Russian folk traditions with unprecedented character-driven narrative and naturalistic movement.
- Rejected the rigid uniformity of classical technique, arguing that movement must serve the dramatic content and emotional truth of each work.
- Pioneered a new aesthetic in which pantomime and ballet were seamlessly integrated rather than kept separate.
- His works emphasized individual character expression over pure virtuosity, democratizing what ballet bodies could convey.
- Influenced successive generations of choreographers including *Balanchine* and *Robbins*, who adapted his principles to different cultural contexts.
- His theories on "natural" movement and psychological realism became foundational to modern dance development throughout the twentieth century.

## connections

- [[ballets-russes]] — the Diaghilev company through which his most influential works premiered.
- [[stravinsky]] — his primary compositional partner, especially on *The Firebird* and *Petrushka*.
- [[nijinsky]] — fellow dancer and collaborator in early Ballets Russes productions.
