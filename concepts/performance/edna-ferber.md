## summary

**Edna Ferber** was a prominent American novelist, short-story author, and dramatist whose sweeping historical panoramas captured the vibrant social, racial, and economic development of the *[[united-states]]*. Flourishing throughout the early to mid-twentieth century, she combined journalistic regional observation with a flair for popular drama, becoming a central figure at the Algonquin Round Table in *[[new-york-city]]*. Her 1926 bestselling novel *Show Boat* served as the direct literary source for *Jerome Kern* and *Oscar Hammerstein II*, inaugurating the modern era of dramatic *[[musical-theatre]]*.

## you gotta know

- Authored the landmark 1926 novel *Show Boat*, tracing three generations of theatrical performers aboard the *Cotton Blossom* floating theater along the Mississippi River.
- The stage adaptation of *Show Boat*, produced by *Florenz Ziegfeld* in 1927, pioneered the integrated musical play by addressing miscegenation, racial injustice, and tragic marriage through song and theatrical *[[dance]]*.
- Awarded the 1925 Pulitzer Prize for the Novel for *So Big*, an evocative portrait of agrarian hardship, maternal devotion, and artistic ambition centered on the resilient schoolteacher *Selina Peake DeJong*.
- Penned the bestselling 1952 epic *Giant*, which dissected the petroleum boom, social stratifications, and systemic prejudice against Mexican Americans in Texas, inspiring an iconic 1956 film starring *James Dean*.
- Forged a successful, long-running theatrical collaboration with playwright *George S. Kaufman*, yielding celebrated Broadway hits such as *The Royal Family*, *Dinner at Eight*, and *Stage Door*.
- Ranked among the sharpest wits of the Algonquin Round Table in Manhattan alongside *Dorothy Parker*, *Robert Benchley*, and fellow Broadway chroniclers.
- Wrote the historical epic *Cimarron* (1929), focusing on the chaotic Oklahoma Land Rush and the transformation of the western frontier, which became an Academy Award-winning film.
- Consistently centered her literary work on independent working women, racial minorities, and ordinary laborers navigating the rapid urbanization and industrial growth of modern America.
