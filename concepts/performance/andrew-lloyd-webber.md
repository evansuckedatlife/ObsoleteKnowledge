## summary

**Andrew Lloyd Webber** is a British composer who revolutionized musical theatre in the 1980s with a series of commercially colossal productions that blended classical instrumentation, pop sensibility, and theatrical spectacle. His works, particularly *Cats* and *The Phantom of the Opera*, defined the modern blockbuster musical and demonstrated that narrative and character could be subordinated to melody, design, and sheer commercial ambition. His influence on contemporary musical theatre remains unmatched.

## you gotta know

- Born in 1948 in London; studied at the Royal College of Music and composed his first musical at age 20.
- *Evita* (1978), co-written with lyricist Tim Rice, established him as a major force: a 1980 film adaptation and Broadway success cemented his reputation.
- *Cats* (1981)—adapted from T. S. Eliot's poetry collection—ran for 18 years on Broadway, making it one of the longest-running musicals ever and proving that spectacle and individual character could trump plot.
- *The Phantom of the Opera* (1986), his masterpiece, became the longest-running Broadway musical in history; its blend of gothic melodrama, soaring tenor vocals, and Puccini-influenced orchestration created a template for modern megamusicals.
- His compositional signature includes lush orchestrations, memorable melodic hooks accessible to pop audiences, and recurring use of the pipe organ and classical motifs.
- Other major works include *Sunset Boulevard* (1993), *Aspects of Love* (1989), and more recently *School of Rock* (2015), each exploring different theatrical genres.
- Won seven Tony Awards and a Grammy; his stage productions have grossed billions of dollars worldwide, making him one of the most financially successful composers in history.
- His influence on musical theatre economics is immeasurable: his works demonstrated that theatrical blockbusters could achieve film-level box-office returns and cultural penetration, reshaping Broadway's investment and production strategies fundamentally.
- Known for accessibility: his melodies are instantly memorable and singable by amateurs, a characteristic that made his works pervasive in concert halls, school productions, and popular culture beyond traditional theatre audiences.
- His compositional method often began with melody first, a departure from the integration of music and text favored by modernist composers, making his works feel immediate and emotionally direct to listeners.
