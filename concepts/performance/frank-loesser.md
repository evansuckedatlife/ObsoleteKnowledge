## summary

**Frank Loesser** was a master American songwriter, lyricist, and composer whose verbal dexterity and melodic range shaped the Golden Age of the Broadway stage. Renowned for capturing the idiosyncratic cadence of urban speech, he wrote the complete music and lyrics for perennial masterpieces such as *Guys and Dolls* and *How to Succeed in Business Without Really Trying*. His remarkable career spanned Tin Pan Alley, patriotic wartime ballads, Hollywood screen songs, and ambitious quasi-operatic musical literature, earning him Tony Awards, an Academy Award, and the Pulitzer Prize for Drama.

## you gotta know

- Began his professional career as a Hollywood lyricist before writing both words and music, scoring early chart successes including the wartime hit *Praise the Lord and Pass the Ammunition*.
- Achieved immortality with *Guys and Dolls* in 1950, setting stories by *Damon Runyon* to an ingenious, slang-laden score featuring *Luck Be a Lady* and *Sit Down, You're Rockin' the Boat*.
- Composed the ambitious quasi-operatic pastoral romance *The Most Happy Fella* in 1956, writing over thirty complex musical numbers including folk arias, choral ensembles, and Broadway showstoppers.
- Won the 1962 Pulitzer Prize for Drama and multiple Tony Awards for the razor-sharp corporate satire *How to Succeed in Business Without Really Trying*.
- Wrote the beloved winter duet *Baby, It's Cold Outside*, which earned him the Academy Award for Best Original Song after its inclusion in the 1949 film *Neptune's Daughter*.
- Established Frank Music Corporation, a major publishing powerhouse that discovered and nurtured rising theatrical songwriting talents across Broadway.
- Celebrated for his distinct ear for vernacular dialogue, contrapuntal vocal writing, and musical characterization, seamlessly balancing comic brassiness with emotional vulnerability.
