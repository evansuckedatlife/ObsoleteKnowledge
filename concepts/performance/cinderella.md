## summary

**Cinderella**, known in classic international variations as **Cendrillon** and **Aschenputtel**, is an archetypal folk narrative recounting the unjust persecution, magical aid, and ultimate elevation of a virtuous heroine. Circulating globally across hundreds of oral variants from antiquity through the early modern era, its dominant literary treatments were codified by *Charles Perrault* in seventeenth-century *[[france]]* and the *Brothers Grimm* in nineteenth-century *[[germany]]*. The narrative serves as a vital touchstone in world folklore, inspiring enduring masterworks across the operatic repertoire, classical *[[ballet]]*, and twentieth-century *[[musical-theatre]]*.

## you gotta know

- The basic narrative pattern follows an orphaned or mistreated girl relegated to domestic servitude by a cruel stepfamily, who attends a royal gathering through supernatural intervention and is subsequently identified by a lost shoe.
- *Charles Perrault* published the most universally recognized Western version in his 1697 collection *Histoires ou contes du temps passé*, which introduced the pumpkin carriage, animal coachmen, the midnight curfew, and the delicate glass slipper.
- The *Brothers Grimm* recorded the grimmer German variant, **Aschenputtel**, in 1812, featuring a magical hazel tree planted at the mother's grave, helpful white doves, and the severe physical mutilation and blinding of the stepsisters.
- *Sergei Prokofiev* composed a celebrated 1945 *[[ballet]]* score for the story, distinguished by lush orchestral color, shifting waltzes, and expressive *[[dance]]* movements premiered by the Bolshoi Ballet.
- *Richard Rodgers* and *Oscar Hammerstein II* adapted the story into a landmark 1957 television broadcast starring *Julie Andrews*, establishing an influential standard for family-oriented *[[musical-theatre]]*.
- *Gioachino Rossini* adapted the tale into his 1817 dramma giocoso opera *La Cenerentola*, replacing magical transformations with the philosophical mentor *Alidoro* and substituting a pair of matching bracelets for the slipper test.
- *Stephen Sondheim* incorporated the character into his revisionist 1987 production *[[into-the-woods-musical]]*, exploring her profound ambivalence about royal life, indecision on the palace stairs, and eventual flight toward self-reliance.
- Ancient antecedents include the Greco-Egyptian account of *Rhodopis*, documented by the geographer *Strabo*, wherein an eagle snatches a bathing maiden's sandal and drops it into the lap of the Egyptian Pharaoh.

## connections

- [[fairy-tale]] — the foundational folkloric genre in which the tale represents Aarne-Thompson-Uther tale type 510A.
- [[ballet]] — the theatrical movement tradition that embraced *Sergei Prokofiev's* dramatic, virtuosic mid-twentieth-century score.
- [[dance]] — the expressive physical medium animating both royal ballroom scenes and technical theatrical choreography.
- [[into-the-woods-musical]] — the landmark *Stephen Sondheim* production that deconstructs the heroine's romantic fulfillment and marital disillusionment.
- [[musical-theatre]] — the Broadway and television performance tradition popularized by the celebrated *Rodgers and Hammerstein* adaptation.
- [[france]] — the cultural setting for *Charles Perrault's* aristocratic, moralizing salon version that fixed the glass slipper motif.
- [[germany]] — the homeland of the *Brothers Grimm*, whose folkloric compilation documented the raw, punitive rural variant.
