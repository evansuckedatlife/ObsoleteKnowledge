## summary

**Hans Christian Andersen** was a nineteenth-century Danish author and poet whose original literary fairy tales transformed storytelling and became lasting cornerstones of world theatre and dance. Rising from poverty in Odense, he traveled to Copenhagen as a teenager seeking a stage career at the Royal Theatre before finding his true calling in imaginative prose. Masterpieces such as *The Little Mermaid*, *The Snow Queen*, and *The Ugly Duckling* departed from traditional folklore by pairing fantasy with poignant emotional melancholy and social satire. His celebrated tales achieved worldwide acclaim and established him as one of the most frequently adapted authors in [[ballet]], [[opera]], and [[musical-theatre]].

## you gotta know

- Penned landmark fairy tales including *The Little Mermaid*, *The Emperor's New Clothes*, *The Ugly Duckling*, and *The Snow Queen*, establishing the archetype of the named author crafting original fables rather than compiling folklore.
- His bittersweet narrative *The Little Mermaid*, in which the heroine dissolves into sea foam to attain an immortal soul, inspired John Neumeier's acclaimed modern [[ballet]] and Disney's Broadway production in [[musical-theatre]].
- Conceived *The Snow Queen*, a tale following *Gerda* on an arduous journey to rescue *Kay* from an icy palace, which provided the narrative framework for Disney's animated film and stage musical *Frozen*.
- Wrote *The Nightingale* out of devotion to soprano *Jenny Lind*, an allegorical fable contrasting a woodland songbird with a mechanical jewel that Igor Stravinsky later adapted into his modernist lyric [[opera]] *Le Rossignol*.
- Authored the dark moral parable *The Red Shoes*, about a girl condemned to dance uncontrollably until an executioner amputates her feet, inspiring the classic 1948 ballet film and Matthew Bourne's dance adaptation.
- Began his theatrical career in Copenhagen attempting to work as an actor, singer, and dancer at the Royal Theatre, where director *Jonas Collin* secured the royal patronage that funded his schooling.
- His tragic alpine tale *The Ice-Maiden* inspired Igor Stravinsky's homage [[ballet]] *Le Baiser de la fée*, which was later choreographed for the New York City Ballet by [[george-balanchine]].
- Entertained salons by reciting stories while cutting elaborate paper silhouettes, though his awkwardness strained an 1857 visit to [[charles-dickens]], who noted that Andersen had overstayed his welcome by five weeks.

## connections

- [[fairy-tale]] — the foundational genre he revolutionized by creating original, psychologically nuanced stories rather than documenting folklore.
- [[ballet]] — the classical and modern dance tradition that repeatedly staged his stories, notably *The Red Shoes* and *The Steadfast Tin Soldier*.
- [[musical-theatre]] — the popular stage medium that adapted his works into productions such as *Once Upon a Mattress* and *The Little Mermaid*.
- [[opera]] — the dramatic musical genre that set his poetic narratives, highlighted by Igor Stravinsky's adaptation of *The Nightingale*.
- [[george-balanchine]] — twentieth-century choreographer who staged dance works drawn from his stories, including *The Fairy's Kiss* and *The Steadfast Tin Soldier*.
- [[charles-dickens]] — Victorian literary contemporary whose household friendship soured after Andersen awkwardly overstayed his visit at Gads Hill Place in 1857.
