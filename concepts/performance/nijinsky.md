## summary

**Vaslav Nijinsky** was a Russian dancer and choreographer of unparalleled technical virtuosity and artistic daring whose brief but revolutionary career redefined what the male dancer could express and accomplish. Rising to stardom with the Ballets Russes under Sergei Diaghilev, he became a legendary performer and created controversial choreography that challenged classical ballet's formal conventions. Though his creative period lasted only a few years before mental illness curtailed his work, his influence on modern dance and ballet aesthetics remains immeasurable.

## you gotta know

- Born in 1889 in Kiev; trained at the Imperial Ballet School in St. Petersburg, where he became the protégé of Sergei Diaghilev.
- Joined the Ballets Russes in 1909 as its principal dancer and quickly became the company's superstar, known for extraordinary elevation, speed, and an androgynous stage presence that captivated European audiences.
- Performed principal roles in *Michel Fokine* choreography including *The Firebird* (1910) and *Petrushka* (1911), becoming the dancer through whom Fokine's revolutionary vision was realized.
- Choreographed *L'Après-midi d'un faune* (1912), a controversial work of deliberate earthiness and sexuality that shocked conservative audiences and marked the beginning of his career as an innovator.
- Created *The Rite of Spring* (1913) to Stravinsky's revolutionary score, choreography that presaged modernism and modern dance; the premiere sparked a famous riot in Paris due to its radical rejection of classical ballet conventions.
- His choreography emphasized angularity, ground-based movement, and psychological intensity—a stark departure from Petipa's verticality and ethereal aesthetics.
- Suffered a mental health crisis in 1917, spending much of his remaining life institutionalized; died in 1950, leaving a body of work smaller than his legend.
- Revolutionized the role of the male dancer from supporting partner to commanding virtuoso and artist-creator.
