## summary

**American folk culture** encompasses the oral lore, vernacular melodies, social dances, and communal religious hymns created by rural and immigrant pioneer communities throughout the [[united-states]]. During the early-to-mid twentieth century, these regional settler expressions became vital creative source material for stage artists seeking an authentic national idiom within [[modernism]]. By translating the unadorned simplicity of Appalachian ballads and Shaker worship into concert works, creators helped define a distinct artistic identity for American [[ballet]], concert music, and [[modern-dance]].

## you gotta know

- Composer [[copland]] famously established a distinctively American populist musical vocabulary by adapting rural fiddle tunes and frontier lore, quoting the 1848 Shaker hymn *Simple Gifts* in the ballet *Appalachian Spring* and weaving traditional cowboy tunes into *Billy the Kid* and *Rodeo*.
- Pioneer choreographer [[martha-graham]] shaped the theatrical depiction of frontier settler life by commissioning *Appalachian Spring*, staging a mythic narrative of a 19th-century pioneer husband, bride, and revivalist preacher that mirrored American democratic resilience during [[world-war-ii]].
- Choreographer *Agnes de Mille* fundamentally altered Broadway [[musical-theatre]] by synthesizing classical [[ballet]] with authentic American square dancing and country reels, establishing vernacular movement as a serious narrative vehicle in [[oklahoma]] and [[carousel]].
- Communal religious traditions, especially those of the celibate United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing, or *Shakers*, provided seminal artistic material through ecstatic sacred dances and enduring devotional hymns that celebrated humility and labour.
- African-American spirituals, work songs, and Southern devotional rites served as the bedrock for modern masterworks, exemplified by choreographer [[alvin-ailey]] in his seminal 1960 concert dance *Revelations*, which translated church sorrow songs and baptism hymns into high art.
- Extensive field recordings sponsored by the Library of Congress during the Great Depression, conducted by folklorists *John Lomax* and *Alan Lomax*, preserved thousands of traditional regional folk songs, cowboy laments, and field hollers that served as direct references for modernist theatrical adaptations.
- The musical heritage of the Appalachian Mountains preserved seventeenth-century English ballads and Scottish fiddle tunes in isolated mountain communities, maintaining modal scale structures that twentieth-century composers utilized to depict expansive rural landscapes.

## connections

- [[copland]] — seminal American composer who incorporated frontier songs, cowboy ballads, and Shaker hymns into landmark orchestral and theatrical scores.
- [[martha-graham]] — titan of [[modern-dance]] who commissioned and choreographed *Appalachian Spring* to evoke American pioneer spirit.
- [[oklahoma]] — Rodgers and Hammerstein production that established vernacular square dance and folk idioms as narrative engines in [[musical-theatre]].
- [[modernism]] — overarching twentieth-century artistic movement in which innovators reclaimed provincial folk expressions to forge high modern art.
- [[alvin-ailey]] — modern dance pioneer who celebrated rural Black folk traditions and spirituals in works like *Revelations*.
- [[carousel]] — mid-century stage musical capturing New England coastal folkways, community customs, and vernacular social dance.
- [[united-states]] — sovereign nation whose regional settler histories, rural folklore, and religious migrations provided this subject's underlying material.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict that intensified domestic demand for comforting, patriotic folk mythologies in the performing arts.
