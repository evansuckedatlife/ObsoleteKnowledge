## summary

**Chance Procedure**, also known across modern arts as **aleatoric composition** and **indeterminacy**, is an avant-garde creative technique in which elements of creation and performance are determined through random operations. Developed primarily in mid-twentieth-century [[new-york-city]] by composer *John Cage* and choreographer [[merce-cunningham]], the method sought to liberate artistic expression from traditional psychological habits, personal ego, and conventional narrative structure. Its application fundamentally reshaped the trajectory of [[modern-dance]], music, and experimental performance under high [[modernism]].

## you gotta know

- Utilized ancient divination texts, most notably the Chinese *I Ching* (*Book of Changes*), casting coins or yarrow stalks to determine pitches, durations, tempos, and spatial trajectories.
- Separated dance and music entirely, allowing [[merce-cunningham]] and *John Cage* to create movement and sound independently so that both components coincided only during the live performance.
- Radically departed from the emotionally driven, expressive choreography of [[martha-graham]], rejecting dramatic narrative in favor of pure, non-referential kinetic energy.
- Employed randomized compositional aids including dice rolls, mathematical tables, imperfect imperfections on paper, and computer algorithms to make decisions outside artistic intention.
- Introduced decentralized stage space, treating all areas of the stage as equally significant rather than subordinating movement to a central, front-facing focal point.
- Embraced ambient noise and unexpected external events as integral components of performance, most notoriously demonstrated in the silent 1952 composition *4'33"*.
- Influenced subsequent postmodern choreographers and interdisciplinary collectives, directly informing the radical experiments of the *Judson Dance Theater* and movement improvisation.

## connections

- [[merce-cunningham]] — the visionary choreographer who made aleatoric operations the foundational methodology of modern stage movement.
- [[modern-dance]] — the twentieth-century genre revolutionized by replacing theatrical pantomime with decentralized choreographic experiments.
- [[modernism]] — the broader philosophical and artistic movement championing radical experimentation and the dismantling of classical forms.
- [[martha-graham]] — the pioneer of expressive physical contraction and release whose narrative psychological works prompted the aleatoric rebellion.
- [[united-states]] — the nation where the mid-century avant-garde flourished across downtown lofts and universities.
- [[realism]] — the conventional mimetic tradition that aleatoric artists sought to transcend through objective randomness.
