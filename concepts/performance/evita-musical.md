## summary

*Evita* is a 1978 musical by composer *Andrew Lloyd Webber* and lyricist *Tim Rice* that chronicles the rise of *Eva Perón* from illegitimate poverty to First Lady of Argentina and a beloved icon who died of cancer at age 33. The show transformed musical theater by combining operatic ambition with pop-rock idioms and complex character development, and it announced both composers as major voices in Broadway's future. *Evita* won the Tony Award for Best Musical and secured Lloyd Webber's reputation as the defining Broadway composer of the late twentieth century.

## you gotta know

- Based loosely on the life of *Eva Perón* (1919–1952), who married Argentine president *Juan Perón* and became a charismatic voice for the working poor before her early death.
- Premiered in London in 1978 before transferring to Broadway, where it opened simultaneously with the major commercial film *Evita* starring Madonna (1996), widening its cultural reach.
- The musical reframes Eva's ambition and seduction as political performance—she climbs using charm, sexuality, and manipulation as tools rather than naive ascent.
- Features the iconic anthem "Don't Cry for Me, Argentina," a defiant declaration sung from a presidential balcony that has become one of musical theater's most recognizable numbers.
- Lloyd Webber's score moves between lavish operatic passages, tango rhythms, and contemporary rock idioms, reflecting Argentina's cultural hybridity and Eva's refusal to fit a single mold.
- The Che Guevara character (unlike the historical figure) serves as narrator and moral antagonist, critiquing Eva's mythology and self-aggrandizement throughout.
- Sparked a major film adaptation (1996) that solidified the musical's place in global pop culture and introduced its songs to audiences who had never seen the stage show.
