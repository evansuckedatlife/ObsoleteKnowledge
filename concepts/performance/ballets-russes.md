## summary

The **Ballets Russes** was a groundbreaking ballet company founded by impresario *Sergei Diaghilev* in 1909 that revolutionized dance and art through radical collaborations across music, visual design, and choreography. Operating out of Paris, the company premiered some of the 20th century's most influential works and elevated ballet from a court entertainment to a modern art form. Its legacy reshaped the cultural landscape of early modernism.

## you gotta know

- Founded by *Sergei Diaghilev*, a Russian impresario with vision and access to elite artists across disciplines.
- Premiered *The Firebird* (1910) with choreography by *Fokine* and *Stravinsky's* revolutionary score, establishing the template for modernist ballet.
- Introduced western European audiences to Russian art, music, and dance, making Paris the new center of ballet innovation.
- Commissioned works from leading composers including *Stravinsky*, *Debussy*, and *Erik Satie*.
- Featured breakthrough choreography by *Fokine*, *Nijinsky*, and later *Balanchine*, each pushing the boundaries of what ballet could express.
- Collaborated with visual artists including *Picasso*, *Matisse*, and *Cocteau*, treating the entire stage as a canvas for modernist aesthetics.
- The company's production values emphasized spectacular costume design and avant-garde stage design as integral to the artistic vision.
- Dissolved in 1929 following Diaghilev's death, but its influence shaped ballet companies, modern dance, and theatrical innovation worldwide.

## connections

- [[fokine]] — the principal choreographer whose works defined the company's early revolutionary period.
- [[nijinsky]] — the dancer-choreographer whose *Le Sacre du Printemps* caused a famous riot.
- [[stravinsky]] — the composer whose scores became synonymous with the company's artistic vision.
- [[the-firebird]] — the company's breakthrough work that announced its modernist mission.
- [[petrushka]] — another signature Ballets Russes success merging Russian tradition with theatrical innovation.
- [[modernism]] — the artistic movement the company embodied and helped define.
- [[paris]] — the city that became the company's home and the capital of its cultural influence.
