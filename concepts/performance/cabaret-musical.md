## summary

**Cabaret**, often indexed as **Cabaret (musical)**, is a landmark 1966 stage musical featuring music by *John Kander*, lyrics by *Fred Ebb*, and a book by *Joe Masteroff*. Set against the decaying backdrop of the Weimar Republic in late-1920s Berlin, [[germany]], the show uses the hedonistic entertainment of the *Kit Kat Klub* as a biting metaphor for the ominous rise of totalitarianism before [[world-war-ii]]. The piece revolutionized [[broadway-musicals]] by pioneering the conceptual musical structure and achieved legendary status through its cinematic adaptation directed by [[bob-fosse]].

## you gotta know

- Adapted from *Christopher Isherwood*'s semi-autobiographical 1939 novella *Goodbye to Berlin* and *John Van Druten*'s 1951 stage adaptation *I Am a Camera*.
- Centered on the grotesque, omniscient *Emcee* of the *Kit Kat Klub*, an iconic role originated on Broadway by *Joel Grey*, who served as a sardonic Greek chorus commenting on creeping political collapse.
- Directed on Broadway by *Harold Prince*, the production won eight *Tony Awards* in 1967, including Best Musical, and established the viability of dark, socially conscious musical drama.
- Features prominent musical numbers including the defiant title song *"Cabaret"*, the sinister Nazi anthem *"Tomorrow Belongs to Me"*, and the satiric duet *"Money, Money"*.
- The celebrated 1972 film adaptation directed and choreographed by [[bob-fosse]] won eight Academy Awards, starring *Liza Minnelli* as the tragic British expatriate singer *Sally Bowles*.
- Eliminated traditional musical breaks in the film version by restricting nearly all songs to the physical stage of the club, using parallel editing to contrast musical numbers with street violence.
- Revived dramatically in 1993 by *Sam Mendes* at London's *Donmar Warehouse*, a production that reimagined the venue as an immersive, sleazy nightclub and recast the *Emcee* with *Alan Cumming*.

## connections

- [[bob-fosse]] — the groundbreaking choreographer and filmmaker whose visionary 1972 movie adaptation set a new aesthetic standard for dance on film.
- [[chicago-musical]] — the subsequent dark musical satire by *Kander* and *Ebb* directed by *Fosse*, sharing its cynical vaudevillian framing.
- [[broadway-musicals]] — the theatrical institution where the original stage production shattered expectations for musical subject matter.
- [[carol-channing]] — fellow mid-1960s Broadway superstar who dominated commercial musical theater during the same pivotal decade.
- [[germany]] — the historical setting whose doomed Weimar cultural blossoming and subsequent fascism form the core narrative drama.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the catastrophic global conflict foreshadowed by the rising nationalist terror portrayed throughout the plot.
- [[modernism]] — the aesthetic and philosophical movement whose principles of Brechtian alienation inspired the show's structure.
