## summary

**Fred Ebb** was an acclaimed American musical theatre lyricist who formed one of Broadway's most enduring and influential writing partnerships alongside composer *John Kander*. Known for cynical wit, biting irony, and incisive dramatic rhythm, he created unforgettable theatrical scores including *Cabaret*, *Chicago*, and *Kiss of the Spider Woman*. His sharp wordplay and affinity for backstage razzle-dazzle redefined modern musical storytelling, providing director-choreographer *Bob Fosse* with the cynical theatrical structures that epitomized twentieth-century musical cynicism.

## you gotta know

- Formed the legendary songwriting duo *Kander and Ebb* with composer *John Kander* in 1962, collaborating continuously for more than four decades until his death in 2004.
- Wrote the lyrics for *Cabaret* in 1966, utilizing decadent Weimar-era nightclub songs as metaphorical commentary on rising fascism in pre-war *Germany*.
- Collaborated with *Bob Fosse* to write the book and lyrics for the 1975 vaudeville-inspired satire *Chicago*, skewering celebrity culture, yellow journalism, and judicial corruption in 1920s America.
- Penned the anthemic lyrics to *Theme from New York, New York*, composed for *Martin Scorsese*'s 1977 film and famously made world-renowned by *Liza Minnelli* and *Frank Sinatra*.
- Maintained a lifelong creative partnership with star *Liza Minnelli*, writing her breakthrough Broadway vehicle *Flora the Red Menace* as well as her Emmy-winning television concert special *Liza with a Z*.
- Received Tony Awards for Best Original Score for *Cabaret*, *Woman of the Year*, and *Kiss of the Spider Woman*, exploring themes of political persecution, moral compromise, and institutional cruelty.
- Pioneered the concept musical format alongside director *Harold Prince*, using cabaret acts and theatrical pastiche to frame thematic commentary rather than linear romantic plots.
