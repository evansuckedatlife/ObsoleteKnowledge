## summary

**Proton** is a positively charged subatomic particle and one of the two main constituents of the atomic nucleus. It is a baryon composed of three quarks (two up quarks and one down quark) and is extraordinarily stable—protons have never been observed to decay. The number of protons in an atom defines its chemical identity and atomic number.

## you gotta know

- Carries a positive electric charge equal and opposite to the electron's negative charge.
- Composed of quarks bound by the strong nuclear force; classified as a *baryon*.
- Nearly 1,836 times more massive than an electron, yet vastly smaller.
- The nucleus of a hydrogen atom—the simplest and most abundant element in the universe.
- Stable over cosmic timescales; proton decay (if it occurs) has an upper limit of 10^34 years.
- When a proton encounters its antimatter counterpart, an *antiproton*, both annihilate into pure energy.
- Confined inside nuclei by the *standard model*'s strong force, which increases in strength with distance.
- The proton's stability underlies atomic matter itself; without protons, chemistry and complex structures would be impossible.
- In particle accelerators, protons collide at near-light speed to unlock the signatures of exotic particles and test fundamental theories.
- The proton-to-electron mass ratio (~1,836) is a dimensionless fundamental constant whose origin remains a deep question in physics.

## connections

- [[baryon]] — the class of particles that includes protons and neutrons.
- [[hadron]] — the broader particle family bound by the strong force.
- [[quark]] — the fundamental constituents (up and down quarks) that make up protons.
- [[nucleus]] — the dense core where protons cluster with neutrons.
- [[standard-model]] — the physics framework explaining proton composition and behavior.
- [[atom]] — the smallest chemical unit, defined by its proton count.
- [[antimatter]] — antiprotons mirror protons but annihilate on contact.
- [[electromagnetism]] — the force mediating proton interactions via photons.
- [[nuclear-fission]] — the splitting of nuclei that releases proton-containing fragments and energy.
