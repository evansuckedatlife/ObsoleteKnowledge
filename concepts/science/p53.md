## summary

**p53**, also known as **tumor protein p53** or by its human gene symbol **TP53**, is a pivotal nuclear transcription factor and tumor suppressor [[protein]] that coordinates cellular responses to genomic stress. First identified in 1979 in transformed mammalian cells, it is famously celebrated as the *guardian of the genome* for preserving chromosomal stability and preventing oncogenesis across animal [[evolution]]. By dictating whether an injured [[cell]] arrests its growth for repair or undergoes programmed elimination, it serves as the ultimate barrier against malignant transformation and ranks as the most frequently mutated molecular target in human [[cancer]].

## you gotta know

- Nicknamed the *guardian of the genome* by *David Lane* in 1992 because it maintains genetic fidelity by coordinating cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, and programmed cell death in response to physiological stress.
- Mutated or functionally inactivated in more than fifty percent of all human [[cancer]] cases, representing the single most frequent genetic lesion across human oncology and an indicator of poor prognosis in many tumors.
- Inherited as a defective germline allele in *Li-Fraumeni syndrome*, an autosomal dominant predisposition disorder that causes early-onset sarcomas, breast cancers, brain tumors, leukemias, and adrenocortical carcinomas across multiple generations.
- Halts the [[cell-cycle]] at the G1/S transition checkpoint following [[dna]] damage by transcribing *p21* (*CDKN1A*), a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor that blocks cyclin E-CDK2 and cyclin D-CDK4 complexes from phosphorylating the retinoblastoma protein.
- Triggers [[apoptosis]] when lesions prove irreparable by upregulating pro-apoptotic *Bcl-2* family proteins including *BAX*, *PUMA*, and *NOXA*, which oligomerize to permeabilize the mitochondrial outer membrane and release cytochrome c into the cytoplasm.
- Kept at low basal levels in unstressed cells by *MDM2*, an E3 ubiquitin ligase [[enzyme]] that targets it for proteasomal degradation in a negative feedback loop disrupted when *ATM* and *CHK2* kinases phosphorylate its amino terminus.
- Neutralized by oncogenic viral proteins such as the *E6* oncoprotein of high-risk human papillomavirus, which recruits the ubiquitin ligase *E6AP* for degradation, as well as adenovirus *E1B-55K* and simian virus 40 *large T-antigen*.
- Assembles as a functional homotetramer within the [[nucleus]] and was initially misclassified as an oncogene upon its 1979 discovery because early isolates were mutant forms; its name reflects an apparent electrophoretic mobility of 53 kilodaltons on SDS-PAGE caused by proline-rich retardation, despite an actual mass of only 43.7 kilodaltons.

## connections

- [[cancer]] — malignant state that emerges in over half of all human cases through inactivating mutations or deletions of this suppressor.
- [[cell-cycle]] — cellular progression sequence arrested at the G1/S transition to prevent replication of damaged genetic material.
- [[cell]] — fundamental unit of life whose genomic integrity, division, senescence, and viability are governed by this surveillance mechanism.
- [[dna]] — genetic polymer containing the damage lesions that trigger activation as well as the sequence-specific response elements bound by this factor.
- [[protein]] — macromolecular class to which this tetrameric transcription factor, its kinase activators, and its downstream effectors belong.
- [[nucleus]] — cellular compartment where this factor accumulates, binds target promoter sequences, and orchestrates gene transcription.
- [[enzyme]] — catalytic class exemplified by the ubiquitin ligase *MDM2* and sensor kinases like *ATM* that tightly control its stability.
