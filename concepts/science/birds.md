## summary

**Birds** (class *Aves*) are feathered, egg-laying vertebrates descended from theropod dinosaurs, representing a living lineage that survived the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago. They possess hollow bones, specialized respiratory systems, high metabolic rates, and complex behaviors including flight. Birds radiated into over 10,000 species occupying nearly every terrestrial and marine ecosystem, making them the most successful amniote vertebrates alive today.

## you gotta know

- Birds are the direct evolutionary descendants of small theropod dinosaurs, having evolved feathers and powered flight during the Jurassic Period.
- Archaeopteryx, discovered in 1861, exhibits both dinosaurian traits and bird features, providing key fossil evidence for evolutionary transition.
- The Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction 66 million years ago eliminated non-avian dinosaurs but allowed birds to diversify and dominate vertebrate niches.
- Feathers serve multiple functions: insulation, waterproofing, display, and most critically, enabling powered flight through precisely structured flight feathers.
- Birds have elevated metabolic rates fueled by efficient lungs with air sacs, requiring high food intake to maintain body temperature.
- Complex behaviors including tool use, problem solving, abstract thought, and elaborate social hierarchies mark birds as highly intelligent animals.
- Modern birds range from the 5-gram bee hummingbird to the 130-kilogram ostrich, occupying ecological roles from predators to seed dispersers to filter feeders.
- Migration patterns in many bird species reflect evolutionary adaptations to seasonal resource availability, with some species traveling over 40,000 miles annually.
- Bird cognitive abilities include tool use, long-term memory, social learning, and metacognition, with corvids and parrots demonstrating reasoning abilities approaching some primates.
