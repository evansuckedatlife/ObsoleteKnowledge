## summary

**Meteorology** is the scientific study of Earth's atmosphere, weather phenomena, and climate dynamics. It integrates physics, chemistry, and thermodynamics to understand how solar energy, atmospheric circulation, and water cycle drive weather patterns and long-term climate. Meteorology has evolved from folklore and observation into a quantitative predictive science, essential for agriculture, aviation, and climate policy.

## you gotta know

- The atmosphere consists of distinct layers: troposphere (where weather occurs), stratosphere (ozone), mesosphere, thermosphere, and exosphere, each with characteristic temperature and composition gradients.
- Solar radiation drives atmospheric circulation: equatorial regions receive more direct sunlight, heating air that rises and spreads poleward, creating jet streams and pressure belts.
- The water cycle connects ocean evaporation, atmospheric moisture transport, and precipitation, redistributing heat and freshwater globally.
- Air pressure, temperature, and humidity are the key observables; meteorologists model their interactions using partial differential equations (Navier-Stokes for fluid motion).
- Weather fronts form where air masses of different temperature and humidity collide, driving convection, cloud formation, and precipitation.
- The Coriolis effect deflects moving air rightward (in Northern Hemisphere) and leftward (Southern), shaping wind patterns and enabling the formation of cyclones and anticyclones.
- Climate models predict long-term atmospheric behavior by accounting for greenhouse gases, ocean heat capacity, and albedo (reflectivity), essential to understanding anthropogenic warming.
- Atmospheric instability occurs when warm air underlies cool air, creating updrafts that fuel severe thunderstorms, tornadoes, and mesoscale convective systems.
- The thermodynamic relationship between dew point, air temperature, and relative humidity determines cloud formation and the potential for precipitation in a given air mass.
- Numerical weather prediction uses computational models initialized with vast observational networks (weather stations, radar, satellites, buoys) to forecast atmospheric evolution over days to weeks.
- Synoptic meteorology examines weather patterns at scales of 1000+ kilometers, tracking air masses and frontal systems using surface and upper-atmosphere observations.
