## summary

**Molecular biology** is the study of biological processes at the molecular scale: how *DNA* encodes genetic information, how that information is copied during replication, and how it is expressed into proteins that execute cellular function. Since the 1950s, when *Watson* and *Crick* elucidated DNA's double-helix structure, molecular biology has unified genetics, biochemistry, and cell biology, revealing the chemical mechanisms of life itself.

## you gotta know

- DNA consists of four nucleotide bases (adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine) paired specifically (A-T, G-C) on two complementary strands wound in a double helix.
- During DNA replication, the double helix unwinds; each strand serves as a template for a new complementary strand, yielding two identical daughter DNA molecules.
- The Meselson-Stahl experiment (1958) used isotope labeling to prove that DNA replication is semi-conservative: each new DNA molecule retains one original strand and one newly synthesized strand.
- Transcription copies DNA into messenger RNA (mRNA), carrying genetic instructions from the nucleus to ribosomes in the cytoplasm.
- Translation converts mRNA codons into amino acid sequences via transfer RNAs (tRNAs) and ribosomal machinery, building proteins according to the genetic blueprint.
- Mutations—deletions, substitutions, or insertions—alter DNA sequence and can change protein structure and function, driving evolution and disease.
- Molecular biology enabled genetic engineering and biotechnology: restriction enzymes cut DNA; plasmids carry foreign genes into cells; polymerase chain reactions amplify DNA for analysis.
- The genetic code is nearly universal across life: three-nucleotide codons encode 20 amino acids, with redundancy allowing multiple codons to code for the same amino acid.
- DNA damage from radiation, chemical exposure, or replication errors is detected and repaired by cellular machinery; defective repair systems lead to cancer and genetic instability.
- Regulation of gene expression occurs at multiple levels: transcription (RNA polymerase access), post-transcriptional modification (RNA splicing), translation (ribosome binding), and post-translational protein modification.
- The central dogma (DNA → RNA → Protein) describes information flow in cells; reverse transcriptase in retroviruses reverses this dogma by synthesizing DNA from RNA.
- Horizontal gene transfer in bacteria and horizontal transfer in eukaryotes (via viruses or other mechanisms) complicates the tree of life by allowing genetic material exchange between distantly related organisms.
