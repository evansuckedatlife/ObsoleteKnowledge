## summary

**Genes** are discrete functional units of DNA that encode instructions for building proteins or producing RNA molecules that cells need to function. They are the molecular basis of heredity — genes are passed from parents to offspring and determine inherited traits. A gene's specific sequence of nucleotides dictates the sequence of amino acids in a protein, linking genotype (genetic makeup) to phenotype (observable traits).

## you gotta know

- Genes consist of a promoter region (where RNA polymerase binds), the coding sequence, and regulatory elements that control when and how often the gene is transcribed.
- One gene typically codes for one polypeptide (protein); some genes produce non-coding RNA (rRNA, tRNA, miRNA) instead.
- Different versions of a gene at the same location are called [[alleles]]; they may differ by one nucleotide or larger stretches.
- Genes are expressed as proteins through two steps: transcription (DNA → mRNA) and translation (mRNA → protein).
- A single mutation in a gene can change one amino acid, potentially causing disease (e.g., sickle-cell anemia from a single nucleotide change in the β-globin gene).
- Not all DNA is coding; only ~1.5% of the human genome consists of protein-coding genes; the rest includes regulatory and non-coding sequences.
