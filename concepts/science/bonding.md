## summary

**Chemical bonding** refers to the attractive physical interactions between [[atom]]s, ions, or molecules that enable the formation and stabilization of chemical compounds. Investigated empirically across centuries and formalized during the twentieth century through [[quantum-mechanics]], these interactions govern molecular geometry, bulk material properties, and chemical reactivity. Understanding the redistribution of valence electrons across adjacent atomic nuclei is central to chemistry, materials science, and molecular biology.

## you gotta know

- Mediated predominantly by valence electrons seeking thermodynamically stable configurations, traditionally approximated by *Gilbert N. Lewis* through the octet rule.
- Categorized into primary strong bonds: covalent bonds formed by shared electron pairs, ionic bonds driven by electrostatic attraction between opposing charges, and metallic bonds featuring delocalized electron seas.
- Codified by *Linus Pauling* in his 1939 monograph *The Nature of the Chemical Bond*, introducing orbital hybridization concepts and an electronegativity scale to predict partial ionic character.
- Described comprehensively by molecular orbital theory, wherein atomic orbitals combine linearly to form constructive bonding and destructive antibonding orbitals characterized by a spatial [[wavefunction]].
- Evaluated quantitatively using measurable physical properties including bond dissociation energy, equilibrium bond length, and electric dipole moment, which correlate directly with bond order.
- Complemented by secondary intermolecular interactions such as hydrogen bonding and London dispersion forces, which dictate liquid cohesion and macromolecular folding without establishing permanent primary connections.
- Governs tangible real-world chemical properties, from the dense covalent networks of [[carbon]] to the ionic dissociation that activates aqueous disinfectants like [[bleach]].

## connections

- [[atom]] — the fundamental elemental units whose valence electron shells interact to assemble molecules.
- [[electron]] — the subatomic leptons whose exchange, sharing, and delocalization produce all chemical attractions.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the physical theory describing molecular orbital mixing, exchange symmetry, and electronic wavefunctions.
- [[carbon]] — the tetravalent element whose versatile sp, sp2, and sp3 hybridizations underpin organic chemistry.
- [[oxygen]] — the strongly electronegative element that forms polar covalent bonds and drives extensive hydrogen bonding.
- [[sodium]] — the electropositive alkali metal that readily surrenders its valence electron to produce ionic salts.
- [[bleach]] — a common chemical disinfectant whose sodium hypochlorite active ingredient features both ionic and polar covalent interactions.
- [[bjorken-scaling]] — the deep inelastic scattering phenomenon that explores subatomic structure at momentum transfers far beyond the energy regime of valence bonds.
