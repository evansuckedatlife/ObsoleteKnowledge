## summary

**Astrobiology** is the multidisciplinary study of the conditions, origins, and distribution of life in the universe beyond Earth. It bridges biology, chemistry, physics, and astronomy to investigate whether life exists elsewhere, how it might arise, and where to search most productively. Astrobiology reframes Earth's biosphere as one possible manifestation of life's chemistry, not the template, asking what extremes life can tolerate and what chemical signatures reveal life's presence.

## you gotta know

- Astrobiology asks three fundamental questions: How did life originate? Does life exist elsewhere? What does the future of life in the universe hold?
- Extremophiles—organisms thriving in Earth's harshest environments (boiling springs, frozen poles, acidic lakes, deep-sea vents)—expand our conception of habitability, suggesting life could survive in unexpected cosmic niches.
- Liquid water, chemical energy, and organic molecules are considered the minimum prerequisites for life as understood; they are sought actively on Mars, *Europa*, *Enceladus*, and elsewhere.
- Panspermia, the hypothesis that life originated elsewhere and was transported to Earth via meteorites or comets, is testable through examining meteorite geochemistry and searching for viable microbes in space.
- The Drake Equation attempts to quantify the number of detectable civilizations in the galaxy by multiplying factors: habitable planets, abiogenesis rates, civilization longevity, and communication technology.
- Biosignatures—atmospheric gases, chemical imbalances, or patterns indicating biological activity—are the tools astrobiologists use to detect life remotely via spectroscopy.
- Moons like *Europa* (hidden subsurface ocean under ice) and *Enceladus* (cryovolcanism venting water) are among the most promising targets for discovering extraterrestrial microbial life.

## connections

- [[europa]] — a moon of Jupiter with a subsurface ocean, prime target for astrobiology missions.
- [[enceladus]] — Saturn's moon with geysers venting water, a leading candidate for extraterrestrial life.
- [[extremophile]] — organisms surviving extreme conditions, expanding the definition of habitability.
- [[panspermia]] — the theory of life's interplanetary or interstellar distribution.
- [[habitability]] — the set of conditions permitting life to exist.
- [[nasa]] — the primary funder and executor of astrobiology research missions.
