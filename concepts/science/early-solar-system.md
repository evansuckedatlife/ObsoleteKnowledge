## summary

The **Early Solar System** was a violent, chaotic period roughly 4.6 to 4.5 billion years ago when planets were still forming through collisions of planetary bodies. Massive impacts—including the giant impact that created Earth's [[moon]]—shaped planetary chemistry, structure, and the distribution of water and organic molecules across the terrestrial planets.

## you gotta know

- The solar system formed from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust roughly 4.6 billion years ago; gravity concentrated material into the central sun and a swirling protoplanetary disk.
- Planetary embryos (planetary "seeds") grew to Mars-size within millions of years, then collided to form terrestrial planets like Earth.
- Collisions at this stage were not gentle accumulations but violent impacts at orbital speeds, melting material and releasing enormous energy.
- The [[giant-impact-hypothesis]] proposes that a Mars-sized object called *Theia* struck Earth, ejecting debris that coalesced into the Moon.
- The "Late Heavy Bombardment" (roughly 4.1 to 3.8 billion years ago) involved intense asteroid and comet impacts, possibly triggered by gravitational scattering of gas giants.
- These impacts delivered significant water, iron, and carbon-based compounds to Earth; without them, Earth's oceans and life chemistry would be radically different.
- Evidence from meteorites, lunar samples, and planetary physics shows that planetary migration and dynamical chaos were central to the Solar System's architecture; the giant planets likely migrated significantly from their birth locations, reshaping the system.
