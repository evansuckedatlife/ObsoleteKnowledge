## summary

A **subsurface ocean** is a body of liquid water hidden beneath the solid icy crust of a moon or planetary body. These oceans, potentially harboring life, exist in the outer solar system on [[europa]] (Jupiter's moon), [[enceladus]] (Saturn's moon), [[ganymede]] (also Jupiter), and others. Kept liquid by internal tidal heating and radioactive decay rather than surface warmth, subsurface oceans represent one of the most compelling targets in the search for extraterrestrial microbial life.

## you gotta know

- Europa's subsurface ocean may contain twice as much liquid water as all of Earth's oceans combined.
- Tidal forces from the host planet's gravity continuously flex the moon, generating internal heat that prevents freezing.
- Enceladus shoots geysers of water vapor directly from its subsurface ocean through cracks in its crust, making it observable from orbit.
- Ganymede, the largest moon in the solar system, is believed to host a multilayered ocean system sandwiched between ice shells.
- The interface between subsurface water and rocky seafloors may support chemosynthetic ecosystems independent of sunlight.
- NASA and ESA missions (e.g., *Europa Clipper*, *JUICE*) are designed to detect chemical signs of habitability and life.
- Unlike Earth's oceans, subsurface moons lack direct sunlight but exploit chemical gradients and hydrothermal vents for energy.
- Ocean circulation on these moons may be driven by convection and tidal pumping rather than wind-driven currents.
- The depth and salinity of subsurface oceans can be inferred from magnetic field measurements, revealing internal structure without drilling.

## connections

- [[europa]] — prime target; likely hosts a global subsurface ocean beneath 100+ km of ice.
- [[enceladus]] — Saturn moon actively venting water; direct evidence of subsurface reservoir.
- [[ganymede]] — largest Jovian satellite; may harbor a layered ocean system.
- [[astrobiology]] — the search for life in these extreme environments.
- [[cryovolcanism]] — the mechanism by which subsurface water erupts through ice shells.
- [[exoplanet]] — subsurface oceans likely exist around other stars too.
- [[water]] — the universal solvent and prerequisite for known life.
