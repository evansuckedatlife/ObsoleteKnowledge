## summary

**Thermodynamics** is the physics of heat, work, and energy transformation in macroscopic systems. Born in the 1800s from the study of steam engines, it describes how energy flows between systems and surroundings through three fundamental laws. The second law—that entropy (disorder) always increases—dictates what spontaneous processes are possible and underpins chemistry, engines, refrigeration, and life itself.

## you gotta know

- The **first law** states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted; ΔU = Q − W.
- The **second law** asserts that entropy in an isolated system always increases, forbidding perpetual motion machines.
- The **third law** holds that absolute zero (0 K) cannot be reached; entropy approaches a minimum but never zero.
- *Enthalpy* (H) measures heat content; *Gibbs free energy* (G) predicts spontaneity: reactions proceed if ΔG < 0.
- [[catalysis|Catalysts]] accelerate reactions without altering the free energy landscape, making slow reactions kinetically feasible.
- [[cooling]] systems exploit temperature gradients and heat transfer mechanisms (conduction, convection, radiation) governed by thermodynamics.
- Predicts [[equilibrium]] conditions and why reactions reverse when driven backward; explains why perfect efficiency is impossible.
- The Carnot cycle defines the theoretical maximum efficiency of any heat engine: η = 1 − (T_cold / T_hot).
- Open systems (like organisms) can decrease local entropy by exporting it to surroundings; the universe's entropy still increases overall.
- Phase transitions (melting, boiling) are governed by competing enthalpy and entropy contributions, making them temperature-dependent.

## connections

- [[energy]] — the fundamental quantity that thermodynamics quantifies and tracks.
- [[catalysis]] — catalysts do not change ΔG or equilibrium; they lower activation barriers.
- [[temperature]] — a macroscopic measure of average molecular kinetic energy.
- [[cooling]] — applies thermodynamic principles to remove heat from systems.
- [[heat]] — transfer of energy driven by temperature differences.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — machinery that harnessed thermodynamic principles for work.
- [[equilibrium]] — the state predicted when ΔG = 0 and net change ceases.
