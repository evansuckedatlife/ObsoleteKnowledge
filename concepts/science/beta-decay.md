## summary

**Beta decay** is a fundamental mode of radioactive disintegration governed by the weak subatomic interaction in which an unstable atomic [[nucleus]] transmutes into an isobar by adjusting its internal proton-to-neutron ratio. The transition proceeds either through beta-minus emission, where a neutron transforms into a proton while emitting an [[electron]] and an electron antineutrino, or through beta-plus emission, where a proton converts into a neutron while yielding a positron and an electron neutrino. Discovered by Ernest Rutherford at the turn of the twentieth century, this phenomenon catalyzed the historical hypothesis of the neutrino and provided essential empirical foundations for electroweak unification within the [[standard-model]].

## you gotta know

- Driven by a fundamental quark flavor change mediated by weak virtual W bosons, where a down [[quark]] converts into an up [[quark]] during beta-minus emission, or an up [[quark]] into a down [[quark]] during beta-plus emission.
- Continuous energy spectrum of observed beta particles prompted Wolfgang Pauli in 1930 to propose the neutral, weakly interacting neutrino to safeguard the conservation of energy, momentum, and angular momentum.
- Enrico Fermi formulated the first quantitative mathematical theory of the process in 1934, modeling a point-like four-fermion contact interaction governed by Fermi's coupling constant.
- Parity violation in weak processes was experimentally discovered in 1956 by Chien-Shiung Wu through the asymmetric emission of beta particles from spin-polarized cobalt-60 nuclei.
- Electron capture operates as a direct competitor to beta-plus emission in proton-rich nuclides, occurring when the [[nucleus]] absorbs an inner orbital [[electron]] to convert a proton into a neutron and emit a monoenergetic neutrino.
- Selection rules categorize transitions into Fermi decays (where the emitted lepton spins couple antiparallel with zero net angular momentum change) and Gamow-Teller decays (where lepton spins align parallel with an angular momentum shift of one unit).
- Double beta decay occurs in heavy isotopes where single decay is kinematically forbidden, serving as the experimental basis for hypothetical neutrinoless searches designed to verify whether neutrinos are Majorana fermions.
- Practical engineering harnesses beta emissions from long-lived radioisotopes like nickel-63 and tritium to generate continuous electric current within a solid-state betavoltaic [[battery]].

## connections

- [[nucleus]] — the dense positive core of nucleons whose binding energy and stability dictate beta transitions.
- [[quark]] — elementary constituent of hadrons that undergoes flavor-changing weak transitions during decay.
- [[electron]] — stable lepton emitted as a beta particle in beta-minus decay or absorbed during orbital capture.
- [[standard-model]] — overarching theoretical framework describing the electroweak unification governing weak decays.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — underlying physical theory governing tunneling probabilities, phase space, and selection rules.
- [[battery]] — betavoltaic power sources convert the continuous emission of beta particles into direct electrical current.
- [[albert-einstein]] — developed mass-energy equivalence, which determines the decay energy from nuclear mass differences.
