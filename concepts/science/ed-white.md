## summary

**Ed White** was an American aeronautical engineer, Air Force officer, and NASA astronaut who became a legendary figure in human space exploration during the 1960s. On June 3, 1965, during the *Gemini 4* mission, he made history by becoming the first American to conduct an extravehicular activity, venturing outside his spacecraft into open orbit. His pioneering career came to a tragic end on January 27, 1967, when he perished alongside *Gus Grissom* and *Roger Chaffee* in the disastrous *Apollo 1* launchpad fire.

## you gotta know

- Achieved the first American extravehicular activity on June 3, 1965, stepping outside the *Gemini 4* spacecraft for twenty minutes while floating over the Pacific Ocean.
- Maneuvered in orbit using a handheld *Hand-Held Maneuvering Unit*, a compressed-oxygen zip gun that allowed him to control his orientation and translations outside the capsule.
- Famously expressed profound reluctance when ordered by flight director *Chris Kraft* to return inside the hatch, declaring that stepping back in was the saddest moment of his life.
- Graduated from the *United States Military Academy* at West Point before earning a master's degree in aeronautical engineering at the *University of Michigan*.
- Selected in September 1962 as part of the *Next Nine*, NASA's Astronaut Group 2, alongside luminaries like *Neil Armstrong* and *Jim Lovell*.
- Flew *Gemini 4* as pilot under command of his close friend and fellow Air Force pilot *James McDivitt*.
- Perished on Launch Pad 34 at *Cape Kennedy* in the cockpit of command module 012 during a plugs-out test for *Apollo 1*, killed by asphyxiation in a flash fire fueled by a pressurized pure oxygen atmosphere.
- Posthumously awarded the *Congressional Space Medal of Honor* in 1997 in recognition of his exemplary courage and pioneering contributions to spaceflight.
