## summary

**Bacteria** are single-celled prokaryotic microorganisms lacking a nucleus, constituting the majority of Earth's biomass and driving essential biogeochemical cycles. First observed by van Leeuwenhoek in the 17th century but truly understood only after germ-theory and microscopy advanced in the 19th century, bacteria are omnipresent in soil, water, air, and living organisms—both as partners in photosynthesis and digestion and as agents of disease and decay.

## you gotta know

- Bacteria are *prokaryotes*: no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles; DNA floats freely in the nucleoid region, and energy production occurs on the cell membrane.
- *Reproduction* is rapid and asexual (binary fission), enabling bacterial populations to double every 20 minutes under ideal conditions—the reason antibiotics must stop reproduction before it accelerates.
- *Photosynthetic bacteria* (cyanobacteria) produce much of Earth's oxygen and drove the *Great Oxidation Event*; non-photosynthetic bacteria are *heterotrophs*, consuming organic matter.
- Bacteria possess a *cell wall* made of peptidoglycan (in Gram-positive species) or lipopolysaccharide (Gram-negative); this structure is the target of many antibiotics like penicillin.
- *Horizontal gene transfer* allows bacteria to acquire antibiotic resistance and other traits from neighboring cells, enabling rapid adaptation—a major public-health challenge.
- Bacteria vastly outnumber all other life by biomass; the human microbiome (gut, skin, respiratory tract) contains trillions of bacterial cells vital to digestion, immunity, and health.

- *Single-celled* means each bacterium is one cell; *binary fission* produces two genetically identical daughters, enabling exponential population growth.
- Bacteria exhibit enormous metabolic diversity: some are autotrophs (making food via photosynthesis or chemosynthesis), while others are heterotrophs (consuming organic matter).
- The human microbiome bacterial composition changes with diet, antibiotics, and health status; dysbiosis (imbalanced microbiome) is linked to inflammatory diseases, obesity, and mental health disorders.

## connections

- [[prokaryote]] — the domain containing bacteria and archaea, distinguished from eukaryotes.
- [[cell]] — bacteria are the simplest cellular life form.
- [[antibiotic]] — drugs that target bacterial cell walls or metabolism to kill or inhibit growth.
- [[bacteriology]] — the scientific study of bacteria, founded by Koch and Pasteur.
- [[germ-theory]] — the idea that disease is caused by microorganisms, vindicated by bacterial studies.
- [[dna]] — bacteria use circular DNA encoding all their proteins, simpler than eukaryotic genomes.
- [[microorganism]] — bacteria are among the oldest and most abundant microorganisms on Earth.
