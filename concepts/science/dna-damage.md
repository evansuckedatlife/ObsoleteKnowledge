## summary

**DNA damage** refers to physical or chemical alterations in the covalent structure of `[[dna]]` within a living `[[cell]]`, distinguishing acute molecular insults from heritable sequence variations. Occurring tens of thousands of times per day in human somatic tissues, these alterations arise spontaneously from endogenous metabolic hazards—such as reactive `[[oxygen]]` species, hydrolytic deamination, and replication errors—as well as exogenous insults including ultraviolet `[[photon]]` irradiation, ionizing radiation, and environmental alkylating agents. If left unaddressed by surveillance mechanisms and specialized `[[enzyme]]` repair machinery, an unresolved **DNA lesion** compromises genomic stability, triggering checkpoint arrest, permanent somatic `[[mutation]]`, neoplastic transformation into `[[cancer]]`, or programmed `[[apoptosis]]`.

## you gotta know

- Ultraviolet radiation directly crosslinks adjacent pyrimidine bases to form cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers and 6-4 photoproducts, bulky structural distortions in the `[[dna]]` backbone that halt replication forks and RNA transcription until removed by nucleotide excision repair.
- Double-strand breaks represent the most lethal structural insult to genomic integrity, repaired either rapidly through error-prone non-homologous end joining (*NHEJ*) via the *Ku70*/*Ku80* heterodimer, or faithfully in the late S and G2 phases of the `[[cell-cycle]]` via homologous recombination (*HR*) using a sister chromatid template.
- The tumor suppressor *p53*, often celebrated as the *guardian of the genome*, is rapidly stabilized by phosphorylation following damage detection, driving the transcription of *p21* to inhibit cyclin-dependent kinase complexes and enforce cell-cycle arrest at the G1/S transition.
- Endogenous metabolic byproducts, particularly reactive `[[oxygen]]` species generated by mitochondrial electron transport, oxidize guanosine into 8-oxoguanine, a mutagenic lesion that pairs aberrantly with adenine and requires excision by specific *DNA glycosylase* `[[enzyme]]` complexes in base excision repair.
- Upstream phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-related sensor kinases, specifically *ATM* and *ATR*, coordinate the cellular response by phosphorylating the histone variant *H2AX* at Serine 139 to generate *gamma-H2AX* chromatin foci, which serve as recruitment platforms for repair scaffold assemblies.
- Inherited germline defects in DNA maintenance pathways cause severe clinical syndromes, including *xeroderma pigmentosum* from disabled nucleotide excision repair, *ataxia-telangiectasia* from mutated *ATM*, *Fanconi anemia* from impaired crosslink repair, and hereditary breast cancers driven by defective *BRCA1* and *BRCA2* recombination.
- In bacterial systems, extensive double-strand breakage or persistent single-stranded gaps trigger the *SOS response*, a coordinated regulatory network wherein activated *RecA* filaments stimulate the self-cleavage of the repressor *LexA*, permitting the expression of error-prone translesion DNA polymerases that bypass replication blocks.

## connections

- [[dna]] — the double-helical nucleic acid macromolecule subjected to base modifications, strand breaks, and crosslinks.
- [[p53]] — transcription factor and tumor suppressor that arrests the cell cycle or triggers cell death upon detecting persistent lesions.
- [[cell-cycle]] — coordinated sequence of growth and division paused at checkpoints to prevent replication of damaged genetic material.
- [[apoptosis]] — regulated cell death cascade initiated when accumulated damage exceeds the metabolic repair capacity of the cell.
- [[mutation]] — permanent sequence alteration arising when damaged or mismatched bases are misread during replication.
- [[cancer]] — malignant pathology resulting from genomic instability and the gradual inactivation of growth control pathways.
- [[enzyme]] — catalytic proteins, including polymerases, helicases, ligases, and glycosylases, that recognize and remediate structural lesions.
