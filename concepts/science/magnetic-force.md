## summary

The **magnetic force** is the force exerted on a moving electric charge or current-carrying conductor within a magnetic field, perpendicular to both the field and the particle's velocity. Described quantitatively by the Lorentz force law, it underlies the operation of motors, generators, mass spectrometers, and cyclotrons. Unlike the electric force, the magnetic force does no work on a particle; instead, it changes the particle's direction of motion.

## you gotta know

- The magnetic force on a charge *q* moving with velocity *v* in a magnetic field *B* is *F* = *q*(*v* × *B*), where × denotes the cross product.
- The force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it cannot accelerate a particle in its direction of motion, only deflect it into circular or helical paths.
- A charge at rest experiences no magnetic force, even in a strong field; only moving charges (or currents) interact with magnetic fields.
- The magnetic force on a current-carrying wire of length *L* in a field *B* is *F* = *I*(*L* × *B*), where *I* is the current.
- Magnetic force is responsible for the deflection of charged particles in particle accelerators (cyclotrons, synchrotrons) where the radius of curvature depends on momentum and field strength.
- The SI unit of magnetic field, the Tesla, is defined as the field strength that produces one Newton of force on a 1-coulomb charge moving at 1 meter per second perpendicular to the field.
- Magnetic forces on moving charges generate the Lorentz force; this is the fundamental mechanism connecting electricity and magnetism into a unified electromagnetic theory.
- The right-hand rule determines the direction of magnetic force: point fingers in the direction of velocity, curl them toward the field direction, and the thumb points along the force.
- Magnetic force can do no work on a moving charge because the force is always perpendicular to the displacement, making kinetic energy change impossible under magnetic force alone.
- Charged particles in strong magnetic fields follow helical paths (in uniform fields) or complex trajectories in non-uniform fields, fundamental to plasma confinement in fusion reactors.

## connections

- [[electromagnetism]] — the unified theory of electricity and magnetism that encompasses magnetic force.
- [[magnetic-field]] — the field through which a moving charge experiences the magnetic force.
- [[lorentz-force]] — the general law combining electric and magnetic forces on a moving charge.
- [[electric-field]] — the complementary field that exerts force on stationary charges.
- [[photon]] — the quantum of electromagnetic radiation, propagated by oscillating electric and magnetic fields.
- [[velocity]] — the vector quantity that determines both the magnitude and direction of magnetic force on a charged particle.
