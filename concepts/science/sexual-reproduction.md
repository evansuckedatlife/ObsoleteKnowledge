## summary

**Sexual reproduction** is the biological process by which offspring are produced from the union of two gametes—haploid cells carrying genetic material from each parent. By combining genes through meiosis and fertilization, sexual reproduction generates genetic diversity that fuels evolution and adaptation. This strategy dominates complex life forms and is fundamental to ecology, agriculture, and the origin of species.

## you gotta know

- Sexual reproduction requires meiosis, which reduces chromosome number by half, producing haploid gametes (sperm and egg cells).
- Fertilization restores the diploid chromosome number by fusing two haploid gametes, creating a genetically unique zygote.
- Genetic diversity arises from two mechanisms during meiosis: crossing over (recombination) between homologous chromosomes and random assortment of chromosome pairs.
- The genetic variation produced by sexual reproduction provides raw material for natural selection and increases population resilience to disease and environmental change.
- Anisogamy—the production of two different-sized gametes (small sperm and large eggs)—is nearly universal in animals; some plants and algae practice isogamy with equal gamete sizes.
- Sexual reproduction typically requires less energy per offspring than asexual reproduction but has lower per-capita reproductive output and requires locating mates.
- Eukaryotes—from fungi to animals to plants—predominantly rely on sexual reproduction, while bacteria and archaea primarily use asexual fission.
- Hermaphroditic organisms produce both sperm and eggs but often have mechanisms to prevent self-fertilization; sequential hermaphrodites change sex during their lifetime based on social conditions.
- Sex determination systems vary: mammals use XY chromosomes, birds use ZW, some fish use environmental temperature, and some species are entirely parthenogenetic without males.
- The evolutionary advantage of sexual reproduction remains debated: though costly, it may reduce disease burden, purge deleterious mutations, or enhance adaptation through genetic novelty.
- Reproductive strategies include r-selection (many small offspring, little parental care) versus K-selection (few large offspring, extensive parental investment); sexual reproduction supports both strategies.
