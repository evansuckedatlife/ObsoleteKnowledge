## summary

The **pistil** is the innermost, ovule-bearing female reproductive organ of a flowering plant, consisting of an individual or fused collection of floral units known as a **carpel**. Positioned at the morphological center of the [[flower]] in an [[angiosperm]], the organ typically comprises three distinct anatomical zones: the receptive terminal stigma, the slender transmitting style, and the swollen basal ovary enclosing one or more ovules. The structure serves as the primary maternal site for [[pollen]] capture, pollen tube germination, and double fertilization, directly directing genetic transmission across plant generations. Its evolutionary emergence enclosed and protected vulnerable ovules from environmental extremes and herbivory, providing the biological foundation for flowering plant diversification, fruit ripening, and global [[seed]] production.

## you gotta know

- Exhibits a classic tripartite architecture consisting of the apical *stigma*, which provides a moist or papillate surface to capture pollen, the columnar transmitting *style*, and the basal *ovary*.
- Operates as the female reproductive organ (collectively termed the *gynoecium*) opposing the male [[stamen]]; flowers possessing both whorls are termed *perfect*, whereas unisexual flowers lacking either organ are termed *imperfect*.
- Undergoes extensive morphological remodeling following fertilization, as the ovary wall develops into the protective pericarp of a fruit while the fertilized inner ovules develop into nutrient-rich [[seed]] structures.
- Differentiated structurally between a *simple pistil*, which arises from a single independent carpel, and a *compound pistil*, formed by the congenital fusion of multiple carpels into a unified structure termed *syncarpy*.
- Classified by its attachment position relative to other floral whorls: *superior* when situated above the perianth in hypogynous flowers, *half-inferior* in perigynous flowers, or *inferior* when embedded beneath the perianth in epigynous flowers.
- Enforces biochemical self-incompatibility mechanisms to prevent inbreeding, utilizing stylar *S-RNase* [[enzyme]] pathways and surface [[protein]] receptors to arrest pollen tube elongation when pollen originates from a genetically identical plant.
- Arose phylogenetically from primitive *megasporophylls*—modified ancestral leaves that folded conduplicately along their margins to enclose naked ovules, representing a pivotal milestone in plant [[evolution]] distinguishing angiosperms from gymnosperms.

## connections

- [[stamen]] — the complementary male floral organ whose anthers synthesize pollen grains that must be deposited on the receptive stigma.
- [[pollination]] — the ecological process transporting microgametophytes from male anthers to the female stigma via wind, water, or animal pollinators.
- [[angiosperm]] — the dominant division of vascular plants defined by the anatomical enclosure of ovules and seeds within protective carpels.
- [[seed]] — the mature, embryo-containing reproductive unit that develops directly from the fertilized ovule sheltered inside the ripening ovary.
- [[pollen]] — the multicellular male microgametophyte that hydrates upon the stigma and extends an elongate tube through stylar transmitting tissue.
- [[plant-reproduction]] — the overarching biological cycle encompassing alternation of generations, floral morphogenesis, gamete delivery, and double fertilization.
- [[cell]] — the fundamental microscopic unit composing both the maternal sporophytic pistil tissue and the eight-nucleate female gametophyte.
- [[evolution]] — the selective process driving the transition from naked gymnosperm ovules to the specialized, enclosed carpels of modern flowering lineages.
