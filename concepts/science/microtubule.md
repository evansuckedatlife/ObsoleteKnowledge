## summary

A **Microtubule** is a rigid cylindrical polymer built from *tubulin* proteins, forming one of the three major components of the cell's cytoskeleton. These 25-nanometer structures organize cellular architecture, enable chromosomal segregation during cell division, and propel cilia and flagella. Microtubules are among the most dynamic cellular structures, constantly polymerizing and depolymerizing in response to cellular signals.

## you gotta know

- Microtubules are assembled from alternating *α*-tubulin and *β*-tubulin dimers, polymerized into hollow tubes with 13 protofilaments arranged in a cylinder.
- The microtubule lattice has polarity: the plus (barbed) end grows faster, while the minus (pointed) end grows slowly; this asymmetry underlies directional transport.
- Microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs) like *tau* and *MAP2* stabilize microtubules and regulate their dynamics, with dysregulation implicated in neurodegenerative diseases.
- During mitosis, centrioles nucleate microtubule organizing centers (MTOCs) that radiate spindle fibers, which grasp chromosomes and pull sister chromatids to opposite poles.
- Cilia and flagella are motile organelles built on a 9+2 core (nine doublet microtubules surrounding two central singlets), driven by dynein motor proteins.
- Microtubules serve as tracks for intracellular transport: *kinesin* motors walk toward the plus end, *dynein* motors toward the minus end, carrying cargo-laden vesicles.
- Microtubule dynamics are tightly regulated by *GTP* hydrolysis: *β*-tubulin carries *GTP*, and polymerization requires *GTP*-bound monomers.
- Catastrophe and rescue events in microtubule polymerization create dynamic instability, where microtubules rapidly switch between polymerization and depolymerization phases.
- The plus end of microtubules has a characteristic CAP of uncrosslinked tubulin, determining the stiffness and stability of the structure during polymerization.
- Taxol and other microtubule-stabilizing drugs are used in cancer chemotherapy because cancer cells rely heavily on microtubule dynamics for rapid mitotic spindle assembly.
- Microtubule severing proteins (*katanin* and *spastin*) break microtubules into fragments, regulating cellular architecture and enabling remodeling of the cytoskeleton during cell division.
- The paclitaxel (Taxol) mechanism of action reveals microtubule importance: by stabilizing microtubules, it paradoxically kills cancer cells that depend on dynamic instability to form the mitotic spindle.

## connections

- [[centriole]] — the microtubule-organizing structure composed of nine triplet microtubules arranged in a cylinder.
- [[cilia]] — motile extensions powered by the coordinated beating of microtubule-based axonemes.
- [[cell-division]] — microtubules form the mitotic spindle essential to chromosome segregation.
- [[cytoskeleton]] — the cellular framework of which microtubules are a major structural component.
- [[tubulin]] — the protein subunit polymerized to form microtubules.
- [[mitochondria]] — organelles transported along microtubule tracks by motor proteins.
