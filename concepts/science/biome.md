## summary

A **biome** is a major global biogeographic classification encompassing distinct biological communities that have formed in response to a shared regional climate and physical environment. Defined primarily by dominant plant physiognomy, temperature regimes, and annual precipitation patterns rather than phylogenetic relatedness, biomes span terrestrial realms such as tropical rainforests, tundras, and deserts, alongside extensive aquatic biomes. Understanding biomes illuminates how macroclimate dictates the geographical distribution of global primary productivity, community trophic structure, and planetary biogeochemical cycling.

## you gotta know

- The *Whittaker biome diagram* categorizes global terrestrial biomes along two primary environmental axes: mean annual temperature and mean annual precipitation.
- *Tropical rainforests* harbor the planet's highest terrestrial biodiversity and exhibit multi-tiered canopy stratification, sustained by high temperatures and abundant precipitation year-round.
- The *tundra* biome is characterized by low temperatures, dwarf vegetation, lichens, minimal precipitation, and a continuous subsurface layer of permanently frozen soil termed *permafrost*.
- *Taiga*, or the boreal coniferous forest, represents the world's largest contiguous terrestrial biome by land area, dominated by cold-tolerant, evergreen gymnosperms.
- Terrestrial biomes exhibit predictable latitudinal and altitudinal zonation, an ecological relationship between latitude and elevation first systematically documented by *Alexander von Humboldt*.
- Desert biomes receive less than 250 millimeters of annual precipitation and feature organisms with specialized xerophytic adaptations, such as CAM photosynthesis, water-storing parenchyma, and nocturnal activity patterns.
- Periodic disturbance regimes, particularly natural wildfire cycles, serve as critical ecological determinants that maintain savannas, grasslands, and Mediterranean scrub against woody forest succession.
- Marine biomes, covering over seventy percent of Earth's surface, are categorized by depth and light penetration into photic, aphotic, and benthic zones rather than terrestrial climatic variables.

## connections

- [[photosynthesis]] — the fundamental solar energy-capturing biochemical process whose rate dictates primary productivity and vegetative density across different biomes.
- [[carbon]] — essential chemical element cycling through terrestrial plant biomass, soil microbial pools, and the atmosphere across global biome reservoirs.
- [[oxygen]] — atmospheric gas generated in vast quantities by marine phytoplankton and terrestrial forest biomes during the light reactions of oxygenic photosynthesis.
- [[biochemistry]] — molecular adaptations, such as osmolyte synthesis in desert succulents and antifreeze glycoproteins in polar fauna, enabling biochemical function across diverse biomes.
- [[evolution]] — natural selection process driving convergent physiological and morphological adaptations among taxonomically distant species inhabiting ecologically analogous biomes worldwide.
- [[cell]] — microscopic building block whose membrane fatty acid unsaturation and thermal stability are dynamically tuned to regional biome climate envelopes.
- [[bipedalism]] — vertebrate terrestrial locomotor adaptation that expanded dramatically as hominins adapted to open savanna and woodland biomes.
