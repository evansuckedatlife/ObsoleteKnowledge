## summary

An **electrical circuit** is a closed loop of conducting material (wires, components) through which electric current flows, driven by a voltage source and controlled by resistances, capacitances, and inductances. Circuits are the foundation of all electronic devices—from household appliances to smartphones—and their behavior is governed by fundamental laws (Ohm's and Kirchhoff's) that relate voltage, current, and resistance.

## you gotta know

- A circuit requires a *complete path* for current to flow; an *open circuit* (broken path) stops current; a *short circuit* (low resistance path) causes excessive current and heat.
- *Voltage* is the electrical potential difference driving current; *current* is the flow rate of charge; *resistance* opposes flow. *Ohm's law relates them: V = IR.
- *Series circuits* have components one after another; current is the same everywhere, but voltages add up. *Parallel circuits* have branches; voltage is the same across branches, but currents add.
- *Kirchhoff's laws* formalize circuit analysis: voltage law (sum of voltages around a loop is zero) and current law (sum of currents entering a junction equals currents leaving).
- *Capacitors* store charge; *inductors* store energy in magnetic fields; *resistors* dissipate energy as heat—each impedes current flow differently.
- *AC circuits* (alternating current) and *DC circuits* (direct current) behave differently; AC circuits involve *impedance* (frequency-dependent resistance) and *power factors* describing efficiency.

- *Resistance* reflects the fundamental property of materials to oppose electron flow. Metals are good conductors (low resistance); rubber and glass are insulators (very high resistance).
- *Circuit topology* matters: changing from series to parallel dramatically alters circuit behavior. Real-world circuits often mix series and parallel sections.
- *Transient behavior* occurs when circuits switch on or off: capacitors and inductors resist instantaneous changes, creating charging/discharging curves and oscillations.

## connections

- [[current]] — the flow of charge through a circuit, measured in amperes.
- [[voltage]] — the electrical potential difference that drives current.
- [[resistor]] — a component that opposes current flow and dissipates energy as heat.
- [[kirchhoffs-laws]] — the fundamental rules governing voltage and current distribution in circuits.
- [[ohms-law]] — the relationship V = IR connecting voltage, current, and resistance.
- [[capacitor]] — a component storing electrical charge and energy.
- [[semiconductor]] — the basis of modern electronics; controls current flow to enable amplification and switching.
