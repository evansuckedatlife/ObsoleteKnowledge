## summary

**Interference** is the phenomenon where two or more waves combine to produce a resultant wave whose amplitude depends on the relative phase of the overlapping waves. Waves that arrive in phase *reinforce* each other (constructive interference, producing larger amplitude), while waves arriving out of phase *cancel* each other (destructive interference, producing smaller or zero amplitude). Interference is evidence that light behaves as a wave, and it is the mechanism behind diffraction patterns, thin-film colors, noise-canceling headphones, and quantum superposition.

## you gotta know

- *Constructive interference* occurs when waves' crests align—amplitudes add, producing a wave of larger amplitude. *Destructive interference* occurs when crests of one wave align with troughs of another—amplitudes subtract, potentially canceling entirely.
- *Path difference* determines interference: if two waves travel distances differing by an integer multiple of wavelengths (λ, 2λ, 3λ...), they arrive in phase (constructive); if differing by half-wavelengths (λ/2, 3λ/2...), they arrive out of phase (destructive).
- The *double-slit experiment* (Thomas Young, 1801) demonstrated interference with light: two slits act as coherent sources, producing alternating bright and dark fringes on a screen—definitive proof light is a wave.
- *Diffraction* is a closely related phenomenon: when waves pass through a narrow opening or around an obstacle, they bend and spread—a result of interference between different parts of the wavefront.
- *Thin-film interference* (oil on water, soap bubbles) produces iridescent colors as light reflects from the film's top and bottom surfaces, and interference between those reflections selectively amplifies some wavelengths and suppresses others.
- In quantum mechanics, *superposition* of wavefunctions produces interference patterns in probability amplitudes; destructive interference explains why certain quantum transitions are forbidden—the amplitudes literally cancel.

- Phase is the key: waves can be in phase (peaks align), out of phase (peaks align with troughs), or partially out of phase (intermediate cancellation). Phase depends on path difference.
- Coherence is crucial: random, incoherent light does not produce interference patterns because different wavelengths and random phases wash out the pattern. Lasers are coherent, so they produce sharp fringes.
- Interference in quantum systems: probability amplitudes (wavefunctions) interfere just like classical waves. Destructive interference explains why certain transitions are forbidden—the amplitudes literally cancel.

## connections

- [[wave]] — the fundamental entity exhibiting interference; any wave (sound, water, light) can interfere.
- [[superposition]] — the principle that waves add linearly; interference is the result of superposition.

- [[diffraction]] — wave bending around obstacles, a consequence of interference between wavefront parts.
- [[constructive-interference]] — the reinforcement of waves to produce larger amplitude.
- [[destructive-interference]] — the cancellation of waves to produce smaller amplitude.
