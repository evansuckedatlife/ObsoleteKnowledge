## summary

**Evolution** is the process by which organisms change over generations through variation, heredity, and differential survival—the unifying framework explaining the diversity of life on Earth. Driven by natural selection and genetic change, it is supported by evidence from comparative anatomy, molecular biology, paleontology, and direct observation. Evolution is not a linear climb toward complexity but a branching tree of adaptive radiation, where populations become increasingly suited to their environments.

## you gotta know

- Proposed formally by *Charles Darwin* and *Alfred Russel Wallace*; *Origin of Species* (1859) synthesized biogeography, comparative anatomy, and domestication evidence.
- Natural selection: heritable traits conferring survival or reproductive advantage increase in frequency across generations; operates on variation within populations.
- Mutations provide raw genetic variation; sexual recombination shuffles alleles; genetic drift causes random frequency changes in small populations.
- Adaptation is the result of selection: traits enhanced by survival/reproductive advantage become common; directional, disruptive, or stabilizing selection shapes populations.
- Fitness (reproductive success) is relative; alleles increase in frequency if bearers produce more viable, fertile offspring than the population average.
- Fossil record shows transitional forms: *Archaeopteryx* (dinosaur-bird), whale ancestors with hind limbs, human-ape shared features; demonstrates temporal change in species.
- Molecular evidence reveals common ancestry: genetic code nearly universal across life; DNA/protein homology quantifies relatedness (humans ~98% similar to chimpanzees).
- Speciation occurs when populations diverge and reproductive isolation prevents gene flow; geographic (allopatric), ecological (sympatric), or polyploid speciation mechanisms recognized.
- Microevolution (allele frequency changes in populations) and macroevolution (new species/taxa over long timescales) operate on identical mechanisms across geological time.
- Key evidence: comparative anatomy (homologous structures), embryology (conserved developmental stages), molecular clocks (genes mutate predictably), and direct observation in fast-reproducing organisms.
- Vestigial organs (appendix, tailbone, wisdom teeth) and genetic "junk" DNA retain traces of ancestral structures, supporting evolutionary continuity from common ancestors.
- Evolution is both a scientific fact (populations change) and a theory (explaining the mechanism).
