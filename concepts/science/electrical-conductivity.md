## summary

**Electrical Conductivity** (quantitatively designated as **Specific Conductance** and symbolized by the Greek letter sigma) is the fundamental physical property quantifying a material's intrinsic ability to conduct electrical current. Reciprocal to electrical resistivity, it describes the ease with which microscopic charge carriers—such as electrons in metals or mobile ions in electrolytic solutions—drift under the influence of an applied electric field. Operating as an essential concept across condensed matter physics, chemistry, and electrophysiology, it dictates phenomena ranging from semiconductor circuitry to the bioelectric signal propagation tracked in diagnostic medicine.

## you gotta know

- Quantified mathematically as the ratio of current density to applied electric field, expressed in SI units as siemens per meter.
- Governed in classical metallic conductors by the *Drude model*, which conceptualizes valence electrons as a mobile gas undergoing periodic scattering against stationary lattice ions.
- Solid materials are categorized into conductors, semiconductors, and insulators based on the electronic band structure explained by [[quantum-mechanics]], particularly the energy gap between valence and conduction bands.
- In aqueous solutions, conductivity arises entirely from the migration of dissolved cations and anions, such as hydrated [[sodium]] and chloride ions acting as electrolytes.
- Exhibits contrasting temperature dependencies in different states of matter: metallic conductivity decreases with heating due to phonon scattering, whereas semiconductor and electrolytic conductivity increases with temperature as charge carrier density and mobility rise.
- *Superconductivity* represents a macroscopic quantum state occurring below a critical transition temperature where electrical resistance vanishes entirely, resulting in infinite conductivity.
- Biological tissues act as volume conductors whose ionic salinity enables diagnostic technologies like [[electrocardiography]] to detect cardiac signals across extracellular fluid.
- The *Wiedemann-Franz law* correlates the electrical and thermal conductivities of metals at given temperatures, reflecting the common role of free electrons in transporting both electric charge and thermal energy.

## connections

- [[electrocardiography]] — biomedical practice that relies fundamentally on the conductive electrolyte environment of human tissues to transmit cardiac voltage signals to skin electrodes.
- [[electron]] — subatomic fundamental lepton whose drift velocity and carrier density determine charge transport in metallic conductors and semiconductor matrices.
- [[atom]] — constituent unit of matter whose nuclear charge and electron configuration determine whether outer valence shells allow delocalized electron conduction.
- [[resistor]] — circuit component whose bulk resistance is calculated from material conductivity, cross-sectional area, and pathway length according to *Pouillet's law*.
- [[sodium]] — ubiquitous alkali metal whose positive ions act as principal charge carriers in physiological solutions and biological fluids.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — fundamental physical theory that explains energy band gaps, electron wavefunctions in crystalline lattices, and cooper-pair transport in superconductors.
