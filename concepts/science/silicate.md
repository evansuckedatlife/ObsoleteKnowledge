# Silicate

## summary

**Silicates** are minerals containing silicon and oxygen, forming the largest and most abundant mineral class in Earth's crust. The characteristic structural unit is the *silicate tetrahedron*—a central silicon atom bonded to four oxygen atoms—which links in chains, sheets, or three-dimensional networks to create the vast diversity of silicate minerals. Over 95% of Earth's crust is composed of silicate minerals, making them foundational to geology, geochemistry, and most crustal rocks.

## you gotta know

- The *silicate tetrahedron* (SiO₄)⁴⁻ is the basic structural unit; silicon atoms at Earth's temperature and pressure form strong tetrahedral bonds with oxygen, and these tetrahedra link to form chains, sheets, or frameworks depending on how many oxygens they share.
- *[[Quartz]]* (SiO₂) is the simplest silicate, a three-dimensional network of tetrahedra where every oxygen is shared between two silicon atoms; it is hard, resistant to weathering, and extremely common in igneous and sedimentary rocks.
- *[[Feldspars]]*, the most abundant minerals in Earth's crust (making up ~60% of it), are framework silicates containing aluminum in addition to silicon and oxygen; they are the primary mineral in granite and many other igneous rocks.
- *Olivine* is an isolated-tetrahedron silicate (no shared oxygens), rich in iron and magnesium; it crystallizes early from cooling magma, forming the greenish mineral visible in basalt and peridotite.
- *Mica* (biotite and muscovite) are sheet silicates where tetrahedra form infinite two-dimensional sheets; they split easily along planes parallel to sheets, producing the characteristic flaky appearance.
- *Amphiboles* and *pyroxenes* are inosilicates—double- and single-chain silicates respectively; they crystallize in igneous rocks at high temperatures and form elongated crystals.
- *Silicate weathering*—the chemical breakdown of silicate minerals by water and CO₂—is a primary mechanism removing atmospheric CO₂ over geological time, regulating Earth's long-term climate over millions of years.

## connections

- [[mineral]] — silicates comprise the vast majority of rock-forming minerals.
- [[quartz]] — the simplest and most common silicate mineral.
- [[feldspar]] — the most abundant crustal mineral, a complex silicate with aluminum.
- [[igneous-rock]] — igneous rocks are composed primarily of silicate minerals like quartz, feldspar, olivine, and mica.
- [[earth]] — Earth's crust is overwhelmingly silicate in composition.
