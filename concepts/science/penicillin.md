## summary

**Penicillin** is the first widely used antibiotic, a compound derived from the mold *Penicillium notatum* that revolutionized medicine by enabling the treatment of bacterial infections. Discovered accidentally by *Alexander Fleming* in 1928, its development and mass production during World War II saved countless lives. Penicillin remains one of the most significant pharmaceutical discoveries in human history.

## you gotta know

- Discovered when Fleming noticed that a mold contaminating a bacterial culture had killed surrounding bacteria, a serendipitous observation that launched modern antibiotic research; Fleming initially named the active compound "mold juice" before developing the name penicillin.
- Works by inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis, causing the cell wall to rupture and killing the bacterium—harmless to human cells, which lack cell walls; this selective toxicity makes it an ideal antibiotic.
- Was initially difficult to isolate and purify; development accelerated during World War II as a military necessity to treat infected wounds and prevent gangrene; Fleming's discovery arrived precisely when it was most needed.
- Mass production by fermentation in large tanks made penicillin widely available and affordable, transforming bacterial infection from often-fatal to treatable; by the war's end, millions of doses were produced monthly.
- Exhibits a relatively broad spectrum of activity against gram-positive bacteria, though many bacteria subsequently evolved resistance to penicillin through genetic mutations that altered cell wall precursor synthesis.
- Derives its name from the mold species that produces it, *Penicillium notatum*, which is now cultivated industrially for pharmaceutical production; different strains of the mold produce varying quantities of penicillin.
- Launched an entire field of antibiotic research, leading to the discovery of streptomycin, tetracycline, and countless other antimicrobial drugs; Fleming, Florey, and Chain shared the 1945 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for this discovery.
