## summary

The **atomic nucleus** is the extremely small, dense, positively charged core situated at the heart of every *atom*. Composed of protons and neutrons collectively known as nucleons, it contains more than 99.9 percent of an atom's total mass while occupying only a tiny fraction of its total volume. First identified by *Ernest Rutherford* in 1911, the nucleus is governed by the strong nuclear force, which overcomes electromagnetic repulsion to bind nucleons together and defines nuclear stability, radioactive decay, and stellar nucleosynthesis.

## you gotta know

- Discovered by *Ernest Rutherford* in 1911 following the gold foil experiment conducted by *Hans Geiger* and *Ernest Marsden*, which disproved *J. J. Thomson*'s plum pudding model by demonstrating that alpha particles occasionally deflected at steep angles.
- Composed of protons and neutrons, which are themselves composite hadrons formed from triplets of valence quarks held together by gluons via the strong interaction described by the [[standard-model]].
- The count of protons in the core constitutes the [[atomic-number]], which identifies the chemical element, whereas variants containing differing numbers of neutrons are designated isotopes.
- Bound by the residual strong nuclear force, which acts over subatomic femtometer distances and is mediated by meson exchange, overpowering the Coulomb electrostatic repulsion between positively charged protons.
- Nuclear size is approximately proportional to the cube root of the mass number, generally described by the empirical radius formula $R \approx r_0 A^{1/3}$, typically spanning roughly one to ten femtometers.
- Modeled by the liquid drop model formulated by *George Gamow* and *Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker*, which gives the semi-empirical mass formula, and the nuclear shell model developed by *Maria Goeppert Mayer* and *J. Hans D. Jensen*, which predicts magic numbers of exceptional stability.
- Instability occurs when the ratio of neutrons to protons falls outside the valley of beta stability, triggering radioactive decay pathways such as alpha emission, beta decay, or spontaneous fission.
- Nuclear binding energy accounts for the nuclear mass defect via *Albert Einstein*'s relation $E=mc^2$, explaining the tremendous energy yields liberated during nuclear fission and nuclear fusion.

## connections

- [[atom]] — the neutral composite structure consisting of the central nucleus surrounded by an electron cloud.
- [[atomic-number]] — the fundamental count of nuclear protons that determines elemental identity.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework necessary to describe nuclear energy levels, spin, and tunneling.
- [[quark]] — fundamental elementary particles that constitute the protons and neutrons of the nucleus.
- [[standard-model]] — the overarching particle physics theory describing the fundamental forces and constituents of nucleons.
- [[electron]] — the negatively charged leptons whose electrostatic attraction to the positive nucleus forms bound atoms.
- [[albert-einstein]] — physicist whose mass-energy equivalence principle governs nuclear binding energy and mass defect.
