## summary

**Digestion** is the physiological process by which complex foodstuffs are mechanically and chemically broken down into absorbable molecular subunits throughout the animal gastrointestinal tract. Occurring via sequential enzymatic hydrolysis across specialized luminal compartments such as the oral cavity, stomach, and small intestine, it converts dietary polymers into bioavailable monomers. Understanding its regulatory hormones, digestive zymogens, and compartmentalized biochemical mechanisms is essential for analyzing metabolic biology and organismal physiology.

## you gotta know

- Amylase initiates carbohydrate breakdown in the oral cavity, cleaving alpha-1,4-glycosidic bonds in starch to produce maltose and short oligosaccharides before salivary inactivation by acidic gastric juices.
- Pepsinogen is synthesized by gastric chief cells and cleaved by hydrochloric acid from parietal cells into active pepsin, an endopeptidase functioning optimally at acidic pH to hydrolyze dietary [[protein]].
- The stomach's parietal cells employ hydrogen-potassium ATPase pumps to acidify the gastric lumen, an energy-dependent process stimulated by gastrin, histamine, and acetylcholine and inhibited by somatostatin.
- Cholecystokinin and secretin are secreted by enteroendocrine cells of the duodenum upon the arrival of acidic chyme, triggering gall bladder contraction, bile salt secretion, and pancreatic bicarbonate discharge.
- Pancreatic zymogens including trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, and procarboxypeptidase enter the duodenum, where mucosal enteropeptidase activates trypsin to initiate a rapid proteolytic activation cascade.
- Lipids undergo mechanical emulsification by amphipathic bile salts before pancreatic lipase and colipase hydrolyze triglycerides into free fatty acids and 2-monoacylglycerols within mixed micelles.
- Monosaccharides and amino acids are absorbed across enterocytes in the jejunum via secondary active transport coupled to [[sodium]] gradients before transport to the liver via the hepatic portal vein.
- Herbivorous vertebrates evolved specialized gastrointestinal adaptations for microbial fermentation, a strategy seen today in ruminants and historically reflected in the immense gut capacities that arose during [[dinosaur-emergence]].

## connections

- [[enzyme]] — macromolecular biological catalysts that accelerate the hydrolytic cleavage of complex macromolecules into monomeric nutrients.
- [[protein]] — dietary polymers broken down into oligopeptides and free amino acids by gastric and pancreatic peptidases.
- [[dipole]] — the electrical polarity of water molecules that enables nucleophilic hydrolytic attacks and polar interactions during aqueous nutrient processing.
- [[cell]] — basic biological units comprising enterocytes that actively absorb processed nutrients across specialized microvillar brush borders.
- [[sodium]] — electrochemical gradients driven by sodium-potassium ATPases power secondary active symporters during intestinal nutrient uptake.
- [[dinosaur-emergence]] — evolutionary development where herbivorous archosaurs evolved specialized cranial mechanics and expansive fermentation tracts for digesting Mesozoic flora.
