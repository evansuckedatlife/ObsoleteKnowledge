## summary

**Zinc** is a transition metal (atomic number 30) that is essential for biology, industry, and materials science. As a component of brass and a protective coating in galvanization, zinc prevents corrosion and is among the most widely used metals globally. Biologically, zinc is an indispensable cofactor for hundreds of enzymes and proteins, supporting immune function and wound healing.

## you gotta know

- Atomic number 30 with electron configuration [Ar]3d¹⁰4s²; a member of the transition metals on the periodic table, located in Group 12 alongside cadmium and mercury.
- Combines with [[copper]] in varying ratios (typically 60% copper, 40% zinc) to form *brass*, an alloy prized for its malleability, corrosion resistance, and distinctive yellow appearance.
- Applied as a protective coating through *galvanization*—submerging steel in molten zinc creates a layer that prevents rust and extends infrastructure lifespan (bridges, vehicles, pipes, roofing).
- Highly reactive as a reducing agent; in *ozonolysis* (ozone cleavage of alkenes), zinc metallic dust decomposes ozonide intermediates and regenerates carbonyl compounds in organic synthesis.
- Essential trace element for human health; zinc is a cofactor for over 300 enzymes; deficiency causes immune suppression, alopecia, impaired wound healing, and loss of taste.
- Extracted primarily from the mineral *sphalerite* (zinc sulfide) via roasting and electrolytic refining; major producers include China (28% of global production), Peru, and Australia.
- Melts at 419°C and boils at 907°C; its moderate volatility and relatively low boiling point (compared to iron at 2,862°C) reflect its position among transition metals.
- Second most abundant metal in the human body after iron; stored primarily in bone, muscle, and liver tissue; required for protein synthesis, wound healing, immune function, and neurotransmission.
- Zinc's electrochemical properties make it valuable as a sacrificial anode in corrosion protection systems and in dry cell batteries (Leclanchè cells) where it serves as the negative electrode.
