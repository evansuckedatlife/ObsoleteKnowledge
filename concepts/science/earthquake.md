## summary

An **earthquake** is the shaking of the Earth caused by sudden, violent release of stress accumulated along tectonic plate boundaries or within the crust. When plates collide, slide, or subduct, they build up strain over years or centuries; release of this strain sends shock waves radiating outward, causing ground motion and sometimes structural damage.

## you gotta know

- Earthquakes occur most frequently at plate boundaries where two plates slide, collide, or separate—these are the world's most seismically active zones.
- Stress accumulates silently over years or decades as plates push against one another; sudden slip releases accumulated energy as seismic waves.
- Seismic waves come in multiple varieties: P-waves (primary, fast, compressional) and S-waves (secondary, slower, shear) propagate through the Earth; surface waves cause the most visible ground shaking.
- The [[richter-scale]] measures amplitude of seismic waves on a logarithmic scale; each unit increase represents roughly 30 times more energy released.
- Aftershocks follow major earthquakes as remaining stress in the rock adjusts; foreshocks can precede large quakes but are not reliably predictive.
- The hypocenter (focus) is the point where rupture initiates underground; the epicenter is the point on Earth's surface directly above it, located using triangulation of seismic station data.
- Earthquakes can trigger [[volcano|volcanic eruptions]], underwater earthquakes create tsunamis, and large quakes can cause landslides and liquefaction of saturated soils.
