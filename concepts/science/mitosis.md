## summary

**Mitosis** is the phase of the cell cycle in which the nucleus divides, producing two genetically identical daughter nuclei, each receiving a complete copy of the parent cell's chromosomes. The hallmark of somatic (non-reproductive) cell division, mitosis enables growth, repair, and asexual reproduction. It occurs in four classical stages—prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase—and is preceded by DNA replication and followed by cytokinesis (division of the cytoplasm).

## you gotta know

- Part of M phase; preceded by interphase (G1, S, G2 subphases) during which DNA replicates (S phase) and cells accumulate resources.
- Prophase: chromatin condenses into chromosomes (each two sister chromatids joined at centromere); nuclear envelope breaks down; spindle apparatus forms from centrosomes/poles.
- Metaphase: chromosomes align at the cell's equatorial plate; spindle fibers attached to kinetochores are under tension, ensuring proper segregation.
- Anaphase: sister chromatids separate as spindle fibers shorten; chromatids move to opposite poles; centromere splits, each chromatid becomes an independent chromosome.
- Telophase: nuclear envelopes reform around separated chromosome sets at each pole; chromosomes decondense; spindle apparatus disappears.
- Produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells from one parent cell; each receives a complete genome copy; ensures genetic continuity.
- Duration roughly 1–2 hours in mammalian cells (prophase ~20 min, metaphase ~5 min, anaphase ~10 min, telophase ~15 min); most cycle time (18–20 hours) spent in interphase.
- Occurs in all eukaryotes; centrioles organize spindle poles in animal cells and some plants; plant spindle poles lack centrioles but form functional spindles.
- Checkpoint controls: G1/S and G2/M checkpoints verify DNA integrity; p53 protein detects damage and halts progression; failure causes genomic instability and cancer.
- Cytokinesis (cytoplasmic division) follows mitosis: animal cells form cleavage furrow (actin ring contraction); plant cells form cell plate (new cell wall).
- Mitosis sustains life: enables growth of organisms, wound healing, immune cell replacement, and continuous renewal of tissues.
