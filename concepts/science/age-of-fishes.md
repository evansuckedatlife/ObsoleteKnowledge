## summary

The **Age of Fishes** is the celebrated paleontological epithet for the **Devonian Period**, an interval of the *Paleozoic Era* spanning from roughly 419 to 359 million years ago. Popularized by nineteenth-century Scottish geologist *Hugh Miller*, the designation commemorates an astonishing evolutionary radiation of jawed aquatic vertebrates that transformed planetary biodiversity. Beyond marine dominance, the epoch witnessed the greening of continents by early forests and the pioneering anatomical transition of lobe-finned fish onto land.

## you gotta know

- Defined primarily by the explosive diversification and ecological supremacy of placoderms, heavily armored jawed fishes exemplified by the monstrous apex marine predator *Dunkleosteus*.
- Saw the concurrent radiation of both major lineages of extant fish, including cartilaginous *Chondrichthyes* and bony *Osteichthyes*, establishing lineages that survive today.
- Yielded the momentous evolutionary emergence of lobe-finned sarcopterygians, whose paired fins and internal bone homologies gave rise to transitional stem tetrapods such as *Tiktaalik* and *Acanthostega*.
- Preserved extensively in historical Old Red Sandstone terrestrial sedimentary facies across Great Britain, Greenland, and North America, yielding exquisite fossils that founded vertebrate paleontology.
- Coincided with the rapid spread of deep-rooted arborescent land plants like *Archaeopteris*, which triggered profound soil pedogenesis, enhanced silicate weathering, and altered continental runoff.
- Culminated in the catastrophic Late Devonian mass extinction, a protracted ecological crisis featuring the Kellwasser and Hangenberg anoxic events that decimated reef-building organisms and annihilated the placoderms.
- Named formally by geologists *Roderick Murchison* and *Adam Sedgwick* for distinctive marine geological strata exposed across the countryside of *Devon*, *England*.

## connections

- [[evolution]] — the unifying biological principle governing the adaptive radiation, morphological divergence, and aquatic-to-terrestrial transitions of Devonian vertebrates.
- [[silurian-period]] — the preceding geological interval whose quiet shallow seas fostered the earliest jawed fishes and primitive non-vascular land plants.
- [[carboniferous-period]] — the succeeding epoch known for vast coal-forming swamp forests and the ecological consolidation of early terrestrial tetrapods.
- [[tetrapods]] — the four-limbed vertebrate clade that evolved from Devonian rhipidistian fish to colonize continental landscapes.
- [[devonian-extinction]] — the major biodiversity crisis near the close of the period that caused severe marine faunal turnover and eradicated placoderms.
- [[carbon]] — the foundational element whose accelerated burial by burgeoning Devonian terrestrial flora altered atmospheric carbon dioxide and global climates.
- [[england]] — the home country of *Devon*, the historic geological type locality where early stratigraphers first defined the strata of the period.
