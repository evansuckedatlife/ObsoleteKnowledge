## summary

**Radioactivity** is the spontaneous emission of energy and particles from unstable atomic nuclei as they decay into more stable configurations. This fundamental nuclear process releases alpha particles, beta particles, and gamma rays, transforming one element into another. Radioactivity is the basis of nuclear medicine, carbon dating, nuclear power, and weapons, making it one of the most consequential discoveries in twentieth-century science.

## you gotta know

- Radioactive decay occurs when a nucleus is unstable—typically due to an unfavorable neutron-to-proton ratio—and spontaneously transforms to achieve stability.
- Alpha decay emits an alpha particle (a helium-4 nucleus: two protons and two neutrons), reducing the atomic number by 2 and mass number by 4.
- Beta decay converts a neutron into a proton (or vice versa), emitting an electron (beta-minus) or positron (beta-plus), changing the element while keeping mass roughly constant.
- Gamma decay releases high-energy electromagnetic radiation (gamma rays) when a nucleus reorganizes from an excited state to a lower energy state, without changing composition.
- Half-life is the time required for half of a radioactive sample to decay; it is constant for each isotope and ranges from fractions of a second to billions of years.
- Natural radioactivity from uranium, thorium, and potassium occurs throughout Earth; artificial radioactivity can be induced by nuclear bombardment.
- Becquerel discovered radioactivity through uranium's penetrating emissions; Marie Curie isolated radium and polonium; their research revealed the existence of atomic energy.
- Radioactive decay follows exponential kinetics: the number of atoms remaining after time t equals the initial number times 1/2 raised to the power of t divided by half-life.
- Medical applications include PET scans (using positron-emitting isotopes), cancer radiotherapy, and thyroid treatment with radioactive iodine.
