## summary

**Active transport** is the cellular process of moving molecules across the cell membrane against their concentration gradient, from low to high concentration, using energy from ATP hydrolysis. This mechanism allows cells to maintain essential ion gradients and accumulate vital nutrients despite thermodynamic resistance. It is fundamental to neuronal signaling, muscle contraction, nutrient uptake, and the maintenance of cellular osmotic balance necessary for cell survival.

## you gotta know

- Requires ATP hydrolysis to break chemical bonds and fuel conformational changes in transport proteins.
- The sodium-potassium pump (Na+/K+-ATPase) is the most abundant cellular protein, pumping three sodium ions out and two potassium ions in per ATP consumed.
- Creates and maintains membrane potential in neurons by establishing the sodium-potassium gradient critical for action potentials.
- Primary active transport uses ATP directly; secondary active transport harnesses the energy from another molecule's gradient, coupling the unfavorable uphill movement to a favorable downhill process.
- Enables accumulation of calcium ions in mitochondria, triggering muscle contraction and intracellular signaling cascades.
- Powers the uptake of glucose and amino acids by intestinal and kidney cells against their concentration gradients.
- Uses approximately 30% of cellular ATP at rest due to the Na+/K+-ATPase alone; disruption causes cell swelling and death.
- Transport proteins undergo conformational cycles: binding substrate, hydrolyzing ATP, changing shape to release substrate on the opposite side, then resetting to accept more incoming substrate molecules.

## connections

- [[cell-membrane]] — the selectively permeable barrier that active transport overcomes through embedded pump proteins.
- [[atp]] — the hydrolyzed energy source driving conformational changes in transport proteins.
- [[mitochondria]] — site of ATP synthesis that provides energy for active transport.
- [[transport-protein]] — channel and carrier proteins that execute active transport.
- [[enzyme]] — active transport proteins are enzymes coupling ATP hydrolysis to molecular movement.
- [[sodium]] — the ion most commonly pumped by active transport via the Na+/K+-ATPase.
- [[cell]] — the living compartment dependent on active transport for survival.
