## summary

The **Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox** (frequently known as the **EPR paradox** or simply **EPR**) is an influential 1935 thought experiment formulated to demonstrate that the standard formulation of [[quantum-mechanics]] provides an incomplete description of physical reality. Authored by *Albert Einstein*, *Boris Podolsky*, and *Nathan Rosen* while working at the Institute for Advanced Study in the [[united-states]], the argument posited that quantum theory demands either the existence of hidden variables or the violation of relativistic locality through instantaneous action at a distance. Although intended as a critique of probabilistic quantum theory, the paradox highlighted the profound reality of quantum entanglement, transforming foundational physics and laying the cornerstone for modern quantum information science.

## you gotta know

- Published in the *Physical Review* under the title *Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?* in May 1935.
- Defined an *element of physical reality* as any quantity whose value can be predicted with certainty without disturbing the physical system in question.
- Formulated the principle of *local realism*, which posits that physical processes occurring at one spatial location cannot instantaneously exert causal influence on events at another spatially separated location.
- Analyzed two spatially separated particles with correlated positions and momenta, demonstrating that measuring one particle allows precise deduction of the other's state without direct mechanical interaction.
- Prompted an immediate published rebuttal from *Niels Bohr*, who defended the *Copenhagen interpretation* by arguing that the two particles constitute an indivisible quantum system whose measurement context cannot be separated.
- Restyled into a mathematically accessible spin-half formulation by *David Bohm* in 1951, substituting entangled electron or [[photon]] polarization states for continuous position and momentum coordinates.
- Northern Irish physicist *John Stewart Bell* derived *Bell's inequality* in 1964, proving that no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce all predictions of [[quantum-mechanics]].
- Landmark laboratory tests by *Alain Aspect* in 1982 and subsequent loophole-free experiments in 2015 confirmed the violation of Bell inequalities, vindicating quantum nonlocality over classical local realism.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — primary theoretical framework targeted by the paper's challenge regarding the completeness of probabilistic state descriptions.
- [[wavefunction]] — mathematical entity whose collapse upon measurement was argued by the authors to imply instantaneous physical changes across space.
- [[albert-einstein]] — lead author and Nobel laureate who sought to demonstrate that quantum indeterminacy masked underlying deterministic physical reality.
- [[photon]] — massless gauge boson widely utilized in modern laboratory realizations of entangled pairs to experimentally test Bell inequalities.
- [[standard-model]] — modern quantum field theory governing subatomic physics that incorporates quantum entanglement as a verified physical property of nature.
- [[united-states]] — host country where the authors composed the seminal paper at Princeton's newly founded Institute for Advanced Study.
