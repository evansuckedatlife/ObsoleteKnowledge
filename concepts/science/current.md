## summary

**Current** is the flow of electrical charge through a conductor, measured in amperes (amps). It represents the rate at which charge moves from one point to another, driven by a potential difference. Current is the foundational quantity in all practical electronics and power systems.

## you gotta know

- Defined as the amount of charge flowing past a point per unit time: measured in coulombs per second, or amperes (1 ampere = 1 coulomb per second); one ampere is roughly the current flowing through a typical light bulb.
- Conventional current flows from positive to negative terminal (the direction opposite to actual electron flow).
- Direct current (DC) flows in one constant direction at constant voltage; alternating current (AC) reverses direction periodically, changing polarity and magnitude sinusoidally.
- Current requires a complete circuit—a closed path with no breaks—to flow; it cannot exist in an open circuit; breaking the circuit (like a light switch) stops current instantaneously.
- *Ohm's Law* relates current, voltage, and resistance mathematically: current = voltage ÷ resistance (I = V/R); doubling voltage doubles current through a fixed resistor, while increasing resistance decreases current proportionally.
- Current produces heat (I²R heating, where R is resistance) and magnetic fields; heat dissipation is a critical concern in circuit design, requiring heat sinks and cooling systems to prevent component damage and failure.

## connections

- [[voltage]] — the potential difference that drives current through a circuit.
- [[resistance]] — opposes current flow and determines how much current flows for a given voltage.
- [[charge]] — current is the quantified flow of charge carriers.
- [[electron]] — the primary charge carrier in metals and conductors.
- [[circuit]] — the path through which current flows.
- [[ampere]] — the standard unit of current measurement.
- [[power]] — the product of current and voltage; current determines energy transfer rate.
