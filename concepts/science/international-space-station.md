## summary

The **International Space Station** (ISS) is a modular research laboratory in low Earth orbit, continuously inhabited since November 2000. A collaborative effort of *NASA*, the Russian Federal Space Agency, and space agencies of Europe, Japan, and Canada, the ISS orbits Earth every 90 minutes, serving as a testbed for microgravity research, long-duration spaceflight, and international cooperation. Post-Cold War partnership forged on the ISS symbolizes the transformation of space from a rivalry arena into a shared human endeavor.

## you gotta know

- The ISS orbits at approximately 250 miles altitude, traveling at 17,500 mph; it is the brightest artificial object in the night sky and visible to the naked eye.
- Construction began in 1998 with the Zarya module (Russia) and Unity node (USA); assembly continued through 2011 with contributions from multiple nations, and resupply continues ongoing.
- The station houses six permanent crew members (typically 3 American, 1 Japanese, 1 European, 1 Russian) on rotating missions lasting 6 months; replacements arrive via *SpaceX* Crew Dragon and Russian Soyuz capsules.
- Microgravity experiments aboard the ISS span materials science, biology, physics, and Earth observation; results cannot be replicated on Earth due to gravity's absence.
- The ISS emerged from Cold War detente: the 1975 Apollo-Soyuz mission pioneered US-Soviet cooperation, paving the way for the ISS partnership after the Soviet Union collapsed.
- The station's solar arrays generate electricity; modules include laboratories (Destiny, Kibo, Columbus) named after space agencies' home nations, reflecting international identity.
- Commercial resupply vehicles, including SpaceX's Dragon and Cygnus, now deliver cargo regularly; the ISS is expected to operate through at least 2030.
