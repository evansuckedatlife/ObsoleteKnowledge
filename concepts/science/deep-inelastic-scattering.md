## summary

**Deep inelastic scattering** is a high-energy particle physics process in which high-energy leptons, such as electrons, shatter hadrons to probe their internal substructure at extremely short distance scales. First performed in the late 1960s at the *Stanford Linear Accelerator Center* (*SLAC*), these landmark experiments provided the first direct empirical evidence for *Richard Feynman*'s parton model and established the physical reality of quarks. It remains a primary experimental pillar for precision tests of quantum chromodynamics (*QCD*) and the modern description of hadron structure.

## you gotta know

- Conducted by the *SLAC-MIT* collaboration between 1967 and 1973, earning *Jerome I. Friedman*, *Henry W. Kendall*, and *Richard E. Taylor* the 1990 *Nobel Prize in Physics*.
- Termed *deep* because the invariant momentum transfer squared ($Q^2$) is large enough that the probe's virtual exchange particle possesses a tiny resolving wavelength.
- Termed *inelastic* because the target proton or neutron does not remain intact; the enormous energy transfer causes fragmentation into a shower of newly produced hadrons.
- Confirmed the phenomenon of *Bjorken scaling*, where structure functions become approximately independent of the scale $Q^2$ at high energies, indicating scattering off point-like constituents.
- Validated *Richard Feynman*'s *parton model*, which postulated that fast-moving hadrons behave as collections of freely moving, non-interacting sub-constituents.
- Demonstrated that spin-1/2 fermions make up the valence structure through the *Callan–Gross relation*, ruling out scalar constituents.
- Revealed that valence quarks carry only roughly half of the nucleon's total momentum, providing the foundational clue that electrically neutral gluons mediate the strong force.
- Relies on an ultra-short [[de-broglie-wavelength]] of the transferred virtual [[photon]] or intermediate vector boson to achieve sub-femtometer spatial resolution.

## connections

- [[quark]] — the point-like, fractionally charged constituents whose physical reality was first demonstrated by this process.
- [[electron]] — the incident charged lepton whose deflection angle and energy loss provided the primary scattering kinematic observables.
- [[standard-model]] — the foundational theoretical framework whose strong-interaction component, *QCD*, was forged through scaling violations in this reaction.
- [[de-broglie-wavelength]] — the probe scale whose inverse proportionality to momentum transfer enables sub-hadronic structural resolution.
- [[nucleus]] — the composite nuclear target whose individual protons and neutrons were shattered during beam collision runs.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the fundamental theory underpinning matrix elements, cross-sections, and relativistic wave scattering.
