## summary

**Magnesium** is a lightweight alkaline earth metal (atomic number 12) with two valence electrons, making it both chemically reactive and biologically essential. In organic chemistry, it forms the basis of *Grignard reagents*, among the most versatile nucleophilic reagents in synthesis. In biology, magnesium sits at the heart of chlorophyll and ATP complexes, powering photosynthesis and cellular energy transfer.

## you gotta know

- Magnesium is the 8th most abundant element in Earth's crust and the 3rd most abundant in seawater, where it exists as Mg²⁺ ions.
- The metal is highly reactive with oxygen and water, burning with an intense white flame often used for flares and emergency signaling.
- In Grignard reactions, magnesium metal inserts into the carbon-halogen bond of an organic halide to form R-Mg-X (organomagnesium reagent), a powerful nucleophile and carbanion equivalent.
- Magnesium-chloride or magnesium-iodide Grignard reagents are stable in aprotic solvents like diethyl ether or THF, allowing efficient carbon-carbon bond formation.
- Chlorophyll, the photosynthetic pigment, chelates Mg²⁺ at its porphyrin center; without it, photosynthesis cannot proceed.
- ATP and other high-energy phosphates bind Mg²⁺ to stabilize the nucleotide structure and facilitate enzyme-catalyzed phosphoryl transfer.
- Dietary magnesium deficiency impairs muscle function and nerve transmission; the mineral is absorbed in the small intestine and stored primarily in bone.
- Magnesium's electron configuration [Ne]3s² gives it consistent +2 oxidation state in virtually all compounds, simplifying prediction of its chemistry.
- Grignard reagents react with polar groups including carbonyls, esters, and carboxylic acids, making them indispensable for building complex molecules in pharmaceutical and fine-chemical synthesis.
- Magnesium hydroxide and magnesium oxide are used industrially as flame retardants, refractory materials, and in pulp bleaching processes.
- Enzymatic cofactors containing magnesium activate thousands of reactions: *hexokinase*, *glucose isomerase*, and DNA polymerase all require Mg²⁺ for catalysis.
