## summary

The **radius**, also referred to as the **radial bone**, is the lateral and shorter of the two long bones constituting the human forearm when viewed in the standard anatomical position. Named from the Latin word for spoke or ray due to its ability to rotate around the adjacent ulna, it articulates proximally with the capitulum of the humerus at the elbow and distally with the scaphoid and lunate bones at the radiocarpal wrist joint. Its rotational kinematics across the longitudinal axis of the forearm permit pronation and supination, an anatomical adaptation that endows humans and other primates with sophisticated manual dexterity and manipulation capabilities.

## you gotta know

- Features a disc-like proximal head whose shallow superior cup glides against the humeral capitulum, while its smooth circumference rotates within the radial notch of the ulna encircled by the annular ligament.
- Expands dramatically at its distal extremity to form the prominent, downward-pointing radial styloid process, which extends further distally than the ulnar styloid and provides an anchorage point for the brachioradialis tendon.
- Enables forearm pronation and supination by crossing obliquely over the relatively stationary ulna, pivoting along a rotational axis anchored by the proximal and distal radioulnar joints and braced by the oblique cord and interosseous membrane.
- Possesses a medial projection below the neck called the radial tuberosity, which serves as the primary site of insertion for the powerful biceps brachii muscle, rendering the biceps a potent supinator when the elbow joint is flexed.
- Subject to Colles' fracture, one of the most common orthopedic injuries in clinical medicine, where a fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH) causes a transverse fracture of the distal metaphysis that displaces the fragment dorsally into a *dinner fork* deformity.
- Vulnerable in young children to nursemaid's elbow, or radial head subluxation, in which sudden longitudinal traction on an extended forearm causes the immature radial head to slide partially out from beneath the encircling annular ligament.
- Represents the preaxial weight-bearing element of the zeugopod across tetrapod [[evolution]]; in equines and ruminants, the radius enlarges and fuses completely with the atrophied ulna to stabilize limb locomotion.

## connections

- [[sternum]] — counterpart skeletal component in the axial skeleton that anchors the pectoral girdle via the clavicle, stabilizing upper limb rotation initiated by the radius.
- [[cell]] — basic biological unit forming osteoblasts and osteoclasts that mediate bone remodeling and micro-fracture repair within the radius.
- [[evolution]] — evolutionary transition that remodeled the pectoral fins of ancient lobe-finned fish into the specialized radius-bearing forelimbs of terrestrial quadrupeds.
- [[protein]] — collagen triple helix providing the flexible structural scaffolding that prevents brittle bone fractures in the loaded radial shaft.
- [[enzyme]] — biological catalyst such as alkaline phosphatase required for the extracellular mineralization of radial osteoid matrix during osteogenesis.
