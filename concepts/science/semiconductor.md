## summary

A **semiconductor** is a solid material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator, enabling the precise manipulation, switching, and amplification of electric current. Governed by electronic band structure in solid-state [[quantum-mechanics]], these crystalline substances form the essential physical foundation for modern microelectronics, bridging fundamental passive components such as the [[resistor]] with complex microprocessors like the modern [[central-processing-unit]]. Developed extensively across industrial research laboratories in the [[united-states]] following [[world-war-ii]], semiconductor technology replaced fragile vacuum tubes with durable solid-state alternatives. Today, these materials drive the global information economy and enable virtually every active electronic technology, from optical communications to planetary spacecraft.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the indispensable physical substrate for microprocessors, memory arrays, and logic gates, providing the microscopic building blocks mounted onto every computer [[motherboard]].
- Characterized by an intermediate energy band gap separating the filled *valence band* from the empty *conduction band*, which prevents spontaneous conduction at absolute zero while allowing thermal energy to liberate mobile charge carriers at room temperature.
- Modified through the deliberate process of chemical doping, introducing pentavalent impurity atoms to produce n-type material dominated by mobile electrons or trivalent atoms to create p-type material dominated by positive *holes*.
- Enables unidirectional current flow through the creation of a *p-n junction*, where mobile charge carriers diffuse across the interface to form an insulating *depletion region* with a built-in electric field that acts as a rectifying [[diode]].
- Facilitates optoelectronic energy conversion in solar cells and light-emitting diodes, absorbing or releasing electromagnetic radiation whenever an excited charge carrier recombines or transitions across the energy threshold by interacting with a [[photon]].
- Revolutionized twentieth-century computing when the American physicists *John Bardeen*, *Walter Brattain*, and *William Shockley* invented the point-contact and junction transistors at Bell Labs, earning the 1956 Nobel Prize in Physics and launching the microelectronics boom of the [[cold-war]] era.
- Explained theoretically through solid-state [[quantum-mechanics]], where the periodic lattice potential created by each constituent [[atom]] broadens discrete atomic energy levels into continuous conduction bands described by an extended spatial [[wavefunction]].
- Encompasses both elemental crystals from the same chemical group as [[carbon]]—predominantly silicon and germanium—and synthetic compound alloys like gallium arsenide, which feature a *direct band gap* that makes them vastly more efficient for laser and high-frequency microwave applications.

## connections

- [[electron]] — the fundamental negative charge carrier whose movement through the conduction band establishes electrical current.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — provides the theoretical foundation of energy band structure, carrier tunneling, and wave propagation in crystal lattices.
- [[central-processing-unit]] — the complex integrated microchip consisting of billions of interconnected semiconductor transistors executing computational instructions.
- [[resistor]] — a foundational passive electrical component that restricts current flow, commonly integrated alongside semiconductors in electronic circuits.
- [[motherboard]] — the primary printed circuit board that mechanically supports, powers, and interconnects various semiconductor components and expansion buses.
- [[photon]] — the elementary particle of light absorbed to generate electron-hole pairs or emitted during radiative carrier recombination.
- [[united-states]] — the nation where pioneering industrial and academic research laboratories originally developed the transistor and silicon microchip.
