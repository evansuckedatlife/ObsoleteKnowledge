## summary

**Potential Difference**, commonly called **voltage**, is the work done per unit charge by an electric field in moving a charge between two points. Expressed in volts (V), it quantifies the driving force for electron movement in a circuit. Potential difference is fundamental to circuit analysis and electrical engineering, analogous to pressure difference driving fluid flow.

## you gotta know

- Defined as the difference in electric potential energy between two points divided by the charge: V = W/q, where W is work and q is charge.
- Measured in volts; one volt equals one joule per coulomb (J/C), representing the energy transferred per unit charge.
- In a circuit, potential difference drives current flow; higher voltage creates stronger electrical force on charge carriers, analogous to higher pressure difference driving fluid faster.
- A circuit element maintains a potential difference across itself equal to the product of current and resistance (Ohm's Law: V = IR); this is the voltage "drop" across the element.
- Battery voltage is the maximum potential difference they can sustain; a 9V battery maintains a 9-volt potential difference between its terminals when supplying current.
- Ground is arbitrarily chosen as the reference point (zero potential); all other voltages in a circuit are measured relative to ground, though only potential *difference* has physical meaning.
- Potential difference determines the energy delivered to a circuit; power = voltage × current (P = VI), so higher voltage enables more power delivery through the same conductor.

## connections

- [[electric-field]] — the fundamental phenomenon underlying potential difference, created by charge distribution.
- [[current]] — the flow of charge driven by potential difference in a conductor.
- [[resistor]] — a circuit element that maintains a voltage drop proportional to current through it.
- [[circuit]] — the electrical pathway where potential difference drives current through components.
- [[electron]] — the charge carrier whose motion constitutes current when driven by potential difference.
