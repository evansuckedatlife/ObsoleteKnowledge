## summary

**Amphibians** are ectothermic, anamniotic tetrapod vertebrates belonging to the taxonomic class *Amphibia*, distinguished by permeable, glandular skin and an ancestral life history bridging aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems. Evolving from sarcopterygian lobe-finned fishes during the *Devonian period*, they represented the earliest vertebrate pioneers on land and achieved enormous ecological dominance throughout the *Carboniferous* and *Permian* periods. Modern clades—comprising frogs (*Anura*), salamanders (*Urodela*), and caecilians (*Gymnophiona*)—serve as sensitive ecological bioindicators due to their vulnerability to environmental pollution, atmospheric changes, and fungal pathogens.

## you gotta know

- The evolutionary emergence of **amphibians** is documented by transitional stem tetrapod fossils such as *Tiktaalik*, *Acanthostega*, and *Ichthyostega*, which developed digited limbs and primitive pulmonary structures during the late *Devonian period*.
- Most **amphibians** undergo an ancestral biphasic life cycle, hatching from water-laid eggs into aquatic larvae with external gills that undergo radical morphological metamorphosis mediated by thyroid hormones into air-breathing adults.
- Unlike amniotes, **amphibians** lack a cleidoic egg with protective extraembryonic membranes, requiring their gelatinous, unshelled egg masses to remain submerged in freshwater or deposited in highly saturated microhabitats to prevent fatal desiccation.
- Cutaneous gas exchange across thin, richly vascularized skin allows **amphibians** to absorb ambient [[oxygen]], with lungless salamanders of the family *Plethodontidae* relying exclusively on dermal and buccopharyngeal respiration.
- During the prehistoric *Carboniferous* and *Permian* periods, massive temnospondyl ancestors measuring several meters in length served as apex predators across swampy freshwater systems prior to the evolutionary ascent of early reptiles.
- Extant populations of **amphibians** face severe global declines and extinctions caused by anthropogenic habitat fragmentation, chemical contamination, and the worldwide epidemic spread of the chytridiomycete fungus *Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis*.
- Certain **amphibians**, most famously the neotenic axolotl (*Ambystoma mexicanum*), possess unparalleled regenerative capacity, capable of fully replacing severed limbs, crushed retinal tissue, and portions of the central nervous system.

## connections

- [[evolution]] — the macroevolutionary transition that transformed aquatic sarcopterygian fishes into terrestrial tetrapods equipped with limbs and lungs.
- [[oxygen]] — the vital diatomic gas extracted by cutaneous diffusion, larval branchial gills, and vascularized pulmonary surfaces.
- [[alleles]] — variable genetic loci whose selective advantages enabled ancestral populations to evolve terrestrial locomotion and amphibious physiology.
- [[cell]] — the basic biological unit comprising the specialized mucus-secreting and osmoregulatory layers of permeable amphibian integument.
- [[carbon]] — the abundant geological element stored in ancient coal forests where ancestral labyrinthodonts flourished.
- [[protein]] — the constituent biomacromolecules forming specialized antimicrobial peptides secreted on amphibian skin to combat pathogens.
