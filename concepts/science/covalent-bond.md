## summary

A **covalent bond** (characterizing the phenomenon of **covalent bonding**) is a fundamental chemical link established between two atoms through the mutual sharing of one or more pairs of valence electrons. Formulated conceptually by *Gilbert N. Lewis* in 1916 and later rationalized through modern quantum chemistry, this interaction enables participating atoms to attain stable, closed valence configurations. It forms the foundational architecture of organic chemistry, polymer science, and molecular biochemistry, dictating the three-dimensional geometry and reactivity of molecules.

## you gotta know

- *Gilbert N. Lewis* introduced the cubic atom and electron-pair model of bonding, establishing the familiar *Lewis dot structures* and the empirical *octet rule*.
- *Linus Pauling* revolutionized chemical bonding theory in *The Nature of the Chemical Bond*, developing the concept of *electronegativity*, orbital hybridization (such as $sp$, $sp^2$, and $sp^3$), and resonance structures.
- Orbital symmetry characterizes these links as head-on cylindrical $\sigma$ (sigma) bonds, parallel unhybridized lateral $\pi$ (pi) bonds, or face-to-face $\delta$ (delta) bonds found in transition metal complexes.
- Differences in *electronegativity* produce polar bonds exhibiting partial charge separation and permanent electric dipole moments, bridging the continuum between purely nonpolar interactions and ionic electron transfer.
- *Valence bond theory*, advanced by *Walter Heitler* and *Fritz London*, models bonding via the constructive overlap of individual atomic orbitals while preserving localized atomic identity.
- *Molecular orbital theory*, formulated by *Friedrich Hund* and *Robert Mulliken*, treats valence electrons as delocalized across the entire molecule within constructive bonding and destructive antibonding orbitals.
- Dative or coordinate covalent bonds occur when a single Lewis base donates both electrons in the shared pair to an electron-deficient Lewis acid or metal center.

## connections

- [[coulomb-force]] — supplies the underlying attractive potential between the negatively charged shared electron density and the two positively charged atomic nuclei.
- [[crystal]] — assembles when directional network bonds extend continuously in three dimensions, as observed in quartz and diamond.
- [[electron]] — acts as the fundamental subatomic constituent whose pairing, spin orientation, and spatial redistribution create the chemical link.
- [[atom]] — represents the elemental nucleocores joined together into stable polyatomic molecules and extended structures.
- [[carbon]] — exploits tetravalent hybridization to build the intricate chains, rings, and macromolecules underlying organic chemistry.
- [[oxygen]] — forms polar bent geometries in water and vital double bonds in atmospheric gas and biochemical metabolites.
- [[wavefunction]] — mathematically describes the spatial probability amplitudes and linear combinations of atomic orbitals during molecular bonding.
