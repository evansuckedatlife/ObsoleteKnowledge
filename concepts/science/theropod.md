## summary

*Theropods* are a diverse group of bipedal carnivorous dinosaurs characterized by hollow bones, three-toed feet, and powerful hind limbs optimized for running and hunting. Ranging from the chicken-sized *Compsognathus* to the apex predator *Tyrannosaurus rex*, theropods dominated terrestrial food chains throughout the Mesozoic. Theropods are not extinct; birds are living theropods, representing an evolutionary continuation of this remarkable lineage.

## you gotta know

- Evolved during the Triassic period; dominated the Jurassic and Cretaceous as the primary large terrestrial carnivores until the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction (~66 million years ago).
- *Bipedal locomotion*: strong hind limbs supported the body; forelimbs were short and powerful, bearing claws for grasping prey; tail provided balance.
- *Hollow bones* (*pneumatic bones*) reduced weight while maintaining strength, facilitating the power-to-weight ratio necessary for predatory speed and agility.
- *Tyrannosaurus rex* is the most famous theropod: ~12 meters long, ~9 tonnes, powerful bite force (~12,000 newtons), apex predator of the Late Cretaceous.
- *Smaller theropods* like *Velociraptor* and *Deinonychus* were highly agile hunters; pack-hunting behavior is inferred from trackways and nesting sites.
- *Feathered theropods*: fossil evidence shows many theropods, especially smaller species and juveniles, possessed feathers or feather-like structures for insulation or display.
- *Birds are theropods*: the evolutionary transition is complete; birds retain theropod skeletal features (hollow bones, three-toed feet modified into perches, wishbone), confirming descent from small theropod ancestors in the Jurassic.
