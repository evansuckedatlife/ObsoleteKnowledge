## summary

The **Coulomb force** (also known as the **electrostatic force** and governed mathematically by **Coulomb's law**) is the fundamental physical interaction between stationary, electrically charged particles. Formulated experimentally in 1785 by French physicist *Charles-Augustin de Coulomb* using a precision torsion balance, it dictates that like charges repel while opposite charges attract with an intensity inversely proportional to the square of their separation distance. This interaction underpins all chemical behavior, planetary-scale matter stability, atomic structures, and classical electrodynamics.

## you gotta know

- *Coulomb's law* expresses the force vector mathematically as $\mathbf{F} = k_e \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}$, where $k_e = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$ is Coulomb's constant and $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity.
- *Charles-Augustin de Coulomb* proved the inverse-square dependence empirically utilizing a sensitive torsion balance originally perfected by *John Michell*.
- *Ernest Rutherford* utilized the repulsive electrostatic potential between alpha particles and target nuclei to explain large-angle scattering in the gold foil experiment, demonstrating the existence of the compact central nucleus.
- Electrostatic fields are conservative, meaning the path integral of the field around any closed loop is zero, permitting the definition of electric potential energy and electric potential (voltage).
- *Gauss's law*, the first of *Maxwell's equations*, reformulates the inverse-square law into a flux relation stating that the total electric flux out of any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by permittivity.
- *Earnshaw's theorem* mathematically proves that a collection of stationary point charges cannot be maintained in a stable static equilibrium solely by electrostatic forces, necessitating quantum mechanics for atomic stability.
- In modern quantum electrodynamics, the force is understood fundamentally as an exchange of virtual photons between charged fermions.

## connections

- [[covalent-bond]] — relies on attractive electrostatic forces between shared electron clouds and adjacent atomic nuclei to maintain molecular cohesion.
- [[crystal]] — derives cohesive lattice energy and structural geometry in ionic solids from pairwise electrostatic sums described by the Madelung constant.
- [[nucleus]] — harbors concentrated positive charge from protons that exerts intense attraction on orbiting negative charges.
- [[electron]] — acts as the elementary negative charge carrier ($e \approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$) whose interactions dominate chemistry and atomic physics.
- [[atom]] — achieves binding through the attractive potential balancing the kinetic energy of surrounding orbital charges.
- [[standard-model]] — embeds classical electrodynamics within the unified electroweak sector mediated by the electromagnetic gauge field.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — prevents classical electrostatic orbital collapse through the uncertainty principle, Fermi-Dirac statistics, and wave mechanics.
