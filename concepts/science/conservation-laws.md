## summary

**Conservation laws** (singular **conservation law**, also termed **physical conservation laws**) are fundamental theoretical principles stating that particular measurable properties of an isolated physical system remain strictly constant during dynamical evolution. Found across classical mechanics, electromagnetism, general relativity, and quantum field theory, these laws govern quantities such as energy, linear momentum, angular momentum, electric charge, color charge, and baryon number. Rather than being arbitrary empirical rules, modern physics identifies conservation laws as direct mathematical consequences of continuous coordinate and internal gauge symmetries.

## you gotta know

- *Noether's first theorem* proves that every differentiable continuous symmetry of an action corresponds to an exact conservation law: time translation yields energy, spatial translation yields linear momentum, and spatial rotation yields angular momentum.
- Conservation of angular momentum accounts for planetary sweep rates under *Kepler's second law*, the stabilization of rotating bodies, and the quantized intrinsic spin of quantum particles.
- Conservation of electric charge represents an exact gauge symmetry under global $U(1)$ transformations in quantum electrodynamics, forbidding any process where net charge varies.
- Local conservation laws are mathematically expressed via continuity equations, relating the temporal rate of change of a local charge density to the divergence of its spatial current flux.
- In the [[standard-model]], continuous internal gauge symmetries mandate the exact conservation of color charge in quantum chromodynamics and weak isospin in unified electroweak interactions.
- Discrete symmetries are not universally conserved; most famously, parity ($P$) symmetry is maximally violated in the weak nuclear interaction, as demonstrated by *Chien-Shiung Wu* in 1956.
- Approximate conservation laws, including the preservation of flavor quantum numbers like strangeness, charm, and isospin, hold rigorously under the strong interaction but are broken by weak decays.

## connections

- [[conservation-of-energy]] — the foundational conservation principle associated with temporal translation symmetry across physical systems.
- [[standard-model]] — unified particle physics framework whose underlying gauge group symmetries mandate exact and approximate conservation laws.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — formalizes conserved observables as self-adjoint operators that commute directly with the Hamiltonian operator.
- [[electron]] — stable elementary lepton whose absolute immunity to radiative decay is guaranteed by conservation of electric charge.
- [[quark]] — fundamental colored fermion subject to exact color charge conservation and flavor-changing weak transitions.
- [[conductor]] — macroscopic system whose electrical conduction currents are governed by the charge continuity equation.
- [[constructive-interference]] — wave phenomenon in which local intensity variations conserve total integrated energy and momentum flux.
- [[photon]] — massless gauge boson mediating electromagnetic interactions while strictly preserving charge, energy, and momentum.
