## summary

**Bacteriology** is the branch of microbiology that studies bacteria—their structure, growth, reproduction, physiology, and role in human health and disease. Emerging in the 19th century with the microscope and germ theory, bacteriology revolutionized medicine by establishing that infectious diseases were caused by specific microorganisms. This discipline laid the groundwork for modern antibiotics, vaccines, and public health.

## you gotta know

- Founded as a formal science by *Robert Koch* and *Louis Pasteur* in the late 1800s, establishing the germ theory of disease.
- Koch's postulates (1876) provided the scientific criteria for proving that a specific microorganism causes a specific disease.
- *Alexander Fleming's* accidental discovery of penicillin (1928) revolutionized bacteriology and medicine, launching the antibiotic era.
- Bacteria reproduce primarily through binary fission, dividing asexually to create identical clones with rapid doubling times.
- Bacterial colonies grow in distinct morphologies on agar plates, enabling identification and isolation of species.
- Antibiotic resistance emerged as bacteria evolved to survive drug treatment, creating an ongoing challenge to bacterial control.
- Bacteria colonize every environment on Earth—soil, oceans, animal guts, and atmosphere—profoundly affecting ecosystems and global cycles.
- Bacteriological techniques like Gram staining differentiate bacteria by cell wall composition, while culture techniques isolate and identify specific species.
- Modern genomic bacteriology sequences bacterial DNA to understand evolutionary relationships, virulence factors, and antibiotic resistance mechanisms at the molecular level.
- Bacteriology has expanded beyond medicine to include industrial fermentation, food preservation, environmental remediation, and synthetic biology applications.
