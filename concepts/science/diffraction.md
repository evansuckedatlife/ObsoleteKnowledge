## summary

**diffraction** is the fundamental physical phenomenon wherein waves spread out, bend, and interfere after encountering an obstacle, aperture, or edge comparable in size to their wavelength. First systematically analyzed in classical optics through the wave descriptions of Christiaan Huygens and Augustin-Jean Fresnel, it serves as primary evidence for the wave nature of both electromagnetic radiation and matter. In modern physics, observing how beams of particles bend around crystal lattices directly demonstrates quantum-mechanical wave-particle duality.

## you gotta know

- Occurs when any traveling wave encounters a slit, edge, or barrier whose physical dimensions are on the order of the radiation's wavelength.
- Formally modeled in classical wave mechanics via the Huygens-Fresnel principle, which treats every point on an advancing wavefront as an independent source of secondary spherical wavelets.
- Delineated experimentally into Fresnel diffraction in the near-field regime and Fraunhofer diffraction in the far-field regime where wavefronts are essentially planar.
- Quantified for crystalline periodic lattices by Bragg's law, which predicts the constructive and destructive geometric interference peaks of scattered x-rays.
- Proved the wave properties of matter in the landmark Davisson-Germer experiment, which scattered an accelerated [[electron]] beam off a nickel crystal target.
- Sets the fundamental optical resolution threshold known as the Abbe diffraction limit, preventing conventional lenses from focusing light into spots smaller than roughly half its wavelength.
- Creates characteristic alternating bright and dark fringes on a detector screen when monochromatic light passes through single or multiple narrow apertures.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework that relies on particle diffraction to validate the de Broglie hypothesis and matter waves.
- [[photon]] — the quantum of electromagnetic energy whose spatial probability distribution displays classical interference patterns.
- [[electron]] — the charged subatomic particle whose crystal scattering experimentally verified the wave nature of matter.
- [[wavefunction]] — the mathematical amplitude whose interference and spatial dispersion give rise to observable quantum intensity fringes.
- [[albert-einstein]] — the physicist whose analysis of the photoelectric effect helped establish the complementary wave-particle description of light.
- [[dielectric]] — an insulating material whose microstructured periodic interfaces can form photonic crystals that diffract electromagnetic radiation.
