## summary

**Calcium** (element 20, Ca) is an alkaline-earth metal essential to nearly all living organisms, functioning as a structural component of bones and teeth and as a critical signaling ion in neurons and muscle cells. The body tightly regulates calcium levels through vitamin D, parathyroid hormone, and dietary intake. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) are second messengers in cellular signaling, mediating muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, and gene regulation.

## you gotta know

- Calcium is the fifth most abundant element in Earth's crust (3.7%), primarily as calcium carbonate (limestone, chalk) and calcium phosphate (the mineral phase of bone).
- Atomic number 20 with electron configuration [Ar]4s²; readily loses two valence electrons to form Ca²⁺ cations, which are stable and inert.
- Approximately 99% of body calcium is stored in bones and teeth as hydroxyapatite (Ca₅(PO₄)₃(OH)), a calcium phosphate compound providing strength and hardness.
- The remaining ~1% of body calcium exists as free or protein-bound Ca²⁺ in blood and cells; serum calcium is tightly controlled (8.5–10.5 mg/dL) because deviations disrupt cellular function.
- Calcium ions are the primary trigger for muscle contraction: Ca²⁺ released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum binds calmodulin, allowing myosin-actin interaction.
- In neurons, Ca²⁺ influx through voltage-gated calcium channels triggers the release of neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft.
- Vitamin D (calcitriol) stimulates calcium absorption in the small intestine; parathyroid hormone (PTH) increases blood calcium by promoting renal reabsorption and bone resorption.
- Milk and dairy products are rich dietary sources of calcium; vegetables like kale and spinach contain calcium but also oxalates that reduce bioavailability.
