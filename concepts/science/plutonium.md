## summary

**Plutonium** is a synthetic, radioactive actinide metal with atomic number 94 and chemical symbol *Pu*, first synthesized by *Glenn T. Seaborg*, *Edwin McMillan*, *Joseph W. Kennedy*, and *Arthur Wahl* at the University of California, Berkeley in 1940. Characterized by its silvery appearance that rapidly oxidizes to a dull yellow-gray in air, it possesses six distinct solid allotropes under ambient pressure and exhibits an anomalous volume contraction upon melting. As a fissile element capable of sustaining an explosive nuclear chain reaction, it served as the core fissile charge for the *Trinity* test and the *Fat Man* atomic bomb dropped on [[japan]] during [[world-war-ii]]. Beyond nuclear armaments, its isotopes are vital energy sources for breeder reactors and radioisotope thermoelectric generators that power deep-space exploratory probes sent by [[nasa]].

## you gotta know

- Discovered late in 1940 by bombarding uranium-238 with deuterons in the Berkeley 60-inch cyclotron, yielding neptunium-238 which beta-decayed into plutonium-238; the breakthrough was kept strictly classified by the [[united-states]] throughout [[world-war-ii]].
- Plutonium-239 is produced in industrial nuclear reactors through neutron capture by uranium-238, forming unstable uranium-239 which undergoes two successive beta decays via neptunium-239 to yield the fissile isotope.
- Served as the fissile core of the *Fat Man* implosion-type atomic bomb detonated over Nagasaki; gun-type weapon designs were unusable because trace contamination with plutonium-240 induced high spontaneous fission rates that would trigger premature detonation.
- Displays notoriously complex metallurgy characterized by six distinct allotropic phases at atmospheric pressure; stabilizing the ductile face-centered cubic delta phase requires alloying the metal with small quantities of gallium to prevent destructive phase shifts.
- Emits high-energy alpha radiation that poses minimal external danger because skin blocks alpha particles, but inhaled aerosol particles settle in bone marrow and the [[sternum]], causing chronic radiation sickness and osteosarcoma.
- Plutonium-238 provides dependable decay heat for radioisotope thermoelectric generators deployed by [[nasa]] to power outer-solar-system probes, including the *Voyager*, *Cassini*, and *New Horizons* missions and the *Curiosity* rover, operating far beyond [[jupiter]].
- Displays four distinct oxidation states simultaneously in equilibrium within dilute aqueous solutions without organic chelators, creating vivid chromatic variations including purple, emerald green, and amber.

## connections

- [[radon]] — fellow dangerous alpha-emitting radionuclide that poses severe inhalation and radiation hazards.
- [[atom]] — atomic species whose actinide configuration gives rise to complex 5f-electron orbital chemistry.
- [[nucleus]] — heavy atomic core whose neutron absorption and fission release massive binding energy.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict that spurred the secret industrial production of plutonium at the Hanford site.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical standoff during which thousands of metric tons of weapons-grade plutonium were stockpiled by the [[united-states]] and the [[soviet-union]].
- [[nasa]] — space exploration agency relying on plutonium-238 decay heat to generate electrical power on long-duration interplanetary probes.
- [[jupiter]] — outer gas giant visited by multiple deep-space probes powered by plutonium thermoelectric cells.
- [[sternum]] — flat marrow-bearing bone that accumulates circulating actinides following accidental inhalation or ingestion.
