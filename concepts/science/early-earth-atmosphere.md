## summary

The **early Earth atmosphere** was the planetary gaseous envelope that enveloped our world during the Hadean and Archean eons, prior to the rise of biogenic atmospheric oxygenation. Emerging from catastrophic meteorite impacts and vigorous volcanic degassing, this primordial envelope was characterized by an almost complete absence of free diatomic *oxygen* and elevated partial pressures of *carbon* dioxide, nitrogen, water vapor, and modest concentrations of reduced gases. It served as the crucible of prebiotic chemical synthesis and the critical planetary incubator for the genesis of the first living organisms.

## you gotta know

- Originated after the loss of the primary hydrogen-helium nebula via solar winds and intense impact degassing during early accretion.
- Lacked appreciable free molecular *oxygen*, remaining strongly anoxic until the Great Oxidation Event approximately 2.4 billion years ago.
- Dominated compositionally by *carbon* dioxide, molecular nitrogen, water vapor, and trace abundances of methane, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen sulfide.
- Simulated famously in the 1952 *Miller-Urey experiment*, which used an electric spark discharge through reduced gases to generate racemic mixtures of [[protein]] building blocks.
- Mitigated the astronomical Faint Young Sun paradox through atmospheric greenhouse warming provided by abundant carbon dioxide and methane.
- Subjected planetary surface waters to intense solar ultraviolet radiation due to the absence of an ozone photoprotective layer.
- Transformed dramatically through oxygenic [[photosynthesis]] developed by ancient cyanobacterial ancestors, causing the deposition of banded iron formations.

## connections

- [[oxygen]] — virtually absent as a free gas in this early regime until biogenic production irrevocably transformed planetary chemistry.
- [[carbon]] — present primarily as oxidized carbon dioxide and volatile methane, driving ancient planetary greenhouse retention.
- [[photosynthesis]] — the evolutionary biological innovation that depleted carbon dioxide and introduced copious oxygen into the atmosphere.
- [[cold-war]] — the historical geopolitical backdrop during which modern geochemical experiments on prebiotic atmospheres blossomed.
- [[earliest-life]] — evolved adaptations to survive under the harsh ultraviolet radiation and anoxic regime of this planetary envelope.
- [[earth-crust]] — engaged in massive geochemical weathering reactions that scrubbed volcanic gases out of the primordial air.
