## summary

**Electrocardiography** (abbreviated as **ECG** or **EKG**) is the clinical and diagnostic practice of recording the electrical activity of the heart over time using cutaneous electrodes placed upon the body. Developed into a robust medical technology during the late [[industrial-revolution]], it translates the coordinated ionic depolarization and repolarization of myocardial tissue into characteristic waveforms that reveal arrhythmias, conduction defects, and ischemic damage. By recording the minute voltage differentials generated by moving ions across cardiac membranes, it serves as one of the most widely utilized noninvasive diagnostic modalities in modern medicine.

## you gotta know

- Dutch physiologist *Willem Einthoven* invented the string galvanometer in 1901 to record cardiac currents with clinical fidelity, earning the 1924 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
- Standard 12-lead recordings capture cardiac electrical vectors from twelve distinct geometric perspectives using ten physical electrodes attached to the limbs and chest.
- Waveform features correspond to distinct physiological phases: the P wave reflects atrial depolarization, the QRS complex represents ventricular depolarization, and the T wave indicates ventricular repolarization.
- *Einthoven's triangle* is an equilateral conceptual model formed by electrodes on the right arm, left arm, and left leg, establishing the bipolar limb leads I, II, and III.
- Relies directly on the [[electrical-conductivity]] of bodily electrolytes, since current generated by the heart propagates through conductive interstitial fluid to surface skin sensors.
- Clinical interpretation evaluates rhythm, intervals, and axis deviations to diagnose myocardial infarction, where ST-segment elevation reveals acute transmural ischemia.
- Pathological prolongation of the QT interval warns of elevated vulnerability to polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, notably the lethal arrhythmia known as *torsades de pointes*.
- Precordial leads labeled V1 through V6 measure unipolar potentials across the horizontal plane of the chest, designed by *Frank Wilson* using a central terminal resistive network.

## connections

- [[electrical-conductivity]] — physical property governing how ionic currents generated by cardiac pacemakers propagate through surrounding tissues to surface electrodes.
- [[cell]] — basic biological unit whose excitable membranes generate action potentials through coordinated opening and closing of voltage-gated ion channels.
- [[sodium]] — essential alkali metal electrolyte whose rapid influx through specialized channel proteins initiates the phase-0 upstroke of ventricular depolarization.
- [[protein]] — structural class of biomacromolecules including ion channels, pumps, and gap junctions that maintain transmembrane potentials and enable electrical conduction.
- [[oxygen]] — vital metabolic gas whose deprivation during arterial occlusion disrupts myocardial metabolism, causing ST-segment shifts on tracings.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical technological epoch that introduced precision galvanometers, photographic recording devices, and electrical engineering into clinical physiology.
- [[resistor]] — passive circuit component utilized within the *Wilson central terminal* to create an electrical reference point averaging limb potentials to zero.
