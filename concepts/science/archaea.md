## summary

**Archaea** constitute one of the three fundamental domains of cellular life, alongside *Bacteria* and *Eukaryota*. First recognized as a separate evolutionary lineage in 1977 by microbiologist *Carl Woese* and his collaborator *George Fox* through ribosomal RNA comparative analysis, they dismantled the traditional two-empire classification of prokaryotes versus eukaryotes. Although structurally resembling bacteria with their lack of membrane-bound organelles and circular chromosomes, their internal information processing pathways closely mirror eukaryotic systems, providing crucial insights into the deepest roots of terrestrial [[evolution]].

## you gotta know

- Discovered as an independent domain by *Carl Woese* and *George Fox* in 1977 using phylogenetic analysis of *16S ribosomal RNA* sequences.
- Possess unique ether-linked membrane lipids with branched, isoprenoid chains, contrasting with the ester-linked unbranched fatty acids found in *Bacteria* and *Eukaryota*.
- Cell walls lack true *peptidoglycan*, instead utilizing *pseudopeptidoglycan* (*pseudomurein*), surface layers composed of glycoproteins (*S-layers*), or specialized complex polysaccharides.
- Information processing mechanisms, particularly [[dna]] replication, RNA transcription, and [[protein-synthesis]], share substantial structural and enzymatic homology with eukaryotic machinery rather than bacterial systems.
- Renowned as extremophiles inhabiting high-stress niches, including hyperthermophiles near deep-sea hydrothermal vents, extreme halophiles in salt lakes, and acidophiles in sulfuric runoff.
- Drive biological *methanogenesis*, with methanogens producing methane gas as an end product of anaerobic respiration using hydrogen to reduce [[carbon]] dioxide.
- Utilize distinct metabolic pathways such as the *Wood-Ljungdahl pathway* for fixing [[carbon]] and novel variants of glycolysis such as the *Entner-Doudoroff pathway*.
- The *Asgard* superphylum represents the closest known prokaryotic relatives of eukaryotes, strongly supporting the two-domain tree of life (*eocyte hypothesis*).

## connections

- [[cell]] — unicellular anucleate organisms lacking membrane-bound organelles yet displaying eukaryotic organizational traits.
- [[bacteria]] — morphologically similar prokaryotes whose cell walls and biochemical membranes diverge fundamentally from archaeal counterparts.
- [[dna]] — replication systems in archaea employ eukaryotic-like polymerases and clamp loader proteins to copy genetic material.
- [[protein]] — specialized chaperonins and heat-shock assemblies allow cellular proteomes to withstand denaturation under boiling conditions.
- [[enzyme]] — source of extreme biocatalysts like thermostable polymerases utilized in molecular biology and industrial synthesis.
- [[photosynthesis]] — light-driven energy capture in haloarchaea relies on retinal pigments like *bacteriorhodopsin* rather than chlorophyll-based photochemical centers.
- [[evolution]] — pivotal taxa supporting models of eukaryogenesis through endosymbiotic partnerships between ancient archaeal hosts and proteobacterial ancestors.
