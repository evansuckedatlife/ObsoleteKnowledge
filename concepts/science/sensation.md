## summary

**Sensation** is the biological and psychological process by which sensory receptors detect stimuli from the environment and transmit signals to the nervous system. It is the first stage of perception, distinct from the conscious interpretation of sensory data. Sensation underlies all human awareness of heat, light, sound, taste, smell, and touch.

## you gotta know

- The process begins with *transduction* — conversion of physical or chemical stimuli (photons, pressure waves, chemical molecules) into electrical signals the nervous system can process.
- Five classical senses (*vision*, *hearing*, *taste*, *smell*, *touch*) are complemented by proprioception (body position awareness) and the vestibular system (balance and motion).
- *Absolute threshold* is the minimum stimulus intensity required to produce a sensation at least 50% of the time; varies by sense and individual sensitivity.
- *Sensory adaptation* — repeated exposure to unchanging stimuli reduces the perceived intensity; the nervous system suppresses constant background signals.
- Taste and smell are *chemoreceptive* senses, responding to dissolved or airborne molecules; the tongue and olfactory epithelium have specialized receptor types for sweet, salty, bitter, sour, and savory (*umami*).
- Touch involves distinct receptors for pressure, temperature, and pain, distributed across the skin; density varies by body region, explaining why fingertips are more sensitive than forearms.
- The *Scoville scale* quantifies spiciness, a pure sensation of pain triggered by capsaicinoids in peppers, independent of flavor.
