# Sedimentary Rock

## summary

**Sedimentary rocks** form from the consolidation and lithification of sediments—fragments of rock and minerals produced by weathering, as well as chemical precipitates and organic material—that accumulate in layers at Earth's surface. They are the only rock type that commonly contains fossils, making them invaluable for understanding past life and environments, and they cover about 75% of Earth's land surface despite comprising only 5% of the crust by volume.

## you gotta know

- Sedimentary rocks form in three stages: *weathering* (breaking down of existing rocks), *transport* (movement of fragments by water, wind, or ice), and *lithification* (compaction and cementation of layers into solid rock).
- *Clastic sedimentary rocks* are composed of mineral fragments cemented together; examples include [[sandstone]] (sand-sized grains) and shale (mud-sized clay); grain size reflects transport distance and energy of the depositional environment.
- *Chemical sedimentary rocks* form when dissolved minerals precipitate out of water; [[limestone]], composed of calcium carbonate, is the quintessential biochemical sedimentary rock, formed from the shells and skeletons of marine organisms accumulating on the seafloor.
- *Fossils* are preserved in sedimentary rocks because organisms are buried quickly, before decay or scavenging can destroy them; fossil layers mark distinct time periods and allow scientists to date rocks and correlate strata across continents.
- *Stratigraphy* is the study of rock layers; the principle of *superposition* states that in undisturbed sequences, older rocks lie beneath younger ones, allowing relative dating of fossils and rocks without radiometric methods.
- Sedimentary rocks are economically important: they host most *oil and natural gas* reserves (formed from organic-rich shales and limestone), *groundwater* aquifers, and valuable minerals like [[salt]] and phosphate.
- *Cross-bedding* and *ripple marks* are sedimentary features formed by flowing water or wind; these structures reveal the direction and energy of ancient currents and are used to reconstruct past environments.

## connections

- [[igneous-rock]] — rocks formed by cooling magma; they break down by weathering to create sediments for sedimentary rocks.
- [[metamorphic-rock]] — rocks altered by heat and pressure; sedimentary rocks can be buried and metamorphosed into metamorphic rocks.
- [[limestone]] — the most common chemical/biochemical sedimentary rock, composed of calcium carbonate from organism remains.
- [[mineral]] — the individual crystalline components that make up rock fragments in clastic sedimentary rocks.
- [[sandstone]] — a clastic sedimentary rock composed of sand-sized mineral grains.
