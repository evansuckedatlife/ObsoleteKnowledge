## summary

**Lava** is molten rock that erupts onto Earth's surface from a [[volcano]], the extrusive form of [[magma]]. Once exposed to the atmosphere, lava cools and solidifies into volcanic rock; the rate and manner of cooling determine the rock's grain size and mineral composition. Lava flows range from slow, viscous streams that advance meters per hour to fast, fluid surges moving kilometers per hour, and their characteristics directly reflect the composition and temperature of the parent magma.

## you gotta know

- Lava composition parallels its parent [[magma]]: low-silica basaltic lava (~50% SiO₂) erupts at ~1200°C and flows freely; high-silica rhyolitic lava (~70% SiO₂) erupts at ~900°C and is sluggish and thick.
- Basaltic lava dominates Earth's surface volcanism, forming the ocean floor at mid-ocean ridges and constructing *shield volcanoes* (gently sloping, like Hawaii) where it erupts in vast quantities with low viscosity.
- Viscosity increases dramatically with silica content and dissolved water content: high-silica lava resists flow and often erupts explosively, building steep *stratovolcanoes* (like Mount Fuji, Mount St. Helens).
- A lava flow's *surface* cools quickly to form a dark, ropy (pahoehoe) or jagged blocky (a'ā) crust, while the interior remains molten and may continue flowing for weeks to months.
- Cooling rate determines grain size: rapid surface cooling produces fine-grained or glassy rock (obsidian, basalt); slow underground cooling produces coarse-grained igneous rock (granite).
- Lava eruptions from shield volcanoes can inundate thousands of square kilometers (the Deccan Traps in India, the Siberian Traps); these flood-basalt events have coincided with mass extinctions.
- Pillow lava forms when lava erupts underwater, quenching rapidly and fragmenting into rounded formations characteristic of seafloor spreading.
- Ancient lava flows, now solidified and buried, can be traced in stratigraphic sequences to date rocks and correlate geological layers across regions.
- Understanding lava behavior is essential for volcanic hazard assessment and forecasting eruptions in regions threatened by active volcanism.

## connections

- [[magma]] — the parent material that becomes lava upon eruption.
- [[volcano]] — the landform through which lava is expelled.
- [[igneous-rock]] — the crystalline solid rock that lava becomes upon cooling.
- [[basalt]] — the most common volcanic rock formed from rapidly cooled basaltic lava.
- [[plate-tectonics]] — the driving mechanism that generates lava at spreading ridges and subduction zones.
