## summary

**Pollination** is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther (male reproductive structure) of one flower to the stigma (female reproductive structure) of another, or occasionally to the same flower. This process enables sexual reproduction in flowering plants and has shaped the evolution of both plants and their animal pollinators through coevolution. Pollination is essential to global food security and ecosystem function.

## you gotta know

- Occurs through various pollination vectors: insects (bees, butterflies, beetles, flies), wind, water, birds, and mammals, each selecting different flower morphologies and colors.
- Wind pollination produces copious lightweight pollen grains with minimal nutritional reward; insect pollination involves fewer, stickier pollen grains and often features colorful petals and nectar rewards.
- Represents a coevolutionary relationship: flowering plants evolved diverse flower structures and rewards to attract specific pollinators, while pollinators evolved specialized body parts and behaviors to access pollen and nectar.
- Cross-pollination (between different plants) typically produces more genetically diverse offspring than self-pollination, promoting fitness and adaptation in plant populations.
- Some plants have evolved elaborate mechanisms ensuring cross-pollination: incompatibility systems that prevent self-fertilization, or spatial separation of male and female reproductive parts.
- Pollination services provided by insects (particularly bees) are economically vital; approximately 75% of global food crops depend partly on animal pollination.
- Habitat loss, pesticides, and climate change threaten pollinator populations, which in turn imperils the plants depending on them and the food systems built upon pollinated crops.

## connections

- [[flower]] — the reproductive structure adapted for pollination and the site where pollen transfer occurs.
- [[plant-reproduction]] — the sexual reproduction process that pollination initiates in flowering plants.
- [[angiosperm]] — the flowering plant group whose reproduction depends entirely on pollination.
- [[pollinator]] — the animal (or wind/water) that transfers pollen between flowers.
- [[pollen]] — the male gametophyte that must reach the stigma for reproduction to occur.
