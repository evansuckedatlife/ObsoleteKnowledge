# Great Oxidation Event

## summary

The **Great Oxidation Event** (GOE), occurring around 2.4 billion years ago at the boundary between the Archean and Proterozoic Eons, was the moment when atmospheric oxygen concentrations rose sharply as photosynthetic cyanobacteria flooded Earth's air with the byproduct of their metabolism. For billions of years, produced oxygen had been consumed by reactions with dissolved iron in the oceans; once those iron sinks were saturated, oxygen accumulated in the atmosphere—a transformation that poisoned anaerobic life but enabled the evolution of complex aerobic organisms.

## you gotta know

- Before the GOE, Earth's atmosphere was largely *anoxic*—dominated by nitrogen, carbon dioxide, methane, and water vapor, with little to no free oxygen; anaerobic prokaryotes ruled.
- *Cyanobacteria* (photosynthetic bacteria, formerly called blue-green algae) evolved around 3 billion years ago and used water and sunlight to make sugars and oxygen—slowly at first, since produced oxygen was "trapped" by dissolving iron.
- As *iron oxide* deposits accumulated in the oceans (creating massive banded iron formations still mined today), iron's capacity to consume oxygen saturated; oxygen began accumulating in the air around 2.4 billion years ago.
- The sudden rise in atmospheric oxygen was catastrophic for anaerobic life—a "great oxygenation crisis"—forcing many species into oxygen-free niches (deep oceans, sediments, or inside other organisms); many went extinct.
- Simultaneously, oxygen-rich atmosphere enabled *aerobic respiration*, which is far more energy-efficient than anaerobic metabolism; aerobic organisms could grow larger and more complex.
- The GOE set the stage for multicellular life; without oxygen's energy yield, animals and fungi (which are aerobic heterotrophs) could never have evolved. It is one of Earth's most consequential environmental transitions.

- The event was not instantaneous—oxygen rose over hundreds of thousands of years as iron became saturated. This whiff of oxygen occasionally appeared and disappeared before the final rise around 2.4 billion years ago.
- Banded iron formations (BIFs) are sedimentary rocks with alternating layers of iron oxide and silica, found worldwide in Archean and early Proterozoic rocks. They record oxygen consuming dissolved iron.
- The oxygen poisoning forced a great oxidation crisis: anaerobic metabolism generates 2 ATP per glucose; aerobic respiration yields ~30-32 ATP—a ~15-fold energy boost that allowed aerobic life to flourish.
