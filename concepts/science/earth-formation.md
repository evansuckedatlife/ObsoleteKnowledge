## summary

**Earth formation** refers to the dynamic astrophysical and geological assembly of our planet roughly 4.54 billion years ago from the circumstellar accretion disk surrounding the nascent Sun. Driven by gravitational attraction, turbulent collisions of planetesimals, and intense radioactive heating, the proto-planet rapidly consolidated matter across tens of millions of years. This turbulent origin culminated in extensive planetary differentiation, the establishment of the early core and mantle, and a catastrophic giant impact that produced the Moon.

## you gotta know

- Occurred approximately 4.54 billion years ago within the solar nebula, an enormous rotating cloud of gas, dust, and microscopic grain particles left over from stellar nucleosynthesis.
- Driven fundamentally by the process of core accretion, where microscopic grains collided, adhered through electrostatic forces, and accumulated into kilometre-sized planetesimals and protoplanetary embryos.
- Experienced the *giant impact hypothesis*, in which a Mars-sized protoplanet named *Theia* collided with the young proto-Earth, vaporising material into orbit that rapidly coalesced to form the Moon.
- Underwent catastrophic early melting known as the magma ocean stage, powered by heat from continuous meteorite bombardment, gravitational collapse, and decay of short-lived radionuclides like aluminum-26.
- Established planetary differentiation during the *iron catastrophe*, in which molten iron and nickel sank toward the centre to form the metallic core while lighter silicates rose to build the primitive mantle.
- Retained volatile elements delivered in part through later carbonaceous chondrite asteroid bombardments, providing the water and chemical building blocks necessary for the hydrosphere and primitive atmosphere.
- Marked the beginning of the *Hadean Eon*, setting the thermal and compositional boundary conditions that governed all subsequent crustal formation and the emergence of life.

## connections

- [[earth-interior]] — the directly differentiated internal layer structure produced by planetary melting and gravitational settling during accretion.
- [[jupiter]] — the giant gas planet whose orbital migration through the early solar disk dynamically perturbed planetesimal orbits and influenced terrestrial accretion.
- [[oxygen]] — the dominant electronegative element bonded into silicate minerals throughout the newly accreted planetary mantle.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical building block whose isotopic ratios provide precise geochronological clocks for dating early planetary solids.
- [[carbon]] — the vital volatile element delivered by chondritic impactors during late accretion phases.
- [[primordial-earth]] — the early molten world resulting directly from the energetic culmination of planetary assembly.
- [[archean-eon]] — the subsequent geologic interval during which crustal stabilization and stable oceans succeeded early assembly.
