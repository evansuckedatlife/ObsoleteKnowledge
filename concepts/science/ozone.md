## summary

**Ozone** is a triatomic allotrope of oxygen (O₃) that forms in Earth's upper atmosphere and plays a crucial role in protecting life from harmful ultraviolet radiation. Though toxic at ground level, stratospheric ozone is essential to planetary habitability. Its depletion by chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) represents one of the major environmental crises of the modern era.

## you gotta know

- Composed of three oxygen atoms bonded together, distinct from the diatomic O₂ that comprises most of the atmosphere; the name derives from the Greek word for smell, as ozone has a distinctive acrid odor.
- Absorbs ultraviolet-B and ultraviolet-C radiation, preventing these high-energy photons from reaching Earth's surface where they damage DNA in living organisms.
- Forms naturally in the stratosphere through photochemical reactions when UV light breaks O₂ molecules and recombines them into O₃.
- At sea level, ozone is a toxic blue gas created as a byproduct of combustion and photochemical smog, harmful to respiratory systems; high ground-level ozone is a major component of urban smog and air pollution.
- Stratospheric ozone concentration declined sharply in the late 20th century, particularly over Antarctica, due to chlorine atoms released from photolyzed CFCs; the Antarctic ozone hole grows and shrinks seasonally.
- The Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs and related ozone-depleting substances, demonstrating effective global environmental cooperation; it is one of the few international treaties considered an environmental success.
- Ozone recovery is projected over the coming decades as CFC concentrations decline in the atmosphere; the ozone layer should return to pre-1980 levels by mid-century.
- Despite its toxicity, atmospheric ozone layer remains essential; a 1% decrease in stratospheric ozone corresponds to roughly a 2% increase in UV-B reaching the surface, with cascading biological effects.
