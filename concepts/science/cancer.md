## summary

**Cancer** is a disease characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and division resulting from mutations that disable tumor suppressors and activate oncogenes, breaking the cell cycle's regulatory checkpoints. Cancer cells lose contact inhibition, evade apoptosis, and proliferate indefinitely, forming tumors that can metastasize to distant tissues. It ranks among the leading causes of death globally and encompasses over 200 distinct diseases with varying molecular causes, symptoms, and prognoses.

## you gotta know

- Arises from accumulation of multiple mutations in a single cell lineage, typically requiring both oncogenic activation and tumor suppressor inactivation.
- Hallmarks include unlimited replicative potential, resistance to cell death, sustained proliferation, and ability to invade and metastasize to distant sites.
- Mutations may be inherited (germline) or acquired (somatic); environmental factors like smoking, UV radiation, and viral infection increase mutation rates.
- Common origins include epithelial tissues (carcinomas), blood-forming cells (leukemias), connective tissues (sarcomas), and immune cells (lymphomas).
- Typically requires 4–7 key mutations for full malignancy, explaining why cancer incidence increases dramatically with age.
- Treatments include surgery, chemotherapy (targeting rapidly dividing cells), radiation, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy against specific mutated proteins.
- Recent advances in immunotherapy exploit cancer cells' ability to hide from immune surveillance, enhancing the immune system's cancer-killing capacity.
- Epigenetic changes can activate cancer genes or silence protective genes without altering DNA sequences, adding complexity to cancer biology beyond simple mutations.
- Cancer heterogeneity—multiple cell types within a single tumor with different mutations—complicates treatment and drives drug resistance, necessitating combination therapies.

## connections

- [[cell]] — the fundamental unit affected by cancer; normal cells lose regulatory control.
- [[mutation]] — the driving cause of cancer through accumulation of oncogenic changes.
- [[cell-division]] — the process cancer hijacks to proliferate uncontrollably.
- [[oncogene]] — mutated genes that drive excessive cell proliferation.
- [[tumor-suppressor]] — protective genes whose loss removes brakes on cell division.
- [[apoptosis]] — programmed cell death that cancer cells evade.
- [[dna-damage]] — the initiating event triggering carcinogenic mutations.
