## summary

A **cooling rate** is the quantitative measure of temperature decrease per unit time experienced by an object, material, or thermodynamic system. Governed fundamentally by Newton's law of cooling and Fourier's law of conduction, it plays a decisive role in physical metallurgy, materials science, vulcanology, and electronic thermal management. Modulating the velocity of temperature decline dictates internal phase transitions, grain boundary microstructures, thermal stresses, and operational stability across industrial and computational platforms.

## you gotta know

- Expressed mathematically in units such as degrees Celsius per second or kelvins per second, indicating the temporal derivative of temperature over time.
- Dictated in classical heat transfer by temperature gradients, thermal conductivity, surface area, and convective heat transfer coefficients.
- Controls metallurgical phase transformations during steel quenching, where rapid cooling suppresses ductile pearlite formation to synthesize hard, brittle martensite.
- Governs igneous crystal growth in geology, where rapid volcanic quenching produces glassy obsidian while sluggish subterranean cooling yields coarse-grained granite.
- Employs continuous cooling transformation diagrams in materials engineering to plot microstructural phases formed under specified thermal trajectories.
- Dictates thermal stress gradients and potential cracking in ceramics, semiconductor wafers, and metallurgical welds subjected to uneven heat loss.
- Influenced heavily in computer hardware by fan velocity and heat sink geometry, which enhance *[[convection]]* away from a running *[[central-processing-unit]]*.

## connections

- [[convection]] — primary heat transfer mechanism through fluid movement whose relative efficiency directly determines the thermal decay curve.
- [[central-processing-unit]] — microelectronic component that requires sustained cooling rates to prevent thermal throttling and permanent silicon breakdown.
- [[control-circuit]] — automated feedback architecture that monitors temperature sensors and regulates cooling systems to stabilize thermal gradients.
- [[carbon]] — interstitial element whose migration kinetics during iron alloy cooling determine austenite, ferrite, and martensite steel phases.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical era in which metallurgical cooling control transformed steam engine manufacturing and steel production.
- [[resistor]] — electrical element generating resistive Joule heating whose equilibrium temperature depends on surrounding cooling efficiency.
