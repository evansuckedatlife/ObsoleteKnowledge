## summary

**Tyrannosaurus rex** was the largest terrestrial predator of the Late Cretaceous period, dominating North American ecosystems approximately 68 to 66 million years ago before the mass extinction event. Standing up to 4 meters tall and weighing 9 metric tons, this bipedal theropod possessed tremendous bite force and sophisticated sensory systems, making it an apex predator of remarkable capability. Fossil evidence reveals that T. rex was likely a skilled hunter with complex behavior, and its lineage represents a direct evolutionary connection to modern birds.

## you gotta know

- Tyrannosaurus rex lived during the Late Cretaceous period in what is now western North America, in environments of subtropical forests and river valleys.
- The massive skull could reach 1.5 meters in length with teeth the size of bananas; the bite force exceeded 12,000 Newtons, unmatched by any living animal.
- T. rex was bipedal—a two-legged walker—with relatively small but powerful forelimbs bearing sharp claws; the hind legs and tail provided balance and propulsion.
- Recent paleontological evidence suggests T. rex was an active hunter capable of moderate speeds (40+ km/h), contrary to the once-popular scavenging hypothesis; trackways show herding behavior.
- The sensory capabilities included excellent vision (comparable to modern eagles), sophisticated olfaction, and hearing sensitive to low frequencies for long-distance communication.
- Fossil evidence of T. rex specimens shows pathology (healed injuries, infections), suggesting they competed violently with each other and survived dangerous hunts.
- The evolutionary lineage from theropods to modern birds is direct; T. rex shares skeletal features with Archaeopteryx and contemporary birds, including a wishbone and hollow bones.
- Growth rings in bone fossils indicate T. rex reached sexual maturity around age 18-20 and lived into their late 20s or early 30s before death.
- Comparative biomechanical studies show T. rex had the highest bite force of any terrestrial animal ever measured; the feeding strategy emphasized massive crushing force to fragment bone and soft tissue.
