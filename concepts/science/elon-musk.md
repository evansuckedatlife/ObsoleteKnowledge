## summary

**Elon Musk** is a South African-born engineer, inventor, and industrialist who transformed 21st-century aerospace in the [[united-states]] by pioneering commercial orbital rocketry and reusable spaceflight architectures. As the founder and chief engineer of [[spacex]], he fundamentally challenged state-dominated aerospace paradigms inherited from the [[cold-war]] by demonstrating that recovering and reflying orbital booster stages could drastically lower the financial barrier to accessing space. Under major [[nasa]] commercial partnerships, his programs restored American domestic human spaceflight capability to the [[international-space-station]] and established private enterprise as the leading driver of deep-space exploration.

## you gotta know

- Founded the private aerospace manufacturer [[spacex]] in 2002 with the express long-term mission of enabling the colonization of Mars, arguing that humanity must establish permanent self-sustaining settlements beyond Earth to guarantee long-term survival against existential catastrophes.
- Spearheaded the design of the *Falcon 9*, the first orbital-class rocket capable of vertical takeoff and propulsive vertical landing, achieving a landmark first booster recovery at Cape Canaveral in December 2015 and proving the technological feasibility of reusable booster stages.
- Directed the development of the *Dragon* and *Crew Dragon* spacecraft under [[commercial-spaceflight]] programs with [[nasa]], making his company the first commercial entity to resupply the [[international-space-station]] in 2012 and the first to launch government astronauts into orbit during Demo-2 in 2020.
- Developed the *Falcon Heavy* launch vehicle, which debuted in 2018 by launching his cherry-red electric sports car into heliocentric orbit, and subsequently carried critical national security payloads as well as planetary science probes bound for [[jupiter]] and its icy moon [[europa]].
- Conceived the fully reusable *Starship* super heavy-lift transportation architecture, utilizing staged-combustion *Raptor* engines fueled by liquid methane and liquid [[oxygen]], which was selected by federal administrators to provide the human landing system for crewed lunar surface exploration under the Artemis successor to the [[apollo-program]].
- Championed rapid hardware prototyping and extreme vertical integration across manufacturing facilities in Texas and California, replacing traditional cost-plus aerospace contracts with iterative testing pipelines guided by real-time guidance telemetry and internal flight computer [[algorithm]] software.
- Conceived the *Mars Oasis* mission in 2001 to land a miniature greenhouse on Mars, attempting to buy converted ballistic missiles in Moscow before calculating that raw materials accounted for merely two percent of a rocket's price, inspiring him to manufacture launch vehicles directly from structural [[carbon]] composites and stainless steel.

## connections

- [[nasa]] — the national space agency that awarded critical cargo and crew contracts, enabling his private launch architectures to flourish.
- [[spacex]] — the commercial aerospace firm he founded in 2002 to pioneer reusable rocketry and interplanetary transport.
- [[falcon-rocket]] — the family of partially reusable orbital launch vehicles he directed to lower the cost of orbital delivery.
- [[dragon-spacecraft]] — the pressurized space capsule line he created to transport orbital cargo and human astronauts.
- [[starship]] — the fully reusable two-stage super heavy-lift spacecraft he designed for deep-space missions and planetary colonization.
- [[apollo-program]] — the historic lunar landing initiative whose exploration records and heavy-lift benchmarks inspired his deep-space ambitions.
- [[space-race]] — the mid-century competition that birthed national space exploration, establishing the government-dominated industry his commercial model transformed.
