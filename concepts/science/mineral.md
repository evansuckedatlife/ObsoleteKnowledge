# Mineral

## summary

**A mineral** is a naturally occurring, inorganic solid with a fixed (or limited range of) chemical composition, an orderly crystalline atomic structure, and reproducible physical properties. Minerals form the building blocks of rocks; their identity is determined by elemental composition and internal structure rather than appearance, and they represent the products of geological processes operating over millions of years.

## you gotta know

- Minerals are defined by four criteria: *natural origin* (not synthesized), *inorganic* (not produced by life, though some contain organic material), *crystalline structure* (atoms arranged in a repeating three-dimensional pattern), and *homogeneous composition* (uniform chemical makeup throughout).
- *Crystalline structure* determines most mineral properties because the geometric arrangement of atoms controls how light interacts with the crystal, how it fractures, how hard it is, and how it conducts electricity or heat.
- The same chemical element can form different minerals depending on pressure and temperature; *diamond* and *graphite* are both pure carbon but have radically different structures and hardness—diamond forms under extreme pressure, graphite at lower pressure.
- *Silicates*, containing silicon and oxygen, are by far the most abundant minerals; they comprise about 95% of Earth's crust and form most igneous rocks (like [[feldspar]] and [[quartz]]).
- Minerals are identified in the field using *physical properties*: color, luster (reflectivity), hardness (Mohs scale), cleavage (fracture along crystal planes), density, and streak (color of powdered mineral on ceramic).
- Minerals often contain *trace elements* or *substitutions* where one atom replaces another in the crystal lattice without destroying the structure; this is why minerals have a range of compositions rather than fixed formulas.
- *Ore minerals* are minerals from which metals can be economically extracted; for example, [[iron]] is extracted from hematite and magnetite.

## connections

- [[igneous-rock]] — rocks formed by crystallization of molten magma; their mineral composition depends on cooling rate and initial composition.
- [[sedimentary-rock]] — rocks formed from compressed and cemented sediments; they contain minerals inherited from source rocks.
- [[metamorphic-rock]] — rocks altered by heat and pressure; existing minerals recrystallize into new mineral assemblages stable at higher temperatures.
- [[silicate]] — the dominant mineral class, comprising most crustal rocks.
- [[quartz]] — a silicate mineral (silicon dioxide) and one of the most common minerals in Earth's crust.
