## summary

**Frequency** is the number of complete cycles or oscillations an event undergoes per unit time, measured in hertz (cycles per second). It is fundamental to understanding waves, signals, vibrations, and periodic phenomena—from sound waves to electromagnetic radiation to electrical oscillations.

## you gotta know

- Frequency is the inverse of period: if a wave completes one cycle in 0.01 seconds (period), its frequency is exactly 100 hertz (100 cycles per second).
- Higher frequencies correspond to shorter wavelengths; the relationship depends on wave speed: wavelength = speed / frequency.
- Sound waves range from roughly 20 hertz (infrasound below human hearing, felt as vibrations) to 20,000 hertz (ultrasound above human hearing, used in medical ultrasound); human speech occupies 100–8,000 hertz, with vocal cords vibrating at fundamental frequencies in this range.
- Electromagnetic radiation spans all frequencies from extremely low frequency (power lines at 50–60 hertz) to gamma rays (above 10^19 hertz).
- Frequency content of a signal can be analyzed using the Fourier transform (a mathematical operation), which decomposes any complex waveform into its constituent sine waves at different frequencies.
- Filters (electronic circuits) can selectively pass, block, or amplify signals at specific frequencies; their "cutoff frequency" determines the dividing point, separating signal from noise in audio, radio, and data transmission applications.
- In quantum mechanics, the energy of a photon particle is directly proportional to its frequency (E = hf, where h is Planck's constant).

## connections

- [[waveform]] — a periodic signal characterized by its frequency content.
- [[period]] — the inverse quantity: period = 1/frequency.
- [[signal-processing]] — relies on frequency analysis to filter, compress, and manipulate signals.
- [[fourier-series]] — decomposes complex signals into their frequency components.
- [[filter-circuit]] — selectively passes or blocks signals based on frequency.
- [[oscillation]] — any periodic motion has a well-defined frequency.
