## summary

**Matter** is any physical substance that possesses rest mass and occupies volume by taking up space. In everyday experience and foundational chemistry, it is composed of microscopic particles called [[atom|atoms]], which are themselves constructed from fundamental subatomic constituents governed by the [[standard-model]]. Understanding the behaviour, transformations, and conservation of this substance forms the bedrock of physical science, bridging classical thermodynamics with the relativistic framework of mass-energy equivalence.

## you gotta know

- Classical states of aggregation—solid, liquid, and gas—arise from kinetic thermal motion competing against intermolecular forces, alongside higher-energy states like plasma, which forms when extreme heat or radiation ionizes gaseous atoms.
- Chemical identity is determined by the [[atom]], whose macroscopic characteristics and chemical bonds are governed by the outer valence shells of the [[electron]] surrounding a compact [[nucleus]].
- The classical principle of conservation of mass, demonstrated during the chemical revolution by *Antoine Lavoisier*, established that chemical reactions cannot create or destroy substance, though modern physics unified mass and energy through the relativistic calculations of [[albert-einstein]].
- Ordinary baryonic substance comprising observable chemical elements accounts for less than five percent of the total mass-energy budget of the cosmos, with the dominant material component consisting of mysterious non-luminous *dark matter*.
- Under the taxonomy of the [[standard-model]], all true structural constituents of substance are *fermions* possessing half-integer spin—primarily the first-generation up and down [[quark]] alongside the [[electron]]—which obey Fermi-Dirac statistics.
- The spatial volume, rigidity, and stability of everyday macroscopic bodies are maintained by the *Pauli exclusion principle*, a fundamental quantum constraint preventing identical fermions from occupying identical states, which generates quantum degeneracy pressure.
- Every elementary particle of substance possesses an antimatter counterpart with identical mass but reversed quantum numbers, such as the *positron*, which releases high-frequency energy in the form of a [[photon]] pair during mutual annihilation.
- At temperatures approaching absolute zero, certain dilute atomic gases undergo a quantum phase transition into a *Bose-Einstein condensate*, causing individual atomic wavepackets to merge into a single macroscopic [[wavefunction]] displaying friction-free superfluidity.

## connections

- [[atom]] — the fundamental structural unit of chemical elements that forms molecules through covalent, ionic, and metallic interactions.
- [[electron]] — the negatively charged subatomic lepton whose spatial orbital configuration dictates the chemical properties and physical phase changes of substance.
- [[quark]] — the fundamental fractionally charged fermion that combines under the strong force to assemble protons and neutrons within the atomic core.
- [[standard-model]] — the comprehensive theoretical framework of particle physics that classifies all matter particles and their mediating force carriers.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the mathematical theory describing submicroscopic phenomena whose exclusion principles explain the stability and volume of physical structures.
- [[albert-einstein]] — the theoretical physicist who demonstrated through special relativity that mass and energy are mutually convertible aspects of the same physical quantity.
- [[photon]] — the massless electromagnetic force carrier that governs atomic binding interactions while representing pure energy rather than material substance.
