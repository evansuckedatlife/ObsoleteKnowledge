## summary

The **Inquisition** was a formal Church tribunal established to identify and punish heresy, most notably the [[roman-empire|Roman]] (or Holy Office) Inquisition founded in 1542 by the [[catholicism|Catholic]] Counter-Reformation. It became infamous in the context of the [[scientific-revolution]] for investigating scientists whose ideas—particularly heliocentrism—contradicted Church doctrine, culminating in the trial of [[galileo-galilei]] in 1633. While often exaggerated in popular accounts, the Inquisition's institutional scrutiny of scientific claims exemplified the tension between empirical inquiry and religious orthodoxy during the Renaissance and early modern period.

## you gotta know

- The Roman Inquisition was established in 1542 as a permanent papal institution to investigate doctrinal violations across [[catholic-countries|Catholic]] lands.
- [[galileo-galilei]] was tried by the Inquisition in 1633 for advocating [[heliocentrism]], a model the Church condemned as contrary to Scripture; he was placed under house arrest for the remainder of his life.
- [[copernicus]]' heliocentric theory was placed on the Church's Index of Forbidden Books in 1616, but his book remained readable by scholars with papal permission.
- The Inquisition prosecuted for heresy broadly, including challenges to Trinitarian theology and papal authority, though its involvement in suppressing science was limited compared to direct theological cases.
- Torture was technically permitted under Inquisitorial procedure but was subject to restrictions and canonical appeals, making actual use variable and less systematic than common portrayals suggest.
- The Inquisition's power derived from its authority to impose spiritual censure (excommunication) and secular penalties (imprisonment, execution), enforced through cooperation with civil authorities.
- Fear of Inquisitorial investigation may have self-censored some scientists, though many continued publishing and corresponding freely, suggesting institutional power was more effective against heretical theology than against natural philosophy.
- The tension between the Inquisition and scientific thought symbolized the broader conflict between dogmatic authority and empirical evidence that propelled the [[scientific-revolution]].

## connections

- [[galileo-galilei]] — tried and convicted by the Inquisition for defending heliocentrism.
- [[heliocentrism]] — the theory the Inquisition explicitly opposed and condemn.
- [[scientific-revolution]] — the intellectual movement that eventually superseded Inquisitorial authority over natural philosophy.
- [[copernicus]] — whose heliocentric theory was censured but whose work remained influential despite Inquisitorial prohibition.
- [[renaissance]] — the intellectual and cultural period during which the Inquisition emerged as an institutional force.
