## summary

**The Moon** is Earth's only natural satellite, orbiting approximately 384,400 kilometers away and tidally locked so that one face always points toward Earth. Formed roughly 4.5 billion years ago from the debris of a giant impact (the Giant Impact Hypothesis), the Moon profoundly influences Earth's tides and axial stability. Its exploration during the Apollo program (1969–1972) remains humanity's only crewed journey beyond Earth.

## you gotta know

- Lunar orbit period (sidereal month) is 27.3 days; the synodic month (lunar phases) is 29.5 days because Earth moves in its orbit during this time.
- Tides are primarily caused by the Moon's gravity: the near side of Earth is pulled harder than the center, creating a tidal bulge on the near side and on the far side simultaneously.
- The Moon is tidally locked: the same face always points toward Earth because its rotation period matches its orbital period—a consequence of Earth's tidal braking over billions of years.
- Lunar surface features include craters (impact sites from asteroids and meteorites), maria (dark lowland plains from ancient volcanic eruptions), and highlands (ancient raised terrain).
- Apollo 11 (1969) achieved the first crewed lunar landing when *Neil Armstrong* and *Buzz Aldrin* walked on the Moon; six crewed landings occurred between 1969 and 1972, bringing back 380 kg of lunar samples.
- The Moon lacks a significant atmosphere (the escape velocity is too low, and solar wind strips gases), so the lunar surface experiences extreme temperatures (120°C in sunlight, −170°C in darkness) and no weather.
- Lunar mass is approximately 1/81 of Earth's mass; gravity at the lunar surface is about 1/6 of Earth's, allowing astronauts to jump far higher.
- The Giant Impact Hypothesis proposes that a Mars-sized body collided with the early Earth ~4.5 billion years ago, with debris from the impact coalescing into the Moon.
