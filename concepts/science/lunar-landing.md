## summary

A **lunar landing** is the successful descent of a spacecraft to the Moon's surface, one of humanity's most ambitious engineering achievements. The first manned landing occurred on July 20, 1969, when *Apollo 11* touched down in the Sea of Tranquility, fundamentally transforming both space exploration and human self-understanding. These missions required unprecedented precision in navigation, propulsion, and life-support systems to overcome the Moon's harsh environment.

## you gotta know

- The Moon's low gravity (1/6 of Earth's) and lack of atmosphere simplify landing in some ways but require specialized hardware for power, guidance, and safe descent rates.
- *Apollo 11*'s lunar module, piloted by *Neil Armstrong*, executed a manual landing with only seconds of fuel remaining after computer guidance initially targeted a boulder-strewn crater.
- The Lunar Module used throttleable descent engines and a two-stage design, with an ascent stage to return the crew to orbit and rendezvous with the command module.
- Lunar landings required retrograde burns to shed velocity and enter a powered descent, solving the problem of landing without an atmosphere to use for braking.
- The *Apollo* missions used a two-stage lunar approach: the command module remained in orbit while the lunar module descended, then ascended to dock for the return to Earth.
- Landing sites were chosen to be relatively flat with good lighting, avoiding steep terrain and deep craters that could tip the fragile spacecraft.
- Twelve astronauts walked on the Moon between 1969 and 1972, collecting 842 pounds of samples that revolutionized planetary geology.
- The lunar module's descent stage remained on the Moon as an abandoned base, while the ascent stage fired to meet the orbiting command module.
- Deployment of experiments included seismic instruments and a laser retroreflector that allowed precise Earth-Moon distance measurements for decades.
- Landing on the Moon required solving the problem of dust adhesion, as lunar regolith clung to spacesuits and equipment, threatening seals and mechanisms.
