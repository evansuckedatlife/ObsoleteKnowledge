## summary

**Spindle fibers** are dynamic structures of microtubules that form during cell division to mechanically separate chromosomes into two daughter cells. Composed of protein filaments that extend from opposite poles of the cell, spindle fibers attach to kinetochores on chromosomes and shorten to pull genetic material apart. This elegant molecular machinery ensures accurate chromosome segregation, and errors in spindle function lead to aneuploidy and cancer.

## you gotta know

- Spindle fibers are made of tubulin dimers polymerized into microtubules, which assemble and disassemble dynamically during mitosis and meiosis.
- The spindle apparatus has two poles, each nucleated by a centrosome (containing centrioles in animal cells), from which microtubules radiate toward the cell center.
- Kinetochores are protein complexes on centromeres that attach spindle fibers to chromosomes, providing the mechanical connection for chromosome movement.
- During metaphase, chromosomes align at the cell's equator; spindle fibers then shorten, pulling sister chromatids to opposite poles during anaphase.
- Motor proteins like dynein and kinesin walk along microtubules, generating forces that position chromosomes and move poles apart during anaphase.
- Errors in spindle formation lead to missegregation, producing aneuploid cells (abnormal chromosome numbers) linked to cancer and genetic disorders.
- Microtubule-targeting drugs like colchicine and paclitaxel disrupt spindle function and are used as cancer chemotherapy agents.
- In plant cells, the spindle apparatus is organized differently without centrioles; instead, spindle pole bodies at nuclear envelope regions nucleate microtubules.
- Checkpoint proteins monitor proper spindle attachment to kinetochores; if chromosomes are not properly attached, checkpoint signals halt cell cycle progression to prevent segregation errors.
- Plus-end and minus-end dynamics of microtubules differ: plus-ends grow and shrink faster, while minus-ends are more stable; this asymmetry is crucial for spindle pole spacing and chromosome movement.
- Nonkinetochore spindle fibers, called interpolar microtubules, overlap at the cell equator and help push spindle poles apart through motor protein activity during anaphase.
