## summary

**Canadarm**, officially designated the **Shuttle Remote Manipulator System** (**SRMS**), was a mechanical robotic arm deployed on *NASA's* Space Shuttle fleet starting in 1981. Engineered in Canada by SPAR Aerospace and funded by the National Research Council, it served as a cornerstone of human orbital spaceflight for three decades. The device proved essential for deploying, capturing, and repairing uncrewed satellites as well as assembling the International Space Station.

## you gotta know

- Debuted aboard the Space Shuttle *Columbia* during the STS-2 mission in November 1981, marking Canada's premier contribution to crewed space exploration alongside the *[[united-states]]*.
- Measured 15.2 meters in length with six degrees of freedom, modeled directly after the human arm with shoulder, elbow, and wrist joints.
- Utilized a specialized end effector consisting of rotating wire snares that captured grapple fixtures mounted on payloads without requiring rigid latches.
- Controlled manually by astronauts looking through the shuttle's aft flight deck windows using translational and rotational hand controllers, interfaced through an onboard *[[central-processing-unit]]*.
- Retrieved and released the Hubble Space Telescope across multiple servicing missions, enabling crucial repairs to its primary optics and instrumentation.
- Following the 2003 *Columbia* disaster, operated the Orbiter Boom Sensor System to inspect the thermal protection tiles and reinforced carbon-carbon leading edges of the orbiter wings for micro-meteorite damage.
- Succeeded by *Canadarm2*, a larger, bidirectional walking robotic manipulator permanently based on the International Space Station.
