## summary

A **bacteriophage**, frequently abbreviated as **phage** and historically termed a **bacterial virus**, is an obligate intracellular parasite that specifically infects and replicates within bacteria and archaea. Discovered independently by English bacteriologist *Frederick Twort* in 1915 and French-Canadian microbiologist *Félix d'Hérelle* in 1917, phages represent the most abundant biological entities on the biosphere, with global populations estimated around $10^{31}$ particles. They served as the quintessential model system for early molecular biology, establishing that genetic inheritance is mediated by nucleic acids, and they remain pivotal tools in genetic engineering, ecology, and antimicrobial therapy.

## you gotta know

- Possesses a complex anatomical structure typically consisting of an icosahedral [[protein]] capsid head containing the viral genome, connected to a contractile or flexible tail assembly with specialized tail fibers that recognize bacterial outer-membrane receptors.
- Executes two principal replication pathways: the *lytic cycle*, which rapidly co-opts the host [[cell]] machinery to manufacture progeny virions before bursting the host via specialized endolysins, and the *lysogenic cycle*, where viral genetic material integrates stably into the host chromosome as a latent *prophage*.
- Served as the model system for the study of heredity in the landmark 1952 *Hershey–Chase experiment* at *Cold Spring Harbor*, where *Martha Chase* and *Alfred Hershey* used isotopic tracers to show that *T2* phage injects radioactive phosphorus-32 labeled [[dna]], rather than sulfur-35 labeled [[protein]], to reprogram host cells.
- Advanced under the auspices of the famous *Phage Group* founded by *Max Delbrück*, *Salvador Luria*, and *Alfred Hershey*, whose rigorous biophysical experiments on the *T-series* phages earned them the 1969 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
- Drove the evolutionary development of bacterial immune systems, leading directly to the discovery of restriction endonucleases that recognize foreign palindromes and the *CRISPR-Cas9* RNA-guided endonuclease system now ubiquitous in gene editing.
- Applied in biotechnology through *phage display*, a technique pioneered by *George Smith* and developed by *Sir Gregory Winter* in which foreign peptides expressed on coat proteins are screened against target ligands to synthesize therapeutic antibodies.
- Pioneered historically as *phage therapy* by *Félix d'Hérelle* and the *George Eliava Institute* in Soviet Georgia, a therapeutic modality currently experiencing a renaissance as an alternative to conventional antibiotics against multidrug-resistant pathogens.

## connections

- [[dna]] — the macromolecule demonstrated definitively to carry the genetic code through viral injection during phage infection experiments.
- [[cell]] — the bacterial host whose transcriptional machinery, ribosomes, and metabolites are commandeered to assemble progeny phage particles.
- [[protein]] — forms the structural head capsomers, internal scaffolding, tail sheath, and muralytic enzymes of the phage virion.
- [[enzyme]] — encompasses phage lysozymes and host polymerases that drive genomic replication, transcript processing, and bacterial wall lysis.
- [[evolution]] — manifest in the reciprocal arms race between host defense mechanisms like CRISPR and phage evasion adaptations like anti-CRISPR proteins.
- [[world-war-ii]] — stimulated military interest in therapeutic phage preparations to treat bacillary dysentery and wound infections among soldier encampments.
- [[united-states]] — served as the institutional home of Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, where the quantitative foundations of phage genetics were laid.
- [[backtracking]] — utilized by bioinformatic algorithms to align phage genomes and reconstruct the optimal fragmentation patterns generated by restriction digestion.
