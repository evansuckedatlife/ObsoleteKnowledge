## summary

The **cytoskeleton** is a complex, dynamic three-dimensional structural scaffolding composed of protein filaments and associated motor proteins suspended throughout the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells. Partitioned biochemically into microfilaments, intermediate filaments, and microtubules, this internal framework establishes cellular architecture, anchors organelles, and drives mechanical remodeling. Its rapid assembly and disassembly enable immune cells to engulf pathogens through phagocytosis and allow developmental tissues to undergo coordinated morphogenesis. It is fundamentally required across cell biology for directed intracellular transport, cell motility, environmental mechanosensation, and the high-fidelity segregation of chromosomes. Because cytoskeletal dysregulation underlies diseases ranging from metastatic cancer to neurodegenerative disorders, its molecular architecture remains a central focus of biomedical inquiry.

## you gotta know

- The eukaryotic cytoskeleton is organized into three major filament systems distinguished by diameter and composition: thin actin microfilaments of seven nanometers, intermediate filaments of ten nanometers, and thick, hollow microtubules measuring twenty-five nanometers.
- Microtubules are polar polymers of alpha- and beta-[[protein]] dimers that exhibit dynamic instability, alternating stochastically between GTP-cap-stabilized growth and rapid catastrophe to remodel the cell during the [[cell-cycle]].
- During cell division, microtubules assemble into the mitotic spindle, generating mechanical forces that capture kinetochores and segregate duplicated chromosomes toward opposite spindle poles organized by centrosomes and centrioles.
- Intracellular cargo transport relies on molecular motors converting ATP hydrolysis into directional motion along cytoskeletal tracks, specifically kinesins toward microtubule plus ends, dyneins toward minus ends, and myosins along actin filaments.
- Eukaryotic cellular motility is coordinated through actin polymerization pushing the plasma membrane into lamellipodia and filopodia, as well as the rhythmic beating of eukaryotic cilia and flagella driven by axonemal dynein.
- While classical eukaryotic cytoskeletons are absent in prokaryotes like [[cyanobacteria]], bacteria utilize ancestral structural homologues, such as the tubulin-like division protein FtsZ and the actin-like cell-shape protein MreB.
- Intermediate filaments, such as keratins, vimentin, and nuclear lamins, lack intrinsic structural polarity and motor proteins, functioning primarily to withstand mechanical shear stress and maintain the structural integrity of the nucleus and tissues.
- Pharmacological agents targeting cytoskeletal dynamics, such as the microtubule-stabilizing drug *paclitaxel* and the actin-disrupting fungal toxin *cytochalasin*, serve as indispensable chemotherapies and experimental probes.

## connections

- [[cell]] — the fundamental unit of biological life whose cytoplasm is organized, supported, and mechanically shaped by the cytoskeleton.
- [[protein]] — the macromolecular building blocks that polymerize into structural filaments, cross-linkers, and ATP-powered molecular motors.
- [[cyanobacteria]] — photosynthetic prokaryotes that lack eukaryotic microtubules but rely on ancestral cytoskeletal homologues like FtsZ to divide.
- [[cell-cycle]] — the tightly regulated sequence of growth and replication that coordinates the dynamic disassembly and reassembly of cytoskeletal networks.
- [[mitochondria]] — cellular organelles that are actively shuttled along microtubule tracks to areas of high metabolic demand.
- [[dna]] — the genomic blueprint whose duplicated chromosomes are partitioned during cell division by the cytoskeletal mitotic spindle.
- [[enzyme]] — biological catalysts, including ATPases like dynein and myosin, that hydrolyze nucleotide triphosphates to power cytoskeletal motility.
