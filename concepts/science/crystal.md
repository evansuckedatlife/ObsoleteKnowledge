## summary

A **crystal** (or **crystalline solid**) is a solid material whose constituent particles are arranged in a microscopic, highly ordered lattice that extends in all spatial dimensions. Crystallography emerged as a rigorous quantitative science in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries through the geometric classification of space groups and the advent of X-ray diffraction analysis. Understanding the regular packing of matter is fundamental across solid-state physics, chemistry, materials engineering, and geology, dictating macroscopic properties ranging from cleavage planes to electrical conduction.

## you gotta know

- A crystal lattice is mathematically classified into one of fourteen distinct three-dimensional space lattices known as *Bravais lattices*, which span seven fundamental crystal systems including cubic, tetragonal, and hexagonal.
- The fundamental repeating structural unit that reproduces the entire macroscopic lattice through pure spatial translation is designated the *unit cell*.
- *Max von Laue* discovered that periodic atomic planes act as three-dimensional diffraction gratings for X-rays, a breakthrough subsequently quantified by *William Henry Bragg* and *William Lawrence Bragg* via *Bragg's law* ($n\lambda = 2d\sin\theta$).
- *Miller indices*, denoted as triplets of integers $(hkl)$, specify the orientation of parallel planes of atoms with respect to the crystallographic axes of the unit cell.
- Bonding mechanisms categorize solids into ionic varieties such as table salt with *[[sodium]]*, metallic frameworks with delocalized seas of *[[electron]]* carriers, molecular lattices held by weak dispersion, and network covalent frameworks like diamond formed of *[[carbon]]*.
- Deviations from ideal periodicity, termed crystal defects, include zero-dimensional point defects like *Schottky* and *Frenkel* defects, one-dimensional edge and screw dislocations, and two-dimensional grain boundaries.
- *Quasicrystals*, discovered in 1982 by *Dan Shechtman*, possess long-range aperiodic orientational order exhibiting rotational symmetries, such as fivefold symmetry, once considered mathematically impossible for periodic crystals.

## connections

- [[covalent-bond]] — locks directional network solids like diamond, quartz, and silicon into rigid, high-melting crystalline architectures.
- [[coulomb-force]] — provides the electrostatic attraction that stabilizes ionic crystals through lattice energy and determines Madelung constants.
- [[atom]] — serves as the physical building block whose regular spatial coordinates define the nodes of every crystallographic lattice.
- [[electron]] — generates periodic Bloch wavefunctions and energy band structures as it propagates through crystalline potentials.
- [[carbon]] — forms allotropes with dramatically different crystalline geometries, ranging from tetrahedral diamond to planar hexagonal graphite.
- [[sodium]] — crystallizes with chlorine into the archetypal face-centered cubic rock-salt lattice held by electrostatic potentials.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — explains quantized lattice vibrations known as phonons and dictates electron band formation within periodic potentials.
