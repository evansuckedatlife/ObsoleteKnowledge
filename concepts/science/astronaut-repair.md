## summary

**Astronaut repair**, known formally as **on-orbit servicing**, is the specialized practice of performing extravehicular maintenance, component replacement, and structural recovery on operational spacecraft in microgravity. Pioneered by [[nasa]] during the 1970s and honed into an operational discipline throughout the [[space-shuttle-program]], orbital servicing demonstrated that complex space infrastructure could be refurbished and upgraded rather than abandoned upon hardware failure. This engineering capability transformed human spaceflight into sustained technical custodianship, most famously saving the flawed [[hubble-space-telescope]] and ensuring decades of orbital observation and discovery.

## you gotta know

- *STS-61* in December 1993 resolved the crippling spherical aberration of the [[hubble-space-telescope]], when spacewalkers installed the Corrective Optics Space Telescope Axial Replacement (*COSTAR*) optical module and the Wide Field and Planetary Camera 2 (*WFPC2*) during five record-setting extravehicular activities.
- The final Hubble servicing mission, *STS-125* in 2009, pushed orbital mechanics and tool development to unprecedented limits by conducting component-level circuit board repairs inside the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (*STIS*) and the Advanced Camera for Surveys (*ACS*), instruments never designed to be opened in vacuum.
- The [[united-states]] space station program proved the viability of emergency orbital salvage in 1973 during *Skylab 2*, when astronauts *Pete Conrad* and *Joseph Kerwin* deployed an improvised parasol sunshade and cut tangled debris to free a jammed solar array, saving the habitat from fatal overheating.
- In 1984, *STS-41-C* executed the first satellite rendezvous, capture, repair, and redeployment in history when astronaut *George Nelson* flew untethered using the Manned Maneuvering Unit (*MMU*) before *Challenger* berthed the defective *Solar Maximum Mission* observatory in its payload bay for electronics replacement.
- During *STS-120* in 2007, astronaut *Scott Parazynski* conducted a perilous emergency repair on the International Space Station, riding an extended inspection boom to the extreme edge of the outpost to sew together a ripped, live high-voltage solar wing using improvised wire stabilizers nicknamed *cufflinks*.
- Commercial satellite recovery reached its peak during *STS-51-A* in 1984, when astronauts *Joseph Allen* and *Dale Gardner* manually wrangled two stranded communication satellites—*Palapa B2* and *Westar VI*—using a specialized *Stinger* docking probe, securing them in *Discovery* for return to Earth.
- Mission success requires grueling simulation in facilities like the Neutral Buoyancy Laboratory (*NBL*), where crews spend hundreds of submerged hours practicing with specialized microgravity hand tools, computer-controlled pistol-grip screwdrivers, and portable foot restraints anchored to the robotic *Canadarm*.

## connections

- [[nasa]] — the government agency that pioneered extravehicular maintenance protocols, orbital tool design, and neutral buoyancy simulation.
- [[hubble-space-telescope]] — the flagship optical observatory whose flawed primary mirror inspired the most ambitious modular servicing missions in history.
- [[space-shuttle-program]] — the reusable space transportation system whose expansive cargo bay, robotic arm, and airlock served as a mobile orbital shipyard.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose aerospace funding, engineering research, and specialized astronaut corps executed every crewed satellite servicing expedition.
- [[apollo-program]] — the lunar initiative whose pressure suit engineering, life support systems, and mission control frameworks established baseline spacewalking techniques.
- [[space-race]] — the geopolitical contest between superpowers that catalyzed the earliest extravehicular activities and accelerated rapid orbital capability development.
- [[cold-war]] — the ideological standoff that drove massive national investments into reusable shuttle architecture and satellite reconnaissance infrastructure.
