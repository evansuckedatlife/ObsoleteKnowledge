## summary

The **water cycle**, also designated the **hydrologic cycle**, describes the continuous, biogeochemical movement and phase changes of water across Earth's hydrosphere, atmosphere, lithosphere, and biosphere. Driven primarily by incoming solar radiation from *[[photon]]* flux and governed by planetary gravity, the cycle redistributes heat energy and freshwater across the globe. It serves as the primary mechanism regulating terrestrial *[[climate]]*, maintaining ecological biomes, and driving global *[[weather]]* phenomena from gentle morning condensation to violent atmospheric disturbances.

## you gotta know

- Powered primarily by solar insolation, which heats surface water to drive evaporation, and gravitational pull, which causes condensed droplets to fall back to the surface as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.
- Plant transpiration releases vast quantities of water vapor into the troposphere through leaf stomata, an active biological flux that couples moisture exchange directly with carbon fixation during *[[photosynthesis]]*.
- Condensation in the atmosphere cannot occur efficiently in pure air; water vapor requires microscopic suspended particles known as *cloud condensation nuclei* (*CCN*), such as sea salt and mineral dust, to initiate droplet coalescence.
- Oceans contain more than ninety-six percent of Earth's total water reserves, whereas freshwater accounts for barely three percent, the overwhelming majority of which remains sequestered in polar ice sheets or trapped in subterranean aquifers.
- Extreme meteorological phenomena such as the *[[tropical-cyclone]]* and *[[hurricane]]* act as planetary heat engines, drawing enormous energy through the evaporation of warm tropical seas and depositing immense rainfall inland.
- Sublimation describes the direct phase change from solid ice to atmospheric vapor without an intermediate liquid state, occurring rapidly in arid, high-altitude environments where low *[[atmospheric-pressure]]* and bright sunlight coincide.
- Atmospheric residence time for a typical water molecule averages approximately nine days, in stark contrast to deep groundwater reservoirs, permafrost, and glacial ice where water can remain isolated for thousands of years.
- Advection facilitates the horizontal transport of moisture-laden air masses by prevailing *[[wind]]* belts, carrying oceanic vapor over landmasses to replenish continental river basins and lakes.

## connections

- [[photosynthesis]] — biological process wherein terrestrial plants draw liquid water from soil and return it to the atmosphere via foliar transpiration.
- [[oxygen]] — fundamental elemental component of water molecules that cycles continuously between the hydrosphere, lithosphere, and atmosphere.
- [[tropical-cyclone]] — vast cyclonic storm systems that transfer enormous quantities of evaporated oceanic water into high-intensity rainfall.
- [[climate]] — long-term planetary weather regime sustained and moderated by the global redistribution of latent heat and precipitation.
- [[weather]] — day-to-day atmospheric conditions of humidity, cloud cover, and precipitation governed by local thermodynamic shifts.
- [[photon]] — solar electromagnetic energy packet whose absorption provides the latent heat necessary to convert liquid water into vapor.
- [[carbon]] — interlocking global cycle in which rainwater dissolves carbon dioxide and drives chemical weathering of continental rocks.
