## summary

A **neurotransmitter** is an endogenous signaling molecule synthesized by a neuron, concentrated into secretory vesicles, and released across the synaptic cleft to transmit physiological instructions to a target [[cell]]. First demonstrated in 1921 by Austrian pharmacologist *Otto Loewi* using dual-perfused frog hearts, chemical transmission represents the foundational mechanism governing neural network computation, motor control, sensory processing, and systemic homeostasis. These diverse chemical messengers are essential to modern neuroscience and medicine because defects in their synthesis, packaging, receptor engagement, or reuptake underlie severe neurological conditions ranging from *Parkinson's disease* and *depression* to *myasthenia gravis*.

## you gotta know

- Regulated exocytosis begins when an electrical action potential invades the presynaptic terminal, triggering the opening of voltage-gated calcium channels whose localized cation influx drives synaptic vesicles to dock and fuse with the active zone membrane.
- Intracellular vesicle trafficking and membrane fusion are orchestrated by the core *SNARE* [[protein]] complex—consisting of vesicle-bound *synaptobrevin* and plasma-membrane *syntaxin-1* and *SNAP-25*—coupled with the calcium-sensing trigger *synaptotagmin*.
- Postsynaptic receptor superfamilies divide functionally into rapid ligand-gated ion channels (*ionotropic* receptors) that alter membrane permeability to cations like [[sodium]] or anions like chloride, and slower G-protein-coupled receptors (*metabotropic* receptors) that initiate second-messenger biochemical cascades.
- The mammalian central nervous system relies primarily on *L-glutamate* for fast excitatory neurotransmission and on *gamma-aminobutyric acid* (*GABA*) and *glycine* for hyperpolarizing inhibitory neurotransmission.
- Signal termination requires rapid clearance from the synaptic cleft, achieved either through active symporter proteins that drive reuptake into neurons and glial cells or through direct enzymatic degradation by a dedicated [[enzyme]] such as *acetylcholinesterase*.
- Potent bacterial neurotoxins target this pathway with surgical specificity: *tetanus toxin* prevents inhibitory transmitter release to cause spastic paralysis, whereas *botulinum toxin* proteolytically cleaves presynaptic *SNARE* proteins to block *acetylcholine* exocytosis at the neuromuscular junction.
- *Otto Loewi* proved the chemical nature of synaptic signaling in his classic 1921 experiment by electrically stimulating the vagus nerve of an isolated frog heart and transferring the perfusion saline to a second heart, thereby identifying *Vagusstoff*.

## connections

- [[cell]] — targeted chemical signaling allows an individual presynaptic neuron to communicate with an adjacent effector or postsynaptic recipient cell across the synaptic cleft.
- [[protein]] — structural and functional macromolecules constitute the vesicular transport pumps, *SNARE* docking complexes, postsynaptic receptors, and ion channels necessary for neurotransmission.
- [[sodium]] — flux of this abundant extracellular cation through ionotropic receptor pores drives post-synaptic membrane depolarization and triggers downstream action potentials.
- [[enzyme]] — specialized catalytic proteins govern the metabolic pathways of neurotransmitter biosynthesis and catalyze rapid synaptic degradation to reset signaling circuits.
- [[alcohol]] — acts as a central nervous system depressant by potentiation of inhibitory *GABA* receptor channels and concurrent inhibition of excitatory *NMDA* glutamate receptors.
- [[carbon]] — provides the stable tetrahedral framework forming the molecular backbone of classical amino acid, monoamine, catecholamine, and neuropeptide messengers.
- [[dna]] — stores the genetic sequences that transcribe and translate peptide neurotransmitter precursors and the enzymes responsible for low-molecular-weight transmitter synthesis.
