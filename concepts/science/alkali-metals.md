## summary

The **alkali metals** comprise the family of highly reactive, soft, silvery chemical elements occupying Group 1 of the periodic table: lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, cesium, and radioactive francium. Possessing a single loosely bound valence electron in their outermost spherical *s*-orbital, each **alkali metal** exhibits the lowest first ionization energies and largest atomic radii in its respective period. Because they react vigorously and exothermically with water to produce caustic basic hydroxides and flammable hydrogen gas, they never occur as free native elements in nature and must be isolated using industrial electrolysis of molten salts.

## you gotta know

- Comprise the univalent elements of periodic Group 1, possessing a characteristic valence electron configuration of *ns¹* that dictates their exceptional chemical reactivity.
- React vigorously and violently with water to form strongly basic hydroxide solutions and flammable hydrogen gas, yielding explosions that escalate in violence descending the group.
- Exhibit distinct, vibrant flame test colors: lithium burns crimson red, sodium displays intense yellow-orange, potassium burns lilac, rubidium yields red-violet, and cesium burns sky blue.
- Characterized by exceptionally low melting points, low densities, and extreme malleability, with lithium, sodium, and potassium being light enough to float on water before reacting.
- Isolated historically by British chemist *Humphry Davy*, who harnessed electric battery currents in 1807 to prepare metallic sodium and potassium from caustic lye.
- React directly with atmospheric oxygen to yield normal oxides with lithium, peroxides with sodium, and stable superoxides with potassium, rubidium, and cesium.
- Cesium serves as the International System of Units standard for the second, defined precisely by the microwave transition frequency between the hyperfine levels of its ground state.

## connections

- [[sodium]] — ubiquitous Group 1 element essential for cellular electrolyte homeostasis, action potential propagation, and industrial salt chemistry.
- [[atom]] — fundamental units of matter whose nucleus-to-shell charge ratios determine the low electronegativity and low ionization energy of Group 1 elements.
- [[electron]] — subatomic leptons whose lone outermost *s*-orbital configuration governs the univalent chemistry and intense reducing power of this group.
- [[oxygen]] — chalcogen element that rapidly tarnishes fresh metallic surfaces by forming oxides, peroxides, and superoxides upon air exposure.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — theoretical physics framework that explains atomic orbital shielding, valence electron delocalization, and the hyperfine transitions utilized in cesium atomic clocks.
- [[alkaloid]] — class of basic organic nitrogenous compounds historically named after the caustic, alkaline solutions produced by wood ash containing Group 1 carbonates.
