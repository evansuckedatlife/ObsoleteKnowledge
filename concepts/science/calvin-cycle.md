## summary

The **Calvin cycle**, also known as the **Calvin-Benson-Bassham cycle**, the **C3 cycle**, or the **light-independent reactions**, is the cyclical biochemical pathway by which photosynthetic organisms fix atmospheric *carbon dioxide* into organic sugars. Taking place within the stroma of chloroplasts in a plant or algal [[cell]], this pathway utilizes the chemical energy stored in adenosine triphosphate (*ATP*) and the reducing power of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (*NADPH*) generated during earlier light-dependent stages. It represents the primary biological mechanism responsible for entry of inorganic [[carbon]] into the planetary food web, sustaining nearly all terrestrial and aquatic life.

## you gotta know

- Takes place in the stroma of chloroplasts, proceeding via three distinct enzymatic stages: carbon fixation, reduction of phosphoglycerate, and regeneration of ribulose bisphosphate.
- Catalyzed initially by the [[enzyme]] ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase (*RuBisCO*), widely regarded as the most abundant [[protein]] on Earth.
- Captures inorganic [[carbon]] from atmospheric *carbon dioxide* and attaches it to the five-carbon acceptor molecule ribulose 1,5-bisphosphate (*RuBP*).
- Yields an unstable six-carbon intermediate that immediately cleaves into two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate (*3-PGA*), defining standard C3 [[photosynthesis]].
- Converts 3-phosphoglycerate into glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (*G3P*) using *ATP* and *NADPH*, with one net triose sugar exported for every three complete turns.
- Competes directly with photorespiration when *RuBisCO* erroneously fixes diatomic [[oxygen]] instead of *carbon dioxide*, leading to metabolic inefficiency under hot and arid conditions.
- Elucidated by *Melvin Calvin*, *James Bassham*, and *Andrew Benson* at the University of California, Berkeley using radioactive carbon-14 tracing and two-dimensional paper chromatography.

## connections

- [[photosynthesis]] — provides the chemical energy carriers *ATP* and *NADPH* that drive light-independent carbohydrate synthesis.
- [[enzyme]] — constitutes the catalytic machinery of the pathway, including *RuBisCO*, kinase, and isomerase complexes.
- [[carbon]] — forms the structural backbone of the atmospheric substrate fixed into high-energy organic triose sugars.
- [[oxygen]] — acts as an alternative substrate for *RuBisCO*, triggering the wasteful metabolic pathway of photorespiration.
- [[cell]] — provides the specialized chloroplast stroma compartment required for cycle intermediates and enzymes to interact.
- [[protein]] — comprises the structural and catalytic units of *RuBisCO* and associated stromal regulatory enzymes.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — produces the essential subunits of stromal enzymes, coded partly by chloroplastic and nuclear genomes.
