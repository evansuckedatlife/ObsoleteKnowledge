## summary

A **volcano** is a geological opening in Earth's crust through which molten rock (magma), ash, gases, and rock fragments erupt onto the surface. Volcanoes are windows into Earth's interior and drivers of planetary geology and climate. They range from quietly effusive shield volcanoes to catastrophically explosive stratovolcanoes, and their distribution marks the boundaries of tectonic plates and regions of crustal instability.

## you gotta know

- Volcanoes form at three main tectonic settings: divergent plate boundaries (mid-ocean ridges), convergent boundaries (subduction zones), and hotspots (intraplate volcanism).
- Extrusive [[igneous-rock|igneous rocks]]—basalt, andesite, rhyolite—form when lava cools rapidly at the surface.
- Shield volcanoes (like those in Hawaii) erupt fluid lava and build broad, gentle slopes; stratovolcanoes (like Mount Fuji and Mount Vesuvius) explode violently and form steep cones.
- Volcanic eruptions inject ash and sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere, temporarily cooling the planet by reflecting sunlight.
- Historic eruptions (Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD, Mount Krakatoa in 1883) have shaped human civilization through destruction and cultural memory.
- Volcanoes on other bodies (Jupiter's [[io]], Saturn's Enceladus) exhibit [[cryovolcanism]]—eruptions of water ice and organic compounds instead of silicate magma.
- Volcanic soils are extraordinarily fertile; regions like [[italy]] and [[mount-fuji]]'s base support dense populations despite eruption risk.
- Lava composition (silica content) determines eruption style: low-silica basalt flows gently, while high-silica rhyolite traps gases and explodes catastrophically.
- Volcanic hazards include lava flows, ash clouds, lahars (mudflows), pyroclastic flows, and toxic gas emissions that can travel miles downwind.
- Dormancy does not guarantee safety; many "extinct" volcanoes have reawakened after centuries or millennia.

## connections

- [[magma]] — the molten rock that powers volcanic eruptions.
- [[igneous-rock]] — the solid products of volcanic activity; defines the extrusive category.
- [[plate-tectonics]] — the tectonic framework explaining where and why volcanoes occur.
- [[italy]] — home to Vesuvius, Etna, and Stromboli; volcanoes central to Italian geology and history.
- [[mount-fuji]] — Japan's iconic dormant stratovolcano, sacred and geologically instructive.
- [[mineral]] — volcanic rocks and their constituent minerals shaped by high-temperature crystallization.
- [[cryovolcanism]] — volcanic phenomena on icy bodies, extending volcanism beyond rocky planets.
