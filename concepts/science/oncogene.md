## summary

An **oncogene** is a mutated or abnormally overexpressed gene whose altered product drives excessive, unregulated [[cell]] proliferation and promotes malignant transformation. In healthy tissues, its unmutated precursor, termed a **proto-oncogene**, encodes an essential [[protein]] that orchestrates physiological processes including signal transduction, [[cell-division]], and survival. When activated by a point [[mutation]], chromosomal translocation, or gene amplification, it functions as a dominant driver of cancer that actively subverts the inhibitory checkpoints maintained by a [[tumor-suppressor]]. Identifying these abnormal genetic switches transformed oncology from broad histological description into precision medicine targeted at specific aberrant biochemical pathways.

## you gotta know

- Arise through gain-of-function alterations to a proto-oncogene, meaning that a single altered allele is typically sufficient to confer a proliferative advantage in a dominant manner, contrasting with the recessive two-hit inactivation typical of a [[tumor-suppressor]].
- First discovered through study of the *Rous sarcoma virus* by *J. Michael Bishop* and *Harold Varmus*, who demonstrated that viral transforming genes (*v-src*) represent hijacked and mutated copies of normal cellular genes (*c-src*), winning the 1989 *Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine*.
- *RAS* family genes (*KRAS*, *NRAS*, and *HRAS*) encode membrane-associated GTP-binding switches and represent the most frequently mutated drivers across human cancers, permanently locking the [[protein]] in an active configuration that continually fires downstream growth signals.
- Chromosomal translocations generate oncogenic fusion proteins, most famously the *Philadelphia chromosome* (*t(9;22)*) that fuses *BCR* to *ABL1*, producing a constitutively active tyrosine kinase driver in chronic myeloid leukemia.
- Amplification or activating mutation of receptor tyrosine kinases such as *HER2* (*ERBB2*) and *EGFR* causes ligand-independent dimerization and signaling, triggering runaway [[protein-synthesis]] and cellular motility.
- Transcription factor drivers like *MYC* reprogram cellular metabolism, accelerate the cell cycle, and block differentiation by directly binding enhancer box sequences and remodeling chromatin structure within the [[nucleus]].
- Evasion of programmed cell death frequently involves overexpression of *BCL2*, an anti-apoptotic factor that prevents mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization and preserves cell viability despite extensive [[dna-damage]].
- Modern targeted cancer therapeutics selectively inhibit oncogenic products, exemplified by small-molecule kinase inhibitors such as *imatinib* and monoclonal antibodies such as *trastuzumab*, rather than relying solely on non-specific cytotoxic chemotherapy.

## connections

- [[cell]] — provides the fundamental biological unit where unregulated proliferation, metabolic reprogramming, and malignant transformation occur.
- [[dna]] — contains the genetic code vulnerable to base substitutions, amplifications, and structural rearrangements that produce oncogenic drivers.
- [[protein]] — encompasses the functional kinases, growth factor receptors, and transcription factors produced by these mutated genes.
- [[tumor-suppressor]] — acts as the essential genomic counterweight whose loss of function cooperates with activated oncogenes to establish malignancy.
- [[mutation]] — provides the initiating gain-of-function change that converts a tightly regulated regulatory sequence into a constitutively active driver.
- [[nucleus]] — houses the chromosomes bearing these loci and serves as the destination where oncogenic transcription factors alter global gene expression.
- [[enzyme]] — represents the primary catalytic class of tyrosine and serine/threonine kinases that are druggable with targeted small-molecule inhibitors.
