## summary

**Atomic structure** refers to the internal organization of atoms—the arrangement of protons, neutrons, and electrons that explains atomic properties, chemical bonding, and the behavior of matter. The evolution from plum-pudding models to nuclear atoms to quantum mechanical orbitals represents one of science's most dramatic conceptual revolutions, anchored by experiments like Rutherford's gold-foil test and the later discovery of electron shells.

## you gotta know

- The *nucleus* (discovered by Rutherford) is a tiny, dense core containing protons and neutrons; the atom is mostly empty space.
- *Electrons* orbit in energy shells or orbitals, not classical planetary orbits; their probability clouds explain chemical bonding and reactivity.
- *Protons* determine an element's identity (atomic number); *neutrons* vary to create isotopes of the same element with identical chemistry but different mass and stability.
- The *Bohr model* (1913) explained hydrogen's spectral lines by quantizing electron orbitals; it was refined by quantum mechanics to today's orbital model.
- *Nuclear forces* (strong and weak) hold the nucleus together, overcoming electromagnetic repulsion between protons; this balance determines nuclear stability and radioactivity.
- Elements heavier than iron cannot be created by fusion in stars; they require supernova or neutron-star collisions, making atomic structure the key to cosmic nucleosynthesis.
- *Isotopes* differ in neutron count; carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes with different mass numbers. Atomic mass represents a weighted average of all natural isotopes, while mass number counts total protons and neutrons in a specific nucleus.
- The discovery of the nucleus was revolutionary: Rutherford's team bombarded thin gold foil with alpha particles and observed that most passed through, but a small fraction bounced backward—revealing a concentrated core of positive charge at the atom's center.
- Atomic structure explains chemistry's *octet rule*: atoms gain, lose, or share electrons to achieve stable electron configurations (often eight electrons in the outermost shell), driving all chemical bonding.

## connections

- [[nucleus]] — the positively charged core at the atom's center, revealed by Rutherford's experiment.
- [[electron]] — the negatively charged particle in orbitals, responsible for chemical properties and bonding.
- [[bohr-model]] — the early quantum model explaining hydrogen's spectrum and orbital quantization.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the framework that replaced classical orbital models with probability waves and orbitals.
- [[alpha-particle]] — helium nuclei used by Rutherford to probe atomic structure.
- [[proton]] — the positively charged nucleon that defines an element's identity.
- [[neutron]] — the neutral nucleon in nuclei, crucial for nuclear stability and isotope variation.
