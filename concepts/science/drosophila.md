## summary

**Drosophila** (or *Drosophila melanogaster*, the common fruit fly) is a small insect that became the most important model organism in genetics. Its short life cycle, simple genetics, and ease of breeding made it ideal for studying inheritance, mutations, and chromosome behavior in the early twentieth century.

## you gotta know

- Tiny insect (3mm long) with a two-week life cycle and massive genetic output—females produce hundreds of offspring.
- *Thomas Hunt Morgan* chose Drosophila in 1908 for experiments on inheritance; mutations appeared spontaneously, providing natural variation.
- Red eye color is normal (wild-type); white eye mutant appeared spontaneously and allowed tracking of inheritance patterns.
- Drosophila has only four pairs of chromosomes, with clear morphological differences that made chromosome tracking visible under microscopes.
- Morgan's experiments linked specific traits to specific chromosomes, providing experimental proof of the chromosome theory of inheritance; he mapped the relative positions of genes on chromosomes, establishing the first genetic maps.
- The fruit fly genome contains roughly 13,600 genes in 165 million base pairs—much smaller and simpler than human DNA but remarkably similar; about 75% of human disease genes have functional homologs in Drosophila, making it invaluable for studying human genetic disorders.
- Continues to be used in developmental biology and genetics research today; entire genome sequenced in 1999; developmental genes controlling body segmentation (Hox genes) discovered in Drosophila have homologs throughout the animal kingdom.
