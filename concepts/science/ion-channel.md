## summary

An **ion channel** is a pore-forming transmembrane [[protein]] assembly that facilitates the rapid, passive transport of specific inorganic ions across biological membranes along their electrochemical gradients. Embedded within the hydrophobic lipid bilayers of every biological [[cell]], these macromolecular complexes switch between distinct conducting and non-conducting conformations in response to physiological cues. Their gating activities establish resting membrane potentials, propagate electrical signals in excitable tissues, regulate cellular volume, and initiate biochemical cascades that trigger [[muscle-contraction]]. Discovered to be central to cellular electrophysiology, they serve as primary drug targets and represent a foundational pillar of biophysics and neurobiology.

## you gotta know

- Recorded at single-channel resolution using the *patch-clamp technique*, an electrophysiological milestone developed by *Erwin Neher* and *Bert Sakmann* that demonstrated discrete open and closed states, earning them the 1991 *Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine*.
- Underlie the generation and propagation of action potentials in nerves, mathematically modeled in the giant axon of the Atlantic squid by *Alan Hodgkin* and *Andrew Huxley* using voltage-dependent conductances for [[sodium]] and [[potassium]].
- Categorized by gating stimulus into voltage-gated channels activated by membrane potential shifts, ligand-gated channels stimulated by chemical binding like an external [[neurotransmitter]], and mechanically-gated *Piezo* channels activated by physical tension.
- Structural architecture and ion selectivity were resolved at atomic scale by *Roderick MacKinnon*, whose 2003 *Nobel Prize in Chemistry*-winning crystal structure of the bacterial *KcsA* channel revealed a carbonyl oxygen selectivity filter that mimics hydration shells.
- Blocked or modulated by targeted biological poisons, notably *tetrodotoxin* from pufferfish and *saxitoxin* from dinoflagellates occluding sodium pores, *dendrotoxin* from mambas inhibiting potassium flux, and *conotoxin* from predatory cone snails paralyzing calcium conduits.
- Inherited dysfunction produces clinical disorders termed *channelopathies*, which include *cystic fibrosis* from lesions in *CFTR*, episodic *periodic paralysis*, neurological conditions like *Dravet syndrome*, and life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias such as *long QT syndrome*.
- Inactivation in voltage-sensitive pores frequently follows the *ball-and-chain model* proposed by *Clay Armstrong*, wherein an amino-terminal cytoplasmic peptide domain physically swings into and plugs the internal vestibule shortly after opening.

## connections

- [[sodium]] — the primary extracellular cation whose rapid inward current through voltage-gated channels initiates cellular depolarization.
- [[potassium]] — the primary intracellular cation whose outward flow through selective tetrameric channels terminates action potentials and sets resting potential.
- [[protein]] — the macromolecular family forming the multi-pass alpha-helical structures that assemble around an aqueous transmembrane pore.
- [[cell]] — the fundamental membrane-enclosed unit whose internal volume and electrochemical potential are regulated by pore permeability.
- [[muscle-contraction]] — physiological excitation-contraction coupling initiated when voltage sensors trigger intracellular channels to flood the cytoplasm with calcium.
- [[neurotransmitter]] — chemical signaling ligands such as acetylcholine and glutamate that bind ionotropic receptor channels to drive fast synaptic transmission.
- [[enzyme]] — active molecular catalysts such as the sodium-potassium ATPase pump that burn metabolic energy to rebuild ion gradients dissipating through channels.
- [[resistor]] — the electrical circuit element corresponding to channel conductance in classic biophysical equivalent-circuit models of cell membranes.
