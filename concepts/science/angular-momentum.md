## summary

**Angular momentum** is the rotational analog of linear momentum—a conserved quantity measuring how much an object or system is rotating and how resistant it is to changes in rotation. In classical mechanics, it is the product of moment of inertia and angular velocity; in quantum mechanics, it is quantized into discrete levels. Angular momentum includes both orbital angular momentum (from orbiting) and spin angular momentum (intrinsic rotation).

## you gotta know

- In quantum mechanics, angular momentum is quantized: an electron's orbital angular momentum can take only specific values determined by quantum numbers.
- Spin is a form of angular momentum without an orbiting motion—an intrinsic quantum property of particles like electrons.
- The Stern-Gerlach experiment demonstrated the quantization of angular momentum by showing discrete spin states along a magnetic field.
- Conserved in isolated systems with no external torques, making it fundamental to planetary orbits, atomic structure, and particle physics.
- Determined by quantum numbers: the orbital angular momentum quantum number *l* and the magnetic quantum number *m_l* constrain possible values.
- Precesses around a magnetic field, a phenomenon exploited in nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR).
- Commutators of angular momentum operators do not all equal zero, reflecting the uncertainty principle for different components of rotation.
- Coupling of orbital and spin angular momentum in atoms produces fine structure in spectral lines, observable through high-resolution spectroscopy.
- Total angular momentum (orbital + spin) determines atomic and nuclear energy levels, influencing chemical properties and nuclear stability.
