## summary

A **base** is a chemical species that yields hydroxide ions in aqueous solution, accepts protons from an acid, or donates an electron pair to form a coordinate covalent bond. Exhibiting a pH greater than 7 at standard conditions, basic substances are historically characterized by a bitter flavor, a slippery texture, and the ability to restore blue pigmentation to red litmus paper. In biological chemistry and chemical synthesis, bases play an indispensable role in maintaining physiological buffering, catalyzing organic rearrangements, and providing electrolytic media for energy storage.

## you gotta know

- Three canonical historical definitions: Svante Arrhenius defined a base as a compound producing hydroxide ions in water; Johannes Brønsted and Thomas Lowry defined it as a proton acceptor; Gilbert N. Lewis generalized it as an [[electron]]-pair donor.
- Quantified by the base dissociation constant ($K_b$), with aqueous solutions exhibiting a pH greater than 7 at 25 °C due to low hydronium concentration and elevated hydroxide concentration on a negative logarithmic scale.
- Strong bases like [[sodium]] hydroxide (caustic soda) and potassium hydroxide dissociate completely in aqueous solution, whereas weak bases such as ammonia and alkylamines establish dynamic equilibria with partial ionization.
- Neutralization reactions occur when a base combines with an [[acid]] to produce an ionic salt and neutral water, an exergonic reaction utilized universally in quantitative acid-base titrations.
- Saponification relies on the nucleophilic alkaline cleavage of ester linkages in triglycerides using strong bases like [[sodium]] hydroxide to yield glycerol and fatty acid soap salts.
- Large-scale chemical manufacture of industrial alkalis, particularly [[sodium]] carbonate through the Leblanc and Solvay processes, formed a foundational chemical pillar of the European [[industrial-revolution]].
- Aqueous potassium hydroxide acts as the standard alkaline electrolyte in commercial [[battery]] architectures, providing high ionic conductivity without accelerating zinc electrode corrosion.
- Nitrogenous bases belonging to purine and pyrimidine families form the core genetic alphabet whose complementary hydrogen bonding stabilizes the macromolecular double helix of [[dna]].

## connections

- [[acid]] — chemical counterpart that neutralizes bases by supplying protons or accepting electron pairs.
- [[battery]] — electrochemical power source frequently employing concentrated basic electrolytes to maximize ionic transport.
- [[sodium]] — alkali metal that serves as the cationic base for caustic soda and soda ash in chemical industry.
- [[electron]] — elementary particle whose lone-pair donation characterizes coordinate bonding in Lewis acid-base theory.
- [[dna]] — double-stranded genetic molecule whose structural stability and sequence depend on heterocyclic nitrogenous bases.
- [[enzyme]] — macromolecular biological catalyst whose tertiary structure and catalytic cleft require strict pH regulation.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical epoch during which mass manufacturing of synthetic alkalis transformed glass and textile industries.
