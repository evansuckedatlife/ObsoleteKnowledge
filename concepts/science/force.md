## summary

A **force** is a push or pull that changes or tends to change an object's state of motion. Quantified in newtons, forces are central to physics and arise from four fundamental interactions: gravity, electromagnetism, strong nuclear force, and weak nuclear force. Understanding forces is essential to predicting motion and interactions in the universe.

## you gotta know

- *Newton's Second Law* defines force mathematically: force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma); a net force on an object causes acceleration.
- Four fundamental forces govern all known interactions: gravity (long-range, weakest, mediates celestial mechanics), electromagnetic (binds atoms and molecules, dominates chemistry), strong nuclear (binds nucleons, dominates atomic nuclei), weak nuclear (governs radioactive decay, enables beta particles).
- Gravity is the attraction between masses; its strength depends on the masses and distance between them (inverse-square law).
- Electromagnetic force comes from interaction between electric charges; it dominates chemistry, binding electrons to nuclei and atoms to each other.
- Forces are vectors with magnitude and direction; multiple forces on an object sum (vectorially) to a net force that determines acceleration.
- Contact forces (like friction and normal force) arise from electromagnetic repulsion; field forces (like gravity and electromagnetism) act over distance without contact but follow the same fundamental physical laws.
- At the scale of atoms and nuclei, quantum mechanics modifies how forces behave; at cosmic scales, general relativity describes gravity as spacetime curvature.
