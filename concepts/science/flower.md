## summary

A **flower** is the reproductive structure of flowering plants ([[angiosperm|angiosperms]]), containing the organs for sexual reproduction. It is also the most visually striking adaptation in plant evolution—the conspicuous petals, colors, and fragrances evolved to attract animal [[pollinator|pollinators]] that transfer pollen between plants.

## you gotta know

- Flowers evolved in flowering plants roughly 130 million years ago during the Early Cretaceous and became the dominant land plants by the end of the Cretaceous, replacing conifers and ferns as the primary vegetation.
- The flower's main reproductive parts are the stamen (male organ, producing pollen) and the pistil (female organ, producing eggs).
- Petals evolved from modified leaves and are typically bright, reflective, or iridescent to attract insects, birds, or mammals; many flowers also produce sweet nectar as a food reward for pollinators in exchange for pollen transfer.
- Pollen grains, produced in the stamen, must reach the stigma (tip of the pistil) for fertilization; this can occur through wind, animal vectors, or water.
- After pollination and fertilization, the ovary develops into a fruit that protects seeds and aids their dispersal.
- Flower shape, color, and scent are often co-evolved with specific pollinators: bee-flowers are typically blue or yellow with landing platforms; hummingbird-flowers are red and tubular.
- Flowering time is often triggered by day length (photoperiodism) or temperature cues (vernalization), ensuring flowers bloom at the optimal season when pollinators are active.

## connections

- [[angiosperm]] — the plant group that produces flowers; flowers are their defining feature.
- [[pollination]] — the process occurring at flowers when pollen is transferred between plants.
- [[pollen]] — the male gamete produced in flower stamens.
- [[plant-reproduction]] — the biological process flowers enable and facilitate.
- [[seed]] — develops from the flower's ovule after fertilization.
- [[fruit]] — the mature ovary protecting seeds; evolved alongside flowers.
