# Wind Speed

## summary

**Wind speed** quantifies the velocity of air movement, typically measured in meters per second (m/s), kilometers per hour (km/h), or miles per hour (mph). Wind arises from pressure differences in the atmosphere caused by differential solar heating; its measurement and characterization are fundamental to meteorology, weather prediction, and the design of structures and wind-energy systems. Standardized scales like the Beaufort Wind Force Scale provide qualitative descriptions linking measured wind speeds to observable effects.

## you gotta know

- Wind speed is measured using an *anemometer*, a device with three or four cups that rotate in the wind; the rotation rate is converted to velocity using calibrated formulas, or by electronic sensors measuring wind pressure.
- The *Beaufort Wind Force Scale*, created in 1805 by Admiral Francis Beaufort, ranks winds from 0 (calm) to 12 (hurricane force) based on visible effects: smoke behavior, leaf movement, wave height, and structural damage.
- Wind speed increases with *altitude* in the lower atmosphere due to reduced friction with the ground; this is why wind turbines are mounted on tall towers to capture faster, more consistent winds.
- *Pressure gradient* drives wind; air accelerates from high-pressure regions toward low-pressure regions; stronger pressure differences produce stronger winds, which is why winds accelerate around intense storms and low-pressure systems.
- The *Coriolis effect*, caused by Earth's rotation, deflects moving air to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere; this is why hurricanes and cyclones spin and why trade winds have consistent directions.
- *Jet streams* are fast-moving currents of air in the upper atmosphere, reaching speeds of 300+ km/h, formed by temperature contrasts between polar and tropical air; they steer weather systems and influence long-range forecasting.
- Wind power generation depends on *power density*, which increases with the cube of wind speed (doubling wind speed increases available power eightfold); this is why wind farms are sited in consistently windy regions.
