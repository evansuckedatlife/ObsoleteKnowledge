## summary

The **Challenger disaster** was a catastrophic aerospace accident that occurred on January 28, 1986, when the *NASA* Space Shuttle orbiter *Challenger* broke apart 73 seconds into mission *STS-51-L*. Launching in unprecedented sub-freezing temperatures from the Kennedy Space Center, the vehicle suffered the mechanical failure of primary and secondary elastomeric O-ring seals in its right solid rocket booster, resulting in aerodynamic breakup and the deaths of all seven crew members. The tragedy grounded the American space shuttle fleet for 32 months, fundamentally transformed aerospace risk assessment, and remains a foundational case study in engineering ethics and organizational failure.

## you gotta know

- Occurred on January 28, 1986, just 73 seconds after liftoff from Launch Complex 39B at Cape Canaveral, claiming the lives of all seven astronauts aboard.
- The crew included New Hampshire educator *Christa McAuliffe*, chosen as the primary participant in the national *Teacher in Space Project*, alongside commander *Dick Scobee*, pilot *Michael J. Smith*, *Ronald McNair*, *Ellison Onizuka*, *Judith Resnik*, and *Gregory Jarvis*.
- Caused by the failure of two rubber O-rings manufactured by *Morton Thiokol* to properly seal a field joint on the right solid rocket booster (*SRB*) under ambient launch temperatures of 36°F (2°C).
- Escaping combustion gases produced a blowtorch flame that impinged upon the external tank, vaporizing the lower booster attachment strut and breaching the liquid hydrogen and oxygen propellant reserves.
- Contrary to common belief, the vehicle did not detonate in an explosion; rather, aerodynamic drag forces exceeding 20 g rapidly tore the orbiter apart within a massive cloud of burning propellant.
- Investigated by the presidential *Rogers Commission*, whose panel included astronaut *Neil Armstrong*, aviation pioneer *Chuck Yeager*, and Nobel laureate physicist *Richard Feynman*.
- Famously clarified during commission hearings by *Richard Feynman*, who submerged an O-ring sample into a cup of ice water to demonstrate on national television that cold exposure severely compromised the material's elasticity.
- Immortalized in organizational sociology by *Diane Vaughan*, who coined the phrase *normalization of deviance* to describe how flight engineers and managers progressively redefined recurring hardware anomalies as acceptable risks.
