## summary

The **Lorentz force** is the combined electric and magnetic force exerted on a charged particle by electromagnetic fields. Formulated mathematically by Dutch physicist *Hendrik Lorentz* in the 1890s, it unifies the separate phenomena of electric and magnetic forces into a single relativistically invariant law: `F = q(E + v × B)`, where the electric field `E` exerts a force independent of motion, while the magnetic field `B` exerts a velocity-dependent force. The Lorentz force is the foundation of classical electromagnetism and explains the motion of charged particles in accelerators, plasma confinement, auroras, and countless other phenomena.

## you gotta know

- The electric-field component `F_E = qE` acts on any charge, whether moving or stationary, in the direction parallel (or antiparallel) to the field.
- The magnetic-field component `F_B = qv × B` acts only on moving charges and is perpendicular to both velocity and field; a stationary charge experiences no magnetic force.
- The cross product `v × B` means the magnetic force depends on the angle between velocity and field: maximum perpendicular, zero parallel, creating circular or helical paths.
- A charged particle in a uniform [[magnetic-field|magnetic field]] perpendicular to its velocity follows a circular arc with radius *r = mv / (qB)* and cyclotron frequency *f = qB / (2πm)*.
- This principle powers *cyclotrons* (accelerators) and *magnetrons* (microwave generators) where charged particles spiral in controlled paths to gain energy or emit radiation.
- The Lorentz force explains why [[compass|compasses]] align with Earth's magnetic field and why the [[aurora|auroras]] glow—solar wind particles spiral along the magnetic field lines near the poles.
- In plasma physics, the Lorentz force on ions and electrons collectively determines plasma behavior, confinement, and instabilities in fusion reactors.
- The force is frame-dependent: an electric force in one reference frame appears partly magnetic in another moving frame, revealing that `E` and `B` are aspects of a single relativistic electromagnetic field tensor.
