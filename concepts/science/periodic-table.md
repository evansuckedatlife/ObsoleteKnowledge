## summary

The **Periodic Table of Elements** is an organized arrangement of all known chemical elements in rows and columns, reflecting their atomic structure and chemical behavior. Developed principally by *Dmitri Mendeleev* in 1869, it has become the foundational reference tool in chemistry, organizing elements by atomic number and revealing deep patterns in their properties and reactivity.

## you gotta know

- Arranges elements in order of increasing atomic number (number of protons), with elements in the same column sharing similar chemical properties due to identical electron configurations in their outermost shell.
- Divided into periods (horizontal rows) and groups (vertical columns); elements in the same group exhibit remarkably similar reactivity and bonding patterns across multiple chemical reactions.
- Mendeleev's genius was not only organizing known elements but predicting unknown ones by leaving gaps in his table; subsequent discoveries confirmed his predictions with astonishing accuracy.
- Reveals the periodicity of chemical properties: as atomic number increases, chemical behavior repeats in a cyclical pattern, reflecting the filling of electron shells.
- Separates elements into categories—metals, nonmetals, and metalloids—and further subdivides them into families (alkali metals, halogens, noble gases, etc.) with shared characteristics.
- The table visually encodes quantum mechanical principles: period number equals the number of electron shells, and group number relates to valence electrons in most main-group elements.
- Modern periodic tables include synthetic elements created artificially, extending far beyond the 63 elements known in Mendeleev's time; the heaviest elements are unstable and radioactive.
