## summary

An **alpha particle** is a helium-4 nucleus consisting of two protons and two neutrons bound together, ejected from certain radioactive atoms during decay. It is one of the three main types of radioactive emission, alongside beta and gamma radiation. Alpha particles are relatively massive and carry a strong positive charge, making them the least penetrating but most ionizing form of radiation.

## you gotta know

- Consists of two protons and two neutrons—identical to a helium-4 nucleus without its electrons.
- Produced during alpha decay when heavy unstable nuclei shed mass and energy by expelling an alpha particle.
- Named by Rutherford, who used alpha particles as projectiles in his famous gold foil experiment, revealing the nuclear model of the atom.
- Cannot penetrate paper or skin due to its large mass and strong positive charge, but extremely dangerous if ingested or inhaled.
- Ionizes surrounding matter intensely due to its high charge, creating a trail of excited and ionized atoms along its path.
- Uranium, thorium, and radon commonly undergo alpha decay, making alpha radiation prevalent in natural radioactivity.
- Stopped by a few centimeters of air or a sheet of paper, allowing its range to be measured and studied easily.
- Rutherford's gold foil experiment used alpha particles scattered by gold atoms to reveal the concentration of mass in a tiny nucleus, overturning the plum pudding model of the atom.
- The term "alpha" originates from the Greek letter naming scheme for three types of radiation discovered in the early 1900s (alpha, beta, gamma).
