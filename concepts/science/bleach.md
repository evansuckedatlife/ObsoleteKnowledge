## summary

**Bleach** is a generic classification for domestic and industrial chemical formulations utilized to decolorize substrates, whiten textiles, and disinfect surfaces via oxidative destruction of microorganisms. Primarily encountered as dilute aqueous solutions of **sodium hypochlorite**, bleaching technology emerged in late eighteenth-century [[france]] and expanded rapidly during the European [[industrial-revolution]]. It remains an essential cornerstone of municipal water sanitation, clinical disinfection, and textile manufacturing worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Formulated in 1785 by French chemist *Claude Louis Berthollet*, who synthesized *eau de Javel* outside Paris by passing chlorine gas through potassium carbonate solutions.
- Industrialized as a solid bleaching powder by Scottish chemist *Charles Tennant* in 1799, who treated slaked lime with chlorine gas in [[england]] to supply textile factories.
- Operates primarily through chemical oxidation, where hypochlorite ions attack conjugated double bonds in chromophores, converting them into non-absorbing saturated moieties to eliminate color.
- Acts as a potent broad-spectrum biocide by penetrating cellular envelopes, unfolding structural [[protein]]s, denaturing enzymes, and causing irreversible oxidative stress in pathogens.
- Manufactured modernly via the chloralkali process, where the electrolysis of brine yields chlorine gas and [[sodium]] hydroxide that are combined to generate hypochlorite salts.
- Produces dangerous hazardous reactions when blended with household chemicals, releasing toxic chloramines when combined with ammonia and toxic chlorine gas when mixed with acids.
- Includes alternative formulations known as oxygen bleaches, such as hydrogen peroxide and sodium percarbonate, which oxidize stains without generating halogenated byproducts.

## connections

- [[sodium]] — the alkali metal cation present in household sodium hypochlorite solutions.
- [[oxygen]] — the electronegative component of hypochlorite and peroxygen compounds driving oxidative bleaching reactions.
- [[bonding]] — the ionic and polar covalent interactions that stabilize hypochlorite and dictate its oxidative reactivity.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical particles whose oxidation numbers change during redox bleaching processes.
- [[electron]] — the elementary charge carriers transferred during redox cycles as hypochlorite extracts electrons from chromophores and microbial membranes.
- [[france]] — the country where *Claude Louis Berthollet* developed the earliest hypochlorite solutions at Javel.
- [[england]] — the industrial hub where solid bleaching powder was patented and distributed for mass manufacturing.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical era of manufacturing transformation that accelerated the demand for chemical bleaching agents.
