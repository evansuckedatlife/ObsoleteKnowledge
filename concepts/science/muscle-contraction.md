## summary

**Muscle contraction** is the coordinated shortening of muscle fibers, powered by *ATP* hydrolysis and driven by the sliding of thick filaments (myosin) past thin filaments (actin). Initiated by nerve signals that trigger calcium release, contraction requires precise orchestration of motor proteins, ion pumps, and contractile machinery. This fundamental process enables movement, posture, and the mechanical work that defines animal physiology.

## you gotta know

- The sliding filament model describes contraction: myosin heads (cross-bridges) bind actin, pull the thin filament in a power stroke fueled by ATP hydrolysis, then release and reset for the next cycle.
- Rigor mortis occurs when ATP production ceases; without ATP, myosin cannot release from actin, locking muscles in contraction.
- Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum bind *troponin*, shifting *tropomyosin* on actin to expose myosin-binding sites.
- Sodium (Na⁺) influx depolarizes the muscle cell membrane; this electrical signal spreads along the sarcolemma and down T-tubules, triggering calcium release.
- Potassium (K⁺) efflux repolarizes the muscle cell after contraction, restoring the resting membrane potential and enabling the next contraction.
- Motor units—a single motor neuron and all muscle fibers it innervates—are the functional units of contraction; recruited in order of size (small units first, large units for forceful movements).
- ATP provides two functions: it energizes the myosin power stroke and it provides the energy to break the myosin-actin cross-bridge, essential for relaxation.
- Z-disks (Z-lines) mark the boundaries of sarcomeres and anchor thin filaments; the distance between Z-disks decreases during contraction as thin filaments slide past thick filaments.
- Muscle fatigue results from depletion of creatine phosphate stores, lactate accumulation, and calcium handling dysfunction; these factors diminish force production over time.
- Muscle fiber types (Type I slow-twitch and Type II fast-twitch) are differentiated by myosin isoforms and metabolic capacity, suited to endurance or power output.

## connections

- [[atp]] — the energy source that drives the myosin power stroke and enables cross-bridge cycling.
- [[potassium]] — the ion whose efflux repolarizes muscle cells and enables repeated contraction.
- [[sodium]] — the ion whose influx depolarizes muscle cells and initiates the contraction cascade.
- [[myosin]] — the thick filament motor protein that pulls actin via ATP-powered cross-bridges.
- [[actin]] — the thin filament that myosin pulls during contraction.
- [[nerve-transmission]] — the nerve signal that initiates muscle contraction via neurotransmitter release.
- [[electrolyte]] — ions essential to the electrical signaling underlying muscle contraction.
