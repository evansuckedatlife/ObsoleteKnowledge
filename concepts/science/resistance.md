## summary

**Resistance**, specifically **electrical resistance**, is the physical property quantifying how strongly a material or electronic [[circuit]] opposes the flow of electric [[current]]. Formulated mathematically by German physicist *Georg Ohm* in his 1827 treatise *Die galvanische Kette, mathematisch bearbeitet*, it establishes that current through an ideal conductor is directly proportional to applied [[voltage]], the principle known as [[ohms-law]]. Measured in the SI unit ohm ($\Omega$), it arises microscopically from charge carriers scattering off lattice ions and impurities, dissipating energy as heat. Mastery of this property is foundational across physics and engineering, governing power transmission, circuit protection, and the boundary separating ordinary conductors from zero-resistance superconductors.

## you gotta know

- Formulated mathematically by [[ohms-law]] as the constant ratio *R = V / I*, measured in the SI unit ohm ($\Omega$), which is defined as the resistance that allows one ampere of [[current]] under an applied potential of one volt.
- Governed intrinsically by conductor geometry via *R = \rho L / A*, meaning resistance scales directly with length *L*, inversely with cross-sectional area *A*, and proportionally with material resistivity *\rho*, the reciprocal of electrical conductivity.
- Arises microscopically from conduction [[electron]] carriers colliding inelastically with vibrating [[atom]] lattice ions and structural defects, a transport mechanism classically modeled by *Paul Drude* to define relaxation time and carrier drift velocity.
- Dissipates electrical power into thermal energy through *Joule heating*, with total power dissipation calculated by *Joule's first law* as *P = I^2 R*, or equivalently *P = V I* and *P = V^2 / R*.
- Increases linearly with temperature in metallic conductors due to heightened phonon scattering, but decreases exponentially in semiconductors as thermal excitation promotes additional charge carriers across the bandgap.
- Combines additively in series circuits as *R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2* and reciprocally in parallel, with precise laboratory measurements historically performed using four-terminal *Kelvin sensing* or a balanced *Wheatstone bridge*.
- Drops abruptly to absolute zero below a material-specific critical temperature in superconductors, a zero-resistance state discovered in mercury by *Heike Kamerlingh Onnes* in 1911 and accompanied by the *Meissner effect*.
- Exhibits exact topological quantization in two-dimensional electron gases subjected to strong magnetic fields via the *quantum Hall effect*, forming plateaus at integer fractions of the *von Klitzing constant*, *R_K = h / e^2*.

## connections

- [[resistor]] — discrete passive circuit component manufactured with carbon, metal film, or wire windings to provide precise, calibrated electrical resistance.
- [[electron]] — subatomic charge carrier whose drift velocity and scattering against lattice ions determine bulk electrical conductivity and resistance.
- [[atom]] — constituent of the conducting crystalline lattice whose thermal vibrations and impurity sites act as the primary scattering centers for conduction carriers.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — theoretical framework explaining electronic band structure, carrier scattering probabilities, and the dissipationless supercurrents of superconductors.
- [[central-processing-unit]] — microchip where parasitic interconnect resistance produces heat dissipation and imposes fundamental limitations on operating clock frequencies.
- [[motherboard]] — printed circuit board utilizing trace geometry and discrete surface-mount resistors to control signal attenuation, termination, and impedance matching.
- [[carbon]] — versatile nonmetallic element historically utilized in solid-rod composition resistors and modern resistive film components due to its thermal stability.
- [[penicillin]] — antibacterial medication whose target pathogens evolve biological resistance, representing an entirely distinct evolutionary phenomenon that shares the name.
