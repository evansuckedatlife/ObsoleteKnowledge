## summary

**Neutrons** are uncharged subatomic particles found in atomic nuclei (except hydrogen-1), composed of three quarks and roughly equal in mass to protons. Slightly heavier than protons and unstable when free (half-life ~10 minutes), neutrons stabilize nuclei against electromagnetic repulsion and are essential for all complex atoms. Their discovery in 1932 by *James Chadwick* completed the model of atomic structure and unlocked nuclear chemistry and physics.

## you gotta know

- Uncharged baryon composed of one up quark and two down quarks; mass ≈ 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, slightly heavier than a proton (1.8 MeV/c²).
- Stable inside nuclei; decays when free via beta-minus decay (n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ), with half-life of ~886 seconds (~15 minutes).
- Number of neutrons (N) determines isotope identity; varying N for fixed proton number (Z) creates stable or radioactive isotopes.
- For light elements, stable nuclei have roughly equal numbers of protons and neutrons (N ≈ Z); heavier nuclei require excess neutrons (N > Z) for stability.
- Nuclear stability depends on the balance of strong nuclear force (attractive, short-range) versus electromagnetic repulsion (repulsive, long-range).
- Discovered in 1932 by *James Chadwick* through beryllium bombardment by alpha particles; completed the model of atomic structure.
- Critical in nuclear fission: one neutron strikes a fissile nucleus (e.g., U-235), producing 2–3 secondary neutrons that trigger a chain reaction.
- Slow neutrons (thermals) are more likely to induce fission in U-235; fast neutrons preferentially cause U-238 capture without fission.
- Used in neutron activation analysis to detect trace elements; neutron scattering probes material structure with wavelengths matching atomic spacings.
- Neutron stars are collapsed stellar cores supported by neutron degeneracy pressure; extremely dense (teaspoon of neutron-star material weighs ~6 billion tons).
- Thermal neutrons (slow, low-energy) are essential for nuclear reactor operation; they sustain chain reactions in fissile materials like uranium.

## connections

- [[proton]] — its positively charged counterpart; both nucleons form stable nuclei.
- [[baryon]] — the particle family to which neutrons belong.
- [[hadron]] — the larger class of particles made from quarks; baryons are one subclass.
- [[nucleus]] — the nucleus is composed of protons and neutrons.
- [[alpha-particle]] — composed of two protons and two neutrons (a helium-4 nucleus).
- [[radioactivity]] — many isotopes with unstable neutron-to-proton ratios decay.
