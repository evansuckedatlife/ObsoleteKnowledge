## summary

**Cell cycle checkpoints** are control mechanisms that cells use to monitor the completion of critical phases and assess cellular conditions before proceeding to the next phase. The most important checkpoints (G1/S, intra-S, G2/M) evaluate DNA integrity, DNA replication fidelity, and chromosome segregation readiness. If problems are detected, checkpoints halt progression and either initiate repair or trigger apoptosis (programmed cell death) if damage is irreparable.

## you gotta know

- The G1/S (restriction point) checkpoint, occurring late in G1 phase, checks for adequate cell size, nutrient availability, growth signals, and DNA damage before committing to DNA replication.
- The G2/M checkpoint, before mitosis, verifies that DNA replication was complete and accurate and that chromosomes are properly attached to spindle fibers; DNA damage detected here prevents mitosis entry.
- Intra-S checkpoints monitor ongoing DNA replication and slow or pause replication fork progression if DNA damage is detected, allowing repair before replication continues.
- The spindle checkpoint (or spindle assembly checkpoint, SAC) prevents anaphase entry until all chromosomes are properly attached to spindle microtubules via kinetochores.
- p53 protein is the central "guardian of the genome"; upon DNA damage detection, p53 accumulates and activates *p21*, which inhibits cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), halting cell-cycle progression.
- If DNA damage cannot be repaired, p53 triggers apoptosis (programmed cell death), eliminating damaged cells that could otherwise become cancerous.
- Checkpoint control involves cyclin-CDK complexes (which promote cycle progression) and CDK inhibitors like p21, p27, and ink4 proteins (which halt progression).
- Loss of checkpoint control through p53 mutations or CDK inhibitor inactivation is a hallmark of cancer; cancer cells often bypass checkpoints, allowing replication of damaged DNA.

## connections

- [[cell-cycle]] — the process whose progression is controlled by checkpoints.
- [[apoptosis]] — the programmed cell death triggered when checkpoints detect irreparable damage.
- [[cancer]] — results from checkpoint loss, allowing DNA-damaged cells to proliferate unchecked.
- [[dna-damage]] — the trigger for checkpoint activation; mutations, breaks, and mismatches are detected.
- [[p53]] — the tumor suppressor gene encoding the master regulator protein activated at checkpoints.
- [[mitosis]] — the phase preceded by G2/M checkpoint; checkpoint failure allows failed mitosis and aneuploidy.
- [[cdh1-protein]] — a regulatory protein involved in anaphase-promoting complex (APC) activation at checkpoints.
