## summary

**Uranus** is the seventh planet from the Sun and an ice giant composed primarily of water, methane, and ammonia ices surrounding a rocky core. Rotated nearly ninety degrees on its side—likely due to an ancient collision—it is the coldest planetary atmosphere in the solar system. Named after the Greek god of the sky, Uranus was the first planet discovered in the modern era and remains enigmatic due to its extreme axial tilt and faint appearance.

## you gotta know

- Discovered in 1781 by William Herschel; the first planet found in nearly 2,000 years, doubling the known size of the solar system and forever expanding humanity's conception of the cosmos.
- Appears as a featureless blue-green disc due to methane in its atmosphere absorbing red light and reflecting blue and green wavelengths, giving it its distinctive cyan hue.
- Rotates at an extreme 98-degree axial tilt, meaning it essentially orbits the Sun on its side; this may result from a collision with a large body early in solar system history, possibly a planet-mass object.
- Composed primarily of water, methane, and ammonia ices (the source of the "ice giant" classification) with a small rocky core, distinct from the gas giants Jupiter and Saturn which lack solid surfaces.
- Takes 84 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun, making it among the slowest-orbiting planets observable from Earth; a year on Uranus lasts nearly a human lifetime.
- Its discovery was predicted mathematically by Urbain Le Verrier based on orbital perturbations detected in Neptune's path, demonstrating the predictive power of celestial mechanics and Newton's laws.
- Has 27 known moons and a subtle ring system; the five largest moons (Titania, Oberon, Umbriel, Ariel, Miranda) display diverse geological features discovered by Voyager 2's historic 1986 flyby.
- Possesses the fastest wind speeds in the solar system, with atmospheric winds reaching up to 900 km/h, surprising given its distant location and low solar heating.
- Emits more heat than it receives from the Sun—an anomaly suggesting internal energy from planetary compression and cooling since its formation.
