## summary

**Plant reproduction** is the biological process by which plants create offspring, occurring through two fundamentally different pathways: sexual reproduction (involving flowers, pollen, and seeds) and asexual reproduction (producing clones through vegetative structures). Flowering plants revolutionized reproduction by developing seeds protected within fruits, while simpler plants spread through spores and fragmentation. The reproductive strategies of plants are central to agriculture, ecosystem function, and the evolution of terrestrial life.

## you gotta know

- Sexual reproduction in angiosperms involves flowers, where pollen from stamens fertilizes ovules in pistils, producing seeds within developing fruits.
- Pollination—transfer of pollen between flowers—occurs via wind, water, or animals; many flowering plants coevolved with specific pollinators like bees and birds.
- Seeds contain an embryonic plant and a food store (endosperm), allowing plants to disperse offspring and survive dormancy before germination.
- Asexual reproduction produces genetically identical offspring via vegetative propagation: runners (strawberries), bulbs (tulips), fragmentation (ferns), or specialized spores.
- Gymnosperms (conifers) reproduce via naked seeds not enclosed in fruits, representing an earlier form of seed evolution than flowering plants.
- Bryophytes (mosses) rely on spores rather than seeds and require water for fertilization, reflecting their dependence on moist habitats.
- The shift from spore reproduction to seed production enabled plants to colonize drier terrestrial environments and become the dominant flora.
- Double fertilization in angiosperms—pollen nucleus fuses with ovule nucleus to form embryo, while another fuses with central cell to form endosperm—is unique to flowering plants.
- Seed dispersal mechanisms evolved diversity: wind-dispersed seeds are light and winged; animal-dispersed seeds have attractive fruits or hooks; water-dispersed seeds are buoyant or streamlined.
