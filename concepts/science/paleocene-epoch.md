## summary

The **Paleocene Epoch**, frequently known simply as the **Paleocene**, represents the opening geological epoch of the *Paleogene Period* and the modern [[cenozoic-era]], lasting from approximately 66 to 56 million years ago. Commencing immediately after the *Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction event*, the epoch was the crucial recovery interval of terrestrial life, witnessing the dramatic ecological rebound and accelerated adaptive radiation of lineages that survived the loss of non-avian dinosaurs. The epoch reached its close with the *Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum*, an extraordinary hyperthermal warming spike that permanently reorganized deep-sea currents, ocean chemistry, and continental mammalian dispersals.

## you gotta know

- Began sixty-six million years ago immediately following the *Chicxulub asteroid impact*, which brought an abrupt close to the [[cretaceous-period]] and eliminated roughly three-quarters of Earth's species, including non-avian dinosaurs and ammonites.
- Witnessed the explosive adaptive radiation of [[mammals]], who rapidly transitioned from small, nocturnal Mesozoic insectivores into diverse herbivores, carnivores, and arboreal specialists across vacant continental landscapes.
- Ended fifty-six million years ago with the *Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum* (*PETM*), a profound hyperthermal interval triggered by thousands of gigatons of [[carbon]] entering the atmosphere, driving global temperatures up by five to eight degrees.
- Maintained a globally warm greenhouse climate with ice-free poles, allowing subtropical forests and lush deciduous vegetation to flourish within high polar latitudes across *Greenland* and *Antarctica*.
- Supported enormous ectothermic predators such as *Titanoboa cerrejonensis*, a forty-five-foot constrictor discovered in *Colombia*, whose massive body size reflects equatorial temperatures far warmer than those of the present day.
- Fostered the rise of *Plesiadapiformes*, an order of archaic climbing mammals with grasping digits and primate-like teeth that represents the stem lineage leading toward true primates and [[human-evolution]].
- Populated by archaic mammalian lineages that would later vanish, such as the bulky herbivorous *Pantodonta*, the predatory *Creodonta* and *Mesonychia*, and archaic rodent-like multituberculates.
- Divided into the *Danian*, *Selandian*, and *Thanetian* stages, and coined in 1874 by paleobotanist *Wilhelm Philipp Schimper* from Greek words for "ancient recent" based on transitional European fossil flora.

## connections

- [[cenozoic-era]] — the grand era of modern life whose environmental and ecological foundation was established during this opening epoch.
- [[cretaceous-period]] — the terminal Mesozoic period whose catastrophic end set the stage for the epoch's biotic rebound.
- [[mammals]] — the vertebrate class whose explosive morphological diversification and ecological ascent began in earnest across the epoch.
- [[carbon]] — the geochemical element whose massive isotopic release into ancient reservoirs precipitated the terminal thermal maximum.
- [[human-evolution]] — the deep primate lineage tracing its earliest ancestral arboreal offshoots to the plesiadapiform radiations of the epoch.
- [[evolution]] — the foundational biological process governing the accelerated radiation of surviving clades into vacant ecological niches.
- [[ice-age]] — the glaciated climate regime of the late Cenozoic, presenting a polar opposite to the epoch's warm greenhouse conditions.
- [[quaternary-period]] — the modern geological period defined by glacial cycles, representing the distant chronological bookend of the Cenozoic.
