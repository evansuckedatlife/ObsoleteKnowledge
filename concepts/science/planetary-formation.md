## summary

**Planetary formation** is the astrophysical process by which planets, moons, and minor bodies coalesce from dense gas and dust within a circumstellar protoplanetary disk orbiting a young star. Beginning with microscopic interstellar dust clumping via electrostatic forces and settling into the disk midplane, this chaotic evolution proceeds through runaway gravitational accretion into planetesimals, protoplanetary embryos, and fully differentiated worlds. Understanding this multi-stage pathway explains the architecture of our own [[solar-system]], the violent collisions that shaped the [[hadean-eon]], and the vast diversity of exoplanetary systems across the galaxy.

## you gotta know

- *Core accretion* serves as the standard model for planet assembly, beginning as microscopic dust settles into the disk midplane around a young protostar before clumping into kilometer-sized planetesimals through mutual gravity.
- *Giant impacts* dominate the chaotic final stages of terrestrial assembly, culminating in catastrophic high-velocity collisions such as the impact between the protoplanet *[[theia]]* and the proto-Earth to generate the Moon under the *[[giant-impact-hypothesis]]*.
- The *frost line* divided the protoplanetary nebula by ambient temperature, ensuring that water, methane, and ammonia condensed into solid ices only beyond roughly 2.7 astronomical units, providing massive solid cores that allowed *[[jupiter]]* to rapidly capture a thick hydrogen-helium envelope.
- *Pebble accretion* resolves the classical timescale problem of giant planet growth, demonstrating how gas drag causes small pebbles to efficiently spiral onto growing embryos, accelerating growth rates before circumstellar gas disperses.
- The *meter-size barrier* is a major hydrodynamic bottleneck where headwind gas drag causes metre-scale boulders to spiral into the host star within centuries, a crisis averted when turbulent gas concentrates solids via the *streaming instability*.
- *Planetary migration* mechanisms, classified into *Type I* and *Type II* migration, demonstrate that newborn planets exchange angular momentum with the surrounding gas disk and shift orbital positions, explaining close-orbiting *hot Jupiters*.
- The *Grand Tack hypothesis* proposes that *[[jupiter]]* migrated inward to roughly 1.5 astronomical units before orbital resonance with Saturn reversed its drift, clearing the asteroid belt and explaining the small mass of Mars.

## connections

- [[solar-system]] — the eight-planet system whose architecture and composition provide the primary empirical benchmark for accretion models.
- [[jupiter]] — the dominant gas giant whose early formation beyond the frost line governed orbital dynamics and mass distribution.
- [[giant-impact-hypothesis]] — the leading scientific theory positing that late-stage terrestrial accretion involved catastrophic collisions between planetary embryos.
- [[theia]] — the Mars-sized protoplanet whose collision with the young Earth represents the signature giant impact of late accretion.
- [[moon-origin]] — the evolutionary problem resolved by catastrophic impact dynamics during the final clearing of the inner disk.
- [[hadean-eon]] — the earliest geologic interval of Earth history, shaped by residual accretionary heat, core differentiation, and relentless bombardment.
- [[impact-crater]] — the morphological feature formed by hypervelocity collisions that physically preserves the geological record of terminal accretion.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical unit whose condensation sequences out of nebular gas determined the bulk composition of rocky worlds.
