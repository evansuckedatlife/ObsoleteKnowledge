## summary

An **integrated circuit** (or **IC**, commonly termed a **microchip** or **chip**) is a miniaturized electronic circuit consisting of semiconductor devices, passive components like the [[resistor]], and metallic interconnections fabricated directly onto a continuous wafer of crystalline material. Independently invented in the late 1950s by *Jack Kilby* and *Robert Noyce* in the [[united-states]], it revolutionized electrical engineering by solving the *tyranny of numbers* that previously constrained discrete component assembly. As the indispensable physical foundation of modern computing and information technology, it catalyzed the digital revolution, enabling the creation of the [[central-processing-unit]], portable telecommunications, and global automation.

## you gotta know

- Invented independently in 1958 and 1959 by *Jack Kilby* at *Texas Instruments* using germanium and *Robert Noyce* at *Fairchild Semiconductor* using silicon, a monumental engineering breakthrough that earned Kilby the 2000 Nobel Prize in Physics following Noyce's premature death in 1990.
- Provided the physical basis for *Moore's Law*, an empirical projection formulated by *Fairchild* and *Intel* co-founder *Gordon Moore* predicting that the density of transistors on an economical microchip would double approximately every two years.
- Achieved its first decisive operational validation during the [[cold-war]] space race, when [[nasa]] and the military procured the vast majority of world output to power guidance computers for the [[apollo-program]] and the Minuteman missile system.
- *Robert Noyce* adapted the silicon *planar process* invented by his colleague *Jean Hoerni*, applying optical photolithography and evaporated aluminum traces to interconnect components internally across an insulating silicon dioxide layer, creating the first monolithic circuit.
- Solved the mid-twentieth-century design bottleneck known as the *tyranny of numbers*, where advanced electronic devices required tens of thousands of manually soldered components, resulting in fragile systems prone to catastrophic interconnection failures.
- Modern fabrication employs extreme ultraviolet photolithography, chemical-mechanical polishing, and ion implantation to pattern billions of nanoscale switches, regulating the transport of the [[electron]] across crystalline wafers with sub-microscopic precision.
- Rapid commercialization beyond aerospace was sparked by the development of the electronic pocket calculator by *Texas Instruments* and Japanese manufacturers, proving that microelectronics could be manufactured cheaply for mass consumer adoption.
- Categorized historically into evolutionary integration scales, advancing from Small-Scale Integration containing fewer than ten transistors to modern Very-Large-Scale Integration and System-on-a-Chip designs housing tens of billions of components on a single die.

## connections

- [[central-processing-unit]] — the primary computational engine of computers, constructed entirely as a complex microchip containing billions of logic gates.
- [[motherboard]] — the primary printed circuit board that mechanically mounts, interconnects, and supplies electrical power to numerous specialized integrated circuits.
- [[resistor]] — a fundamental passive circuit element historically wired by hand but now etched directly alongside active transistors on the semiconductor substrate.
- [[electron]] — the elementary charge carrier whose movement through doped silicon conduction channels dictates binary switching states and digital logic.
- [[apollo-program]] — the [[nasa]] lunar effort whose guidance computer became the pioneering platform proving microchip reliability in high-stakes environments.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical conflict that drove massive government defense procurement, subsidizing production learning curves and lowering manufacturing costs.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the physical framework governing semiconductor band structures, carrier tunneling, and leakage currents that dictate physical limits at nanometer scales.
