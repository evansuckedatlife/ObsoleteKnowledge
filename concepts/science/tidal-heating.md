## summary

**Tidal heating** is the internal heating of a moon or planet caused by gravitational friction as it orbits a larger body or in resonance with other moons. The deforming tidal force generates friction inside the body, dissipating gravitational potential energy as heat. This mechanism powers the intense volcanic activity on Jupiter's moon *Io* and drives the hypothesized subsurface oceans of *Europa* and Saturn's moon *Enceladus*, making it a crucial process in planetary science.

## you gotta know

- Arises when gravitational force varies across a body's diameter as it orbits or when orbital resonance creates changing tidal forces over time.
- *Io* is the most volcanically active body in the solar system because of tidal heating: it orbits close to Jupiter (422,000 km) and is in orbital resonance with Europa and Ganymede; flexing tides heat its interior to ~1,800 K.
- *Europa* likely harbors a subsurface ocean beneath its icy crust, kept liquid by tidal heating from Jupiter's gravity; this ocean could harbor life-supporting conditions.
- *Enceladus* (Saturn's moon) ejects water-vapor plumes from its south polar region, powered by tidal heating; orbital resonance with other Saturnian moons contributes to this ongoing dissipation.
- Tidal heating depends on orbital parameters: distance from primary, orbital eccentricity, and resonances with other moons; a perfectly circular orbit (zero tidal forcing) would generate minimal heating.
- *Tidal locking* occurs when a moon's rotation synchronizes with its orbit (always showing the same face), but tidal heating can prevent complete locking if orbital eccentricity remains.
- The *Love number* quantifies a body's deformability and thus its tidal heating efficiency; rigid bodies dissipate less heat than deformable ones.
