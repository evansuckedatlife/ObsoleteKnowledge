## summary

**Plate Tectonics** is the theory that Earth's lithosphere is divided into several rigid plates that move across the asthenosphere, colliding, diverging, and sliding past one another. This framework explains continental drift, mountain building, volcanic activity, and earthquake patterns. Developed from the 1960s onward, plate tectonics unified geology into a coherent, predictive science.

## you gotta know

- Earth's outer layer consists of discrete lithospheric plates (typically 15-20 major plates) that move at rates of millimeters to centimeters per year over geological timescales; the Pacific Plate moves roughly 7-11 cm per year.
- At divergent boundaries, plates move apart and new oceanic crust forms through seafloor spreading, evident at mid-ocean ridges where magma rises and cools; this process creates new lithosphere continuously.
- At convergent boundaries, plates collide; one plate may subduct beneath the other (subduction zones), producing deep trenches, volcanic arcs, and intense metamorphism; subduction zones generate the largest earthquakes on Earth.
- At transform boundaries, plates slide horizontally past each other, generating earthquakes along strike-slip faults like the San Andreas Fault; the sideways motion releases stress through frequent seismic activity.
- The driving mechanism involves mantle convection: hot material rises beneath mid-ocean ridges and descends at subduction zones, pulling and pushing the plates along; this convection is ultimately powered by heat from the planet's interior.
- Plate tectonics elegantly explains continental drift—Alfred Wegener's earlier hypothesis—by providing a mechanism for continents to move across the globe's surface; continental motion has profoundly shaped biodiversity and climate patterns.
- The theory predicts geological phenomena with remarkable accuracy: earthquake locations, volcanic distribution, mountain ranges, and the shapes and ages of ocean basins; it unified disparate observations into a single coherent framework.

## connections

- [[earth]] — the planet whose dynamic surface plate tectonics describes.
- [[lithosphere]] — the rigid outer shell composed of tectonic plates.
- [[magma]] — molten material generated by decompression melting at plate boundaries.
- [[volcano]] — one manifestation of plate tectonic activity, especially at subduction zones.
- [[earthquake]] — the result of stress release when plates collide, subduct, or slide.
