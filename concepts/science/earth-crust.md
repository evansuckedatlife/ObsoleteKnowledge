## summary

**Earth's crust** is the thin, rigid outermost solid shell of the terrestrial globe, situated above the planetary mantle and separated from it by the *Mohorovičić discontinuity*. Ranging from roughly five kilometers in depth beneath ocean basins to over seventy kilometers beneath major mountain orogens, it comprises less than one percent of the planet's total volume. It preserves the primary physical and geochemical record of rock cycles, tectonic dynamics, and surface habitability throughout planetary history.

## you gotta know

- Separated from the underlying silicate mantle by a sharp seismic velocity boundary discovered by *Andrija Mohorovičić* in 1909.
- Divided into buoyant, thick continental crust rich in felsic aluminosilicate rocks, and denser, thinner oceanic crust dominated by mafic basalt and gabbro.
- Predominantly composed elemental-wise of *oxygen* and silicon, which together form silica tetrahedra constituting the vast majority of rock-forming silicate minerals.
- Undergoes continuous destruction and genesis, with oceanic crust newly generated at mid-ocean ridges and recycled into the mantle via subduction zones over two hundred million years.
- Contains the oldest preserved continental fragments within ancient stable continental interiors known as cratonic shields, dating back nearly four billion years.
- Hosts vital concentrations of economic ore deposits, fossil fuel reservoirs, and water resources critical to terrestrial life and modern civilization.
- Forms the brittle upper portion of tectonic plates within the lithosphere, driving earthquakes and mountain building via continental drift.

## connections

- [[oxygen]] — the single most abundant chemical element by mass in crustal rock complexes, primarily bound within silicate matrices.
- [[carbon]] — cycled across crustal rocks through carbonate sediment deposition, metamorphism, and planetary volcanism.
- [[sodium]] — an abundant alkali metal residing within common crustal framework silicates like plagioclase feldspars.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental constituent unit whose crystal packing geometry determines mineral cleavage and hardness.
- [[earliest-life]] — anchored within the geological strata and hydrothermally altered rock formations of the ancient crust.
- [[early-earth-atmosphere]] — exchanged greenhouse volatiles and degassed steam through widespread crustal volcanism.
