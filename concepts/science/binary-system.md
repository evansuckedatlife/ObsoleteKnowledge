## summary

A **binary system** is an astronomical system composed of two gravitationally bound celestial bodies that orbit around a common center of mass, known as a barycenter. While the designation most frequently refers to a **binary star**—which comprises a substantial fraction of all star systems in the stellar neighborhood—it equally applies to planetary binaries, binary asteroids, and double dwarf planets. In observational astrophysics, binary systems provide direct, empirical measurements of fundamental stellar properties, including mass, orbital radius, and gravitational interaction under general relativity.

## you gotta know

- Visual, spectroscopic, astrometric, and eclipsing binaries represent the four primary observational classes used to identify and measure co-orbiting stellar pairs.
- In *Pluto* and *Charon*, the mutual barycenter resides outside the surface of *Pluto*, making the pair the solar system's best-known planetary-scale **binary system** as confirmed in close-up imaging by [[nasa]]'s [[new-horizons]] flyby.
- Contact binaries and *Roche lobe* overflow lead to mass transfer between companion bodies, frequently triggering astronomical transients such as classical novae and *Type Ia supernovae*.
- The *Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar* (*PSR B1913+16*) provided the first indirect empirical evidence for gravitational waves predicted by [[albert-einstein]], winning the 1993 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- *Eclipsing binaries*, famously exemplified by *Algol* (the "Demon Star") in the constellation *Perseus*, exhibit periodic dips in apparent magnitude as one star passes in front of the other from Earth's perspective.
- The *Algol paradox* was resolved by understanding stellar evolution and mass transfer, showing that a more massive star can evolve faster and dump mass onto its initially lighter companion.
- High-mass binary systems consisting of neutron stars or stellar-mass black holes eventually coalesce through gravitational radiation, producing the cataclysmic mergers observed by *LIGO* and *Virgo*.
- Spectroscopic binaries are detected through periodic Doppler shifts in their spectral lines, yielding single-lined (*SB1*) or double-lined (*SB2*) radial velocity curves.

## connections

- [[albert-einstein]] — developed general relativity, which accounts for periastron advance and predicts the gravitational wave emissions radiating orbital energy away from compact relativistic binaries.
- [[jupiter]] — gas giant whose orbital dynamics and gravitational dominance over trojan asteroids mirror massive stellar companion interactions in compact multi-body orbits.
- [[nasa]] — space agency whose space telescope missions, notably *Kepler* and *TESS*, have catalogued thousands of eclipsing binary systems and circumbinary exoplanets.
- [[new-horizons]] — interplanetary space probe that resolved the mutual orbit and surface features of the *Pluto*-*Charon* dwarf planet pair.
- [[europa]] — Jovian moon subjected to strong gravitational tidal heating, illustrating the tidal locking and deformation phenomena common in close stellar binaries.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — dictates the electron and neutron degeneracy pressures that halt collapse in white dwarf and neutron star components of interacting compact binaries.
- [[atom]] — atomic transitions within stellar photospheres produce the characteristic absorption lines analyzed to measure orbital velocities and component masses.
