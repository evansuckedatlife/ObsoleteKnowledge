## summary

The **atomic number**, symbolized as **Z** or termed the **proton number**, is the fundamental quantity of positive elementary charges present within an *atom*'s core. It uniquely defines the chemical identity of an element, determining its specific placement within the periodic table and dictating its neutral electron configuration. Experimentally confirmed through X-ray spectroscopy by *Henry Moseley* in 1913, the atomic number replaced atomic weight as the governing organizational principle of chemistry.

## you gotta know

- Designated by the symbol *Z*, from the German term *Zahl*, meaning number or figure, and represents the integer count of protons in the [[atomic-nucleus]].
- In a neutral [[atom]], exactly equals the number of bound orbital particles, the [[electron]], which dictates valence configurations and chemical bonding behavior.
- Empirically established as a physical property by English physicist *Henry Moseley* in 1913, whose eponymous law related characteristic X-ray emission frequencies directly to the integer nuclear charge.
- Resolved historical anomalies in *Dmitri Mendeleev*'s original periodic table, correcting mass inversions where heavier elements preceded lighter ones, such as tellurium before iodine and cobalt before nickel.
- Differentiates isotopes of an element, which share identical atomic numbers but exhibit different mass numbers due to variable neutron counts within the central core.
- Defines hydrogen as one, [[carbon]] as six, [[oxygen]] as eight, and [[sodium]] as eleven, establishing the continuous linear sequence of all known natural and synthetic elements.
- Determines the Moseley staircase, a graphical representation exhibiting a strict linear relationship between the square root of characteristic X-ray frequencies and elemental sequence.
- Constrains the synthesis of superheavy transuranic elements, where increasing nuclear charge amplifies repulsive Coulomb forces, leading to structural limits investigated in nuclear physics.

## connections

- [[atomic-nucleus]] — the central structure where the protons quantified by the atomic number reside.
- [[atom]] — the basic unit of a chemical element whose fundamental identity is established by its proton count.
- [[electron]] — subatomic particles that balance nuclear charge in neutral atoms, whose configurations govern chemical reactivity.
- [[oxygen]] — chemical element defined by an atomic number of eight.
- [[carbon]] — chemical element defined by an atomic number of six, serving as the basis of organic chemistry.
- [[sodium]] — alkali metal defined by an atomic number of eleven.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework that determines electronic shells and orbitals based on nuclear charge.
