## summary

In physical science, a **period** designates the fundamental interval across which a repeating physical phenomenon recurs, unifying cyclical dynamics in wave mechanics and structural recurrence in chemistry. In physics and signal processing, the wave period ($T$) is the duration of time required to complete one full cycle of an oscillation, defined as the mathematical reciprocal of frequency ($T = 1/f$). In chemistry, a period denotes a horizontal row of the periodic table, grouping chemical elements that share the same principal energy level and outermost [[electron]] shell. Together, these two foundational concepts anchor the scientific study of harmonic motion, spectroscopic transitions, and the atomic periodicity of the elements.

## you gotta know

- In wave mechanics and harmonic oscillations, the temporal period ($T$) represents the inverse of frequency ($f$), governed by $T = 1/f$, quantifying the precise number of seconds elapsed during one complete sinusoidal cycle or waveform vibration.
- Across the periodic table of the elements, the seven horizontal rows constitute chemical periods, with each consecutive row indicating the progressive filling of a higher principal quantum shell ($n = 1$ to $n = 7$) around the atomic [[nucleus]].
- Periodic trends dictate that traversing a period from left to right causes atomic radii to contract while first ionization energy, electron affinity, and electronegativity steadily climb due to increasing nuclear charge with no intervening shielding.
- For an idealized simple pendulum undergoing small-angle displacement, the period depends exclusively on string length and local gravitational acceleration via $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$, proving strictly independent of bob mass as first deduced by *Galileo Galilei*.
- In wave propagation, period and wavelength ($\lambda$) serve as temporal and spatial conjugates related by wave speed ($v = \lambda / T = \lambda f$), while *Fourier* analysis decomposes complex periodic signals into infinite sums of harmonically related sinusoidal functions.
- In orbital mechanics, *Johannes Kepler*'s third law establishes that the square of a celestial body's orbital period is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit, a relation later derived rigorously from *Isaac Newton*'s law of universal gravitation.
- Period 6 and Period 7 incorporate thirty-two elements each due to the inclusion of the inner transition metals—the lanthanides and actinides—whose $4f$ and $5f$ subshell filling drives the *lanthanide contraction*, making subsequent $5d$ transition metals nearly identical in atomic radius to their $4d$ congeners.

## connections

- [[electron]] — determines the length and configuration of each chemical period as subshells fill according to the *Aufbau* principle and *Hund*'s rule.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical unit whose effective nuclear charge increases across a period, shaping electronegativity and bonding behavior.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — provides the theoretical framework of quantum numbers and quantization that underpins periodic shell structures.
- [[wavefunction]] — exhibits periodic oscillatory behavior across space and time according to the *Schrödinger* equation in quantum systems.
- [[photon]] — the fundamental quantum of electromagnetic radiation whose oscillation period relates directly to photon energy through *Planck*'s constant.
- [[sodium]] — the reactive alkali metal whose single valence electron initiates the filling of Period 3 of the periodic table.
- [[oxygen]] — a nonmetallic element positioned in Period 2 whose high electronegativity drives aerobic cellular respiration and oxidation reactions.
