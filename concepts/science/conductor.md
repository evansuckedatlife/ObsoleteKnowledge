## summary

A **conductor** (or **electrical conductor**) is a material containing mobile electric charges that flow freely under the influence of an applied electric field. In condensed matter physics and engineering, conductors are characterized by partially filled energy bands where delocalized valence electrons form an electron gas throughout a metallic crystal lattice. Because of their minimal opposition to electric current, conductors form the backbone of electrical power grids, microelectronic circuits, and electromagnetic technologies.

## you gotta know

- Silver boasts the highest electrical and thermal conductivity of any metal at room temperature, but copper is far more widely employed in global electrical transmission and wiring due to its relative affordability and ductility.
- *Ohm's law* describes the macroscopic behavior of an ohmic conductor, asserting that electric current is directly proportional to applied voltage and inversely proportional to resistance.
- Microscopic transport was first modeled classically by *Paul Drude* in the 1900 *Drude model*, which treats conducting electrons as a gas bouncing off stationary ionic cores with a characteristic relaxation time.
- In solid-state physics, *band theory* explains conductivity through overlapping valence and conduction bands, eliminating the energy band gap and allowing electrons to enter unoccupied states with infinitesimal thermal energy.
- In electrostatic equilibrium, excess charge placed upon an ideal conductor resides entirely on its external surface, ensuring that the interior electric field vanishes completely, a shielding mechanism utilized in a *Faraday cage*.
- The empirical *Wiedemann-Franz law* establishes that the ratio of thermal conductivity to electrical conductivity in metals is proportional to absolute temperature, scaled by the fundamental *Lorenz number*.
- Below critical temperatures, certain conductors undergo a phase transition into superconductors, entirely eliminating electrical resistivity and actively expelling interior magnetic fields via the *Meissner effect*.

## connections

- [[resistor]] — circuit component designed to impede charge flow, representing the dissipative counterpart to an ideal conductor.
- [[electron]] — elementary subatomic lepton whose mobility in delocalized bands constitutes electric current in metallic conductors.
- [[atom]] — constituent unit whose electronic configuration dictates whether a substance acts as a conductor, semiconductor, or insulator.
- [[sodium]] — alkali metal whose single loosely bound $3s$ valence electron makes it a quintessential free-electron metal.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — provides the theoretical framework for the *Fermi surface*, *Bloch waves*, and *Fermi-Dirac statistics* governing conduction.
- [[conservation-of-energy]] — dictates that electrical potential energy dissipated against internal resistance transforms quantitatively into heat through *Joule heating*.
- [[standard-model]] — foundational particle framework classifying the electron as the charged lepton mediating electrical conduction.
