## summary

The **chromosome theory of inheritance** states that genes are located on chromosomes and that the inheritance of traits follows the behavior of chromosomes during meiosis and fertilization. Developed in the early 20th century by Thomas Hunt Morgan and Walter Sutton, it unified Mendelian genetics (the rules of inheritance) with the physical reality of chromosomal structure, providing the material basis for heredity and founding modern genetics.

## you gotta know

- Sutton and Boveri independently noticed (1902–1903) that chromosome behavior during meiosis matched Mendel's laws of segregation and independent assortment—genes must be *on* chromosomes.
- *Thomas Hunt Morgan* proved the theory experimentally using fruit flies (*Drosophila*), showing that the eye-color gene co-inherited with the sex chromosome, confirming genes have physical locations.
- *Crossing over* (recombination) during meiosis explains why linked genes don't always stay together; the farther apart two genes are on a chromosome, the more likely they'll be separated.
- The theory explained *sex-linked* traits (like color blindness in humans), which appear more often in males because males have only one X chromosome.
- Chromosomes are composed of DNA and proteins; genes are discrete segments of DNA, each coding for a protein or RNA product.
- The theory unified the microscopic world of chromosome behavior with the mathematical patterns Mendel observed, transforming inheritance from a mystery into a testable biological mechanism.

- The theory unified microscopy with mathematics: scientists could now explain why Mendel's 3:1 ratios emerged—genes segregating on chromosomes during meiosis produce predictable inheritance patterns.
- Sex-linked inheritance occurs because the X chromosome is much larger than the Y; males (XY) have only one copy of X-linked genes, making recessive traits appear more often in males than females.
- Modern evidence: gene mapping, DNA sequencing (confirming genes are DNA segments), and transgenic organisms all validate the theory.
