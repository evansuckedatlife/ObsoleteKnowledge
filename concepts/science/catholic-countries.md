## summary

**Catholic countries** refers to the historical and geopolitical bloc of European and global sovereign states—principally centered in [[france]], [[spain]], Portugal, the states of [[italy]], and the Habsburg realms—where the Roman Catholic Church exercised dominant spiritual, civic, and institutional authority. During the Renaissance, Scientific Revolution, and [[enlightenment]], the political apparatus and religious hierarchies within these lands played a dual role in the history of science, actively founding major observatories and university chairs while simultaneously exerting strict doctrinal censorship through inquisitorial bodies. From the worldwide implementation of the Gregorian calendar to state-funded geodesic expeditions across the globe, the scientific institutions of these nations profoundly shaped the timeline and geographical distribution of early modern natural philosophy.

## you gotta know

- Adopted the Gregorian calendar immediately upon its promulgation by Pope *Gregory XIII* in October 1582, jumping ten calendar days ahead of Protestant realms that resisted the papally decreed astronomical correction for over a century and a half.
- Formed the primary testing ground for the controversial Merton thesis, which posited that Protestant religious values accelerated experimental science while Counter-Reformation orthodoxy hindered scientific momentum across Catholic Mediterranean societies.
- Maintained the *Index Librorum Prohibitorum* (Index of Forbidden Books) through the Roman and Spanish Inquisitions, famously censoring heliocentric treatises by *Nicolaus Copernicus* and condemning *Galileo Galilei* in 1633.
- Fostered extensive networks of elite scientific scholarship through religious orders such as the *Society of Jesus* (Jesuits), who operated pioneering astronomical observatories, mathematical academies, and global seismological stations from Rome to South America.
- Sponsored monumental overseas cartographic and astronomical enterprises through royal patronage, including King *Louis XIV*'s Royal Academy of Sciences in Paris, which recruited Italian astronomer *Giovanni Domenico Cassini* to direct the Paris Observatory.
- Sent landmark geodesic expeditions to the equator in the Viceroyalty of Peru, coordinating with Arctic missions that included Swedish astronomer *Anders Celsius* to empirically resolve the debate over Earth's oblateness against French court astronomers.
- Navigated the secularization and institutional reorganization of scientific research during the French Revolution and Napoleonic era, transforming royal patronages into modern secular research institutes and medical universities that study cellular processes like [[cdh1-protein]].
- Modern astronomical legacies rooted in these states include the *Vatican Observatory* (Specola Vaticana), one of the oldest operating astronomical institutes in the world, and major European Southern Observatory optical facilities that support missions like the [[cassini-spacecraft]].

- [[cassini-spacecraft]] — named for Italian-born astronomer *Giovanni Domenico Cassini*, whose foundational career flourished under Papal Bologna and Bourbon Paris.
- [[celsius]] — the metric thermal system developed by *Anders Celsius*, whose Lapland expedition directly tested the geodetic models developed by astronomers in Catholic Paris.
- [[cdh1-protein]] — molecular checkpoint target investigated in medical research centers and oncology institutes across contemporary European nations.
- [[christianity]] — the overarching monotheistic religious tradition whose Roman ecclesiastical hierarchy governed civic and scientific life across these states.
- [[roman-empire]] — the ancient classical state whose administrative divisions, Latin language, and imperial geography laid the structural foundations for Western Catholic Christendom.
- [[spain]] — the preeminent Iberian empire that enforced strict Catholic orthodoxy while funding transatlantic navigation, natural history, and imperial cartography.
- [[france]] — the leading Catholic kingdom whose centralized crown institutions fostered Enlightenment natural philosophy, the Paris Observatory, and the metric system.
- [[italy]] — the Mediterranean cultural center of the Renaissance that nurtured pioneering physics and astronomy despite ecclesiastical inquisitorial trials.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical movement that challenged ecclesiastical hegemony and transformed scientific methodology across continental Europe.
