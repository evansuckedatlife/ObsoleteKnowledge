## summary

An **atmosphere** is a layer of gases surrounding a planet or moon, held in place by gravity. It provides oxygen for respiration, regulates temperature through greenhouse effects, shields surface life from harmful radiation, and enables weather and wind. Earth's atmosphere is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, while other worlds like Venus, Mars, and Titan possess radically different compositions that shape their surface conditions.

## you gotta know

- Earth's atmosphere consists primarily of nitrogen and oxygen, with trace amounts of argon, carbon dioxide, and water vapor.
- Divided into layers: the troposphere (weather), stratosphere (ozone), mesosphere, thermosphere, and exosphere, each with distinct temperatures and properties.
- Density decreases exponentially with altitude, roughly halving every 5.5 kilometers, causing the Kármán line boundary at 100 kilometers.
- Protects life by filtering ultraviolet radiation through the ozone layer and maintaining a greenhouse effect that keeps the planet habitable.
- Wind, weather, and ocean currents are driven by differential solar heating of the atmosphere and the Coriolis effect from planetary rotation.
- Venus has a crushing carbon dioxide atmosphere 92 times denser than Earth's, creating surface temperatures of 462°C; Mars has almost none.
- Titan is the only moon with a substantial atmosphere, denser than Earth's, composed primarily of nitrogen with liquid methane lakes on its surface.
- The thermosphere contains ionized gases that interact with solar wind, creating auroras when charged particles are deflected by Earth's magnetic field.
- Atmospheric composition and density profiles vary dramatically between planets, making comparative planetary atmospherology a key tool for understanding planetary evolution.

## connections

- [[oxygen]] — the primary respirable gas in Earth's atmosphere.
- [[nitrogen]] — the most abundant atmospheric gas at 78%.
- [[carbon]] — present in atmospheric carbon dioxide and methane, crucial to the greenhouse effect.
- [[pressure]] — atmospheric pressure results from the weight of gases above.
- [[wind-speed]] — wind is an atmospheric phenomenon driven by pressure and temperature gradients.
- [[meteorology]] — the study of atmospheric phenomena, weather, and climate.
- [[greenhouse-effect]] — the warming mechanism by which atmospheric gases trap heat.
