## summary

A **B cell**, or **B lymphocyte**, is a type of white blood cell essential to the adaptive immune system that mediates humoral immunity. Originally characterized in the bursa of Fabricius in birds and maturing within the bone marrow in mammals, these cells recognize foreign antigens through membrane-bound immunoglobulins known as B cell receptors. Upon activation, they differentiate into antibody-secreting plasma cells and long-lived memory cells, conferring targeted, enduring defense against pathogens.

## you gotta know

- Generates diversity in its antigen receptors through somatic V(D)J recombination, an enzyme-guided process mediated by the *RAG1* and *RAG2* recombinases acting on genomic [[dna]].
- Displays surface B cell receptors comprising membrane-bound immunoglobulin complexes associated with signaling heterodimers *CD79a* and *CD79b*.
- Undergoes negative selection during central tolerance within the mammalian bone marrow to eliminate or edit self-reactive receptor clones through receptor editing or apoptosis.
- Requires activation in secondary lymphoid tissues such as lymph nodes and spleen, typically through either T cell-dependent pathways involving helper T cells or T cell-independent pathways driven by repeating polymeric antigens.
- Forms germinal centers within lymphoid follicles during an active immune response, where dividing cells undergo somatic hypermutation directed by the *activation-induced cytidine deaminase* [[enzyme]].
- Differentiates into short-lived or long-lived plasma cells specialized for high-output [[protein-synthesis]], mass-producing soluble antibodies with identical epitope specificity to the original membrane-bound receptor.
- Executes immunoglobulin class switch recombination, shifting the constant region of heavy chains from *IgM* and *IgD* to *IgG*, *IgA*, or *IgE* to deploy diverse effector functions.
- Generates long-lived memory cells that persist for decades in lymphoid tissues, enabling rapid secondary antibody responses upon repeated pathogen exposure.

## connections

- [[cell]] — the fundamental biological unit of life, of which lymphocytes represent specialized immune lineages.
- [[protein]] — the functional biochemical class to which synthesized antibodies and surface signaling receptors belong.
- [[enzyme]] — biological catalysts such as *RAG1*, *RAG2*, and *AID* that mediate receptor diversification and class switching.
- [[dna]] — the genomic material rearranged and mutated during V(D)J recombination and somatic hypermutation.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — the metabolic cellular machinery heavily upregulated in plasma cells to manufacture immunoglobulins.
- [[cell-cycle]] — the regulated sequence of division and proliferation driving clonal expansion in germinal centers.
