## summary

**Earliest life** refers to the emergence, primordial biology, and fossil record of the first self-sustaining cellular entities that arose on Earth over 3.7 billion years ago during the Eoarchean or late Hadean eon. Progressing from simple prebiotic chemical ensembles through an *RNA world* to the Last Universal Common Ancestor, these ancestral single-celled organisms laid down the universal architecture of genetics and metabolism. Their survival, proliferation, and biogeochemical activity fundamentally remade planetary surface systems, paving the way for all subsequent biological complexity.

## you gotta know

- Emerged at least 3.5 to 3.8 billion years ago, as documented by macroscopic layered biosedimentary structures known as *stromatolites* and light carbon isotope signatures.
- Converged phylogenetically on the Last Universal Common Ancestor (*LUCA*), a single-celled anaerobic prokaryote that possessed a universal genetic code.
- Relied upon a shared biochemical core including a [[cell]] membrane, [[protein-synthesis]] machinery, and high-energy phosphodiester currency like ATP.
- Hypothesized to have originated in alkaline hydrothermal vents, where natural proton gradients across porous serpentinite chimneys catalyzed primitive bioenergetics.
- Predated modern deoxyribonucleic systems through a postulated *RNA world* stage where ribonucleic polymers acted simultaneously as genetic storage and catalytic ribozymes.
- Comprised strictly anaerobic, single-celled prokaryotic micro-organisms that metabolized through chemolithoautotrophy or fermentation before the advent of oxygenic phototrophy.
- Catalyzed the earliest stages of biological [[evolution]], diversifying rapidly into the distinct domains of Bacteria and Archaea.

## connections

- [[cell]] — the fundamental membrane-bound structural unit that bounded primordial life from surrounding geochemical chaos.
- [[evolution]] — the unifying biological paradigm that guided the diversification of primordial ancestral forms through natural selection.
- [[dna]] — the stable double-stranded nucleic acid that superseded RNA as the primary long-term informational archive for descendants.
- [[protein]] — the catalytic and structural macromolecules whose ribosomal manufacture became standardized in the universal common ancestor.
- [[early-earth-atmosphere]] — the anoxic, volatile-rich gaseous environment under which primordial organisms originated and evolved.
- [[earth-crust]] — provided the geothermal heat, mineral catalysts, and sedimentary basins essential for early cellular habitation.
