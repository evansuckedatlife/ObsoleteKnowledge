## summary

**Current electricity** is the physical phenomenon defined by the continuous, net directional transport of electric charge through a conductive medium or closed circuit. Unlike static electricity, which involves stationary electrical charges, current electricity enables the continuous transfer and transformation of electrical energy into mechanical work, thermal energy, and computational logic. The systematic investigation of electric currents by *Alessandro Volta*, *André-Marie Ampère*, and *Michael Faraday* catalyzed the Second Industrial Revolution and established the foundation of modern technology. From high-voltage continental transmission lines to nanoscale semiconductor gates, the precise regulation of electric current remains indispensable to human society. Today, controlled electrical currents drive global power distribution and underpin microelectronic computer hardware.

## you gotta know

- Electric current is defined as the net quantity of electric charge passing through a cross-sectional boundary per unit time, measured in amperes, where one ampere represents one coulomb of charge per second.
- In solid metallic conductors, current is carried by the drift velocity of free electrons navigating a periodic [[crystalline]] lattice, moving at fractions of a millimeter per second while the electrical field propagates at nearly the speed of light.
- *Ohm's law* dictates the operational behavior of ideal conductors, stating that current is directly proportional to applied voltage and inversely proportional to electrical resistance.
- The late nineteenth-century "War of the Currents" pitted *Thomas Edison*, who backed direct current, against *Nikola Tesla* and *George Westinghouse*, who demonstrated that alternating current could be efficiently stepped up and down using transformers.
- In modern computing architectures, direct current is carefully stepped down, filtered, and distributed across the traces of a [[motherboard]] to supply microprocessors with low-voltage, high-amperage power.
- *Kirchhoff's circuit laws* govern current flow in electrical networks, with the junction rule asserting that the total current entering any node must equal the total current exiting, embodying the conservation of electric charge.
- Beyond solid metals, electric current flows through liquid electrolytes and gaseous plasmas via the coordinated migration of positive cations and negative anions under an applied potential difference.
- In superconducting materials cooled below their critical temperature, Cooper pairs form and carry electric current indefinitely without electrical resistance or thermal dissipation.

## connections

- [[electron]] — the elementary subatomic charge carriers whose collective drift through conductive materials constitutes electric current.
- [[resistor]] — a passive circuit component engineered to oppose electric current, establishing voltage drops and dissipating electrical energy.
- [[crystalline]] — solid-state structures whose regular atomic geometry and band structure govern electron scattering and electrical resistivity.
- [[motherboard]] — the main printed circuit assembly that channels regulated direct currents to computing chips and peripheral devices.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical era transformed by the deployment of electric current generators, power grids, and factory motors.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical discipline explaining electron conduction bands, tunneling phenomena, and zero-resistance supercurrents.
- [[central-processing-unit]] — the computational logic processor whose microscopic transistors switch tiny electric currents billions of times per second.
