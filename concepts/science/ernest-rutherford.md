## summary

**Ernest Rutherford**, widely celebrated as the father of nuclear physics, was a New Zealand-born British physicist who revolutionized the understanding of atomic architecture. He discovered radioactive half-life, proved that radioactivity involves the transmutation of one chemical element into another, and identified alpha and beta radiation. His famous gold foil experiment overturned the plum pudding model by demonstrating that virtually all atomic mass and positive charge is concentrated in a tiny central core.

## you gotta know

- Awarded the 1908 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his investigations into the disintegration of the elements and the chemistry of radioactive substances.
- Supervised the 1909 Geiger-Marsden gold foil experiment, discovering that alpha particles deflected at large angles when fired at thin metal films.
- Proposed the planetary model of the *[[atom]]* in 1911, establishing that a dense, positively charged *[[nucleus]]* is orbited by distant negative charges.
- Achieved the first artificial nuclear transmutation in 1919 by bombarding nitrogen gas with alpha particles to produce *[[oxygen]]* and eject a proton.
- Coined the terms *alpha particle*, *beta particle*, *gamma ray*, *half-life*, and *proton*, and theoretically postulated the existence of the neutron in 1920.
- Directed the Cavendish Laboratory at the University of Cambridge, mentoring brilliant figures including *Niels Bohr*, *James Chadwick*, and *John Cockcroft*.
- Has his legacy commemorated in element 104, rutherfordium, synthesized via bombardment in heavy ion accelerators.

## connections

- [[nucleus]] — the dense, positively charged atomic center whose existence he deduced from backscattered alpha radiation.
- [[atom]] — the basic unit of matter whose internal architecture he radically overturned from Thomson's uniform sphere.
- [[electron]] — the light charged particle whose planetary orbits in his atomic model prompted Bohr's quantum revisions.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the physical discipline developed to resolve the electrostatic collapse predicted by his classical planetary model.
- [[fluorine]] — a light chemical element whose nine nuclear charges follow the structural laws he discovered.
- [[oxygen]] — the element synthesized during his historic 1919 nitrogen-disintegration transmutation experiment.
- [[albert-einstein]] — contemporary theoretical physicist whose mass-energy equivalence underpins the nuclear reactions he first observed.
