## summary

**Electrons** are negatively charged subatomic particles found in atoms, orbiting nuclei and defining chemical behavior through their arrangement in electron shells. As the lightest charged lepton, they were among the first elementary particles discovered and remain central to understanding atomic structure, chemical bonding, and electricity. Their mass, charge, and quantum properties are precisely defined constants in the Standard Model.

## you gotta know

- Elementary particle with negative charge (−1 elementary charge); mass ≈ 1/1836 of a proton (9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg).
- Classified as a lepton, the only charged lepton that is stable outside the nucleus; neutrinos are uncharged leptons.
- Discovered by J.J. Thomson in 1897 through cathode-ray experiments; charge-to-mass ratio quantified by Robert Millikan's oil-drop experiment.
- Spin-½ fermion following the Pauli Exclusion Principle; no two electrons in an atom can occupy identical quantum states.
- Configured in orbitals around nuclei according to quantum mechanics; orbital shapes (s, p, d, f) determine bonding geometry.
- Forms chemical bonds by sharing (covalent) or transferring (ionic) between atoms; valence electrons determine chemical properties and reactivity.
- Antiparticle is the positron, with identical mass but positive charge; electron-positron annihilation releases 1.022 MeV energy as gamma rays.
- Flow of electrons through conductors is electric current; properties like conductivity, semiconductivity, and superconductivity depend on electron availability and mobility.
- Photoelectric effect demonstrates particle nature; *Einstein* explained photon energy overcomes work function to eject electrons, proving photons carry discrete energy quanta.
- Wave-particle duality: electrons exhibit both wave properties (diffraction, interference) and particle properties (localized charge, momentum), reconciled by quantum mechanics.
- Electron shells and valence electrons determine chemical properties; s, p, d, f orbital families predict bonding geometry, electronegativity, and periodic trends in reactivity.
- In metals, electrons are delocalized across the crystal, forming a "sea of electrons" that conducts electricity and heat; in insulators, electrons are tightly bound to atoms.
