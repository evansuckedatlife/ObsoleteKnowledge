## summary

The **moon landing** represents humanity's first footfall on an extraterrestrial body, achieved on July 20, 1969, when astronauts *Neil Armstrong* and *Buzz Aldrin* descended to the lunar surface aboard Apollo 11's lunar module. This singular achievement, watched by nearly half a billion people worldwide, symbolized the culmination of the Space Race, validated space exploration as a human endeavor, and inspired a generation to pursue science and engineering.

## you gotta know

- *Neil Armstrong*, the mission commander, became the first human to set foot on the Moon, uttering the iconic phrase "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."
- *Buzz Aldrin*, the lunar module pilot, joined Armstrong on the surface for a 2.5-hour moonwalk, collecting samples and conducting experiments while Michael Collins orbited above in the command module.
- The two astronauts deployed a retroreflector that scientists still use today to measure the Earth-Moon distance by bouncing lasers off the Moon.
- Apollo 11 launched on July 16, 1969, from Cape Kennedy; the mission lasted 8 days and returned safely to Earth on July 24, carrying 47.5 kilograms of lunar samples.
- The landing site, the Sea of Tranquility, was chosen for its relatively flat, safe terrain; scientific instruments left behind recorded moonquakes and atmospheric data for years.
- The achievement required unprecedented coordination of 400,000 engineers and scientists, from NASA headquarters to contractors across America.
- The moon landing vindicated the feasibility of human spaceflight to the Moon and beyond, paving the way for five more successful lunar landings through 1972.
- Armstrong's famous phrase may have included the article "a," intended as "one small step for a man," but transmission quality made it ambiguous to radio audiences worldwide.
- The lunar module descent involved a risky low-fuel situation when computer-guided landing target proved boulder-strewn; Armstrong manually piloted to a safe spot with mere seconds of fuel remaining.
- The Apollo 11 samples were analyzed for composition, age (3.8 billion years old), and the absence of water, revolutionizing understanding of lunar geology and evolution.
