## summary

The **cell cycle** is the regulated sequence of events by which a cell grows, replicates its DNA, and divides into daughter cells. Divided into distinct phases—G1 (gap 1), S (synthesis), G2 (gap 2), and M (mitosis)—the cycle is orchestrated by checkpoint controls that ensure DNA integrity before progression. Dysregulation of the cell cycle underlies cancer, making it a central topic in biology and medicine.

## you gotta know

- The cell cycle consists of interphase (G1, S, G2 phases) during which the cell grows and prepares for division, followed by mitosis (M phase) where the nucleus divides and cytokinesis splits the cell.
- G1 phase is the growth phase after cell division; S phase is when DNA replication occurs via semi-conservative replication; G2 is final preparation for mitosis.
- Checkpoint mechanisms, particularly the G1/S and G2/M checkpoints, monitor DNA integrity, nutrient availability, and growth signals before allowing progression; damaged DNA can trigger cell-cycle arrest or apoptosis.
- CDKs (cyclin-dependent kinases) and cyclins are the molecular regulators of the cell cycle; cyclin levels rise and fall in concert with cell-cycle phases, activating CDKs at precise times.
- The restriction point (R point) in G1 is the critical "point of no return"—after passing it, cells are committed to DNA replication regardless of external signals.
- Prokaryotes lack a complex cell cycle, dividing by binary fission without the regulatory checkpoints seen in eukaryotes; eukaryotic cell cycles are far more elaborate and highly controlled.
- Cancer cells bypass cell-cycle checkpoints through mutations in tumor suppressors like p53 and oncogenes like RAS, allowing unchecked division and genomic instability.

## connections

- [[mitosis]] — the nuclear division phase of the M phase of the cell cycle.
- [[dna-replication]] — the copying of the entire genome during S phase.
- [[checkpoint]] — control mechanisms ensuring cells only proceed when conditions are met.
- [[cell]] — the living unit whose cycle is being described.
- [[apoptosis]] — programmed cell death triggered when checkpoints fail.
- [[p53]] — tumor suppressor that halts the cell cycle in response to DNA damage.
