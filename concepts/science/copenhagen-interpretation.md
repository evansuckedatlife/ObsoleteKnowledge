## summary

The **Copenhagen interpretation** is an orthodox conceptual framework in *[[quantum-mechanics]]* formulated during the late 1920s by *Niels Bohr* and *Werner Heisenberg*. Synthesized largely at Bohr's institute in *Denmark*, it asserts that physical systems do not possess determinate classical properties prior to measurement, existing instead as superpositions described by an evolving state vector. The interpretation establishes wave-particle duality, probabilistic outcomes governed by the Born rule, and wave packet reduction as core tenets of quantum reality.

## you gotta know

- Asserts that physical entities such as the *[[electron]]* and *[[photon]]* display complementary wave or particle behaviors depending exclusively on the observational apparatus chosen.
- Postulates that the *[[wavefunction]]* provides an exhaustive mathematical description of an isolated quantum system, whose absolute square gives the probability density of measurement outcomes.
- Assumes the collapse postulate, wherein non-unitary state reduction abruptly transitions an indeterminate superposition into a single discrete eigenstate upon physical measurement.
- Incorporates Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, establishing that conjugate dynamical observables such as position and momentum cannot simultaneously possess arbitrarily sharp values.
- Drew famous philosophical opposition from *[[albert-einstein]]*, who rejected inherent indeterminism with the quip that God does not play dice with the universe.
- Provoked *Erwin Schrödinger* to devise his feline thought experiment to satirize the counterintuitive implications of superpositions persisting into macroscopic realms.
- Posits Bohr's correspondence principle, demanding that quantum mechanical statistical predictions asymptotically reproduce classical mechanics in the limit of large quantum numbers.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — foundational branch of physics that the Copenhagen framework historically served to interpret and philosophically ground.
- [[wavefunction]] — core mathematical vector in Hilbert space whose physical nature and collapse under measurement are defined by Copenhagen dogma.
- [[albert-einstein]] — prominent theoretical physicist who formulated the EPR paradox to argue against Copenhagen completeness in favor of local realism.
- [[photon]] — quantum of electromagnetic radiation whose interference patterns and particulate absorption illustrate Copenhagen complementarity.
- [[electron]] — subatomic particle whose orbital configurations and double-slit behaviors confirmed the probabilistic nature of wave mechanics.
- [[atom]] — atomic-scale physical system whose discrete spectral lines and stable ground states necessitated Copenhagen quantum conceptualization.
