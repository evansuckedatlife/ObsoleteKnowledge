# Oort Cloud

## summary

The **Oort Cloud** is a hypothesized spherical shell of icy bodies surrounding the solar system at vast distances—up to 200,000 astronomical units from the Sun (roughly one light-year away). Although never directly observed, the Oort Cloud is inferred from the trajectories of long-period comets; its existence explains why comets with orbital periods of thousands of years approach Earth from random directions, and why stellar perturbations occasionally dislodge objects that fall into the inner solar system as visible comets.

## you gotta know

- The Oort Cloud is named after Dutch astronomer *Jan Oort*, who in 1950 proposed it to explain the origins of long-period comets and their apparent isotropic distribution in the sky.
- It lies far beyond *Pluto* and the *Kuiper Belt* (which ends around 50 AU), at distances of 2,000 to 200,000 AU from the Sun, making it the outermost boundary of the solar system and partially within the Sun's gravitational dominion.
- Comets in the Oort Cloud are thought to consist of volatile ices (water, methane, ammonia, carbon dioxide) mixed with rock and dust; when a nearby star passes or galactic tides perturb orbits, they may be knocked inward toward the Sun.
- The Oort Cloud is not spherically symmetric: it has a denser inner region and a more diffuse outer zone; some models propose a flattened disk component as well, reflecting the complexity of planetary formation and gravitational scattering early in solar-system history.
- Comets arriving from the Oort Cloud have very high velocities and nearly random orbital inclinations, explaining why periodic comets (like *Halley's Comet*, which returns every 76 years) come from much closer reservoirs like the Kuiper Belt.
- The cloud contains an estimated 1–2 trillion icy bodies, each larger than 1 kilometer; these are the leftover planetesimals from the solar system's formation 4.6 billion years ago, ejected outward by interactions with the giant planets.
- The Oort Cloud is dynamically unstable on cosmic timescales: stellar encounters within the Milky Way slowly erode its outer boundary, and the cloud may have shed half its initial objects over the age of the solar system.
- Occasionally, a star passes close enough to perturb Oort objects significantly; the nearest star, *Proxima Centauri*, has had its path modeled to see if historical close approaches could have triggered comet showers toward Earth.
