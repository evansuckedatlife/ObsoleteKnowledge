## summary

A **capsaicinoid** is any member of a class of bioactive chemical compounds produced as secondary metabolites in chili peppers of the genus *Capsicum*. These molecules act as chemical deterrents against mammalian herbivores by selectively binding to specific sensory receptors. They are of major scientific and culinary importance due to their role in producing the sensation of pungency and spice, quantified on the Scoville scale.

## you gotta know

- Elicits a burning sensation by binding to and activating the *transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily V member 1* (*TRPV1*) receptor on sensory nerve fibers.
- The most abundant and potent naturally occurring compound in the class is **capsaicin** (8-methyl-*N*-vanillyl-6-nonenamide), followed closely in concentration by *dihydrocapsaicin*.
- *Wilbur Scoville* devised the organoleptic Scoville scale in 1912 to quantify chili heat by measuring the degree of dilution in sugar water necessary to eliminate detectable pungency.
- Today, heat levels are quantitatively determined via high-performance liquid chromatography (*HPLC*) and subsequently converted to standard Scoville Heat Units (*SHU*).
- Mammals perceive capsaicin as painful heat because *TRPV1* typically serves as a nociceptive receptor activated by actual thermal temperatures exceeding 43 degrees Celsius.
- Birds lack capsaicin-sensitive variants of the *TRPV1* receptor, allowing them to consume chili fruits and disperse intact seeds without experiencing irritation.
- Prolonged or repeated exposure leads to receptor desensitization and depletion of *substance P*, giving capsaicin clinical applications as a topical analgesic for neuropathic pain.
