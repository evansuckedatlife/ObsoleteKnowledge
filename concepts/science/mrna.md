# mRNA

## summary

**Messenger RNA (mRNA)** is a single-stranded RNA molecule that carries genetic instructions from DNA to the ribosome, specifying the amino-acid sequence of proteins to be synthesized. Transcribed temporarily from DNA in the nucleus (in eukaryotes) or directly in the cytoplasm (in prokaryotes), mRNA is translated into protein by ribosomes in a sequence of three-nucleotide codons, each corresponding to a specific amino acid. mRNA is the link between genotype and phenotype, translating static genetic information into dynamic protein products.

## you gotta know

- *mRNA is transcribed* from DNA by [[RNA polymerase]] in a process mirroring DNA replication, except that *uracil (U)* replaces thymine (T) in RNA, and the molecule remains single-stranded.
- In *prokaryotes*, mRNA is used directly for translation without modification; in *eukaryotes*, mRNA undergoes processing: a 5' cap is added, a 3' poly-A tail (multiple adenines) is added, and introns (non-coding sequences) are spliced out, leaving only exons (coding sequences).
- Each group of three consecutive nucleotides on mRNA is a *codon*, which specifies one amino acid (or a stop signal); there are 64 possible codons, coding for 20 amino acids plus stop signals, making the genetic code *redundant* (multiple codons for one amino acid).
- *Translation* occurs at the ribosome: [[transfer RNA (tRNA)]] molecules bring amino acids matched to codons; the ribosome catalyzes peptide bonds between amino acids, growing the protein chain until a stop codon terminates translation.
- mRNA is *unstable* and degrades over hours to days (in eukaryotes) or minutes (in prokaryotes); this transience allows cells to rapidly adjust protein production by controlling mRNA synthesis without waiting for protein degradation.
- *Polycistronic mRNA* (common in prokaryotes) can encode multiple proteins; one mRNA is translated into several proteins, allowing coordinated synthesis of functionally related proteins.
- The *mRNA vaccine* technology (used in COVID-19 vaccines) delivers synthetic mRNA encoding viral spike proteins directly into cells, allowing the cells themselves to produce the antigen and trigger immune response without using live virus.

## connections

- [[dna]] — the source template from which mRNA is transcribed; DNA is the permanent genetic storage.
- [[rna]] — mRNA is one of several RNA species; others include tRNA and rRNA (ribosomal RNA).
- [[protein-synthesis]] — mRNA is translated at the ribosome into protein.
- [[ribosome]] — the molecular machine that reads mRNA codons and catalyzes protein synthesis.
- [[codon]] — three-nucleotide units on mRNA, each specifying an amino acid.
