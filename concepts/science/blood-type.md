## summary

A **blood type** is an immunological classification of blood based on the presence or absence of inherited antigenic molecules on the surface of erythrocytes. First identified in 1900 in Vienna by physician *Karl Landsteiner*, this taxonomic system unlocked safe blood transfusions and laid the foundations of modern immunohematology. Variations in these surface antigens dictate transfusion compatibility, organ transplantation matching, maternal-fetal health, and population-level susceptibility to infectious disease.

## you gotta know

- Discovered in 1900 by Austrian physician *Karl Landsteiner*, who recognized the ABO system by mixing serum and erythrocytes from colleagues, winning the 1930 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
- Directed genetically by the ABO gene on chromosome 9, which codes for glycosyltransferase enzymes that attach specific terminal carbohydrate residues like N-acetylgalactosamine or D-galactose to the core H antigen.
- Incorporates the major Rhesus system discovered by *Karl Landsteiner* and *Alexander S. Wiener* in 1940, where presence of the transmembrane RhD antigen confers positive status.
- Dictates strict transfusion compatibility patterns where type O-negative red blood cells act as universal donor units, whereas type AB-positive individuals serve as universal recipients.
- Triggers dangerous acute hemolytic transfusion reactions when incompatible blood is introduced, causing host antibodies to activate the complement system and lyse donor cells.
- Underlies hemolytic disease of the newborn when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive child, a complication routinely prevented with RhoGAM anti-D immunoglobulin injections.
- Includes rare variants such as the Bombay phenotype, where an absence of the FUT1 gene prevents synthesis of the precursor H antigen entirely, yielding universal ABO incompatibility.
- Mobilized into large-scale national blood banks and plasma preservation systems during [[world-war-ii]] through innovations led by *Charles R. Drew*.

## connections

- [[cell]] — the red blood erythrocytes whose lipid membranes display polymorphic antigen complexes.
- [[protein]] — the structural transmembrane antigens and biosynthetic enzymes that establish blood group characteristics.
- [[dna]] — the genomic blueprint encoding the polymorphic alleles responsible for inherited blood types.
- [[evolution]] — the selective evolutionary process that preserved blood group diversity across human populations, partly influenced by malaria resistance.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the wartime emergency that spurred industrialized plasma fractionation, collection drives, and typing protocols.
- [[germany]] — the central European scientific environment where early twentieth-century serological and hematological research flourished.
- [[bonding]] — the covalent glycosidic bonds that link specific monosaccharide determinants onto core oligosaccharide antigens.
