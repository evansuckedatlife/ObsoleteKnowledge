## summary

**Actin** is an abundant, extraordinarily conserved eukaryotic cytoskeletal protein that reversibly polymerizes into dynamic linear microfilaments critical for structural integrity and mechanical force generation. Alternating between monomeric **G-actin** and filamentous **F-actin**, it forms the structural backbone of cell motility, morphological changes, and cytokinesis. In muscular tissue, its coordinated mechanical interaction with motor proteins forms the molecular basis of voluntary and involuntary contraction.

## you gotta know

- Exists dynamically as a monomeric globular state that polymerizes in a magnesium- and nucleotide-dependent manner into a two-stranded right-handed helical polar microfilament.
- Exhibits structural polarity with a fast-growing barbed plus end and a slower-growing pointed minus end, establishing directional tracks for molecular motor transport.
- Displays the hallmark phenomenon of treadmilling at steady state, where monomer addition at the barbed end balances subunit dissociation from the pointed end fueled by nucleotide hydrolysis.
- Serves as the primary thin filament of the muscle sarcomere, flanked by regulatory proteins *tropomyosin* and *troponin* that mask myosin-binding sites in the absence of calcium.
- Undergoes cross-bridge cycling with motor protein partners, wherein motor head domains perform cyclical power strokes against the filament to generate sliding contraction.
- Nucleates at the leading edge of crawling cells via the *Arp2/3 complex* and formins, assembling dense branched networks that form lamellipodia and tactile filopodia.
- Forms the transient contractile ring during eukaryotic cytokinesis, constricting the equatorial plasma membrane to pinch dividing cells into two separate daughters.
- Targeted selectively by natural chemical reagents, including the death-cap toxin *phalloidin* which locks filaments in place, and fungal *cytochalasins* that cap polymer ends.

## connections

- [[myosin]] — the primary ATP-hydrolyzing molecular motor that binds actin thin filaments to pull sarcomeres and drive cellular contraction.
- [[atp]] — the high-energy nucleotide bound by monomeric subunits, whose slow internal hydrolysis dictates the kinetics of filament assembly and disassembly.
- [[protein]] — the fundamental category of biological macromolecules to which this ubiquitous cytoskeletal polymer and its regulatory complexes belong.
- [[cell]] — the fundamental microscopic unit of life whose internal architecture, cytoplasmic streaming, and division depend upon microfilaments.
- [[nerve-transmission]] — the physiological signalling process that triggers calcium release at the neuromuscular junction to initiate actomyosin mechanical activity.
- [[sodium]] — the monovalent cation that mediates depolarizing action potentials across excitable membranes, ultimately prompting muscle filament sliding.
- [[potassium]] — the major intracellular cation whose physiological balance governs the resting membrane potential necessary for muscular stimulation.
