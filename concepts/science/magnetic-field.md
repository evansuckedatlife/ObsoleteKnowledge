## summary

A **magnetic field** is a vector field that exerts a force on moving electric charges and magnetic dipoles, analogous to how an electric field acts on static charges. Represented by magnetic field lines emanating from poles, magnetic fields are generated by moving charges, permanent magnets, and quantum spin. Magnetism is intertwined with electricity through *electromagnetism*, described by Maxwell's equations, and is fundamental to countless technologies from motors to MRI scanners.

## you gotta know

- Magnetic fields are measured in Tesla (T) in SI units; Earth's magnetic field is approximately 25–65 microtesla depending on location.
- The Lorentz force on a charged particle moving through a magnetic field is *F* = *q*(*v* × *B*), perpendicular to both velocity and field; this is the basis of cyclotron motion and particle accelerators.
- Bar magnets have north and south poles; opposite poles attract, like poles repel. Unlike electric charges, isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist (though theoretically predicted).
- Moving electric charges create magnetic fields; a current-carrying wire generates a circular magnetic field around itself (right-hand rule governs direction).
- Electromagnets use electric current through coils to create strong magnetic fields; by varying current, they can be switched on/off, enabling electromagnetic relays and actuators.
- Earth's magnetic field, likely generated by convection currents in the liquid outer core, protects the planet from solar wind and radiation; its poles precess slowly over millennia.
- Quantum mechanically, electrons possess intrinsic magnetic moment called *spin*; aligned spins in atoms or materials create permanent magnetism as in ferromagnets.
- The Stern-Gerlach experiment revealed spin by splitting a beam of silver atoms in an inhomogeneous magnetic field, demonstrating that spin is quantized.
