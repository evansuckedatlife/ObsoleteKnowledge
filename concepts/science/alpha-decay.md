## summary

**Alpha-decay** is a prevalent form of radioactive decay in which an unstable atomic [[nucleus]] disintegrates spontaneously by expelling an alpha particle, which consists of a tightly bound helium-4 core containing two protons and two neutrons. First classified by *Ernest Rutherford*, this nuclear transmutation reduces the atomic number of the parent radionuclide by two and decreases its mass number by four. The physical mechanism of **alpha-decay** provided a historic validation of [[quantum-mechanics]], demonstrating that alpha particles escape the attractive nuclear well through quantum barrier tunneling.

## you gotta know

- *Ernest Rutherford* discovered and named this process in 1899 based on its low penetrating power, later proving in 1908 with *Thomas Royds* that emitted alpha particles are doubly ionized helium-4 nuclei ($^4\text{He}^{2+}$).
- In 1928, *George Gamow*—and independently *Ronald Gurney* and *Edward Condon*—solved the theoretical paradox of decay energies by demonstrating that the [[wavefunction]] of the alpha particle tunnels through the high electrostatic Coulomb barrier.
- The empirical *Geiger-Nuttall law* reveals that the half-life of an alpha emitter is inversely and exponentially related to the kinetic energy of the ejected particle, meaning minor increases in disintegration energy produce enormous decreases in half-life.
- The nuclear transmutation strictly conserves nucleon number and electric charge, transmuting heavy radionuclides such as uranium-238 into thorium-234 while releasing millions of electronvolts of kinetic energy.
- Almost all alpha emitters are heavy nuclides clustered beyond bismuth on the periodic table, where electrostatic repulsion between large numbers of protons overcomes the short-range residual strong nuclear force.
- Due to their heavy mass and double positive charge, alpha particles deposit their kinetic energy over very short distances with high linear energy transfer, being stopped completely by a sheet of paper or the dead epidermis.
- Practical technologies utilizing this radiation include domestic smoke detectors that harness americium-241 to ionize air and radioisotope thermoelectric generators fueled by plutonium-238 to power deep-space exploratory probes.

## connections

- [[nucleus]] — the dense, positively charged core of an atom that undergoes mass shedding and structural transmutation during radioactive decay.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework of quantum tunneling without which alpha particles could never overcome the nuclear potential barrier.
- [[wavefunction]] — the mathematical amplitude function describing the probability of an alpha particle penetrating the repulsive Coulomb barrier.
- [[atom]] — the elemental chemical entity whose elemental identity shifts two positions to the left on the periodic table upon particle ejection.
- [[standard-model]] — describes the fundamental gauge interactions governing the competition between the strong nuclear force and electromagnetic repulsion.
- [[ampere]] — the standard unit of current used to measure the rate of charge transported by concentrated beams of emitted alpha radiation.
- [[albert-einstein]] — formulated the mass-energy equivalence principle that explains the nuclear mass deficit converted into particle kinetic energy.
