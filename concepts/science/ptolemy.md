## summary

**Ptolemy** (*Claudius Ptolemaeus*) was an influential Greco-Roman mathematician, astronomer, astrologer, and geographer who lived in Alexandria during the second century AD under the [[roman-empire]]. His landmark mathematical treatise, the *Almagest*, codified the classical [[geocentrism|geocentric model]] of the cosmos, which placed a stationary Earth at the center of the celestial spheres. This comprehensive mathematical framework governed astronomical thought across the Mediterranean, the [[middle-east]], and Europe for over fourteen hundred years until the [[scientific-revolution]].

## you gotta know

- Author of the *Almagest* (originally titled *Syntaxis Mathematica* and later known in Arabic as *al-Majisṭī*), the definitive thirteen-volume astronomical manual that established the mathematical orthodoxy of [[geocentrism]] for over a millennium.
- Explained the apparent retrograde motion and changing brightness of planets like [[jupiter]] through complex geometric mechanisms, asserting that each planet traveled along a small circular *epicycle* whose center rotated around Earth upon a larger circle called a *deferent*.
- Introduced the controversial *equant point*, an off-center geometric construction from which a planet's motion appeared to sweep out uniform angles, an innovation that accurately predicted planetary positions but compromised pure uniform circular motion and was later attacked by [[copernicus]].
- Compiled the foundational cartographic atlas *Geography* (*Geographia*), which documented the geographical coordinates of thousands of locations across the known world, popularized the system of terrestrial latitude and longitude, and introduced mathematical map projections that influenced navigators during the [[age-of-exploration]].
- Cataloged 1,022 stars across forty-eight classical constellations, synthesizing and expanding upon the earlier observational work of the Hellenistic astronomer *Hipparchus* while recording star brightness according to a magnitude scale.
- Wrote the foundational astrological treatise *Tetrabiblos* ("Four Books"), which attempted to transform judicial astrology into a respectable branch of natural philosophy by harmonizing celestial influence with Aristotelian physics and humoral theory.
- Formulated an early empirical law of optical refraction in his treatise *Optics*, measuring angles between air, water, and glass, and developed *Ptolemy's theorem* regarding cyclic quadrilaterals in Euclidean geometry to calculate accurate trigonometric chord tables for his astronomical calculations.

## connections

- [[geocentrism]] — the Earth-centered cosmological paradigm he codified into a predictive mathematical system.
- [[heliocentrism]] — the sun-centered model that eventually displaced the Ptolemaic universe.
- [[copernicus]] — Renaissance astronomer whose *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium* eliminated the equant and initiated the overthrow of Ptolemaic astronomy.
- [[galileo-galilei]] — Italian polymath whose telescopic discoveries of sunspots, lunar craters, and the phases of Venus provided direct physical refutations of the Ptolemaic model.
- [[jupiter]] — gas giant whose retrograde motion was modeled using Ptolemaic epicycles and whose Galilean moons proved that celestial bodies could orbit centers other than Earth.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical Mediterranean empire under whose rule he lived and conducted his observations in Roman Alexandria.
- [[ancient-greece]] — intellectual civilization whose traditions of Euclidean geometry, spherical trigonometry, and Aristotelian physics provided the foundation for his scientific works.
- [[islam]] — civilization whose medieval astronomers translated, tested, and critiqued his texts, transmitting them back to Europe under the title *Almagest*.
