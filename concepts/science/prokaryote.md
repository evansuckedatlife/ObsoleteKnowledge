## summary

A **prokaryote** is a single-celled organism lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, encompassing bacteria and archaea. Prokaryotes evolved billions of years before eukaryotes and dominate Earth's biosphere in numbers and metabolic diversity; they occupy nearly every environment from deep ocean vents to human stomachs, and they drive critical biogeochemical cycles like nitrogen fixation and photosynthesis in the oceans.

## you gotta know

- Prokaryotes lack a nucleus; their DNA is a circular chromosome located in the nucleoid region, not enclosed by a membrane, and often accompanied by plasmids (small, circular DNA molecules carrying extra genes).
- The cell membrane is the key barrier; prokaryotic cell walls (made of peptidoglycan in bacteria, distinct lipids in archaea) sit outside and provide rigidity, making their cells smaller and more efficient than eukaryotic cells.
- Many prokaryotes possess *flagella* (whip-like structures) or *pili* (hair-like appendages) for motility and attachment; these are fundamentally different in structure from eukaryotic flagella, reflecting their prokaryotic ancestry.
- Prokaryotes reproduce asexually via *binary fission* (splitting into two identical daughters), giving them exponential growth rates and rapid adaptation; they also exchange genetic material via horizontal gene transfer (picking up DNA from their environment or neighbors).
- *Photosynthetic prokaryotes* (cyanobacteria) liberate molecular oxygen as a waste product, fundamentally reshaping Earth's atmosphere in the *Great Oxygenation Event* around 2.4 billion years ago.
- The ribosome in prokaryotes (70S) differs structurally from eukaryotic ribosomes (80S), making it an antibiotic target: many antibiotics exploit this difference to kill bacteria while leaving human cells untouched.
- *Archaea*, once thought to be extreme-environment oddities, are now known to be ubiquitous; they possess cell membranes with ether linkages (not ester), making them structurally distinct from bacteria despite superficial morphological similarity.
- Prokaryotes collectively have genomes 5–10 million base pairs (much smaller than eukaryotic genomes of billions), enabling compact, streamlined metabolism and rapid generation times that allow bacterial populations to evolve antibiotic resistance in months.

## connections

- [[cell]] — prokaryotes are the simpler cell type that came first.
- [[bacteria]] — the most abundant prokaryotes, critical to human microbiomes and biotechnology.
- [[dna]] — prokaryotic DNA is structured differently (circular, in the nucleoid) than eukaryotic DNA.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — prokaryotic ribosomes perform translation using the same genetic code but a structurally distinct machine.
- [[photosynthesis]] — cyanobacteria perform oxygenic photosynthesis, altering planetary chemistry.
- [[enzyme]] — prokaryotes produce enzymes for industrial use (CRISPR cas9, heat-stable polymerases).
