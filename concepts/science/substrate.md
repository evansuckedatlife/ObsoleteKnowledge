## summary

A **substrate** is the molecule or molecules upon which an enzyme acts to catalyze a chemical reaction. Enzymes are highly specific for their substrates, recognizing them through shape and chemical complementarity; the substrate binds to the enzyme's active site, undergoes chemical transformation, and is released as product. This substrate-enzyme interaction is the fundamental mechanism of biochemical regulation and metabolism.

## you gotta know

- The enzyme-substrate complex forms when a substrate binds to the *active site* of an enzyme; specificity arises from complementary shape (*lock-and-key model*) and chemical properties.
- *Substrate concentration* affects reaction rate: at low concentrations, rate increases linearly; at high concentrations, the enzyme becomes saturated and rate plateaus (*Michaelis-Menten kinetics*).
- Enzymes are selective: *lactase* breaks down lactose but not other sugars; *pepsin* cleaves proteins but not lipids; this specificity allows precise metabolic control.
- The enzyme stabilizes the transition state of the reaction, lowering activation energy and accelerating the reaction by factors of 10⁶ to 10¹² relative to the uncatalyzed rate.
- *Competitive inhibitors* compete with substrate for the active site; *non-competitive inhibitors* bind elsewhere on the enzyme and reduce its efficiency without competing.
- Examples: *glucose* is the substrate for hexokinase (first step of glycolysis); *hydrogen peroxide* is the substrate for catalase (detoxification).
- Substrate depletion or accumulation of product can regulate enzyme activity, linking metabolism to cellular needs.
