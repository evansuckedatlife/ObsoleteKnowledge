# Dwarf Planet

## summary

**A dwarf planet** is a celestial body that orbits the Sun, possesses sufficient mass to achieve hydrostatic equilibrium (round shape), but has not cleared its orbital neighborhood of other debris. The category was created in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union; [[Pluto]], once the ninth planet, was famously reclassified as a dwarf planet, alongside [[Ceres]] and other icy bodies, fundamentally altering the Solar System's taxonomy and igniting ongoing debate among astronomers.

## you gotta know

- The *IAU definition* of a planet requires three conditions: orbit the Sun, possess enough mass to be round, and have *cleared the neighborhood* around its orbit of other objects—removing gravitational competitors.
- *Dwarf planets* satisfy the first two criteria but fail the third: they orbit in regions with other similar bodies (Kuiper Belt objects, asteroids) and have not achieved gravitational dominance.
- *[[Pluto]]* was reclassified from planet to dwarf planet in 2006; it shares its orbital region (the Kuiper Belt) with thousands of similar-sized or larger icy bodies, violating the neighborhood-clearing requirement.
- *[[Ceres]]*, the largest asteroid in the asteroid belt, was reclassified from asteroid to dwarf planet in 2006 under the same decision; it comprises about one-third of the asteroid belt's total mass.
- Other recognized dwarf planets include *Haumea*, *Makemake*, and *Eris* (more distant than Pluto and more massive, with highly elliptical orbits); dozens more are suspected and await confirmation.
- *Eris*, discovered in 2005, prompted the IAU's reclassification debate; its discovery showed that Pluto was not unique and that many Kuiper Belt objects were comparable in size, making "planet" scientifically ambiguous.
- Dwarf planets are scientifically interesting as *preserved remnants* of the Solar System's formation; their compositions and orbits reveal the conditions in the protoplanetary disk and the history of gravitational interactions.

## connections

- [[pluto]] — the iconic dwarf planet, former ninth planet, and prototype for the category.
- [[ceres]] — the largest asteroid and a dwarf planet, dominating the asteroid belt.
- [[kuiper-belt]] — the region beyond Neptune populated by dwarf planets and icy bodies.
- [[solar-system]] — dwarf planets are integral members of the Solar System's architecture.
- [[asteroid-belt]] — the region between Mars and Jupiter containing asteroids and the dwarf planet Ceres.
