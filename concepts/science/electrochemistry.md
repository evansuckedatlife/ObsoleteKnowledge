## summary

**Electrochemistry** is the branch of physical chemistry that studies the relationship between electrical potential difference and identifiable chemical change involving the movement of charge. Emerging into prominence during the *Enlightenment* and expanding dramatically across the *Industrial Revolution*, it encompasses phenomena driven by an applied electric potential as well as those that generate electric currents spontaneously. Mastery of these interfacial redox processes forms the foundation of modern chemical manufacturing, energy storage devices, bioelectric sensing, and materials refinement.

## you gotta know

- Governed fundamentally by oxidation-reduction reactions occurring across the physical interface between an electronic conductor, known as an electrode, and an ionic conductor, known as an electrolyte.
- Spontaneous chemical reactions in galvanic or voltaic cells convert chemical energy into usable electrical work, forming the working operational principle behind dry batteries and rechargeable storage accumulators.
- Non-spontaneous reactions are forced to proceed in electrolytic cells by supplying external electrical energy, governed quantitatively by *Michael Faraday*'s laws of electrolysis.
- The standard electrode potential quantifies the thermodynamic driving force for reduction relative to the standard hydrogen electrode, whose potential is fixed by convention at zero volts.
- The *Nernst equation* determines the equilibrium electromotive force of an electrochemical cell under non-standard conditions as a function of temperature, transferred charges, and reaction quotients.
- Crucial for industrial isolation of reactive alkali metals, such as the molten-salt electrolysis of *sodium* chloride inside the specialized Downs cell.
- The kinetics of charge-transfer reactions across electrode interfaces are described mathematically by the *Butler-Volmer equation*, modeling overpotential against current density.
- Essential for extracting elements through the *Hall-Héroult process* to produce aluminum, as well as protecting infrastructures against spontaneous galvanic corrosion.

## connections

- [[electron]] — the fundamental charge carriers whose transfer between donor and acceptor species drives all redox transformations.
- [[sodium]] — an alkali metal isolated on an industrial scale via molten-salt electrolysis inside the Downs cell.
- [[oxygen]] — liberated at the anode via water oxidation in aqueous electrolytic systems and fuel cell operations.
- [[electroluminescence]] — an optical phenomenon where non-thermal photon generation occurs via electric excitation in semiconductors.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical era accelerated by the development of continuous electrochemical power and metallurgy.
- [[resistor]] — an electrical circuit component that regulates current flow through external electrochemical measurement circuits.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical entity that exchanges valence electrons to form ions during redox events.
