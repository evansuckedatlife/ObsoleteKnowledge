## summary

**Asaph Hall** (1829–1907) was an American astronomer who discovered the two moons of Mars, *Phobos* and *Deimos*, in 1877 at the U.S. Naval Observatory. His discoveries dramatically expanded humanity's understanding of planetary systems and demonstrated that Mars, like Earth, possessed natural satellites orbiting at predictable intervals. Hall's meticulous observational work established him as one of the leading astronomers of the 19th century, profoundly influencing planetary science.

## you gotta know

- Discovered Phobos and Deimos within days of each other in August 1877 during a close approach of Mars to Earth.
- Used the 26-inch refractor telescope at the U.S. Naval Observatory in Washington, D.C., the largest telescope in the world at that time.
- Named the moons after Greek mythological figures: Phobos (fear) and Deimos (panic), the attendants of Ares, the god of war.
- Phobos is unique among planetary moons for its rapid orbital period—it orbits Mars three times in a single Martian day.
- The discovery confirmed theoretical predictions and demonstrated the power of large telescopes in planetary astronomy.
- Also observed Saturn's rings and Jupiter's moons, contributing extensively to planetary science during the telescopic era.
- His work predated space probes by nearly a century, making his observations the sole source of Mars data for generations.
- After his death, the International Astronomical Union honored him by naming a lunar crater after him.
- Hall's discoveries fundamentally changed scientific understanding of planetary systems and demonstrated that multi-moon systems were surprisingly common throughout our solar system.
