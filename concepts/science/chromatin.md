## summary

**Chromatin** is the macromolecular complex of genomic DNA, basic histone proteins, and non-histone regulatory factors found in the nucleus of eukaryotic cells. Its fundamental architectural role is to package long linear genomic molecules into compact structures that fit inside the nuclear envelope while preventing mechanical entanglement. By dynamically shifting between open, accessible conformations and dense, repressed assemblies, it exerts critical control over transcription, replication, and DNA repair.

## you gotta know

- The basic repeating structural subunit is the *nucleosome*, which resembles a bead on a string and wraps approximately 147 base pairs of DNA.
- Nucleosome core particles consist of an octamer of basic *histone* proteins containing two copies each of the core dimers *H2A*, *H2B*, *H3*, and *H4*.
- The linker histone *H1* binds to the entry and exit sites of the nucleosome core, helping compact the 10-nanometer fiber into higher-order 30-nanometer structures.
- Divided into loosely packed, transcriptionally active *euchromatin* and densely packed, transcriptionally silent *heterochromatin*.
- Heterochromatin is further classified into *constitutive heterochromatin*, which remains permanently condensed at centromeres and telomeres, and reversible *facultative heterochromatin*.
- Epigenetic post-translational modifications of histone amino-terminal tails, such as *acetylation*, *methylation*, and *phosphorylation*, regulate accessibility to transcriptional machinery.
- Condenses dramatically into discrete, cytologically visible *chromosomes* during prophase of mitosis and meiosis to ensure faithful chromosomal segregation.
- First described and coined in the 1870s and 1880s by German cytologist *Walther Flemming*, who stained cellular nuclei with basic aniline dyes.

## connections

- [[dna]] — the negative-charged nucleic acid polymer that winds around positive histone octamers.
- [[protein]] — constitutes the histone core octamers and non-histone architectural scaffolding of chromatin.
- [[nucleus]] — the membrane-bound organelle of eukaryotic cells housing the entire nuclear chromatin genome.
- [[cell-cycle]] — coordinates the regulated condensation and decondensation of chromatin during interphase and mitosis.
- [[cell]] — the fundamental living unit whose nuclear processes rely on proper genomic chromatin compaction.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — influenced upstream by the epigenetic chromatin remodeling that dictates gene expression and messenger RNA synthesis.
