## summary

**Cardiac physiology** is the scientific study of the biological mechanisms governing the function, electrical conduction, and mechanical action of the heart. Originating as a formalized discipline during the nineteenth century, it explains how cardiac muscle cells generate rhythmic contractions to pump blood throughout the circulatory system. Understanding these principles is foundational to modern cardiology, diagnostics, and hemodynamic pharmacology.

## you gotta know

- Electrical conduction originates intrinsically in the *sinoatrial node* (*SA node*), commonly recognized as the physiological pacemaker of the mammalian heart.
- Cardiac action potentials propagate from the atria through the *atrioventricular node* (*AV node*), which delays conduction to ensure complete ventricular filling before ventricular contraction begins.
- Rapid impulse distribution through the ventricles is facilitated by the specialized bundle branches and terminal *Purkinje fibers*.
- The *Frank-Starling law of the heart* dictates that the force of ventricular contraction is directly proportional to the end-diastolic volume and initial stretching of myocardial muscle fibers.
- In contrast to skeletal muscle cells, ventricular myocytes exhibit a prolonged plateau phase (Phase 2) governed by the inward movement of calcium ions through L-type channels.
- Depolarization and repolarization waves traversing the heart are routinely measured via surface electrodes using electrocardiography, yielding distinct *P*, *QRS*, and *T* waveforms.
- Cardiac output is defined quantitatively as the product of stroke volume and heart rate, modulated continually by autonomic nerve input.

## connections

- [[cell]] — individual cardiomyocytes and pacemaker cells that form cardiac tissue and conduct action potentials.
- [[protein]] — contractile elements like actin and myosin, as well as regulatory troponin complexes.
- [[sodium]] — the ion responsible for the initial rapid Phase 0 depolarization spike in working myocytes.
- [[oxygen]] — essential gas delivered to myocardial tissue through coronary vessels to sustain aerobic respiration.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of rapid technological and medical instrumentation that enabled modern physiological measurement.
