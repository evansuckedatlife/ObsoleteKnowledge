## summary

The **Cambrian explosion**, alternatively termed the **Cambrian radiation** or the metaphorical **biological Big Bang**, was an unprecedented evolutionary event occurring approximately 538 million years ago during the early Paleozoic era. Across a geologically brief interval of roughly twenty to twenty-five million years, nearly all major modern animal body plans and phyla emerged simultaneously in the fossil record. This dramatic evolutionary diversification fundamentally reorganized marine ecologies, transitioning multicellular life from simple benthic mats into complex, tiered predator-prey ecosystems characterized by mineralized exoskeletons and active locomotion.

## you gotta know

- Occurred during the early Cambrian period roughly 538 to 515 million years ago, marking the definitive boundary between the Ediacaran and Cambrian stages.
- Documented extensively within exceptional preservation Lagerstätten, most famously the Canadian *Burgess Shale* and the Chinese *Maotianshan Shales* of Chengjiang.
- Witnessed the sudden diversification of major bilateral metazoan body plans, establishing ancestor lineages for arthropods, chordates, molluscs, and echinoderms.
- Driven ecologically and environmentally by rising oceanic levels of dissolved [[oxygen]], expanding developmental gene networks, and escalating predatory arms races.
- Yielded bizarre morphological forms such as the five-eyed predator *Opabinia*, the spiked *Hallucigenia*, and the apex predatory stem-arthropod *Anomalocaris*.
- Accompanied by the rapid evolution of hard biomineralized armor, complex compound eyes, active predatory behaviors, and deep sediment burrowing.
- Investigated extensively by paleontologists such as *Charles Doolittle Walcott*, who discovered the Burgess Shale in 1909, and popularized by *Stephen Jay Gould* in *Wonderful Life*.

## connections

- [[evolution]] — represents one of the most rapid and expansive macroevolutionary radiations preserved across the terrestrial fossil record.
- [[cell]] — underwent sophisticated multicellular differentiation into specialized organ systems, nervous networks, and sensory tissues.
- [[oxygen]] — accumulated within shallow marine shelves to exceed critical physiological thresholds necessary for high-metabolism active predation.
- [[protein]] — formed the structural collagen matrices and enzymatic machinery required to secrete hard calcium carbonate and phosphate shells.
- [[dna]] — housed the homeobox regulatory gene clusters whose expansion and duplication permitted rapid modular body plan modifications.
- [[cell-cycle]] — regulated the coordinated tissue division and embryonic morphogenesis that constructed complex bilaterian anatomical architectures.
