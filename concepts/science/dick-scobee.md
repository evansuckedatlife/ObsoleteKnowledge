## summary

**Dick Scobee** was an American aviator, engineer, and astronaut who served as the spacecraft commander on the fateful final flight of the Space Shuttle *Challenger* in 1986. Before entering the civilian space program, he flew combat missions during the Vietnam War and worked as an experimental test pilot for the *United States Air Force*. His leadership during mission STS-41-C accomplished the first on-orbit satellite repair, but he is most universally remembered for his courage and tragic death leading the STS-51-L crew during the space race of the late [[cold-war]].

## you gotta know

- Commanded the Space Shuttle *Challenger* mission STS-51-L, which suffered a catastrophic structural breakup 73 seconds after launch on January 28, 1986.
- Flew his first space mission in April 1984 as the pilot of STS-41-C aboard *Challenger*, successfully deploying the Long Duration Exposure Facility and retrieving the disabled Solar Maximum Mission satellite.
- Began his military career by enlisting in the *United States Air Force* as a reciprocating engine mechanic before earning his aerospace engineering degree and commission.
- Graduated from the prestigious USAF Aerospace Research Pilot School at Edwards Air Force Base, logging thousands of flight hours in dozens of military aircraft.
- Selected by [[nasa]] in January 1978 as part of Astronaut Group 8, nicknamed the "Thirty-Five New Guys," the very first astronaut class selected specifically for the shuttle program.
- posthumously awarded the Congressional Space Medal of Honor by the president of the [[united-states]] in recognition of his service and ultimate sacrifice.
- Remembered alongside teacher Christa McAuliffe and his fellow crewmembers through the nationwide establishment of Challenger Centers for Space Science Education by his surviving spouse, June Scobee Rodgers.
