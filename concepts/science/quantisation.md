## summary

**Quantisation** is the fundamental principle that certain physical quantities—particularly energy, angular momentum, and electric charge—can only take discrete values rather than continuous ranges. Discovered through quantum mechanics, quantisation reveals that at atomic scales, nature operates in fundamental "chunks" or quanta. This principle profoundly violates classical intuition and underlies nearly all of modern physics.

## you gotta know

- Energy is quantised: atoms can only occupy specific energy levels, not arbitrary energies; transitions between levels involve absorption or emission of photons with precise frequencies.
- Angular momentum is quantised: electrons in atoms have orbital angular momenta that are integer multiples of ℏ (h-bar), explaining why atoms remain stable and don't collapse into the nucleus.
- Electric charge is quantised: all charge in the universe is an integer multiple of the elementary charge (e ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs), a fact demonstrated by Millikan's oil-drop experiment.
- Planck's constant (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) sets the scale of quantisation; the quantum of energy is E = hf, where f is frequency.
- Quantisation explains atomic spectra: atoms absorb and emit photons of only specific wavelengths because transitions occur only between discrete energy levels.
- The Stern-Gerlach experiment demonstrates quantisation of spin angular momentum: a beam of atoms splits into discrete spots, not a continuous spread, revealing quantised spin states.
- Quantisation is not merely an approximation at small scales; it is a fundamental feature of nature, giving rise to chemistry, solid-state physics, and most of modern technology.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework predicting and explaining quantisation.
- [[energy-level]] — the discrete energy states that atoms and molecules can occupy due to quantisation.
