## summary

**Collagen** is the primary structural fibrous *[[protein]]* found in the extracellular matrix of animal connective tissues, representing roughly thirty percent of total mammalian protein mass. Composed of a distinctive right-handed triple helix assembled from three polypeptide chains rich in glycine and proline, it imparts tensile strength and structural integrity to skin, bone, tendons, cartilage, and vasculature. Biosynthesis requires enzymatic hydroxylation mediated by *vitamin C*, making its molecular biology central to the pathology of connective tissue disorders such as scurvy and *osteogenesis imperfecta*.

## you gotta know

- Constitutes the most abundant single *[[protein]]* in the animal kingdom, serving as the foundational scaffold of vertebrate connective architecture.
- Exhibits an unusual primary sequence characterized by recurring Gly-X-Y tripeptide repeats, where X and Y are frequently proline and hydroxyproline.
- Glycine must appear at every third residue because its tiny hydrogen side chain is the only group small enough to fit inside the crowded core of the triple helix.
- Formed by the assembly of three polyproline type II left-handed helices coiled together into a rigid, right-handed superhelical monomer termed *tropocollagen*.
- Post-translational hydroxylation of proline and lysine residues by *[[enzyme]]* catalysts requires molecular *[[oxygen]]*, ferrous iron, alpha-ketoglutarate, and ascorbic acid.
- Deficiency in *vitamin C* prevents stable cross-linking by prolyl hydroxylase, impairing extracellular assembly and provoking the systemic tissue breakdown of scurvy.
- Extracellular cross-linking between fibrils is catalyzed by lysyl oxidase, conferring higher tensile strength per unit weight than structural steel.
- Genetic mutations in type I collagen genes *COL1A1* or *COL1A2* disrupt triple-helix folding, causing the brittle-bone disease *osteogenesis imperfecta*.

## connections

- [[protein]] — the macro-biomolecule class to which fibrous collagen and its tropocollagen subunits belong.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — the ribosomal translation process responsible for generating initial pre-procollagen polypeptide chains.
- [[cell]] — the fundamental unit, particularly fibroblasts and osteoblasts, that synthesizes and secretes procollagen.
- [[enzyme]] — the biological catalysts like prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl oxidase necessary for processing and cross-linking.
- [[carbon]] — the foundational backbone element of amino acid residues comprising the polypeptide strands.
- [[oxygen]] — the cosubstrate required by hydroxylase enzymes during essential post-translational modifications.
- [[evolution]] — the development of multicellular metazoan body plans dependent on extracellular matrix adhesion.
