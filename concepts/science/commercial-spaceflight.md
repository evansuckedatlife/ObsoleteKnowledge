## summary

**Commercial spaceflight** refers to the development, manufacturing, and operation of orbital and suborbital spacecraft by privately owned commercial entities rather than governmental agencies. Historically, human space exploration and satellite deployment were exclusive monopolies of sovereign nations driven by geopolitical rivalries such as the [[space-race]] and institutional efforts like the [[apollo-program]]. Spurred by legal deregulation and investment from venture capital, the modern sector, often termed **NewSpace**, relies on fixed-price procurement contracts from public institutions such as [[nasa]]. By prioritizing vehicle reusability, vertical manufacturing integration, and competitive market pricing, the commercial space industry has dramatically decreased the cost of sending mass into orbit, revitalizing space exploration and creating an expanding global space economy.

## you gotta know

- [[spacex]], founded by [[elon-musk]], pioneered orbital booster reuse with the [[falcon-rocket]] family, dramatically reducing commercial launch prices and dominating global orbital lift capacity.
- NASA transformed aerospace procurement by establishing the Commercial Orbital Transportation Services (*COTS*) and Commercial Resupply Services (*CRS*) programs, contracting private firms as commercial logistics providers rather than purchasing government-owned hardware on cost-plus contracts.
- The [[dragon-spacecraft]] made history during the *CRS-1* mission as the first commercial vehicle to berth with the [[international-space-station]], inaugurating routine private cargo delivery in low Earth orbit.
- NASA's Commercial Crew Program ended post-Space Shuttle reliance on the [[soviet-union]]'s successor state and its *Soyuz* spacecraft by funding the *Crew Dragon* and Boeing's *CST-100 Starliner* to transport astronauts.
- Suborbital space tourism emerged through private aerospace ventures such as *Blue Origin*, with its reusable *New Shepard* rocket, and *Virgin Galactic*, with its air-launched *SpaceShipTwo* spaceplane.
- Legislative frameworks in the [[united-states]], beginning with the *Commercial Space Launch Act of 1984* and continuing through the *Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act of 2015*, established regulatory pathways for private launches and recognized private rights to resources extracted from celestial bodies.
- The private space sector received an early financial and technical catalyst in 2004 when the *Ansari X Prize* was won by *Scaled Composites* and aerospace designer *Burt Rutan* with *SpaceShipOne*, the first privately funded crewed vehicle to reach space.

## connections

- [[nasa]] — the government agency that catalyzed the industry by transitioning from spacecraft operator to commercial anchor customer.
- [[spacex]] — the commercial aerospace company that demonstrated the viability of private orbital rocketry and established market dominance in commercial launch.
- [[falcon-rocket]] — the family of partially reusable launch vehicles that achieved routine first-stage propulsive landings, drastically slashing orbital launch costs.
- [[international-space-station]] — the multinational orbital research laboratory that served as the primary destination and customer demand driver for commercial cargo and crew missions.
- [[dragon-spacecraft]] — the reusable private capsule that validated the commercial resupply concept and restored domestic astronaut launch capabilities to the [[united-states]].
- [[elon-musk]] — the industrialist whose aggressive vertical integration and risk-tolerant engineering philosophy accelerated the commercialization of orbital flight.
- [[space-race]] — the Cold War geopolitical competition that created state-run space programs, establishing the expensive government model that commercial spaceflight sought to disrupt.
- [[apollo-program]] — the historic government-directed lunar initiative whose cost-plus contracting structure served as the legacy procurement model replaced by commercial fixed-price agreements.
