## summary

The **Ediacara fauna** represents the earliest known complex, multicellular, macroscopic organisms that populated global marine environments during the late Neoproterozoic Era between roughly 575 and 541 million years ago. Preserved primarily as enigmatic impressions beneath microbial mats in shallow and deep marine sediments, these soft-bodied organisms exhibited strange quilted and frond-like morphologies quite distinct from modern animal body plans. Their rise and sudden decline immediately preceded the *Cambrian explosion*, making them crucial for understanding the evolutionary origins of animal life.

## you gotta know

- First discovered in significant diversity by *Reginald Sprigg* in 1946 in the *Ediacara Hills* of the *Flinders Ranges* in South Australia.
- Characterized predominantly by soft-bodied, macroscopic organisms preserved in sandstone and quartzite, often shielded by resistant microbial mats in a taphonomic style dubbed *Ediacaran-style preservation*.
- Featured iconic enigmatic taxa including the disk-shaped *Dickinsonia*, the tri-radially symmetrical *Tribrachidium*, the segmented crawler *Kimberella*, and rooted frondose organisms like *Charnia*.
- Divided paleontologically into three distinct chronological assemblages: the deep-water *Avalon assemblage*, the diverse *White Sea assemblage*, and the late-stage, carbonate-rich *Nama assemblage*.
- Possessed mysterious morphological body architectures termed *Vendobionta* by German paleontologist *Adolf Seilacher*, who argued many belonged to an extinct, modular, air-mattress-like branch of life.
- Yielded chemical biomarker fossils, such as fossilized cholesteroid lipid molecules extracted from *Dickinsonia* specimens, confirming their affinity as true eukaryotic metazoans.
- Vanished abruptly from the fossil record around 541 million years ago, likely due to a combination of evolutionary ecosystem engineering by burrowing animals, predation, and fluctuating oceanic conditions.
