## summary

An **alkaline earth metal** is any of the six divalent, highly electropositive chemical elements that constitute Group 2 of the periodic table: beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium, barium, and radium. Characterized by their silvery-white metallic luster, relatively low densities, and two valence electrons in their outermost *s*-orbital shell, these elements historically acquired their family name because their heat-resistant, basic oxides were classified as alkaline *earths* by early alchemists. Governed by fundamental principles of [[quantum-mechanics]] and progressive electron shielding, they bridge inorganic mineralogy with essential biochemistry, ranging from chlorophyll-mediated [[photosynthesis]] to the structural calcium scaffolding of vertebrate skeletons. In laboratory and industrial contexts, their distinctive chemical reactivity provides vital reagents for metallurgical extraction, pyrotechnics, and organic synthesis.

## you gotta know

- Characteristic flame emission colors identify several group members during qualitative chemical analysis, producing an orange-brick red flame for calcium, an intense crimson scarlet for strontium, and an apple-green hue for barium.
- Possess an outer ground-state valence electron configuration of $ns^2$, readily surrendering both electrons to achieve an octet noble-gas configuration and forming divalent $M^{2+}$ cations with an invariant +2 oxidation state.
- Exhibit predictable periodic trends descending the column: atomic and ionic radii increase while electronegativity and ionization energies decline, leading to greater reactivity with water alongside increasing hydroxide solubility and decreasing sulfate solubility.
- *Calcium* represents the most abundant metallic element in the human body, serving as the essential structural component of hydroxyapatite in teeth and bones while orchestrating intracellular signal transduction and [[muscle-contraction]].
- *Magnesium* coordinates the central porphyrin ring of chlorophyll to drive plant [[photosynthesis]], complexes with *ATP* to enable enzymatic phosphate transfers, and combines with organic halides to form nucleophilic carbon-carbon bond-forming *Grignard reagents*.
- *Barium sulfate* is routinely administered as a medical radiocontrast suspension for gastrointestinal X-ray imaging because its high [[electron]] density strongly attenuates X-rays, while its extreme insolubility prevents lethal heavy-metal toxicity.
- *Beryllium* displays an anomalous diagonal relationship with aluminum, forming covalent, highly toxic, and amphoteric compounds rather than basic ionic salts, a divergence caused by its small ionic radius and high charge density.
- *Radium* was famously isolated from pitchblende uranium ore by *Marie Curie* and *Pierre Curie* in 1898, whose intense alpha-particle radioactivity revolutionized early nuclear physics before its severe bone-seeking radiological hazards became understood.

## connections

- [[electron]] — the pair of outer valence particles whose ionization governs the family's reactivity, reduction potentials, and metallic conductivity.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental structural unit whose increasing nuclear charge and added electron shells dictate the group's classic periodic properties.
- [[sodium]] — the adjacent Group 1 alkali metal, illustrating the lower melting points, smaller ionization energies, and monovalent state of the preceding column.
- [[oxygen]] — combines readily with Group 2 metals at elevated temperatures to yield basic oxides, stable peroxides, and caustic alkaline hydroxides.
- [[carbon]] — binds magnesium directly in synthetic organometallic chemistry and joins calcium to form widespread sedimentary limestone and marble formations.
- [[enzyme]] — biological macromolecule that universally exploits divalent magnesium and calcium cofactors to stabilize catalytic transition states and coordinate substrates.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical foundation explaining the energetic stability of the filled $ns^2$ orbital and the discrete emission lines in flame tests.
