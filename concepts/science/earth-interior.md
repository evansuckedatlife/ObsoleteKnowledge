## summary

**Earth interior** encompasses the concentric geosphere layers extending from the planet's brittle outer crust down to its dense metallic core at a depth of over 6,370 kilometres. Stratified both compositionally into crust, mantle, and core, and rheologically into lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, and inner and outer cores, this deep system governs terrestrial geodynamics. Its internal heat budget, sustained by primordial accretion energy and radioactive decay, powers mantle convection, planetary volcanism, continental drift, and the geodynamo that generates the protective geomagnetic field.

## you gotta know

- Stratified compositionally into a buoyant silicate crust, a vast peridotite and bridgmanite mantle, and a dense iron-nickel core.
- Divided mechanically into the rigid outer lithosphere, the ductile convecting asthenosphere, the solid mesosphere, the liquid outer core, and the solid inner core.
- Probed and mapped primarily through observational seismology by tracking the propagation, refraction, and reflection of compressional *P-waves* and shear *S-waves*.
- Features the famous *Mohorovičić discontinuity*, or Moho, marking the compositional boundary and seismic velocity jump separating the crust from the underlying upper mantle.
- Contains the liquid outer core, whose turbulent thermal and compositional convection driven by planetary rotation operates the self-sustaining *geodynamo* responsible for Earth's magnetic field.
- Displays the *Gutenberg discontinuity* at roughly 2,900 kilometres depth, where S-waves cease propagating entirely because liquids cannot transmit shear stress, revealing the outer core boundary.
- Harbors the deepest structural province at the core-mantle boundary known as the *D'' layer*, which hosts large low-shear-velocity provinces and ultralow-velocity zones where mantle plumes originate.
- Reaches temperatures exceeding 5,000 kelvins and pressures topping 360 gigapascals in the solid inner core, where iron crystallizes into a hexagonal close-packed lattice discovered by Danish seismologist *Inge Lehmann*.

## connections

- [[earth-formation]] — the initial accretionary event and subsequent iron catastrophe that established the planet's fundamental internal compositional layering.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical species whose electronic configurations and crystal lattices dictate mineral stability across extreme deep-Earth pressures.
- [[oxygen]] — the most abundant element by mass and number within the silicate mineral structures of the mantle and crust.
- [[sodium]] — a major lithophile alkali metal concentrated in low-density feldspar minerals within the differentiated continental crust.
- [[electron]] — the subatomic particle whose orbital transitions and high-pressure metallic screening properties govern electrical conductivity in the core.
- [[nucleus]] — the atomic core whose unstable isotopes of uranium, thorium, and potassium release the radiogenic decay heat powering mantle convection.
