## summary

**Capacitance**, including both **electrical capacitance**, **self-capacitance**, and **mutual capacitance**, is the physical property of an electric conductor or system of conductors that quantifies its ability to store separated electric charge per unit change in electrical potential. Formally defined by the ratio of charge to voltage, it depends strictly upon the geometric arrangement of conductive elements and the permittivity of the intervening insulating medium. Foundational to classical electromagnetism and electrical circuits, it governs phenomena ranging from high-voltage energy storage to signal propagation across semiconductor microchips.

## you gotta know

- Defined mathematically as charge divided by electric potential difference, expressed by the foundational relation *C = Q / V*.
- Measured in the *SI* derived unit of the farad (*F*), named in honour of British experimental physicist *Michael Faraday*.
- Depends directly on the surface area of opposing conducting plates and inversely on the physical separation distance between them.
- Increased proportionally by inserting an insulating dielectric material between electrodes, which polarizes bound atomic charges to weaken the interior electric field.
- Stores potential energy within the electrostatic field established between charged surfaces, proportional to capacitance and the square of voltage.
- Dictates transient voltage response when combined in networks with a [[resistor]], establishing the characteristic exponential relaxation time constant.
- Emerged historically during the early [[industrial-revolution]] following the invention of the *Leyden jar* by *Ewald Georg von Kleist* and *Pieter van Musschenbroek*.

## connections

- [[capacitor-filter]] — exploits high electrical capacitance to smooth rectified alternating currents into stable direct-current power.
- [[resistor]] — pairs with capacitive components to form fundamental *RC* timing networks, differentiators, and frequency-selective filters.
- [[electron]] — provides the elementary negative electric charge that gathers on negative electrodes to establish potential differences.
- [[atom]] — contains the bound orbital charges that undergo microscopic polarization within intervening dielectric media.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — explains the microscopic quantum capacitance and tunnelling limits governing nanoscale semiconductor gates.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — fostered the technical telegraphy, early dynamos, and industrial power grids that demanded commercial capacitors.
