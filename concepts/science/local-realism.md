## summary

**Local realism** is the classical worldview combining locality—the principle that physical interactions cannot propagate faster than the speed of light—with realism, the assumption that physical entities possess definite, objective properties prior to and independent of measurement. Articulated most famously in 1935 by [[albert-einstein]], *Boris Podolsky*, and *Nathan Rosen* in their seminal *EPR paper*, the concept served as the conceptual cornerstone for arguing that the probabilistic description provided by [[quantum-mechanics]] was incomplete and required underlying local hidden variables. The principle represents one of the most profound battlegrounds in modern physics, ultimately challenged in 1964 when [[john-bell]] proved that no local realistic theory could reproduce all quantum predictions, a theoretical divergence later confirmed by groundbreaking experiments on [[quantum-entanglement]].

## you gotta know

- The 1935 *Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) paradox* used the thought experiment of spatially separated entangled particles to argue that if [[local-realism]] holds, [[quantum-mechanics]] must be incomplete because measurement of one particle cannot instantly alter the physical reality of another.
- [[albert-einstein]] rejected violations of locality as *spooky action at a distance* (*spukhafte Fernwirkung*), maintaining that deterministic hidden variables must explain correlations between separated particles without superluminal communication or signaling.
- In 1964, Northern Irish physicist [[john-bell]] mathematically formalized the joint assumptions of locality and counterfactual definiteness into [[bell-inequality]], establishing an absolute upper limit on statistical correlations achievable by any local realistic theory.
- The *CHSH inequality*, derived in 1969 by *John Clauser*, *Michael Horne*, *Abner Shimony*, and *Richard Holt*, adapted Bell's theoretical theorem into experimentally testable mathematical bounds based on coincident polarization detections of the [[photon]].
- Pioneering optical experiments conducted by *John Clauser* in 1972 and *Alain Aspect* in 1982 demonstrated unambiguous violations of Bell-type inequalities, culminating in the 2022 *Nobel Prize in Physics* awarded to *Aspect*, *Clauser*, and *Anton Zeilinger* for establishing the failure of [[local-realism]].
- Physicists systematically eliminated experimental loopholes that could preserve local realism, specifically closing the *locality loophole* through ultra-fast random measurement settings and closing the *detection loophole* using high-efficiency superconducting detectors.
- Rigorous loophole-free Bell tests were finally achieved in 2015 by research teams in *Delft*, *Vienna*, and *Boulder*, while subsequent tests of *Leggett's inequality* revealed that even broad classes of non-local realistic theories conflict with observed quantum behavior.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the foundational theory whose probabilistic nature and entanglement predictions fundamentally violate local realistic assumptions.
- [[albert-einstein]] — prominent physicist who co-authored the 1935 EPR paper and championed local realism against the Copenhagen interpretation.
- [[john-bell]] — Northern Irish theorist who derived the mathematical theorem demonstrating that quantum predictions cannot be reproduced by local realism.
- [[bell-inequality]] — set of mathematical bounds derived from local realism whose experimental violations conclusively rule out local hidden-variable models.
- [[quantum-entanglement]] — non-classical physical phenomenon connecting distant particles whose correlated measurements demonstrate that nature cannot be both local and real.
- [[photon]] — quantum particle of light whose entangled polarization states served as the primary physical system in experimental Bell tests.
- [[wavefunction]] — mathematical object in quantum mechanics whose instantaneous state reduction upon observation conflicts directly with relativistic locality.
