## summary

**Climate** is the long-term average pattern of temperature, precipitation, wind, and other atmospheric conditions in a region, typically computed over decades or centuries. Distinct from weather (which varies day-to-day), climate emerges from the interaction of solar radiation, atmospheric composition, ocean currents, and surface albedo. Climate zones (tropical, temperate, polar) determine biome distribution and human habitability, while climate change—driven by greenhouse gas emissions—poses a defining challenge to modern civilization.

## you gotta know

- Climate is defined by 30-year averages of temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind speed, and other metrics; weather is the instantaneous state and varies over days to weeks.
- Solar insolation (incoming solar radiation) drives climate; Earth's axial tilt (23.5°) creates seasons, and the tilt's precession (41,000-year cycle) influences ice-age cycles.
- Greenhouse effect: atmospheric gases (CO₂, CH₄, H₂O, N₂O) absorb outgoing longwave radiation, warming the atmosphere; increased greenhouse gas concentration amplifies warming.
- Ocean currents (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio Current, Antarctic Circumpolar Current) redistribute heat poleward, moderating climate extremes and making maritime regions milder than continental interiors.
- Albedo (surface reflectivity) affects climate: bright ice and snow reflect sunlight (high albedo), while dark ocean and forest absorb it (low albedo); melting ice caps reduce albedo, creating warming feedback.
- Atmospheric circulation patterns (trade winds, jet streams, monsoons) transport moisture and heat; the Coriolis effect deflects moving air, creating pressure systems and wind patterns.
- Climate classification systems (Köppen, Holdridge, Trewartha) categorize regions by temperature and precipitation; tropical wet, tropical dry, temperate, and polar zones each support distinct ecosystems.
- Climate change markers include rising global temperatures, sea-level rise (from thermal expansion and ice melt), shifting precipitation patterns, and increased frequency of extreme weather (hurricanes, droughts, floods).

## connections

- [[atmosphere]] — the medium containing greenhouse gases and circulating heat and moisture.

- [[meteorology]] — the science studying atmosphere and weather; climatology is its long-term counterpart.
