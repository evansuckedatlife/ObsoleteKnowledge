## summary

**Bjorken scaling** is a high-energy scattering phenomenon in particle physics where the internal structure functions of hadrons become independent of the momentum transfer scale. Predicted in 1968 by theorist *James Bjorken* and confirmed experimentally at the Stanford Linear Accelerator Center, it provided the first direct empirical evidence that nucleons are composite bodies rather than indivisible particles. By demonstrating that protons and neutrons contain point-like, fractionally charged constituent entities called partons, scaling laid the empirical cornerstone for the modern [[standard-model]].

## you gotta know

- Formulated by *James Bjorken* in 1968 using current algebra sum rules, predicting that deep inelastic scattering structure functions become invariant under scale transformations at asymptotically large energies.
- Experimentally confirmed between 1968 and 1969 in celebrated deep inelastic scattering experiments at SLAC led by *Jerome Friedman*, *Henry Kendall*, and *Richard Taylor*, earning them the 1990 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- Parameterized primarily by the dimensionless Bjorken scaling variable *x*, which represents the fraction of the parent nucleon's longitudinal momentum carried by the struck constituent.
- Physically interpreted by *Richard Feynman* through the parton model, which established that relativistic hadrons act during collisions as collections of quasi-free, point-like subparticles.
- Verified the Callan-Gross relation, demonstrating that the ratio of longitudinal to transverse structure functions vanishes and proving that partons carry spin-1/2, directly matching the properties of a [[quark]].
- Exhibits logarithmic scaling violations at higher energies and extreme kinematics, deviations that are precisely modeled by quantum chromodynamics via the DGLAP evolution equations.
- Contrasts fundamentally with the low-energy domain of [[quantum-mechanics]] where particles bind into composite shells, illustrating the onset of asymptotic freedom at short distance scales.

## connections

- [[quark]] — the fundamental spin-1/2 partons whose point-like existence was proven through scaling behavior.
- [[standard-model]] — the overarching framework of particle physics verified by early deep inelastic scattering measurements.
- [[electron]] — the lepton beam probe used at SLAC to scatter off target nucleons.
- [[nucleus]] — the composite nuclear core whose constituent nucleons were demonstrated to possess inner structure.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical foundation governing quantum states, wave-particle duality, and field operators in scattering.
- [[photon]] — the virtual gauge boson mediating electromagnetic momentum transfer between probe leptons and internal partons.
- [[bonding]] — the contrasting low-energy force regime where electromagnetic interactions assemble stable chemical compounds rather than quasi-free asymptotic partons.
