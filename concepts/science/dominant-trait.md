## summary

A **dominant trait** is an inherited phenotypic characteristic that is expressed in an organism whenever at least one copy of the corresponding dominant allele is present at a genetic locus. First rigorously articulated by *Gregor Mendel* through his hybridization experiments with garden peas in nineteenth-century Austria, this phenomenon forms a cornerstone of classical transmission genetics. Understanding dominance is essential for analyzing inheritance pedigrees, medical genetic disorders, and the transmission of evolutionary adaptations across successive generations.

## you gotta know

- Discovered by Augustinian friar *Gregor Mendel* during pea plant breeding experiments published in 1866, establishing the Law of Segregation and the Law of Independent Assortment.
- Masks the phenotypic manifestation of a recessive allele in a heterozygous genotype carrying one dominant and one recessive variant.
- Requires only a single copy of the causal allele to appear in the organism's physical phenotype, exhibiting complete penetrance in classical Mendelian schemes.
- Contrasts with codominance—in which both alleles in a heterozygote express fully, such as AB blood types—and incomplete dominance, which yields an intermediate phenotype like pink snapdragons.
- Often operates molecularly via haplosufficiency, where fifty percent of functional [[protein]] or [[enzyme]] product generated from the single intact allele suffices for a wild-type phenotype.
- Can occasionally arise from toxic gain-of-function or dominant-negative mutations, as exemplified by *Huntington's disease* or *achondroplasia* in human clinical genetics.
- Does not imply higher evolutionary fitness, superior biological frequency, or natural selection preference in a population compared to alternative recessive alleles.

## connections

- [[dna]] — stores the nucleotide sequence variations that encode alleles conferring dominant or recessive characteristics.
- [[protein]] — the functional macromolecules whose synthesis levels determine whether an allele behaves with haplosufficiency.
- [[enzyme]] — catalyst proteins often displaying complete dominance because half-normal concentrations adequately sustain metabolic fluxes.
- [[evolution]] — population-level dynamic where dominant traits change frequency based on fitness rather than dominance alone.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — cellular translation and transcription pathways through which dominant genetic messages are expressed.
- [[cell-cycle]] — regulated process of cellular duplication ensuring the accurate mitotic transmission of dominant alleles.
