## summary

**Platinum** (**Pt**) is a dense, malleable, ductile, and highly unreactive transition metal with atomic number 78, situated in group 10 and period 6 of the periodic table. Valued as one of the preeminent precious metals, it exhibits extraordinary resistance to chemical corrosion, high melting stability, and catalytic versatility. It plays an indispensable role in contemporary chemistry, vehicle emissions abatement, jewelry fabrication, and precision thermometry across international standards named after [[lord-kelvin]].

## you gotta know

- Possesses the chemical symbol **Pt** and atomic number 78, classifying it as a heavy noble metal of the transition block.
- Derives its modern designation from the Spanish diminutive *platina*, meaning "little silver," encountered by European explorers in the river gravels of the *Chocó* region of modern *Colombia*.
- Described scientifically in eighteenth-century Europe by the naval officer *Antonio de Ulloa* and metallurgist *Charles Wood*, having long been worked by pre-Columbian indigenous metallurgical artisans.
- Functions as an indispensable catalyst in automotive catalytic converters, accelerating the oxidation of hazardous [[carbon]] monoxide and unburnt hydrocarbons into benign [[carbon]] dioxide and water vapor.
- Serves as the core reactive component in chemotherapy antineoplastic agents such as *cisplatin*, *carboplatin*, and *oxaliplatin*, which coordinate to biological [[dna]] to cross-link strands and trigger apoptotic cancer death.
- Defines standard temperature measurement scales through the standard **platinum** resistance thermometer, calibrated against the absolute scale of [[lord-kelvin]].
- Exhibited historic prominence in metrology through the manufacture of the international prototype kilogram and meter artifacts, cast from an alloy composed of ninety percent **platinum** and ten percent iridium.
