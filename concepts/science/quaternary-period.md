## summary

The **Quaternary Period** is the most recent geological period, spanning roughly the last 2.6 million years from the Pliocene to the present day. Defined by cyclical ice ages interrupted by warmer interglacial periods, the Quaternary witnessed the rise of human civilization during its final millennia. This period reflects the instability of Earth's climate during an ice-house world and the dramatic megafauna extinctions that reshaped ecosystems worldwide.

## you gotta know

- The Quaternary is subdivided into the Pleistocene Epoch (2.6 million years ago to 11,700 years ago) and the Holocene Epoch (the modern interglacial to the present).
- Multiple glacial cycles dominated the Pleistocene, with massive ice sheets advancing and retreating across North America, Europe, and Asia approximately every 100,000 years.
- The most recent glacial maximum occurred about 20,000 years ago, when sea levels were hundreds of meters lower and ice covered much of the Northern Hemisphere.
- Quaternary climatic shifts drove human evolution and migration; humans adapted to diverse environments from African savannas to frozen tundra to Pacific islands.
- Megafauna extinctions—including woolly mammoths, saber-toothed cats, giant ground sloths, and giant wombats—occurred as climates warmed and human hunting pressure increased at the end of the Pleistocene.
- The interglacial warm period following the last ice age (approximately 11,700 years ago) enabled the development of agriculture and human civilization in Mesopotamia and beyond.
- Some scientists now classify the modern era as the Anthropocene, reflecting humanity's dominant influence on Earth's climate and ecosystems.
- Milankovitch cycles—variations in Earth's orbital eccentricity, axial tilt, and precession—explain the roughly 100,000-year glacial-interglacial rhythm of the Pleistocene.
- The Quaternary experienced dramatic environmental and ecological shifts: forests contracted during glacials, replaced by tundra and grasslands; megafauna evolved adaptations like thick hair and fat reserves.
