## summary

The **Hubble Space Telescope** (HST) is a space-based optical observatory launched by [[nasa]] in 1990 and deployed by the [[space-shuttle-program]], orbiting Earth at an altitude of ~570 km. Originally crippled by a spherical aberration in its primary mirror, it was repaired by astronauts in 1993 and has since become one of the most productive scientific instruments ever built, revolutionizing our understanding of the universe's age, expansion rate, and the prevalence of planets around other stars. Its longevity and continuous servicing by shuttle missions demonstrate the viability of space-based astronomy and human spaceflight for scientific discovery.

## you gotta know

- Launched in 1990 as the flagship of [[nasa]]'s Great Observatories program, but suffered from spherical aberration that severely blurred initial images.
- Named after astronomer *Edwin Hubble*, who discovered the expansion of the universe and measured galaxy distances in the 1920s.
- Repaired by [[space-shuttle-program]] astronauts in December 1993 when corrective optics were installed, restoring the telescope to full clarity.
- Observed the first definitive evidence of black holes at galactic centers and measured the supermassive black hole in *Andromeda*.
- Determined the Hubble Constant more precisely by observing Cepheid variable stars in distant galaxies, refining estimates of the universe's age.
- Detected the acceleration of cosmic expansion (attributed to dark energy), leading to the 1998 Nobel Prize in Physics discoveries.
- Captured iconic images including the Hubble Deep Field (1995), revealing thousands of galaxies in a tiny patch of sky.
- Serviced five times by [[space-shuttle-program]] astronauts between 1993 and 2009, installing new instruments and replacing degraded components.
- Operating at ultraviolet and visible wavelengths, Hubble cannot observe infrared radiation like its successor, the James Webb Space Telescope, but remains crucial for optical observations and as a calibration standard for modern astronomy.
- The 1998 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized Hubble observations showing the universe's accelerating expansion, which revealed the existence of dark energy comprising most of the universe's mass-energy content.
