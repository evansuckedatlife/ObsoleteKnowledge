## summary

**Brass** is a versatile substitutional alloy composed primarily of copper and zinc, recognized since antiquity for its golden appearance, mechanical workability, acoustic resonance, and resistance to corrosion. Produced historically through the cementation of copper with calamine ore, it catalyzed breakthroughs in metallurgy, precision instrument fabrication, and marine engineering. Its low friction, non-magnetic character, and biocidal oligodynamic properties make it an indispensable material spanning classical decorative arts, industrial machinery, and scientific instrumentation.

## you gotta know

- Composed of copper alloyed with zinc, with zinc concentrations typically varied from 5% to over 40% to modify mechanical ductility, tensile strength, and color.
- Exhibits distinct metallurgical phases depending on composition: alpha alloys with less than 37% zinc form ductile face-centered cubic structures suitable for cold-working, while duplex alpha-beta alloys above 37% zinc form stronger body-centered cubic structures optimized for hot-working.
- Manufactured in antiquity and the Middle Ages via calamine cementation, where zinc carbonate ore and charcoal were heated directly with copper before isolated elemental zinc was understood.
- Emitted a golden luster that caused classical civilizations to value it as *orichalcum*, using it extensively for imperial coinage, ornamental metalwork, and ceremonial armaments.
- Formed the material backbone of scientific instrument making, chosen for sextants, compasses, and clocks because it resists marine rust and will not disturb magnetic navigation needles.
- Became vital during the [[industrial-revolution]] for mass-producing interchangeable precision steam fittings, clockwork gears, plumb bobs, and military cartridge casings.
- Naturally resists microbial pathogens through the oligodynamic effect, wherein released copper ions disrupt cellular membranes and neutralize viral capsids.
