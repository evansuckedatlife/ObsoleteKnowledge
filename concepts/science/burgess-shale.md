## summary

The **Burgess Shale** is a celebrated Cambrian fossil deposit located in Yoho National Park within the Canadian Rockies of British Columbia. Discovered in 1909 by paleontologist *Charles Doolittle Walcott*, this middle Cambrian *Konservat-Lagerstätte* preserves one of the world's most exquisite records of early multicellular animal radiation dating to approximately 508 million years ago. Its exceptional preservation of non-mineralized tissues revealed an astonishing array of stem-group arthropods, enigmatic body plans, and early chordates that transformed scientific understanding of the Cambrian explosion.

## you gotta know

- Discovered along the ridge between Mount Field and Mount Wapta by *Charles Doolittle Walcott*, who quarried tens of thousands of specimens for the Smithsonian Institution.
- Famous for preserving soft-bodied organisms as thin, reflective carbon and aluminosilicate films, capturing delicate gut tracts, eyes, gills, and nervous systems rarely fossilized elsewhere.
- Underwent a historic reinterpretation during the 1970s led by *Harry Whittington*, *Derek Briggs*, and *Simon Conway Morris*, which demonstrated that many taxa represented extinct stem lineages rather than modern taxonomic classes.
- Popularized in *Stephen Jay Gould*'s 1989 volume *Wonderful Life*, which framed its bizarre morphological disparity as evidence for evolutionary contingency and widespread decimation.
- Yields iconic bizarre taxa including the five-eyed predator *Opabinia* with its frontal nozzle, the spine-legged lobopodian *Hallucigenia*, the armored crawler *Wiwaxia*, and the giant radiodont predator *Anomalocaris*.
- Houses critical early chordate specimens including *Pikaia gracilens*, displaying a rudimentary notochord, dorsal nerve cord, and segmented myomeres that predate the evolutionary emergence of [[bone]].
- Formed by underwater density currents and sediment mudslides depositing fauna into deep, anoxic waters at the base of the Cathedral Escarpment, preventing decay and scavenging.

## connections

- [[evolution]] — provides pivotal morphological evidence regarding body plan diversification, stem lineages, and disparity during the Cambrian radiation.
- [[cell]] — preserves subcellular and cellular histological structures, including digestive glands and muscle bundles, in non-biomineralized animals.
- [[bone]] — records early vertebrate ancestors like *Pikaia* flourishing long before the biomineralization of calcium-rich skeletal architectures.
- [[oxygen]] — low dissolved gas concentrations at the deposition floor halted aerobic decomposition and permitted exquisite carbonaceous film preservation.
- [[carbon]] — forms the elemental composition of the compressed dark films detailing soft anatomical membranes against shale matrices.
- [[dna]] — serves as an essential paleontological baseline for calibrating metazoan molecular clocks and phylogenetic divergence intervals.
