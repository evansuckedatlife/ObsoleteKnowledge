## summary

The **Columbia disaster** was the fatal disintegration of *[[nasa]]*'s Space Shuttle *Columbia* during atmospheric reentry on February 1, 2003, concluding the *STS-107* mission. All seven crew members were lost when superheated atmospheric plasma penetrated a breach in the orbiter's left wing, which had been damaged by insulating foam striking reinforced carbon-carbon panels during launch. The tragedy underscored severe systemic deficiencies within *[[nasa]]*'s risk assessment and safety protocols, ultimately precipitating the phased retirement of the Space Shuttle fleet.

## you gotta know

- Occurred on February 1, 2003, during the deorbit descent over Texas and Louisiana, only sixteen minutes prior to the scheduled landing at *Kennedy Space Center*.
- The mission, designated *STS-107*, was a dedicated microgravity and earth science flight crewed by seven astronauts, including *Ilan Ramon*, the first Israeli astronaut in space.
- Caused physically by a piece of insulating foam that shed from the bipod ramp of the external tank eighty-two seconds after liftoff, striking the leading edge of the left wing.
- The high-speed impact punctured the reinforced *[[carbon]]*-carbon panels, allowing atmospheric gases reaching thousands of degrees to hollow out the internal wing structure during reentry.
- Flight telemetry registered asymmetric drag and hydraulic loss moments before the orbiter experienced catastrophic structural failure and aerodynamic breakup.
- The independent *Columbia Accident Investigation Board* (*CAIB*), led by retired Admiral *Harold Gehman*, severely condemned organizational complacency and normalized deviance.
- *CAIB* demonstrated that management had repeatedly dismissed foam shedding as an operational maintenance annoyance rather than an existential safety-of-flight issue.
- Prompted the indefinite grounding of the shuttle fleet until *STS-114* in 2005 and directly motivated the 2004 *Vision for Space Exploration* retirement directive.
