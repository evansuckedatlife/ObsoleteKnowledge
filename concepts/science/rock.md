## summary

**Rocks** are solid aggregates of minerals forming the lithosphere and comprising the bulk of Earth's crust, mantle, and deeper layers. Classified into three main categories—igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic—rocks tell the story of Earth's dynamic interior, surface weathering, and tectonic processes. The rock cycle describes the continuous transformation of rocks as they are buried, melted, uplifted, and eroded over geological time.

## you gotta know

- Igneous rocks form from the cooling and crystallization of magma; large crystals indicate slow cooling deep underground, while fine crystals indicate rapid surface cooling (basalt, granite).
- Sedimentary rocks form from compaction and cementation of weathered rock fragments and minerals; they often contain fossils and preserve evidence of ancient environments (sandstone, limestone, shale).
- Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are subjected to extreme heat and pressure deep in the crust, causing mineral recrystallization without melting (marble, slate, schist).
- The rock cycle describes rocks transforming through processes of melting, burial, uplift, weathering, and erosion; no rock is permanent on a geological timescale.
- Granite is the most common igneous rock in Earth's continental crust; basalt is the most common in the oceanic crust.
- Limestone is a sedimentary rock made primarily of calcium carbonate shells and skeletons; it often recrystallizes into marble under metamorphic conditions.
- Rock types can be identified by crystal size, mineral composition, layering, and the presence of fossils, each property reflecting its formation environment.
- Extrusive (volcanic) igneous rocks cool rapidly at Earth's surface and have fine grains; intrusive (plutonic) rocks cool slowly underground and have coarse grains that can be visible to the naked eye.
- Foliation in metamorphic rocks results from mineral alignment during directional pressure; non-foliated metamorphic rocks like marble lack layering despite their recrystallization.
