## summary

**Ions** are atoms or molecules that have gained or lost one or more electrons, acquiring a net electric charge. Atoms that lose electrons become positively charged cations; atoms that gain electrons become negatively charged anions. Ions are fundamental to chemistry and biology, mediating ionic bonding, salt dissolution, electrical conductivity in solutions, and countless cellular processes like muscle contraction and nerve transmission.

## you gotta know

- An atom's charge state is determined by comparing its electron count (negative charge per electron: −1) to its proton count (positive charge per proton: +1); neutral atoms have equal protons and electrons.
- Cations are positively charged ions formed by electron loss; metals readily form cations because valence electrons are loosely held—sodium (Na) loses one electron to become Na⁺, magnesium (Mg) loses two to become Mg²⁺.
- Anions are negatively charged ions formed by electron gain; nonmetals readily form anions because they attract electrons strongly—chlorine (Cl) gains one to become Cl⁻, oxygen (O) gains two to become O²⁻.
- Ionization energy is the energy required to remove an electron from an atom; lower ionization energy predicts easier cation formation, so metals have lower ionization energies than nonmetals.
- Electron affinity is the energy change when an atom gains an electron; elements with high electron affinity readily form anions (halogens and chalcogens).
- Ions differ dramatically from neutral atoms in properties: sodium metal is highly reactive and burns in air, but sodium cations (Na⁺) in salt are inert; chlorine gas is toxic, but chloride anions (Cl⁻) are essential electrolytes.
- Polyatomic ions contain multiple atoms covalently bonded together but with a net charge, such as sulfate (SO₄²⁻), nitrate (NO₃⁻), and ammonium (NH₄⁺).
- In aqueous solution, ions dissociate and become solvated—surrounded by water molecules oriented by electrostatic attraction (cations by δ− oxygen, anions by δ+ hydrogen).

## connections

- [[atom]] — neutral atoms become ions through electron gain or loss.
- [[electron]] — the particles gained or lost in ionization, determining ionic charge.
- [[chemical-bond]] — ionic bonds form between cations and anions through electrostatic attraction.
- [[electronegativity]] — predicts which atoms preferentially lose (low electronegativity, metals) or gain (high electronegativity, nonmetals) electrons.
- [[electrolyte]] — solutions containing ions that conduct electricity.
- [[salt]] — the archetypal ionic compound, formed from cations and anions.
- [[muscle-contraction]] — driven by calcium (Ca²⁺) and potassium (K⁺) ion flux.
