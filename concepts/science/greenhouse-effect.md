# Greenhouse Effect

## summary

The **greenhouse effect** is the warming mechanism by which atmospheric gases trap solar radiation, preventing heat from escaping to space. While a natural process essential for life, increasing concentrations of gases like carbon dioxide amplify the effect, leading to global warming and climate change.

## you gotta know

- Solar radiation passes through the atmosphere to warm Earth's surface; Earth re-emits this energy as infrared radiation.
- Greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide, methane, water vapor, nitrous oxide, ozone, and chlorofluorocarbons) are transparent to incoming visible light but strongly absorb outgoing infrared radiation.
- The absorbed heat is re-radiated in all directions, some escaping to space but much returning to the surface, causing net warming.
- Without the natural greenhouse effect (maintained by water vapor, methane, and carbon dioxide at pre-industrial levels), Earth would be roughly 60°F colder and largely uninhabitable; the effect became problematic only when human industrial activity intensified gas concentrations starting around 1800.
- Fossil fuel combustion and deforestation release atmospheric carbon dioxide; agriculture and industry release methane and nitrous oxide; the concentration of these greenhouse gases has risen dramatically since industrialization began.
- A doubling of atmospheric carbon dioxide is estimated to cause 1.5–4.5°C warming, depending on climate feedback mechanisms (water vapor, clouds, ice albedo).
- The greenhouse effect explains why the Moon (with no atmosphere) has temperature extremes while Earth maintains a stable, life-supporting climate—until human-driven amplification.
