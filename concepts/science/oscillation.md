## summary

**Oscillation** is the repetitive variation, typically in time, of a physical quantity or state about a central equilibrium point or between two or more values. In classical physics, it describes phenomena ranging from the mechanical swing of a pendulum to the alternating currents passing through a [[resistor]] and inductor in an electronic circuit. The phenomenon is central to modern physics and engineering, governing how energy travels as a [[wavefunction]] or electromagnetic wave, and providing the mathematical bedrock for [[quantum-mechanics]] through the model of the harmonic oscillator. Understanding these cyclical disturbances enables the analysis of atomic vibrations in an [[atom]], the propagation of light via the [[photon]], and the transmission of data in computational systems.

## you gotta know

- *Simple harmonic motion* serves as the fundamental archetype of oscillatory behavior, characterized by a linear restoring force proportional to displacement that obeys *Hooke's law* and produces sinusoidal displacement curves over time.
- Every periodic oscillation is defined by its [[period]], the elapsed time required to complete one full cycle of motion, and its [[frequency]], the number of complete cycles occurring per second measured in hertz.
- Real physical systems lose mechanical energy to their environment through friction and drag, resulting in *damping* unless sustained by a periodic external driving force, which produces dramatic *resonance* when driven near the natural frequency.
- Joseph Fourier proved that any arbitrary repetitive waveform can be decomposed into an infinite sum of harmonically related sinusoids via a [[fourier-series]], establishing the mathematical cornerstone of acoustics, telecommunications, and digital [[signal-processing]].
- In electronic systems, oscillator circuits generate stable high-frequency clock signals using combinations of capacitors, inductors, and the [[resistor]], supplying timing references that coordinate instructions inside a [[central-processing-unit]].
- Within [[quantum-mechanics]], the harmonic oscillator is one of the few exactly solvable models, possessing discrete, evenly spaced energy states that retain a minimum nonzero *zero-point energy* dictated by the uncertainty principle.
- At microscopic scales, lattice vibrations in an [[atom]] generate acoustic and thermal phonons, while the propagation of a [[photon]] represents mutually perpendicular oscillating electric and magnetic fields in space.
- Modern particle physics reveals quantum-mechanical oscillations between fundamental states, such as solar neutrino flavor transitions and the oscillatory behavior of an [[electron]] moving through an optical or crystalline periodic potential.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — provides the theoretical framework for quantized harmonic oscillators and discrete vibrational energy levels.
- [[wavefunction]] — characterizes the spatial probability distribution and phase evolution of oscillating matter waves over time.
- [[resistor]] — dissipates energy as thermal loss to govern damping factors in electrical and mechanical oscillating networks.
- [[photon]] — embodies the fundamental quantum packet of electromagnetic oscillation traveling through space at light speed.
- [[atom]] — serves as the physical mass in molecular and lattice vibrations that store thermal energy.
- [[electron]] — exhibits collective plasma oscillations and transitions between atomic orbitals under oscillatory electromagnetic stimulation.
- [[signal-processing]] — utilizes mathematical transforms to filter, modulate, and interpret oscillatory signals in communication channels.
- [[central-processing-unit]] — synchronizes instruction cycles and pipeline execution using quartz crystal electronic clock oscillations.
