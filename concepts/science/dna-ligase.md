## summary

**DNA ligase** is an [[enzyme]] that catalyzes the formation of phosphodiester bonds between the sugar-phosphate backbone of adjacent DNA fragments. It seals nicks (breaks in the backbone) by joining a 3'-hydroxyl group of one fragment to a 5'-phosphate of another, effectively gluing DNA pieces together. Without it, organisms could not replicate their DNA or repair damage; it is also essential to molecular cloning and genetic engineering.

## you gotta know

- Works by cleaving ATP and using the energy to drive the formation of the phosphodiester bond.
- Essential during DNA replication: after DNA polymerase synthesizes the Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand, ligase seals them into one continuous strand.
- In cells, also repairs spontaneous breaks in DNA and is part of the base-excision and nucleotide-excision repair pathways.
- Different ligases exist: T4 DNA ligase (isolated from bacteriophage T4) is widely used in molecular cloning; bacteria use different ligases.
- Requires a cofactor (ATP or NAD+, depending on the source) to generate the high-energy intermediate.
- Only works on double-stranded DNA with a nick — requires a 3'-OH next to a 5'-phosphate; single-stranded breaks cannot be sealed.
