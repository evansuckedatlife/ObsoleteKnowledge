## summary

**Torque**, or the *moment of force*, is the rotational analogue of linear force, quantifying the tendency of an applied force to cause rotation about an axis or pivot point. Formulated mathematically as the cross product of the position vector and the force vector, it governs rotational kinematics and angular momentum across classic mechanics, engineering, and astrophysics. Understanding torque is fundamental to analyzing mechanical advantage in simple machines, engine output, gyroscopic precession, and orbital tidal interactions.

## you gotta know

- Defined mathematically as the vector cross product $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}$, where $\mathbf{r}$ is the displacement vector from the fulcrum or axis of rotation to the point where force $\mathbf{F}$ is applied.
- Measured in SI units of newton-meters ($N\cdot m$), which are dimensionally equivalent to joules but represent a vector turning moment rather than scalar energy or work.
- The magnitude of torque depends on the applied force, the length of the lever arm, and the sine of the angle between them ($\tau = r F \sin\theta$), maximizing when the force acts perpendicularly.
- Governs the rotational form of *Newton's second law*, $\boldsymbol{\tau} = I \boldsymbol{\alpha}$, relating net external torque directly to moment of inertia $I$ and angular acceleration $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$.
- Equals the time rate of change of angular momentum ($\boldsymbol{\tau} = \frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt}$), meaning a system with zero net external torque conserves its total angular momentum.
- Induces gyroscopic precession when applied perpendicular to an existing angular momentum vector, causing rotating systems like bicycle wheels, tops, and planets to sweep out a cone.
- Appears in electromagnetism as the torque experienced by an electric or magnetic dipole situated in an external field, given by $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{p} \times \mathbf{E}$ or $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \boldsymbol{\mu} \times \mathbf{B}$.
- Generates tidal friction and orbital locking in celestial bodies, transferring rotational angular momentum into orbital angular momentum over geologic time.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — provides the microscopic basis for intrinsic spin and magnetic dipole precession under applied field torque.
- [[electron]] — possesses an intrinsic spin magnetic moment that undergoes Larmor precession when subjected to magnetic torque.
- [[atom]] — experiences mechanical and magnetic torques that dictate fine structure splitting and Zeeman effects in external fields.
- [[albert-einstein]] — expanded classical mechanics to general relativity, which introduces relativistic frame-dragging and geodetic precession torques.
- [[jupiter]] — exerts massive gravitational tidal torques on its Galilean moons, driving internal volcanic heating and tidal locking.
