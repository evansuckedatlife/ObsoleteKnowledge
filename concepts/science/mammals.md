## summary

**Mammals** are a class of endothermic vertebrates characterized by hair, mammary glands, and a single bone in the lower jaw (dentary), uniquely positioned for jaw articulation. They evolved from therapsid synapsids during the *Triassic* period but remained small and marginal until the *Cenozoic* era, when the extinction of dinosaurs allowed rapid diversification. Today, mammals inhabit every terrestrial ecosystem and the oceans, from the smallest insectivores to the largest whales.

## you gotta know

- Mammals possess three middle-ear bones (malleus, incus, stapes) derived from reptilian jaw bones, an innovation that enhances hearing and marks a key evolutionary transition.
- Mammary glands secrete milk to nourture offspring, reducing parental burden and allowing mothers to provision young with precisely calibrated nutrition.
- Hair provides insulation and thermoregulation, enabling mammals to maintain high body temperature and activity in cold climates where reptiles cannot survive.
- The diaphragm, a sheet of muscle beneath the lungs, enables efficient air exchange in mammals, supporting higher metabolic rates than reptiles require.
- Monotremes (egg-laying mammals like the platypus) are considered the most primitive living mammals, retaining features lost in marsupials and placental mammals.
- Placental mammals achieve higher fetal development in utero, gestation lasting months, giving newborns a competitive advantage over marsupial young.
- The Cenozoic "Age of Mammals" saw rapid adaptive radiation: primates, carnivores, ungulates, and cetaceans diversified within roughly 66 million years.
- Mammals are the only animals with a four-chambered heart and a muscular diaphragm, innovations allowing efficient circulation and respiration at high metabolic rates.
- The mammalian jaw consists of a single bone (the dentary) articulating directly with the skull, contrasting sharply with the multiple bones in reptile jaws.
- Over 5,400 species of mammals exist today, ranging from insectivores weighing less than a gram to blue whales exceeding 100 tons, filling nearly every terrestrial and marine ecological niche.
- Internal temperature regulation enabled mammals to exploit nocturnal and polar habitats unavailable to cold-blooded reptiles.
