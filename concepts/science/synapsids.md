## summary

**Synapsids** were a diverse group of amniotes ("mammal-like reptiles") that dominated terrestrial ecosystems from the Carboniferous through the Permian periods and into the Triassic. They are the ancestors of all mammals, including humans; the synapsid line was the dominant vertebrate group until a mass extinction at the Permian-Triassic boundary devastated them. Understanding synapsids reveals the evolutionary bridge between ancient reptiles and modern mammals.

## you gotta know

- Characterized by a single temporal fenestra (hole) on the skull behind the eye, distinguishing them from sauropsids (dinosaurs and living reptiles) which have a different skull pattern.
- *Pelycosaurs* (early synapsids, Carboniferous–early Permian) included the sail-backed *Dimetrodon*, which may have used its sail for thermoregulation or display.
- *Therapsids* (later synapsids, Permian–Triassic) were more mammal-like, with improved limb posture, jaw structure evolving toward the single mandibular bone of mammals, and possible insulation via hair.
- During the Permian period, synapsids were the apex predators and dominated biomass; dinosaurs did not become dominant until synapsids were decimated by the Permian-Triassic extinction (~252 million years ago).
- Some therapsids like *Cynognathus* were large, active predators with specialized teeth and forward-facing limbs, suggesting endothermic metabolism (warm-bloodedness).
- Jaw structure evolved incrementally: reptilian jaw has multiple bones (quadrate, articular); mammalian jaw has one (dentary); intermediate stages are preserved in the synapsid fossil record.
- Ear bones (*malleus*, *incus*, *stapes*) of modern mammals derived from synapsid jaw bones, evidenced by embryology and fossils.
