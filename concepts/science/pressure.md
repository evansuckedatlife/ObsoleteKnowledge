## summary

**Pressure** is force exerted per unit area, arising from molecules colliding with surfaces or from gravitational weight of a fluid above. Measured in pascals (Pa), atmospheres (atm), or bars, pressure is a fundamental concept in fluids, thermodynamics, and weather. Atmospheric pressure (≈101,325 Pa at sea level) decreases with altitude and drives winds, weather patterns, and phase transitions; pressure gradients are the engine of meteorology.

## you gotta know

- Defined as P = F/A (force divided by area); SI unit is pascal (Pa); 1 atm = 101,325 Pa ≈ 760 mmHg.
- Arises from molecular impacts on surfaces; kinetic theory: pressure ∝ number density × average kinetic energy.
- Ideal Gas Law (PV = nRT) relates pressure, volume, temperature, and mole count; deviations occur near phase transitions and high pressures.
- Atmospheric pressure is the weight of air column above a point; decreases exponentially with altitude (P(h) ∝ e^(-Mgh/RT)), dropping to ~10% at 16 km.
- Pressure gradients are the fundamental driver of wind: air flows from high-pressure anticyclones toward low-pressure cyclones, causing weather systems.
- Vapor pressure is the pressure exerted by a liquid-vapor equilibrium; increases with temperature following the Clausius-Clapeyron equation; determines boiling point at any pressure.
- Gauge pressure (P_gauge = P_absolute − P_atm) is the practical measure for tire/tank pressure; absolute pressure includes atmospheric baseline.
- Osmotic pressure (Π = iMRT, where i is van 't Hoff factor) governs water movement in cells; hydrostatic pressure opposes osmotic flow in equilibrium.
- Barometers measure atmospheric pressure; manometers quantify pressure differences; U-tube manometers exploit hydrostatic pressure of liquid columns.
- Blood pressure (typically 120/80 mmHg systolic/diastolic) must exceed osmotic pressure (~25 mmHg) to prevent fluid accumulation in tissues (edema).
- Deep-sea organisms withstand extreme pressure (>1000 atm); proteins and lipids have evolved special adaptations to remain functional under crushing force.
