## summary

The **Compton effect**, or **Compton scattering**, is the inelastic scattering of a high-energy *photon* by a charged particle, most notably a stationary or loosely bound *electron*. Discovered in 1923 by *Arthur Compton* at Washington University in St. Louis, the interaction results in a decrease in energy and an increase in wavelength for the scattered photon. This experimental confirmation definitively proved that electromagnetic radiation possesses particle-like momentum, serving as a pillar of early *quantum mechanics*.

## you gotta know

- Demonstrated that X-ray photons collide with electrons like billiard balls, causing the scattered photon to have a longer wavelength and lower frequency.
- Provided undeniable experimental confirmation of the light quantum hypothesis previously proposed by *Albert Einstein* to explain the photoelectric effect.
- The shift in wavelength depends solely on the scattering angle $\theta$ and is given by the formula $\Delta \lambda = \lambda' - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1 - \cos\theta)$.
- The quantity $\frac{h}{m_e c}$ is known as the *Compton wavelength* of the electron, measuring approximately $2.426 \times 10^{-12}$ meters or 2.43 picometers.
- Earned *Arthur Compton* the 1927 Nobel Prize in Physics, which he shared with cloud-chamber pioneer *C. T. R. Wilson*.
- Inverse Compton scattering occurs when low-energy photons gain energy by scattering off ultra-relativistic electrons, a mechanism critical to high-energy astrophysics and cosmic ray astronomy.
- Forms the dominant mode of gamma-ray and hard X-ray radiation interaction with matter at intermediate energies between the photoelectric effect and pair production.

## connections

- [[quantum-mechanics]] — fundamental theoretical framework verified by the particle-like momentum observed in the scattering.
- [[photon]] — the quantum of electromagnetic radiation whose momentum is transferred during the collision.
- [[electron]] — the charged target particle that recoils and carries away kinetic energy during the scattering process.
- [[albert-einstein]] — theoretical physicist whose photon hypothesis of light was vindicated by Compton's experiments.
- [[atom]] — the physical matter whose bound electrons scatter incident hard X-rays and gamma radiation.
- [[standard-model]] — modern quantum field theory framework describing quantum electrodynamics and photon-electron interactions.
