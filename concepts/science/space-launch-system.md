## summary

The **Space Launch System** (**SLS**) is an American super heavy-lift expendable launch vehicle developed by [[nasa]] to serve as the backbone of deep-space human exploration in the twenty-first century. Authorized by the [[united-states]] Congress in 2010 following the cancellation of the *Constellation* program, the rocket is the central launch vehicle for the *Artemis* campaign to return astronauts to the lunar surface and eventually venture toward Mars. Utilizing modified hardware and infrastructure derived from the retired *Space Shuttle* program, the massive vehicle completed its maiden uncrewed flight during the *Artemis 1* mission in November 2022, restoring domestic super heavy-lift launch capability.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the primary heavy-lift rocket for the *Artemis* program, successfully launching the uncrewed *Orion* spacecraft around the Moon and back during *Artemis 1* from Launch Complex 39B at Kennedy Space Center in November 2022.
- Relies heavily on heritage components from the *Space Shuttle*, featuring a towering orange core stage powered by four refurbished *RS-25* liquid-propellant engines and flanked by two five-segment solid rocket boosters that provide the bulk of liftoff thrust.
- Designed in evolving configurations, progressing from the initial *Block 1* crew and cargo variants to the taller *Block 1B* and *Block 2*, which incorporate the more powerful *Exploration Upper Stage* (*EUS*) to significantly boost deep-space payload capacity.
- Employs an *Interim Cryogenic Propulsion Stage* (*ICPS*), powered by a single *RL10* engine burning liquid hydrogen and liquid [[oxygen]], to perform the critical trans-lunar injection burn on early flights.
- Succeeded the canceled *Ares V* heavy-lift rocket after the termination of the *Constellation* program, directly mandated by the [[united-states]] Congress through the NASA Authorization Act of 2010 to preserve aerospace jobs and manufacturing supply chains.
- Faces widespread public, scientific, and political scrutiny over its billions in development budget overruns, repeated scheduling delays, and an estimated per-launch operational cost exceeding two billion dollars under cost-plus government contracting.
- Competes philosophically with fully reusable commercial super heavy-lift architectures such as SpaceX's [[starship]], despite remaining an entirely expendable governmental system designed primarily for institutional exploration missions.

## connections

- [[nasa]] — the civilian space agency of the [[united-states]] that commissioned, manages, and operates the launch vehicle as part of its lunar exploration architecture.
- [[apollo-program]] — the historic lunar exploration campaign whose *Saturn V* rocket established the benchmark for American super heavy-lift capabilities that this modern system aims to replicate.
- [[starship]] — the reusable commercial mega-rocket developed by SpaceX that was selected to provide the human landing system connecting with this vehicle's payloads in lunar orbit.
- [[cold-war]] — the twentieth-century geopolitical contest that established American lunar infrastructure and shaped the long-term legislative imperative to maintain sovereign deep-space access.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose federal budget, congressional mandates, and aerospace industrial base funded and engineered the rocket.
- [[europa]] — the icy Jovian moon targeted by the *Europa Clipper* mission, which was initially mandated by Congress to launch aboard this vehicle before shifting to a commercial alternative.
- [[jupiter]] — the outer solar system destination for planned robotic planetary science missions evaluated as potential secondary payloads for the rocket's heavy cargo variants.
