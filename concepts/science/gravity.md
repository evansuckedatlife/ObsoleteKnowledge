## summary

**Gravity** is the fundamental force of attraction between all objects with mass, the weakest of the four known forces but dominant at cosmic scales. *Isaac Newton* quantified gravity mathematically in the 17th century through his law of universal gravitation, while *Albert Einstein* reinterpreted it in the 20th century as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. Gravity governs planetary orbits, stellar structure, black holes, and the large-scale expansion of the universe.

## you gotta know

- Newton's law of universal gravitation: *F* = *G*(*m*₁*m*₂)/*r*², where *G* is the gravitational constant (~6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²), *m*₁ and *m*₂ are masses, and *r* is distance.
- Gravity is fundamentally an attractive force; there is no "antigravity" or gravitational repulsion (masses always attract).
- Gravitational acceleration at Earth's surface is approximately 9.8 m/s² (sometimes denoted *g*), independent of an object's mass—all objects fall at the same rate in a vacuum (Galileo's principle).
- Escape velocity is the minimum speed needed to leave a gravitational field permanently; for Earth, it is ~11.2 km/s; for the Moon, ~2.4 km/s.
- Einstein's general relativity describes gravity not as a force but as the geometric curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy; massive objects warp spacetime, and smaller objects follow geodesic paths.
- Tidal forces arise because gravity decreases with distance; the near side of an object experiences stronger gravitational pull than the far side, creating stress and deformation (causing Earth's ocean tides from the Moon's gravity).
- Black holes are objects so massive that their escape velocity exceeds the speed of light; not even light can escape their event horizon.
- Gravitational lensing is the bending of light by gravitational fields, predicted by general relativity and observed in astronomical observations of distant galaxies.

## connections

- [[mass]] — the source of gravitational attraction; more massive objects exert stronger gravitational forces.
- [[force]] — gravity is one of the four fundamental forces, alongside electromagnetism, weak force, and strong force.
- [[newton-laws-of-motion]] — Newton's law of universal gravitation is a natural extension of his laws of mechanics.
- [[general-relativity]] — Einstein's modern geometric description of gravity as curved spacetime.
- [[planetary-motion]] — gravity governs orbits; Kepler's laws of planetary motion are consequences of Newton's gravity.
- [[astronomy]] — gravity is the organizing principle of astronomy, determining stellar structure, galaxy rotation, and cosmic evolution.
- [[tidal-heating]] — tidal forces (a consequence of gravity gradients) generate internal heat in moons and planets.
