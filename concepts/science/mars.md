# Mars

## summary

**Mars** is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest in the Solar System, distinguished by its rusty red surface caused by iron oxide dust and its two small moons. It holds central importance in scientific investigation due to evidence of ancient water, the search for microbial life, and its designation as humanity's primary target for crewed space exploration and potential colonization within the coming decades.

## you gotta know

- *Mars* orbits just inside the asteroid belt; the gravitational influence of [[jupiter]] has shaped the formation and dynamics of both the planet and nearby asteroids throughout Solar System history.
- The planet's distinctive red hue comes from *iron oxide* (rust) covering its surface, giving it the ancient Roman name after the god of war.
- *Valles Marineris*, a canyon system over 4,000 km long and 7 km deep, dwarfs Earth's Grand Canyon and marks ancient tectonic activity.
- Evidence of ancient *river valleys* and *mineral deposits* (like clay minerals that form in water) strongly suggests Mars once had liquid water on its surface billions of years ago.
- The planet's thin atmosphere is primarily *carbon dioxide* and provides almost no protection from solar radiation or insulation of heat, making surface temperatures drop to –80°C at night.
- *Olympus Mons*, a shield volcano about 25 km tall and 600 km across, is the largest volcano in the Solar System, indicating geological activity ceased relatively recently (in cosmic terms, within the last billion years).
- Mars hosts two tiny moons, *Phobos* (irregular and slowly falling toward the planet) and *Deimos*, thought to be captured asteroids.

## connections

- [[jupyter]] — gravitational anchor shaping Mars's orbital stability and asteroid-belt dynamics.
- [[nasa]] — primary institutional driver of Mars exploration missions and rover programs.
- [[earth]] — terrestrial sibling; Mars is more similar to Earth than any other planet, offering clues to planetary evolution.
- [[asteroid-belt]] — Mars sits at the inner boundary; the belt's gravitational perturbations influence Mars's long-term orbital configuration.
- [[solar-system]] — Mars is the proving ground for understanding planetary formation and habitability.
