## summary

**Gene regulation** is the process by which cells control when, where, and how frequently genes are expressed, turning transcription and translation on or off as needed. Without it, every cell would express every gene constantly, which is incompatible with specialization and survival. Gene regulation allows identical cells with identical DNA to differentiate into neurons, liver cells, or immune cells, and lets cells respond to environmental signals.

## you gotta know

- Transcriptional control occurs before mRNA synthesis: transcription factors bind regulatory DNA (promoters, enhancers, silencers) to activate or repress RNA polymerase.
- Epigenetic regulation involves chemical modifications to DNA or histone proteins without changing the DNA sequence; these marks can be heritable through cell divisions.
- Post-transcriptional control includes alternative splicing (combining different exons), RNA degradation, and localization.
- microRNAs (miRNAs) and other small RNAs can bind mRNA and trigger degradation or block translation.
- Translational control determines whether mRNA is translated into protein; regulatory proteins can block ribosome binding or trigger mRNA destabilization.
- Feedback loops (negative and positive) allow cells to self-regulate: a protein product may inhibit its own gene's transcription to maintain stable levels.
