## summary

The **centromere** is the specialized, constricted chromosomal locus that directs faithful genetic segregation during mitotic and meiotic cell division in eukaryotic organisms. It serves as the assembly platform for the *kinetochore*, a massive macromolecular complex that captures spindle microtubules to partition sister chromatids into daughter cells. Beyond mechanical anchoring, the centromere acts as an indispensable signaling domain for the spindle assembly checkpoint, shielding dividing tissues from aneuploidy, developmental disorders, and genomic instability.

## you gotta know

- Primary site for the assembly of the *kinetochore*, the multiprotein structure that captures spindle fibers, generates poleward motile force during anaphase, and coordinates mitotic checkpoint signaling.
- Visually distinguished under light microscopy as the *primary constriction*, which establishes chromosomal morphology into *metacentric* (central), *submetacentric* (offset), *acrocentric* (distal with satellites), or *telocentric* (terminal) configurations.
- Epigenetically specified in humans and most higher eukaryotes by nucleosomes incorporating *CENP-A* (centromere protein A), a specialized variant of *histone H3* that recruits the constitutive centromere-associated network (*CCAN*).
- Underlaid in human genomes by extensive tandem repeat arrays termed *alpha satellite DNA*, consisting of high-order multimers of 171-base-pair monomer units spanning megabases of heterochromatin.
- Regulated by the tension-sensing serine/threonine kinase *Aurora B*, a key catalytic subunit of the chromosome passenger complex (*CPC*) that destabilizes erroneous, non-bioriented kinetochore-microtubule attachments.
- Enriched with ring-shaped *cohesin* complexes across the pericentromeric domain, which resist microtubule pulling forces until targeted proteolytic cleavage by *separase* triggers sister chromatid disjunction.
- Replaced in non-canonical organisms like *Caenorhabditis elegans* and certain lepidopterans by *holocentric* chromosomes, where diffuse kinetochore plates establish microtubule attachments along the entire chromatid length.
- Subject to the *centromere paradox*, an evolutionary puzzle where centromeric satellite sequences turn over rapidly despite the strict functional conservation of the underlying segregation machinery across eukaryotes.

## connections

- [[cell]] — fundamental structural unit whose mitotic division requires precise spindle-mediated partitioning of nuclear chromosomes.
- [[cell-cycle]] — series of coordinated growth and replication phases that arrest at the spindle checkpoint if centromeric tension is absent.
- [[dna]] — macromolecular carrier of genetic information organized into repetitive satellite arrays at centromeric loci.
- [[protein]] — diverse structural and catalytic molecules forming the histone octamers, motor enzymes, and inner and outer kinetochore complexes.
- [[nucleus]] — membrane-bound compartment that breaks down in open mitosis to allow cytoplasmic spindle microtubules access to centromeres.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — translation machinery supplying checkpoint kinases, cohesin subunits, and architectural factors throughout interphase.
- [[evolution]] — mechanism underlying the *centromere paradox*, explaining rapid sequence turnover through centromeric drive and genetic conflict.
