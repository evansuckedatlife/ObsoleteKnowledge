## summary

**Gemstones** are minerals or rocks prized for their beauty, rarity, and durability, commonly used in jewelry and ornamentation. They are typically crystalline, transparent or translucent, and harder than most rocks, surviving cutting and polishing without degradation. The term encompasses both "precious" stones (diamond, ruby, sapphire, emerald) and "semi-precious" stones (amethyst, topaz, opal), though the distinction is economic rather than scientific.

## you gotta know

- Hardness is a key property; gemstones must resist scratching in daily wear — measured on the [[Mohs scale]] (1–10, with diamond at 10).
- [[Diamond]] is the hardest natural substance and most valued economically; [[corundum]] (ruby and sapphire) is the second-hardest.
- Color and clarity are graded professionally; inclusions, color saturation, and transparency determine value.
- Most gemstones are minerals with a fixed chemical formula and crystal structure; some (like opal) are amorphous or polycrystalline.
- Formation requires specific geological conditions: pressure, temperature, and time. Rubies and sapphires form in metamorphic rocks; emeralds grow in pegmatites.
- Synthetic gemstones (lab-created diamonds, rubies) are chemically identical to natural stones but lack the pricing premium.

## connections

- [[diamond]] — the hardest and most expensive gemstone.
- [[corundum]] — source of rubies and sapphires.
- [[quartz]] — the most abundant gemstone mineral (amethyst, citrine, rose quartz varieties).
- [[mohs-scale]] — the hardness scale for gemstones.
- [[crystal]] — the ordered atomic structure of gemstones.
- [[mineral]] — gemstones are typically minerals by definition.
