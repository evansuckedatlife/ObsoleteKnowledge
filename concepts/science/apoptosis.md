## summary

**Apoptosis** is programmed cell death, a controlled process in which a cell disassembles itself in response to signals, without damaging neighboring cells through inflammation. Unlike necrosis (traumatic cell death), apoptosis is an organized, energy-dependent process involving the activation of proteases (caspases) and endonucleases, DNA fragmentation, and membrane blebbing. Essential for development, immune function, and preventing cancer, apoptosis is triggered by DNA damage checkpoints, growth-factor withdrawal, or external stressors.

## you gotta know

- Intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway: triggered by DNA damage, oxidative stress, or growth-factor withdrawal; p53 activates pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family members (Bax, Bak).
- Mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization (MOMP) releases cytochrome c into cytoplasm; cytochrome c binds Apaf-1, recruiting procaspase-9 into the apoptosome complex.
- Extrinsic (death-receptor) pathway: ligands (TNF-α, FasL, TRAIL) bind death receptors on cell surface; recruits adaptor proteins (DISC) that directly activate caspase-8.
- Executioner caspases (caspase-3, -6, -7) cleave hundreds of protein substrates, disassembling the nuclear lamina, condensing chromatin, and fragmenting DNA (~180 bp laddering).
- Hallmark features: cell shrinkage, membrane blebbing (bulging), chromatin condensation and fragmentation, PS externalization ("eat-me" signal), formation of apoptotic bodies.
- Phagocytes recognize phosphatidylserine on apoptotic cell surfaces; engulf intact cells without rupturing membranes, preventing inflammatory cytokine release (immunologically silent).
- Critical during development: digit sculpting (removing webbing), elimination of interdigital tissue, matching neuron number to targets (survival factor competition).
- Cancer cells evade apoptosis via p53 mutations (loss of sensor function), Bcl-2 overexpression (blocks cytochrome c release), or caspase pathway deletions.
- Therapeutic exploitation: chemotherapy and radiation kill cancer cells partly by triggering apoptosis; BCL2 inhibitors (venetoclax) and death-receptor agonists restore apoptosis in resistant cancers.
- Dysregulation: insufficient apoptosis (cancer, autoimmunity); excessive apoptosis (neurodegeneration, AIDS, ischemic injury); stroke damages neurons partly through apoptotic cascades.
- Apoptosis is a fundamental quality-control mechanism, ensuring only healthy cells persist and damaged cells exit silently without triggering inflammation or immune alarms.
- Understanding apoptosis opened new drug development approaches targeting cancer, viral infection, and degenerative diseases.
