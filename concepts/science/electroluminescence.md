## summary

**Electroluminescence** is an optical and electrical phenomenon in which a solid-state material emits light in direct response to the passage of an electric current or the application of a strong electric field. Discovered experimentally in silicon carbide crystals by *H. J. Round* in 1907 and further developed throughout the twentieth century, it distinguishes itself from incandescence by releasing energy directly as photons rather than blackbody thermal radiation. It provides the central physical operating mechanism behind light-emitting diodes, solid-state lasers, instrument displays, and organic luminescent panels.

## you gotta know

- Emits non-thermal radiative output when electrical energy excites charge carriers within an active material, distinguishing it completely from heat-driven incandescence.
- Relies in forward-biased semiconductor p-n junctions on radiative electron-hole recombination, where energetic electrons drop into vacant electron states and release the surplus bandgap energy as light.
- First observed in carborundum crystals by British radio pioneer *H. J. Round*, who noted yellowish emission when applying electrical currents.
- Further investigated systematically by Russian physicist *Oleg Losev*, who published detailed analyses of diode luminescence and light emission thresholds during the 1920s.
- The characteristic wavelength and spectral color of the emitted radiation depend directly on the intrinsic semiconductor bandgap, according to the fundamental *Planck-Einstein relation*.
- Direct bandgap semiconductors, such as gallium arsenide and gallium nitride, demonstrate vastly superior emission efficiency compared to indirect bandgap semiconductors like silicon.
- Operates in alternative solid configurations via high-field impact ionization, wherein accelerated hot electrons collide with embedded luminescent dopant centers like manganese in zinc sulfide phosphors.
- Powers modern organic light-emitting diodes, in which electric potential injects excitons into thin organic molecular films to produce bright flat displays.

## connections

- [[photon]] — the elementary packet of electromagnetic radiation emitted when excited electrons drop across the forbidden bandgap.
- [[electron]] — the negatively charged lepton whose recombination with positive valence holes releases optical energy.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework establishing discrete energy bands, bandgaps, and transition rules in semiconductors.
- [[wavefunction]] — the mathematical function whose spatial overlap determines the quantum transition probability of radiative recombination.
- [[electrochemistry]] — the adjacent study of electrical and chemical transformations, contrasting surface ion-transfer with solid-state carrier recombination.
- [[resistor]] — an essential passive element paired with light-emitting diodes to prevent catastrophic thermal runaway from unrestricted current.
