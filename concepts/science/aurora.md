## summary

An **aurora**, commonly referred to as the **polar lights**, is a natural luminous display occurring predominantly in high-latitude regions of a planet's atmosphere. Generated when charged magnetospheric particles, primarily guided by planetary magnetic field lines, precipitate into the upper atmosphere and collide with neutral gas atoms and molecules, the phenomenon converts kinetic energy into radiant emission. Known as the *aurora borealis* in the northern hemisphere and *aurora australis* in the southern hemisphere, it serves as a prominent optical manifestation of space weather and planetary magnetospheric dynamics.

## you gotta know

- Named by *Galileo Galilei* and *Petrus Gassendi* in the seventeenth century after *Aurora*, the Roman goddess of dawn, and *Boreas*, the Greek personification of the north wind.
- Driven by geomagnetic storms and coronal mass ejections from the sun, during which magnetic reconnection in the Earth's magnetotail accelerates energetic charged particles downward along magnetic field lines.
- Precipitating particles consist predominantly of energetic leptons, specifically the fast [[electron]], alongside positively charged ions and the bare [[atomic-nucleus]] of hydrogen or helium.
- Characteristic vibrant emerald-green and crimson emissions arise from forbidden electronic transitions in atomic [[oxygen]], specifically the transitions from the $^1S$ and $^1D$ metastable excited states.
- Atmospheric molecular nitrogen produces blue and deep violet luminescence at lower altitudes through the ionization and excitation of first negative band systems.
- Confined to oval-shaped bands known as auroral ovals, typically centered around the geomagnetic poles between sixty and seventy-five degrees magnetic latitude, expanding equatorward during extreme geomagnetic disturbances.
- Displays varied dynamic morphological forms including quiet homogenous arcs, streaming rays, shimmering curtains, and high-altitude coronas aligned with local magnetic zenith vectors.
- Extends beyond Earth to other magnetized planetary bodies, prominently documented across the polar regions of gas giants such as [[jupiter]] and Saturn.

## connections

- [[electron]] — the charged subatomic particle whose precipitation into atmospheric gases causes electronic excitation.
- [[photon]] — the quantum of electromagnetic radiation released when excited atmospheric atoms relax to ground states.
- [[oxygen]] — the atmospheric element responsible for the primary green and red emission lines of the aurora.
- [[atomic-nucleus]] — constitutes the accelerated ionic component of solar wind plasma, notably protons and alpha particles.
- [[jupiter]] — gas giant whose intense magnetic field drives persistent, powerful ultraviolet and optical polar auroras.
- [[nasa]] — space agency that operates satellites such as *THEMIS* and *IMAGE* to study auroral substorms and solar interactions.
