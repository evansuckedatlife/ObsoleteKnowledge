## summary

The **Space Shuttle program** was NASA's initiative to develop a reusable spacecraft for regular orbital missions, operating from 1981 to 2011. Five shuttle orbiters (*Columbia*, *Challenger*, *Discovery*, *Atlantis*, *Endeavour*) flew 135 missions, deploying the Hubble Space Telescope, servicing it repeatedly, and constructing the International Space Station. The program revolutionized spaceflight as a routine transportation system but was marked by two catastrophic failures.

## you gotta know

- First launch: *Columbia* on April 12, 1981; first spacecraft to reach orbit and return intact for reuse.
- Shuttles could carry up to eight crew and ~27 tonnes of cargo to low Earth orbit; distinctive delta-wing design enabled gliding reentry and landing on a runway.
- *Challenger disaster* (January 28, 1986): O-ring failure in solid rocket booster led to structural failure; all seven crew killed, program suspended for 32 months.
- *Columbia disaster* (February 1, 2003): foam insulation struck the heat shield during launch; orbiter disintegrated on reentry; all seven crew killed, program modified but continued.
- Deployed 355 satellites, conducted 24 *Hubble Space Telescope* servicing missions, and launched the permanent modules of the International Space Station.
- Extraordinarily complex; thermal tiles required painstaking inspection; each mission cost ~$500 million and took months of preparation.
- Final mission (*Atlantis* STS-135) in July 2011; shuttles retired after 30 years; replaced by commercial crew programs and the *Space Launch System*.
