## summary

**Fluorine** is the chemical element with atomic number 9, represented by the symbol F, and the lightest of the halogens. It is an extremely toxic, pale yellow diatomic gas at standard conditions and ranks as the most electronegative and chemically reactive of all elements. Isolated by *Henri Moissan* in 1886, its fierce affinity for electrons makes it fundamental to industrial chemistry, pharmaceuticals, nuclear fuel enrichment, and refrigerant synthesis.

## you gotta know

- Holds the highest electronegativity value on the Pauling scale at 3.98, drawing valence *[[electron]]* density more strongly than any other element.
- Exists naturally as a single stable isotope, fluorine-19, with an *[[atom]]* comprising nine protons and ten neutrons.
- Isolated via the electrolysis of anhydrous hydrogen fluoride dissolved in molten potassium bifluoride, earning *Henri Moissan* the 1906 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
- Forms exceptionally strong carbon-fluorine bonds, the cornerstone of non-stick fluoropolymers like Teflon (polytetrafluoroethylene) and durable fluorocarbon coatings.
- Used in the form of uranium hexafluoride gas during gaseous diffusion and centrifuge enrichment to separate uranium isotopes for nuclear reactors.
- Added in microgram quantities as inorganic fluoride to municipal water supplies and dentifrices to convert hydroxyapatite in tooth enamel into acid-resistant fluorapatite.
- Exhibits an anomalous weak F-F single bond energy compared to other halogens due to electrostatic repulsion between lone pairs in its compact valence shell.

## connections

- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical unit whose ninth elemental configuration possesses an unfilled 2p subshell.
- [[electron]] — the subatomic lepton whose strong attraction to the halogen core gives the element its ferocious reactivity.
- [[oxygen]] — one of the only elements that forms compounds where it adopts a positive oxidation state due to halogen electronegativity.
- [[sodium]] — the alkali metal that donates a valence charge to yield standard ionic fluorite-type salts.
- [[nucleus]] — the compact positive core containing nine protons, around which nine bound leptons circulate.
- [[ernest-rutherford]] — the physicist whose alpha-particle scattering experiments clarified the nuclear charge that defines light halogens.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the theoretical framework explaining electron shielding, electronegativity, and orbital hybridization.
