## summary

**Heat** is energy transferred between systems due to a temperature difference, flowing spontaneously from hot to cold without external work. Distinct from temperature (which measures average kinetic energy of particles), heat is the *transfer process* itself and is measured in joules or calories. Heat is central to thermodynamics, chemistry, and virtually all physical processes, from engine efficiency to weather to stellar fusion.

## you gotta know

- Energy transferred by conduction, convection, or radiation across a temperature gradient; always flows spontaneously from hot to cold, never reversed without work.
- Defined quantitatively as Q = mcΔT (for sensible heat in a substance), where m is mass, c is specific heat capacity, and ΔT is temperature change.
- Conduction: heat diffusion through matter via atomic/molecular vibrations; occurs in solids and depends on thermal conductivity (high in metals, low in insulators).
- Convection: bulk fluid motion carrying thermal energy; dominant in liquids and gases; natural convection driven by density differences, forced convection by pumps/fans.
- Radiation: electromagnetic emission from matter at all temperatures; increases with temperature (Stefan-Boltzmann law: P ∝ T⁴).
- First Law of Thermodynamics: ΔU = Q − W; heat input to a system increases internal energy or does work on surroundings.
- Entropy governs spontaneity: heat flows from high to low temperature, increasing total entropy of the universe (Second Law).
- Latent heat (L) is energy needed to change phase (solid ↔ liquid, liquid ↔ gas) without changing temperature; melting and vaporization have distinct latent heats.
- Specific heat capacity varies dramatically by material; water's unusually high specific heat (~4.18 kJ/(kg·K)) makes it ideal for thermal regulation and climate moderation.
- Mechanical equivalent of heat: *Joule* showed 1 calorie ≈ 4.186 joules, unifying heat and mechanical work and refuting the "caloric fluid" theory.
- Heat capacity varies with temperature and phase; supercooled liquids and heated solids exhibit anomalies near phase transitions.
