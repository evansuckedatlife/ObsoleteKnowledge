## summary

The **sternum**, commonly known as the **breastbone**, is an elongated, flat bone positioned along the anterior midline of the human thorax. It serves as the keystone of the thoracic skeleton by articulating directly with the clavicles and the costal cartilages of the upper ribs, shielding the underlying heart, lungs, and great vessels from direct trauma. Through its mobile junctional attachments, it facilitates the thoracic volume changes required for pulmonary ventilation while providing anchorage for major muscles of the neck, chest, and anterior abdominal wall. In clinical medicine, it represents a primary anatomical landmark for diagnostic chest palpation, a site for red marrow aspiration, and the primary surgical approach for open-heart operations via median sternotomy.

## you gotta know

- Comprises three anatomically fused segments arranged from superior to inferior: the broad manubrium, the elongated central body (*gladiolus*), and the cartilaginous inferior xiphoid process.
- Articulates directly with the pectoral girdle at the paired sternoclavicular joints and anchors the costal cartilages of the first seven pairs of ribs (the true ribs) to permit thoracic cage elevation during the inhalation of [[oxygen]].
- Bears the palpable sternal angle, or *angle of Louis*, at the manubriosternal junction, which aligns horizontally with the second rib and marks the transverse thoracic plane dividing the superior and inferior mediastinum at vertebrae T4 and T5.
- The xiphoid process begins in early childhood as a flexible hyaline plate and gradually ossifies into bone by middle age; during cardiopulmonary resuscitation, improper compression hand placement risks fracturing this process and lacerating the underlying liver or diaphragm.
- Serves as an accessible adult site for red marrow needle biopsy because its thin cortical plates enclose rich vascular trabeculae that house hematopoietic stem cells generating every [[cell]] type in systemic circulation.
- Median sternotomy involves bisecting the bone longitudinally with an oscillating saw to grant cardiac surgeons comprehensive exposure to the pericardial cavity and coronary vessels.
- Modified dramatically across avian vertebrate [[evolution]] into a gigantic sagittal keel called the *carina*, which supplies the vast surface area needed to anchor flight muscles such as the pectoralis major.

## connections

- [[radius]] — fellow skeletal element formed by endochondral ossification that relies on common calcium homeostatic pathways.
- [[cell]] — microscopic building block continuously produced inside the cancellous hematopoietic marrow of the sternal cavity.
- [[evolution]] — morphological lineage showing the transformation of primitive tetrapod ventral plates into modern mammalian breastbones and avian keeled carinae.
- [[oxygen]] — vital atmospheric gas drawn into the pulmonary tree when sternal expansion enlarges the thoracic cavity.
- [[protein]] — collagenous structural fiber providing the flexible extracellular framework upon which sternal hydroxyapatite crystals deposit.
