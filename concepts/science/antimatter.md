## summary

**Antimatter** is the counterpart to ordinary matter, composed of antiparticles—particles with identical mass but opposite charge and quantum numbers to their regular-matter equivalents. For every particle, an antiparticle exists; the electron's counterpart is the *positron*, the proton's is the antiproton. When matter and antimatter meet, they annihilate in a burst of energy described by Einstein's *E = mc²*, making antimatter theoretically the most efficient energy source imaginable.

## you gotta know

- Each fundamental particle has an antimatter counterpart: the positron (antielectron), antiproton, antineutron, and exotic antiparticles like antineutrinos.
- Matter-antimatter annihilation converts all mass directly into energy, releasing approximately 10 billion times more energy per kilogram than chemical combustion.
- The Standard Model predicts equal amounts of matter and antimatter should have been created in the Big Bang, yet the observable universe is composed almost entirely of matter—this asymmetry remains an unsolved mystery.
- Antimatter is routinely produced in particle accelerators and has practical applications in medical imaging: PET (Positron Emission Tomography) scanners detect positrons emitted by radioactive tracers.
- Antiparticles have identical mass but opposite charge; an antiproton has negative charge where a proton has positive, maintaining overall balance in antimatter systems.
- Antimatter cannot be stored indefinitely; even trace contact with ordinary matter causes annihilation, requiring elaborate magnetic confinement or advanced vacuum storage techniques.
- Theoretically, antimatter engines could power interstellar spacecraft by converting antimatter-matter reactions into thrust, though practical engineering remains science fiction.

## connections

- [[positron]] — the antiparticle of the electron, the most accessible antimatter particle.
- [[baryon]] — heavy particles like protons and neutrons, each with antimatter equivalents.
- [[fermion]] — matter particles obeying the Pauli exclusion principle; fermions include antiparticles.
- [[standard-model]] — the framework predicting the existence and properties of antimatter.
- [[particle]] — the fundamental building blocks, each with an antimatter dual.
- [[annihilation]] — the catastrophic interaction between matter and antimatter.
