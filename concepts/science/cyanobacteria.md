## summary

**Cyanobacteria**, formerly designated as *blue-green algae*, constitute a major phylum of photosynthetic, gram-negative prokaryotic organisms found ubiquitously across marine, freshwater, and terrestrial ecosystems. Emerging over two and a half billion years ago during the Archean Eon, they pioneered oxygenic photosynthesis, fundamentally transforming planetary biogeochemistry by saturating the atmosphere with molecular oxygen during the Great Oxidation Event. Their resilient adaptations allow them to thrive from hyper-arid desert crusts and Antarctic lakes to geothermal hot springs, where they form the trophic foundation of extreme environments. Through primary endosymbiosis, an ancestral cyanobacterial lineage gave rise to chloroplasts, initiating the evolutionary radiation of all photosynthetic eukaryotes. Their ecological dominance continues to drive global biogeochemical cycles and aquatic primary production.

## you gotta know

- Cyanobacteria are the only known prokaryotes capable of oxygenic photosynthesis, utilizing chlorophyll a alongside specialized antenna complexes called phycobilisomes to extract electrons from water.
- Metabolic oxygen production by early cyanobacteria drove the Great Oxidation Event, oxidizing dissolved ferrous iron in the oceans to precipitate massive banded iron formations and iron oxide beds worldwide.
- The sudden accumulation of cyanobacterial oxygen destabilized Earth's methane greenhouse, triggering the global Huronian glaciation and plunging the planet into extreme *Snowball Earth* conditions.
- Colonial cyanobacterial mats trapped and bound mineral grains to produce layered organosedimentary structures called stromatolites, which represent the earliest macroscopic fossil evidence of terrestrial life.
- These fossilized reefs, preserved famously at Shark Bay in Australia, demonstrate how microbial communities dominated the shallow waters of the Precambrian.
- Under the endosymbiotic theory championed by *Lynn Margulis*, the engulfment and integration of an ancient photosynthetic cyanobacterium by an ancestral eukaryotic host cell gave rise to the chloroplast.
- Many filamentous cyanobacteria resolve the incompatibility between oxygen and the nitrogen-fixing enzyme nitrogenase by differentiating thick-walled, non-photosynthetic specialized cells called heterocysts.
- Although cyanobacteria lack the complex eukaryotic [[cytoskeleton]], they employ ancient prokaryotic homologues, such as the tubulin-like GTPase FtsZ, to establish the division ring during binary fission.

## connections

- [[photosynthesis]] — the light-harvesting metabolic pathway pioneered by cyanobacteria that uses water to generate chemical energy and oxygen.
- [[oxygen]] — the elemental byproduct of cyanobacterial water-splitting that permanently oxygenated Earth's atmosphere and oceans.
- [[evolution]] — the planetary biological timeline transformed by cyanobacterial metabolic innovation and endosymbiotic integration into plant cells.
- [[cell]] — the basic biological unit, structured in cyanobacteria as a prokaryotic compartment bounded by peptidoglycan and containing thylakoids.
- [[cytoskeleton]] — eukaryotic structural networks whose microtubule machinery is absent in cyanobacteria except for ancestral homologues like FtsZ.
- [[carbon]] — the elemental building block fixed from inorganic carbon dioxide into sugars within specialized microcompartments called carboxysomes.
- [[enzyme]] — catalytic proteins, notably Rubisco and nitrogenase, that perform vital metabolic carbon and nitrogen fixation in cyanobacterial cells.
