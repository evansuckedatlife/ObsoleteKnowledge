# Igneous Rock

## summary

An **igneous rock** is any rock formed by the cooling and solidification of molten rock (magma or lava). Igneous rocks constitute the vast majority of Earth's crust by volume and are classified into two main groups based on where and how they cool: intrusive (or plutonic) rocks that cool slowly underground within the crust, producing large mineral crystals, and extrusive (or volcanic) rocks that cool rapidly at the surface, forming fine-grained or glassy textures. Understanding igneous rocks reveals Earth's internal heat, plate tectonics, and the mineral compositions that make up planetary bodies.

## you gotta know

- **Intrusive igneous rocks** cool slowly beneath the surface, allowing minerals to grow large enough to see with the naked eye; *granite* is the quintessential example—a coarse-grained rock of feldspar, quartz, and mica that forms the cores of mountains.
- **Extrusive igneous rocks** cool rapidly at the surface or in thin flows, producing fine-grained textures or even volcanic glass (*obsidian*); *basalt*, the most common extrusive rock, erupts from *mid-ocean ridges* and oceanic hotspots.
- Igneous rock composition is tied to magma chemistry: *felsic* magmas (rich in silica and aluminum) produce light-colored rocks like granite; *mafic* magmas (rich in magnesium and iron) produce dark, dense rocks like basalt. *Ultramafic* rocks (even richer in iron and magnesium) are rare at the surface but common in the mantle.
- The *cooling rate* directly determines crystal size: magma cooling over millions of years (intrusive) produces centimeter-sized crystals; lava cooling in days or hours (extrusive) produces millimeter-sized crystals; super-rapid cooling produces *amorphous* (non-crystalline) volcanic glass.
- *Kimberlite*, an ultramafic volcanic rock, originates in the mantle and erupts explosively, carrying diamonds and other high-pressure minerals to the surface; it is the primary source of gem-quality diamonds in the world.
- Igneous rocks form along *subduction zones* (where oceanic plates plunge into the mantle), *mid-ocean ridges* (where plates separate and new crust forms), and *hotspots* (mantle plumes rising through the lithosphere, forming volcanic chains).
- The **Bowen's Reaction Series** describes the order in which minerals crystallize as cooling magma—olivine freezes first (at ~1200°C), then pyroxene, amphibole, and mica, finally feldspar and quartz at lower temperatures; this explains why different igneous rocks have different mineral suites.
- *Pumice*, a vesicular (hole-filled) lava cooled so quickly that dissolved gases formed bubbles, is so light it floats on water; *scoria* is a darker, denser vesicular rock formed from more iron-rich lava.
