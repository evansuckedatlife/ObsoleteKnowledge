## summary

An **electrolyte** is a substance that conducts electricity when dissolved in a solvent (usually water) or in molten form. It contains [[ion|ions]]—charged particles—that move freely and carry electrical current. Electrolytes are essential in batteries, physiological processes, and any application requiring ionic conduction.

## you gotta know

- Electrolytes are compounds that dissociate (break apart) into positively charged cations and negatively charged anions when dissolved.
- Common examples include salts (like sodium chloride), acids (like hydrochloric acid), and bases (like sodium hydroxide).
- The conductivity of an electrolyte depends on ion concentration, temperature, and the mobility of ions in the medium.
- In batteries, electrolytes allow ions to flow between the anode and cathode internally, completing the circuit and enabling sustained electron flow through an external circuit—the basis for all electrochemical power sources.
- Physiological electrolytes like sodium, potassium, and chloride regulate nerve signals, muscle contraction, and fluid balance in living organisms.
- Strong electrolytes (like salts sodium chloride and strong acids such as hydrochloric acid) dissociate nearly completely into ions in solution; weak electrolytes (like acetic acid and ammonia) only partially dissociate, reaching equilibrium with their molecular form.
- Electrolyte imbalance in the body can cause serious health consequences: hyponatremia (low sodium) causes confusion and seizures; hyperkalemia (high potassium) disrupts heart rhythm and can cause cardiac arrest; athletes and kidney patients are especially vulnerable.

## connections

- [[ion]] — the charged particles that make electrolytes conductive.
- [[charge]] — the property that electrolytes carry and transmit.
- [[electrical-conductivity]] — the property electrolytes exhibit in solution or molten state.
- [[muscle-contraction]] — depends on electrolyte gradients across cell membranes.
- [[sodium]] — a major physiological electrolyte regulating nerve and muscle function.
- [[potassium]] — essential electrolyte for heart rhythm and cellular function.
- [[nerve-transmission]] — relies on electrolyte ion gradients for action potentials.
