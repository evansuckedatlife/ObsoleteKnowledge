## summary

The **cochlea** is the spiral-shaped, fluid-filled auditory portion of the inner ear responsible for converting acoustic pressure waves into electrical neural impulses. Resembling a snail shell, this complex organ houses the organ of Corti and separates mechanical frequencies along its basilar membrane. Its discovery and physiological elucidation, particularly *Georg von Békésy's* traveling wave mechanism, revealed how sensory systems resolve delicate frequency spectra before transmission to the brain.

## you gotta know

- Houses three fluid-filled compartments: the scala vestibuli and scala tympani containing perilymph, and the middle scala media filled with potassium-rich endolymph.
- Contains the organ of Corti, the neuroepithelial receptor structure populated with inner and outer hair cells topped with microscopic stereocilia.
- Exhibits precise tonotopic organization along the basilar membrane, where stiff, narrow basal regions register high frequencies and wide, flexible apical regions respond to low frequencies.
- Relies on outer hair cells to function as active mechanical amplifiers through the somatic motility of the motor *[[protein]]* prestin.
- Converts mechanical shearing into electrical receptor potentials when stereocilia deflect, opening tip-link gated ion channels that admit potassium and *[[sodium]]* ions into the sensory *[[cell]]*.
- Connects through spiral ganglion neurons that coalesce into the cochlear branch of the vestibulocochlear nerve (cranial nerve VIII).
- Restored functionally in profound sensorineural deafness using surgically implanted cochlear implants that directly stimulate auditory nerve fibers with an electrode array.

## connections

- [[cell]] — the specialized sensory hair and supporting epithelial units that translate fluid shear into action potentials.
- [[protein]] — the structural macromolecules forming mechanotransductive tip links and the motor unit prestin.
- [[sodium]] — an essential cation involved in maintaining resting gradients and generating downstream auditory nerve action potentials.
- [[oxygen]] — the vital metabolic gas supplied by the vascularized stria vascularis to maintain high endolymphatic potentials.
- [[enzyme]] — the metabolic catalysts like sodium-potassium ATPase driving endocochlear ion homeostasis.
- [[dna]] — the genetic code directing the developmental morphogenesis of the organ of Corti and its stereocilia bundles.
- [[resistor]] — the electrical analog used in biophysical circuit models of hair cell membrane resistance and endocochlear currents.
