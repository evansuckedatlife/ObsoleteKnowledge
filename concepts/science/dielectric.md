## summary

A **dielectric** is an electrical insulator that can be polarized by an applied electric field without supporting a large-scale conduction current of free charge. When subjected to an external voltage, positive charges shift slightly in the direction of the field while negative charges shift oppositely, creating microscopic internal dipole moments that oppose and reduce the net internal field. Dielectrics are foundational components of electrical circuits and electronic hardware, serving as the essential charge-storing and insulating medium within capacitors.

## you gotta know

- Exhibits negligible electrical conduction because its constituent electrons are tightly bound within valence bands rather than free to drift.
- Characterized by its relative permittivity or dielectric constant, a dimensionless factor measuring how many times greater its electric displacement field is compared to a vacuum.
- Increases the capacitance of parallel-plate capacitors proportionally to its dielectric constant by diminishing the internal electric field for a given accumulated surface charge.
- Experiences dielectric breakdown when exposed to an electric field exceeding its breakdown voltage, ionizing the substrate and permitting destructive conductive spark discharge.
- Polarizes through multiple distinct microscopic mechanisms, including electronic displacement polarization, permanent molecular dipole orientation, and ionic displacement within crystalline lattices.
- Possesses a frequency-dependent response described by complex permittivity, where the imaginary component governs dielectric loss and thermal energy dissipation.
- Includes ubiquitous industrial insulators such as mica, porcelain, ceramic oxides, transformer oil, air, and specialized polymers like Teflon.

## connections

- [[resistor]] — a complementary circuit component that governs resistive current dissipation rather than capacitive electrostatic storage.
- [[electron]] — the negatively charged subatomic particle whose displacement relative to the nucleus causes molecular polarization.
- [[atom]] — the fundamental chemical unit whose electronic clouds deform when placed within an applied electrostatic field.
- [[diffraction]] — the wave-scattering behavior exploited in periodic photonic crystals constructed from alternating layers of contrasting dielectric media.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical era of electrification and machine power that spurred modern commercial manufacturing of reliable insulators.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the physical framework explaining electronic band gaps, insulating states, and field-induced atomic dipole perturbations.
