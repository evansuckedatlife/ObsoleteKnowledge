# Antigen

## summary

**An antigen** is any foreign molecule or substance that triggers an immune response by binding to antibodies or being recognized by immune cells. Antigens are typically proteins, polysaccharides, or nucleic acids present on pathogen surfaces, toxins, or allergens; their recognition initiates the adaptive immune system's cascade of B-cell and T-cell activation, forming the cornerstone of immunological defense and vaccination.

## you gotta know

- The term *antigen* derives from "*antibody generator*"—any molecule that provokes antibody production is, by definition, an antigen.
- Most antigens are *proteins* or protein-polysaccharide complexes; the immune system recognizes specific three-dimensional regions of the antigen called *epitopes* (or antigenic determinants), which serve as the "lock" for antibody binding.
- Antigens must be *foreign* to the body—present on invading pathogens, viruses, toxins, or pollen—or abnormal, such as tumor-associated proteins on cancer cells; the immune system normally ignores self-antigens through a process called *tolerance*.
- *Blood group antigens* (like ABO types) are glycoproteins on red blood cell surfaces; these are inherited and determine transfusion compatibility because mismatched antigens trigger severe immune reactions.
- *Major histocompatibility complex (MHC)* molecules present antigens to T cells; MHC class I presents intracellular antigens (from viruses), while MHC class II presents extracellular antigens (from pathogens) to different T-cell subsets.
- The *specificity* of immune recognition is extraordinary—antibodies can distinguish between antigens that differ by a single amino acid or sugar; this specificity is why the immune system can respond to millions of distinct pathogens.
- *Allergens* are antigens that trigger inappropriate (IgE-mediated) immune responses in sensitive individuals; examples include pollen, peanut proteins, and dust-mite fecal matter, which are harmless but provoke histamine release and inflammation.

## connections

- [[antibody]] — the Y-shaped protein that binds antigens with exquisite specificity, marking them for destruction.
- [[immunology]] — the science of immune recognition and response to antigens.
- [[protein]] — most antigens are proteins; recognition depends on protein structure and folding.
- [[cell]] — antigens are displayed on cell surfaces (MHC molecules) or exist as secreted molecules; immune cells identify antigens to trigger responses.
- [[enzyme]] — some antigens are enzymes whose disruption incapacitates pathogens.
