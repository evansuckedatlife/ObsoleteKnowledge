## summary

**Acoustic pressure**, frequently referred to as **sound pressure**, is the local dynamic deviation from the ambient atmospheric or static equilibrium pressure caused by the propagation of a sound wave through a medium. In classical mechanics and acoustics, it serves as the primary physical quantity measured by pressure-sensitive microphones and perceived by the human ear as loudness. Understanding this oscillating mechanical disturbance is fundamental to architectural acoustics, audio engineering, noise mitigation, and the study of mechanical waves.

## you gotta know

- Measured in pascals in the *International System of Units*, quantifying the alternating compressive and rarefying force per unit area exerted by propagating sound waves.
- Represented conventionally by its root-mean-square amplitude rather than instantaneous value, allowing meaningful quantification of time-varying acoustic power over sustained intervals.
- Expressed on a decibel scale as sound pressure level, benchmarked against the standard human auditory threshold of twenty micropascals in air at one kilohertz.
- Differs from sound intensity in that pressure is a scalar dynamic field variable, whereas intensity represents the directional vector flux of acoustic energy per unit area.
- Governed mathematically by the acoustic wave equation derived from linearising the continuity equation, the *Euler equations* of fluid motion, and an isentropic equation of state.
- Forms standing waves in enclosed resonant cavities such as organ pipes and acoustic resonators, generating distinct pressure nodes where fluctuations vanish and anti-nodes where variations peak.
- Relates directly to local particle velocity through acoustic specific impedance, a constitutive property of the medium equal to the product of ambient density and the *speed of sound*.
- Scales inversely with linear distance from an idealised omnidirectional monopole source in an unobstructed spherical free field according to the acoustic inverse-distance law.
