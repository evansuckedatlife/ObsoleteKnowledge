## summary

The **phalanges** (singular **phalanx**, or **phalanx bones**) are the tubular digital bones that articulate to constitute the fingers and thumbs of the hand as well as the toes of the foot in vertebrate tetrapods. In human anatomy, fifty-six individual bones comprise the manual and pedal digits, arranged hierarchically into proximal, middle, and distal series. Their structural evolution and precise motor attachments facilitated tool manufacture, locomotion, fine manipulation, and the morphological radiation of terrestrial vertebrates.

## you gotta know

- Total fourteen bones per limb in typical humans, yielding twenty-eight in both hands and twenty-eight across both feet, totaling fifty-six in the adult skeleton.
- Classified anatomically into three longitudinal tiers: proximal bones articulating with metacarpals or metatarsals, intermediate bones, and ungual distal bones supporting nails and claws.
- The pollex and the hallux each contain only two bones, lacking the intermediate skeletal element found within the other four digits.
- Derive their etymological name from the *phalanx* military infantry formation of [[ancient-greece]], reflecting how parallel rows of close-ordered hoplites resemble aligned skeletal digits.
- Feature prominent apical tufts on the distal tips that provide an anchoring bed for fibrous tactile pads, vascular beds, and protective keratinous plates.
- Anchor vital tendons including the *flexor digitorum profundus*, *flexor digitorum superficialis*, and opposing extensor tendons that permit grasping and bipedal pushoff.
- Show extensive adaptive modifications across vertebrate evolutionary lineages, such as elongation in bat wings, reduction in equine hooves, and hyperphalangy in cetacean flippers.

## connections

- [[evolution]] — the macroevolutionary process that remodeled the primitive pentadactyl limb into specialized human grasping hands and cursorial feet.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization whose linear military battle lines inspired the anatomical designation coined by ancient anatomists.
- [[cell]] — the basic unit of living matter, including osteoblasts, osteocytes, and osteoclasts, that synthesizes and continually remodels bone mineral.
- [[protein]] — the family of macromolecules comprising type-I collagen matrices that grant tensile durability to mineralized skeletal tissues.
- [[dna]] — the hereditary genetic sequence expressing homeobox transcription factors that orchestrate anteroposterior limb patterning during embryogenesis.
