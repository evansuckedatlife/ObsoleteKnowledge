## summary

**DNA helicase** is a specialized motor enzyme that utilizes the chemical energy of nucleoside triphosphate hydrolysis to unwind and separate double-stranded nucleic acids into single-stranded templates. Operating during replication, transcription, recombination, and repair across all domains of life, these molecular motors translocate directionally along phosphodiester backbones to disrupt base pairing. Its structural mechanics, conserved hexameric ring architecture, and coordinated regulation across the [[cell-cycle]] make it a cornerstone of molecular genetics.

## you gotta know

- Utilizes energy derived from ATP hydrolysis to break the complementary hydrogen bonds stabilizing double-stranded nucleic acids, overcoming the attractive [[dipole]] interactions between nitrogenous bases.
- In eukaryotic replication, the minichromosome maintenance complex (MCM2-7) acts as the replicative core, forming a heterohexameric ring loaded onto origin sites during the G1 phase of the [[cell-cycle]].
- The eukaryotic pre-replicative complex remains inactive until S phase, when cyclin-dependent kinases and Dbf4-dependent kinase phosphorylate initiation factors to recruit Cdc45 and GINS, forming the active CMG holoenzyme.
- Bacterial replication relies on DnaB, a homohexameric helicase loaded at *oriC* by the initiator DnaA and chaperone DnaC, which translocates with 5'-to-3' polarity along the lagging-strand template.
- Classified into six major superfamilies based on conserved primary sequence motifs, including Walker A and Walker B nucleotide-binding pockets that coordinate magnesium ions and facilitate ATP cleavage.
- Generates positive superhelical strain ahead of the advancing replication fork, requiring topoisomerases like DNA gyrase to introduce transient strand breaks that relieve torsional resistance.
- Single-stranded binding proteins rapidly coat exposed nucleotides immediately behind the unwinding motor to prevent spontaneous re-annealing and destructive secondary hairpin formations.
- Inherited mutations in human RecQ family helicases, such as *BLM*, *WRN*, and *RECQL4*, disrupt genomic stability pathways and lead to progeroid and cancer-predisposition disorders like Bloom and Werner syndromes.

## connections

- [[dna]] — the double-stranded genetic polymer whose complementary antiparallel strands are separated to allow template copying and repair.
- [[enzyme]] — catalytic proteins that lower activation energies, here converting nucleoside triphosphate hydrolysis into mechanical force.
- [[protein]] — macromolecular polypeptide assemblies comprising conserved structural domains such as Walker motifs and oligonucleotide-binding folds.
- [[cell-cycle]] — the eukaryotic regulatory cycle whose G1 licensing and S-phase kinase activation govern the initiation of replicative unwinding.
- [[dipole]] — permanent molecular charge separations responsible for the base-pairing hydrogen bonds that the helicase mechanically disrupts.
- [[protein-synthesis]] — downstream gene expression that requires transcription-coupled helicases to open chromatin and expose gene sequences for RNA polymerases.
