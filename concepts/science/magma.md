## summary

**Magma** is molten rock beneath Earth's crust, the parent material for all igneous rocks and volcanic lava. When magma remains underground, it cools slowly to form coarse-grained plutonic rocks; when it erupts through volcanoes, it cools rapidly as lava, creating fine-grained volcanic rocks. Understanding magma composition, temperature, and viscosity is essential to predicting volcanic behavior and interpreting Earth's internal chemistry.

## you gotta know

- Magma forms primarily at divergent plate boundaries (mid-ocean ridges) and convergent boundaries (subduction zones) where pressure and temperature lower the melting point of rock.
- Composition varies widely: basaltic magma (low silica, ~1200°C) flows freely and erupts as shield volcanoes; rhyolitic magma (high silica, ~900°C) is thick and explosive.
- Magma is lighter than the solid rock surrounding it, creating buoyancy that drives it upward through cracks and weak zones in the crust.
- Crystallization begins as magma cools: minerals with high melting points crystallize first (olivine, pyroxene), followed by lower-melting minerals (feldspar, quartz).
- Fractional crystallization and magma mixing can change composition, explaining how different eruption products emerge from the same magma chamber.
- The presence of dissolved water and other volatiles dramatically lowers magma viscosity and can trigger explosive degassing during eruption.
- Ancient magma, now cooled to solid rock, preserves information about early Earth: its composition reveals the mantle's chemical evolution.
- Magma chambers act as thermal reservoirs, accumulating beneath volcanoes and building pressure that eventually triggers eruption when overpressure exceeds rock strength.
- The viscosity of magma determines eruptive style: low-viscosity basaltic magma produces gentle effusive eruptions, while high-viscosity rhyolitic magma creates violent explosive events.
- Partial melting of the mantle beneath mid-ocean ridges produces new oceanic crust that continuously spreads, a process driven by convection of magma and rock.
- Erupted lava flows cool into basalt; slower cooling of magma underground produces plutonic rocks like granite, preserving a record of the magma's composition.

## connections

- [[volcano]] — the surface expression where magma erupts as lava and pyroclastic material.
- [[igneous-rock]] — the rock formed when magma cools, either underground (plutonic) or above ground (volcanic).
- [[plate-tectonics]] — the mechanism that generates most magma through decompression melting and subduction.
- [[mineral]] — the crystalline phases that form as magma cools in a predictable sequence.
- [[lava]] — the extrusive form of magma that erupts onto Earth's surface.
- [[basalt]] — the most common volcanic rock, formed from rapid cooling of basaltic magma.
