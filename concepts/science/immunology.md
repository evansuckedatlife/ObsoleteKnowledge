# Immunology

## summary

**Immunology** is the scientific study of the body's defense system against pathogens, toxins, and foreign substances. It encompasses the mechanisms by which white blood cells, antibodies, and other immune components recognize, respond to, and neutralize threats, forming the basis for understanding disease resistance, vaccination, allergies, and autoimmune disorders.

## you gotta know

- The immune system's primary function is *recognition and elimination* of foreign antigens—proteins or molecular markers present on pathogens or abnormal cells—through a cascade of cellular and molecular responses.
- *Lymphocytes* are white blood cells specialized for immune response; *B cells* produce antibodies (soluble proteins that bind antigens), while *T cells* directly kill infected or cancerous cells or coordinate immune activity.
- *Antibodies* are Y-shaped proteins that bind to specific antigens with high precision, acting as biological locks whose keys are the unique three-dimensional shapes of target molecules.
- The *innate immune system* responds immediately and non-specifically to any pathogen (via complement proteins and phagocytes), while the *adaptive immune system* develops targeted responses over days, with memory of prior infections.
- *Vaccination* works by exposing the immune system to a harmless form of a pathogen (weakened or killed organism, or isolated antigen) so that B and T cells develop memory; upon real infection, the adaptive response is rapid and strong.
- *Autoimmune diseases* occur when the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissues—examples include lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and type 1 diabetes—because self/non-self recognition breaks down.
- *Immunological memory* persists for years or decades; the secondary immune response to a previously encountered antigen is faster and stronger than the primary response, which is why some vaccinations require boosters.

## connections

- [[antigen]] — the foreign molecule or protein marker that triggers immune recognition and response.
- [[antibody]] — the Y-shaped protein produced by B cells that binds antigens with high specificity.
- [[enzyme]] — many immune components function as enzymes (e.g., lysozyme breaks down bacterial cell walls).
- [[protein]] — antibodies and immune receptors are proteins; antigen recognition depends on protein structure.
- [[cell]] — the fundamental unit of immune response; white blood cells identify and destroy infected or foreign cells.
