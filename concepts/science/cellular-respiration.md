# Cellular Respiration

## summary

**Cellular respiration** is the catabolic process by which cells break down organic molecules—primarily glucose, fatty acids, and amino acids—to extract chemical energy stored in their bonds and convert it into ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the cell's universal energy currency. The process occurs in stages: glycolysis in the cytoplasm, the citric acid cycle in the mitochondrial matrix, and oxidative phosphorylation at the inner mitochondrial membrane, with oxygen serving as the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration.

## you gotta know

- *Glycolysis* occurs in the cytoplasm and splits one glucose molecule into two pyruvate molecules, yielding a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH (electron carriers); no oxygen is required for this stage.
- The *citric acid cycle* (also called the Krebs cycle or TCA cycle) occurs in the mitochondrial matrix and oxidizes acetyl groups derived from pyruvate, releasing CO₂ and generating NADH, FADH₂, and a small amount of ATP per glucose.
- *Oxidative phosphorylation* is the energy-harvesting stage: electron carriers (NADH and FADH₂) transfer high-energy electrons through a chain of protein complexes in the inner mitochondrial membrane, pumping protons that drive ATP synthase to produce approximately 30-32 ATP per glucose.
- The *electron transport chain* consists of four protein complexes and ultimately transfers electrons to [[oxygen]], forming water; this stepwise transfer captures energy that would be wasted if electrons fell directly to oxygen.
- *Anaerobic respiration* (fermentation) occurs when oxygen is absent; pyruvate is converted to lactate or ethanol, regenerating NAD+ to allow glycolysis to continue, but yielding only 2 ATP per glucose (far less efficient).
- The *efficiency* of aerobic respiration is approximately 38–40% (measured as ATP energy captured vs. total energy in glucose), while fermentation recovers only 2–3%; this vast difference explains why aerobic organisms can support higher metabolic rates.
- *Metabolic rate* increases dramatically during exercise because muscles demand more ATP; the body can increase respiration rate 10–20-fold by increasing oxygen delivery and ATP production.

## connections

- [[photosynthesis]] — the inverse process; photosynthesis captures solar energy into glucose, while respiration releases that energy.
- [[atp]] — the product of cellular respiration and the primary energy currency for all cellular work.
- [[glucose]] — the primary fuel for cellular respiration in most cells.
- [[oxygen]] — the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration; its absence forces cells into inefficient fermentation.
- [[enzyme]] — every step of respiration is catalyzed by specific enzymes; enzyme function is critical to respiration efficiency.
