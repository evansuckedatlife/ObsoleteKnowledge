## summary

**Weather** denotes the instantaneous or day-to-day state of the *troposphere* at a specific geographic location, characterized by variables including [[temperature]], atmospheric moisture, barometric [[pressure]], cloud cover, precipitation, and [[wind-speed]]. Driven fundamentally by differential solar radiation heating Earth's spherical surface unevenly and planetary rotation producing the *Coriolis effect*, these dynamic atmospheric shifts dictate ecological patterns and human activity. Understanding these short-term fluctuations distinguishes fleeting local conditions from the long-term statistical baselines of [[climate]], forming the central observational foundation of modern [[meteorology]].

## you gotta know

- Conceptually distinguished from [[climate]] by timescale, as weather reflects immediate or weekly atmospheric variability, whereas climate represents the statistical distribution of weather observations compiled over standard thirty-year periods defined by the *World Meteorological Organization*.
- Driven fundamentally by the unequal distribution of incoming solar energy across planetary latitudes, creating equator-to-pole thermal gradients that power major atmospheric circulation cells—namely *Hadley cells*, *Ferrel cells*, and polar cells—alongside high-altitude jet streams.
- Barometric pressure differentials dictate airflow and stability: low-pressure systems, or cyclones, feature convergence and ascending air that fosters cloud development and stormy conditions, whereas high-pressure systems, or anticyclones, promote divergence, descending air, and clear skies.
- Frontal weather systems emerge at the sharp interfaces between distinct air masses with differing [[temperature]] and moisture profiles, classified into cold fronts, warm fronts, stationary boundaries, and complex occluded systems often associated with mid-latitude extratropical cyclones.
- Nearly all observable weather phenomena unfold within the *troposphere*, the lowest atmospheric layer bounded above by the *tropopause*, which concentrates approximately eighty percent of atmospheric mass and almost all atmospheric water vapor.
- The *Coriolis effect*, an apparent inertial force arising from Earth's axial rotation, deflects moving air masses to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere, governing the spin direction of tropical hurricanes and synoptic weather systems.
- The quantitative study of weather accelerated during the scientific revolution through the development of fundamental instruments, notably the mercury barometer invented by *Evangelista Torricelli* to measure atmospheric [[pressure]] and early liquid-in-glass thermometers developed by *Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit* and *Anders Celsius*.
- Modern numerical weather prediction applies fluid dynamics and thermodynamics to forecast atmospheric states using partial differential equations first formulated by *Vilhelm Bjerknes* and *Lewis Fry Richardson*, though sensitive dependence on initial conditions discovered by *Edward Lorenz* limits deterministic forecasts to approximately two weeks.

## connections

- [[atmosphere]] — the stratified envelope of gases surrounding Earth whose lowest stratum contains nearly all moisture and convective activity.
- [[climate]] — the statistical synthesis and long-term regime of weather conditions measured across multi-decadal intervals.
- [[meteorology]] — the scientific branch of atmospheric physics focused on observing, explaining, and forecasting weather processes.
- [[oxygen]] — the diatomic atmospheric gas that, alongside nitrogen and argon, forms the bulk non-condensable medium of dry air.
- [[greenhouse-effect]] — the selective absorption of terrestrial infrared radiation by trace atmospheric gases, warming the lower atmosphere.
- [[nasa]] — the agency operating polar-orbiting and geostationary satellites that collect vital atmospheric and meteorological observations.
- [[jupiter]] — the planetary body whose turbulent outer atmosphere features violent storms and long-lasting anticyclonic vortices like the Great Red Spot.
- [[ocean-current]] — planetary marine circulation systems that redistribute thermal energy poleward and heavily influence maritime and coastal weather.
