## summary

**Friedrich Nietzsche** (1844–1900) was a German philosopher whose radical critique of Western morality, reason, and religion fundamentally reshaped modern thought across philosophy, literature, psychology, and politics. He diagnosed a crisis of meaning he called *nihilism* and proposed that culture must be remade through the creation of new values. His aphoristic style, psychological insight, and provocative persona made him as influential in 20th-century art and literature as in academic philosophy.

## you gotta know

- Declared "God is dead"—not as atheist celebration but as a diagnosis of modernity's loss of meaning and the collapse of inherited values.
- Developed the concept of the *will to power*, arguing that all life seeks not merely survival but dominance, creation, and the overcoming of limits.
- Critiqued traditional morality (especially Christian and Kantian ethics) as a slave revolt against life, designed by the weak to constrain the strong.
- Proposed the *Übermensch* (overman/superman)—not a master race but an ideal figure who creates new values and transcends herd morality.
- Wrote in fragmented, poetic aphorisms rather than systematic treatises, deliberately resisting academic convention and systematic philosophy.
- His works *Beyond Good and Evil* and *On the Genealogy of Morals* expose the hidden interests and origins behind supposedly universal ethical systems.
- Introduced *perspectivism*: there are no absolute truths, only interpretations shaped by the interpreter's values, health, and power.
- Championed the *revaluation of all values*: Western culture must overcome Christian slave morality and create life-affirming values rooted in vitality and creativity.
- Diagnosed *decadence* in European culture and sought cultural renewal through the recovery of Dionysian (creative, destructive, ecstatic) impulses alongside Apollonian order.
- Profoundly influenced modernist artists, psychoanalysts, and existentialists; distorted by Nazi ideology (his sister's editorial interventions were complicit).

## connections

- [[nihilism]] — his diagnosis of modern meaninglessness and the consequent need for value creation.
- [[will-to-power]] — his central concept explaining motivation and the structure of reality.
- [[existentialism]] — later existentialists adopted his emphasis on radical freedom and the need to create one's own meaning.
- [[morality]] — his genealogical attack on the origins of Western ethics transformed moral philosophy.
- [[modernism]] — profoundly shaped modernist literature's rejection of 19th-century bourgeois values.
- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — a literary precursor whose *Crime and Punishment* explored moral transgression and the death of God.
- [[jean-paul-sartre]] — an existentialist heir to Nietzschean ideas about freedom and value creation.
