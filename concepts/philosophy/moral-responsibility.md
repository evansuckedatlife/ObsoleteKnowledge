## summary

**Moral responsibility** is the property of being a proper subject of moral blame, praise, and accountability for one's actions; it assumes an agent is the originator of an act and is subject to justified reward or punishment for it. The question of what grounds moral responsibility—whether free will, conscious choice, or other conditions—is one of philosophy's enduring puzzles.

## you gotta know

- Responsibility requires *agency*: the person acted, rather than merely being acted upon; they caused the outcome through their own choice or will.
- Most philosophers agree responsibility requires *knowledge* and *intent*: one cannot be blamed for an action done in ignorance or without conscious choice.
- The "free will requirement": libertarians argue genuine freedom (the ability to do otherwise) is necessary for responsibility; without it, praise and blame seem unjust.
- *Compatibilists* argue moral responsibility is compatible with determinism; what matters is acting from one's own desires and deliberations, not whether those desires were determined.
- Diminished responsibility cases: young children, the insane, and the coerced are not fully responsible because they lack mature agency or freedom of choice.
- Collective responsibility: can groups (corporations, nations, communities) be morally responsible? Debates over corporate and state accountability rest on this question.
- *Strawson*'s reactive attitudes approach: moral responsibility is grounded not in metaphysics but in the natural human responses (resentment, gratitude, indignation) that constitute a moral community.
- Luck and responsibility: philosophers debate whether brute luck (circumstances beyond control) can undermine responsibility; a person born into poverty faces different options than a born heir.

## connections

- [[free-will]] — the metaphysical condition most philosophers take to be necessary for responsibility.
- [[freedom]] — agency and autonomy; responsibility requires freedom to act otherwise or at least to act from one's own values.
- [[determinism]] — challenges traditional responsibility; raises the question of whether determinism undermines accountability.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — responsibility is tied to character; virtuous agents are those worthy of praise for their reliable, excellent choices.
- [[kant]] — grounded responsibility in the rational autonomy of the moral agent; duty and dignity.
- [[causality]] — philosophers debate whether causal participation is sufficient for responsibility or whether non-causal conditions matter.
- [[moral-philosophy]] — the broader discipline encompassing theories of right action, virtue, and what agents owe to each other.
