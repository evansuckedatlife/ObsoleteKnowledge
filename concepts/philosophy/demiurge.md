## summary

The **Demiurge**, also designated as **the Craftsman** or **demiourgos**, is the divine entity responsible for fashioning and maintaining the physical cosmos in the philosophy of *[[plato]]*. Introduced in *[[ancient-greece]]* in the cosmological dialogue *Timaeus*, this benevolent architect takes pre-existing, disorderly matter and arranges it into a harmonious and beautiful universe using the eternal Forms as an unchangeable model. Crucial to ancient cosmology, *[[theology]]*, and the history of metaphysics, the concept later underwent a dramatic inversion in Gnosticism, where the figure was reimagined as an ignorant or malicious subordinate creator.

## you gotta know

- Introduced in *[[plato]]*'s dialogue *Timaeus*, where the Locrian astronomer *Timaeus* delivers a *likely account* (*eikos mythos*) of the origin, structure, and ensoulment of the cosmos.
- Distinct from the traditional Judeo-Christian Creator because the Craftsman does not create matter *ex nihilo* (out of nothing), but instead imposes order, geometric proportion, and harmony upon chaotic, recalcitrant matter.
- Driven by supreme benevolence and freedom from envy, intending the sensible universe to be as good, rational, and perfect as physical nature allows by imitating the intelligible realm of Forms.
- Crafts the *World Soul* (*anima mundi*) prior to the material body of the cosmos, placing reason within soul and soul within body so that the cosmos is a living, ensouled, and intelligent animal.
- Imposes geometric and mathematical order on the four classical elements using the regular polyhedra, now called *Platonic solids*, which govern matter through principles aligned with *[[deductive-reasoning]]*.
- Generates time as a moving, dynamic likeness of motionless eternity, coordinating celestial spheres and planetary rotations to provide measurable units of temporal existence.
- Radically reinterpreted in late antiquity by Gnostic sects, such as the Valentinians and Sethians, as a defective, blind, and prideful lesser archon named *Yaldabaoth* or *Saklas*, who falsely claimed to be the ultimate deity.
- Influenced Middle Platonism, Neoplatonic thinkers like *Plotinus*, and early Church Fathers in *[[christianity]]*, who wrestled with reconciling the Platonic Craftsman with divine omnipotence and biblical creation.

## connections

- [[plato]] — the Athenian philosopher who introduced the benevolent divine Craftsman in his dialogue *Timaeus*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical culture whose rational inquiries into nature and cosmology produced the concept.
- [[theology]] — the metaphysical study of divinity, creation, and cosmological order influenced by the Demiurge.
- [[dualism]] — the ontological division between the eternal realm of intelligible Forms and the imperfect, sensible physical cosmos.
- [[materialism]] — the opposing cosmological view of the atomists, who denied any teleological craftsman in favor of random material collisions.
- [[deductive-reasoning]] — the mathematical and geometrical principles through which the Craftsman structures chaotic matter into Platonic solids.
- [[socrates]] — Plato's mentor whose demand for teleological explanations of nature inspired the cosmology of the *Timaeus*.
- [[christianity]] — the theological tradition that adopted and contested Platonic demiurgic ideas during the formation of early Christian doctrine.
