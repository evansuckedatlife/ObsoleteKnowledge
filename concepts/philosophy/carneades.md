## summary

**Carneades**, also known as **Carneades of Cyrene**, was an eminent Greek philosopher and the leading scholarch of the *Platonic Academy* during its New Academy skeptical period in the second century BCE. Renowned throughout [[ancient-greece]] as one of antiquity's most formidable dialecticians and orators, he attacked dogmatic assertions of certainty, waging a brilliant intellectual campaign against the foundational epistemology of [[stoicism]]. By denying the possibility of infallible knowledge while providing a practical framework for human action based on probability, he transformed Hellenistic [[philosophy]] and shaped the development of philosophical skepticism.

## you gotta know

- Traveled to the [[roman-empire]] in 155 BCE as part of the famous Embassy of the Three Philosophers alongside the Stoic *Diogenes of Babylon* and the Peripatetic *Critolaus* to negotiate the repeal of a devastating diplomatic fine imposed on Athens for sacking Oropus.
- Shocked Roman intellectuals by delivering back-to-back public lectures on justice, eloquently praising natural justice on the first day and thoroughly refuting his own arguments on the second day by demonstrating that justice is merely conventional self-interest in conflict with wisdom.
- Outraged the conservative statesman *Cato the Elder*, who condemned his dazzling rhetorical relativism as an existential moral hazard and urged the Roman Senate to dismiss the Athenian embassy immediately to safeguard Roman youth from subversion.
- Formulated the enduring ethical thought experiment known as the plank of Carneades, asking whether a shipwrecked survivor who pushes a weaker companion off a floating spar to save their own life acts unjustly or out of reasonable necessity.
- Dedicated his philosophical career to attacking the core Stoic doctrine of the cognitive impression (*kataleptike phantasia*), arguing that no perceptual impression carries an unmistakable intrinsic guarantee distinguishing reality from convincing illusion or dream states.
- Famously remarked, "If *Chrysippus* had not been, I would not be," recognizing that his skepticism functioned as a parasitic yet razor-sharp dialectical response to the systematic metaphysics and epistemology of his Stoic predecessor.
- Introduced the concept of *pithanon*, often translated as plausibility or probability, arguing that although human beings cannot attain absolute certainty, practical life can be reasonably guided by impressions that are persuasive, uncontradicted by surrounding context, and thoroughly investigated.
- Left no written texts behind, choosing to philosophize entirely through spontaneous oral dialectic, meaning his arguments were recorded and preserved for posterity solely through the extensive writings of his loyal Carthaginian pupil *Clitomachus*.

## connections

- [[stoicism]] — primary rival school whose dogmatic assertions of infallible cognitive perception and divine providence he systematically dismantled.
- [[plato]] — founder of the *Academy* whose institutional mantle Carneades held while guiding the school to the height of its skeptical phase.
- [[ancient-greece]] — intellectual civilization whose dialectical tradition and competitive philosophical schools provided the arena for his debates.
- [[roman-empire]] — emerging imperial power where his controversial 155 BCE embassy lectures captivated young intellectuals and provoked conservative panic.
- [[logic]] — analytical tool he weaponized against dogmatic philosophers to demonstrate that equally persuasive arguments can be constructed for opposing viewpoints.
- [[empiricism]] — epistemological stance echoed in his practical epistemology, which grounded fallible human judgment in empirical testing and contextual scrutiny.
- [[rationalism]] — philosophical doctrine whose claims to deduce indubitable truths about reality through unaided human reason he relentlessly attacked.
