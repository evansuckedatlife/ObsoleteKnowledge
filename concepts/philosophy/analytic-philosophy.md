## summary

**Analytic philosophy** is a dominant twentieth-century intellectual tradition that originated in [[england]], [[germany]], and Austria before becoming the predominant methodology across the English-speaking world. Characterized by an emphasis on formal [[logic]], conceptual clarity, rigorous semantic analysis, and close alignment with the natural sciences, the movement arose as a decisive rejection of absolute idealism and speculative metaphysics. Through foundational revolutions spanning mathematical logic, ordinary language, and naturalized epistemology, it redefined the scope of [[philosophy]] and laid the groundwork for modern [[linguistics]] and cognitive science.

## you gotta know

- Pioneered in Cambridge by *Bertrand Russell* and *G. E. Moore* as a revolt against British absolute idealism, culminating in *Russell* and *Alfred North Whitehead*'s monumental *Principia Mathematica*, which attempted to reduce all mathematics to formal [[logic]].
- Revolutionized by *Ludwig Wittgenstein*'s *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*, which asserted that philosophical problems stem from misunderstandings of the logic of language, proposed a picture theory of meaning, and concluded that whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.
- Adopted radical [[empiricism]] through the Vienna Circle's logical positivism, led by *Rudolf Carnap* and *Moritz Schlick*, which used the verificationist criterion of meaning to dismiss traditional metaphysics and [[theology]] as cognitively meaningless.
- Pivoted toward ordinary language philosophy following *Wittgenstein*'s posthumous *Philosophical Investigations*, which replaced truth-conditional semantics with language-games, forms of life, and the private language argument, influencing thinkers like *J. L. Austin* and *Gilbert Ryle*.
- Shattered internal orthodoxy when *W. V. O. Quine* published "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" in 1951, dismantling the boundary between analytic and synthetic truths, rejecting reductionism, and proposing a holistic web of belief.
- Reinvigorated modern metaphysics and semantics through *Saul Kripke*'s *Naming and Necessity*, which introduced rigid designators, causal theories of reference, and the existence of necessary a posteriori truths like "water is H2O."
- Built on *Gottlob Frege*'s logicist foundations and his distinction between sense (*Sinn*) and reference (*Bedeutung*), as well as *Russell*'s landmark paper "On Denoting," which analyzed definite descriptions using sentences like "the present King of France is bald."
- Championed common-sense realism through *G. E. Moore*'s famous "Here is one hand" refutation of radical skepticism, as well as his introduction of the naturalistic fallacy in *Principia Ethica*, which argued that good cannot be defined in terms of natural properties.

## connections

- [[logic]] — the primary formal instrument utilized to formalize propositions and clarify philosophical grammar.
- [[empiricism]] — the epistemological foundation adapted by early logical positivists to argue that meaningful claims must rest on sensory verification.
- [[kant]] — the pivotal figure whose doctrine of the synthetic a priori was challenged by early analytic logicians and positivists.
- [[david-hume]] — the British forebear whose skepticism regarding induction and metaphysics inspired positivist verificationism.
- [[phenomenology]] — the prominent continental counterpart that favored experiential description over formal language analysis.
- [[mind-body-problem]] — the core puzzle of modern analytic metaphysics, approached through physicalism, functionalism, and studies of [[consciousness]].
- [[materialism]] — the prevailing ontological framework embraced by contemporary analytic philosophers of mind and science.
