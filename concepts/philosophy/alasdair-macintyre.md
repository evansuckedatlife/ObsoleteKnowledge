## summary

**Alasdair MacIntyre** is a Scottish-born philosopher celebrated as one of the most prominent moral and political theorists of the twentieth century. Best known for his 1981 masterwork *After Virtue*, he mounted an influential critique of modern moral discourse and spearheaded the contemporary revival of [[virtue-ethics]]. By championing a teleological framework rooted in [[aristotle]] and later synthesized with [[thomas-aquinas]], he challenged the foundational claims of the [[enlightenment]] and fundamentally reshaped contemporary philosophy.

## you gotta know

- Authored the landmark 1981 treatise *After Virtue*, which argued that modern moral language is in a state of grave disorder, reduced to fragmented linguistic survivals severed from the teleological worldviews that once gave them coherent meaning.
- Framed the central intellectual dilemma of modernity as a stark choice between [[aristotle]] and *Friedrich Nietzsche*, arguing that if the moral project of the [[enlightenment]] cannot be vindicated, *Nietzsche*'s radical nihilism and will to power remain the only consistent alternative.
- Formulated the famous "disquieting suggestion" opening thought experiment, describing a post-apocalyptic world in which natural science was obliterated by a public catastrophe and later rebuilt from detached fragments and formulas without any genuine comprehension of their underlying theoretical principles.
- Defined a virtue through three ascending stages: first as an acquired human quality necessary to achieve goods internal to a cooperative *practice*, second as an essential component of the narrative unity of an entire human *life*, and third as sustained by a living historical *tradition*.
- Diagnosed modern liberal culture as thoroughly captive to emotivism, the meta-ethical theory that moral judgments are nothing more than subjective expressions of personal approval or preference masquerading as objective rational claims.
- Developed his historicist critique of liberal neutrality in major sequels including *Whose Justice? Which Rationality?* and *Three Rival Versions of Moral Enquiry*, contending that all standards of practical rationality are embedded within particular, competing traditions.
- Addressed human physical vulnerability and disability in his 1999 work *Dependent Rational Animals*, maintaining that human flourishing depends on acknowledging bodily frailty, reciprocal social dependence, and cultivating the "virtues of acknowledged dependence."
- Began his academic career in [[england]] within the New Left, seeking during the 1950s and 1960s to synthesize [[marxism]] with radical [[christianity]] before abandoning both secular liberalism and orthodox Marxism in favor of Roman Catholic Thomism.

## connections

- [[aristotle]] — provided the foundational teleological ethics, virtue concepts, and theory of human flourishing at the core of his philosophical project.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — the ancient moral tradition he successfully revitalized as a primary alternative to modern consequentialist and rule-based systems.
- [[thomas-aquinas]] — medieval thinker whose synthesis of Christian revelation and classical philosophy shaped his mature understanding of moral traditions.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual movement whose attempt to establish an autonomous, universal secular foundation for morality he diagnosed as an inevitable failure.
- [[marxism]] — early political and theoretical allegiance that deeply shaped his ongoing critique of market capitalism, managerial elites, and liberal individualism.
- [[kant]] — eighteenth-century thinker whose deontological framework serves as a key example of the modern attempt to ground morality in pure reason alone.
- [[david-hume]] — Scottish philosopher whose desire-based sentimentalism exemplifies the fragmentation of practical reason during the modern era.
- [[theology]] — scholarly discipline informing his conversion to Catholicism and his defense of traditional communal inquiry against secular liberalism.
