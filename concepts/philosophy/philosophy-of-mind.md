## summary

**Philosophy of Mind** is the branch of philosophy examining the nature of mind, consciousness, and mental states, and their relationship to the physical body. It confronts foundational questions: What is consciousness? How do physical brains generate subjective experience? Do minds and bodies constitute two substances or one? These questions sit at the heart of metaphysics and epistemology.

## you gotta know

- Originated as a distinct discipline with *Descartes*, whose mind-body dualism made the relationship between mind and matter philosophically urgent and created the "mind-body problem."
- The *hard problem of consciousness* (articulated by David Chalmers) asks why physical processes produce subjective experience rather than occurring mechanically "in the dark" — this distinguishes the hard problem from merely explaining behavior.
- Major positions include *dualism* (mind and body are fundamentally different substances), *materialism* (mind reduces to physical brain processes), *functionalism* (mental states defined by functional roles), and *panpsychism* (consciousness fundamental to matter).
- *Qualia* — the subjective, qualitative character of experiences (redness, pain, taste) — remain central to philosophy of mind; how can physics explain why seeing red *feels* a specific way?
- Phenomenology offers an alternative methodological approach, describing the structures of conscious experience from the first-person perspective rather than seeking third-person physical reduction.
- The "explanatory gap" highlights how even complete neural knowledge seems unable to explain subjective experience; mental states have properties (intentionality, meaning) that seem irreducible to physical properties.

## connections

- [[consciousness]] — the central phenomenon philosophy of mind investigates, including its nature, origin, and relationship to physical processes.
- [[mind-body-problem]] — the core philosophical puzzle driving philosophy of mind research: how do physical brains generate or relate to consciousness?
- [[dualism]] — one major philosophical position claiming mind and body are fundamentally distinct substances or properties requiring separate explanation.
- [[materialism]] — opposing physicalist position claiming all mental phenomena reduce to or can be explained entirely by physical brain processes.
- [[qualia]] — the subjective, qualitative aspects of conscious experience that pose special problems for physicalist and functionalist theories.
- [[phenomenology]] — alternative philosophical method studying consciousness through careful first-person description rather than seeking third-person physical reduction.
- [[descartes]] — early modern originator of the mind-body problem who argued mind and body are distinct substances requiring explanation of their interaction.
