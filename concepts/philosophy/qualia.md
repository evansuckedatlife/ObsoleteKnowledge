## summary

**Qualia** are the subjective, qualitative features of conscious experience—the way things feel or appear to you. The redness of red, the painfulness of pain, the taste of coffee—these are qualia. The philosophical puzzle is how to account for qualia scientifically: if the brain is purely physical and objective, how do subjective qualities of experience arise from it? This explanatory gap between physical processes and subjective properties constitutes one of philosophy of mind's most vexing problems.

## you gotta know

- Qualia (singular: quale) are intrinsically subjective and first-personal: your experience of red is only knowable directly to you.
- The *hard problem of consciousness* turns largely on qualia: explaining why and how physical brain processes generate subjective experience.
- *Materialism* struggles with qualia: it seems that knowing all physical facts about the brain would not tell you what seeing red is like (the knowledge argument).
- The *explanatory gap*: there appears to be an unbridgeable gap between objective, third-person brain science and subjective, first-person experience.
- *Functionalism* tries to reduce qualia to functional roles (the property of responding to red in certain ways), but critics argue this misses the subjective feel.
- The *knowledge argument* (Frank Jackson's case of Mary the color scientist): could a person who knows all physical facts about color still be ignorant of what color looks like?
- Inverted spectra thought experiment: could your subjective experience of red be the mirror opposite of mine, yet we both call it "red"?
- The *representationalist* response: qualia are not intrinsic properties of experience but representations of external world features.
- *Illusionists* argue qualia as typically conceived do not really exist; what we call qualitative properties are really just ways things seem.
- *Panpsychism* is one response: if consciousness is fundamental, qualia are not anomalous—they are basic features of reality.

## connections

- [[consciousness]] — qualia are the defining feature of conscious experience that makes the mind-body problem acute.
- [[mind-body-problem]] — qualia embody the core mystery: how subjective properties relate to objective physical facts.
- [[materialism]] — faces the challenge of explaining how purely physical processes generate qualitative experience.
- [[dualism]] — qualia motivated some philosophers to posit a separate mental realm to accommodate subjective properties.
- [[philosophy-of-mind]] — qualia are central to contemporary philosophy of mind and cognitive science debates.
- [[panpsychism]] — one proposed solution: if consciousness is fundamental, qualia are not explanatorily problematic.
- [[descartes]] — raised questions about the divide between extended body and thinking mind that qualia now sharpen.
