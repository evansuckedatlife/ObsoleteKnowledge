## summary

**Ataraxia**, translating from Greek as "untroubledness" or "imperturbability," is the state of serene psychological calm and freedom from emotional turmoil prized across Hellenistic [[philosophy]] in [[ancient-greece]] and the later [[roman-empire]]. While most famously celebrated by *Epicurus* as the supreme good—achieved through simple living, natural understanding, and the removal of irrational anxiety—it was also pursued by Pyrrhonian Skeptics and esteemed alongside *apatheia* within [[stoicism]]. It represents one of antiquity's most enduring ethical ideals, offering a therapeutic framework for inner peace grounded in rational self-command and intellectual liberation.

## you gotta know

- Defined by *Epicurus* alongside *aponia* (the absence of bodily pain) as the essence of static pleasure (*katastematike hedone*), meaning that the highest human happiness consists not in active sensory stimulation, but in the complete tranquility of an undisturbed soul.
- Attained in Epicurean thought through the *tetrapharmakos* ("four-part cure"), a concise philosophical remedy declaring that the gods need not be feared, death brings no sensation, what is good is easy to get, and what is terrible is easy to endure.
- Diverges sharply from [[stoicism]], which viewed moral virtue (*arete*) as the sole intrinsic good and treated tranquil serenity as a secondary byproduct of *apatheia* (freedom from destructive passions), whereas Epicureanism regarded virtue as merely an instrument to secure personal calm.
- Cultivated socially through *lathe biosas* ("live unnoticed"), the Epicurean doctrine advising thinkers to withdraw from politics, honors, and civic ambition to share modest fellowship within secluded communities like the Athenian *Garden*.
- Emerged independently in Pyrrhonian Skepticism, where thinkers such as *Pyrrho of Elis* and *Sextus Empiricus* held that serenity arises naturally only after practicing *epoche* (suspension of judgment) on unresolved dogmatic claims.
- Illustrated in *Outlines of Pyrrhonism* by the famous anecdote of the painter *Apelles*, who, unable to depict lather on a horse's mouth, flung his sponge at the panel in exasperation, instantly producing the desired foam by accident rather than through anxious effort.
- Underpinned in Epicurean natural philosophy by an atomistic [[materialism]] inherited from *Democritus* and celebrated by *Lucretius*, which taught that the soul dissolves at physical death, freeing humans from superstitious terror regarding divine wrath or afterlife punishments.

## connections

- [[stoicism]] — shares the goal of emotional resilience and freedom from anxiety, though grounding peace in cosmic duty and virtue rather than pleasure.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural landscape where rival Hellenistic academies transformed speculative metaphysics into practical therapies for daily life.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — the classical moral tradition that Epicurean thinkers reinterpreted, viewing justice, temperance, and prudence primarily as tools for preserving mental calm.
- [[materialism]] — the mechanistic physics of indivisible atoms and void that dispelled religious anxieties and provided an empirical foundation for peace of mind.
- [[roman-empire]] — the historical era in which thinkers like *Lucretius*, *Seneca*, and *Marcus Aurelius* adapted Greek techniques of tranquil living for Roman citizens.
- [[philosophy]] — the overarching intellectual discipline that Hellenistic thinkers conceived as medicine for the soul rather than mere abstract contemplation.
- [[free-will]] — the concept protected by the Epicurean atomic swerve (*clinamen*), which preserved moral agency from fatalistic determinism.
