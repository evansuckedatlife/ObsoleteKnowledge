## summary

**God**, philosophically, is understood as the transcendent, supreme being in Western religious and philosophical traditions—variously conceived as infinite, omnipotent, omniscient, and morally perfect. Philosophers have constructed elaborate arguments for God's existence (cosmological, ontological, teleological) and debated God's nature intensely (whether God is personal or abstract, temporal or eternal, whether God's knowledge is compatible with human freedom). God is both a core religious concept and a central topic in metaphysics, epistemology, and philosophy of religion. The question of God's existence has shaped Western philosophy from ancient times through the present, driving major developments in logic, metaphysics, and natural theology.

## you gotta know

- *Descartes* offered proof of God through the idea of the infinite: only an infinite being could implant the idea of infinity in a finite mind.
- *Thomas Aquinas'* "Five Ways" argue for God's existence from motion, causality, contingency, degrees of perfection, and cosmic order.
- The *ontological argument* (from Anselm) claims God necessarily exists because God is defined as the greatest conceivable being, and existence is greater than non-existence.
- *Problem of evil*: if God is omnipotent and good, why does evil exist? Philosophers have developed theodicies attempting to reconcile God's perfection with a flawed world.
- Medieval theology (especially Aquinas) tried to reconcile God's omniscience with human free will — if God knows future choices, are they truly free?
- Modern philosophy has increasingly questioned whether God-existence claims are rationally justifiable (atheism, agnosticism) or whether faith transcends rational proof.

## connections

- [[descartes]] — formulated proofs of God's existence as central to his Rationalist philosophy and epistemology.
- [[trinity]] — the Christian theological doctrine of God's threefold nature (Father, Son, Holy Spirit) that philosophical theology must reconcile with monotheism.
- [[theology]] — the discipline investigating God's nature, attributes, and relationship to creation and humanity.
- [[thomas-aquinas]] — medieval theologian who systematized classical proofs for God's existence through reason, influencing all subsequent natural theology.
- [[existence-of-god]] — the fundamental philosophical question at the center of metaphysics, epistemology, and philosophy of religion.
- [[rationalism]] — philosophical tradition from Descartes through Leibniz attempting to prove God through reason and logic.
- [[christianity]] — the religious tradition for which God is the transcendent creator and judge, making philosophical theology central to faith.
