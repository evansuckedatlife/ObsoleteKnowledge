## summary

**Meaning** in philosophy concerns the significance, purpose, or intelligibility that humans perceive in existence, actions, and the world. The question "What is the meaning of life?" became urgent in modernity as traditional religious frameworks weakened, spawning existentialism, nihilism, and other responses. Philosophers ask whether meaning is discovered, created, or merely projected onto an indifferent universe.

## you gotta know

- *Nihilism* challenges meaning itself: if nothing has inherent value or purpose, all meaning might be illusory.
- *Existentialists* like *Sartre* argue that life has no predetermined meaning; we must create meaning through our choices and commitments.
- *Absurdism* (associated with *Camus*) accepts that the universe is meaningless but insists we can still live authentically without self-deception.
- Religious and moral frameworks traditionally grounded meaning in God, cosmic order, or universal duties; their erosion creates the modern "meaning crisis."
- Phenomenology examines how meaning emerges through lived experience and our engagement with the world.
- Psychology and neuroscience explore whether meaning is neurologically constructed or reflects something real about purpose.
- *Narrative* has become central to meaning-making: humans find meaning by weaving their experiences into coherent stories or life projects.
- The difference between *meaning* (overall purpose) and *significance* (temporary importance): a life can have meaning without every moment feeling significant.
- Meaning-making involves *values*: what we consider meaningful depends on what we prioritize and value.
- Psychological research suggests that meaning-making is not optional: humans naturally seek meaning, and the absence of meaning correlates with depression and despair.
- *Transcendence* (connecting to something beyond the self) is often central to meaningful living: relationships, spirituality, art, or causes larger than oneself.
- The search for meaning is not merely abstract: it drives human motivation, resilience through suffering, and the creation of art, ritual, and community.

## connections

- [[nihilism]] — denies the existence of objective meaning; frames the central challenge meaning philosophy must address.
- [[existentialism]] — responds to meaninglessness by asserting that humans are radically free to create meaning.
- [[morality]] — closely linked; moral meaning (the significance of ethical action) is inseparable from the larger question of life's purpose.
- [[absurdism]] — *Camus*' response to meaninglessness: live fully despite the universe's silence.
- [[consciousness]] — the awareness through which meaning is perceived and constructed.
- [[philosophy]] — the discipline that makes meaning itself a problem to be examined.
- [[friedrich-nietzsche]] — diagnosed the crisis of meaning in modernity and called for the creation of new values.
