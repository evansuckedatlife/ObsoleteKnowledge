## summary

**Epictetus** was a Greek-born Stoic philosopher who lived as a slave in *Rome* before gaining his freedom and establishing an influential philosophical academy in *Nicopolis*. Flourishing during the late 1st and early 2nd centuries CE under the *[[roman-empire]]*, he taught that true peace of mind comes from distinguishing what is within our control from what is not. His practical moral philosophy, recorded by his pupil *Arrian*, became one of the defining expressions of late *[[stoicism]]* and profoundly shaped ancient ethics.

## you gotta know

- Emphasised the fundamental dichotomy of control (*prohairesis*), dividing all reality into things up to us (*eph' hemin*), such as our judgments, desires, and impulses, and things not up to us (*ouk eph' hemin*), such as our bodies, property, reputations, and deaths.
- Did not write down his philosophy himself; his spoken lectures and informal seminars were compiled and preserved by his devoted student *Arrian* in the *Discourses* (*Diatribai*) and the concise ethical handbook known as the *Enchiridion*.
- Born into slavery in *Hierapolis* in *Phrygia*, he served the cruel freedman *Epaphroditus* in *Rome*, who allegedly broke his leg or allowed him to be crippled, leading to a permanent limp that Epictetus accepted with characteristic equanimity.
- Studied under the renowned Stoic teacher *Musonius Rufus* while still enslaved, acquiring the rigorous intellectual discipline that he later imparted after being manumitted following the death of Emperor *Nero*.
- Banished from *Rome* around 93 CE when Emperor *Domitian* expelled all philosophers from *[[italy]]*, prompting Epictetus to relocate to *Nicopolis* in northwestern *[[ancient-greece]]*, where his school attracted prominent Roman aristocrats.
- Maintained that freedom (*eleutheria*) is an internal condition rather than a legal status, arguing that an emperor slave to his passions is far less free than a shackled captive who retains moral autonomy.
- Greatly inspired the personal reflections of Roman Emperor *Marcus Aurelius*, who repeatedly referenced Epictetus in his private journal, the *Meditations*, especially thanking his tutor *Junius Rusticus* for introducing him to Epictetus's notes.
- Held that achieving *[[eudaimonia]]* requires aligning human will with cosmic reason (*logos*) and providence, viewing external adversities as athletic training (*askesis*) designed by God to fortify the soul's virtue.

## connections

- [[stoicism]] — the philosophical tradition of which he is one of the three great late Roman exponents alongside *Seneca* and *Marcus Aurelius*.
- [[eudaimonia]] — the state of flourishing and peace (*ataraxia*) achieved by mastering the dichotomy of control and living virtuously.
- [[freedom]] — the central ethical ideal he redefined as internal rational autonomy rather than physical liberty or civic privilege.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial civilization where he lived as a slave in *Rome* and later taught provincial elites in *Nicopolis*.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — the ethical framework prioritizing moral character and rational will over worldly consequences or external fortune.
- [[free-will]] — the doctrine of moral choice (*prohairesis*) he defended as invulnerable to external physical coercion.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural homeland where he was born, spoke his native Greek, and established his philosophical school.
