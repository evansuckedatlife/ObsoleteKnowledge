## summary

**Authenticity** is an existentialist value meaning living in accordance with one's genuine self and freely chosen values rather than conforming to social expectations or adopting prescribed roles. *Heidegger* pioneered the philosophical distinction between authentic and inauthentic modes of existence, arguing that most people live inauthentically in the "they-self" — absorbed in social conventions. Authenticity requires courage: the willingness to face one's freedom and responsibility for oneself.

## you gotta know

- *Heidegger* contrasted authentic existence (*Eigentlichkeit*) with falling into the anonymous "they" (*das Man*) — the realm of social conformity, idle talk, and distraction.
- Authenticity demands confronting anxiety, finitude, and death — recognizing one's own mortality and thrownness into a world not of one's making, which separates genuine existence from distraction in social roles.
- For *Sartre*, authenticity is inseparable from freedom and radical responsibility; *bad faith* is its opposite: pretending you must obey predetermined roles or that your choices are not truly your own.
- The authentic self is not a fixed essence to be discovered but something continuously created through free choice, commitment to values, and willingness to face one's situation.
- Authenticity became a central value in twentieth-century culture, yet paradoxically becomes commodified and corrupted when marketed as a lifestyle commodity — "authentic" fashion, music, experience.
- Existentialist authenticity differs from Romantic authenticity (expressing inner feeling); it emphasizes responsibility and conscious commitment to values rather than spontaneous emotional expression.
- Living authentically requires rejecting the comforting illusions of social conformity and false certainties; it is existentially precarious but philosophically necessary for genuine freedom and meaning.

## connections

- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical tradition that made authenticity a central ethical and existential concern in twentieth-century thought.
- [[martin-heidegger]] — originator of the philosophical distinction between authentic existence (*Eigentlichkeit*) and inauthentic immersion in the "they."
- [[jean-paul-sartre]] — elaborated authenticity through the concept of *bad faith*, the inauthentic attempt to deny one's freedom and responsibility.
- [[bad-faith]] — existentialism's name for inauthentic self-deception; pretending to lack freedom when authenticity demands accepting it.
- [[freedom]] — philosophical prerequisite for authenticity; one must be conscious of one's freedom to live authentically.
- [[phenomenology]] — the descriptive method both Heidegger and Sartre employed to analyze authentic and inauthentic modes of human existence.
