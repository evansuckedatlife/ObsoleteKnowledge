## summary

**Metaphysics** is the philosophical inquiry into the fundamental nature of reality: what exists, what is the relationship between mind and matter, whether change and time are real, and whether there are universal substances or forms underlying appearances. The name derives from *Aristotle's* works arranged "after the physics," but the questions predate him—the pre-Socratics obsessed over being and non-being, permanence and change. Metaphysics remains the most ambitious and controversial branch of philosophy because it claims knowledge about what lies "beyond the physical," yet its objects (being, substance, causation) are inherently difficult to define and verify.

## you gotta know

- *Parmenides* posed the founding problem: if something changes, does "it" become something other than itself, violating the law of non-contradiction? His radical conclusion—that change is illusion and true being is eternal and unchanging—haunted all subsequent metaphysics.
- *Aristotle* named the field and gave it structure: metaphysics asks what is *substance* (the independent thing that exists), what is *form* (the organizing principle), what is *actuality* (realized being), and what is *causation* (the four causes: material, efficient, formal, and final).
- The *mind-body problem* is metaphysics' most urgent modern puzzle: if the physical world is all that exists, how do consciousness and subjective experience arise? Is the mind physical, immaterial, or a fundamental feature of reality?
- *Substance dualism* (*Descartes*, *Descartes*' model) posits two substances—mind and matter—solving the problem by denying physicalism, but it generates the interaction problem: how do immaterial thoughts cause physical actions?
- *Materialism* (or physicalism) argues that only physical substances and properties exist; mental phenomena are either identical to brain states or reduced to them. This avoids dualism's problems but struggles to explain consciousness.
- *Idealism* flips the equation: reality consists fundamentally of mind, ideas, or consciousness; the physical world is mind-dependent. This preserves the uniqueness of consciousness but seems counterintuitive about external objects.
- Metaphysicians debate whether *universals* (properties, kinds, numbers) exist independent of the physical world or are merely conventional patterns imposed by minds—this is the problem of universals, dating to Plato and *Aristotle*.
- Modern physics (relativity, quantum mechanics) has unsettled metaphysics: simultaneity is relative; particles exist in superposition until measured; spacetime is curved. The metaphysical interpretation of physics remains contested.

## connections

- [[aristotle]] — named metaphysics and systematized its central questions about substance, form, and causation.
- [[parmenides]] — posed the founding metaphysical crisis: the problem of change and being.
- [[pre-socratic-philosophy]] — the pre-Socratics were the first metaphysicians, debating the nature of ultimate reality.
- [[descartes]] — revived substance dualism in the modern era and sharpened the mind-body problem.
- [[kant]] — argued that space and time are structures of the mind, not properties of things-in-themselves (transcendental idealism).
- [[philosophy]] — metaphysics is the heart of philosophy; all other branches depend on metaphysical assumptions.
- [[consciousness]] — the central phenomenon metaphysics struggles to explain.
