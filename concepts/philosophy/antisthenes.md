## summary

**Antisthenes** (c. 445–365 BCE) was an Athenian philosopher and direct student of *Socrates*, remembered as the founder of the *Cynic* school. He argued that virtue is the only true good and that happiness comes from rejecting society's false values and material desires. His radical asceticism and indifference to convention became the blueprint for one of the ancient world's most contrarian philosophical movements.

## you gotta know

- A student of *Socrates* whose teachings led him to reject conventional pursuits like wealth, honor, and physical pleasure.
- Founded Cynicism, a school emphasizing virtue, self-sufficiency, and a return to nature as the path to freedom and happiness.
- Believed that true happiness comes through the hard work of overcoming bodily desires, not through acquiring possessions.
- Taught that a philosopher should live simply and publicly, practicing extreme modesty and indifference to social shame.
- Established the principle of *askesis* (rigorous training or discipline), arguing that virtue must be practiced like an athlete trains the body.
- Rejected contemporary Athenian education and rhetoric as hollow: wisdom lies not in clever speech but in virtuous living and self-mastery.
- His philosophy was less systematized than *Plato's*; he left no written works, known only through the accounts of later figures.
- Argued that a person's worth is determined solely by their virtue and character, not by birth, wealth, or social status.
- Maintained that the ideal sage should be free from all desires for luxury, honor, and bodily comfort to achieve inner freedom.
- His student *Diogenes* carried his ideas to their most extreme, becoming the legendary archetype of the wandering, countercultural Cynic.

## connections

- [[socrates]] — his teacher; the Socratic emphasis on virtue became the core of Cynicism.
- [[diogenes]] — his most famous student, who radicalized Cynical practice to notorious extremes.
- [[cynicism]] — the school he founded, centered on virtue and rejection of social convention.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — his framework rooted happiness in the cultivation of virtue, not pleasure.
- [[stoicism]] — later drew on Cynic ideas about virtue and indifference to external circumstance.
- [[plato]] — contemporary pupil of *Socrates* with a very different philosophical trajectory.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural context in which his radical ideas challenged Athenian values.
