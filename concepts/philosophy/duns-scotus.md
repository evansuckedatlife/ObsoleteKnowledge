## summary

**Duns Scotus**, full name **John Duns Scotus**, was an eminent Scottish Franciscan philosopher and theologian of the High Middle Ages who earned the traditional scholastic title **Doctor Subtilis** for his exceptionally intricate logical analyses. Teaching at Oxford and Paris, he mounted formidable critiques of *Thomas Aquinas* and *Henry of Ghent*, fundamentally reshaping medieval metaphysics and epistemology. His pioneering doctrines of the univocity of being, the reality of haecceity, and the primacy of the will established him as a central figure of Western scholasticism.

## you gotta know

- Celebrated throughout scholastic literature as the *Subtle Doctor* (*Doctor Subtilis*) due to the unprecedented rigor and layered distinctions characteristic of his theological disputations.
- Introduced the metaphysical principle of *haecceity* ("thisness"), proposing that an individual is not individuated merely by matter or form, but by an ultimate, irreducible entity that makes a specific thing uniquely itself.
- Championed the radical thesis of the *univocity of being*, maintaining against [[thomas-aquinas]] that the concept of "being" (*ens*) possesses an identical, uniform meaning whether predicated of God or finite creatures.
- Defended theological voluntarism, asserting that divine and human free will takes precedence over intellect, and that moral truths depend directly on God's sovereign choices rather than rational necessity.
- Formulated the definitive theological justification for the *Immaculate Conception* of Mary, resolving long-standing scholastic objections by arguing that Christ's redemption preserved Mary from original sin in advance through *praeredemptio*.
- Devised the *formal distinction* (*distinctio formalis a parte rei*), positing an intermediate distinction where entities are inseparable in reality yet possess distinct objective definitions prior to mental abstraction.
- Instructed students at Oxford, Paris, and Cologne, dying abruptly in 1308 in Cologne, where his tomb famously commemorates his European academic journey across Scotland, England, France, and Germany.
- Mocked centuries later by Renaissance humanists and Protestant reformers as an excessively pedantic scholastic whose devoted followers were dismissed as "dunces," giving rise to the modern insult.

## connections

- [[thomas-aquinas]] — preeminent Dominican master whose doctrines of analogy, intellectualism, and individuation were directly challenged by Scotus.
- [[theology]] — academic discipline within which Scotus constructed his most celebrated arguments on divine freedom and Marian dogma.
- [[christianity]] — religious tradition whose medieval Catholic theological doctrine was permanently influenced by Scotus's Franciscan teachings.
- [[aristotle]] — classical philosopher whose metaphysical categories and syllogistic structures were refined and adapted by Scotus.
- [[free-will]] — central philosophical problem elevated by Scotus's robust voluntarism, which asserted the radical contingency of the created world.
- [[logic]] — analytical tool wielded by Scotus with peerless precision to construct his formal distinctions and proof of the first principle.
- [[dialectics]] — scholastic practice of rigorous disputation through which Scotus presented conflicting opinions before synthesizing his resolutions.
