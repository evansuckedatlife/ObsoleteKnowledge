## summary

**Auguste Comte** was a nineteenth-century French thinker widely recognized as the father of modern sociology and the founder of the philosophical system of positivism. Active in [[france]] amid the social upheaval following the [[french-revolution]] and the rise of the [[industrial-revolution]], he sought to reorganize human civilization upon an objective, scientific foundation. His formulation of the *law of three stages* and his rigorous classification of the sciences profoundly shaped modern social theory, epistemology, and the tradition of [[empiricism]].

## you gotta know

- Formulated the famous *law of three stages*, asserting that human intellect and society inevitably evolve through three distinct epochs: the theological stage (governed by gods and animism), the metaphysical stage (governed by abstract forces and philosophical essences), and the positive stage (governed by empirical observation, scientific inquiry, and natural laws).
- Coined the term *sociology* in his six-volume masterwork *Course in Positive Philosophy* (*Cours de philosophie positive*), having initially used *physique sociale* ("social physics") before abandoning the moniker when Belgian statistician *Adolphe Quetelet* appropriated it.
- Organized an influential hierarchy of the sciences arranged by increasing complexity and decreasing generality: mathematics, astronomy, physics, chemistry, biology, and sociology as the crowning "queen of sciences"; he famously denied psychology an independent status, absorbing it into biology and sociology.
- Coined the moral term *altruism* (*altruisme*) from the Italian *altrui*, defining it as the ethical obligation of selfless devotion to others and formulating the positivist motto to "live for others" (*vivre pour autrui*).
- Established the secular *Religion of Humanity* in his later four-volume *System of Positive Polity*, creating a positivist liturgical calendar and temples dedicated to great historical figures; his motto "Order and Progress" (*Ordem e Progresso*) was later adopted onto the national flag of Brazil.
- Served as secretary and intimate collaborator to early French socialist *Henri de Saint-Simon* from 1817 until an acrimonious split in 1824 over intellectual credit and the proper direction of industrial society.
- Endured a severe nervous breakdown in 1826 that led to an attempted suicide leap into the Seine from the Pont des Arts, later attributing his spiritual rebirth to his intense platonic romance with *Clotilde de Vaux*.
- Achieved widespread international readership after British author *Harriet Martineau* translated and brilliantly condensed his six-volume *Course* into English, producing an edition Comte himself praised as clearer than his French original.

## connections

- [[empiricism]] — provided the philosophical foundation for positivism by rejecting theological speculation in favor of verifiable sensory observation.
- [[french-revolution]] — produced the political chaos and social fragmentation that Comte dedicated his life to reforming and stabilizing.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — reshaped European class structures and urban life, creating the urgent demand for a systematic, scientific study of society.
- [[david-hume]] — pioneer of British empiricism whose critique of causation and metaphysics directly foreshadowed Comte's positive method.
- [[rationalism]] — embodied the abstract, deductive metaphysical stage of thought that positivism was designed to overcome.
- [[france]] — his birthplace and primary sphere of influence, where he taught at the École Polytechnique and established the Positivist Society.
- [[materialism]] — mechanistic worldview related to positivism, though Comte cautioned that positivism must avoid metaphysical dogmas about physical substances.
