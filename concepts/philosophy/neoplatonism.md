## summary

**Neoplatonism** is a philosophical school that reinterpreted *Plato* through a mystical, hierarchical lens, emerging in the 3rd century CE and dominating late ancient thought until the closure of philosophical schools in the 6th century. It posits a hierarchy of reality descending from *the One* (ultimate reality) through intelligence and soul to matter, with the goal of achieving mystical union with the divine. Its synthesis of reason and mysticism profoundly influenced Christian theology, Islamic philosophy, and Renaissance art.

## you gotta know

- Founded by *Plotinus* (204–270 CE), who systematized Platonism into a coherent metaphysical system centered on emanation—the flow of reality from the One.
- Posits *the One*: an utterly transcendent, ineffable source beyond being, knowledge, and description; all reality emanates from it.
- The hierarchy of emanation: the One → Intellect (divine mind) → Soul (world-soul and individual souls) → Matter (inert, the lowest principle).
- Mysticism is integral: through philosophy, purification, and contemplation, the soul can achieve *henosis*, mystical union with the One.
- *Plotinus* taught in Rome and his followers compiled his writings into the *Enneads* (fifty-four treatises), the foundational texts of Neoplatonism.
- The principle of emanation differs from creation: reality flows from the One necessarily and eternally, not through will or choice.
- *Porphyry*, Plotinus's student, systematized Neoplatonic doctrines and added a crucial modification: souls have individual responsibilities and levels of spiritual development.
- Heavily influenced medieval and Renaissance thought through Christian, Islamic, and Jewish philosophers who integrated Neoplatonic ideas into monotheistic theology.
- *Michelangelo* drew on Neoplatonic aesthetics: the fusion of divine and human beauty, the soul's ascent through sensory to intellectual beauty.
- Distinct from *Stoicism* but shares the emphasis on a unified, ordered cosmos and the soul's return to cosmic principles.

## connections

- [[plato]] — the ancient philosopher whose ideas Neoplatonism reinterprets and systematizes.
- [[the-one]] — the transcendent source of all reality in Neoplatonic metaphysics.
- [[stoicism]] — shares emphasis on cosmic order and the integration of individual soul into universal principles.
- [[christianity]] — deeply influenced Christian theology through thinkers like Augustine and Pseudo-Dionysius.
- [[michelangelo]] — Renaissance artist whose philosophy of beauty and form reflects Neoplatonic ideals.
- [[renaissance]] — the period in which Neoplatonic texts were recovered and reintegrated into Western thought.
- [[being]] — central to Neoplatonic metaphysics; hierarchy of being from the One downward.
