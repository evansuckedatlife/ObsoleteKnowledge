## summary

**Autarkeia** is the classical Greek philosophical ideal of self-sufficiency, inner independence, and ethical liberation from reliance on external fortune and material desire. Emerging prominently through the dialectic of *Socrates* and the radical lifestyle of the Cynics, the concept evolved into an essential standard of happiness throughout [[ancient-greece]]. In classical [[virtue-ethics]], it designates the state in which a person or a community possesses all internal resources necessary to live well, rendering the pursuit of flourishing resilient against sudden changes in circumstance.

## you gotta know

- Represents the foundational conviction across Hellenistic thought that true happiness requires mental autonomy and freedom from unnecessary physical or societal dependencies.
- *Aristotle* designated self-sufficiency as a mandatory criterion for the supreme good in the *Nicomachean Ethics*, insisting that true flourishing (*eudaimonia*) must be complete in itself and leave life in want of nothing.
- Exemplified in its most uncompromising form by *Diogenes of Sinope* and the Cynics, who renounced conventional wealth, lived in public tubs or porticos, and proved that minimal material needs yielded maximum personal [[freedom]].
- *Socrates* served as the classical archetype of the ideal, with *Xenophon* recording in his *Memorabilia* that while the divine has no needs, those who need the least come nearest to divine perfection.
- In [[stoicism]], thinkers such as *Epictetus*, *Seneca*, and *Marcus Aurelius* argued that moral virtue is entirely sufficient for happiness, framing external possessions, status, and health as morally indifferent things.
- *Epicurus* embraced self-sufficiency not as ascetic self-punishment, but as the practical art of simplifying desires, famously stating that plain bread and water bring supreme pleasure when hunger is genuine.
- Transferred from individual psychology to political theory by *Plato* and *Aristotle*, who argued in the *Politics* that a community only becomes a genuine city-state when it reaches structural and economic independence.
- Contrasted in modern philosophical thought with political and economic autarky, transitioning from an ancient interior virtue of self-governance into modern debates surrounding national isolationism and state isolation.

## connections

- [[stoicism]] — positioned inner self-sufficiency at the heart of ethics, asserting that virtue alone suffices for a serene life.
- [[aristotle]] — identified self-sufficiency as the defining hallmark of the highest human good and the ultimate purpose of the political community.
- [[socrates]] — modeled the austere lifestyle that inspired both Cynic asceticism and Stoic detachment from external luxuries.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — incorporates the concept as a psychological discipline that shields moral character from external corruptions and dependencies.
- [[freedom]] — serves as the central moral payoff of self-sufficiency, liberating the philosopher from social coercion and irrational cravings.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the historical and cultural milieu where classical thinkers debated whether independence belonged to the individual soul or the polis.
- [[roman-empire]] — provided the turbulent imperial backdrop where Stoics cultivated interior self-reliance against political persecution and exile.
- [[plato]] — explored self-sufficiency in the *Republic* by investigating whether individuals can truly fulfill their own physical and moral needs in isolation.
