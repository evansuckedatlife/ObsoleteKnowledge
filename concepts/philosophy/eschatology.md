## summary

**Eschatology** is the branch of *[[theology]]* and philosophy concerned with the "last things" (*ta eschata*), including death, the final judgment, the destiny of the soul, and the ultimate end of history. Originating in ancient apocalyptic traditions of the *[[middle-east]]*, particularly within *[[zoroastrianism]]*, it became central to Western and Near Eastern religious thought. The concept examines how human existence and historical time derive meaning, culmination, and moral resolution from an envisioned catastrophic or redemptive finale.

## you gotta know

- Etymology derives from the Greek words *eschatos* (meaning "last" or "furthest") and *logos* ("study" or "discourse"), designating both individual afterlife expectations and universal cosmic consummations.
- Pioneered in its systematic cosmic form by *[[zoroastrianism]]*, which introduced ideas of a final cosmic purification, the renovation of the world (*Frashokereti*), a general resurrection, and the destruction of evil.
- Features a decisive cosmic battle between absolute moral forces, deeply rooted in cosmic *[[dualism]]*, wherein a savior figure (*Saoshyant*) assists in establishing eternal divine righteousness.
- Strongly influenced post-exilic *[[judaism]]*, particularly the apocalyptic literature of the Second Temple period such as the Book of Daniel, introducing notions of bodily resurrection and divine cosmic reckoning.
- Constitutes a foundational pillar of *[[christianity]]*, centering on the Second Coming of Christ (*Parousia*), the resurrection of the dead, the Last Judgment, and the establishment of the New Jerusalem depicted in the Book of Revelation.
- Distinguishes between individual eschatology (personal death, particular judgment, purgation, heaven, and hell) and general or cosmic eschatology (the collective destiny of humanity, cosmic renovation, and the end of time).
- Categorized in modern theological studies into competing interpretive models, including realized eschatology (the kingdom of God present now), inaugurated eschatology ("already but not yet"), and futurist or dispensationalist apocalypticism.
- Secularized in modern political philosophy by thinkers who transposed religious narratives of historical culmination into teleological philosophies of history, such as *[[marxism]]*'s utopian classless society.

## connections

- [[theology]] — the overarching study of religious doctrine and divine matters within which doctrines of the end times are formulated.
- [[zoroastrianism]] — the ancient Iranian faith that originated linear theological time, universal resurrection, and the final renovation of the cosmos.
- [[christianity]] — the religion whose New Testament narrative culminates in the Apocalypse, the general resurrection, and Christ's final victory over death.
- [[judaism]] — the prophetic tradition that developed visions of the Day of the Lord and the Messianic Age of universal peace and justice.
- [[dualism]] — the philosophical and religious paradigm dividing reality between competing forces of light and darkness until the final triumph of good.
- [[middle-east]] — the historical region whose interlocking sacred traditions birthed linear apocalyptic and messianic visions.
- [[marxism]] — the political ideology that translated theological end-of-history narratives into a secular, materialist dialectic of revolution.
