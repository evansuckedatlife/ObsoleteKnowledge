## summary

**Bad faith** is *Jean-Paul Sartre*'s term for radical self-deception—a denial of one's own freedom and responsibility by fleeing into predetermined social roles or pretending to lack agency. It is the core failure of existentialist authenticity, the deliberate lie one tells oneself to escape the burden of radical freedom.

## you gotta know

- Sartre argues humans are "condemned to be free"—we cannot escape our freedom or the responsibility it entails.
- Bad faith is the denial of this freedom; the conscious yet dishonest flight into false determinism ("I am the waiter" becomes an identity rather than a role).
- It involves a doubled consciousness: one part knows the truth of one's freedom, while the other pretends not to, maintaining the lie through self-imposed ignorance.
- Factical situations (class, body, history) constrain choice but do not eliminate freedom; bad faith confuses constraint with necessity.
- Examples include the woman who treats her body as an object to avoid sexual choice; the café waiter who merges with the job's mechanics to avoid personal responsibility.
- Authenticity demands acknowledging one's freedom and taking full responsibility for one's choices—the difficult opposite of bad faith.
- Often intertwined with social pretense: adopting rigid personas that conceal agency; blaming circumstances or roles to evade moral judgment.
- Bad faith is universal and inescapable in society—we all succumb to it to some degree; recognizing it is the first step toward existential authenticity.
- The lie is paradoxical: one must believe the lie to tell it effectively; it is not simple hypocrisy but a sophisticated self-deception maintained through psychological effort.
- Sartre used bad faith to critique both traditional philosophy (which denies freedom) and those who flee into mass conformity to avoid the anguish of choice.

## connections

- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical framework that grounds bad faith as a fundamental human problem.
- [[jean-paul-sartre]] — the philosopher who developed and centred the concept in existentialism.
- [[freedom]] — the freedom bad faith denies or evades through self-deception.
- [[authenticity]] — the existentialist virtue that requires acknowledging and embracing one's freedom.
- [[responsibility]] — the burden one avoids through bad faith; accepting it is existential maturity.
- [[phenomenology]] — the descriptive method Sartre used to uncover the structures of consciousness and self-deception.
- [[kant]] — Kant's categorical imperative and duty provide a contrasting framework for ethical obligation.
