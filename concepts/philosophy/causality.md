## summary

**Causality** is the relationship between cause and effect: the principle that one thing brings about or necessitates another through some mechanism, force, or connection. Causality is essential to science and common sense, yet philosophers vigorously debate what causation truly is and whether it is a real feature of nature. *Hume* famously challenged the view that we directly perceive causal necessity, arguing we only observe constant conjunction of events followed by mental habit. *Kant* reframed the debate by arguing causality is a structure imposed by the human mind rather than directly perceived. Modern physics raises profound questions about whether causality governs the universe at fundamental levels and whether determinism is compatible with quantum indeterminacy.

## you gotta know

- *Hume* distinguished between the constant conjunction of events (what we observe) and necessary causation (which we cannot perceive), suggesting causation is a mental habit, not a feature of reality.
- *Kant* responded to Hume by arguing causality is an a priori structure of human understanding — a category that organizes experience rather than existing in things themselves.
- Causality underlies the logic of scientific explanation: science seeks causal laws governing natural phenomena.
- Determinism claims that causality is universal — every event has prior causes and the future is determined by the past.
- Quantum mechanics challenges classical causality by introducing fundamental indeterminacy; at the subatomic level, causation appears probabilistic, not deterministic.
- The relationship between causality and *free will* is central to philosophy of action: if all events are causally determined, can human choices be genuinely free?
- Philosophers distinguish between efficient causality (one thing producing an effect) and formal, material, and final causality, expanding beyond simple mechanical cause-and-effect models.

## connections

- [[free-will]] — directly challenged if causality is universal, making all events causally determined and freedom illusory.
- [[determinism]] — the philosophical view that universal causality determines all future events from prior causes.
- [[science]] — relies on causal explanation as its fundamental method; scientific laws describe causal relationships.
- [[david-hume]] — empiricist philosopher who skeptically analyzed what causation truly is, questioning whether we perceive anything beyond constant conjunction.
- [[kant]] — argued causality is an a priori structure of human understanding, not something directly perceived in things themselves.
- [[empiricism]] — philosophical tradition emphasizing direct experience of cause and effect as the basis for knowledge.
