## summary

**Effective altruism**, commonly abbreviated as **EA**, is a 21st-century philosophical project and social movement that applies evidence, quantitative reasoning, and consequentialist ethics to determine the most impactful ways to improve the world. Emerging from analytical ethics at Oxford and spreading through philanthropic communities in [[england]] and the [[united-states]], it prioritizes cost-effectiveness over emotional appeal in charitable giving. It has profoundly reshaped contemporary debates concerning philanthropic transparency, animal liberation, and long-term existential risk mitigation.

## you gotta know

- Rooted philosophically in *Peter Singer*'s 1972 essay *Famine, Affluence, and Morality*, which introduced the shallow pond thought experiment to argue that affluent individuals have a moral obligation to prevent suffering comparable to saving a drowning child.
- Established institutional foundations in Oxford during the late 2000s under philosophers *William MacAskill* and *Toby Ord*, who co-founded *Giving What We Can* and *80,000 Hours* to encourage pledge-based giving and high-impact careers.
- Employs empirical metrics such as Quality-Adjusted Life Years (*QALYs*) and Disability-Adjusted Life Years (*DALYs*) to evaluate interventions, relying heavily on independent charity evaluators like *GiveWell*.
- Popularized the controversial strategy of "earning to give," which advises morally motivated individuals to seek lucrative careers in banking, software engineering, or finance in order to donate substantial sums to highly effective charities.
- Categorizes philanthropic focus into three major cause areas: global poverty and health, farmed animal suffering in industrial agriculture, and existential threats that could permanently extinguish humanity's potential.
- Embraced the sub-philosophy of *longtermism*, articulated in works like *The Precipice* by *Toby Ord* and *What We Owe the Future* by *William MacAskill*, prioritizing the welfare of astronomical future generations over immediate localized concerns.
- Heavily influenced by *Derek Parfit*'s 1984 treatise *Reasons and Persons*, particularly its rigorous analysis of population ethics and the paradoxes surrounding the *Repugnant Conclusion*.
- Criticized by political theorists and traditional humanitarians for prioritizing quantifiable technocratic interventions while allegedly neglecting systemic political reform, institutional critique, and democratic accountability.

## connections

- [[virtue-ethics]] — rival normative framework prioritizing character and moral virtues rather than utilitarian impact calculations.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical movement whose commitment to scientific reason and universal human welfare anticipates effective altruism.
- [[david-hume]] — Scottish philosopher whose early articulation of moral sentiments and utility helped pave the way for modern utilitarianism.
- [[rationalism]] — epistemological stance reflected in the movement's commitment to Bayesian probabilistic reasoning and quantitative metrics.
- [[england]] — country where Oxford University served as the intellectual birthplace and early organizational hub of the movement.
- [[united-states]] — nation housing major affiliated funding foundations, tech hubs, and non-profit research institutes.
- [[east-asian-philosophy]] — contrasting ethical system that traditionally emphasizes relational duties and concentric circles of care rather than universal impartiality.
