## summary

**Materialism** is the philosophical position that all that exists is ultimately physical matter (or its arrangements and interactions), with no immaterial substances or properties. It directly opposes *dualism* by rejecting the existence of a separate mind or soul distinct from the body. Materialism underpins much of modern science and philosophy of mind, though it faces the persistent challenge of explaining subjective experience.

## you gotta know

- Claims that all phenomena, including thought and consciousness, can be reduced to or explained by physical processes; there is no soul, spirit, or immaterial mind.
- Often paired with *mechanism* — the view that all causation reduces to physical laws and forces governing matter and its arrangements.
- The ancient atomists (especially *Democritus*) argued that only atoms and void are real, an early form of materialism rejected by *Plato* and *Aristotle*.
- *Marxist materialism* (historical materialism) applies the doctrine to history: material economic conditions and relations of production shape social consciousness, culture, and institutions.
- Challenged most acutely by the "hard problem of consciousness" — explaining why physical processes in the brain produce subjective experience (*qualia*) rather than proceeding "in the dark."
- Contemporary versions include *physicalism* and *functionalism*, which attempt to accommodate mental states, intentionality, and meaning within a strictly materialist framework.
- The explanatory gap: even if we map all neural correlates of consciousness, it remains unclear how objective physical processes generate subjective felt experience.

## connections

- [[dualism]] — the opposing philosophical position claiming immaterial mind exists alongside physical body, demanding materialists explain away apparent non-physical properties.
- [[consciousness]] — the greatest challenge for materialism; explaining how subjective felt experience (*qualia*) arises from matter is the core philosophical problem.
- [[mind-body-problem]] — the foundational philosophical puzzle materialism attempts to solve by reducing mental phenomena to physical brain processes.
- [[qualia]] — subjective qualities of experience (pain, redness, taste) that seem to resist reduction to physical properties and pose the hardest problem for materialism.
- [[marxism]] — applies materialist principles to history and political economy, arguing material conditions determine consciousness rather than vice versa.
- [[panpsychism]] — an alternative view that rejects pure materialism by attributing consciousness or consciousness-like properties to fundamental matter, solving the hard problem differently.
- [[descartes]] — founder of modern philosophical dualism, the tradition against which materialism developed as a counterposition.
