## summary

The **Critique of Metaphysics** represents the systematic dismantling and redefinition of traditional speculative philosophy initiated by *Immanuel Kant* in his 1781 masterpiece *Critique of Pure Reason*. Seeking to rescue philosophy from dogmatic rationalist speculation on one side and radical empiricist skepticism on the other, this inquiry sets strict epistemological limits on what human reason can legitimately know. By demonstrating that human cognition is restricted to phenomena (appearances shaped by space, time, and categories) while noumena (things-in-themselves) remain forever unknowable, it revolutionized modern epistemology and shaped the philosophy of science.

## you gotta know

- Sparked by *David Hume*, whose skeptical analysis of causality famously awoke *Immanuel Kant* from his dogmatic slumber and prompted a comprehensive investigation into the limits of reason.
- Introduced the Copernican Revolution in philosophy, proposing that objects must conform to human cognition rather than human cognition conforming to objects.
- Distinguishes between synthetic *a priori* judgments (which provide new, informative knowledge with universal necessity, such as mathematics and physics) and empty metaphysical speculation.
- Separates the world into phenomena (the realm of empirical appearances structured by the pure forms of intuition, space and time, and twelve *a priori* concepts of the understanding) and noumena (the inaccessible realm of things-in-themselves).
- Demolishes the three traditional branches of special metaphysics (*metaphysica specialis*): rational psychology (the immortality and substantiality of the soul), rational cosmology (the ultimate nature and origin of the cosmos), and rational theology (theoretical proofs of the existence of God).
- Exposes the paralogisms of pure reason, where reason commits fallacies by confusing the transcendental unity of apperception (the "I think") with a substantial, immortal Cartesian soul.
- Identifies the antinomies of pure reason, in which speculative reason can construct equally valid and contradictory proofs regarding cosmic limits, indivisible atoms, freedom versus determinism, and necessary causes.
- Reinterprets God, immortality, and freedom not as objects of theoretical knowledge or [[certainty]], but as regulative ideas and indispensable postulates of practical reason required to make sense of moral duty and the [[categorical-imperative]].

## connections

- [[kant]] — the German philosopher who authored the critical project establishing the boundaries of legitimate cognition.
- [[david-hume]] — the Scottish thinker whose skeptical doubts regarding induction and causality spurred this critical investigation.
- [[rationalism]] — the philosophical school represented by *Descartes* and *Leibniz* whose claims to knowledge beyond experience were refuted.
- [[empiricism]] — the tradition tracing through *Locke* and *Hume* that correctly emphasized sensory experience but failed to account for synthetic *a priori* truths.
- [[certainty]] — the epistemic goal pursued by early modern thinkers that this critique restricted strictly to the phenomenal realm.
- [[categorical-imperative]] — the practical moral command that occupies the space cleared by the restriction of theoretical metaphysics.
- [[deontological-ethics]] — the moral philosophy whose objective necessity survives the critique of speculative theology.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual epoch in *Europe* dedicated to human autonomy, critique, and the limitation of dogmatic authority.
