## summary

**Thomas Aquinas** (1225–1274) was a Dominican friar and the greatest medieval philosopher-theologian, whose synthesis of *Aristotelian* logic with Christian doctrine shaped Catholic thought for centuries. He pioneered a new method of philosophical theology called *Scholasticism*, proving God's existence through rational argument and reconciling faith with reason. His *Summa Theologiae* remains one of the most influential theological works ever written.

## you gotta know

- Born in Sicily to a minor noble family; educated at the University of Naples and later studied under *Albertus Magnus*.
- Integrated *Aristotle's* physics and metaphysics into Catholic theology at a time when Aristotle was being rediscovered in medieval Europe through Arabic translations.
- Authored the *Summa Theologiae* (Summary of Theology), an encyclopedic work of systematic theology written in question-and-answer format.
- Formulated the *Five Ways* — five rational arguments for God's existence based on observation of the natural world (motion, causality, contingency, perfection, teleology).
- Distinguished between revealed truth (known through Scripture and Church authority) and natural reason (known through philosophy), arguing both lead toward the same truth.
- Asserted that *Aristotle's* virtue ethics could be reconciled with Christian ethics through the addition of the theological virtues (faith, hope, charity).
- Canonized in 1323; declared a Doctor of the Church; his authority became second only to Scripture in Catholic intellectual tradition.

## connections

- [[aristotle]] — whose logic, metaphysics, and natural philosophy Aquinas integrated into Christian theological thought, previously seen as dangerous pagan learning.
- [[scholasticism]] — the philosophical method he perfected, using dialectical argument to reconcile apparent contradictions in Scripture and Church fathers.
- [[medieval-philosophy]] — the intellectual tradition he dominated and defined; his synthesis became the standard framework for medieval Christian thought.
- [[theology]] — his primary discipline; he revolutionized theology by making systematic rational argument central to theological inquiry rather than mere scriptural commentary.
- [[reason-and-faith]] — the fundamental tension he worked to resolve through Scholastic method, arguing reason and revelation are complementary paths to divine truth.
- [[existence-of-god]] — he offered systematic philosophical proofs (the Five Ways) that influenced all subsequent natural theology and philosophy of religion.
- [[christianity]] — the faith tradition his work shaped for seven centuries; his thought remains authoritative in Catholic theology and philosophy.
