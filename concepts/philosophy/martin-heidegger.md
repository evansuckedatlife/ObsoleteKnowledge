## summary

**Martin Heidegger** (1889–1976) was a German philosopher who radically transformed the study of Being and human existence through phenomenology and existential analysis. His monumental *Being and Time* (1927) made questions about the meaning of Being central to philosophy and introduced the concept of authentic existence as liberation from social conformity. Though his collaboration with Nazism remains deeply controversial, his philosophical innovations profoundly influenced existentialism, hermeneutics, and continental philosophy.

## you gotta know

- His central question: "What does it mean to be?" — reframing ontology from an abstract metaphysical puzzle into an urgent inquiry into human existence.
- Developed *Dasein* (being-there) as his term for human existence, emphasizing that we are always already embedded in a world of practical concerns and meanings.
- Introduced the distinction between *authentic* and *inauthentic* existence: authentic being requires owning our freedom and mortality, rather than hiding in social convention.
- Pioneer of *phenomenology*, the method of carefully analyzing the structures of lived experience and consciousness.
- Introduced the concept of *thrownness* (Geworfenheit): we find ourselves cast into a world we did not choose, with circumstances we did not select.
- Emphasized *mood* and *anxiety* as fundamental to human existence: anxiety reveals our freedom and the groundlessness of our choices.
- Developed *hermeneutics*, the study of interpretation, arguing that all understanding is fundamentally interpretive—we cannot access a meaning-independent reality.
- His *Being and Time* became foundational to existentialism, profoundly influencing *Sartre*, *Camus*, and later continental philosophy.
- Explored *technology* (Technik) as a mode of revealing that risks reducing nature and humanity to mere resources to be exploited.
- His Nazi party membership (1933–1945) and ambiguous postwar reflections remain a tragic and contested chapter; scholars debate whether his philosophy enabled or was corrupted by fascism.

## connections

- [[existentialism]] — his investigation of Being and authenticity became foundational to the existentialist movement.
- [[phenomenology]] — his method of analyzing lived experience revolutionized phenomenological philosophy.
- [[being]] — his central preoccupation; he made the question of Being itself a philosophical problem.
- [[authenticity]] — his distinction between authentic and inauthentic modes of existence deeply influenced existential thought.
- [[consciousness]] — his analysis of Dasein redirected philosophy toward the structures of human awareness and involvement.
- [[jean-paul-sartre]] — his existentialist heir who developed Heidegger's ideas within a more humanistic framework.
- [[philosophy]] — transformed 20th-century philosophy through his ontological and hermeneutic innovations.
