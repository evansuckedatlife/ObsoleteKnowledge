## summary

**Science** is a systematic, empirical inquiry into the natural world governed by the scientific method: forming hypotheses, conducting experiments, and revising theories in light of evidence. As both a body of knowledge and a process, science emerged from the Renaissance and crystallized during the *Enlightenment*, fundamentally transforming human understanding of nature, society, and even knowledge itself. Philosophy of science now grapples with questions of objectivity, causation, confirmation, and the relationship between theory and reality.

## you gotta know

- The *scientific method* emphasizes empirical observation, reproducible experiments, and falsifiability as hallmarks of genuine scientific knowledge.
- The *Enlightenment* elevated science as the model for rational inquiry, contrasting systematic observation with religious dogma and superstition.
- *Positivism* took science as the only meaningful form of knowledge, attempting to apply scientific rigor to ethics, society, and metaphysics.
- The *scientific revolution* (16th–17th centuries) including figures like *Newton* and *Galileo*, displaced medieval philosophy with mathematical physics.
- *Karl Popper* argued that science advances through falsification: a theory is scientific if it can be proven false; confirmation alone is not enough.
- *Thomas Kuhn* challenged the linear progress model with *paradigm shifts*: science progresses through revolutionary overthrows of existing frameworks, not gradual accumulation.
- Empiricism and rationalism both contributed: empiricism insists on sensory evidence; rationalism on logical structure and mathematical laws.
- *Imre Lakatos* proposed research programs: science consists of competing frameworks that evolve by protecting their core assumptions and adjusting their peripheral hypotheses.
- Philosophy of science debates: Is scientific truth objective or constructed? Does science discover nature or impose frameworks upon it?
- The *demarcation problem*: what distinguishes science from pseudoscience? This boundary remains philosophically contested.
- Science reshaped civilization: *industrialization*, medicine, technology, and modern worldviews are inseparable from scientific knowledge and method.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the historical period in which science became the supreme form of rational inquiry.
- [[positivism]] — the philosophical movement that elevated science as the model for all meaningful knowledge.
- [[empiricism]] — the epistemological tradition emphasizing observation and sensory evidence as the foundation of knowledge.
- [[reason]] — science combines reason (logic, mathematics) with empirical observation.
- [[causality]] — scientific inquiry seeks causal explanations for natural phenomena.
- [[verification]] — central to debates about scientific confirmation and what makes a claim scientifically meaningful.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — applied science transformed production, energy, and human society.
