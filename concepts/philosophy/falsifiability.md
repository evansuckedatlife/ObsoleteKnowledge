## summary

**Falsifiability** is *Karl Popper*'s criterion for scientific meaningfulness: a statement is scientific (and potentially meaningful) if it can be proven false by empirical evidence; if no conceivable observation could falsify it, the statement lies outside science. This concept revolutionized philosophy of science by replacing verification with falsification as the mark of scientific knowledge.

## you gotta know

- Popper's response to the logical positivists: the verification principle (statements are meaningful only if verifiable by observation) faces fatal problems and cannot account for universal laws.
- Falsifiability avoids those problems: a statement like "All swans are white" is scientific because a single black swan would falsify it; it makes a testable claim about the world.
- Unfalsifiable statements (e.g., "God exists," "the universe is governed by hidden forces") fall outside science; they may be true or meaningful, but they are not *scientific* in the strict sense.
- Scientific progress: science advances through bold conjecture followed by rigorous testing and attempted refutation; theories that survive attempted falsification gain credibility.
- Popper distinguished between science and pseudoscience partly through falsifiability: Freudian psychology and Marxism, he argued, are unfalsifiable and therefore pseudo-scientific.
- The difference between falsifiability and refutation: a theory is falsifiable if falsification is logically possible; refutation occurs when falsification actually happens.
- Criticisms: some scientific laws (like quantum mechanics) are intrinsically probabilistic and not strictly falsifiable; the criterion may be too strict or too loose.
- Ad hoc hypotheses: scientists can rescue a theory from falsification by introducing new, untestable assumptions—Popper warned against this methodological cheating.
- Demarcation problem: despite its elegance, falsifiability does not cleanly separate science from pseudoscience; astrology makes falsifiable predictions, yet few consider it scientific.
- Influence: despite criticisms, falsifiability revolutionized philosophy of science and remains central to scientific practice; many scientists adopt it as their standard.

## connections

- [[verification]] — the earlier criterion Popper critiqued; his alternative framework.
- [[science]] — the domain to which falsifiability applies; it is a criterion for demarcating science from non-science.
- [[empiricism]] — falsifiability depends on empirical evidence; rooted in the empiricist tradition.
- [[logical-positivism]] — the philosophical movement Popper engaged and revised.
- [[karl-popper]] — the philosopher who developed falsifiability as a cornerstone of his philosophy of science.
