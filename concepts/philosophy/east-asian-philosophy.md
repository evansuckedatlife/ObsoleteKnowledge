## summary

**East Asian philosophy**, historically referenced in comparative traditions as **Eastern philosophy**, comprises the major intellectual, spiritual, and moral currents originating across China, Korea, [[japan]], and Vietnam. Centered primarily on the dynamic interplay of Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism, this tradition prioritizes relational ethics, self-cultivation, and harmonious alignment with the cosmos over Western abstract metaphysics. It forms the foundational cultural and ethical bedrock of East Asian civilization, influencing governance, aesthetics, and modern comparative thought.

## you gotta know

- Originated during the turbulent Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods in ancient China, an era of intense intellectual ferment remembered as the *Hundred Schools of Thought*.
- Dominated by [[confucianism]], inaugurated by *Kongzi* (*Confucius*) and developed by *Mengzi* (*Mencius*) and *Xunzi*, which teaches the cultivation of *ren* (benevolence), *li* (ritual decorum), and filial piety (*xiao*).
- Formulated a naturalistic metaphysics through Daoism, attributed to the semi-legendary *Laozi* in the *Daodejing* and developed by *Zhuangzi*, emphasizing *wu wei* (spontaneous or effortless action) and living in harmony with the *Dao*.
- Developed the realpolitik doctrine of Legalism (*Fajia*), articulated by *Han Feizi*, which rejected moral benevolence in favor of strict codified laws (*fa*), bureaucratic methods (*shu*), and autocratic authority (*shi*).
- Synthesized classical texts into Neo-Confucianism during the Song and Ming dynasties, with *Zhu Xi* establishing the Cheng-Zhu school based on the dualism of *li* (cosmic principle) and *qi* (material energy).
- Countered by the Neo-Confucian School of the Mind led by *Wang Yangming*, who rejected external study to proclaim the "unity of knowing and acting" (*zhixing heyi*) and the innate presence of moral intuition (*liangzhi*).
- Innovated distinctive schools in [[japan]], notably the 20th-century Kyoto School led by *Kitaro Nishida*, which integrated Zen Buddhist notions of absolute nothingness (*mu*) with European [[phenomenology]] and [[dialectics]].
- Debated passionately in Joseon Korea through metaphysical controversies such as the *Four-Seven Debate* between *Yi Hwang* (*Toegye*) and *Yi I* (*Yulgok*) over the origins of human emotion and moral nature.

## connections

- [[confucianism]] — predominant philosophical and social doctrine governing ethics, family structure, and statecraft in East Asia.
- [[japan]] — East Asian nation that developed Zen philosophical aesthetics and modern comparative movements like the Kyoto School.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — Aristotelian framework frequently compared to Confucian moral education and character cultivation.
- [[phenomenology]] — Continental tradition synthesized with Buddhist emptiness by modern Japanese philosophers.
- [[dialectics]] — philosophical method paralleled in the complementary polarities of *yin* and *yang* and the Kyoto School's logic of contradictory self-identity.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Western civilization whose Axial Age philosophers addressed virtue and cosmic order concurrently with Chinese thinkers.
- [[effective-altruism]] — modern ethical theory whose impartial universalism contrasts with the classical Confucian ethic of graduated care.
