## summary

**First philosophy** is *Aristotle's* designation for the study of being qua being, investigating the ultimate principles, primary causes, and fundamental structures of reality. Originating in the early cosmological inquiries of *ancient Greece*, this branch examines what must exist before any specialized natural science can begin its investigations. Later titled *metaphysics* by ancient editors, it remains a cornerstone of Western speculative thought from antiquity through the early modern meditations of *René Descartes*.

## you gotta know

- *Aristotle* coined the term *prote philosophia* in his metaphysical treatises to distinguish the fundamental study of existence from *second philosophy*, which investigates nature and physical change (*physics*).
- The pre-Socratic pursuit of the *arche*—the single originating material principle proposed by thinkers such as *Thales* and *Anaximenes*—served as the historical seedbed for first philosophy.
- *Andronicus of Rhodes*, an editor of Aristotle's corpus in the first century BCE, placed these writings after the physical treaties, giving rise to the title *Metaphysics* (*meta ta physika*).
- In Book IV of the *Metaphysics*, Aristotle establishes the principle of non-contradiction as the most secure axiom of first philosophy, demonstrating that without it, rational discourse and meaningful assertion collapse.
- The investigation culminates in Aristotle's conception of the *unmoved mover*, framing first philosophy as natural *theology* that accounts for eternal motion and primary substance (*ousia*).
- *René Descartes* explicitly revived the ancient title in his 1641 seminal text *Meditations on First Philosophy*, seeking an indubitable epistemological bedrock upon which to erect the new sciences.
- *Immanuel Kant* radically reformed the enterprise in the *Critique of Pure Reason*, arguing that traditional first philosophy exceeded human cognitive bounds unless reconfigured as the critique of pure understanding.

## connections

- [[aristotle]] — the foundational Greek thinker who conceived and systematised first philosophy.
- [[pre-socratic-philosophy]] — the intellectual movement that inaugurated the quest for the ultimate *arche*.
- [[plato]] — Aristotle's master whose Theory of Forms prompted the classic formulations of substance and essence.
- [[descartes]] — the modern rationalist who framed his foundational epistemology in *Meditations on First Philosophy*.
- [[theology]] — the metaphysical summit of first philosophy treating the primary cause and divine substance.
- [[philosophy]] — the overarching discipline of which first philosophy represents the foundational inquiry.
- [[fallacy]] — the logical errors that first philosophy guards against when establishing valid foundational axioms.
