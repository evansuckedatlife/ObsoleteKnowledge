## summary

**Dialectics**, also termed the **dialectical method**, is a discourse-driven framework of philosophical inquiry and historical explanation that resolves contradictions through reasoned exchange and developmental synthesis. Originating in [[ancient-greece]] with the conversational refutations of *Socrates* and the metaphysical paradoxes of *Heraclitus*, it was systematically elevated in 19th-century German idealism by *G. W. F. Hegel* as the fundamental engine of human consciousness and spirit. In modern social theory, it was subsequently inverted into the foundation of historical and dialectical materialism by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels.

## you gotta know

- Practiced in antiquity as the Socratic method of *elenchus*, in which *Socrates* cross-examined interlocutors in [[plato]]'s dialogues to uncover internal inconsistencies and latent contradictions in their definitions of virtue and justice.
- Formulated systematically by *G. W. F. Hegel* in the *Phenomenology of Spirit* and *Science of Logic*, where concepts advance through an internal dynamic often popularly schematized as thesis, antithesis, and synthesis, though Hegel termed this process *Aufhebung* or sublation.
- Inverted by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels into *dialectical materialism* (*Diamat*), which abandoned Hegelian idealism to argue that material conditions, economic structures, and class antagonisms drive the real historical development of human society in [[marxism]].
- Traced metaphysically to the pre-Socratic philosopher *Heraclitus*, whose doctrine of eternal flux asserted that the fundamental unity of the world is generated through constant tension and strife between opposing forces.
- Critiqued by *Immanuel Kant* in the *Critique of Pure Reason*, whose "Transcendental Dialectic" analyzed the natural illusions of human reason that inevitably generate insoluble antinomies when applied beyond sensory experience.
- Reinterpreted by *Theodor Adorno* and the Frankfurt School in *Negative Dialectics*, which rejected Hegel's affirmative reconciliation and totality, maintaining instead that contradictions remain irreconcilable in the wake of modern atrocities.
- Employed in medieval scholasticism as the formal disputation method (*disputatio*), providing the argumentative architecture utilized by figures like [[duns-scotus]] and [[thomas-aquinas]] to evaluate counterarguments before establishing doctrinal conclusions.

## connections

- [[marxism]] — foundational political theory that restructured Hegelian idealism into historical materialism driven by class struggle.
- [[plato]] — classical dialogues that preserved the conversational and refutational dialectic of Socrates.
- [[kant]] — Enlightenment thinker whose Transcendental Dialectic exposed the necessary antinomies of ungrounded metaphysics.
- [[materialism]] — metaphysical position combined with dialectical movement to explain physical and historical change.
- [[logic]] — analytical discipline traditionally contrasted with dialectic, contrasting static formal deductions with developmental contradictions.
- [[ancient-greece]] — birthplace of dialectical reasoning, originating from the paradoxes of Zeno of Elea and the dialogic method of Athens.
- [[duns-scotus]] — Franciscan scholastic master who employed dialectical disputations to formulate subtle metaphysical distinctions.
