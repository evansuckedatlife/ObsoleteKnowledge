# Humanism

## summary

**Humanism** is an intellectual movement that emphasizes human dignity, reason, and the study of classical learning as paths to understanding and improvement. Emerging in Renaissance Italy (14th–17th centuries) as a reaction against medieval scholasticism, humanism championed the *studia humanitatis* (humanities): the study of grammar, rhetoric, history, poetry, and moral philosophy grounded in classical texts. Humanists believed that through education and the cultivation of reason, humans could perfect themselves and society. Humanism became the philosophical foundation of the Renaissance, animating the period's art, literature, and political thought, and continues to influence modern philosophy's emphasis on human agency and secular ethics.

## you gotta know

- *Renaissance humanism* originated in Italy, particularly in Florence, as scholars like *Petrarch* sought to recover and study classical Greek and Roman texts lost or neglected during the Middle Ages.
- Humanists championed the *studia humanitatis*—a curriculum centered on rhetoric, poetry, history, and moral philosophy—over medieval scholasticism's abstract theological disputations.
- The movement emphasized *human potential and dignity*: humans, through education and reason, could understand nature and perfect society; this optimism contrasted with medieval Christianity's emphasis on human depravity and salvation.
- Classical learning was central: humanists collected and studied texts by *Plato*, *Aristotle*, *Cicero*, and *Virgil*, using them as models for philosophical thinking and aesthetic expression.
- Humanism influenced the development of *political realism* (*Machiavelli*'s *The Prince*) and scientific empiricism; it encouraged skepticism toward received authority and reliance on human reason.
- *Civic humanism* emerged in Renaissance Florence: the belief that educated citizens have a civic duty to participate in governance and improve their communities through reason and virtue.
