## summary

**Theology** is the philosophical and rational inquiry into the nature of God, divine revelation, and humanity's relationship to the divine. Distinct from mere faith or religious practice, theology attempts to understand religious claims through logic, reason, and argument. Medieval *scholasticism* treated theology as the queen of sciences; modern theology grapples with conflicts between revelation and reason, and between ancient doctrines and contemporary knowledge. Theology bridges philosophy and religion by applying philosophical rigor to religious questions.

## you gotta know

- *Natural theology* seeks to prove God's existence through reason alone (the cosmological argument, the argument from design), without appeal to revelation.
- *Scholasticism* synthesized Christian theology and Aristotelian philosophy, making reason and faith mutual allies rather than rivals.
- *Thomas Aquinas* exemplified medieval theology: he argued that reason and revelation are compatible and that God's existence can be philosophically demonstrated.
- The question of *reason and faith*: Can reason prove theological claims, or does faith rest on revelation beyond reason's grasp?
- *The Five Ways* are *Aquinas's* arguments for God's existence: through motion, causation, contingency, degrees of perfection, and design.
- *Leibniz's theodicy* attempted to solve the problem of evil by claiming this is "the best of all possible worlds"—though *Voltaire* famously ridiculed this.
- *Negative theology* argues that God transcends all human categories and language; we can only say what God is *not*, not what God *is*.
- Modern theology confronts the challenge posed by science, history, and secular philosophy: how to ground religious authority in an age of critical inquiry.
- Process theology reconceives God as evolving alongside creation rather than immutable and all-powerful, seeking to resolve tensions with free will and suffering.
- Different religious traditions develop sophisticated theological systems: Christian, Islamic, Jewish, Buddhist, and Hindu theologies each grapple with ultimate reality and meaning.
