# Religious Freedom

## summary

**Religious Freedom** is the philosophical and legal principle that individuals have the right to hold, practice, and change their religious beliefs without government interference or coercion. Enshrined in the *First Amendment* to the *United States Constitution* and in international human rights instruments, religious freedom is considered a foundational right in democracies, though its limits and application remain contested.

## you gotta know

- The *First Amendment* prohibits government establishment of religion and protects free exercise of religion, creating a dual principle: the government cannot promote or sponsor religion (Establishment Clause) and cannot suppress it (Free Exercise Clause).
- *Enlightenment* thinkers, particularly *John Locke*, argued for religious toleration and freedom of conscience as natural rights, rejecting theocratic government and religious monopoly.
- The principle of religious freedom emerged partly from the exhaustion of European religious wars (16th–17th centuries); advocates sought peace through mutual toleration and separation of religious and political authority.
- *Separation of church and state* is the corollary principle: government must remain neutral on religious matters, neither favoring nor disfavoring any religion, protecting both religious liberty and democratic governance.
- Religious freedom protections extend to diverse manifestations: worship, belief, religious education, conscientious objection, and—in some cases—religiously motivated refusal to participate in state-mandated activities.
- Tensions arise between religious freedom and other rights (anti-discrimination law, reproductive rights, LGBTQ+ equality); courts must balance claims of religious conscience against competing interests.
- The scope of "religion" is contested: courts must determine which beliefs qualify for religious freedom protection (e.g., is secular philosophy protected? Pseudoreligious beliefs?).
- Global religious freedom is unevenly protected: many countries suppress minority religions, and religious persecution remains a major human rights concern.
