## summary

**Panpsychism** is the philosophical view that consciousness or some form of experience is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of the physical world—not confined to human brains or even biological systems, but present in all matter to some degree. Rather than treating consciousness as an anomalous emergent property that somehow arises from non-conscious matter, panpsychism suggests that mentality is woven into reality at the most basic level. It offers a radical response to the *mind-body problem* and the explanatory gap between physical processes and subjective experience.

## you gotta know

- Proposes that consciousness is fundamental, not emergent—a basic feature of reality like mass or charge.
- Avoids both *dualism* (the implausible split between mind and matter) and the apparent inadequacy of standard materialism to explain subjective experience.
- Takes seriously the puzzle of *qualia*: how do objective physical processes give rise to subjective, qualitative feelings?
- Ancient roots in *Spinoza* and *Leibniz*; revived in contemporary philosophy of mind by thinkers like Galen Strawson.
- Addresses the *combination problem*: if electrons have micro-experiences, how do they combine into the unified, large-scale consciousness we enjoy?
- Raises hard questions: Does an electron have experience? If so, how does individual consciousness arise from billions of micro-experiences?
- Contrasts with *emergence* theories that treat consciousness as novel, non-reducible phenomenon arising only at higher levels of complexity.
- *Constitutive panpsychism* argues consciousness constitutes or grounds physical properties rather than vice versa.
- *Cosmopsychism* extends the view: the universe itself, not just its parts, is conscious or experiences as a unified whole.
- Motivates alternative frameworks and offers a solution to the hard problem by reconceptualizing consciousness as part of nature's fundamental architecture.
