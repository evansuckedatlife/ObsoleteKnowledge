## summary

**Compatibilism** is the philosophical thesis that determinism and moral agency can be reconciled without contradiction. Developed extensively from early modern thought into contemporary metaphysics, it maintains that an act is free whenever it springs from an agent's own internal desires, deliberation, and values rather than external compulsion. By grounding freedom in human agency rather than uncaused metaphysical spontaneity, compatibilism preserves the framework of moral accountability within a lawful universe.

## you gotta know

- Commonly designated as *soft determinism*, a pejorative label coined by the American psychologist and philosopher *William James*.
- Championed during the Scottish [[enlightenment]] by [[david-hume]], who argued that liberty consists of acting according to the determinations of one's own will rather than random chance.
- Argues that moral accountability requires determinism, because an agent can only be blamed if their actions stem reliably from their stable [[character]].
- Reinterpreted in twentieth-century analytic philosophy by *Harry Frankfurt*, whose famous counterexamples challenged the principle of alternate possibilities.
- Distinguishes free action from coerced action by whether the immediate causal chain passes through an agent's own cognitive beliefs and unimpeded desires.
- Subjected to critique by incompatibilist libertarians and hard determinists alike, who argue that causal determinism ultimately traces human intention back to origins outside the agent's control.

## connections

- [[free-will]] — the central philosophical capacity whose existence compatibilists seek to defend in a deterministic world.
- [[character]] — the stable internal state from which compatibilists argue free and responsible actions must proceed.
- [[david-hume]] — Scottish empiricist who provided classic formulations reconciling human liberty with necessity.
- [[kant]] — thinker who sought to harmonize deterministic phenomenal nature with moral freedom in the noumenal realm.
- [[rationalism]] — philosophical current whose deterministic cosmological systems prompted robust debates over free will.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual era in which modern scientific mechanics spurred widespread reassessments of causal necessity.
- [[stoicism]] — ancient school that combined cosmic fate with rational assent and internal moral responsibility.
