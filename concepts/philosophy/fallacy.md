## summary

A **fallacy** is an error in reasoning that undermines the structural validity or epistemic soundness of an argument. Formalised as a serious field of study in *ancient Greece* by *Aristotle*, the analysis of fallacious argumentation distinguishes legitimate deductive inferences from deceptive rhetorical moves. Understanding fallacies remains essential across philosophy, formal logic, legal analysis, and competitive debate to safeguard rational inquiry against cognitive bias and sophistry.

## you gotta know

- *Aristotle* founded the systematic study of fallacies in his treatise *Sophistical Refutations* (*Sophistici Elenchi*), where he classified thirteen distinct fallacies into linguistic and non-linguistic categories.
- Deductive errors divide fundamentally into *formal fallacies*, which violate structural rules of inference like affirming the consequent or denying the antecedent, and *informal fallacies*, which suffer from flaws in content, ambiguity, or context.
- The classic informal fallacy of *ad hominem* attacks the arguer rather than evaluating the argument's premises, while the *straw man* fallacy distorts an opponent's position into an extreme, easily refutable caricature.
- Begging the question (*petitio principii*) occurs when an argument assumes the very proposition it seeks to prove, a core topic within epistemology and Aristotle's *Prior Analytics*.
- *John Locke* contributed significantly to informal logic by popularising several *ad* arguments, including *argumentum ad verecundiam* (appeal to inappropriate authority) and *argumentum ad ignorantiam* (appeal to ignorance).
- In modern philosophy of science, *David Hume* famously articulated the is–ought problem, identifying the naturalistic fallacy of deriving normative moral duties solely from descriptive factual premises.
- Mathematical and inductive reasoning also suffer from quantitative fallacies, such as the *gambler's fallacy* and the *base rate fallacy*, which frequently deceive unguided human intuition.

## connections

- [[logic]] — the foundational formal discipline whose normative rules fallacies violate.
- [[aristotle]] — the thinker who first systematised logical fallacies in *Sophistical Refutations*.
- [[first-philosophy]] — the investigation of fundamental metaphysical axioms that grounding argumentation relies upon.
- [[socrates]] — the philosopher who relentlessly exposed fallacious definitions and sophisms in Plato's dialogues.
- [[rationalism]] — the epistemological stance that relies on rigorous deduction free from formal fallacies.
- [[empiricism]] — the philosophical tradition that cautions against hasty generalisations and inductive errors.
- [[david-hume]] — the philosopher who diagnosed the fundamental fallacy of deriving prescriptive values from descriptive facts.
