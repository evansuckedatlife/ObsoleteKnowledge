## summary

**Reason** is the faculty of mind that processes logic, derives conclusions from premises, and aspires to universal truth independent of sensory experience. Since the ancient Greeks, reason has been held as humanity's distinctive mark and the path to knowledge; yet what reason is, how it works, and how much it can achieve remain contested. Rationalists elevated reason above experience; empiricists insisted experience constrains reason; and the Enlightenment weaponized reason as the tool to reform society, science, and politics. Today, reason competes with intuition, emotion, and evolutionary psychology for authority over belief and behavior.

## you gotta know

- *Plato* and *Aristotle* established reason as the highest human faculty, distinct from sensation and emotion; Aristotle called humans "rational animals."
- *Descartes* made reason the foundation of philosophy: "I think therefore I am"—reason aware of itself is the one indubitable truth. He believed pure reason (not experience) could derive truths about God, mind, and matter.
- *Rationalists* (*Descartes*, *Spinoza*, *Leibniz*) argued that reason, using mathematics and logic, could determine fundamental metaphysical truths—the existence of God, the nature of substance, the laws of nature—independent of observation.
- *Empiricists* (*Locke*, *Hume*, *Berkeley*) countered that reason works on data provided by sensation; you cannot reason your way to knowledge of the world without first sensing it. Pure reason alone yields only logical tautologies, not facts about reality.
- *Kant* attempted synthesis: reason is the mind's fundamental structure (we experience space, time, and causation through reason), but reason requires sensory input to produce knowledge; reason without sensation is empty, sensation without reason is blind.
- The *Age of Reason* (the Enlightenment) deployed reason as a tool for social reform: rational critique of tradition, superstition, and authority. Philosophers like *Voltaire* and *Rousseau* used reason to challenge feudalism, religious dogma, and the divine right of kings.
- Modern neuroscience has complicated reason's status: reasoning is slow, effortful, and often post-hoc; evolutionary psychology suggests intuition, emotion, and heuristic judgment often outpace conscious reasoning. Behavioral economics shows humans systematically deviate from rational choice theory.
- *Stoics* argued that aligning reason with the cosmic logos (divine reason) is the path to virtue and tranquility; reason reveals what is in your control and what is not, enabling acceptance of fate.

## connections

- [[rationalism]] — the philosophical position that reason alone yields knowledge of fundamental truths.
- [[empiricism]] — the opposing view that sensory experience is the foundation of knowledge.
- [[enlightenment]] — the historical movement that wielded reason as the tool for intellectual and social reform.
- [[descartes]] — elevated reason (the thinking subject) as the foundation of philosophy.
- [[kant]] — synthesized rationalism and empiricism by arguing reason structures experience.
- [[stoicism]] — taught that aligning reason with cosmic order is the path to virtue.
- [[logic]] — the formal study of valid reasoning and inference.
