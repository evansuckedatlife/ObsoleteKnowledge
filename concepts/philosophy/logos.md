## summary

**Logos** (Greek: λόγος, "word," "reason," "principle") is a foundational concept spanning pre-Socratic philosophy and Christian theology. In *Heraclitus*, it denotes the rational, cosmic principle governing all change and unity amid apparent flux. In *Christian* theology, it names the divine Word—*Jesus* understood as the principle of creation and redemption made incarnate in human form.

## you gotta know

- In *Heraclitus*: logos is the underlying rational order or law (*nomos*) that governs all natural processes; fire symbolizes the flux, but logos explains its harmony and pattern.
- Heraclitus emphasizes logos as *rationality* itself—the universal reason accessible through philosophical inquiry; it is not merely personal reason but cosmic intelligence.
- The concept influenced Stoic philosophy, which adopted logos as the divine rational principle animating the universe (*pneuma*); every soul partakes in this universal logos.
- In Christian theology (esp. *John*'s Gospel): "In the beginning was the Logos, and the Logos was with God, and the Logos was God"—the divine Word or Son of God.
- The theological logos represents divine reason, creative power, and incarnate redemption; Jesus is understood as the logos made flesh (*sarx*), bridging divine and human realms.
- Later Christian theology integrated Heraclitean and Platonic philosophy with theology: logos became a way to articulate how the transcendent God relates to creation and humanity.
- Wordplay in Greek: logos means both "word" (speech) and "reason" (logic); this ambiguity enriches both philosophical and theological usage.
- The concept bridges philosophy and theology: it allows Christian thinkers to claim their faith is rational (grounded in logos) while transcending mere reason.
- Neoplatonism also inherited and transformed logos, connecting it to the *One* and the *Nous* (divine intellect)—a synthesis of Greek and religious thought.

## connections

- [[heraclitus]] — the pre-Socratic philosopher who developed logos as cosmic principle and rational order.
- [[incarnation]] — the theological doctrine that God's logos became human in Jesus Christ.
- [[pre-socratic-philosophy]] — the philosophical context from which logos emerged as a cosmological principle.
- [[rationalism]] — logos embodies the rationalist insight that universal reason governs reality.
- [[theology]] — Christian theology adopted and transformed the philosophical logos into a central theological concept.
- [[jesus]] — understood in Christian tradition as the incarnate logos, God's Word made human.
- [[stoicism]] — later philosophical school that inherited and developed Heraclitean logos concepts.
