## summary

**Determinism** is the philosophical position that all events, including human choices, are entirely causally determined by prior conditions and the laws of nature, leaving no room for genuine freedom or alternative possibilities. It stands in sharp contrast to libertarian free will, and raises fundamental questions about responsibility, meaning, and agency.

## you gotta know

- In a deterministic universe, the past and the laws of nature entail all future events; nothing could have been otherwise given those conditions.
- Determinism is often conflated with *fatalism*, but fatalism claims outcomes are fixed regardless of our choices, whereas determinism says our choices are themselves determined.
- *Hard determinism* (e.g., *Derk Pereboom*) accepts determinism and denies free will and moral responsibility as traditionally understood; requires rethinking punishment and desert.
- *Compatibilism* (e.g., *David Hume*) argues determinism is compatible with a meaningful notion of freedom—freedom as the absence of external constraint, not the absence of causation.
- Physical determinism: every event is fully caused by prior physical events and laws; challenged by quantum indeterminacy in modern physics, which may allow genuine indeterminism at subatomic levels.
- Theological determinism arises from divine omniscience—if God knows all future events, those events seem fixed, threatening human freedom; compatibilists respond that foreknowledge differs from causation.
- The question is not whether causation exists (nearly all philosophers accept causality) but whether causation exhausts all events, leaving none undetermined.
- Probabilism: some argue the future is probabilistically determined rather than strictly determined; the laws of nature specify probabilities, not certainties.
- Practical determinism: even if strict determinism is true, belief in free will may be psychologically necessary for humans to function; fatalism (acting as if nothing matters) leads to paralysis.
- Modern physics: quantum mechanics introduced indeterminacy at fundamental levels, though Einstein famously resisted this, declaring "God does not play dice with the universe."

## connections

- [[causality]] — the causal principle underlying determinism; every event has a cause.
- [[free-will]] — the opposing conception that agents have genuine alternatives available to them.
- [[freedom]] — the existential and political notion of agency; determinism challenges its coherence.
- [[kant]] — argued for transcendental freedom while accepting causal determinism in the phenomenal world.
- [[david-hume]] — a founder of compatibilism; argued necessity (determination) is compatible with liberty.
- [[responsibility]] — moral responsibility seems to require alternatives; determinism raises the challenge of how to assign blame or praise.
- [[moral-responsibility]] — the ethical problem: can agents be responsible if their acts are entirely determined?
