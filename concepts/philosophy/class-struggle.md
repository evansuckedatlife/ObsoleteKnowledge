## summary

**Class struggle** is *Karl Marx*'s central concept of history: the antagonistic conflict between social classes (particularly between the working class and the capitalist class) that Marx argued is the driving force of social change and historical progress. It is the engine of revolution and the transition between economic systems.

## you gotta know

- Marx opens *The Communist Manifesto* with: "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles"—the foundational principle of historical materialism.
- In capitalism, two classes struggle: the *bourgeoisie* (owners of capital and means of production) and the *proletariat* (workers who sell their labour); Marx saw no middle ground.
- The struggle arises from economic interest: capitalists seek to maximize profit by minimizing wages and exerting control; workers resist exploitation and organize for better conditions.
- Capitalism is self-undermining: competition drives technological advancement and labor displacement, which concentrates capital, impoverishes workers, and eventually creates the conditions for revolution.
- Class consciousness: workers must become aware of their common interests and exploited status to organize effectively; Marx saw this awareness as essential to revolution.
- Historical stages: Marx theorized that class struggle propels history through slave societies, feudalism, capitalism, and finally socialism—each stage's contradictions generating the next.
- Surplus value: workers create wealth that exceeds their wages; capitalists pocket the difference as profit, constituting hidden exploitation at the heart of capitalism.
- Alienation: under capitalism, workers become alienated from their own labor, the products they make, and their human potential—reduced to commodities themselves.
- Critique: critics argue Marx oversimplified history, misunderstood markets, neglected non-class identities (nation, religion, ethnicity), and that class struggle often cooperates rather than antagonizes.
- Legacy: despite criticisms, Marx's class-struggle framework profoundly shaped 20th-century politics, revolutions, and social movements worldwide.

## connections

- [[marxism]] — the philosophical and economic framework Marx built around class struggle.
- [[communism]] — the communist revolution is understood as the culmination of class struggle.
- [[capitalism]] — the system generating class antagonism; the target of revolutionary change.
- [[socialism]] — the system established after the workers' victory in class struggle.
- [[materialism]] — Marx's philosophical method; material conditions and production relations drive history and class formation.
- [[karl-marx]] — the philosopher and economist who theorized class struggle as the motor of history.
- [[working-class]] — the proletariat, the revolutionary class in Marxist theory.
