## summary

**Human Rights** are moral and legal entitlements believed to belong to all people simply by virtue of their humanity, independent of social status, national origin, or circumstance. The philosophical concept emerged from Enlightenment thinking about natural rights and the intrinsic dignity of rational persons. Modern human rights frameworks (international treaties, declarations, constitutional law) attempt to protect basic freedoms and protections: conscience, expression, assembly, equality, and freedom from torture, enslavement, and discrimination. Philosophers debate whether rights are grounded in natural law, universal reason, divine creation, or social contract. Human rights remain both philosophically grounded and politically contested: what rights exist, who holds them, and how societies should balance conflicting rights.

## you gotta know

- Natural rights theory (from *Locke* and *Rousseau*) claims humans possess inherent rights—life, liberty, property—that precede government and that governments must protect.
- *Kant's* moral philosophy grounds rights in human dignity: rational beings have inherent worth that demands respect and cannot be treated as mere means to others' ends.
- The Enlightenment's emphasis on universal reason led to claims that rights belong to all humans equally, challenging hierarchies of privilege and caste.
- The *United Nations Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (1948) attempted a global consensus on fundamental human entitlements.
- Rights can conflict (free speech versus protection from harassment) and their precise scope remains philosophically and politically debated.
- Philosophers distinguish negative rights (freedom from interference) from positive rights (entitlement to assistance or resources).
- Contemporary debates explore universal human rights versus cultural relativism: whether rights claims are grounded in objective truths or reflect Western cultural values imposed globally.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical tradition from which modern human rights theory emerged through thinkers like Locke and Rousseau.
- [[kant]] — argued rights derive from universal human dignity and the rational nature of persons who must be treated as ends, never mere means.
- [[capital-punishment]] — raises fundamental questions about whether states can legitimately violate rights, including the right to life, even in punishment.
- [[religious-freedom]] — considered a fundamental right to freedom of conscience and belief, emerging from Enlightenment thinking.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — embedded rights protections (Bill of Rights) influenced by Enlightenment philosophy and natural rights theory.
- [[moral-responsibility]] — closely linked to rights; only responsible agents can be bearers of rights and duties.
