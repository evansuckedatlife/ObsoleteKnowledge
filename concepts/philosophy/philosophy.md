# Philosophy

## summary

**Philosophy** is the systematic study of fundamental questions about reality, knowledge, existence, value, and conduct—seeking wisdom through reason and argument rather than authority or tradition. Born in ancient Greece with *Thales* and the pre-Socratics asking what the world is made of, philosophy has been humanity's primary tool for examining assumptions, testing ideas rigorously, and building coherent worldviews. It encompasses metaphysics (what exists), epistemology (how we know), ethics (how we should live), and logic (how to reason correctly), and remains essential because every scientific theory, legal system, and personal decision rests on unstated philosophical presuppositions.

## you gotta know

- Philosophy began in ancient Greece around 600 BCE when *Thales* asked "What is the fundamental substance of all things?" rather than accepting mythological explanations; this shift from myth to reason marked the birth of Western philosophy.
- The major branches are *epistemology* (theory of knowledge: how do we know what we know?), *metaphysics* (what exists and what is its nature?), *ethics* (how should we live?), *logic* (what makes an argument valid?), and *aesthetics* (what is beauty and art?).
- Ancient philosophy produced schools of thought that still dominate today: *Platonism* (universals and forms exist independently), *Aristotelianism* (knowledge comes from observation and classification), *Stoicism* (virtue is the highest good), and *Epicureanism* (pleasure, rightly understood, is good).
- The medieval period synthesized *Aristotle*'s philosophy with Christian theology (*Thomas Aquinas*), while the *Enlightenment* brought empiricism (knowledge from sense experience: *Hume*, *Locke*) and rationalism (knowledge from reason: *Descartes*, *Leibniz*).
- *Kant* revolutionized epistemology by asking how the mind structures experience; he argued that space, time, and causality are built into our cognition, not discovered in nature, explaining both the reliability of science and its limits.
- 19th-century *idealism* (*Hegel*), *positivism* (*Comte*, *Mill*), and *pragmatism* (*Peirce*, *James*, *Dewey*) challenged older systems; *Marx* applied philosophy to history and economics, while *Nietzsche* critiqued Western values wholesale.
- 20th-century philosophy fractured into multiple traditions: *analytic philosophy* (Anglo-American, emphasizing logic and language: *Wittgenstein*, *Russell*, *Quine*), *existentialism* (*Sartre*, *Heidegger*, *Camus*), *phenomenology* (*Husserl*, *Merleau-Ponty*), and *structuralism* (*Lévi-Strauss*, *Foucault*, *Derrida*).
- Philosophy's perennial value lies in its willingness to question everything: it challenges implicit assumptions in science, law, and society; it forces clarity about concepts (justice, consciousness, freedom) that feel obvious but remain philosophically mysterious.

## connections

- [[aristotle]] — the systematizer of logic and foundational metaphysics and ethics.
- [[plato]] — the founder of idealism and the theory of forms.
- [[socrates]] — the method of questioning (Socratic method) remains philosophy's primary tool.
- [[pre-socratic-philosophy]] — the earliest philosophers who first asked about the nature of reality.
- [[logic]] — the study of valid reasoning is the foundation of philosophical argumentation.
- [[descartes]] — revolutionized epistemology with methodical doubt and the mind-body problem.
- [[kant]] — transformed philosophy by asking how the mind structures experience.
- [[virtue-ethics]] — the ancient school emphasizing character and virtue as central to ethics.
