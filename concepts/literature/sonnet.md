## summary

A **sonnet** is a 14-line poem in iambic pentameter structured by a particular rhyme scheme and argument. Originating in 13th-century Italy with *Petrarch*, the form became a vehicle for exploring love, time, mortality, and philosophical inquiry. The sonnet achieved its English apotheosis with *Shakespeare* and represents the intersection of literary and musical formalism—its rhyme scheme and meter create an architecture for thought itself.

## you gotta know

- The *Petrarchan sonnet* (Italian sonnet) divides into octave (8 lines, ABBAABBA) and sestet (6 lines, CDECDE or similar), creating a volta or "turn" between statement and response.
- The *Shakespearean sonnet* (English sonnet) follows three quatrains and a couplet (ABABCDCDEFEFGG), allowing for a more dramatic final reversal in the closing couplet.
- *Shakespeare* composed 154 sonnets exploring love, procreation, beauty, time, and absence—collectively presenting a philosophical meditation on desire and mortality.
- The sonnet form's strict constraints (meter, rhyme, line count) create what aestheticians call "the freedom of form"—the paradox that rigid structure enables philosophical depth.
- Sonnets were often composed in sequences—connected poems exploring a single relationship or theme—a proto-novelistic form that influenced later narrative poetry.
- The form's musical dimension extends beyond meter: sonnets were set to music, particularly as *madrigals*, creating a hybrid of verbal and musical artistry.
- The sonnet remains the most influential closed form in English poetry, surviving centuries of modernist and postmodern challenges to its conventions.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — author of the 154 sonnets that defined the English form.
- [[petrarch]] — Italian poet who invented the sonnet form and established its thematic preoccupations.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the period when the sonnet became central to English poetic practice.
- [[iambic-pentameter]] — the metrical foundation of sonnets, creating a ten-syllable rhythmic line.
- [[madrigal]] — musical form that often set sonnetic texts, particularly Italian poetry.
- [[poetry]] — the broader genre of which the sonnet is a defining subform.
- [[love-poetry]] — the traditional subject matter of sonnets, though expanded to philosophy and temporality.
