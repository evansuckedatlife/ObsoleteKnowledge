## summary

The **future** is a foundational temporal concept, philosophical problem, and imaginative horizon in literature, denoting the time yet to come and the alternate realities shaped by human agency, technological expansion, and social change. While ancient and medieval traditions viewed time through cyclical patterns or divine eschatology, modern literary representations of futurity emerged from the Enlightenment and the [[industrial-revolution]]. The concept serves as the primary canvas for speculative extrapolation, utopian social engineering, and dystopian political warning.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a secular literary destination during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, moving away from biblical prophecy toward human-driven historical progress.
- Pioneered in speculative literature by *Louis-Sebastien Mercier*'s 1771 novel *L'An 2440* (*The Year 2440*), which envisioned an enlightened Parisian society governed by rational philosophy.
- Formalized through the scientific romances of *H. G. Wells*, notably *The Time Machine* and *The Shape of Things to Come*, which used evolutionary theory and technological trends to forecast humanity's distant fate.
- Institutionalized in the early twentieth century by the artistic movement of Italian and Russian *Futurism*, founded by *Filippo Tommaso Marinetti*, which glorified velocity, industrial mechanization, and radical breaks with antiquity.
- Shaped decisively by twentieth-century totalitarian anxieties into the dystopian masterworks of *Aldous Huxley* (*Brave New World*) and *George Orwell* (*Nineteen Eighty-Four*).
- Systematized by Golden Age science fiction writers such as *Arthur C. Clarke* and *Isaac Asimov*, whose future histories plotted human space colonization and interstellar governance.
- Complicated by postmodernism and cyberpunk, which introduced fragmented, corporatized futures summarized by *William Gibson*'s dictum: "The future is already here—it's just not evenly distributed."
- Utilized in feminist, queer, and postcolonial literature to deconstruct naturalized social hierarchies and imagine radically restructured social orders beyond binary paradigms.

## connections

- [[science-fiction]] — primary literary genre dedicated to extrapolating plausible scientific advances, alternative futures, and alien encounters.
- [[modernism]] — twentieth-century cultural movement that grappled with industrial acceleration, temporal discontinuity, and utopian artistic manifestos.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical conflict that cast a shadow of nuclear annihilation over twentieth-century visions of human survival and planetary futurity.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical transformation that accelerated technological innovation and established modern linear models of historical progress.
- [[gender-studies]] — theoretical discipline that interrogates speculative futures to project post-patriarchal societies, artificial reproduction, and non-binary kinship.
- [[gangster-fiction]] — genre synthesized with futuristic tropes in cyberpunk narratives featuring corporate syndicates, black-market cyberware, and dystopian underworlds.
- [[fujiwara-clan]] — historical dynasty whose classical aesthetics contrast with linear modern progress, embodying cyclical court life and Buddhist notions of the coming dark age.
