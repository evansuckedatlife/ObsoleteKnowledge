## summary

**Five Wits** refers to an allegorical personification and classical psychological taxonomy representing the bodily senses and internal mental faculties. In late medieval English morality drama, particularly the seminal allegorical play *Everyman*, the figure serves as a central companion whose inevitable abandonment dramatizes bodily decay and the limits of intellect. Rooted in scholastic philosophy and prominent throughout the [[english-renaissance]], the concept provided writers with a structural metaphor for human cognition, sensory perception, and the ultimate isolation of the soul at death.

## you gotta know

- Depicted in the morality play *Everyman* as an allegorical counselor who personifies human intellect and sensory awareness, counseling the protagonist alongside *Strength*, *Discretion*, and *Beauty*.
- Delivers an orthodox defense of the priesthood and sacraments in *Everyman*, exalting the holy order above angels while advising *Everyman* to receive holy unction and confession.
- Abandons the protagonist at the mouth of the grave alongside *Beauty*, *Strength*, and *Discretion*, dramatizing the biological and cognitive dissolution of the dying body.
- Classified in scholastic philosophy and medieval psychology into two distinct categories: the external wits (the five physical senses) and the internal wits (common sense, imagination, fantasy, estimation or judgment, and memory).
- Referenced by [[william-shakespeare]] in *Much Ado About Nothing*, where *Beatrice* remarks that *Benedick* has four wits halted in a duel of tongues, leaving only one to govern him.
- Invoked in [[king-lear]] during *Lear*'s descent into madness on the storm-swept heath, where *Edgar* in the disguise of *Poor Tom* laments that the foul fiend vexes his five wits.
- Appears in Sonnet 141 by [[william-shakespeare]], where the speaker contrasts his physical senses with his inward faculties, declaring that neither his five wits nor his five senses can dissuade his foolish heart from love.

## connections

- [[good-deeds]] — the only virtuous companion who remains faithful and descends into the grave when **Five Wits** flees.
- [[christianity]] — the theological framework that subordinated intellectual and sensory capacity to the divine grace of holy sacraments.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the cultural era in which medieval scholastic faculties were adapted into early modern drama and psychological analysis.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the dramatist who repeatedly invoked both the internal faculties and physical senses across his sonnets and plays.
- [[king-lear]] — the tragedy featuring *Edgar* as *Poor Tom*, crying out that demonic torments harass and unsettle his mental wits.
- [[poetry]] — the artistic medium wherein writers deployed the five wits to dissect romantic obsession and spiritual crisis.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical tradition concerned with human mortality and the radical isolation left behind when faculties fail.
