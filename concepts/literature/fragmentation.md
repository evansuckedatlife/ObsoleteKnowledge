## summary

**Fragmentation** is the modernist and postmodernist formal strategy of breaking narrative coherence, linear time, and unified perspective into discontinuous shards. Rather than presenting a stable, knowable world, fragmented texts mirror consciousness itself—scattered, associative, resistant to synthesis. Fragmentation became the defining aesthetic signature of 20th-century experimental literature.

## you gotta know

- Emerged in modernist literature as writers abandoned Victorian narrative omniscience and continuous plot in favour of fractured, subjective experience; modernists believed fragmented form could represent consciousness more truthfully than traditional realism.
- *The Waste Land* uses fragments of myth, multiple voices, and linguistic rupture to represent post-World War I spiritual and cultural dissolution; *Eliot's* poem refuses the comfort of coherence to enact the era's spiritual emptiness.
- Stream-of-consciousness narrative—interior monologue rendered as raw thought—is fragmentation's psychological technique, making consciousness itself the disordered medium; *Joyce* and *Woolf* proved that fragmented form could be more intimate than conventional narration.
- Postmodern literature radicalised fragmentation: meaning becomes unstable, identity is decentred, and texts collapse their own coherence through metafiction and pastiche; *Pynchon* and *DeLillo* pushed fragmentation beyond modernism into radical plurality.
- Spatial fragmentation (multiple plots, shifting locations) and temporal fragmentation (non-linear chronology, cyclical time) both deny the reader's narrative comfort; disorientation becomes a constitutive part of reading experience.
- Fragmented form enacts the content: a novel about alienation fractures; a text about relativism refuses stable interpretation; a story about trauma splinters its chronology; form becomes meaning itself rather than mere vehicle.
- The reader becomes responsible for assembling meaning from shards; interpretation is no longer guaranteed but required—demanding, unstable, participant; this shift from passive consumption to active construction redefines what reading means.
- Fragmentation challenged the assumption that unity, coherence, and closure are aesthetic ideals; it suggested that broken, fragmented, provisional forms might be more honest to experience than seamless narratives.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — the aesthetic movement that made fragmentation a primary value.
- [[postmodernism]] — extended fragmentation as radical formal and philosophical principle.
- [[the-waste-land]] — *T.S. Eliot's* masterpiece of fragmented allusion and broken mythology.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — the psychological technique that renders fragmented interiority.
- [[american-modernism]] — the indigenous American expression of fragmented form.
- [[james-joyce]] — author who pushed fragmentation to linguistic extremity in *Ulysses* and *Finnegans Wake*.
- [[thomas-pynchon]] — postmodern master of sprawling, fragmented narratives that resist closure.
