## summary

**Walt Whitman** (1819–1892) was an American poet who revolutionized modern verse through his embrace of free form, vernacular language, and the democratic voice. His masterwork *Leaves of Grass*, constantly revised throughout his lifetime, celebrates America's diversity, human sexuality, and the individual as part of the broader collective. Whitman's expansive style and egalitarian vision made him foundational to American poetry and a prophet of modernism.

## you gotta know

- *Leaves of Grass* (1855) introduced American poetry to free verse, abandoning traditional meter and rhyme to capture the conversational rhythm and cadence of American speech and vernacular language.
- His poem "Song of Myself" exemplifies his technique of cataloguing diverse American experiences and his democratic vision, presenting a speaker who contains contradictions and encompasses multitudes.
- Whitman celebrates the body, sexuality, and sensuality explicitly throughout his work, breaking Victorian taboos and shocking contemporary readers with his frank erotic content and celebration of desire.
- He worked as a journalist, nurse during the Civil War caring for wounded soldiers, and government clerk, experiences that directly informed his poetry's engagement with American life and suffering.
- Whitman's influence on modernism and Beat poets like *Allen Ginsberg* (who directly addressed him in "A Supermarket in California") was profound and transformative for twentieth-century poetry.
- His vision of poetry as a democratic art form — addressing all Americans regardless of education or status, not just an elite audience — redefined the poet's social role and literary mission.
- *Leaves of Grass* was revised repeatedly throughout his lifetime, growing larger and more ambitious, showing Whitman's ongoing meditation on America, democracy, and the self as process.
- Whitman's influence extends to contemporary poets of identity and political engagement, who inherit his profound belief in poetry as an essential tool for social change and democratic representation.
