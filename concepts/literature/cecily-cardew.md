## summary

**Cecily Cardew** is a principal comic heroine in *Oscar Wilde*'s 1895 masterpiece of Victorian [[satire]], *The Importance of Being Earnest*. Living under the strict moral tutelage of her guardian *Jack Worthing* in rural [[england]], she subverts conventional late-Victorian domestic propriety through an extravagantly romantic imagination and ruthless social candor. Her earnest obsession with marrying a man named Ernest parodies sentimental courtship novels while anchoring one of the sharpest farcical double-plots in English theatre.

## you gotta know

- Introduced as the eighteen-year-old ward of *Jack Worthing*, residing at his country estate in Hertfordshire under the instruction of her governess, *Miss Prism*.
- Escapes the tedium of German grammar and political economy lessons by recording an elaborate fictional diary, which she treats as an absolute factual record of emotional life.
- Invents and documents an entire whirlwind engagement to Jack's fictional, profligate brother Ernest months before ever laying eyes on him, complete with broken engagements and love letters written to herself.
- Falls immediately into mutual infatuation with *Algernon Moncrieff*, who arrives at the estate masquerading as the dissolute brother Ernest under the practice of *Bunburying*.
- Engages in the celebrated polite tea-table warfare of Act Two against *Gwendolen Fairfax*, weaponizing lumps of sugar and slices of cake over their conflicting claims to be betrothed to Ernest.
- Demands uncompromising romantic style over substance, confessing that she could never love a suitor whose Christian name was anything other than Ernest.
- Possesses an immense personal fortune of one hundred and thirty thousand pounds in the funds, which instantly dissipates *Lady Bracknell*'s snobbish aristocratic reservations.
