## summary

**The Hobbit, or There and Back Again** is a 1937 fantasy novel by English philologist and author *J. R. R. Tolkien*. Set in the mythic world of *Middle-earth*, the book follows the unexpected journey of the respectable hobbit *Bilbo Baggins*, who is swept away from his comfortable domestic life to assist a band of dwarves in reclaiming their mountain kingdom and treasure from a dragon. Celebrated for its rich linguistic invention, gentle humor, and moral gravity, the story established the imaginative geography and lore that Tolkien would later expand in his monumental epic *The Lord of the Rings*.

## you gotta know

- Recruited by the wizard *Gandalf* and the dwarf leader *Thorin Oakenshield*, *Bilbo Baggins* leaves his comfortable home at *Bag End* in *The Shire* to serve as a designated burglar on an expedition to the *Lonely Mountain* (*Erebor*).
- Bilbo encounters the wretched creature *Gollum* deep within the caverns of the *Misty Mountains*, where he discovers a magic ring of invisibility and survives by winning a high-stakes riddle contest in the dark.
- The climactic *Battle of Five Armies* erupts beneath the Lonely Mountain, uniting the men of *Lake-town*, the wood-elves of *Mirkwood*, and the dwarves of the *Iron Hills* against invading goblin and warg legions.
- Tolkien famously revised Chapter 5, "Riddles in the Dark," in the 1951 second edition to alter *Gollum's* demeanor, transforming the ring from a harmless novelty into the corrupting *One Ring* central to *The Lord of the Rings*.
- The dragon *Smaug* is killed during his fiery raid on *Lake-town* when the archer *Bard the Bowman* shoots him with a family heirloom, the *Black Arrow*, targeting a bare patch in the beast's encrusted underbelly.
- Seeking to avert an all-out war between rival armies over dragon treasure, Bilbo secretly steals the *Arkenstone*, the luminous "Heart of the Mountain," delivering it to *Bard* and the *Elvenking* to use as bargaining leverage against the stubborn Thorin.
- The narrative draws extensively from Old Norse sagas and medieval Germanic poetry, with Tolkien borrowing the name *Gandalf* and the names of nearly all thirteen dwarves directly from the *Dvergatal* catalogue in the *Poetic Edda*.
- Tolkien began writing the tale in the early 1930s after jotting down the spontaneous opening sentence, "In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit," while grading school examination papers.

## connections

- [[england]] — homeland of Tolkien whose rural countryside directly inspired the pastoral peace of the Shire.
- [[linguistics]] — academic discipline in philology that motivated Tolkien's elaborate fictional dialects, place names, and runic systems.
- [[poetry]] — artistic tradition woven through the narrative through dwarven laments, goblin marching chants, and elven drinking songs.
- [[world-war-i]] — military conflict whose devastating trench warfare deeply influenced Tolkien's portrayals of ruined landscapes, industrial cruelty, and unlikely courage.
- [[winnie-the-pooh]] — fellow landmark British interwar children's classic featuring woodland adventures and gentle humor.
- [[the-very-hungry-caterpillar]] — iconic children's classic demonstrating the enduring universal appeal of illustrated journeys.
