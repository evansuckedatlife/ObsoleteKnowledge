## summary

**Cinema**, also referred to as *motion pictures* or *film*, is a visual storytelling medium and art form that originated in the late nineteenth century. Emerging from experiments in photographic motion and stage illusion, it evolved into an international storytelling vehicle that combined dramatic literature, photography, architecture, musical scoring, and actor performance. Its development revolutionized narrative structure through editing and visual perspective, establishing a pervasive twentieth-century visual grammar that influenced dramatic writing, popular mythology, and global culture.

## you gotta know

- Originated in the 1890s through public exhibitions of projected photographic sequences, notably the *Cinématographe* developed by *Auguste and Louis Lumière* in [[france]] and the *Kinetoscope* created by *Thomas Edison* in the [[united-states]].
- Advanced from brief non-narrative actualities to complex dramatic fiction through early pioneers such as French illusionist *Georges Méliès*, who demonstrated theatrical fantasy, and *Edwin S. Porter*, who established dynamic parallel editing.
- Developed an expressive, universally legible visual grammar during the [[silent-film]] era, cultivating sophisticated pantomime and visual symbolism under directors such as *D. W. Griffith*, *F. W. Murnau*, and *Buster Keaton*.
- Formulated revolutionary theories of montage in post-revolutionary Russian film culture, where theorist *Sergei Eisenstein* demonstrated that juxtaposition between shots generates conceptual and visceral meanings absent in individual frames.
- Underwent an aesthetic and industrial revolution with the advent of synchronized optical sound in 1927, transforming performance styles, dialogue writing, and studio economics across the world.
- Generated classical studio conventions in Hollywood alongside influential international art movements, including German Expressionism, Italian Neorealism, and the French New Wave, which infused cinematic writing with psychological depth and [[realism]].
- Incorporates sophisticated narrative architectures, misdirection, and climactic psychological revelations, epitomized in modern supernatural storytelling by characters such as the haunted youth [[cole-sear]] in *The Sixth Sense*.

## connections

- [[silent-film]] — the foundational period of cinematic history before the widespread adoption of synchronized dialogue.
- [[cole-sear]] — the child medium whose perception of the deceased forms the core of one of the most famous twist endings in modern screenwriting.
- [[modernism]] — the overarching twentieth-century artistic movement that shared techniques of montage, nonlinear time, and fragmented perspective with narrative motion pictures.
- [[realism]] — the literary and visual doctrine that inspired postwar film movements to depict ordinary working lives on authentic urban locations.
- [[france]] — the European center where early cinematic projection was demonstrated and where post-war film theory flourished.
- [[united-states]] — the primary industrial base of commercial cinematic production and narrative genre conventions centered in Hollywood.
