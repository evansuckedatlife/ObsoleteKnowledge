# Romanticism

## summary

**Romanticism** was an artistic, literary, and philosophical movement that emerged in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, spanning roughly 1798–1850, in reaction against the Enlightenment's emphasis on reason and classical restraint. It elevated *emotion*, *imagination*, *nature*, and the *individual* as supreme values, celebrating the sublime, the exotic, the medieval, and the transgressive. Romanticism reshaped Western culture's understanding of the artist, the self, and humanity's place in nature.

## you gotta know

- Arose partly in response to the *French Revolution*'s rationalist ideals and partly as a reaction against industrial and urban civilization; Romantics looked backward to preindustrial nature and backward to medieval romance and legend.
- Elevated personal *emotion* and *subjective experience* as sources of truth and meaning, reversing the Enlightenment's ranking of reason above feeling; the Romantic artist became a visionary who accessed deeper truths through imagination.
- *Nature* became a primary symbol: wild, sublime, infinite, often indifferent or hostile to humans, serving as a mirror for the soul and a source of spiritual transcendence; mountains, storms, and untamed landscapes feature prominently.
- Celebrated the *genius* or exceptional individual as heroic agent; the Romantic hero—often isolated, passionate, transgressive—became a cultural archetype, replacing the classical exemplar of virtue.
- Drew heavily from medieval, folk, and non-European sources: ballads, Gothic tales, Oriental exotica, Celtic mythology; these sources provided alternatives to classical order.
- Produced major literary figures: in poetry, *William Wordsworth*, *Samuel Taylor Coleridge*, *John Keats*, *Lord Byron*, *Percy Bysshe Shelley*; in prose, *Mary Shelley*, *James Fenimore Cooper*; in music, *Ludwig van Beethoven*, *Franz Schubert*.
- *Frankenstein* and *Gothic literature* exemplify Romantic preoccupations: the dangers of overreaching reason, the power of the irrational, the isolation of the exceptional individual, and nature's sublime terror.

## connections

- [[emotion]] — Romanticism privileges emotional authenticity and intensity as a path to truth; the cultivation of feeling becomes moral and aesthetic duty.
- [[nature]] — central to Romantic imagination; both as external landscape and as an inner natural force of passion and instinct.
- [[imagination]] — the Romantic faculty par excellence; imagination is the power that transcends mundane reality and accesses sublime vision.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — American Romantic who weaponized Romantic excess into psychological horror and melancholic atmosphere.
- [[mary-shelley]] — *Frankenstein* is a foundational Romantic text, exploring the Promethean overreach and the creature's passionate isolation.
- [[french-revolution]] — catalyst for Romanticism; Romantics wrestled with the Revolution's ideals and its failures, seeking alternatives to rationalist utopia.
- [[existentialism]] — later inheritors of Romantic emphasis on individual choice, authenticity, and confrontation with an absurd or indifferent universe.
