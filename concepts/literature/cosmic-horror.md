## summary

**Cosmic horror** is a subgenre of speculative fiction and horror that emphasizes humanity's profound insignificance within an incomprehensible and indifferent universe. Championed in the pulp magazines of the early twentieth century, it shifts focus away from traditional supernatural monsters toward unfathomable extraterrestrial entities, forbidden ancient knowledge, and the inevitable destruction of human sanity. Its underlying philosophy, often termed cosmicism, rejects anthropocentric morality and divine benevolence in favor of an alien, uncaring cosmos.

## you gotta know

- Emerged from nineteenth-century weird tales and *gothic-literature*, drawing deeply on the psychological dread pioneered by *Edgar Allan Poe*.
- Codified by American pulp author *H. P. Lovecraft* in essays such as *Supernatural Horror in Literature* and stories establishing the Cthulhu Mythos.
- Subverts traditional folklore by substituting classic demons and ghosts with vast, godlike extraterrestrial beings like *Cthulhu*, *Azathoth*, and *Yog-Sothoth*.
- Central motif involves cursed relics, alien geometries, and forbidden occult texts—most famously the fictional grimoire *Necronomicon*.
- Protagonists are typically antiquarians, scholars, or scientists whose pursuit of forbidden knowledge leads directly to madness, suicide, or physiological degeneration.
- Strongly influenced late twentieth-century and contemporary speculative fiction, providing philosophical frameworks for authors like *Stephen King*, *Thomas Ligotti*, and *Jeff VanderMeer*.

## connections

- [[gothic-literature]] — the ancestral aesthetic mode providing eerie atmospheres, haunted ruins, and ancestral curses.
- [[science-fiction]] — the analytical genre whose scientific cosmology, deep time, and extraterrestrial themes merge directly into cosmic dread.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — foundational American master of the macabre whose psychological tales directly inspired early cosmic horror pioneers.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical parallel examining human alienation and the crisis of meaning in a silent, godless universe.
- [[cold-war]] — historical period whose nuclear terrors and fear of unseen extinction renewed apocalyptic cosmic themes.
- [[american-literature]] — the overarching tradition encompassing the weird pulp magazines of Providence and the American Northeast.
