## summary

**Oscar Wilde** was an Irish playwright, novelist, and wit (1854–1900) whose works challenged Victorian morality through paradox and theatrical brilliance. He elevated the social comedy and aesthetic philosophy into high art, before his public trials and imprisonment for homosexuality cast him into exile. His legacy spans both his witty comedies and his tragic biography—a figure whose life became as performative as his art.

## you gotta know

- Wilde's plays *The Importance of Being Earnest* and *An Ideal Husband* are masterpieces of British comedy, using wordplay and absurdity to expose Victorian hypocrisy.
- His novel *The Picture of Dorian Gray* explores aestheticism and the Faustian bargain of eternal youth at the cost of moral corruption.
- Wilde championed "art for art's sake"—the principle that aesthetic beauty need not serve moral or social purposes.
- He was a celebrated wit in London society; his conversational paradoxes ("I can resist anything except temptation") became legendary.
- His love affair with Lord Alfred Douglas and subsequent trial for "gross indecency" (1895) resulted in two years of hard labor in prison, becoming a watershed moment in queer history.
- His imprisonment devastated his health and career; he died in Paris in 1900 at age 46, largely estranged from society.
- Post-imprisonment, he wrote *The Ballad of Reading Gaol*, a devastating critique of the prison system and an elegy for the executed prisoner.
