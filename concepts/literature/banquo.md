## summary

**Banquo** is a Scottish general and kinsman to *Macbeth* in Shakespeare's *Macbeth*, whose loyalty, suspicion, and tragic death form a counterpoint to Macbeth's ambition. Encountered alongside Macbeth by the witches, Banquo also receives a prophecy — his descendants will be kings — yet he does not pursue power as Macbeth does. His murder becomes the psychological crisis that undoes his killer, and his ghost haunts Macbeth at the pivotal feast scene.

## you gotta know

- Banquo meets the witches with Macbeth and receives the prophecy that his children will be kings, yet he does not act upon it with murderous ambition or paranoid scheming.
- His constancy and moral resistance to temptation contrast sharply with Macbeth's rapid descent into tyranny and madness, making him a crucial moral foil to the protagonist's ambition.
- Macbeth murders Banquo out of paranoia and existential fear — the prophecy that Banquo's line will rule makes him an intolerable threat in Macbeth's mind even as Macbeth seizes power.
- Banquo's ghost appears at Macbeth's state banquet, visible to no one but Macbeth, confirming his guilt and driving his mental disintegration and violent paranoia at a critical moment.
- His disappearance from the play after his murder (only returning as a ghost) paradoxically makes him more powerful and present, haunting Macbeth's conscience and shaping the tragedy's outcome.
- The witches' prophecies about Banquo's heirs frame Macbeth's final battle and defeat — his desperate attempts to prevent the prophecy by murdering Banquo ultimately and ironically fulfill it completely.
- Banquo's silence about his suspicions of Macbeth, and his passivity in the face of evil, raises moral questions about complicity and cowardice that add psychological complexity to his character beyond simple contrast.
- The dramatic irony of Banquo's murder — that Macbeth kills him to prevent a prophecy yet this act itself ensures Banquo's descendants inherit power — exemplifies the play's tragic logic of self-fulfilling prophecy.
