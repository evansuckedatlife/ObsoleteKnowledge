## summary

**Drama** is the literary form composed of dialogue and action designed for live performance before an audience, encompassing tragedy, comedy, and all genres in between. Distinguished from narrative literature by its direct representation of character and action through performers on stage, drama has been a central mode of human storytelling since ancient Greece and remains vital to culture. The form uniquely engages an audience through immediate, physical presence—the unfolding of events in real time, the actor's body and voice, the architecture of the theatre space itself—making drama simultaneously an art of text and performance.

## you gotta know

- Drama is structured around dialogue; the audience experiences the story through what characters say and do, rather than through narration or description.
- Classical dramatic structure, codified by *Aristotle* and refined over centuries, involves exposition, rising action, climax, and resolution—a framework that remains influential.
- Major genres include tragedy (ending in disaster), comedy (ending in resolution and celebration), and variations like tragicomedy, farce, and melodrama.
- Greek drama, Elizabethan drama (Shakespeare), French neoclassical drama, and modern/contemporary drama each transformed the form's conventions and possibilities.
- Drama in the modern era has exploded beyond the proscenium stage to include film, television, radio, and experimental performance, but the core elements of character, dialogue, and conflict remain central.
- Playwrights like *Anton Chekhov* revolutionised drama by eliminating traditional climactic action in favour of psychological subtlety and the emotional texture of ordinary life.

## connections

- [[tragedy]] — the highest form of drama in classical aesthetics, centring on the fall of noble figures and producing catharsis.
- [[comedy]] — the counterpart to tragedy, centring on human folly, mistakes, and resolution through wit and recognition.
- [[greek-tragedy]] — the foundational dramatic form, establishing the conventions of unity, action, and moral seriousness that influenced all subsequent drama.
- [[elizabethan-theatre]] — the era when drama reached extraordinary heights in the hands of *William Shakespeare* and his contemporaries.
- [[performance]] — the embodied realisation of dramatic text; drama exists fully only in performance.
- [[anton-chekhov]] — a master playwright who transformed drama by centering psychological realism and the interior life over external action.
