## summary

**Canadian Literature** comprises the oral traditions and written literary works produced by writers from Canada in English, French, and Indigenous languages. Shaped by vast geographic wilderness, colonial dualities within the [[british-empire]], and questions of national identity, the tradition blossomed internationally in the twentieth century through psychological [[realism]] and regional storytelling. Its landmark figures include master short-story stylist *Alice Munro*, novelist *Margaret Atwood*, and poet-novelist *Michael Ondaatje*.

## you gotta know

- Conceptually historicised by critic *Northrop Frye* through the "garrison mentality," describing early settler writing obsessed with surviving an unforgiving wilderness.
- *Margaret Atwood*'s critical treatise *Survival* (1972) famously framed victimhood and perseverance against harsh natural forces as the central thematic motif of the nation's canon.
- Southern Ontario Gothic emerged as a major regional current through writers like *Alice Munro* and *Robertson Davies*, exposing dark undercurrents beneath tranquil rural towns.
- *Alice Munro* became the sole Canadian-based author to win the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2013, celebrated as the "master of the contemporary short story."
- Celebrated novelists such as *Margaret Laurence* created enduring regional sagas, including the fictional prairie town of Manawaka in *The Stone Angel*.
- French-Canadian literature, notably during Quebec's Quiet Revolution, explored linguistic identity and urban alienation in works like *Gabrielle Roy*'s *The Tin Flute* and *Michel Tremblay*'s plays.
- Major annual literary awards like the Scotiabank Giller Prize and the Governor General's Awards serve as key barometers for contemporary national fiction.
- Modern developments vigorously challenge historical settler perspectives through prominent Indigenous authors such as *Thomas King*, *Eden Robinson*, and *Richard Wagamese*.

## connections

- [[realism]] — the dominant aesthetic mode of regional short fiction documenting everyday town life.
- [[modernism]] — early-to-mid-twentieth-century aesthetic movement that revitalised Canadian verse and fiction.
- [[gothic-literature]] — stylistic influence reflected in Southern Ontario Gothic's dark psychological portraits.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial framework that governed early colonial Canadian identity and letters.
- [[colonialism]] — historical dynamic critically interrogated by modern and contemporary Canadian authors.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — psychological narrative technique adopted by postwar Canadian novelists.
