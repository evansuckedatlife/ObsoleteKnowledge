## summary

**Tony Kushner** is an American playwright and screenwriter (born 1956) whose magnum opus, *Angels in America*, stands as one of the most significant theatrical works of the late twentieth century. A two-part epic drama encompassing the AIDS crisis, Cold War politics, and American spiritual crisis, *Angels in America* established Kushner as a major voice in contemporary theater and secured his position as an indispensable commentator on American identity, queerness, and moral responsibility. His work combines theatrical ambition, political urgency, and emotional depth.

## you gotta know

- Grew up in Louisiana and New Jersey; both his parents were artists and politically engaged, shaping his aesthetic and activist sensibilities.
- *Angels in America: A Gay Fantasia on National Themes* premiered regionally in 1990; Part One (*Millennium Approaches*) opened on Broadway in 1993, Part Two (*Perestroika*) in 1994.
- The play integrates realistic character drama with surrealism, mime, rapid scene shifts, and fantastical visitations—a technique Kushner calls "a gay fantasia"—to match the disorientation of crisis.
- Won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama (1993) and multiple Tony Awards; adapted as an HBO miniseries (2003) directed by *Mike Nichols*, extending its cultural reach.
- Openly gay; his work centers LGBTQ+ characters and experiences during the catastrophic early years of the AIDS pandemic, making visible those the government and media ignored.
- Continues to write for stage and screen; *Lincoln* (2012) and *The Intelligent Homosexual's Guide to Capitalism and Socialism with a Key to the Scriptures* (2009) demonstrate his range across historical and contemporary themes.
- *Angels in America* dramatizes the experiences of four people—*Prior Walter* living with AIDS, his lover *Louis*, conservative lawyer *Joe Pitt*, and his wife *Harper*—during the Reagan administration.
- The play features an actual angel visitation; prior knowledge of the playwright's perspective makes it clear that spectacle and spiritual vision coexist with material suffering and political indifference.
- *Kushner's* dialogue combines heightened language, humor, and brutal honesty; the play moves between domestic realism and epic theatrical ambition.
- His work demonstrates that theater is a viable medium for political engagement and LGBTQ+ representation; *Angels in America* remains the most significant American play of the late twentieth century.
