## summary

**Catch-22** is a satirical 1961 novel by American author *Joseph Heller*, universally celebrated as a landmark of twentieth-century black comedy and anti-war literature. Set during the Italian campaign of [[world-war-ii]], the narrative chronicles the desperate stratagems of Captain *John Yossarian*, an American bombardier attempting to survive insane military bureaucracy. The book gave world culture its eponymous phrase for an inescapable circular dilemma, exposing how institutional authority weaponizes absurdity to commodify and destroy the individual.

## you gotta know

- Codifies the titular bureaucratic paradox, explained by flight surgeon Doc Daneeka: airmen who are insane may be grounded from dangerous combat flights, but requesting to be grounded demonstrates rational concern for self-preservation, which instantly proves the airman sane and therefore required to fly.
- Follows Captain *John Yossarian*, a bombardier stationed with the fictional 256th Squadron on the Mediterranean island of Pianosa off [[italy]], who is driven by the conviction that millions of strangers are actively trying to kill him.
- Driven by the tyrannical vanity of Colonel Cathcart, who continually raises the required number of combat flight missions from twenty-five toward eighty whenever airmen approach completion of their tour.
- Lampoons wartime capitalism through mess officer *Milo Minderbinder*, whose international syndicate *M&M Enterprises* trades military supplies with the enemy and eventually contracts with the German *Luftwaffe* to bomb his own American airfield for cost-plus-six-percent profit.
- Uses a disjointed, recursive, non-linear chronology anchored by the horrifying, gradually revealed death of the young radio gunner *Snowden*, whose spilled entrails force Yossarian to recognize that humanity is merely fragile matter.
- Populated by grotesque bureaucratic archetypes, such as *Major Major Major Major* (who only permits visitors into his office when he is away) and General Scheisskopf (whose singular military obsession is marching parades).
- Emerged alongside contemporaries like [[cat-s-cradle]] during the [[cold-war]], quickly becoming an iconic countercultural touchstone whose critique of mechanized violence mirrored rising disillusionment during the [[vietnam-war]].

## connections

- [[cat-s-cradle]] — peer masterpiece of mid-century American apocalyptic [[satire]] critiquing institutional hubris and technocratic absurdity.
- [[kurt-vonnegut]] — contemporary American satirist who similarly fused black humor with traumatic memories of [[world-war-ii]].
- [[world-war-ii]] — historical setting providing the material reality of Mediterranean bombing campaigns and militarized mass mobilization.
- [[satire]] — primary literary vehicle deployed to dismantle military hierarchy, jingoistic patriotism, and capitalist greed.
- [[american-literature]] — pivotal postwar text that transitioned American prose from heroic realism to postmodern black humor.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical framework animating Yossarian's desperate defense of personal bodily survival against absurd systems.
- [[vietnam-war]] — historical conflict during which the novel's anti-authoritarian stance transformed it into an anthem of draft resistance.
