## summary

**Greed**, or *avarice*, is an insatiable desire for material wealth, possessions, or gain that serves as one of the most enduring moral and psychological themes across world literature. Classically codified within *Christian theology* as one of the *Seven Deadly Sins*, it drives narrative tension by blinding individuals to ethical limits, spiritual fulfillment, and mortal vulnerability. In literary traditions extending from medieval allegories to nineteenth-century *realism*, the pursuit of excessive accumulation consistently precipitates devastating personal downfall, social disintegration, and existential despair.

## you gotta know

- Traditional Christian theology categorizes **greed** (*radix malorum est cupiditas*) as a cardinal sin, famously expounded in *Geoffrey Chaucer's* *"The Pardoner's Tale"*, where three rioters murder each other over a hoard of gold florins found beneath an oak tree.
- In *Leo Tolstoy's* celebrated moral parable *"How Much Land Does a Man Need?"*, the protagonist *Pakhom* is ruined by an unrelenting appetite for property, ultimately collapsing and dying of exhaustion after running across Bashkir lands, needing only six feet of earth for his grave.
- The archetype of the miserly accumulator receives defining portraits in nineteenth-century *realism*, notably *Charles Dickens's* *Ebenezer Scrooge* in *A Christmas Carol* and the rapacious usurer *Grandet* in *Honoré de Balzac's* *Eugénie Grandet*.
- In *Dante Alighieri's* *Inferno*, the avaricious and prodigal are condemned to the fourth circle of hell, compelled to eternally roll giant boulders against one another in symbolic futility.
- Playwright *Molière* satirized compulsive accumulation in *L'Avare* (*The Miser*), where the protagonist *Harpagon* is reduced to frantic paranoia over his buried cashbox.
- In *Frank Norris's* naturalist masterpiece *McTeague*, excessive avarice corrupts *Trina Sieppe* and her dentist husband, culminating in murder and McTeague's isolation in *Death Valley*, handcuffed to a corpse beside a bag of gold.
- In Renaissance drama, the destructive hunger for sovereign power and material dominion is depicted in *Christopher Marlowe's* *The Jew of Malta* and *William Shakespeare's* *Macbeth*, illustrating how unrestrained appetite erodes human empathy.

## connections

- [[leo-tolstoy]] — author of *"How Much Land Does a Man Need?"*, which depicts land accumulation resulting in mortal tragedy.
- [[charles-dickens]] — nineteenth-century novelist who immortalized capitalist avarice through figures like *Ebenezer Scrooge*.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — dramatist who explored avarice, ambition, and covetousness across his major tragedies.
- [[christianity]] — religious tradition that codified avarice as a capital vice leading to spiritual decay.
- [[realism]] — literary movement that analyzed socioeconomic avarice within emerging industrial societies.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical framework examining the emptiness and absurdity of material accumulation in the face of death.
- [[grotesque]] — aesthetic mode frequently depicting physical and moral deformities brought on by pathological appetites.
