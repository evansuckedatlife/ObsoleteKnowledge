## summary

**Face the servant**, originally named *Jeremy*, is the cunning domestic housekeeper and antihero of *Ben Jonson*'s celebrated 1610 Jacobean city comedy *The Alchemist*. Left unsupervised in his master's London townhouse during an outbreak of the bubonic plague, he assumes the swaggering persona of *Captain Face* to run a lucrative confidence racket alongside a fraudulent alchemist and a quick-witted prostitute. Face is revered in dramatic history as a brilliant English Renaissance evolution of the clever servant archetype, whose amoral adaptability and comic survival form the satirical heart of the play.

## you gotta know

- Orchestrates the criminal enterprise in *Ben Jonson*'s *The Alchemist* by turning his master *Lovewit*'s vacated home in Blackfriars into a fraudulent laboratory while London flees the plague.
- Adopts multiple fluid personae, moving seamlessly between the blustering military officer *Captain Face*, the soot-stained alchemical assistant *Lungs* (or *Zephyrus*), and the submissive domestic butler *Jeremy*.
- Forms an uneasy criminal triumvirate with the faux-sorcerer *Subtle* and the resourceful courtesan *Dol Common*, relying on volatile mutual contracts of shared profit.
- Gulls a broad satirical cross-section of early modern Londoners, including the epicurean knight *Sir Epicure Mammon*, the naive clerk *Dapper*, the tobacconist *Abel Drugger*, and hypocritical Anabaptist puritans.
- Parodies scientific inquiry by utilizing elaborate alchemical jargon, transforming the public's gullibility into immediate wealth through bogus laboratory demonstrations.
- Navigates disaster upon the unexpected early return of master *Lovewit* by instantly betraying his criminal accomplices and engineering his master's marriage to the wealthy widow *Dame Pliant*.
- Embodies the dramatic tradition of the Roman *servus callidus* (cunning slave) popularized by *Plautus* and *Terence*, directly anticipating later theatrical schemers like *Molière*'s *Scapin* and *Beaumarchais*'s *Figaro*.
