## summary

**Narrative technique** encompasses the deliberate structural, stylistic, and psychological devices that writers, dramatists, and filmmakers employ to craft stories, control perspective, and guide audience interpretation. Refined across centuries from classical rhetoric through nineteenth-century [[realism]] to the formal innovations of [[modernism]], it dictates how information is sequenced, pace is modulated, and emotional distance is maintained between the audience, the narrator, and the characters. Understanding these mechanics is essential for analyzing how storytellers transcend basic plot exposition to orchestrate suspense, foster empathy, and embed complex thematic meaning.

## you gotta know

- Information control forms the bedrock of narrative tension by managing the disparity between what the audience knows and what characters perceive, a principle famously illustrated by *[[alfred-hitchcock|Alfred Hitchcock]]* in his comparison of sudden surprise versus prolonged suspense, wherein spectator foreknowledge transforms an ordinary scene into agonizing anticipation.
- Point of view dictates the optical and psychological vantage point of a work, evolving from traditional omniscient third-person narration to [[stream-of-consciousness]] and free indirect discourse, a signature method of literary [[realism]] that fluidly merges a narrator's voice with a protagonist's unfiltered thoughts.
- Dramatic irony and unreliable narration deliberately exploit gaps between character awareness and audience comprehension, presenting skewed testimonies that compel the audience to decipher subtext, a device popularized in [[gothic-literature]] and psychological tales by *[[edgar-allan-poe|Edgar Allan Poe]]*.
- Narrative economy and misdirection rely on conventions like *Chekhov's gun*, which stipulates that every emphasized detail must ultimately influence the plot, alongside red herrings and narrative decoys strategically planted to mislead audience deductions in [[detective-fiction]].
- Temporal structure disrupts straightforward chronology through *in medias res* beginnings, analepsis (flashbacks), and prolepsis (flash-forwards), as well as nested frame stories that layer perspectives within an overarching narrative scaffolding.
- Defamiliarization, or *ostranenie*, introduced by formalist critic *Viktor Shklovsky*, revitalizes perception by describing conventional events and objects from an uninitiated viewpoint, famously exemplified by *[[leo-tolstoy|Leo Tolstoy]]* when depicting social rituals and battle scenes throughout *[[war-and-peace|War and Peace]]*.
- Pacing and delayed gratification control the rhythm of consumption by manipulating the duration of scenes, using deliberate pauses, scene dilation, and summary transitions to prolong tension immediately prior to narrative climaxes.

## connections

- [[leo-tolstoy]] — mastered defamiliarization and panoramic shifting perspective in nineteenth-century realist epics to lay bare human psychology and historical myth.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — revolutionized twentieth-century literature by capturing the unstructured, associative cognitive flow of inner mental life.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — pioneered the philosophy of composition and the neurotic unreliable narrator to achieve a unified emotional effect in psychological tales.
- [[detective-fiction]] — codified rigid structural formulas centered on informational asymmetry, deceptive clues, and retrospective narrative resolution.
- [[realism]] — cultivated psychological verisimilitude through free indirect discourse and meticulous environmental observation.
- [[gothic-literature]] — deployed discovered manuscripts, eerie framing devices, and oppressive atmosphere to evoke psychological terror and dread.
- [[charles-dickens]] — weaponized serialized cliffhangers, intertwined plotlines, and heightened character caricatures across expansive social panoramas.
