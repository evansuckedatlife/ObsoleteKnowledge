## summary

**Postmodern Literature** is fiction that embraces fragmentation, recursion, and self-reflexivity as formal responses to the impossibility of grand narrative and unified meaning. Instead of pursuing *modernism's* goal of aesthetic innovation toward coherence, postmodern writers foreground narrative artifice, collapse boundaries between genres, and treat literature itself as a game to be played with readers. The form reflects an epistemological crisis: truth becomes multiple, unstable, and ironic; history becomes a contested text rather than fixed fact.

## you gotta know

- Emerged in the 1960s–1970s as writers lost faith in *modernism's* promise that formal innovation could produce new meaning; instead, postmodern writers weaponize form to show meaning's fragility.
- *Metafiction* is central: literature that calls attention to its own artificiality, breaks the fourth wall, foregrounds the author, treats narrative as construction rather than illusion of reality.
- *Gabriel García Márquez's* *One Hundred Years of Solitude* exemplifies the form: cyclical narrative, magical realism, unreliable history, self-referentiality all create a novel that undermines its own authority even as it narrates.
- *Intertextuality* as aesthetic principle: postmodern works layer quotations, parodies, and allusions; they treat all prior texts as available material to remix rather than traditions to respectfully inherit.
- *Fragmentation* of narrative: rejected linear plot in favor of *non-linear* or *anti-narrative* structures; readers must actively construct meaning from disordered or competing narratives.
- *Irony* as default mode: the narrator often adopts attitudes opposite to apparent intent, creating distance between statement and meaning; certainty is treated with suspicion.
- Unlike *modernist* elitism, postmodern literature often celebrates *pastiche* and the mixing of high and low culture, treating mass media and literary canon with equal ironic distance.

## connections

- [[postmodernism]] — the broader cultural and philosophical movement of which postmodern literature is the aesthetic manifestation.
- [[magical-realism]] — the technique that treats the impossible as fact, undermining realism's epistemological claims; a key postmodern strategy.
- [[one-hundred-years-of-solitude]] — the canonical postmodern novel combining magical realism, circularity, and historical unreliability.
- [[metafiction]] — the self-reflexive narrative technique central to postmodern literature's deconstruction of meaning.
- [[thomas-pynchon]] — American postmodern master of fragmentation, conspiracy, and ironic distance.
- [[fragmentation]] — the formal strategy through which postmodern literature denies narrative coherence and stable meaning.
