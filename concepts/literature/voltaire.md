# Voltaire

## summary

**Voltaire** (1694–1778) was a French Enlightenment writer, philosopher, and polemicist whose wit, prolific output, and combative advocacy for reason, tolerance, and individual liberty made him the intellectual voice of the 18th century. Through satire, history, philosophy, and correspondence with royalty and fellow thinkers, he attacked superstition, religious dogmatism, and tyranny, establishing the template for the modern public intellectual.

## you gotta know

- Born François-Marie Arouet into a middle-class family; adopted the pen name *Voltaire*, which became synonymous with Enlightenment ideals and the power of satire as a weapon against authority.
- Imprisoned in the Bastille twice for his writings and political affiliations; exiled from France multiple times, yet continued publishing under pseudonyms and through clandestine networks, making suppression of his work impossible.
- *Candide* (1759), his novella-length masterpiece of satire, mocks the philosophy of his day—especially *Leibniz's* claim that "all is for the best in this best of all possible worlds"—by chronicling a naive young man's brutal education through suffering and absurdity.
- Prolific across genres: plays (over 50), historical works, philosophical essays, and thousands of letters; correspondence was itself a medium of Enlightenment thought, connecting European intellectuals.
- Advocated relentlessly for the separation of church and state, religious toleration, freedom of conscience, and legal reform; his campaigns contributed to real-world changes, including interventions in cases of judicial injustice.
- Cultivated relationships with power: corresponded with Frederick II of Prussia, Catherine the Great of Russia, and French nobility, using access to influence policy toward Enlightenment goals.
- Championed reason and empirical observation against superstition and revealed religious dogma; his attacks on religious fanaticism (captured in the phrase *"Écrasez l'infâme!"* — "Crush the infamy!") defined the Enlightenment's combat against clerical authority.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical movement he embodied and shaped; he gave Enlightenment its voice, wit, and moral urgency.
- [[french-revolution]] — the revolution that followed his death drew directly on his writings on liberty, reason, and the critique of tyranny and privilege.
- [[candide]] — his most famous work; a masterwork of satirical form that exposes the inadequacy of naive optimism in the face of injustice.
- [[existentialism]] — later thinkers inherited his insistence on human responsibility and choice in a universe not designed for human comfort.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — Voltaire championed Shakespeare in France, translating and defending him against French classical orthodoxy, helping establish the Bard as universal.
- [[rationalism]] — he stood at the philosophical intersection of empiricism and reason, defending systematic thought against superstition.
- [[philosophy]] — his essays *Philosophical Dictionary* and countless treatises made philosophy accessible and combative, not merely academic.
