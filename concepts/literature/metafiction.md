## summary

**Metafiction** is fiction that reflexively examines its own nature as constructed narrative, exposing the artifices of storytelling rather than concealing them. A metafictional work might break the fourth wall, reveal its narrator as unreliable or invented, comment on its own plot, or foreground the process of writing itself. This technique emerged as a conscious literary strategy in the twentieth century, particularly among modernist and postmodernist writers who were skeptical of the novel's capacity to represent reality transparently or objectively.

## you gotta know

- Metafiction rejects the realist contract where the reader suspends disbelief in exchange for the illusion of a real world represented faithfully.
- *Jorge Luis Borges* is often credited with inventing metafiction as a major literary mode through stories like *The Library of Babel* and *Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius*.
- Stories may feature characters who are also writers, novels within novels, or the text acknowledging moments when its own plot becomes confused or contradictory.
- Metafictional techniques include: footnotes that claim questionable authenticity, unreliable narrators who admit they are inventing, and stories that collapse the distinction between reality and fiction.
- *Flann O'Brien's* *At Swim-Two-Birds* features an author writing a novel within a novel, with his characters rebelling against the author's authority over their fates.
- Metafiction challenges the reader: it demands active interpretation and acknowledgment that all narratives are constructed, not discovered.
- The technique intensified in postmodernism but has roots in earlier self-conscious literature like *Laurence Sterne's* *Tristram Shandy*.
- Metafictional works often expose narrative conventions through parody or direct commentary, forcing readers to recognize how stories rely on arbitrary rules and reader cooperation.

## connections

- [[jorge-luis-borges]] — Argentine master of metafiction; his stories are foundational to the form's modern development.
- [[at-swim-two-birds]] — Irish novel that features layered narrative, characters who contest their author's control, and comedy derived from narrative instability.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — related technique of interior representation that reveals the fragmentary nature of consciousness and language.
- [[postmodernism]] — metafiction became a defining practice of postmodern literature, which embraced artificiality and formal self-awareness.
- [[modernism]] — modernist writers like *Joyce* and *Woolf* pioneered some metafictional techniques through narrative innovation.
