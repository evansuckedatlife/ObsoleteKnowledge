# Sophocles

## summary

**Sophocles** (496–406 BCE) was an Athenian tragedian and one of the three great Greek playwrights, alongside Aeschylus and Euripides. His surviving plays—*Oedipus Rex*, *Electra*, and *Antigone*, among others—explore the collision between individual will and cosmic destiny, human dignity and fate. His works defined tragic drama and shaped how Western literature treats moral ambiguity and psychological depth.

## you gotta know

- Survived nearly 90 years through most of the 5th century BCE, witnessing Athens' rise to power, its wars, and the Peloponnesian conflict; his long life gave him authority as both artist and cultural figure.
- Won at least 18 drama competitions at the City Dionysia, Athens' premier theater festival, establishing him as the most celebrated tragedian of his time.
- Authored over 120 plays during his lifetime; only 7 survive today, making him far better preserved than Aeschylus, whose work predated and influenced his.
- *Oedipus Rex* is often considered the perfect tragedy: it turns on the hero's belated discovery that he has already fulfilled the oracle's curse, making him simultaneously victim and agent of his doom.
- *Antigone* pits familial loyalty and moral conscience against state law, staging the conflict between *Creon*'s authoritarian decree and *Antigone*'s sacred duty to bury her brother—a dilemma still debated in ethics.
- His plays feature what became the model for Greek tragedy: a protagonist of high status, often blindness (literal or metaphorical) to a terrible truth, and *peripeteia* (reversal of fortune) triggered by recognition or revelation.
- Introduced the third speaking actor to the Greek stage, expanding dramatic possibilities beyond the two-actor limit of Aeschylus' time.

## connections

- [[aeschylus]] — his predecessor and influence; Aeschylus shaped the tragic form that Sophocles refined into philosophical precision.
- [[euripides]] — his contemporary rival; where Sophocles emphasized fate and the gods' inscrutability, Euripides questioned divine justice itself.
- [[oedipus-rex]] — his masterpiece; the model tragedy for centuries, studied for its inevitability and irony.
- [[antigone]] — explores moral conflict between individual conscience and political authority, a perennial question in dramatic art.
- [[athens]] — the city-state where his plays were performed during the height of the Athenian empire and democracy's flourishing.
- [[the-oresteia]] — Aeschylus' trilogy that set the template for tragic structure Sophocles inherited and perfected.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — later dramatist whose works echo Sophocles' exploration of tragic flaw and fate.
