## summary

**Henri Bergson** (born **Henri-Louis Bergson**) was an eminent French philosopher whose revolutionary investigations of time, memory, and consciousness reshaped early twentieth-century intellectual life and European literature. Honored with the 1927 *Nobel Prize in Literature* for his rich, life-affirming ideas and lucid prose style, he vigorously opposed the mechanistic determinism of nineteenth-century science by asserting the primacy of lived experience and intuition. His foundational doctrine of *duration* offered a radical alternative to spatialized clock time, exerting an enormous formative influence on literary [[modernism]] and the emergence of [[stream-of-consciousness]] narrative.

## you gotta know

- Formulated the seminal philosophical doctrine of *la durée* (duration) in *Time and Free Will* (1889), arguing that real time is a fluid, indivisible qualitative flow of consciousness rather than a sequence of spatialized, measurable increments.
- Advanced the concept of the *élan vital* ("vital impetus") in his 1907 treatise *Creative Evolution*, postulating an immanent creative surge that drives biological evolution beyond simple mechanistic selection.
- Authored the classic psychological and aesthetic essay *Laughter: An Essay on the Meaning of the Comic* (1900), famously arguing that comedy occurs when we observe "something mechanical encrusted on the living."
- Distinguished between bodily habit-memory and spontaneous, spirit-bearing recollection in *Matter and Memory* (1896), proposing that the brain acts as an organ of action and filtering rather than a storehouse of thoughts.
- Strongly influenced his younger relative by marriage, novelist Marcel Proust, whose multi-volume masterpiece *In Search of Lost Time* serves as the quintessential literary translation of Bergsonian memory and subjective temporality.
- Drew immense cosmopolitan audiences of writers, academics, and aristocrats to his packed public lectures at the *Collège de France*, giving rise to an international intellectual phenomenon dubbed "Bergsonism."
- Participated in an iconic 1922 debate in Paris against Albert Einstein, where he defended the psychological and philosophical authenticity of lived simultaneity against purely mathematical models of relativity.
- Concluded his major philosophical works with *The Two Sources of Morality and Religion* (1932), setting static, legalistic closed societies against open societies energized by creative moral mysticism.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — overarching aesthetic movement whose preoccupation with subjective perception, nonlinear memory, and internal monologue drew inspiration from his philosophy.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — literary technique created by twentieth-century novelists to capture the continuous, unfragmented flow of mental life that he called duration.
- [[france]] — nation where he achieved intellectual prominence as a professor at the Collège de France and an influential public philosopher of the Third Republic.
- [[virginia-woolf]] — English modernist novelist whose experimental representations of internal mental time and moments of being mirror his temporal concepts.
- [[james-joyce]] — Irish writer whose detailed evocation of inner mental associations over a single day in *Ulysses* reflects the fluid temporality Bergson theorized.
- [[henry-g-wells]] — English author whose fictional device of treating time as a fourth spatial coordinate in *The Time Machine* exemplified the spatialized time Bergson critiqued.
- [[existentialism]] — subsequent continental movement that built upon his defense of human agency, intuition, and radical open-ended freedom.
- [[world-war-i]] — conflict during which he undertook diplomatic missions to the [[united-states]] on behalf of France, subsequently chairing the League of Nations' International Commission on Intellectual Cooperation.
