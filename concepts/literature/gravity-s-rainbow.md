## summary

**Gravity's Rainbow** is an encyclopedic 1973 postmodern novel by the American author Thomas Pynchon, set in Europe during the waning months and immediate aftermath of the Second World War. Centered on the pursuit of the mysterious Nazi V-2 rocket and its secretive component, the *Schwarzgerät*, the novel weaves together paranoia, ballistics, occultism, behavioral psychology, and corporate cartels. It stands as one of the most ambitious, complex, and influential works of twentieth-century American literature.

## you gotta know

- Opens with the iconic line "A screaming comes across the sky," describing an incoming German V-2 rocket which travels faster than the speed of sound.
- Follows American lieutenant Tyrone Slothrop, whose sexual encounters across wartime London appear to anticipate the exact impact sites of incoming supersonic V-2 rocket strikes.
- Reveals that Slothrop was conditioned in infancy as "Infant Tyrone" by behaviorist Laszlo Jamf using the fictional synthetic polymer Imipolex G, creating a bizarre link to the weapon.
- Depicts the shadowy rocket commander Major General Walter Dornberger alongside SS General Hans Kammler and rocket battery commander Captain Blicero (Weissmann), who launches a mystical rocket numbered 00000.
- Roams across the lawless, partition-era "Zone" of occupied Germany, where Slothrop adopts multiple personas, including the costumed hero "Plechazstl" (the Rocketman).
- Features an immense cast of over four hundred characters, including the Soviet officer Vasily Tchitcherine, the Herero rocket-scavengers known as the *Schwarzkommando*, and occult spiritualist séances.
- Selected unanimously by the Pulitzer Prize fiction jury in 1974, but denied the award by the advisory board on grounds of alleged obscenity and turgidity.
- Ends abruptly inside the Orpheus Theatre in Los Angeles with an intercontinental ballistic missile hovering poised above the roof while an apocalyptic film snaps.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the catastrophic global conflict providing the physical setting, technological crucible, and geopolitical ruin of the novel.
- [[cold-war]] — the looming postwar reality foreshadowed by corporate military-industrial maneuvering and intelligence rivalries in the Zone.
- [[united-states]] — the nationality of Tyrone Slothrop and the cultural homeland of postwar corporate power and paranoia.
- [[satire]] — the tonal mode Pynchon frequently deploys through bawdy musical limericks, slapstick chases, and grotesque character names.
- [[science-fiction]] — the intersecting genre invoked through futuristic rocketry, psychic espionage, synthetic plastics, and speculative alternate history.
- [[modernism]] — the antecedent high-literary tradition whose encyclopedic difficulty and fragmentation Pynchon extends into postmodernism.
