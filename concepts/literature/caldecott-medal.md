## summary

**Caldecott Medal**, officially known as the **Randolph Caldecott Medal**, is an annual literary prize awarded by the American Library Association to the artist of the most distinguished American picture book for children. Established in 1937, it serves as the premier honor for visual storytelling in [[american-literature]] and functions as the companion prize to the text-oriented Newbery Medal. The award honours British illustrator *Randolph Caldecott*, whose nineteenth-century artwork pioneered dynamic visual pacing.

## you gotta know

- Established in 1937 by publisher *Frederic G. Melcher*, who also conceived the Newbery Medal to celebrate excellence in children's publishing.
- Administered annually by the Association for Library Service to Children (ALSC), a division of the American Library Association in the [[united-states]].
- Awarded strictly to the illustrator, who must be a citizen or resident of the [[united-states]], focusing on visual narrative distinction rather than text alone.
- Named after nineteenth-century English illustrator *Randolph Caldecott*, whose dynamic depiction of the *John Gilpin* ride is embossed on the bronze medal face.
- Prominent winners include *Maurice Sendak* for *Where the Wild Things Are* (1964) and *Chris Van Allsburg* for *Jumanji* (1982) and *The Polar Express* (1986).
- *Robert McCloskey* became the first two-time recipient, capturing mid-century American regional [[realism]] in *Make Way for Ducklings* and *Time of Wonder*.
- Works earning runner-up recognition are designated as Caldecott Honor Books, distinguished by silver foil seals rather than gold.
- A select few books, such as *Last Stop on Market Street* (illustrated by *Christian Robinson*), have achieved simultaneous honors across both Caldecott and Newbery rosters.

## connections
