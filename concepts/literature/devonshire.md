## summary

**Devonshire**, a county in southwestern *England* known historically for its rugged coastline and expansive wild moors, occupies a celebrated position in British literature as a landscape of gothic dread and ancient folklore. Most notably embodied by the desolate wastes of Dartmoor, the region frequently serves as an atmospheric antagonist where primordial nature threatens modern rationalism. Its isolated manors and treacherous mires provide the ideal backdrop for mystery, psychological tension, and regional romance.

## you gotta know

- Forms the central setting of *Arthur Conan Doyle's* classic detective novel *The Hound of the Baskervilles*, where Dartmoor and the deadly Grimpen Mire personify primeval menace.
- Hosted Baskerville Hall, an ancient stone manor haunted by ancestral curses, escaped convicts, and prehistoric neolithic huts scattered across bleak granite tors.
- Inspired *Agatha Christie*, who was born in the coastal resort town of Torquay and set classic murder mysteries like *And Then There Were None* on an isolated island off the Devon coast.
- Featured extensively in the historical romances of *R. D. Blackmore*, whose seventeenth-century masterpiece *Lorna Doone* unfolds across the wild northern expanse of Exmoor bordering Somerset.
- Celebrated by *Samuel Taylor Coleridge*, a native of Ottery St Mary, whose early romantic imagination was steeped in local river landscapes and folk superstitions.
- Served *Thomas Hardy* as lower "Wessex", where the rural traditions, distinct dialect, and windswept pastures form the tragic backdrop for pastoral realism.
- Provided naval history and maritime legend linked to Plymouth Sound, Sir Francis Drake, and Elizabethan voyages across the Atlantic during the Age of Exploration.
