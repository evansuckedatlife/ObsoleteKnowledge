## summary

**Early Modern Psychology** designates the evolving systems of thought concerning the human mind, soul, passions, and nervous sensibility that developed between the Renaissance and the advent of experimental psychoanalysis. Shifting from classical humoral balance and Scholastic faculty psychology toward mechanistic empiricism, sensory associationism, and romantic interiority, this intellectual currents interrogated the nature of identity and moral agency. It is indispensable for understanding Western literature because dramatists, novelists, and essayists directly employed these emerging psychological models to invent modern dramatic soliloquy, depict irrational madness, and articulate the dark subterranean impulses of gothic fiction.

## you gotta know

- Originated in the transition away from Galenic humoral theory, which explained character, mood, and pathology through four bodily fluids.
- Relied heavily on faculty psychology, conceptualizing the mind as a hierarchical arena divided into distinct powers including reason, will, memory, and imagination.
- Revolutionized by *John Locke*'s *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, which proposed the mind as a blank slate shaped entirely by sensory experience and association.
- Explored in seventeenth-century philosophical treatises such as *René Descartes*'s *Passions of the Soul* and *Baruch Spinoza*'s *Ethics*, which analyzed emotional mechanics.
- Infiltrated Renaissance drama, most visibly through the exploration of melancholy, obsessive jealousy, and divided consciousness in the tragedies of *William Shakespeare*.
- Influenced the sentimental and gothic novel through eighteenth-century nervous physiology, which emphasized nervous sensibility and the pathology of sympathetic excess.
- Directly anticipated modern psychoanalytic theory by dramatizing repression, psychic fragmentation, doubling, and the uncanny in romantic and gothic narratives.
- Investigated intense non-rational mental states, connecting extreme terror, visionary trance, and emotional [[ecstasy]] to the underlying operations of the human psyche.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical epoch that privileged empirical inquiry, sensory education, and mechanistic analyses of human understanding.
- [[romanticism]] — the literary rebellion that championed unconscious feeling, intuition, and interior passion against dry rationalist psychology.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the narrative genre that dramatized psychic pathology, family trauma, guilt, the return of the repressed, and the uncanny double.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the English playwright whose characterizations incorporated contemporary ideas of melancholia, delirium, and conflicting internal faculties.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — the American author whose macabre fiction systematically explored morbid psychology, perversity, claustrophobia, and mental disintegration.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the socio-economic upheaval whose urban pressures and mechanization reshaped societal concepts of nervous exhaustion and alienation.
- [[ecstasy]] — the heightened emotional and spiritual state whose cognitive, somatic, and visionary dimensions puzzled early modern theorists.
- [[england]] — the intellectual and literary epicenter where Lockean empiricism and Victorian alienism fundamentally reconfigured mental science.
