## summary

**Alexei Ivanovich**, commonly called **Alexei**, is the narrator and tragic protagonist of the 1866 novel *The Gambler* by the Russian master [[fyodor-dostoevsky]]. Serving as an educated but destitute household tutor to an indebted aristocratic family in the fictional German spa town of *Roulettenburg*, he becomes hopelessly infatuated with his employer's stepdaughter and descends into a self-destructive compulsion at the roulette tables. His psychological trajectory embodies Dostoevsky's exploration of emotional degradation, irrationality, and the illusion of sudden redemption through risk, making him one of the foundational portraits of pathological addiction in nineteenth-century [[realism]].

## you gotta know

- Narrates his desperate enslavement to the roulette wheel in *Roulettenburg*, a fictionalized German resort where he wagers recklessly under the compulsive belief that a single lucky spin can transform his destiny.
- Works as an impoverished domestic tutor for an arrogant, ruined *General* and falls into an agonizing, masochistic passion for the General's stepdaughter, *Polina Alexandrovna*, willing to commit violence or public humiliation at her slightest whim.
- Witnesses the catastrophic arrival of the wealthy matriarch *Antonida Vasilyevna Tarasevicheva*, known as *Baboulinka*, who defies the family's greedy inheritance schemes by gambling away almost her entire fortune on zero at the tables.
- Wins a staggering fortune of two hundred thousand francs during an ecstatic, trance-like winning streak at the casino, only for *Polina* to accuse him of trying to purchase her love and throw the bank notes in his face.
- Squanders his remaining wealth in *Paris* while living as a pliant, passive consort to the calculating French courtesan *Mademoiselle Blanche*, who systematically exhausts his money before abandoning him.
- Sinks into complete vagrancy across [[germany]], spending years working as a menial servant and footman while scraping together petty coins for roulette, sustained only by the tragic delusion that "tomorrow, tomorrow it will all end."
- Encounters the English gentleman *Mr. Astley*, who informs him that *Polina* genuinely loved him but condemns him as an irredeemable gambler whose soul and intellect have been permanently extinguished by the gaming table.
- Parallels the real-life gambling mania of [[fyodor-dostoevsky]], who composed the text under extreme duress in twenty-six days alongside stenographer *Anna Grigoryevna Snitkina* to prevent a predatory publisher from claiming all his royalties.

## connections

- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — the Russian novelist who based the character's manic psychological frenzy on his personal roulette addiction at Saxon and Hessian spas.
- [[realism]] — the literary movement underpinning the unvarnished depiction of economic desperation, mental obsession, and social dislocation.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical worldview prefigured by his self-destructive assertion of agency and abandonment to arbitrary fate.
- [[tragedy]] — the narrative arc charting his inevitable spiritual ruin from an insightful intellectual to a helpless compulsive vagabond.
- [[satire]] — the biting comedic framework used to dissect the vanity, greed, and moral hypocrisy of the cosmopolitan elites surrounding him.
- [[germany]] — the geographic setting of *Roulettenburg*, whose casinos attract displaced Russians and European opportunists.
- [[france]] — the nationality of his cunning rival *Monsieur de Grieux* and the materialistic courtesan *Mademoiselle Blanche*.
