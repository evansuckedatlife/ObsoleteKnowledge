## summary

**The Death of Artemio Cruz** (*La muerte de Artemio Cruz*) is a 1962 novel by Mexican author [[carlos-fuentes|*Carlos Fuentes*]] that stands as a foundational masterwork of the Latin American Boom. Set as an elderly media tycoon and former revolutionary lies on his deathbed in Mexico City, the narrative reconstructs his ruthless ascent alongside the turbulent history of post-revolutionary Mexico. Celebrated for its experimental tripartite narrative voices and temporal fragmentation, the novel fuses [[modernism]], [[realism]], and [[stream-of-consciousness]] to indict the betrayal of revolutionary ideals.

## you gotta know

- Employs a tripartite narrative structure alternating between three voices and tenses: first-person ("yo") in the present recording his physical agony and sensory decay; second-person ("tú") in the future acting as a poetic subconscious questioning his choices; and third-person ("él") in the past narrating twelve crucial days between 1903 and 1955.
- Operates as a scathing allegory for the betrayal of the Mexican Revolution, depicting how idealistic fighters abandoned land reform and social justice to build a corrupt, self-enriching political elite.
- Gained wealth and social prestige by marrying *Catalina Bernal* after the revolution, having cynically abandoned her brother *Gonzalo Bernal* to a firing squad in prison and manipulated her grieving father to seize their estate.
- Remembers *Regina*, his sole authentic romantic love, an innocent camp follower during the revolution whose hanging by federal troops destroyed his capacity for tenderness and cemented his cynicism.
- Sent his beloved son *Lorenzo* to fight against fascism in the [[spain|Spanish]] Civil War to vicariously redeem his own corrupted youth, resulting in the boy's death in combat.
- Amassed immense wealth as a corrupt newspaper magnate, extortionist, and broker of American corporate concessions, spending his final decades alienated from family and accompanied by a bought mistress, *Lilia*.
- Portrays the dying patriarch surrounded by his estranged wife and daughter *Teresa*, who despise him while hovering alongside a priest and a lawyer to extract the location of his will.
- Unveils his origins as the unacknowledged son of an Afro-Mexican servant, *Isabel Cruz*, and a cruel landowner, *Atanasio Menchaca*, on a Veracruz plantation, raised by his uncle *Lunero* before fleeing into the revolution.

## connections

- [[carlos-fuentes]] — Mexican author whose 1962 novel established him as a leading figure of the Latin American Boom.
- [[modernism]] — literary movement whose techniques of non-linear chronology and fragmented interiority govern the novel's structure.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — stylistic technique driving the first-person deathbed passages as Cruz drifts through delirium and pain.
- [[mexican-literature]] — national tradition in which the work is regarded as a defining masterpiece alongside *Pedro Páramo*.
- [[octavio-paz]] — Mexican author whose exploration of identity in *[[the-labyrinth-of-solitude|The Labyrinth of Solitude]]* deeply parallels Fuentes' political critique.
- [[spain]] — European nation where Cruz sends his son *Lorenzo* to fight in the Spanish Civil War.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical current informing Cruz's confronting of mortality, bad faith, and the permanence of his choices.
- [[realism]] — aesthetic mode integrated with modern experimentation to depict the social realities of post-revolutionary Mexico.
