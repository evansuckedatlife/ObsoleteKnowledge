## summary

**Rhetoric** is the art of persuasion through language—the strategic deployment of words, figures, and arguments to move an audience toward belief or action. Born in ancient Greek and Roman education as one of the three arts of discourse (alongside grammar and logic), rhetoric has long been studied, taught, and practiced across all human communication. In literature, rhetoric appears both as craft (an author's techniques of persuasion) and as theme (characters deploy rhetoric to sway others); understanding rhetoric is essential to reading any text that argues, moves, or seduces through language.

## you gotta know

- Ancient rhetoric emerged from the Greek courts and assemblies, where speakers needed to persuade juries or citizens without access to documents; it became formalised as a discipline by *Aristotle* and Roman teachers like *Cicero*.
- Rhetorical devices include tropes (metaphor, irony, metonymy) that shape how language means, and schemes (parallelism, repetition, antithesis) that shape its sound and rhythm; both work together to persuade.
- The three appeals Aristotle identified—*ethos* (credibility), *pathos* (emotion), and *logos* (reason)—remain the foundation of persuasion; effective rhetoric typically combines all three.
- In literature, rhetoric is both a technical tool and a subject of suspicion: eloquence can tell truth or lie; a character's eloquence may seduce readers as it seduces fictional audiences, raising questions about language and deception.
- Shakespeare's *Antony* in *Julius Caesar* is the great dramatic example of rhetoric's power; his funeral oration transforms public opinion through calculated word-choice and emotional manipulation.
- Rhetoric was traditionally contrasted with *dialectic* (logical debate between equals) by its capacity to address crowds and sway emotion; this distinction has been complicated by modern theory.
- Modern rhetoric extends beyond oratory to all communication: advertising, propaganda, political speech, and literature itself are all rhetorical, employing language to convince and move.
