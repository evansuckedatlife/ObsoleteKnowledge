## summary

A **locked-room mystery** is a detective fiction subgenre centered on an apparently impossible crime: a murder committed in a space that is sealed, guarded, or inaccessible, with no obvious means of escape for the perpetrator. The form emerged as a pure puzzle, challenging the detective—and reader—to reconcile the irrefutable fact of the crime with the apparent impossibility of its commission. Pioneered by [[edgar-allan-poe]] and perfected by [[agatha-christie]] and other Golden Age writers, the locked-room mystery became a hallmark of intellectual detective fiction, prizing logic and misdirection over action or psychology.

## you gotta know

- [[edgar-allan-poe]]'s "The Murders in the Rue Morgue" (1841) effectively invented the form, featuring a seemingly impossible double murder in a room the police believe no human could have entered or escaped from.
- The "impossible crime" is resolved through reason and lateral thinking—the detective realizes the killer had access the police never imagined, or that assumptions about the crime were false.
- [[agatha-christie]]'s *The Murder of Roger Ackroyd* became a canonical example, with the locked study of the victim becoming the novel's central puzzle; the solution hinges on narrative reliability rather than physical impossibility.
- The form appeals to the puzzle-logic side of detective fiction: readers are given clues and can theoretically solve the mystery themselves if they think hard enough.
- Locked-room mysteries often depend on misdirection—the reader's and detective's focus is drawn to impossible means of entry, when the solution lies in redefining the crime itself or the criminal's relationship to the space.

## connections

- [[detective-fiction]] — the broader tradition within which locked-room mysteries operate as a specialized subgenre.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — inventor of the form, using the locked room as a vehicle for pure deduction.
- [[agatha-christie]] — perfected the form; her locked-room mysteries remain unsurpassed in ingenuity and fair play.
- [[the-murders-in-the-rue-morgue]] — the foundational text establishing the impossible crime as a literary puzzle.
- [[the-murder-of-roger-ackroyd]] — the modernist transformation of the locked-room into a narrative puzzle, not merely a physical one.
- [[mystery]] — the larger category; locked-room mysteries are a specific intensification of the mystery's central appeal: how to solve the unsolvable.
