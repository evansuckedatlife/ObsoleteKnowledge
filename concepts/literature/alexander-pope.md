# Alexander Pope

## summary

**Alexander Pope** (1688–1744) was an English poet and translator whose career spanned the transition from the Restoration to the Enlightenment. Despite physical disability from a childhood illness, he became one of England's greatest poets and the most influential literary figure of the early 18th century. His wit, technical mastery of the heroic couplet, and biting satire made him both beloved and feared. His translations of *Homer*'s *Iliad* and *Odyssey* shaped how generations of English readers encountered classical literature, while his original works—especially *The Rape of the Lock* and *An Essay on Criticism*—remain models of versification and satirical genius.

## you gotta know

- Pope's *Translation of Homer's Iliad* (1715–20) was a monumental achievement and a commercial success; it became the standard English Homer for over a century and made Pope's reputation and fortune.
- *The Rape of the Lock* (1712) is a mock-heroic poem satirizing the triviality of aristocratic life; a trivial incident (the cutting of a lock of hair) is treated with the gravity of Homeric warfare, revealing the shallow vanity of high society through the poem's elaborate, precise language.
- The *heroic couplet*—two rhyming lines of iambic pentameter—was Pope's medium; he perfected the form and demonstrated its capacity for both elevated expression and cutting satire.
- *An Essay on Criticism* (1711) is a didactic poem on the principles of literary judgment; it contains several famous aphorisms ("True ease in writing comes from art, not chance") and influenced how generations understood literary taste.
- Pope was a *master of satire*: *The Dunciad* (1728–43) savagely mocks bad poets and critics, creating an mock-epic about literary dunces; the poem's ferocity and precision make it one of literature's great satires.
- His disability (a severe spinal curvature from tuberculosis of the spine) made him 4'6" tall and reclusive; this physical reality informed both his psychological sensitivity and his acerbic wit.
