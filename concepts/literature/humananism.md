## summary

**Humananism** (widely designated as **humanism**, and specifically *Renaissance humanism*) was the transformative cultural and intellectual movement originating in fourteenth-century [[italy]] that drove the revival of classical antiquity. Centered on the *studia humanitatis*—grammar, rhetoric, history, [[poetry]], and moral philosophy—the movement shifted European scholarly focus away from medieval scholastic abstractions toward human potential, civic virtue, and philological accuracy. By recovering and studying ancient Greek and Roman literature, it sparked the wider European [[renaissance]] and radically reshaped education, statecraft, and vernacular arts across [[england]] and the continent.

## you gotta know

- Instituted the *studia humanitatis*, a five-subject educational curriculum encompassing grammar, rhetoric, history, [[poetry]], and moral philosophy designed to cultivate eloquent, virtuous citizens capable of active civic leadership.
- Founded conceptually by fourteenth-century Italian poet and scholar *Petrarch*, recognized as the father of humanism, who recovered long-forgotten classical Latin manuscripts—including *Cicero*'s letters—and spurred a pan-European hunt for ancient texts.
- Championed the scholarly imperative *ad fontes* ("to the sources"), demanding direct critical study of original Greek, Latin, and Hebrew manuscripts rather than reliance on corrupted medieval commentaries.
- Fostered Northern Christian humanism through scholars such as *Desiderius Erasmus*, whose 1516 bilingual Greek New Testament exposed inaccuracies in the Latin Vulgate and laid theological groundwork for church reform.
- Articulated the core philosophy of human dignity and intellectual freedom in *Giovanni Pico della Mirandola*'s 1486 *Oration on the Dignity of Man*, widely celebrated as the manifesto of the Renaissance.
- Restored classical Roman writers such as [[horace]], *Virgil*, and *Cicero* as supreme stylistic models, leading vernacular writers to adopt classical meters, epistolary forms, and satiric modes.
- Revolutionized English Renaissance drama and literature by reshaping grammar-school educations, instilling the rhetorical versatility and classical literacy evident in the works of [[william-shakespeare]].

## connections

- [[renaissance]] — supplied the central philosophical and educational framework for the rebirth of European literature and art.
- [[ancient-greece]] — provided the foundational philosophical treatises, lyric forms, and dramatic texts translated anew by humanist scholars.
- [[rome]] — furnished the primary rhetorical standards, Latin linguistic ideals, and republican civic virtues revered by humanists.
- [[horace]] — was celebrated as an essential classical authority on poetic craftsmanship, lyrical elegance, and moral moderation.
- [[iconic-locations]] — imbued classical topoi, archaeological ruins, and humanist academies with symbolic meaning as spaces of wisdom.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — inherited the humanist curriculum's rhetorical training during his education, shaping his dramatic poetry.
- [[enlightenment]] — emerged out of humanist commitments to secular inquiry, empiricism, and critical textual analysis.
