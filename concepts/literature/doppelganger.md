## summary

**doppelganger**, originating from the German for "double-goer," is a literary archetype representing a paranormal or psychological twin, shadow-self, or uncanny alter ego. Prominent across European folklore, *Romanticism*, and *Gothic literature*, the motif dramatizes the fracture of human identity and the eruption of repressed desires. Encountering one's counterpart traditionally functions as an omen of madness, moral disintegration, or impending mortality.

## you gotta know

- Coined in German by the Romantic novelist *Jean Paul* in his 1796 novel *Siebenkäs*, where it designated a person who sees their own phantom twin.
- Formulated psychoanalytically by *Sigmund Freud* in his 1919 essay *The Uncanny* (*Das Unheimliche*), tracing the figure to repressed narcissism and ego splitting.
- Developed in German Romanticism by *E. T. A. Hoffmann* in *The Devil's Elixirs* and tales featuring eerie automata and disassociated personalities.
- Featured centrally in *Edgar Allan Poe*'s 1839 tale *William Wilson*, in which an unprincipled narrator is stalked by a moralistic namesake double.
- Adapted into psychological realism by *Fyodor Dostoevsky* in his 1846 novella *The Double*, chronicling the bureaucrat *Yakov Petrovich Golyadkin*'s ruin by a sinister replica.
- Manifested in late Victorian horror through *Robert Louis Stevenson*'s *Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde* and *Oscar Wilde*'s *The Picture of Dorian Gray*.
- Explored in modern cinema and literature as a manifestation of paranoia, authoritarian estrangement, and fractured conscience.

## connections

- [[gothic-literature]] — horror tradition that popularized spectral twins, haunted houses, and uncanny omens.
- [[romanticism]] — literary epoch whose fascination with the irrational and the subconscious birthed the term.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — master of the macabre who codified the rivalrous double in American prose.
- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — author who transplanted the spectral double into existential and bureaucratic madness.
- [[dmitri-karamazov]] — passionate protagonist whose moral fractures and shadow relationships reflect Dostoevskian duality.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — dramatist who explored twins, mistaken identities, and shadow selves in comedies and tragedies.
- [[realism]] — movement that rationalized the supernatural double into internal psychological pathology.
