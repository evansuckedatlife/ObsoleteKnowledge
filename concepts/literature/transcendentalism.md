## summary

**Transcendentalism** was an American philosophical and literary movement of the 1830s–1850s centered in New England, championing individual intuition, nature as spiritual authority, and the transcendent potential of the human soul. Thinkers like *Ralph Waldo Emerson* and *Henry David Thoreau* rejected formal religion and empirical rationalism, arguing that truth is accessed through direct experience and inner vision. The movement reshaped American letters and moral thought, anchoring the American Renaissance.

## you gotta know

- Emerged in Concord, Massachusetts, around *Emerson's* circle; members included *Thoreau*, *Bronson Alcott*, and *Margaret Fuller*; the movement was deliberately anti-institutional and utopian.
- Rooted in reaction against Calvinist Protestantism and Enlightenment materialism; drew from Romanticism (especially German idealism and Coleridge), Platonism, and Eastern philosophy.
- Central doctrine: the individual soul has direct access to divine truth through intuition ("the Over-Soul"), bypassing clergy, scripture, and rationalist intermediaries; this is transcendence.
- Nature is the primary text: *Emerson*'s "Nature" (1836) argued that the natural world is a language of divine ideas; Thoreau's *Walden* (1854) practiced this through deliberate retreat to a cabin on Walden Pond.
- Radical individualism: *Emerson's* "Self-Reliance" (1841) made nonconformity a moral virtue; *Thoreau's* "Civil Disobedience" (1849) justified resistance to unjust government through personal conscience.
- Optimism about human potential: humans are divine beings capable of self-transformation; social reform and radical living (communal experiments like Brook Farm) were moral imperatives.
- Literary influence: *Walt Whitman's* "Leaves of Grass" is transcendentalism in verse—the celebration of the individual body and democratic self as sacred and ecstatic.

## connections

- [[ralph-waldo-emerson]] — the movement's philosopher and moral founder; "Self-Reliance" and "Nature" are canonical texts.
- [[henry-david-thoreau]] — the radical practitioner; *Walden* and "Civil Disobedience" translate transcendentalist theory into lived practice.
- [[walt-whitman]] — the transcendentalist poet; *Leaves of Grass* is the democratic, ecstatic embodiment of the movement's vision.
- [[american-renaissance]] — transcendentalism is the intellectual and spiritual heart of this period's literary flowering.
- [[american-literature]] — foundational to American identity, individualism, and moral imagination.
- [[romanticism]] — transcendentalism is the American branch of the larger Romantic reaction against rationalism and industrial modernity.
- [[individualism]] — the transcendentalist principle of the sovereign self; the basis of American democratic ideals.
