## summary

**Ashikaga Yoshimitsu** was the third *shōgun* of the Ashikaga shogunate, ruling medieval Japan during the late fourteenth century and inaugurating the brilliant cultural era known as *Kitayama culture*. He is celebrated in literary and theatrical history as the decisive patron who elevated *sarugaku* actor-playwrights *Kan'ami* and his son *Zeami*, granting them aristocratic protection and court legitimacy. Through Yoshimitsu's passionate cultural sponsorship, their performances were transformed from popular folk entertainments into the high classical art of *Noh* drama and linked-verse poetry.

## you gotta know

- Attended a watershed performance of *sarugaku* at Kyoto's *Imakumano* Shrine in 1374, where he was immediately enraptured by the theatrical virtuosity of *Kan'ami* and the delicate beauty of his young son *Zeami*.
- Took the twelve-year-old *Zeami* into his inner court as an artistic protégé and aristocratic companion, scandalizing conservative court nobles like *Sanjō Kintada*, who complained bitterly in his diary that a base performer was permitted to share the ruler's carriage and banqueting mat.
- Provided the generous financial patronage and refined aristocratic milieu that allowed *Zeami* to formulate the aesthetic theories of *yūgen* (mysterious depth and elegance) and compose foundational dramatic treatises like the *Fūshikaden*.
- Built the celebrated Golden Pavilion (*Kinkaku-ji*) at his *Kitayama* estate outside Kyoto, creating an architectural masterpiece that fused noble palace styling with Zen Buddhist design to become the enduring emblem of Muromachi artistic sophistication.
- Ended the protracted fifty-six-year civil war between the Northern and Southern Courts (*Nanboku-chō*) in 1392 by brokering an agreement with Southern Emperor *Go-Kameyama*, successfully uniting the imperial line and sacred regalia in Kyoto under shogunal control.
- Re-established official tribute trade and diplomatic ties with Ming dynasty China, accepting the title "King of Japan" (*Nihon kokuō*) from the Chinese emperor in 1404 to secure lucrative trade tallies and monopolize continental commerce.
- Presided over a renowned literary salon that institutionalized linked-verse poetry (*renga*), tea gatherings, and Zen scholarship, heavily promoting the Five Mountain System (*Gozan*) of Rinzai Zen monasteries as engines of high culture.
- Abdicated the office of *shōgun* to his nine-year-old son *Ashikaga Yoshimochi* in 1394, yet retained supreme autocratic authority over state affairs, diplomacy, and artistic life as retired leader until his death in 1408.

## connections

- [[zeami]] — the actor, playwright, and aesthetician he took as a court companion and patronized, establishing Noh drama.
- [[noh]] — the masked Japanese theatrical tradition he transformed from rustic entertainment into elite aristocratic drama.
- [[kanami]] — the innovative theatre leader whose 1374 performance at Imakumano Shrine won his lifelong patronage.
- [[japan]] — the country he ruled as third Muromachi shōgun, presiding over the Kitayama cultural renaissance.
- [[china]] — the continental empire with which he reopened maritime trade, accepting investiture as King of Japan.
- [[poetry]] — the art of linked-verse renga that flourished at his salon alongside tea culture and Zen scholarship.
- [[tragedy]] — the dramatic tradition of ghostly longing, tragic warfare, and pathos central to classic Noh plays.
- [[satire]] — the comedic kyōgen interludes that developed in tandem with serious Noh plays during his reign.
