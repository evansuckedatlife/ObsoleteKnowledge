## summary

**Molière**, the stage name of **Jean-Baptiste Poquelin**, was a seventeenth-century French playwright, actor, and poet who is widely celebrated as one of the greatest masters of European comedy. Working under the royal patronage of King *Louis XIV* during the classical age of *[[france]]*, he transformed Western theater by synthesizing physical *[[farce]]* with sophisticated, literate character comedy. His incisive dramatic works exposed religious hypocrisy, social climbing, medical charlatanism, and aristocratic vices, establishing comedy as an indispensable vehicle for moral reflection and social *[[satire]]*.

## you gotta know

- Authored the incendiary comedy *Tartuffe, or The Impostor*, a devastating assault on religious sanctimony and moral fraudulence that provoked fury among devout Catholic courtiers and faced repeated public performance bans by King *Louis XIV*.
- Collapsed on stage while coughing violently during his fourth performance playing the title hypochondriac in *The Imaginary Invalid* (*Le Malade imaginaire*), dying hours later at home; because the Church denied unrepentant actors Christian burial, his widow required special royal dispensation for a nocturnal funeral.
- Composed *The Misanthrope* (*Le Misanthrope*), widely considered his crowning psychological comedy, which follows the uncompromising protagonist *Alceste* as he fiercely condemns the hollow flatteries and hypocritical manners of Parisian high society while remaining hopelessly enamored of the frivolous gossip *Célimène*.
- Skewered obsessive avarice and domestic tyranny in *The Miser* (*L'Avare*), whose central skinflint *Harpagon* values a buried cash-box above his own children and suspects everyone, even frantically grabbing his own left hand with his right hand, when the gold vanishes.
- Ridiculed social climbing and bourgeois pretension in *The Bourgeois Gentleman* (*Le Bourgeois Gentilhomme*), an enduring comedy-ballet immortalizing the naive merchant *Monsieur Jourdain*, who rejoices upon learning from a philosophy tutor that he has been speaking prose his entire life without knowing it.
- Provided the artistic bedrock, original acting company, and core repertoire for the prestigious *Comédie-Française*, the royal French national theater established shortly after his death, which is still celebrated across the globe as "The House of Molière."
- Spent over a decade touring the provinces with a traveling troupe after his first commercial venture, the *Illustre Théâtre*, collapsed into bankruptcy and landed him in debtors' prison, using those itinerant years to assimilate the slapstick improvisation and masks of Italian *[[commedia-dell-arte]]*.
- Pioneered the theatrical *comédie-ballet* genre in close collaboration with royal court composer *Jean-Baptiste Lully*, weaving spoken drama together with musical interludes, chorus numbers, and choreographed dances to entertain the royal court at Versailles and Chambord.

## connections

- [[satire]] — his primary dramatic mode, used to expose societal vices, institutional hypocrisy, and human folly.
- [[france]] — the kingdom in which he lived and worked under the royal patronage of King *Louis XIV*.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — his English contemporary counterpart in canonical theatrical stature and practical actor-manager experience.
- [[farce]] — the lowbrow, slapstick comedic tradition that he elevated into sophisticated psychological drama.
- [[christianity]] — the religion whose devout authorities, notably the *Compagnie du Saint-Sacrement*, attacked his depictions of pious hypocrisy.
- [[opera]] — the emerging musical art form intertwined with his development of elaborate court *comédies-ballets*.
- [[enlightenment]] — the subsequent philosophical era that championed his rationalist skepticism, anti-clerical humor, and critique of social pretension.
