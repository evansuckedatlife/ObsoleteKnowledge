## summary

**Tiresias** is a blind prophet in ancient Greek mythology and literature, most famous for his roles in *Sophocles's Oedipus Rex* and *Homer's Odyssey*. Punished with blindness by *Athena*, he paradoxically gained the gift of prophecy and lived for seven generations, accumulating wisdom across centuries. In dramatic literature, Tiresias serves as the voice of truth that mortals refuse to hear, embodying the tragic irony that knowledge and authority often fail to prevent catastrophe.

## you gotta know

- Blinded by *Athena* (accounts vary: for seeing her naked, for striking her serpents, or for taking Zeus's side in a dispute between Zeus and Hera).
- Compensated with prophetic power and extreme longevity; lived through multiple human generations, making him an archival witness to divine and human history.
- In *Oedipus Rex*, Tiresias is summoned to reveal the truth about *Oedipus's* parentage and guilt; his reluctance and eventual revelation drive the play's tragic recognition.
- *Oedipus* accuses Tiresias of complicity in his suffering; Tiresias responds with controlled anger, speaking truth that Oedipus refuses initially to accept.
- In *The Odyssey*, *Odysseus* consults Tiresias in the underworld for guidance on his homecoming; the prophet's knowledge spans past, present, and future.
- Symbolizes the paradox of wisdom: sight and blindness are inverted; true vision requires loss of physical sight; truth is often unwelcome to the powerful.
- According to some myths, Tiresias lived as both a man and a woman at different points in his life, giving him unique insight into gender and desire.
- His prophecies are always accurate, yet they are frequently rejected or misinterpreted by those who hear them; his credibility is established only after disaster confirms his words.
- The blind prophet appears frequently across Greek literature because he represents the archetype of the marginalized truth-teller whose authority derives from supernatural knowledge.
- In modern interpretations, Tiresias embodies the archetype of the outsider who sees what society refuses to acknowledge; he is valued only posthumously.
