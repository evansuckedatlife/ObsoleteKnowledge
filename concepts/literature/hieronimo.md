## summary

**Hieronimo** is the grief-stricken protagonist of *The Spanish Tragedy*, the groundbreaking Elizabethan drama written by *Thomas Kyd* in the late 1580s. Serving as the Knight Marshal of [[spain]], he is plunged into madness and moral turmoil after discovering the murdered body of his son *Horatio* hanging in his family arbor. His agonizing quest for justice in a corrupt court defined the conventions of early modern [[revenge-tragedy]], profoundly shaping the development of Renaissance theater in [[england]] and providing the primary dramaturgical blueprint for *William Shakespeare's* [[hamlet]]. Through his profound psychological unraveling, the character established theatrical precedents for grief, simulated lunacy, and the catastrophe of private retribution.

## you gotta know

- Protagonist of *The Spanish Tragedy* by *Thomas Kyd*, which became the defining theatrical sensation and foundational commercial hit of the [[english-renaissance]].
- Discovers the corpse of his murdered son *Horatio* suspended in a garden bower, sparking an obsessive quest for vengeance that consumes his remaining sanity.
- Holds the prestigious legal office of Knight Marshal, creating a central thematic paradox where the judge responsible for crown justice cannot obtain legal redress.
- Feigns lunacy as a protective subterfuge while genuinely suffering acute psychological unraveling, pioneering the theatrical trope of simulated madness later refined in [[hamlet]].
- Delivers celebrated soliloquies lamenting cosmic injustice, including the renowned speech beginning "O eyes! no eyes, but fountains fraught with tears," which became widely parodied by Elizabethan rivals.
- Receives supernatural encouragement from the ghost of *Don Andrea* and the personified figure of *Revenge*, who frame the entire dramatic action as an underworld pageant.
- Orchestrates an ingenious revenge plot by staging a multilingual court play entitled *Soliman and Perseda*, using actual daggers to slaughter the murderers during performance.
- Defies royal interrogation after the massacre by biting out his own tongue, subsequently killing the Duke of Castile and himself with a concealed penknife.

## connections

- [[revenge-tragedy]] — theatrical genre marked by ghosts, sensational violence, and delayed private vengeance, established through his dramatic arc.
- [[english-renaissance]] — golden age of British playwriting and public theater in Elizabethan [[england]] that embraced his violent tale.
- [[hamlet]] — Shakespearean tragedy that directly inherited his dynamic of feigned madness, melancholic delay, and a climactic play-within-a-play.
- [[spain]] — setting of the play, whose Catholic court provided Elizabethan audiences with an exotic atmosphere of intrigue, corruption, and tyranny.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — preeminent dramatist who drew heavily upon the dramaturgical techniques and psychological models introduced by his story.
- [[tragedy]] — classical dramatic tradition revitalized on the early modern popular stage through bloody Senecan violence and moral catastrophe.
- [[t-s-eliot]] — twentieth-century modernist poet who quoted his famous mad refrain ("Hieronymo's mad againe") in the concluding lines of *The Waste Land*.
- [[hero]] — dramatic archetype he inhabits and ultimately subverts, transitioning from an upright legal officer into an obsessive revenger.
