## summary

**Damnation**, also known as **eternal damnation** or **perdition**, is the theological and literary concept of irrevocable condemnation to eternal punishment and separation from God in hell. Originating in religious eschatology, particularly within [[christianity]], the dread of ultimate spiritual doom served as a central animating crisis in Western literature from medieval morality plays to modern drama. It functions not merely as an afterlife destination, but as an existential and psychological threshold where human hubris, transgression, and despair collide with absolute cosmic judgment.

## you gotta know

- *Dante Alighieri*'s *Inferno* presents the definitive poetic cartography of the damned, categorizing sinners across nine concentric circles governed by *contrapasso*, where punishment symbolically mirrors the sin.
- *Christopher Marlowe*'s *Doctor Faustus* dramatizes the psychological agony of imminent doom, culminating in Faustus's desperate final soliloquy as the clock strikes midnight and devils arrive to claim his soul.
- *John Milton*'s *Paradise Lost* reimagines the state of the condemned through *Satan*, whose realization that "myself am Hell" transforms external punishment into an internal, inescapable psychological torment.
- *William Shakespeare*'s [[macbeth]] depicts the creeping terror of spiritual perdition, as the regicide fears he has surrendered his "eternal jewel" to the common enemy of man for a temporary earthly crown.
- In [[revenge-tragedy]], as seen in *The Revenger's Tragedy* (frequently attributed to [[cyril-tourneur]]), protagonists risk eternal judgment by usurping divine vengeance, orchestrating gruesome retribution that seals their own spiritual doom.
- *Jean-Paul Sartre*'s mid-century play *No Exit* radically secularizes the concept within [[existentialism]], famously concluding that "hell is other people" as three damned souls torment one another without physical torture.
- The Calvinist theology of double predestination profoundly unsettled Early Modern English drama, generating intense anxiety over whether an individual was irreversibly reprobate before birth.
- Medieval mystery and morality cycles popularized the dramatic "Harrowing of Hell" and the terrifying gaping mouth of the Hellmouth prop, designed to physically consume sinful characters before the spectators.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — provides the theological doctrine, scriptural foundation, and eschatological framework of final judgment and hellfire.
- [[tragedy]] — hinges upon characters whose fatal choices, pride, or moral transgressions precipitate catastrophic and unredeemable ruin.
- [[revenge-tragedy]] — explores the spiritual hazard of blood vengeance, where seeking retribution guarantees the revenger's moral damnation.
- [[cyril-tourneur]] — dramatizes corrupt courts and macabre vengeance where villains and avengers alike hurtle toward spiritual annihilation.
- [[macbeth]] — showcases a tragic hero acutely conscious that murdering an anointed king forfeits his eternal salvation.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — psychological horror master whose tales explore acute guilt, perversity, and the claustrophobia of damnation.
- [[existentialism]] — reinterprets theological perdition as the agonizing consequence of bad faith, moral abandonment, and interpersonal alienation.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — repeatedly dramatizes characters like *Claudius* in *Hamlet* wrestling with unrepentant guilt and the impossibility of prayer.
