## summary

**Golyadkin**, fully named Yakov Petrovich Golyadkin, is the titular protagonist of Fyodor Dostoevsky's early 1846 novella *The Double*. A meek and socially insecure titular councillor in Saint Petersburg, he suffers a catastrophic psychological unraveling following the appearance of a scheming, physically identical rival bearing his exact name. He stands as the foundational prototype for Dostoevsky's long lineage of humiliated, hyper-conscious, and socially alienated antiheroes.

## you gotta know

- Occupies the ninth rank of the imperial Table of Ranks as a humble titular councillor working within the sprawling civil bureaucracy of Tsarist Saint Petersburg.
- Visits his personal physician, Dr. Krestyan Ivanovich Rutenspitz, in the opening chapters, confessing deep anxieties about social intrigue and asserting his preference for quiet, unassuming integrity.
- Crashes the lavish birthday ball of Klara Olsufyevna, daughter of his civil patron Berendeyev, only to suffer public humiliation before being expelled into the cold midnight rain.
- Encounters his terrifying duplicate while walking home along the Fontanka canal across an ominous, stormy Saint Petersburg night.
- Discovers the next morning that his physical double, dubbed "Golyadkin Junior," has been hired at his exact administrative office and sits directly opposite him.
- Contrasts sharply with Golyadkin Junior, who is smooth, obsequious, manipulative, and relentlessly successful in usurping the original Golyadkin's social ties and standing.
- Descends into acute paranoia, delusion, and delirium as he attempts to confront his nemesis, ultimately being bundled into a carriage by Dr. Rutenspitz and carted away to an asylum.

## connections

- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — the Russian master who created the character and later considered the psychological concept of the double his finest artistic contribution.
- [[realism]] — the nineteenth-century movement depicting social class and civil servant misery, though Dostoevsky infuses it with grotesque psychological reality.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the stylistic and thematic source for the uncanny *doppelgänger* motif popularized by German writers like E. T. A. Hoffmann.
- [[satire]] — the dark comedic critique directed at the stifling, sycophantic hierarchy of the Tsarist bureaucracy.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical tradition that later claimed Dostoevsky's fragmented, alienation-racked protagonists as early literary precursors.
- [[soviet-union]] — the Russian geographic and administrative heartland whose bureaucratic legacy traced directly back to Imperial Saint Petersburg.
