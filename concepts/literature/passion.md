## summary

**Passion** is an overwhelming, irrational affective and erotic force in literature that subordinates human reason, moral strictures, and social order to uncontrollable impulse. From the catastrophic frenzy of classical [[tragedy]] to the psychological crises of 19th-century [[realism]], it serves as the primary engine driving characters beyond the boundaries of civic law and domestic peace. Writers across traditions explore passion as a destabilizing intensity that institutions such as marriage cannot satisfy or contain, making it a foundational catalyst for catastrophic violence, moral ruin, and tragic transfiguration.

## you gotta know

- Exemplified in [[william-shakespeare]]'s drama [[othello]], where intense romantic devotion is twisted by *Iago* into murderous [[jealousy]], showing how erotic obsession dismantles a noble general's reason and drives him to smother *Desdemona*.
- Dissected ruthlessly in [[the-kreutzer-sonata]] by [[leo-tolstoy]], whose protagonist *Pozdnyshev* confesses that carnal passion is a humiliating, animalistic frenzy that marital convention cannot domesticate, terminating inevitably in domestic homicide.
- Conceptualized in classical [[ancient-greece]] drama by playwrights such as [[sophocles]] and *Euripides* as a terrifying divine madness that obliterates civic moderation, famously dramatized in Euripides's *Medea* and *Jean Racine*'s neoclassical tragedy *Phèdre*.
- Reimagined during the [[romanticism]] era as the supreme manifestation of personal authenticity and creative genius, where writers like *Lord Byron* and *Johann Wolfgang von Goethe* celebrated radical emotional fervor over the detached rationalism of the [[enlightenment]].
- Depicted across 19th-century [[realism]] as a ruinous social transgression, where trapped heroines like *Gustave Flaubert*'s *Emma Bovary* and Tolstoy's *Anna Karenina* pursue adulterous ecstasy in defiance of bourgeois society, resulting in financial ruin, social ostracism, and suicide.
- Portrayed in [[fyodor-dostoevsky]]'s psychological masterpieces as a feverish, destructive spiritual tempest, epitomized by *Parfyon Rogozhin*'s lethal, chaotic obsession with *Nastasya Filippovna* in *The Idiot* and *Dmitri Karamazov*'s sensual storm in *The Brothers Karamazov*.
- Contrasted in Renaissance and early modern philosophy with the governance of the soul, where passions were understood as violent somatic perturbations that must be subdued, establishing the dramatic engine of [[english-renaissance]] [[revenge-tragedy]].

## connections

- [[othello]] — the Shakespearean tragedy in which passionate love is poisoned into paranoid jealousy, leading to domestic slaughter.
- [[the-kreutzer-sonata]] — Tolstoy's controversial novella arguing that sensual passion is an uncontrollable animal drive that marriage fails to tame.
- [[tragedy]] — the dramatic tradition that pivots on the fatal conflict between uncontrollable emotional passions and moral or cosmic order.
- [[leo-tolstoy]] — the Russian novelist who recurrently condemned carnal passion as an illusory, destructive force hostile to genuine spiritual peace.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the dramatist whose tragedies repeatedly demonstrate the collapse of political authority and sanity when passion overmasters intellect.
- [[romanticism]] — the literary movement that elevated intense emotional experience and passionate rebellion above classical restraint and rational calculation.
- [[desire]] — the appetitive physical and emotional urge that generates passion and resists the regulatory constraints of social convention.
