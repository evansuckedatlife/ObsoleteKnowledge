## summary

**Charlotte Perkins Gilman**, born **Charlotte Anna Perkins**, was a groundbreaking American feminist author, sociologist, and social reformer whose writings critiqued the domestic subjugation of women in the [[united-states]]. She is internationally renowned for her semi-autobiographical 1892 short story *The Yellow Wallpaper*, a chilling fusion of psychological [[realism]] and [[gothic-literature]] that attacks the medical establishment's silencing of female intellectual autonomy. Her theoretical texts, most notably *Women and Economics*, established her as a major intellectual voice demanding economic independence for women.

## you gotta know

- Authored *The Yellow Wallpaper*, chronicling an unnamed narrator's descent into madness while subjected to the infantilizing "rest cure" enforced by her physician husband.
- Wrote the story following her own harrowing encounter with the actual rest cure prescribed by the renowned Philadelphia neurologist *Silas Weir Mitchell*.
- Published the landmark 1898 treatise *Women and Economics*, arguing that the economic dependence of women on men warped human evolution and natural social progress.
- Advocated for radical domestic reorganization, envisioning apartment complexes with collective professional kitchens and communal daycares to liberate women from domestic drudgery.
- Penned the 1915 feminist utopian novel *Herland*, describing an isolated, egalitarian society composed entirely of parthenogenically reproducing women free of conflict and poverty.
- Founded, wrote, and single-handedly edited the monthly progressive journal *The Forerunner* from 1909 to 1916 to disseminate her socio-political ideas.
- Related distantly to prominent abolitionists and writers through the Beecher family, including *Harriet Beecher Stowe*.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — occupies a foundational position in nineteenth-century American feminist prose, psychological horror, and utopian fiction.
- [[gothic-literature]] — deployed uncanny haunted house motifs and suffocating domestic confines to articulate psychological terror.
- [[realism]] — documented the suffocating day-to-day realities of middle-class domestic confinement and medical condescension.
- [[united-states]] — challenged late-nineteenth-century American cultural norms that limited women to the domestic sphere.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — adapted the tradition of psychological claustrophobia and decaying interior spaces to expose gendered oppression.
- [[child-trauma]] — exposed the devastating psychological damage caused by the medical and domestic infantilization of women.
- [[cecily-cardew]] — offers an ideological counterpoint to late-Victorian drawing-room expectations regarding feminine innocence and domesticity.
