## summary

**Regicide** is the act of killing a king or queen, and the political and moral transgression it represents. In medieval and early modern thought, a monarch was divinely appointed, making regicide not merely murder but sacrilege—a violation of cosmic order. Yet regicide is also the ultimate political act, the violent seizure of power by those excluded from succession; it appears throughout literature as both crime and rebellion, and becomes a obsessive theme in drama, where it tests the boundaries between justice and tyranny, ambition and righteousness.

## you gotta know

- In medieval and Renaissance political theory, the king's two bodies (the natural body and the body politic) meant that regicide was understood as both murder and treason against the state itself.
- Shakespeare's *Macbeth* and *Hamlet* make regicide their moral centre: both plays explore whether killing a king can ever be justified, and both characters are haunted by the act's violation of natural order.
- Regicide appears in *Macbeth* as the crossing of a threshold that cannot be uncrossed; murdering *Duncan* to seize the throne produces only further violence and madness, suggesting the act carries its own punishment.
- In *Hamlet*, the murder of King *Claudius* is presented as unavoidable revenge yet also as a violation that taints the avenger; *Hamlet's* hesitation reflects deeper philosophical uncertainty about the legitimacy of killing a king.
- Historically, regicide became a political reality during the English Civil War (the execution of *Charles I*) and French Revolution; literature's obsession with it reflects real political upheaval.
- Regicide triggers questions about legitimate authority: Is a tyrant truly a king? Can his murder be justified as removing a usurper rather than killing the rightful sovereign?
- The psychological weight regicide carries in literature exceeds ordinary murder; the killer is often destroyed by guilt, madness, or nemesis, as if the act itself carries unavoidable consequences.

## connections

- [[macbeth]] — a play centred on the murderer's descent into madness and tyranny following regicide.
- [[hamlet]] — a play in which regicide is the hidden crime at the heart of the action, and revenge is the driver of plot.
- [[monarchy]] — regicide is only possible within monarchical systems; it challenges the legitimacy of divine right.
- [[power]] — regicide is the ultimate seizure of power; it asks who has the right to rule and how that right is established.
- [[ambition]] — the drive that leads to regicide in both *Macbeth* and historical record.
