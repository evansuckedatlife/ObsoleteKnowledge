## summary

A **psychological thriller** is a narrative work—in literature, film, or television—that generates dread through the protagonist's internal conflict, unreliable perception, or complicity in criminal or disturbing acts. Rather than external physical danger, the threat emanates from the human psyche itself: paranoia, deception, madness, or moral corruption. The form bridges literature and cinema, making it a paradigmatic example of how storytelling techniques migrate across media.

## you gotta know

- The psychological thriller prioritizes the subjective experience of the protagonist over objective plot events, making the audience complicit in their distorted perspective.
- Unreliable narration is a defining technique: the reader or viewer cannot trust what they are told, creating sustained ambiguity about what is real or fabricated.
- *Fight Club* exemplifies the form's intersection with postmodern narrative structure—the twist reveals that the narrator has been unreliably withholding crucial information from both audience and himself.
- *The Sixth Sense* uses psychological realism to mask supernatural elements, making the viewer's own assumptions about reality the source of the thriller's power.
- *The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari* pioneered the visual vocabulary of psychological instability through German Expressionism's distorted angles and shadowed cinematography.
- The protagonist is often complicit in or responsible for the horror they experience, inverting the victim role of traditional thriller conventions.
- The form thrives on the viewer or reader's inability to distinguish between internal and external threat—madness becomes indistinguishable from reality.
