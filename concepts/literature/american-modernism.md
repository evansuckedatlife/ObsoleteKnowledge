## summary

**American modernism** is the literary movement that developed in the United States during the early-to-mid 20th century, corresponding to broader modernist experimentation in Europe while forging distinctive themes. American modernists embraced fragmented narrative, psychological depth, and formal innovation to capture the alienation and complexity of modern urban life. Writers like *Ralph Ellison* and *Saul Bellow* produced works grappling with identity, race, and belonging in postwar America.

## you gotta know

- Emerged after *World War I*, responding to industrialization, urbanization, and the perceived failure of traditional literary forms to capture modern experience.
- American modernists adopted formal techniques from European models (stream-of-consciousness, nonlinear narrative, interior monologue) while centering distinctly American concerns.
- Major themes include alienation, racial identity, immigrant experience, the search for meaning in a fragmented world, and the individual's struggle against mass society.
- *Ralph Ellison's* *Invisible Man* stands as a defining American modernist text: formally inventive and explicitly about the African American experience.
- *Saul Bellow* brought modernist sophistication to explorations of Jewish American identity and philosophical inquiry in urban America.
- The movement overlapped with the *Harlem Renaissance*, creating productive tensions between avant-garde form and cultural identity politics.
- Influenced by existentialism and psychoanalysis; inner consciousness and moral ambiguity often take precedence over plot.
- Distinguished from European modernism by its engagement with American social conditions: capitalism, racism, immigration, and the dream of democratic possibility.
- The movement produced enduring masterworks that reshaped world literature and established the American novel as a serious rival to European literary traditions.
