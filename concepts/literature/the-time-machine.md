## summary

***The Time Machine*** is *H.G. Wells's* pioneering science fiction novella about an unnamed scientist who invents a machine to travel through time. He journeys to the distant future and discovers humanity has evolved into two separate species: the delicate *Eloi*, who live in ease above ground, and the brutish *Morlocks*, who toil underground. The narrative explores themes of social class, technological progress, and the cyclical nature of human civilization through this vision of evolutionary divergence.

## you gotta know

- Published in 1895, it is arguably the first modern science fiction novel, establishing time travel as a narrative device and speculative tool.
- The Time Traveller moves far into the future, observing a world 800,000 years hence, where evolutionary forces have stratified society into two distinct humanoid species.
- The *Eloi* are intelligent but passive and dependent; the *Morlocks* are nocturnal cannibals who care for and harvest the *Eloi* like livestock—a bitter inversion of progress.
- The machine itself breaks down during the Traveller's stay, forcing him to understand his predicament and the bitter sociological realities he has encountered.
- Wells uses the journey as social critique: the novel's future reflects anxieties about Victorian class divisions widening into biological separation.
- The ending remains ambiguous; the Time Traveller's further journey into deep time witnesses the death of Earth under a dying red sun.
- The Traveller arrives as an observer at a dinner party and describes his findings; his narrative framing device allows Wells to explore speculation within a realistic setting.
- *Morlocks* maintain underground machinery and industrial systems, suggesting that working-class labor literally maintains the Eloi's existence—a dark commentary on Victorian economics.
- The novel engages with Darwinian evolution as both mechanism and social philosophy; biological adaptation becomes visible evidence of social hierarchy.
- *Wells* wrote extensively as both novelist and social theorist; *The Time Machine* exemplifies his use of fiction as a vehicle for social warning and philosophical inquiry.
