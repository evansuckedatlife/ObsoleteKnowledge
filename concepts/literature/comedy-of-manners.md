## summary

**Comedy of manners** is a dramatic genre that satirizes the behaviors, hypocrisies, and artificial social codes of an aristocratic or upper-middle-class society. Flourishing during the English Restoration and revitalized in the late nineteenth century, the form relies heavily on sparkling repartee, epigrammatic wit, and cynical intrigues of courtship and reputation. It remains a cornerstone of Western theatrical history for demonstrating how polished dialogue and social decorum can be deployed as ruthless instruments in elite power struggles.

## you gotta know

- *Oscar Wilde* brought the Victorian revival of the genre to its satirical peak with *The Importance of Being Earnest*, mocking aristocratic triviality through deadpan aphorisms and the fictional artifice of Bunburying.
- *William Congreve* defined late Restoration elegance with *The Way of the World*, famous for the sophisticated battle of wits and the contractual marriage negotiations between *Mirabell* and *Millamant*.
- *Richard Brinsley Sheridan* and *Oliver Goldsmith* led the eighteenth-century Georgian reaction against sentimental weeping comedies with masterpieces like *The School for Scandal* and *She Stoops to Conquer*.
- *William Wycherley* scandalized late seventeenth-century London with *The Country Wife*, using *Horner's* notorious feigned impotence and the bawdy China scene to lay bare the vanity and hypocrisy of polite society.
- *Molière* provided the essential continental archetype in seventeenth-century *France* through verse comedies like *The Misanthrope* and *Tartuffe*, puncturing bourgeois pretension, courtly flattery, and moral posturing.
- *George Etherege* inaugurated the quintessential Restoration rake archetype with the character of *Dorimant* in *The Man of Mode*, establishing the primacy of sartorial poise and ruthless amorous gaming.
- *Noël Coward* modernized the tradition between the world wars in sparkling domestic satires such as *Hay Fever* and *Private Lives*, transforming aristocratic drollery into brittle twentieth-century cocktail banter.

## connections

- [[satire]] — provides the critical comedic machinery used to unmask vanity, affected virtue, and conversational posturing in high society.
- [[england]] — the historical stage where the restored court of *Charles II* and later fashionable London salons cultivated the genre's distinct dramatic forms.
- [[france]] — origin of the seventeenth-century neoclassical prototypes created by *Molière* that directly shaped English playwrights returning from continental exile.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — earlier theatrical predecessor whose romantic comedies like *Much Ado About Nothing* pioneered rapid-fire gendered verbal duels.
- [[compression]] — stylistic principle governing the epigrammatic economy, pithy aphorisms, and highly concentrated verbal wit vital to the genre's dialogue.
- [[realism]] — late nineteenth-century movement that pushed back against artificial drawing-room conventions by dramatizing gritty social determinism over polished banter.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical era whose faith in rationalism, urbanity, and salon wit paralleled the satirical critiques mounted by Georgian stage comedies.
