## summary

**The House of the Seven Gables** is an 1851 novel by *Nathaniel Hawthorne* that chronicles the enduring moral legacy of seventeenth-century Puritan witchcraft persecutions in Salem, Massachusetts. The story follows the decaying aristocratic *Pyncheon* family, who inhabit an ominous, seven-gabled mansion tainted by a deadly generational curse. Blending dark psychological realism with New England folklore, the work is a foundational text of nineteenth-century [[american-literature]] and a masterpiece of psychological [[romanticism]] and [[gothic-literature]].

## you gotta know

- Originates from the wrongful execution of *Matthew Maule*, who was condemned for witchcraft during the Salem hysteria and famously cursed *Colonel Pyncheon* from the scaffold, prophesying that "God will give him blood to drink."
- Centers on the elderly, nearsighted spinster *Hepzibah Pyncheon*, whose patrician pride and habitual scowl are challenged when financial ruin forces her to open a humble cent-shop within the gloomy ancestral home.
- Features the return of Hepzibah's brother *Clifford Pyncheon*, a sensitive, broken lover of beauty who was wrongfully imprisoned for thirty years for the alleged murder of their uncle.
- Introduces *Phoebe Pyncheon*, a sunny, industrious country cousin whose youthful vitality and domestic competence revitalize the decaying mansion, nurture Clifford, and tend the decrepit breed of *Pyncheon* chickens.
- Involves the mysterious lodger *Holgrave*, a radical daguerreotypist who writes a story about *Alice Maule* and is ultimately unmasked as the last descendant of the executed wizard.
- Culminates in the sudden death of the hypocritical *Judge Jaffrey Pyncheon*, who suffers a fatal seizure in the ancestral armchair while attempting to blackmail Clifford, fulfilling the family curse.
- Concludes when Holgrave and Phoebe marry, uniting the feuding bloodlines after finding a worthless colonial deed to vast territories in Maine hidden behind the portrait of the Colonel.
- Reflects *Hawthorne's* personal obsession with hereditary guilt, directly influenced by his great-great-grandfather *John Hathorne*, the only unrepentant magistrate of the Salem witch trials.

## connections

- [[the-scarlet-letter]] — *Hawthorne's* celebrated preceding romance that similarly probes Puritan moral dogmatism and inherited spiritual isolation.
- [[american-literature]] — the foundational national canon in which the novel established major conventions of psychological narrative and historical allegory.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the literary tradition that supplied the tropes of a cursed dynasty, haunted architecture, and sins of the fathers.
- [[romanticism]] — the literary movement defining Hawthorne's aesthetic theory, detailed in his preface distinguishing a romance from a novel.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — contemporary dark romantic author who examined parallel concepts of psychological terror, dynastic decline, and decaying houses.
- [[christianity]] — the religious background informing the novel's pervasive themes of ancestral transgression, repentance, and divine justice.
- [[the-crucible]] — twentieth-century dramatic work that revisits the historical Salem witch trials that sparked the original curse against the Pyncheons.
- [[united-states]] — the developing nineteenth-century nation whose modern egalitarian ideals displace the crumbling vestiges of colonial feudalism.
