## summary

**An Ideal Husband** is a play by *Oscar Wilde*, first performed in 1895, that explores marriage, morality, politics, and the possibility of secrets in respectable society. When a threatening blackmailer reveals that a prominent politician once committed a financial crime, the play unspools questions of honor, forgiveness, and whether an "ideal" marriage can survive past transgressions. Wilde's trademark wit masks darker investigations into hypocrisy and the cost of maintaining appearances.

## you gotta know

- Written as a comedy of manners but infused with melodramatic tension, philosophical argument, and moral complexity.
- Sir Robert Chiltern, the protagonist, is a successful politician whose youthful dishonesty is about to be exposed by a blackmailer.
- His wife, Lady Gertrude Chiltern, represents idealized virtue and an unforgiving moral standard; the play interrogates whether her ideal marriage can survive Robert's secret.
- The character *Lord Goring* serves as Wilde's surrogate voice: witty, amoral, ultimately wise, and capable of engineering a resolution that the play seems to question.
- Themes include the hypocrisy of Victorian morality, the impossibility of absolute purity, and the tension between personal honesty and social propriety.
- The blackmailer, Mrs. Cheveley, is a sophisticated, formidable female antagonist—unusual for Victorian drama and a vehicle for Wilde's critique of both gender and morality.
- The play climaxes not with punishment but with conditional forgiveness, suggesting that moral absolutes are untenable in adult life.
- Distinguished from *The Importance of Being Earnest* by its deeper engagement with serious social and political issues, though both deploy Wilde's characteristic epigrammatic style.
