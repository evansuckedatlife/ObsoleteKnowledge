## summary

**Prague**, the capital of Bohemia and the Czech lands, became a major literary setting and symbolic geography in modernist literature, particularly through *Franz Kafka*, whose novels transmute the city's streets, bureaucracies, and anxieties into surreal, alienated landscapes. A city of cultural collision—German and Czech speakers, Christians and Jews, centre and periphery of the Austro-Hungarian Empire—Prague's linguistic and political fragmentation shaped a distinctly modernist sensibility of displacement and incomprehensibility. For Kafka and others, Prague was both birthplace and spiritual prison, the source of the alienation they transfigured into art.

## you gotta know

- Prague was a multilingual city in the Austro-Hungarian Empire, where German was the language of authority and Czech of local resistance, creating a linguistic dislocation central to Kafka's sense of not-quite-belonging.
- Kafka lived his entire life in Prague and set much of his fictional work there, though his Prague is transformed into a labyrinthine, impossible bureaucratic geography that bears only metaphorical relation to the actual city.
- The city was home to a vibrant Jewish cultural community, which Kafka was part of despite complex ambivalence; Jewish identity, tradition, and alienation are woven through his fiction.
- Prague's position as a provincial capital within a larger empire gave it a sense of being governed by distant, incomprehensible forces—a geographical experience that maps onto the powerlessness in Kafka's work.
- The city was a centre of *Austro-Hungarian* cultural ferment, producing figures like *Franz Kafka*, composers, and avant-garde artists, all responding to the tensions and contradictions of empire.
- Kafkaesque alienation is partly rooted in Prague's specific historical moment: a city caught between empires, languages, and identities, where communication seemed perpetually obstructed and authority always remained opaque and distant.

## connections

- [[franz-kafka]] — Prague's most famous literary son; his entire corpus is shaped by the city's atmosphere and structures.
