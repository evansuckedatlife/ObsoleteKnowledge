## summary

**Beowulf** is the oldest major work of English literature, an Old English epic composed in the 8th century and surviving in a single manuscript. It tells of the warrior Beowulf's three great battles: against the monster Grendel, Grendel's mother, and finally a dragon guarding treasure. The poem blends Germanic heroic tradition, pagan mythology, and Christian morality, exploring heroism, fate, mortality, and the passage from youth to age.

## you gotta know

- Composed in Old English (Anglo-Saxon) around 700 CE, preserved in a single manuscript (Nowell Codex) that barely survived a fire in 1731; it consists of 3,182 lines in alliterative verse (not rhymed).
- The hero Beowulf is a Geat warrior (from what is now Sweden) who comes to Denmark to aid King Hrothgar, whose mead-hall Heorot is plagued by Grendel, a monster terrorizing the court.
- Beowulf defeats Grendel bare-handed (ripping off the monster's arm), then hunts Grendel's mother to her underwater lair and slays her with a giant's sword—a descent into the supernatural underworld.
- The poem's final movement shifts tone: Beowulf, now king for fifty years, must face a dragon hoarding ancient treasure; his victory comes at the cost of his own life, a darkening vision of heroism.
- Themes shift across the three sections: youthful prowess and triumph (Grendel), the moral ambiguity of revenge and hunting evil into dark places (Grendel's mother), the approach of death and the fragility of human glory (the dragon).
- The poem mingles pagan fatalism (*wyrd*, fate) with Christian theology; Beowulf's victories are attributed both to strength and to God's will; death is inescapable but glorious.
- Literary influence is immense: Tolkien (a Beowulf scholar) drew on it for *The Lord of the Rings*; it established the template for heroic fantasy, epic quests, and battles with monsters.

## connections

- [[seamus-heaney-beowulf]] — the poet's celebrated 1999 translation, which brought Beowulf to contemporary readers with musical precision and accessibility.
- [[j-r-r-tolkien]] — a Beowulf scholar and medievalist; the poem directly influenced *The Lord of the Rings* (the hero's journey, the burden of power, the dragon).
- [[old-english]] — the language of *Beowulf*; the poem is foundational to English linguistic and literary history.
- [[anglo-saxon-literature]] — *Beowulf* is the apex of surviving Anglo-Saxon verse; nearly all other Old English literature is fragmentary or lost.
- [[epic-poetry]] — *Beowulf* is the English epic; the poem established the genre in English and was modeled on classical epics (*The Iliad*, *The Odyssey*).
- [[heroism]] — the poem's central concern; what constitutes heroic virtue, how it is tested, and how it endures beyond death in reputation.
- [[monsters]] — Grendel and the dragon are archetypal monsters; they represent evil, the untamed natural world, and the unknowable forces that test human courage.
