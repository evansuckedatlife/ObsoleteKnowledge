## summary

**Henry V** is a history play by *William Shakespeare* (c. 1598–99) that depicts the ascension and military triumph of King *Henry V* of England, culminating in his legendary victory at the Battle of *Agincourt* (1415). The play traces Henry's transformation from the dissolute Prince Hal into a disciplined warrior-king who unites his fractious nobles and army through sheer force of will and rhetoric. It is Shakespeare's most patriotic work, a meditation on the costs and glories of kingship, the nature of military leadership, and the paradoxes of justified warfare.

## you gotta know

- *Henry V* inherits the throne after his father *Henry IV*'s death; his reformation from wild youth to serious monarch is Shakespeare's theme of maturation and duty.
- The *Battle of Agincourt* (1415) is the play's climax: Henry's outnumbered English archers devastate the French nobility in a stunning victory that solidifies his claim to French territory and his legend as warrior-king.
- Henry's famous rallying speeches—especially the "St. Crispin's Day" oration before Agincourt—are among Shakespeare's greatest rhetorical achievements; they galvanize his troops through appeals to honor, brotherhood, and English identity.
- The play's *comic subplot* involves soldiers and common men (Bardolph, Fluellen, Pistol) whose cowardice, larceny, and stumbling heroism provide counterpoint to the kings' grand designs, humanizing the war's costs.
- *Katharine of France*, the French princess, becomes Henry's romantic interest; their marriage is negotiated as part of the peace settlement, merging personal affection with political alliance.
- The play wrestles with the *ethics of warfare*: Henry must weigh his soldiers' lives against his territorial ambitions, and the text does not entirely endorse his ruthlessness (the execution of prisoners, the destruction of towns).

- [[william-shakespeare]] — the author; *Henry V* completes the second tetralogy of history plays.
- [[agincourt]] — the historical battle that defines the play's action and Henry's legend.
- [[kingship]] — the central theme; Henry embodies an ideal of just, effective kingship grounded in both martial prowess and rhetorical power.
- [[history-play]] — one of Shakespeare's greatest works in the genre, balancing personal drama with historical events.
- [[england]] — the play's stage; it is deeply invested in English national identity and patriotic mythology.
- [[war]] — examined both as glorious and destructive; the play offers no simple moral about military conflict.
