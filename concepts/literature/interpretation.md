## summary

**Interpretation** (or *hermeneutics*) is the practice and theory of constructing meaning from a text, artefact, or utterance. In literature, it encompasses both the act of reading—the personal encounter with a work—and the discipline of criticism, which systematises and contests readings. Interpretation rests on the premise that texts are not self-evident; they require an active, skilled reader to decode their layers of meaning, and different readers (or different historical moments) may legitimately produce different interpretations.

## you gotta know

- No text carries a single, fixed meaning; interpretation is the inevitable work readers must do to make sense of linguistic and symbolic meaning.
- *Hermeneutics* originated in biblical exegesis (the interpretation of scripture) and was systematised by 19th and 20th-century philosophers like *Schleiermacher* and *Gadamer*.
- *Literary translation* is a form of interpretation: every translation embodies choices about meaning, tone, and cultural reference that reveal how the translator reads the source text.
- *Semiotics* (the study of signs) provides a framework for interpretation: how do signs generate and convey meaning? What is the difference between what a text says and what it means?
- *Reader-response criticism* argues that meaning is not inherent in the text but created in the encounter between reader and text; different readers produce different interpretations.
- Postcolonial and feminist criticism argue that dominant interpretations often reflect the power of their interpreters; recovering suppressed meanings requires actively reinterpreting canonical texts.
- The question of interpretation's limits: if a text can mean multiple things, does it mean anything? This tension defines much literary theory and practice.

## connections

- [[literary-translation]] — interpretation as linguistic and cultural negotiation between languages.
- [[semiotics]] — the study of how signs (including words) generate and convey meaning.
- [[literary-criticism]] — the formalised discipline of interpretation; systematic methods for reading and analysis.
- [[symbolism]] — the practice of encoding meaning through symbols that require interpretation.
- [[meaning-making]] — the broader process of constructing sense from language and culture.
- [[reader-response-theory]] — a critical school arguing that interpretation is fundamentally an act of the reader.
- [[poststructuralism]] — a theory arguing that meaning is unstable and interpretation is endlessly deferred.
