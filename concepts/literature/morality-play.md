## summary

A **morality play** is a medieval and early modern theatrical form in which abstract virtues and vices—Charity, Despair, Greed, Conscience—are personified as characters who struggle for the soul of a human protagonist, usually called *Everyman* or *Mankind*. These plays were didactic allegories staging the Christian battle for salvation, and they bridge medieval mystery plays and modern drama by pioneering the *individual* as the focus of moral action rather than biblical spectacle.

## you gotta know

- Flourished from the 14th to 16th centuries, primarily in England and France; performed in towns and courtyards, appealing to general audiences, not just the clergy.
- Dramatic structure: a protagonist (representing humanity) is tempted by Vice, aided by Virtue, and guided by figures like Conscience or Good Deeds toward salvation or damnation; the outcome is never certain until the end.
- Abstract personification: characters have names like *Everyman*, *Good Deeds*, *Strength*, *Knowledge*, *Gluttony*, *Sloth*—making moral psychology visible and tangible on stage.
- *Everyman* (circa 1495) is the most famous example and only complete survivor; its protagonist must account for his life before God after Death summons him; companions abandon him except Good Deeds.
- Moral urgency: unlike mystery plays, which retell biblical narrative, morality plays stage the *present spiritual crisis* of each viewer—your soul is on trial, now, as you watch.
- Comic and tragic: morality plays mix serious theology with crude humor and slapstick Vice-characters, making moral doctrine accessible and memorable through laughter and spectacle.
- Influence on Renaissance drama: the interplay of Vice and Virtue anticipated the psychological complexity of Shakespeare's villains (*Iago*, *Edmund*) and the introspective protagonists of tragedy.

## connections

- [[everyman]] — the canonical morality play; the most complete surviving text and model for the genre.
- [[medieval-drama]] — morality plays are the late flowering of the mystery and miracle play tradition, updated for moral rather than biblical focus.
- [[allegory]] — the governing technique; abstract concepts embodied as speaking characters to teach moral truth.
- [[late-medieval-drama]] — the period of morality play dominance; the bridge to Renaissance theater.
- [[death-and-mortality]] — the theme that haunts morality plays; the sudden reckoning with mortality and judgment.
- [[christianity]] — the theological framework; salvation and damnation, virtue and sin, are the stakes of every play.
- [[vice]] — the Vice character is a theatrical creation of morality plays; proto-villains who entertain while corrupting.
