## summary

The Duke of **Cornwall** is a major antagonist in *William Shakespeare*'s tragedy *King Lear*. Husband to Lear's cruel second daughter, *Regan*, he embodies unbridled political brutality, tyranny, and sadism in the fractured realm of pre-Christian Britain. Cornwall is most notorious for personally gouging out the eyes of the loyal Earl of *Gloucester*, an act of visceral stage violence that precipitates his own fatal wounding by an indignant household servant.

## you gotta know

- Married to *Regan*, joining forces with Lear's eldest daughter *Goneril* and her husband the Duke of Albany to carve up the British kingdom.
- Represents ruthless, tyrannical executive force, in direct contrast to the more hesitant and morally conflicted Duke of Albany.
- Places Lear's loyal courtier the Earl of Kent into the stocks, demonstrating open contempt for the aged king's authority and royal dignity.
- Forms a treasonous alliance with Gloucester's illegitimate son *Edmund*, who betrays his own father's secret French correspondence in exchange for title and power.
- Orchestrates the interrogation and torture of the elderly Earl of *Gloucester* at his castle, declaring "Out, vile jelly!" while gouging his eyes.
- Slain as an immediate consequence of his brutality when one of his own servants revolts in horror, drawing a sword and delivering a mortal wound.
