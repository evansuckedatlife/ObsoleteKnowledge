## summary

**Haemon** (frequently rendered in classical scholarship as **Haimon**) is the tragic prince of Thebes in *Sophocles*' fifth-century BCE drama *Antigone*. As the son of the autocratic king *Creon* and the betrothed of *Antigone*, he finds himself torn between filial obedience to royal authority and love for his condemned fiancée. His impassioned philosophical defense of moderation, followed by his suicide in *Antigone*'s subterranean tomb, shatters *Creon*'s family and demonstrates the catastrophic consequences of tyrannical pride in classical [[tragedy]].

## you gotta know

- Pleads with his father *Creon* to commute *Antigone*'s capital sentence, employing natural metaphors of flexible trees bending safely before winter torrents and vessels with eased sails to urge political wisdom and flexibility.
- Reports the clandestine murmurings of the Theban populace (*demos*), informing *Creon* that ordinary citizens view *Antigone* not as a criminal traitor, but as an honorable woman deserving glory for burying her slain brother *Polynices*.
- Clashes with *Creon* in a sharp stichomythic confrontation over sovereignty and law, declaring that a state belonging exclusively to a single autocrat ceases to be a genuine city (*polis*).
- Leaves *Creon*'s presence in furious grief after his father threatens to execute *Antigone* directly before his eyes, vowing that *Creon* will never behold his face again.
- Hurries frantically to the sealed rock chamber where *Antigone* has been condemned to starve, discovering that she has already hanged herself by her woven linen bridal veil.
- Lunges in blinded grief with his drawn blade at his father when *Creon* enters the vault; after missing his target, he turns his sword upon his own chest, dying while clasping *Antigone*'s corpse.
- Causes the despairing suicide of his mother, Queen *Eurydice*, who slays herself at the household altar upon hearing the news, cursing *Creon* with her dying breath as the slayer of their sons *Haemon* and *Megareus*.

## connections

- [[sophocles]] — the Athenian tragic poet who dramatized his doomed moral stand against autocracy in *Antigone*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the fifth-century BCE cultural and civic milieu in which dramatists staged vital debates concerning natural justice versus civic decrees.
- [[tragedy]] — the foundational literary form centered on insoluble ethical crises, family curses, and irreversible catastrophic falls.
- [[hamlet-characters]] — tragic youth facing parental oppression, sharing dramatic kinship with figures like *Ophelia* who perish in dynastic feuds.
- [[revenge-tragedy]] — early modern dramatic genre tracing its roots to classical dramaturgy, often reenacting the destruction of young heirs amid sovereign overreach.
- [[hallucination]] — psychological unraveling following traumatic death and tyrannical guilt, as experienced by *Creon* in his final lamentations.
