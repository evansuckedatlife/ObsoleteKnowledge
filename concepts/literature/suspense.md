## summary

**Suspense** is the narrative and emotional state induced when a reader or viewer awaits an outcome of uncertain resolution, typically involving danger or conflict. It is not merely surprise (the unexpected) but rather the *withholding of information* combined with stakes that matter: the audience knows some crucial fact or question remains unresolved, and this knowledge generates dread. *Alfred Hitchcock* systematized suspense into an exact science, demonstrating that how information is revealed (or concealed) matters more than what actually happens.

## you gotta know

- Suspense is distinct from surprise: you can tell an audience a bomb is under the table, and suspense builds as they wait for it to explode; if you show it suddenly, that's surprise, not suspense.
- Suspense requires a *ticking clock*—some deadline or window of possibility that closes; without temporal pressure, mere uncertainty becomes diffuse rather than focused.
- The technique of *delayed revelation*—showing the protagonist's fear or desperation without yet revealing what they fear—forces the audience into complicity and dread.
- Hitchcock discovered that suspense works best when the audience knows more than the protagonist; we watch the danger approaching and are helpless to warn them, making us active participants in the unfolding tragedy.
- Suspense operates through visual and auditory means as much as plot; camera angles, music, and sound design create anticipatory dread independent of dialogue or action.
- Psychological thrillers weaponize suspense through *uncertainty about truth*: the audience is unsure what is real, who is trustworthy, or whether the protagonist is sane, sustaining dread through interpretive ambiguity.
