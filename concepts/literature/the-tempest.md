# The Tempest

## summary

**The Tempest** is *William Shakespeare*'s final solo-authored play, believed written in 1610–1611 and first performed before King James I in 1611. It tells the story of *Prospero*, a sorcerer shipwrecked on a remote island with his daughter *Miranda*, who uses magic to conjure a storm that brings his usurping brother and other nobles ashore, leading to reconciliation, forgiveness, and a marriage that restores harmony.

## you gotta know

- *Prospero* was the rightful Duke of Milan until his brother *Antonio* seized power while Prospero studied magic; cast adrift with infant *Miranda*, Prospero washes ashore on an island.
- The island is inhabited by *Caliban*, a enslaved creature whom Prospero controls through magic after Caliban attempts to assault *Miranda*; *Caliban* resents his master and is central to debates about colonialism and exploitation.
- *Ariel*, a supernatural spirit bound to Prospero's service, performs magical effects (the titular tempest, invisible music, spectral visions) in exchange for eventual freedom, which Prospero grants at the play's end.
- The tempest shipwrecks *Antonio*, *Alonso* (the King of Naples), and *Ferdinand* (Alonso's son) on the island; *Ferdinand* falls in love with *Miranda* at first sight, and Prospero orchestrates their betrothal to heal the breach between kingdoms.
- The play explores themes of forgiveness, the limits of power and magic, the relationship between colonizer and colonized (through *Prospero* and *Caliban*), and reconciliation after wrongdoing.
- Often read as Shakespeare's farewell to the stage; Prospero's renunciation of magic and return to Milan parallel Shakespeare's retirement from playwriting.
- Unlike *Romeo and Juliet*'s tragic division, *The Tempest* ends in reconciliation through marriage and magical intervention, moving toward harmony rather than destruction.
