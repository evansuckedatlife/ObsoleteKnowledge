## summary

**Horace** (*Quintus Horatius Flaccus*) was the leading Roman lyric poet and satirist of the Augustan Age during the early consolidation of the [[roman-empire]]. Writing in the first century BCE across [[rome]] and his countryside estate, he created masterworks including the *Odes*, *Epodes*, *Satires*, *Epistles*, and the foundational critical treatise *Ars Poetica*. He remains indispensable to world literature for formulating enduring maxims such as *carpe diem* and *in medias res*, and for establishing the gentle, conversational mode of social commentary known as Horatian [[satire]].

## you gotta know

- Coined dozens of Western literature's most famous Latin aphorisms, including *carpe diem* ("seize the day") from the *Odes*, *in medias res* ("into the middle of things") and *dulce et utile* ("sweet and useful") from *Ars Poetica*, and *dulce et decorum est pro patria mori* ("it is sweet and fitting to die for one's country").
- Established the tradition of Horatian [[satire]] as an urbane, smiling, and reflective critique of human vices and societal foolishness, offering a measured contrast to the furious indignation of *Juvenal*.
- Composed the *Ars Poetica* (*The Art of Poetry* / *Epistle to the Pisos*), an influential verse epistle establishing principles of classical decorum, organic unity, craft discipline, and the avoidance of contrived resolutions like *deus ex machina*.
- Enjoyed the patronage of *Gaius Maecenas*, the wealthy cultural adviser to Emperor *Augustus*, who gifted the poet his beloved Sabine farm in the hills northeast of [[rome]].
- Adapted intricate ancient Greek lyric metres—notably Alcaic and Sapphic stanzas—into classical Latin verse across four books of *Odes* (*Carmina*), celebrating friendship, wine, philosophical moderation, and civic responsibility.
- Commissioned directly by Emperor *Augustus* in 17 BCE to compose the *Carmen Saeculare* (*Centennial Hymn*), performed publicly before Roman citizens by a ceremonial chorus of youths and maidens during the Secular Games.
- Fought during his youth on the losing Republican side under *Brutus* at the Battle of Philippi in 42 BCE against *Mark Antony* and *Octavian*, later humorously noting in his verse that he abandoned his shield and fled.

## connections

- [[satire]] — originated the temperate, witty, and conversational genre of social critique named in his honor.
- [[poetry]] — defined classical Latin lyric meters and bequeathed foundational aesthetic standards to Western verse.
- [[roman-empire]] — flourished during the Golden Age of Augustan literature alongside contemporaries like *Virgil* and *Ovid*.
- [[rome]] — worked at the center of Roman intellectual culture while balancing urban obligations with rural retreat.
- [[humananism]] — served as a vital curriculum author championed by Renaissance scholars for his moral balance and stylistic polish.
- [[iconic-locations]] — immortalized the Sabine farm as an enduring literary archetype of tranquil artistic independence away from urban corruption.
- [[enlightenment]] — provided eighteenth-century neoclassical English poets, particularly *Alexander Pope*, with a primary model for urbane satire.
