## summary

**Puritanism** was a religious reform movement that originated within the Church of England during the late sixteenth century, seeking to cleanse the Protestant establishment of Catholic ritual and hierarchical authority. Following the Great Migration of the 1630s, the **Puritans** founded the Massachusetts Bay Colony and established an influential theocratic culture that shaped the social, political, and educational foundations of the [[united-states]]. In [[american-literature]], the movement originated enduring forms of spiritual autobiography, captivity narratives, and sermons, while its strict moral scrutiny and fixation on inherited guilt became foundational concerns for later American writers.

## you gotta know

- The 1692 Salem witch trials in colonial Massachusetts saw theological hysteria and spectral evidence lead to nineteen executions, a moral crisis later dramatized in Arthur Miller's play *[[the-crucible]]*.
- Governor *John Winthrop* outlined the colony's religious destiny in his 1630 sermon *A Model of Christian Charity*, urging the settlers to construct an exemplary "city upon a hill" for the Christian world.
- Author *Nathaniel Hawthorne* made the psychological reality of seventeenth-century New England his central subject, probing ancestral guilt, hypocrisy, and dogmatic cruelty in *[[the-scarlet-letter]]* and *[[house-of-the-seven-gables]]*.
- In [[england]], Puritan religious zeal directed the Parliamentary faction during the English Civil War under *Oliver Cromwell*, resulting in the execution of King *Charles I* and the rule of the Commonwealth.
- Core theology centered on Calvinist doctrines of total depravity and double predestination, driving believers to search their inward lives constantly for signs of divine election and saving grace.
- Revivalist theologian *Jonathan Edwards* brought Puritan preaching to a dramatic height in the First Great Awakening with his 1741 sermon *Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God*, portraying sinners suspended over hell like a spider.
- Early colonial [[poetry]] emerged from devout households, notably through *Anne Bradstreet*, author of *The Tenth Muse Lately Sprung Up in America*, and the private metaphysical verse of minister *Edward Taylor*.
- Confronted by declining zeal among younger generations, New England clergy adopted the Half-Way Covenant of 1662, granting partial church membership to baptized adults who had not undergone an individual conversion experience.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — foundational movement whose providential narratives, captivity accounts, and moral symbolism shaped early American writing and nineteenth-century romances.
- [[england]] — the realm where Elizabethan and Stuart reformers challenged ecclesiastical hierarchies and ritualism, eventually sparking civil war and regicide.
- [[christianity]] — the overarching religious tradition within which Reformed theologians developed doctrines of biblical authority, covenant theology, and predestination.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose civic culture, democratic town meeting traditions, and rhetoric of moral exceptionalism trace back to New England settlements.
- [[gothic-literature]] — literary genre that transformed Puritan anxieties regarding demonic temptation, hereditary guilt, and secret sin into psychological horror.
- [[english-renaissance]] — cultural epoch whose public theaters and secular spectacles were fiercely condemned and closed by victorious Puritan authorities in 1642.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical movement centered on empirical reason and human liberty that challenged and ultimately supplanted strict Calvinist orthodoxy across eighteenth-century societies.
