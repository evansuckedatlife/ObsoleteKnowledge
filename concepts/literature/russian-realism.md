# Russian Realism

## summary

**Russian realism** is a distinctive tradition within 19th-century realist literature, distinguished by its psychological depth, moral seriousness, and engagement with Russian social and philosophical questions. Unlike Western European realism's focus on social surfaces and material conditions, Russian realism plunges into the interior lives of characters, exploring consciousness, guilt, faith, and ideological conflict. Figures like *Tolstoy*, *Dostoevsky*, *Turgenev*, and *Chekhov* created works of unprecedented psychological sophistication that remain foundational to modern fiction. Russian realism embodies a particular combination of artistic mastery and philosophical inquiry—the novel as a vehicle for exploring the deepest questions of meaning, morality, and human nature.

## you gotta know

- *Leo Tolstoy*'s *War and Peace* (1869) and *Anna Karenina* (1877) are the supreme examples of Russian realist fiction: panoramic in scope, psychologically intricate, and philosophically ambitious.
- *Fyodor Dostoevsky* brought unprecedented psychological complexity to the novel; *Crime and Punishment* explores the interior life of a murderer with terrifying honesty, treating the crime as a spiritual crisis rather than merely a legal transgression.
- Russian realism is characterized by *moral seriousness*: the novel is a medium for grappling with questions of faith, suffering, justice, and the meaning of human existence—not merely depicting social conditions.
- *Ivan Turgenev* developed a more lyrical, contemplative realism, emphasizing interiority and the unresolved tensions within consciousness.
- *Anton Chekhov*'s stories and plays refine Russian realism into concentrated form: minimal plot, emphasis on the unspoken, the capture of psychological truth through precisely observed detail.
- Russian realist fiction emerged partly from Russia's social crises (serfdom, autocracy, radical movements) and partly from a philosophical tradition emphasizing the individual soul's worth and complexity.
