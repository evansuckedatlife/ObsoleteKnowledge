## summary

**Duncan** is the rightful King of Scotland in William Shakespeare's *Macbeth*, a virtuous and ineffectual ruler whose murder by *Macbeth* and *Lady Macbeth* sets the tragic action of the play in motion. Though he appears only briefly before his assassination, Duncan's character—honest, trusting, and beloved—defines the moral order that *Macbeth's* ambition destroys. His murder is not merely regicide but a violation of both the natural and divine law, making his death the catalyst that unleashes the horror that consumes Macbeth and Scotland.

## you gotta know

- Beloved king whose gentle rule has earned him the affection of his nobles, including *Macbeth*, whom he favours and trusts implicitly.
- His goodness and wisdom make him unaware of the depths of ambition and malice in those around him; he cannot imagine that *Macbeth* would betray him.
- *Macbeth* becomes thane of Cawdor and then earns the prediction that he will be king, but Duncan's life stands in the way of this prophecy.
- Duncan's decision to name his son *Malcolm* as his successor precipitates *Macbeth's* decisive turn toward murder.
- His murder is portrayed with particular horror: he is killed while a guest in *Macbeth's* home, a violation of the sacred duty of hospitality.
- Duncan's blood cannot be washed from *Macbeth's* hands; his murder haunts *Macbeth* throughout the play, emblematic of how the crime against innocence can never be undone.

## connections

- [[macbeth]] — the play and the protagonist who murders Duncan, setting the tragedy in motion.
- [[lady-macbeth]] — Duncan's killer's wife, who steels her husband's resolve to commit the regicide.
- [[ambition]] — Duncan's position stands in the way of *Macbeth's* ambition; his destruction is ambition's price.
- [[kingship]] — Duncan represents the legitimate exercise of royal authority; his murder is also a violation of divinely ordained order.
- [[malcolm]] — Duncan's son, named heir, whose survival and eventual restoration of order mirrors the play's moral arc.
- [[banquo]] — Duncan's kinsman and friend, who remains loyal and whose suspicions about *Macbeth* foreshadow the king's doom.
