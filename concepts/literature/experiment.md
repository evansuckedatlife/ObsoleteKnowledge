## summary

An **experiment** is an empirical, controlled procedure undertaken to test a scientific hypothesis, validate a causal relationship, or discover unknown natural phenomena. Codified during the Scientific Revolution and championed throughout the *Enlightenment*, the experimental method displaced scholastic authority in favor of reproducible observation and inductive reasoning. In literary history, the experiment functions both as the foundational plot motor of *science-fiction* and as a methodological model that inspired naturalism and formal innovation across the modern era.

## you gotta know

- Articulated philosophically by *Francis Bacon* in his 1620 treatise *Novum Organum*, establishing the inductive scientific method against medieval Aristotelian deduction.
- Applied rigorously to mechanics and optics by *Isaac Newton*, whose pivotal prism demonstrations proved that white light is a compound of the visible spectrum.
- Inaugurated modern *science-fiction* through *Mary Shelley*'s 1818 novel *Frankenstein*, which framed the experimental creation of life as a cautionary tale of hubristic scientific inquiry.
- Reinterpreted as a literary theory by *Émile Zola* in his 1880 manifesto *The Experimental Novel*, which argued that the novelist should observe and manipulate characters under controlled conditions like a physiologist.
- Dramatized as a macabre moral crisis in *H. G. Wells*'s *The Island of Doctor Moreau*, where vivisectionist surgical trials destabilize the boundary separating humanity from bestiality.
- Captured memorably in fine art by *Joseph Wright of Derby*'s 1768 *Enlightenment* masterpiece *An Experiment on a Bird in the Air Pump*, illustrating public awe and ethical ambivalence toward pneumatic trials.
- Lampooned ruthlessly in early modern literature, notably in *Jonathan Swift*'s *Gulliver's Travels*, which parodied the Royal Society through the absurd, impractical experiments at the Academy of Lagado.
- Requires essential methodological standards in empirical science, including baseline control groups, reproducible conditions, and the systematic isolation of variables.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual epoch that established empirical testing and rational deduction as the supreme arbiters of truth.
- [[science-fiction]] — the literary genre fundamentally anchored in extrapolating the societal and philosophical consequences of experimental discoveries.
- [[experimental-fiction]] — the artistic movement that translated empirical laboratory exploration into formal, typographic, and structural novelistic trials.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the era of rapid mechanical transformation powered by applied chemical, thermodynamic, and metallurgical experimentation.
- [[satire]] — the comedic mode frequently deployed by dramatists and novelists to mock pedantic or fraudulent scientific experimenters.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the historical era that witnessed the transition from occult alchemy toward formal natural philosophy.
- [[face-the-servant]] — the comedic character who mimics experimental alchemical operations to exploit the material obsessions of his victims.
