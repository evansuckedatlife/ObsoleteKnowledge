## summary

**Racism** is the ideology and practice of viewing some human populations as inherently inferior based on perceived racial characteristics, and the systems of discrimination that follow from that belief. In literature, racism appears both as historical context and as a central theme — particularly in works like Shakespeare's *Othello*, where Moor's outsider status and perceived racial difference drive tragedy. Understanding racism in literary texts requires recognizing how prejudice shapes character, plot, and moral meaning.

## you gotta know

- Racial discrimination predates modern racial categories and appears throughout literary history, often encoded in language about "Moor," "blackness," or foreign origin.
- In *Othello*, racism is the structural force that makes Iago's manipulation possible — Othello's status as an outsider makes him vulnerable to suspicion of infidelity and makes his tragedy inevitable.
- Iago explicitly invokes racial hatred as a weapon, calling Othello a "black ram" to incite Brabantio's fury and setting the tragedy in motion with deliberately inflammatory language.
- *Aaron the Moor* in *Titus Andronicus* represents a figure where blackness is invoked alongside villainy and sexuality, complicating early modern English prejudices about race, morality, and character.
- Desdemona's choice to marry Othello across racial lines becomes a transgression in the eyes of her father and Venice's society, proof of the social stakes and danger of racism.
- Racism in Renaissance literature often uses the Moor as a figure of simultaneous fascination and fear, revealing anxieties about power, sexuality, and cultural otherness that haunt European identity.
- The play interrogates whether racism is inherent to Othello's character or deliberately constructed by those around him, making racism itself the central tragedy rather than individual vice.
- Literary representations of racism reveal how prejudice operates through language, rumor, and visual markers of difference, showing racism as a social construction maintained by rhetoric and gossip.

## connections

- [[othello]] — the protagonist whose racial status as a Moor is the lynchpin of his tragedy.
- [[iago]] — villain who weaponizes racism to destroy Othello, using racial hatred as his primary tool of manipulation.
