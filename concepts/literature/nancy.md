## summary

**Nancy** is the tragic heroine of *Charles Dickens'* *Oliver Twist*, a young woman caught between the criminal underworld and the possibility of redemption. Her murder at the hands of *Bill Sikes* stands as one of literature's most brutal and transformative moments, the pivot point where the novel shifts from Oliver's individual rescue to a reckoning with systemic moral failure. Nancy embodies the vulnerability of the poor and the dignity that persists even in degradation.

## you gotta know

- Raised in Fagin's criminal gang, Nancy has learned survival through theft and deception, yet retains an instinctive moral sense and capacity for love.
- She falls genuinely in love with Bill Sikes, a brutal career criminal, and her attempts to protect Oliver and escape that world are rooted in maternal instinct; her love is both genuine and destructive.
- Nancy's decision to betray Fagin and Sikes to help Oliver Twist represents a moral turning point—she chooses goodness despite the cost; redemption becomes possible even for those born into crime.
- Her murder by Sikes in a fit of jealous rage is one of Victorian literature's most shocking acts of violence, shocking both for its brutality and for Dickens' frank depiction of domestic violence.
- Nancy's death galvanizes moral awakening in other characters and initiates the social justice reckoning that concludes the novel; her sacrifice becomes the novel's moral center.
- Dickens' portrayal of Nancy challenged Victorian hypocrisy by depicting a sex worker with full humanity, emotional depth, and moral agency; she refuses the role of fallen woman without redemption.
- Nancy's famous "Please, sir, I want to go back" scene demonstrates her conflict between survival and morality; she cannot imagine a world outside Sikes' control even as she seeks escape.
- Her character influenced subsequent literary depictions of marginalized women, particularly sex workers and victims of violence; Dickens treats her as worthy of tragedy and dignified death.
