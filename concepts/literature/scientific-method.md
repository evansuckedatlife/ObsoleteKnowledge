## summary

**The scientific method** is the systematic procedure of observation, hypothesis, experimentation, and verification that became the foundation of modern natural philosophy and science. Formalised in the work of *Isaac Newton* and empiricist philosophers, the method was revolutionary: it replaced appeals to authority or tradition with evidence gathered through experiment and reproducible observation. In literature, the scientific method appears both as a source of wonder (the boundless possibility of rational inquiry) and as a source of anxiety (the reduction of the world to mechanism, the threat of a disenchanted universe); science fiction is arguably the literary form that fully engages with the scientific method's imaginative possibilities and dangers.

## you gotta know

- The scientific method emerged gradually across the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries; *Isaac Newton's* *Principia* (1687) is often considered its fullest statement and triumph.
- Core elements include: observing phenomena, formulating testable hypotheses, designing experiments to test those hypotheses, and revising theory based on evidence rather than received authority.
- A central innovation was the insistence that claims be falsifiable and repeatable; any experiment must be replicable by others, making knowledge objective rather than personal.
- The method separated science from philosophy and theology by limiting inquiry to the natural world and excluding appeals to divine will or metaphysical authority.
- Science fiction, as a genre, is fundamentally about exploring the imaginative possibilities of the scientific method—worlds shaped by technologies derived from rational inquiry, societies transformed by scientific discovery.
- The method's success in producing knowledge and manipulating nature gave it enormous cultural authority; literature often engages with anxiety about that authority—the fear that reason alone cannot govern human conduct or meaning.
- The scientific method is often contrasted with artistic and humanistic ways of knowing, though modern literature frequently blurs that boundary, suggesting that both science and art are ways of investigating experience.

## connections

- [[science-fiction]] — the literary genre most directly engaged with the scientific method as both source of wonder and anxiety.
- [[isaac-newton]] — the figure most closely associated with formalising and exemplifying the scientific method.
- [[empiricism]] — the philosophical foundation of the scientific method; knowledge derived from observation and experience rather than reason alone.
- [[reason]] — the intellectual tool deployed in the scientific method; logical inference from evidence.
- [[technology]] — the practical application of scientific method; literature often explores technology's social consequences.
