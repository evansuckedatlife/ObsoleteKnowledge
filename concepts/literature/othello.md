## summary

**Othello** is a tragedy by *William Shakespeare* (c. 1603–04) that depicts the moral destruction of a Moorish general in Venice, undone by the manipulations of his ensign Iago, who systematically convinces him that his wife is unfaithful. The play is a masterwork of psychological warfare: Iago's motiveless malignity corrodes Othello's noble character and love into murderous paranoia, culminating in tragedy. It stands as Shakespeare's most penetrating exploration of how prejudice, jealousy, and the power of suggestion can unravel even the most dignified person.

## you gotta know

- *Othello* is a general of the Venetian army, the only non-European character in prominent European drama of its era—a fact the play exploits to devastating effect.
- *Iago* is Othello's ensign, passed over for promotion in favor of *Cassio*; he harbors a consuming resentment and schemes to destroy Othello out of what he admits is motiveless envy and possible racist contempt.
- *Desdemona* is a Venetian noblewoman who marries Othello in secret against her father's wishes, defying social convention; Iago targets her as the mechanism of Othello's destruction.
- Iago's manipulation is linguistic and psychological: he plants false evidence (a handkerchief), makes insinuations, and asks leading questions that allow Othello to construct his own narrative of infidelity.
- Othello's tragic flaw is not pride or ambition but a vulnerability to suggestion and an internalized doubt about his own worthiness—Iago weaponizes Othello's own insecurity about being an outsider.
- The play culminates in Othello's murder of Desdemona in a jealous rage, followed immediately by his realization of her innocence and his suicide.
- The *handkerchief* is the play's central symbol—a token of love that Iago manipulates into false evidence of infidelity, driving the tragedy.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — the author; *Othello* is one of his four great tragedies alongside *Hamlet*, *King Lear*, and *Macbeth*.
- [[iago]] — the villain whose motiveless malignity and linguistic manipulation drive the tragedy.
- [[revenge-tragedy]] — the play inherits the structural conventions of the revenge play but inverts them: Othello murders the innocent rather than avenging a genuine wrong.
- [[jealousy]] — the passion that transforms Othello from a rational general into a deluded murderer.
- [[manipulation]] — Iago's power to reshape reality through language and suggestion.
- [[race-in-literature]] — the play's treatment of Othello's race is foundational to understanding early modern European drama and continues to provoke debate about racism and the text's own complicity.
