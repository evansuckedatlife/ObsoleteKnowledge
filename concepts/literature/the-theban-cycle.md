## summary

**The Theban Cycle** is the interconnected body of Greek myths and tragic dramas centered on the city of *Thebes* and the cursed house of *Laius*. Spanning multiple generations, the cycle encompasses the doom of *Oedipus*, the consequences for his children, and the conflicts that arise after his fall. Though no unified ancient text exists, the cycle was the subject of numerous Greek tragedies, most famously the surviving works of *Sophocles*, which explore how individual choices reverberate across families and generations.

## you gotta know

- Begins with *Laius*, king of Thebes, who violates the guest-friendship code, setting a familial curse in motion.
- *Oedipus*, unknowingly fulfilling a prophecy, kills his father and marries his mother *Jocasta*, only to be undone when the truth emerges.
- After *Oedipus* blinds himself and goes into exile, his sons *Eteocles* and *Polynices* quarrel over the throne, leading to fratricidal war.
- *Antigone*, *Oedipus's* daughter, defies the king's edict and buries her brother *Polynices*, choosing family loyalty over state law—the subject of *Sophocles'* *Antigone*.
- The cycle exemplifies the Greek concept of *hamartia* (tragic flaw) and *anagnorisis* (tragic recognition), where knowledge arrives too late to prevent catastrophe.
- Playwrights including *Sophocles*, *Aeschylus*, and *Euripides* each adapted different points in the cycle, creating variations on shared mythic material.
- The curse originates when *Laius* abducts and seduces *Chrysippus*, a guest under his protection; *Chrysippus's* father *Pelops* curses *Laius* and all his descendants.
- *Oedipus* is exposed as an infant to die, but is rescued and raised by a shepherd; his parents' attempt to escape prophecy ironically fulfills it.
- *Jocasta's* suicide and *Oedipus's* self-blinding occur when he discovers his sins; the revelation drives the play's unbearable emotional climax.
- The final generation's destruction (*Eteocles* and *Polynices* killing each other) leaves only *Antigone* and *Ismene*; the cycle's devastation spans three generations and demonstrates the inescapability of ancestral guilt.

## connections

- [[sophocles]] — the tragedian whose three Theban plays remain the definitive literary treatments.
- [[oedipus-rex]] — the centerpiece play; *Oedipus* discovering his own guilt represents the archetype of tragic recognition.
- [[antigone]] — the sequel drama, where the next generation inherits the curse's consequences.
- [[greek-tragedy]] — the cycle embodies the form's central concerns: fate, knowledge, justice, and moral choice.
- [[ancient-greece]] — rooted in the mythological world but reflecting Greek civic anxieties about kinship, law, and divine will.
