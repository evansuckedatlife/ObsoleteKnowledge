## summary

**Annabella** is the tragic female protagonist of *John Ford*'s Caroline-era drama *'Tis Pity She's a Whore*, published in 1633 in [[england]]. Caught in an incestuous romance with her scholar brother *Giovanni*, she represents the doomed intersection of passionate defiance and societal entrapment in late [[english-renaissance]] theatre. Her gruesome death at her brother's hands cements her place as one of the most provocative heroines in Jacobean and Caroline [[revenge-tragedy]].

## you gotta know

- Central heroine of *'Tis Pity She's a Whore*, a controversial landmark of late [[english-renaissance]] drama written by *John Ford*.
- Engages in an incestuous affair with her brother *Giovanni*, who convinces her to consumate their mutual passion despite ecclesiastical and moral prohibitions.
- Courted by multiple aristocratic suitors in Parma, including the nobleman *Soranzo*, the buffoon *Bergetto*, and the scheming *Grimaldi*.
- Discovers she is pregnant by *Giovanni*, compelling her confidante and tutor *Putana* to arrange an expedient marriage with *Soranzo* to conceal the scandal.
- Endures horrific domestic violence when *Soranzo* discovers her pregnancy through her confession and a physician's examination, culminating in her imprisonment.
- Penitent before her end, she writes a warning letter in her own blood to *Giovanni*, entreating him to shun *Soranzo*'s trap.
- Murdered in her bedchamber by *Giovanni*, who kisses her tenderly before stabbing her to preserve their forbidden union from external dishonour.
- Her severed heart is dramatically carried onstage upon *Giovanni*'s dagger during *Soranzo*'s banquet, unleashing the play's concluding bloodbath.

## connections

- [[tragedy]] — the overarching dramatic genre defining her catastrophic descent and inevitable death.
- [[revenge-tragedy]] — the theatrical tradition of bloodfeuds and sensational violence that frames her murder.

- [[england]] — the country where Caroline theatre developed complex, transgressive psychological plots.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the dramatist whose *Romeo and Juliet* heavily inspired Ford's poetic treatment of star-crossed lovers.
- [[gothic-literature]] — later literary genre that drew directly upon her tale's macabre and sensational themes.
