## summary

**Macduff**, the Thane of Fife in Shakespeare's *Macbeth*, is the instrument of the tyrant's fall. Though he remains loyal to the rightful Scottish king [[malcolm]], Macduff is driven to rebellion when Macbeth murders his entire family in a rage. His final confrontation with Macbeth, wherein he reveals that he was "from his mother's womb untimely ripped," fulfills the witches' prophecies and restores rightful rule to Scotland. Macduff embodies the moral clarity and righteous vengeance that contrast with Macbeth's corruption.

## you gotta know

- Leaves Scotland to join Malcolm's forces in England, fleeing Macbeth's paranoid tyranny but leaving his wife and children behind; this separation becomes the source of his deepest anguish.
- Macbeth, fearing Macduff's loyalty and power, orders the massacre of Macduff's wife, children, and household in revenge; the murder occurs offstage, but Macduff learns of it in England.
- The murder of his innocent family becomes Macduff's tragic burden and his motive for pursuing Macbeth with single-minded vengeance; his grief transforms into righteous rage.
- In battle, Macduff reveals to Macbeth that he was delivered by caesarean section—"not of woman born"—thus fulfilling the witches' prophecy that no man born of woman can harm Macbeth; the revelation overturns Macbeth's false security.
- Defeats Macbeth in single combat and beheads him, restoring Malcolm to the Scottish throne and restoring order to the kingdom; the tyrant's head becomes an emblem of restored justice.
- Represents the noble lord who refuses tyranny and whose personal grief becomes the instrument of political justice; his arc demonstrates how individual suffering can serve the larger good.
- Unlike Macbeth, Macduff acts from moral conviction rather than ambition, making him the exemplary counterfigure to the corrupted thane.

## connections

- [[scotland]] — the kingdom torn by Macbeth's evil and restored by Macduff's triumph.
