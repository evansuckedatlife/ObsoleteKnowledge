## summary

In literature, **witches** embody supernatural evil and forbidden power—especially feminine power. *Shakespeare's* three witches in *Macbeth* are the canonical representation: agents of fate who speak in riddling prophecy, seduce ambition, and orchestrate tragedy. They challenge straightforward morality by making us ask whether they control destiny or merely speak it; whether they corrupt or merely reveal the darkness already in human hearts.

## you gotta know

- Shakespeare's witches in *Macbeth* (**the weird sisters**) appear on a heath and prophesy that Macbeth will be king; their ambiguous language invites him to murder while concealing responsibility for his choices.
- Witches occupy a liminal space: they are supernatural (can vanish, conjure spirits) yet embodied and interactive; neither divine nor demonic, but a third category operating by different rules.
- The witches' prophecies are technically true but misleading: "none of woman born shall harm Macbeth" comes to pass through Birnam Wood coming to Dunsinane—literal truth twisted into false assurance.
- Witchcraft in early modern literature reflects anxieties about female autonomy, sexuality, and power; witches often seduce men into ruin (the temptress witch) or operate outside patriarchal control (the autonomous witch).
- Witches in literature are often associated with marginality—age, childlessness, poverty, deformity; they are excluded from normal society and embody its fears and discontents.
- Witch-craft as conspiracy: sometimes witches are presented as a collective threat, a secret organization working against Christian order (*The Tempest*, *The Crucible*).
- Literary witches blur the boundary between manipulation and destiny; are they powerful agents or merely readers of fate? The ambiguity is their power over the audience.

## connections

- [[macbeth]] — the defining text; the witches' prophecy initiates the tragedy and haunts every scene.
- [[banquo]] — Macbeth's companion, also addressed by the witches; his response contrasts with Macbeth's ambition.
- [[supernatural]] — witches are the primary supernatural agents in Renaissance drama; magic, prophecy, and the impossible are their domain.
- [[prophecy]] — the witches' speech; they speak in riddles and prophecy, blurring truth and deception.
- [[evil]] — witches embody evil or at least moral ambiguity; whether they are evil or merely amoral is an open question.
- [[female-power]] — witches represent dangerous female power; sexuality, knowledge, or autonomy outside male control.
- [[the-tempest]] — Prospero, a sorcerer (masculine witch), uses magic to orchestrate fate; his relationship to the witches' art differs by gender and legitimacy.
