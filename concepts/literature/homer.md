# Homer

## summary

**Homer** (8th century BCE, precise dates disputed) was the ancient Greek poet credited with composing the *Iliad* and *Odyssey*, the two foundational epics of Western literature. Whether Homer was a single historical person or a composite of oral poets remains debated, but his name attached to these works makes him the earliest canonical author in the tradition and the voice through which Greek mythology and Mycenaean civilization reached posterity.

## you gotta know

- Composed or compiled the *Iliad*, an epic of roughly 24,000 lines recounting the Trojan War's final year, centered on the wrath of *Achilles*; it is the oldest surviving work of Greek literature and among the oldest in all of European culture.
- Authored (or inspired) the *Odyssey*, a narrative of *Odysseus*' ten-year return voyage after Troy's fall, balancing adventure, divine intervention, and the domestic yearning for home (*nostos*).
- Likely composed his works orally and for oral performance, using formulaic epithets (e.g., "swift-footed Achilles," "wine-dark sea") to aid memory and rhythm; writing may have come later.
- Presented the Olympian gods—*Zeus*, *Athena*, *Apollo*—as powerful, capricious, and intimately involved in human affairs, establishing the mythological framework that shaped all subsequent Greek culture.
- Immortalized *Mycenaean civilization* of the Late Bronze Age (c. 1200 BCE), though Homer wrote centuries later, making him the chief source for how ancients imagined their heroic past.
- The epics establish narrative patterns that persist: the hero's journey, the homecoming, the tragic flaw, divine favor and wrath, the roll-call of warriors, and the tension between fate and individual agency.
- Became the basis of Greek education; young Greeks memorized and studied Homer as the supreme literary authority and moral teacher.

- [[the-odyssey]] — the homecoming epic; a narrative of suffering, disguise, and divine protection on the path to restoration.
- [[the-trojan-war]] — the historical/mythological event that frames the *Iliad* and anchors Greek mythology.
- [[achilles]] — the greatest warrior of the *Iliad*; his wrath and honor-bound choices drive the epic's central conflict.
- [[odysseus]] — the hero of the *Odyssey*; embodiment of cunning (*metis*) and the long struggle for home.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Homer's works defined Greek cultural identity, education, and understanding of their mythic heritage.
- [[zeus]] — the king of gods; repeatedly invoked and obeyed throughout Homer's epics, representing cosmic order.
