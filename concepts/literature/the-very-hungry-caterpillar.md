## summary

**The Very Hungry Caterpillar** is a 1969 children's picture book written and illustrated by American author and designer *Eric Carle*. The book follows the life cycle of a newborn caterpillar as it eats through an escalating variety of foods across the days of the week before spinning a cocoon and emerging as a colorful butterfly. Celebrated for its brilliant collage technique, playful die-cut holes, and integration of early educational concepts, the story is widely recognized as one of the most influential and best-selling picture books of the twentieth century.

## you gotta know

- Carle created the book's iconic visual style through painted-tissue collage, hand-painting sheets of tissue paper with acrylics using sponges, brushes, and fingers before cutting and pasting the textured layers onto illustration board.
- The physical book features die-cut holes punched directly through the pages, allowing young readers to follow the caterpillar's progress with their fingers as it seemingly eats its way through each food item.
- The narrative ingeniously weaves together multiple foundational early childhood concepts, including the days of the week, numbers from one to five, primary food groups, and biological metamorphosis.
- The caterpillar's diet escalates from fruit—one apple on Monday, two pears on Tuesday, three plums on Wednesday, four strawberries on Thursday, and five oranges on Friday—into a massive Saturday feast of ten items, including chocolate cake, cherry pie, and salami.
- Suffering a severe stomachache after Saturday's indulgence, the creature recovers on Sunday by consuming a single wholesome green leaf before entering its pupation phase.
- Although the text states that the caterpillar built a "small house, called a cocoon," butterflies scientifically emerge from a *chrysalis*, a biological discrepancy that Carle acknowledged but retained because he favored the comforting poetic sound of the phrase.
- The book originated from an idea about a bookworm named "Willi," but Carle's editor, *Ann Beneduce*, suggested that a caterpillar would offer a far more satisfying and visually spectacular transformation into a butterfly.
- Because American publishers lacked the equipment to bind pages of differing widths with precision die-cut holes at a reasonable price, Carle found a printer in *Japan* willing to manufacture the innovative original edition.
