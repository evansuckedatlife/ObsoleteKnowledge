## summary

**Honoré de Balzac** was a 19th-century French novelist and playwright who stood as a foundational figure of literary [[realism]]. Working during the turbulent aftermath of the [[french-revolution]] in [[france]], he systematically mapped the social, moral, and financial landscape of his country under the Bourbon Restoration and July Monarchy. His life's work culminated in *La Comédie humaine* (*The Human Comedy*), a vast sequence of nearly one hundred interlinked novels, novellas, and essays. Through relentless observation, vivid characterization, and an acute focus on the corrupting power of money, he transformed the novel into a comprehensive portrait of society.

## you gotta know

- Created the monumental cycle *La Comédie humaine*, an ambitious project divided into scenes of private, provincial, and Parisian life designed to catalog every class and profession in post-revolutionary French society.
- Wrote the celebrated masterpiece *Le Père Goriot*, which reworks *King Lear* through an old vermicelli maker who impoverishes himself to finance the lavish lifestyles of his ungrateful daughters.
- Introduced the quintessential social climber *Eugène de Rastignac*, an impoverished student in the boarding house *Maison Vauquer*, who abandons his provincial morality and challenges the capital with the famous declaration, "It's between the two of us now!"
- Featured the sinister criminal mastermind *Vautrin* (*Jacques Collin*), an escaped convict who preaches cynical opportunism to young men and recurs across multiple volumes as an agent of subversion.
- Authored *Eugénie Grandet*, a study of provincial avarice centering on the tyrannical miser *Félix Grandet*, whose obsessive hoarding of gold ruins the romantic hopes of his devoted daughter.
- Chronicled the rise and fall of the naive poet *Lucien de Rubempré* in *Illusions perdues* (*Lost Illusions*), exposing the venality, corruption, and financial desperation underlying Parisian journalism and publishing.
- Explored philosophical themes in *La Peau de chagrin* (*The Wild Ass's Skin*), in which *Raphaël de Valentin* obtains a magical talisman that grants his desires while shrinking his lifespan.
- Penned the aesthetic fable *Le Chef-d'œuvre inconnu* (*The Unknown Masterpiece*), in which the painter *Frenhofer* spends a decade on a secret canvas that ends up a chaotic swirl containing only a single, perfectly rendered foot.

## connections

- [[realism]] — established the foundational principles of the movement through exhaustive physical descriptions, attention to material objects, and frank depictions of financial ambition.
- [[france]] — served as the primary setting and sociological focus of his work, capturing the transformation of Parisian society and the provinces.
- [[french-revolution]] — provided the historical turning point that dismantled the old hierarchy, unleashing the bourgeois capitalism and social mobility that drive his plots.
- [[charles-dickens]] — contemporaneous English counterpart who similarly captured urban life, memorable caricatures, and the struggles of the poor in Victorian [[england]].
- [[gustave-flaubert]] — younger French realist who drew upon Balzac's bourgeois subject matter while reacting against his prolific, unpolished style with rigorous aesthetic discipline.
- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — began his career translating *Eugénie Grandet* into Russian and drew inspiration from Balzac's depictions of criminal obsession, debt, and psychological torment.
- [[romanticism]] — influenced his early Gothic melodramas, sensational plotting, and depiction of monomaniacal passion and the occult.
