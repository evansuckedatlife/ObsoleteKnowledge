## summary

**Captain Nemo**, whose assumed Latin moniker translates to *"Captain Nobody"*, is a fictional antihero and scientific prodigy created by the French pioneering writer *Jules Verne*. Serving as the commander of the technologically advanced submarine *Nautilus*, he makes his monumental entrance in *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas* (1870) before receiving his definitive backstory and death in *The Mysterious Island* (1874). Driven by profound misanthropy, fierce anti-imperialist vengeance, and an absolute devotion to liberty, the character stands as one of the defining figures in the origins of modern *science fiction*.

## you gotta know

- Commands the electric submarine *Nautilus*, designed with unparalleled technological sophistication, navigating the world's oceans in complete self-sufficiency apart from human society.
- Adopts the Latin motto *"Mobilis in mobili"* (*"Moving within the moving element"*), expressing his longing for complete freedom amidst the uncontrollable sea.
- Holds captive the French marine biologist Professor *Pierre Aronnax*, his loyal servant *Conseil*, and the hot-tempered Canadian harpooner *Ned Land* after they survive a collision with the *Nautilus*.
- Originates in *The Mysterious Island* as an Indian prince named *Dakkar*, the son of a Raja of Bundelkhand, who harbored an unyielding hatred for the *British Empire* following the crushed 1857 Sepoy Mutiny.
- Originally conceived by *Jules Verne* as a Polish aristocrat seeking revenge against the Russian Empire for the brutal suppression of the January Uprising of 1863, an origin blocked by editorial intervention.
- Uses his salvaged undersea gold fortunes to fund oppressed people around the Mediterranean, secretly financing Greek insurgents fighting for independence against the Ottoman Empire during the Cretan Revolt.
- Plays haunting melodies on a magnificent pipe organ in his submarine's opulent salon, which is lined with masterpieces by *Raphael*, *Rembrandt*, and *Titian*, alongside a vast scientific library.
- Dies peacefully of old age inside his marooned vessel in a subterranean cavern on Lincoln Island, requesting that the marooned American castaways scuttle the *Nautilus* to serve as his watery tomb.

## connections

- [[science-fiction]] — established the quintessential prototype of the misunderstood technological genius operating on the frontiers of discovery.
- [[france]] — conceived and popularized by *Jules Verne* as part of his groundbreaking *Voyages extraordinaires* series.
- [[colonialism]] — embodies militant resistance against European imperialism, dedicating his life to combating imperial domination.
- [[censorship]] — publisher *Pierre-Jules Hetzel* enforced editorial control to alter his nationality, fearing a Polish rebel would alienate the lucrative Russian book market.
- [[romanticism]] — exemplifies the brooding, solitary Byronic hero who rejects human society and conventions for cosmic exile.
- [[british-empire]] — identifies British imperial conquest in India as the ultimate destroyer of his family, homeland, and peace.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — wields advanced electric propulsion, battery technology, and deep-sea diving gear far ahead of contemporary nineteenth-century engineering.
