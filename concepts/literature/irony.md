## summary

**Irony** is a rhetorical and literary mode in which the speaker or text implies meaning opposite to, or sharply different from, what is literally said, creating a gap between appearance and reality. It ranges from simple *sarcasm* (saying the opposite of what you mean) to dramatic irony (the audience knows something the characters do not) to situational irony (reality contradicts expectation). Irony has been essential to literature across genres and centuries, serving as both a tool for critique and a source of philosophical complexity.

## you gotta know

- *Verbal irony* occurs when what is said contradicts what is meant; *sarcasm* is irony used for mockery or contempt.
- *Dramatic irony* arises when the audience or reader knows more than the characters on stage; they see the full implications of the characters' words and actions.
- *Situational irony* occurs when reality inverts expectation: Oedipus kills his father while trying to prevent it; the weather refuses to cooperate with lovers' plans.
- Irony is central to *comedy*, which exploits incongruity and the audience's superior knowledge; it is also fundamental to *satire*, which uses ironic praise to expose folly.
- *Romantic irony* (early 19th-century) emphasises the gap between human aspiration and reality; the subject recognises the futility of desire yet persists in desiring.
- *Postmodern irony* is irony about irony: the simultaneous commitment to and detachment from meaning, sincerity and skepticism; a mode of engagement without earnestness.
- Excessive irony can collapse into cynicism: if everything is ironised, nothing is affirmed, and critique loses force.

## connections

- [[comedy]] — irony is essential to comic effect; the audience's sense of superiority creates laughter.
- [[satire]] — ironic praise is the primary weapon of satire; irony makes critique bite.
- [[postmodernism]] — irony became the dominant mode of postmodern art and literature.
- [[wit]] — verbal cleverness often depends on ironic or unexpected juxtaposition.
- [[rhetoric]] — the study of persuasion and language; irony is a fundamental rhetorical device.
- [[greek-drama]] — the ancient source of dramatic irony as a literary principle.
- [[sarcasm]] — the crude, personal version of irony; contempt made explicit through inversion.
