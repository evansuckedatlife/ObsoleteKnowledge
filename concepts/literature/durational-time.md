## summary

**Durational Time**, rooted in the philosophical concept of *durée* formulated by French thinker *Henri Bergson*, denotes subjective, continuous, and indivisible psychological temporality as experienced by human consciousness. Developed in late nineteenth- and early twentieth-century [[france]], the concept sharply contrasts internal lived experience with the artificial, spatialized ticking of mechanical clock time. The idea revolutionized narrative architecture across [[modernism]], providing the theoretical foundation for interior monologues and non-linear representations of human memory and experience.

## you gotta know

- Formulated philosophically by *Henri Bergson* in his 1889 doctoral dissertation *Time and Free Will* (*Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience*) and expanded in *Matter and Memory*.
- Argues that clock time falsely spatializes experience into separate, discrete units, whereas true conscious experience is an indivisible, qualitative, and continuously unfolding flow.
- Formed the aesthetic backbone of [[stream-of-consciousness]] writing, enabling authors to compress decades into a passing thought or expand a single moment across hundreds of pages.
- Heavily influenced *Marcel Proust*'s epic multivolume novel *In Search of Lost Time* (*À la recherche du temps perdu*), particularly through involuntary memory triggered by sensory impressions like the famous madeleine cake.
- Shaped *Virginia Woolf*'s distinct narrative structure in *Mrs Dalloway*, where the rigid striking of *Big Ben* repeatedly punctures the continuous internal impressions and memories of *Clarissa Dalloway* and *Septimus Warren Smith*.
- Underpins *James Joyce*'s *Ulysses*, which confines eighteen hours of external Dublin day to hundreds of pages tracing the wandering inner chronologies of *Leopold Bloom* and *Stephen Dedalus*.
- Influenced modern philosophical currents including phenomenological approaches to memory, existentialist ontology in [[existentialism]], and psychological explorations of trauma.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — the artistic movement that rejected linear nineteenth-century realism in favor of subjective internal temporality.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — the literary technique specifically developed to depict the unbroken stream of lived thoughts.
- [[virginia-woolf]] — the British novelist who counterposed the measured chimes of mechanical clocks with internal memory.
- [[james-joyce]] — the Irish modernist master who rendered epic psychological odysseys within the confines of a single day.
- [[france]] — the intellectual birthplace of *Bergson*'s philosophical critiques of mechanistic positivism.
- [[existentialism]] — the philosophical tradition that later built upon lived experiential reality over objective abstract models.
- [[dresden-bombing]] — an event whose traumatic aftershocks shattered linear chronology in works like *Slaughterhouse-Five*.
