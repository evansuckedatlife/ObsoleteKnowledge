## summary

**governessing** was the private employment of educated, middle-class women to teach children within affluent households, flourishing as a prominent social practice in nineteenth-century Britain. Situated uncomfortably between the servant class and the gentry, women in this profession endured acute social isolation, precarious economic dependence, and ambiguous domestic authority. The practice became a central preoccupation of Victorian literature, serving as an enduring symbol of female vulnerability and social mobility.

## you gotta know

- Represented virtually the sole respectable occupation available to impoverished, unmarried middle-class women in nineteenth-century England who had to earn their own livelihood.
- Placed women in an agonizing social limbo: educated and refined like their employers, yet wage-earning dependents excluded from both servant companionship and drawing-room society.
- Generated widespread moral anxiety about downward social mobility, leading to the creation of philanthropic relief organizations such as the Governesses' Benevolent Institution in London.
- Experienced directly by Charlotte, Anne, and Emily Brontë, whose grueling domestic employments profoundly shaped novels like *Jane Eyre* and *Agnes Grey*.
- Manifests famously in *Jane Eyre*, where the title heroine takes up a post at Thornfield Hall to teach Edward Rochester's ward, Adèle Varens.
- Forms the narrative pivot of William Makepeace Thackeray's *Vanity Fair*, where the scheming Becky Sharp exploits her initial position as a governess to infiltrate high society.
- Appears prominently in sensational and psychological fiction, notably in Henry James's novella *The Turn of the Screw*, where a naive governess at Bly confronts haunting spectral figures.

## connections

- [[england]] — the primary geographical and cultural setting where the Victorian governess system developed and dominated social discourse.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the economic upheaval that produced a prosperous new bourgeois class demanding domestic tutors while impoverishing unprotected middle-class families.
- [[realism]] — the nineteenth-century aesthetic mode dedicated to chronicling the exact socioeconomic and psychological realities of working women.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial domestic sphere where wealthy colonial fortunes funded country estates and fashionable private educations.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the genre that capitalized on isolated young governesses arriving at brooding country mansions harboring dark secrets.
- [[charles-dickens]] — the Victorian novelist who frequently attacked the mistreatment and marginalization of domestic educators across London.
