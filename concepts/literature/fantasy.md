## summary

**Fantasy** is a literary and imaginative genre that presents worlds, beings, and events governed by magical or supernatural principles rather than physical laws. Distinct from science fiction (which extrapolates from known science) and from magical realism (which embeds magic in our world), fantasy constructs complete alternate worlds with their own internal coherence. As a mode of storytelling, fantasy privileges wonder, enchantment, and the possible over the probable, and has roots reaching back to myth and fairy tale.

## you gotta know

- Fantasy as a formal literary genre solidified in the 20th century, though fantastical tales are ancient—myth, legend, and fairy tales predate the modern category but lack the self-aware genre consciousness.
- A "secondary world" is a complete imaginative universe with its own rules, geography, and history—the reader enters it fully, suspending disbelief at the threshold; this concept is central to modern fantasy theory.
- Magic systems in modern fantasy can be hard-defined (rule-governed, with costs and limitations) or soft (mysterious, poetic, less mechanistic); the choice reflects the writer's philosophical approach to wonder.
- Epic or "high fantasy" typically features a struggle between good and evil across a detailed world; urban or contemporary fantasy relocates magic to the modern present, grounding the marvelous in familiar geography.
- Fantasy allows exploration of psychological, moral, and spiritual themes through the distance and safety of the marvelous rather than the literal; allegory and symbol operate freely within fantasy's logic.
- The genre encompasses many subgenres: sword-and-sorcery, paranormal romance, cozy fantasy, portal fantasy, and others; the diversity reflects fantasy's capacity to absorb different narrative concerns and tones.

## connections

- [[j-r-r-tolkien]] — architect of the modern literary fantasy world as a complete imaginative system.
- [[the-lord-of-the-rings]] — the canonical modern fantasy epic that defined the genre's narrative scope and aesthetic.
- [[science-fiction]] — the complementary genre that explores the possible through scientific rather than magical extension.
- [[magical-realism]] — embeds the magical in realist settings, creating a different kind of marvelous encounter.
- [[myth]] — the ancient source from which fantasy draws its sense of the enchanted.
- [[fairy-tale]] — folkloric predecessor to modern fantasy; both traffic in magic and wonder.
- [[wonder]] — the emotional and philosophical substrate of fantasy, the capacity to be amazed by the impossible.
