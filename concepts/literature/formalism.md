## summary

**Formalism** (or **Russian Formalism**) was a pioneering movement in literary theory and criticism that emerged in Russia during the 1910s. Rather than viewing literature as a reflection of culture, sociology, or the author's personal psychology, formalists analyzed the literary text as an autonomous, intricately crafted assembly of specialized linguistic devices. By shifting scholarly inquiry from thematic interpretation to the mechanisms of poetic language and narrative construction, the movement revolutionized twentieth-century literary analysis and laid the groundwork for structuralism and modern narratology.

## you gotta know

- Introduced the concept of defamiliarization or estrangement (*ostranenie*), formulated by *Viktor Shklovsky* in his 1917 essay *Art as Technique*, which argued that the primary purpose of art is to overcome habitual, automatic perception by making the familiar seem strange.
- Established the vital distinction between *fabula* (the basic chronological sequence of raw events in a story) and *syuzhet* (the artistic plot structure, pacing, and narrative framing used to present those events to the audience).
- Defined the proper subject of literary scholarship as *literariness* (*literaturnost*), a term introduced by linguist *Roman Jakobson* to denote the formal, aesthetic features that distinguish poetic works from ordinary informative speech.
- Identified the artistic strategy of laying bare the device (*obnazhenie priyoma*), famously demonstrated in Shklovsky's analysis of *Laurence Sterne*'s *Tristram Shandy*, which he playfully labeled the most typical novel in world literature for exposing its own mechanics.
- Originated within two vibrant intellectual societies during the 1910s: the *Moscow Linguistic Circle*, established by Jakobson, and the St. Petersburg Society for the Study of Poetic Language, known by its Russian acronym *OPOJAZ*.
- Founded structural narrative analysis through *Vladimir Propp*'s seminal 1928 treatise *Morphology of the Folktale*, which identified thirty-one immutable plot functions and seven character spheres across Russian wonder tales.
- Classified literary motifs through *Boris Tomashevsky*, who separated bound motifs that cannot be omitted without disrupting story logic from free motifs that introduce non-essential stylistic color, atmosphere, and digression.
- Suffered political condemnation and suppression during the late 1920s in the [[soviet-union]], where state authorities denounced formalist autonomy as bourgeois escapism and enforced ideologically compliant socialist [[realism]], driving theorists into exile or silence.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — supplied the structural models and phonetic rigor that formalist scholars used to treat poetic language as an autonomous linguistic system.
- [[modernism]] — shared a core aesthetic preoccupation with experimental form, self-reflexivity, and the deliberate fragmentation of traditional narrative conventions.
- [[leo-tolstoy]] — cited by Shklovsky as the preeminent master of defamiliarization for depicting social institutions and an opera through uninitiated eyes, most famously in *Kholstomer*.
- [[detective-fiction]] — served as the quintessential genre for analyzing how narrative structure (*syuzhet*) deliberately withholds, misdirects, and rearranges chronological narrative facts (*fabula*).
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — championed deliberate formal craftsmanship and narrative compression in the [[short-story]], providing a model for viewing literature as an engineered artifact.
- [[soviet-union]] — dismantled formalist institutions and banned formalist publications under Stalinist cultural consolidation in favor of state-sanctioned socialist [[realism]].
- [[t-s-eliot]] — articulated parallel critical doctrines of poetic impersonality and objective form that mirrored formalist arguments and shaped Anglo-American *New Criticism*.
