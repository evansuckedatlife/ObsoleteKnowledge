## summary

**Tennessee Williams** (1911–1983) was an American playwright whose works transformed modern drama through psychologically intense explorations of desire, fragility, and Southern decay. His plays *A Streetcar Named Desire* and *The Glass Menagerie* rank among the greatest works of American theater, combining poetic language with unflinching portrayals of characters trapped by circumstance and their own limitations. His legacy defines American dramatic realism and the theatrical possibilities of emotional vulnerability.

## you gotta know

- *The Glass Menagerie* pioneered the "memory play" form, blurring the boundary between objective reality and subjective memory through expressionistic staging and Tom's narration.
- *A Streetcar Named Desire* depicts the collision between Blanche DuBois's genteel Southern past and Stanley Kowalski's brutal working-class present, culminating in psychological devastation.
- Williams's characters are often socially marginalized—unmarried women, failed artists, homosexual men—whose interior richness contrasts with their social powerlessness.
- His plays employ poetic language and symbolic imagery alongside domestic realism, creating a hybrid theatrical style that influenced generations of dramatists.
- Williams himself was gay at a time when homosexuality was criminalized and closeted; his plays frequently contain coded explorations of desire and masculine threat.
- His later career was marked by alcoholism, depression, and creative struggles, though he continued producing plays until his death.
- His work is foundational to the post-war American theater, defining a mode of psychological realism that prioritizes emotional truth over external action.
