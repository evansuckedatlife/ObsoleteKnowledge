## summary

**Animal Farm** is *George Orwell's* 1945 allegorical novella—a satirical fable in which farm animals rebel against their human master and establish a utopian society, only to have their revolution betrayed and corrupted by the pigs who seize power. Each character and event parallels the Russian Revolution and the rise of Stalinism; it is Orwell's bitterly clever warning that revolutionary ideals can be perverted into tyranny by those who capture the narrative and rewrite history.

## you gotta know

- Published in 1945 after World War II; Orwell wrote it during the war but faced resistance because the Soviet Union was an Allied power; the allegory was considered too inflammatory.
- The animals of Manor Farm rebel against Mr. Jones (Tsar Nicholas) and establish Animal Farm based on *Seven Commandments*—but the pigs, especially Napoleon and Snowball, quickly assume leadership and begin to concentrate power.
- Napoleon (Joseph Stalin) uses propaganda and violence to eliminate his rival Snowball (Leon Trotsky), then rewrites history and the Commandments to justify his rule and privileges.
- The narrative is deceptively simple—farm animals talking, working, rebelling—but every detail is coded: the pigs' ascent, the corruption of ideals, the falsification of history, the complicity of the other animals.
- Final commandment: the pigs alter the original "All animals are equal" to "All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others"—the fable's most piercing political irony.
- Style: Orwell uses a naive narrator (who doesn't fully grasp events) and fairy-tale tone; the gap between surface innocence and dark reality creates devastating satire.
- Influence: *Animal Farm* became the most widely read anti-totalitarian allegory of the 20th century; required reading in schools as a warning against tyranny and propaganda.

## connections

- [[george-orwell]] — the author; his most bitterly satirical work and most perfectly executed fable.
- [[nineteen-eighty-four]] — Orwell's fuller, more direct dystopian vision; where *Animal Farm* is allegory, *1984* is brutal realism.
- [[political-satire]] — *Animal Farm* is satire at its finest; it mocks totalitarian ideology by embodying it in animal characters.
- [[totalitarianism]] — the theme: how revolutionary ideals are corrupted into totalitarian control and propaganda.
- [[soviet-union]] — the historical target; the Bolshevik Revolution and Stalinist regime are the fable's referent.
- [[cold-war]] — the ideological conflict *Animal Farm* addresses; the book became a Cold War text, weaponized against communist ideology.
- [[allegory]] — the form; every character, event, and detail maps to historical counterparts; the power of allegory is its simultaneity—fable and history at once.
