## summary

**Environmental collapse** is a prominent literary and speculative concept depicting the catastrophic breakdown of regional or planetary biospheres caused by human exploitation, pollution, or technological hubris. While foreshadowed by nineteenth-century anxieties over industrial smoke and resource depletion, the theme crystallized during the twentieth century amidst nuclear threats, pesticide awareness, and anthropogenic climate change. In contemporary literature and critical discourse, it forms the ideological core of climate fiction (cli-fi), apocalyptic science fiction, and ecocritical examinations of industrialized civilization.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a cultural anxiety during the [[industrial-revolution]], inspiring Romantic resistance to "dark Satanic Mills" and the mechanization of pastoral landscapes.
- Accelerated dramatically in twentieth-century speculative thought following the atomic bombings of World War II and subsequent [[cold-war]] fears of nuclear winter.
- Popularized in mid-century American discourse by Rachel Carson's groundbreaking 1962 nonfiction work *Silent Spring*, which exposed industrial chemical devastation.
- Explored in ecological [[science-fiction]] classics such as Frank Herbert's *Dune*, which depicted arid planetary engineering and fragile ecosystem balances.
- Central to New Wave speculative literature of the 1960s and 1970s, exemplified by J.G. Ballard's disaster novels portraying world-drowning floods and planetary droughts.
- Articulated in contemporary climate fiction through post-apocalyptic drought and resource starvation, such as Paolo Bacigalupi's *The Water Knife* and Cormac McCarthy's *The Road*.
- Frequently deployed in cinematic and literary dystopias as the fundamental backdrop compelling interstellar migration, authoritarian rationing, or civilizational warfare.

## connections

- [[science-fiction]] — the principal literary genre modeling future biospheric degradation, resource wars, and planetary survival.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical transformation whose fossil-fuel extraction and mass manufacturing catalyzed modern environmental destruction.
- [[romanticism]] — aesthetic and philosophical rebellion that championed wild nature against industrial pollution and urban sprawl.
- [[cold-war]] — historical period whose nuclear arms race produced global anxieties regarding sudden planetary biosphere annihilation.
- [[realism]] — nineteenth-century movement that documented the grim urban squalor, smog, and social decay of industrialized cities.
- [[modernism]] — early twentieth-century literary movement whose wasteland tropes captured ecological and civilizational barrenness.
- [[united-states]] — geographic and geopolitical center for major twentieth-century environmental literature and political conservation movements.
