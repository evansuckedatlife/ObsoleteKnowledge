## summary

**Good Deeds** is the central allegorical character in the late fifteenth-century morality play *Everyman*, personifying the protagonist's accumulated acts of charity and virtue. When *Everyman* is summoned by *Death* to render a final account before God, this figure is initially discovered lying bound and enfeebled upon the earth by the weight of his sins. Restored to vigor through spiritual purgation, the character serves as the sole companion willing to descend into the tomb to ensure salvation, epitomizing the orthodox medieval doctrine of justification.

## you gotta know

- Introduced in the English morality play *Everyman* as an allegorical personification lying helpless and bound on the cold earth, chained down by the protagonist's vices.
- Explains to *Everyman* that although she would gladly accompany him on his journey toward *Death*, his negligence has left her too weak to stand or walk.
- Introduces her sister *Knowledge*, who guides *Everyman* to the holy man *Confession* to begin the process of redemptive mortification.
- Regains full strength and rises joyfully from the ground after *Everyman* scourges himself with the whip of penance to atone for his transgressions.
- Provides *Everyman* with the garment of contrition, also called the sorrowful robe of tears, which cleanses his spiritual balance sheet before God.
- Remains loyal at the lip of the grave after *Beauty*, *Strength*, *Discretion*, and [[five-wits]] flee in terror at the sight of bodily extinction.
- Accompanies *Everyman* down into the earth, where an angel receives their transfigured souls to grant everlasting redemption.
- Reflects the pre-Reformation Catholic doctrine of soteriology, asserting that faith must be realized through active charitable works to achieve eternal life.
