## summary

**Count Alexander Rostov**, also known in the narrative as **Aleksandr** or **Alexander Rostov**, is the fictional protagonist and central consciousness of Amor Towles's 2016 historical novel *A Gentleman in Moscow*. Sentenced by an emergency Bolshevik tribunal in 1922 to spend the remainder of his life confined to the Hotel Metropol in Moscow, he witnesses more than three decades of dramatic political and social upheaval across the [[soviet-union]]. An embodiment of Old World elegance, cultivated wit, and aristocratic honor, he resists despair by adapting to domestic confinement and seeking purpose through service, friendship, and quiet defiance. His moral journey explores how personal integrity and human affection can flourish even when personal freedom has been stripped away by totalitarian authority.

## you gotta know

- Condemned to house arrest inside the grand Hotel Metropol on June 21, 1922, escaping execution because an influential revolutionary poem from 1913 was attributed to his name.
- Relocated from his lavish third-floor suite to an austere, cramped attic servant's room on the sixth floor, where he reconstructs a dignified life around books, memory, and routine.
- Embraces a guiding life philosophy adapted from Montaigne, insisting that if a man does not master his circumstances, he is inevitably bound to be mastered by them.
- Befriends the precocious nine-year-old Nina Kulikova, who possesses a brass passkey unlocking every door in the hotel, introducing him to the building's hidden passages, rooftops, and forgotten storerooms.
- Enters into a long-lasting, devoted romance with the glamorous Soviet actress Anna Urbanova, whose visits and enduring loyalty sustain his spirit across decades of ideological purges and war.
- Accepts employment as the headwaiter of the Boyarsky restaurant, collaborating with chef Emile Zhukovsky and maître d' Andrey Durasov in an esteemed culinary partnership dubbed the Triumvirate.
- Fosters Nina's daughter Sofia after Nina disappears into the Siberian labor camps, raising the young girl into a virtuoso pianist and coordinating her dangerous defection to the West during a concert in Paris.
- Learns that the subversive poem *Where Is It Now?*, which originally saved his life before the tribunal, was actually authored by his idealistic university friend Mishka Mindich.
