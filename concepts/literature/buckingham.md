## summary

**Buckingham**, representing the historical figure of *Henry Stafford*, 2nd **Duke of Buckingham**, is a major theatrical character in *William Shakespeare*'s historical [[tragedy]] *Richard III*. Functioning as the Machiavellian co-conspirator and political strategist who engineers Richard's ascension to the English throne, he serves as a paramount study in complicity and political betrayal. When his conscience balks at child murder, he falls out of favor, is executed, and returns to haunt Richard on the eve of the Battle of Bosworth Field.

## you gotta know

- Prominent figure in *William Shakespeare*'s *Richard III*, depicted as Richard of Gloucester's chief advisor and confederate during the Wars of the Roses.
- Masterminds the political campaign that discredits the legitimacy of King Edward IV's offspring and manipulates London's citizenry into offering Richard the crown.
- Stage-manages the famous pious spectacle at Baynard's Castle, presenting Richard meditating between two clergymen with prayer book in hand.
- Promises of rich estates, specifically the earldom of Hereford, incentivize his treacherous allegiance to Gloucester's faction in [[england]].
- Breaks with the tyrant when Richard demands the assassination of the two young Princes in the Tower, hesitating and requesting time to reflect.
- Flees court in terror when he realises he has lost the king's favor, leading a failed rebellion in Wales alongside Henry Tudor's sympathisers.
- Captured following heavy storms that scatter his troops, condemned to death without trial, and beheaded at Salisbury on All Souls' Day.
- Appears as a vengeful phantom before the Battle of Bosworth Field, cursing Richard with despair while blessing Richmond's righteous cause.
