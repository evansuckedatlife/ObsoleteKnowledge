## summary

**Jealousy** is the painful emotion arising from fear of loss, real or imagined, often coupled with suspicion, possessiveness, and a desire to punish the perceived rival or the beloved. In literature, jealousy is a foundational tragic passion that destroys individuals and societies, turning love into rage and reason into madness. It appears across genres and centuries, from *Greek* drama to *Shakespeare* to the modernist novel, always as a force that unmakes the self and corrupts relationships.

## you gotta know

- Jealousy differs from *envy*: jealousy fears loss of what one possesses; *envy* desires what another has.
- In *Shakespeare's* *Othello*, jealousy is the driving passion; *Iago* manufactures suspicion, and *Othello* transforms from a rational general into a deluded murderer consumed by jealous rage.
- Jealousy in classical drama often appears as *divine punishment* or the working-out of a curse: the gods make mortals jealous to orchestrate their downfall.
- *Tolstoy's* *The Kreutzer Sonata* takes jealousy as the foundation of sexuality and marriage itself: possession, suspicion, and violence inherent in the erotic bond.
- Psychologically, jealousy threatens identity: it reveals the self as contingent, dependent on another's faithfulness, vulnerable to abandonment.
- Jealousy often escalates without external cause; imagination and fear generate "evidence" of infidelity, making jealousy self-fulfilling and cyclical.
- In modernist literature, jealousy becomes a mode of epistemological anxiety: how can one *know* another's fidelity or desire? Certainty is impossible.

## connections

- [[othello]] — the canonical tragedy of jealousy; the play most often cited as the definitive treatment of the passion.
- [[the-kreutzer-sonata]] — *Tolstoy's* novella making jealousy the core of marital sexuality and possession.
- [[tragedy]] — jealousy is one of the primary tragic passions; it corrupts the tragic protagonist.
- [[passion]] — an intense, irrational emotion that overwhelms reason and drives action.
- [[desire]] — jealousy is desire turned inward, transformed into fear of loss.
- [[marriage-and-sexuality]] — jealousy is intrinsic to literary treatment of intimate relationships.
- [[iago]] — the character who manufactures jealousy as a weapon; the dramatisation of jealousy's destructive potential.
