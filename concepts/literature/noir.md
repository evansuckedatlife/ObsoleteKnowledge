## summary

**Noir** is an aesthetic and narrative tradition in detective and crime fiction characterized by moral ambiguity, cynicism, and urban despair. Originating in 1940s American pulp fiction and cinema, it depicts a world of corruption where protagonists navigate crime-ridden cities with a fatalistic worldview. The style became one of the most influential modes of storytelling, defining a sensibility that extends far beyond its historical moment.

## you gotta know

- The term "noir" was retroactively applied to 1940s-50s films and fiction by French critics, borrowed from the *Serie Noire* pulp imprint.
- Hard-boiled detective fiction by writers like *Raymond Chandler* and *Dashiell Hammett* established the literary foundation for noir's cynical voice and morally compromised protagonists.
- Visual noir in cinema featured shadowed cinematography, low-key lighting, Dutch angles, and urban decay—formal techniques that encoded philosophical pessimism.
- The noir protagonist is often a flawed detective or criminal who operates in a corrupt system where the line between law and lawlessness is blurred.
- Women in noir are frequently femmes fatales or tragic figures, reinforcing the genre's misogynistic worldview alongside its fatalism.
- Economic desperation and class conflict permeate noir narratives, reflecting anxieties of post-war American capitalism and inequality.
- Noir influence persists across modern detective fiction, film, television, and gaming—a template for depicting cynical protagonists in corrupt worlds.
