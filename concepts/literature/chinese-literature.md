## summary

**Chinese literature** represents one of the longest continuous literary traditions in human history, spanning more than three millennia from archaic divination inscriptions and early classical poetry to monumental vernacular novels and modern prose. Rooted in the philosophical foundations of Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism, the tradition established enduring forms such as classical verse and historical narrative that deeply shaped East Asian culture. In the Ming and Qing dynasties, vernacular prose flourished with the compilation of the Classic Chinese Novels, producing complex social panoramas and allegories that remain touchstones of world literature.

## you gotta know

- The tradition begins with the *Classic of Poetry* (*Shijing*), an anthology of folk songs, hymns, and court odes compiled during the Zhou dynasty and revered as one of the Five Confucian Classics.
- Classical poetry reached its artistic apex during the Tang dynasty, led by celebrated poets *Li Bai*, renowned for his romanticism and Daoist imagery, and *Du Fu*, celebrated for his moral conscience and social realism.
- The Song dynasty transformed poetic practice through the lyric *ci* form, characterized by uneven line lengths set to musical tunes, mastered by figures such as *Su Shi* and the female poet *Li Qingzhao*.
- The Four Great Classical Novels of vernacular Chinese literature comprise *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*, *Water Margin*, *Journey to the West*, and *Dream of the Red Chamber* (*Honglou Meng*).
- *Dream of the Red Chamber*, composed by *Cao Xueqin* during the Qing dynasty, is celebrated for its encyclopedic realism, tragic romance between *Jia Baoyu* and *Lin Daiyu*, and profound psychological insight into the decline of an aristocratic family.
- The satirical novel *The Scholars* (*Rulin Waishi*) by *Wu Jingzi* offered a biting critique of the imperial civil service examinations and the hypocrisy of the literati class.
- The early twentieth century saw radical transformation during the May Fourth Movement and New Culture Movement, when reformers like *Lu Xun* pioneered modern vernacular short fiction with works such as *Diary of a Madman* and *The True Story of Ah Q*.

## connections

- [[poetry]] — foundational artistic medium that defined the civil service examinations and elite scholarly expression.
- [[satire]] — comic mode deployed by Qing masters and modern reformers like *Lu Xun* to critique societal decay.
- [[realism]] — aesthetic current seen in Tang social verse, late imperial family sagas, and modern vernacular stories.
- [[japan]] — neighbouring civilization whose classical literary traditions were heavily influenced by Chinese script and poetic models.
- [[linguistics]] — study of written characters, classical syntax, and the shift from literary *wenyan* to vernacular *baihua*.
