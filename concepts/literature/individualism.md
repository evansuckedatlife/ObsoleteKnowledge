## summary

**Individualism** is a philosophical and cultural emphasis on the autonomy, rights, and agency of the individual rather than the collective, family, or state. As a literary and intellectual force, it emerged from *Enlightenment* thought and found powerful expression in *Romantic* poetry and American frontier mythology. In literature, individualism appears as a celebration of personal genius, heroic self-invention, and resistance to social constraint, though also as a source of alienation, egoism, and conflict.

## you gotta know

- *Enlightenment* philosophy grounded individualism in natural rights and reason; each person possesses inalienable dignity and the capacity for self-determination.
- *Romantic* poets made individualism a central aesthetic: the genius, the visionary, the rebel—figures who transcend conventional morality and social order through imagination and authenticity.
- American frontier mythology—the self-made settler, the pioneer—became the cultural apotheosis of individualism: a figure carving a new world through will and courage.
- *Thoreau's* *Walden* is a canonical text of literary individualism: withdrawal from society to achieve spiritual and intellectual autonomy.
- Individualism in literature often stands in tension with community, duty, and tradition; conflicts between personal desire and social obligation recur across genres.
- 19th-century American literature (particularly *Transcendentalism*) elevated individualism as a moral and spiritual ideal.
- Modernist and postmodern literature often inverted this: the isolated individual not as hero but as alienated, fragmented, adrift in a meaningless world.
- *Ralph Waldo Emerson* and other *Transcendentalists* preached "self-reliance": the individual conscience supersedes institutional authority, and nature reveals truth directly to the attuned soul.
- The American myth of the self-made individual—embodied in figures like Benjamin Franklin and the frontier settler—masks systemic inequality; later American literature (especially African American and immigrant narratives) exposes individualism as a false promise for those excluded from its ideal.

## connections

- [[romanticism]] — the movement that made individualism a central aesthetic principle.
- [[american-frontier]] — the historical and mythological embodiment of individualism in American culture.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical tradition that grounded individualism in reason and natural rights.
- [[transcendentalism]] — an American philosophical movement that sacralised individual intuition and nature.
- [[existentialism]] — a 20th-century philosophy that radicalised individualism through radical freedom and responsibility.
- [[american-identity]] — individualism as a defining feature of how Americans understand themselves culturally and politically.
- [[thoreau]] — the writer most emblematic of literary individualism and voluntary withdrawal.
