## summary

**As You Like It** is a pastoral comedy written by [[william-shakespeare]] around 1599 and first published in the *First Folio* of 1623. Set primarily in the *Forest of Arden*, the play chronicles the flight of the witty heroine *Rosalind* after she is banished from court by her usurping uncle, Duke Frederick. Disguised as the young shepherd *Ganymede*, she journeys into the pastoral green world, tests the devotion of her ardent suitor *Orlando*, and counsels fellow exiles on the nature of love. Renowned for its subversion of conventional gender roles, brilliant courtly satire, and celebrated philosophical monologues, it stands as an enduring masterpiece of the [[english-renaissance]] stage.

## you gotta know

- Features the melancholic traveler *Jaques* delivering the famous "All the world's a stage" monologue in Act II, which divides human existence into seven distinct ages: the infant, schoolboy, lover, soldier, justice, pantaloon, and second childishness.
- Centers on the intricate courtship of *Rosalind* and *Orlando*, in which *Rosalind*, while disguised as the youth *Ganymede*, persuades her lovesick suitor to woo her as if she were his beloved mistress in order to cure him of love.
- Catalyzed when *Orlando* overcomes the royal wrestler *Charles* in open combat, provoking the enmity of Duke Frederick and his brother *Oliver*, which drives *Rosalind* to flee into exile alongside her cousin *Celia* and the jester *Touchstone*.
- Borrows its primary plot from *Thomas Lodge's* prose pastoral romance *Rosalynde, Euphues Golden Legacie* (1590), though Shakespeare invented key philosophical commentators not present in the original novella, notably *Jaques* and *Touchstone*.
- Deconstructs pastoral romanticism by contrasting Duke Senior's idealized praise of exile in nature against physical suffering from winter weather, the economic precarity of the shepherd *Corin*, and the caustic, realist mockery of *Touchstone*.
- Resolves its romantic complications through a mass wedding ceremony presided over by *Hymen*, the classical god of marriage, who descends to unite four couples and restore political legitimacy to the ducal court.
- Concludes with an unconventional epilogue delivered by *Rosalind*, who steps out of character to address the audience directly, playfully drawing attention to the early modern theatrical convention where male actors performed all female roles.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — playwright whose golden period of romantic comedy culminated in this inventive pastoral exploration.
- [[english-renaissance]] — literary era whose thriving commercial theatre culture enabled experiments with disguise and genre.
- [[theatrum-mundi]] — classical motif of life as theatre, given its most famous expression in Jaques's monologue.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical tradition anticipating the play's emphasis on social performance, identity, and role-playing.
- [[hamlet]] — tragedy written shortly afterward that shares the play's preoccupation with melancholy, acting, and death.
- [[satire]] — comic mode used by Touchstone and Jaques to dismantle romantic sentimentality and courtly pretensions.
- [[poetry]] — artistic medium showcased when Orlando pins his romantic verses upon the barks of woodland trees.
- [[england]] — home country whose native folklore and Warwickshire woodlands inspired Shakespeare's conception of Arden.
