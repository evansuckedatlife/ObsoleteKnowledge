## summary

**J.R.R. Tolkien** (1892–1973) was an English philologist, medievalist, and author who created *Middle-earth* and authored *The Lord of the Rings* and *The Hobbit*, works that essentially defined modern fantasy literature. A Catholic convert and Oxford academic, he developed an entire secondary world with its own languages, histories, and mythologies, grounding his fiction in scholarly rigour and deep knowledge of Northern European mythology and medieval literature. His work elevated fantasy from children's literature to a serious literary art form.

## you gotta know

- Tolkien was a philologist and medievalist scholar; his fiction grew from linguistic and mythological interests rather than pure imagination.
- *The Hobbit* (1937) began as a story for his children, featuring *Bilbo Baggins* and his quest to reclaim a dragon's gold, establishing many conventions of heroic fantasy.
- *The Lord of the Rings* (1954–1955), his masterwork, is a trilogy epic of unprecedented scope, following *Frodo* Baggins through *Middle-earth* as he seeks to destroy a ring of power.
- Tolkien created an entirely constructed mythology: *The Silmarillion* contains the history of the First Age, the creation myth and fall of the Elves, and the theological framework of his world.
- His languages (especially *Elvish*) were constructed with linguistic discipline; they were not mere ornamentation but the expressive core of his creative vision.
- Tolkien consciously worked in the tradition of medieval literature and Norse mythology, embedding allusions and structures from *Beowulf*, *The Kalevala*, and Germanic sagas.
- His work influenced virtually all subsequent fantasy literature; the archetype of the quest, the fellowship, the dark lord, the magic ring—all became standard because of Tolkien.
