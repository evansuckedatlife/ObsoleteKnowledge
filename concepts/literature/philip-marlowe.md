## summary

**Philip Marlowe** is the private detective protagonist of seven novels by *Raymond Chandler*, beginning with *The Big Sleep* (1939). A cynical, honorable detective prowling Los Angeles' corrupt underworld, Marlowe defined the American hard-boiled detective archetype—tough, witty, impoverished, scrupulously moral in a morally compromised world. His investigations uncover Hollywood corruption, police brutality, and the moral rot beneath the city's glittering surface.

## you gotta know

- Created by *Raymond Chandler* and appearing in seven novels (*The Big Sleep*, *Farewell, My Lovely*, *The High Window*, *The Lady in the Lake*, *The Little Sister*, *The Long Goodbye*, *Playback*) and numerous short stories.
- Typical Marlowe setup: he is hired to find something (usually a person), discovers a murder, navigates police obstruction and threats, and solves the crime while remaining morally intact, even as the case reveals systemic corruption.
- Marlowe's code: he works for small fees, turns down bribes, protects clients from legal consequences where conscience allows, and respects women while noting their manipulation—a feminized honor in a masculinized profession.
- His relationship to Los Angeles is central: the city is not a backdrop but a character—sprawling, stratified, violent, seductive. Marlowe is the consciousness that sees through its facades.
- Dialogue as characterization: Marlowe's voice is sardonic, precise, self-aware; his wisecracks and observations establish his integrity and intelligence; he talks his way through situations, talking revealing truth.
- The Long Goodbye (1953), Chandler's final and most ambitious Marlowe novel, deepens psychological complexity and questions Marlowe's own moral compromises; it is considered his masterpiece.
- Marlowe influenced subsequent detective fiction and the noir tradition; he became the template for the honest detective in a corrupt system.

## connections

- [[raymond-chandler]] — Marlowe's creator, who used the detective to explore Los Angeles and moral ambiguity in American society.
- [[the-big-sleep]] — Marlowe's first case; the novel that introduced him and the hard-boiled formula.
- [[los-angeles]] — Marlowe's city; he is inseparable from its sprawl, corruption, and noir atmosphere.
- [[detective-fiction]] — Marlowe is a landmark in the genre; the hard-boiled detective at his most articulate and principled.
- [[noir]] — the aesthetic and moral universe of Marlowe's world; cynicism, corruption, femmes fatales, moral ambiguity.
- [[hard-boiled-fiction]] — Marlowe is the American hard-boiled detective; cynical, action-oriented, wise to the world.
- [[moral-ambiguity]] — Marlowe operates in a world where right and wrong are unclear, and his integrity is tested constantly.
