## summary

**Film genre** refers to the structural categorization of narrative cinema based on recurring formulas, stylistic codes, visual iconography, and thematic conventions recognized by both creators and audiences. Developed during the classical Hollywood studio era of the early-to-mid twentieth century, genre systems allowed studios to industrialize production and manage spectator expectations through established formulas. Understanding cinematic taxonomy is critical for analyzing narrative theory, ideological mythmaking, and the dynamic adaptation of literary traditions into visual media.

## you gotta know

- Film theorist *Rick Altman* formulated the influential semantic/syntactic model of genre, separating a genre's surface elements (costumes, settings, props) from its deeper grammatical structures and ideological relations.
- French film theorist *André Bazin* famously described the American Western as the cinema's purest realization of collective mythmaking and historic self-mythologizing.
- The film noir cycle of the 1940s and 1950s adapted hard-boiled [[detective-fiction]] using German Expressionist chiaroscuro lighting, moral ambiguity, and fatalistic voice-over narration.
- Horror cinema directly inherits structural conventions and uncanny motifs from nineteenth-century [[gothic-literature]], emphasizing terror, haunted architectural spaces, and the grotesque.
- Melodrama, historically denigrated as women's pictures, was re-evaluated by feminist film theorists like *Laura Mulvey* and *Linda Williams* for expressing domestic entrapment and emotional excess under patriarchy.
- Cinematic [[science-fiction]] developed distinct aesthetic conventions during the [[cold-war]], shifting from 1950s atomic mutant parables to high-concept speculative allegories exploring artificial intelligence and social engineering.
- Genre revisionism and pastiche became dominant in New Hollywood and postmodern cinema, where directors systematically deconstructed traditional mythologies through hybridity and subversion.

## connections

- [[realism]] — provides the stylistic antithesis against which stylized genre cinema operates and periodically negotiates for dramatic authenticity.
- [[romanticism]] — supplies foundational tropes of heroism, sublime nature, and individualism that permeate classic adventure films and Westerns.
- [[detective-fiction]] — supplied the primary literary templates for crime cinema, film noir, and modern procedural thrillers.
- [[gothic-literature]] — provided essential narrative archetypes, dread atmospheres, and psychological monsters directly adopted by cinematic horror.
- [[science-fiction]] — represents one of cinema's most visually technical genres, examining technological advancement and philosophical crisis through speculative visual worlds.
- [[feminist-speculative-fiction]] — frequently intersects with cinematic adaptation to interrogate gender hierarchies and reproductive dystopias on screen.
