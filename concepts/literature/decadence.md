## summary

**Decadence** is a late-nineteenth-century literary and artistic movement that celebrated refined artifice, sensory indulgence, aestheticism, and the beauty of decay while rejecting utilitarian morality. Emerging in *France* and spreading to *England*, the movement defiantly reclaimed a derogatory label used by hostile critics to describe cultural decline, turning it into a badge of sophisticated rebellion. By prizing style over didactic substance, it served as a vital evolutionary bridge linking late [[romanticism]] to early twentieth-century [[modernism]].

## you gotta know

- The movement traced its spiritual origins to *Charles Baudelaire's* 1857 poetry collection *Les Fleurs du mal*, which discovered sublime aesthetic pleasure in urban melancholy, corruption, and vice.
- The definitive prose manifesto of the sensibility was *Joris-Karl Huysmans's* 1884 novel *À rebours* (*Against Nature*), chronicling the reclusive aristocrat *Des Esseintes* as he creates an artificial sensory sanctuary.
- In Britain, the movement flourished in the eighteen-nineties under the leadership of *Oscar Wilde* and the artists associated with the avant-garde quarterly *The Yellow Book*.
- *Oscar Wilde's* 1890 novel *The Picture of Dorian Gray* directly embodies Decadent philosophy, depicting a young protagonist corrupted by sensual exploration and an influential French "yellow book."
- Writers of the movement deliberately elevated synthetic artifice, exotic perfumes, rare gems, and hothouse flowers over the simplicity of the natural world.
- Recurring themes included spiritual exhaustion, languid boredom (*ennui*), neurosis, sexual ambiguity, and morbid fascination with ancient imperial decline, particularly late Rome and Byzantium.
- The movement favored an ornate, highly stylized prose and poetic vocabulary enriched with rare archaisms, precious neologisms, and synesthetic metaphors.
- British Decadence suffered a devastating public collapse in 1895 following the trials and imprisonment of *Oscar Wilde*, after which its stylistic experiments dispersed into emerging Symbolism.

## connections

- [[romanticism]] — the preceding literary epoch whose fascination with melancholy, the macabre, and subjective passion was intensified and aestheticized by Decadent writers.
- [[modernism]] — the twentieth-century movement that absorbed Decadence's formal experimentation, fragmented psychology, urban alienation, and rejection of Victorian sentimentality.
- [[poetry]] — the essential artistic medium through which French masters like *Paul Verlaine*, *Arthur Rimbaud*, and *Stéphane Mallarmé* refined Decadent musicality.
- [[france]] — the European center where the movement's core philosophy and aesthetic rebellions against bourgeois positivism were first formulated.
- [[england]] — the Victorian society where the aesthetic movement clashed dramatically with imperial moral earnestness, industrial capitalism, and puritanical standards.
- [[gothic-literature]] — the darker narrative tradition sharing Decadence's fascination with ancestral doom, haunted psychological states, and forbidden sensory experiments.
