## summary

**Flash Fiction**, also known as microfiction, sudden fiction, or postcard fiction, is a literary form characterized by extreme narrative brevity, typically spanning fewer than one thousand words. While short fables and parables date back to antiquity, the modern form flourished through twentieth-century avant-garde experiments that stripped prose to its barest aesthetic essence. Demanding rigorous narrative economy, the form relies on compressed imagery, implied subtext, and swift structural turns to evoke complete psychological and thematic arcs.

## you gotta know

- Defined as a fictional narrative of extreme brevity, usually capped at one thousand words, with specific subgenres like drabbling limited to exactly one hundred words.
- Championed in Latin American literature through celebrated short texts, such as the famous one-sentence narrative *El Dinosaurio* by *Augusto Monterroso*.
- Traced historically to classical fables, parables, and nineteenth-century prose poems crafted by authors experimenting with dense poetic compression.
- Influenced by the critical theory of [[edgar-allan-poe]], whose doctrine of the single effect in short narratives demanded absolute unity of tone and narrative economy.
- Paralleled by the prose epiphanies of [[james-joyce]], who recorded tiny luminous snapshots of Dublin street scenes before integrating them into *Stephen Hero*.
- Anticipated by modernist short story cycles such as *In Our Time* by *Ernest Hemingway*, featuring vignette-length interchapters that capture visceral wartime shocks.
- Practiced in the contemporary era by authors such as *Lydia Davis*, whose sparse, philosophical sketches dismantle domestic situations and linguistic conventions.
- Associated apocryphally with the six-word legend about baby shoes for sale, an urban literary anecdote showcasing maximum emotive impact via omission.

## connections

- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — the foundational American theorist who established the necessity of single effect and deliberate compression in short prose.
- [[james-joyce]] — the pioneer whose early epiphany fragments refined the craft of encapsulating psychological revelation within miniature sketches.
- [[flann-o-brien]] — the satirical innovator who deployed absurdist vignettes, parody items, and microscopic parables throughout his journalistic columns.
- [[modernism]] — the literary movement whose emphasis on fragmentation and formal experimentation legitimized ultra-short prose forms.
- [[realism]] — the movement against whose conventional narrative plots concise sudden fiction often reacted by spotlighting isolated mundane truths.
- [[satire]] — the rhetorical genre that utilizes micro-narratives and miniature parables to puncture intellectual pretension and political hypocrisy.
- [[poetry]] — the neighboring discipline whose attention to rhythm, cadence, and line-level compression directly informs sudden prose.
