## summary

**Imagism** was an early 20th-century modernist movement in poetry that demanded precise imagery, concision, and the abolition of 19th-century poetic conventions. Founded and theorised by *Ezra Pound* around 1912, it championed the direct presentation of images without rhetorical ornament or emotional commentary. Though the movement itself was short-lived (roughly 1912–1917), its principles profoundly shaped modernist and contemporary poetry.

## you gotta know

- *Ezra Pound* formulated the core doctrine: "Direct treatment of the thing"; no excess words; every word must earn its place.
- Heavy influence from *haiku* and East Asian poetry, which prized economy and image over narrative or discursive meaning.
- Imagism rebelled against the late-*Romantic* tradition of elaborate description, didacticism, and sentimentality; each poem was a single, crystallised moment of perception.
- The *haiku* form's structure—an intense image rendered in the fewest words—became the implicit template for imagist aesthetics.
- Major imagist poets included *Pound*, *Amy Lowell*, and *H.D.* (*Hilda Doolittle*), though their differing visions led to quick factional splits.
- Imagism was never a large movement, but it redirected the entire energies of modernist verse: the principle of sharp, unadorned imagery became fundamental to 20th-century poetry.
- The movement published chiefly in the periodical *Poetry* and collections like *Des Imagistes* (1914), which circulated the aesthetic across the English-speaking literary world.
- *Amy Lowell* took over leadership of the movement after *Pound*'s departure and broader artistic interests pulled him toward other experiments like *Vorticism*, expanding the imagist circle and extending its influence.
- The imagist principle—"no ornament, no rhetoric, the image is the thing"—outlasted the movement itself, becoming a default assumption of modern and contemporary poetry.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — the broader movement of which imagism was an avant-garde faction.
- [[ezra-pound]] — the movement's chief theorist and strategist.
- [[haiku]] — the Eastern form that influenced imagist principles of concision and image.
- [[poetry]] — the art form imagism sought to revolutionise through purity and economy.

- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — another modernist technique emphasising direct perception over conventional narration.
- [[amy-lowell]] — a major imagist poet and publisher who promoted and eventually led the movement after Pound's departure.
