## summary

**Los Angeles** functions in literature—particularly in noir and hard-boiled detective fiction—as more than a setting: it is a character in itself, embodying American sprawl, moral corruption, social stratification, and the clash between wealth and desperation. [[raymond-chandler]], who immortalized the city through the private detective [[philip-marlowe]], depicted Los Angeles as a sprawling landscape of class divisions and hidden crimes, where the glamour of Hollywood and the glittering heights of the wealthy masked pervasive corruption and violence. The city became synonymous with noir and has remained a central backdrop for twentieth-century American crime fiction.

## you gotta know

- [[raymond-chandler]]'s detective novels, beginning with *The Big Sleep* (1939), used Los Angeles's geography—from downtown tenements to Pacific coast estates—as a staging ground for moral investigation.
- The city's stratification by neighborhood and class is central to the noir aesthetic: the detective moves between worlds (rich and poor, legitimate and criminal) that would otherwise be sealed from one another.
- Hollywood, the film industry, and the entertainment world provide a recurring backdrop in Los Angeles noir, offering a zone of artifice, sexual intrigue, and hidden identities; the dream factory becomes a crime factory.
- Los Angeles in the 1930s-1950s, the heyday of hard-boiled fiction, was rapidly expanding, morphing from a frontier town into a sprawling metropolis—this transformation fed the noir anxiety about urban alienation and faceless growth.
- Modern Los Angeles continues as a major setting for crime and thriller fiction, maintaining the noir association even as the city and genre have evolved; contemporary writers build on Chandler's legacy of using geography as character.
- The city's vastness, its collection of distinct communities and neighborhoods barely connected by geography or history, makes it an ideal setting for crime fiction: secrets hide easily in sprawl.

## connections

- [[raymond-chandler]] — the definitive chronicler of noir Los Angeles; his vision of the city shaped how generations would imagine it.
- [[the-big-sleep]] — Chandler's first and most iconic novel, using Los Angeles's spaces (the Sternwood mansion, the oil fields, downtown streets) as a moral landscape.
- [[hard-boiled-fiction]] — Los Angeles became the capital of the hard-boiled tradition; the city's character is inseparable from the genre's development.
- [[detective-fiction]] — Los Angeles provides the stage for the detective's moral investigations and social mobility.
- [[united-states]] — the broader American context; Los Angeles as a specifically American city, emblematic of American contradictions.
