## summary

**Contemporary American literature** spans the diverse body of fiction, poetry, drama, and non-fiction produced in the *United States* from the mid-twentieth century to the present. Shaped decisively by the aftermath of *World War II*, the *Civil Rights Movement*, shifting geopolitical power, and digital hyperconnectivity, it broke from high *modernism* to interrogate historical master narratives. The field is celebrated for expanding the American literary canon to encompass marginalized racial, ethnic, and queer voices alongside experimental and post-industrial aesthetics.

## you gotta know

- Chronologically demarcated as beginning around 1945 following *World War II*, gathering momentum through the social counterculture of the 1960s.
- Marked by the institutionalization of creative writing programs (notably the Iowa Writers' Workshop) and the vast expansion of university presses and grants.
- Enriched by the *Civil Rights Movement*, Black Arts Movement, and indigenous and immigrant writing, centering authors like *James Baldwin*, *Toni Morrison*, and *Audre Lorde*.
- Fostered major poetic evolutions including the Confessional school, the Beat Generation, the New York School, and contemporary spoken word movements.
- Saw American authors win global accolades, including Nobel Prizes in Literature awarded to *Saul Bellow*, *Toni Morrison*, and *Louise Glück*.
- Produced vital theatrical masterpieces addressing national conscience, labor, and domestic anguish by dramatists such as *Arthur Miller*, *August Wilson*, and *Tony Kushner*.
- Continuously engages with globalization, systemic inequity, post-truth media environments, and the legacy of American imperial intervention abroad.
