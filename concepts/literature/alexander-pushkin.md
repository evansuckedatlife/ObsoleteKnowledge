## summary

**Alexander Pushkin** was a nineteenth-century Russian poet, playwright, and novelist revered as the founder of modern Russian literature and standard literary language. Writing at the height of [[romanticism]] while pioneering psychological [[realism]], he reshaped European narrative traditions into an expressive Russian vernacular across celebrated works in prose and verse. His tragic death in an 1837 pistol duel cut short a brilliant career, cementing his enduring legacy as Russia's national poet whose technical virtuosity influenced every subsequent generation of Russian writers.

## you gotta know

- Composed the verse novel *Eugene Onegin*, which pioneered the fourteen-line *Onegin stanza* in iambic tetrameter and introduced the seminal literary archetype of the Russian superfluous man through its world-weary aristocratic protagonist.
- Wrote *The Queen of Spades*, a famous psychological masterpiece of supernatural dread and obsessive gambling set in [[st-petersburg]], in which the officer *Hermann* seeks the secret three-card sequence from an aging countess with fatal consequences.
- Penned the celebrated narrative poem *The Bronze Horseman*, which contrasts the imperial ambitions of *Peter the Great* against the tragic fate of the impoverished clerk *Evgenii* during the devastating 1824 flood of [[st-petersburg]].
- Created the historical drama *Boris Godunov*, heavily influenced by [[william-shakespeare]], which chronicles the guilt of the usurper tsar during the Time of Troubles and served as the basis for a landmark [[opera]] by *Modest Mussorgsky*.
- Authored *The Tales of the Late Ivan Petrovich Belkin*, an essential prose cycle containing stories like *The Station Master* and *The Shot*, celebrated for concise narration that broke away from sentimental conventions.
- Died after sustaining a mortal wound in an 1837 pistol duel defending the honor of his wife, *Natalia Goncharova*, against the French officer *Georges-Charles de Heeckeren d'Anthès*.
- Drew personal pride from his African heritage through his maternal great-grandfather, *Abram Petrovich Gannibal*, an enslaved African page who became a prominent military engineer under *Peter the Great*, inspiring his unfinished novel *The Blackamoor of Peter the Great*.

## connections

- [[the-queen-of-spades]] — his celebrated Gothic short story chronicling *Hermann*'s fatal obsession with a supernatural gambling secret.
- [[romanticism]] — the artistic movement that shaped his early poetry of freedom and exile before he developed a grounded realistic style.
- [[realism]] — the emerging literary approach he helped initiate by depicting ordinary Russian society, social pressures, and psychological depth.
- [[poetry]] — the medium of his greatest stylistic innovations, culminating in the intricate fourteen-line stanza designed for *Eugene Onegin*.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the English playwright whose dramatic freedom and historical chronicles inspired the open episodic structure of *Boris Godunov*.
- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — delivered the celebrated 1880 *Pushkin Speech*, hailing him as the prophetic embodiment of universal compassion and Russian genius.
- [[leo-tolstoy]] — cited his crystalline, unpretentious prose as an artistic ideal and drew direct inspiration from his narrative fragments.
- [[opera]] — the musical form that adapted his literary masterpieces, including *Eugene Onegin* and *The Queen of Spades* by *Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky*.
