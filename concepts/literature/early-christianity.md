## summary

**Early Christianity** designates the historical and theological development of the Christian movement from the ministry of *Jesus of Nazareth* in the first century through the First Council of Nicaea in 325 CE. Emerging as an apocalyptic sect within Second Temple [[judaism]] across the Hellenistic [[middle-east]], the nascent faith rapidly traversed the Mediterranean basin through apostolic missions, epistolary networks, and urban congregations within the [[roman-empire]]. It is essential to literary, historical, and philosophical studies because its foundational scriptures, apologetic treatises, and theological debates transformed classical rhetoric, established Western scriptural canons, and reshaped ancient cultural consciousness.

## you gotta know

- Emerged directly out of first-century Jewish sectarianism in Roman Judea, centering on the crucifixion and reported resurrection of *Jesus of Nazareth*.
- Shifted rapidly from an Aramaic-speaking Jewish community to a broader Gentile movement through the itinerant missionary journeys and theological epistles of *Paul the Apostle*.
- Adopted the Greek Septuagint as its primary scripture, producing original theological narratives and letters in the common Greek dialect of the Hellenistic world.
- Endured periodic and localized imperial suppressions, culminating in the empire-wide persecutions under Emperors *Decius* and *Diocletian* before legal toleration under *Constantine the Great*.
- Cultivated charismatic spiritual expressions, including prophetic utterances and mystical [[ecstasy]], which early church leaders eventually sought to regulate into institutional order.
- Developed major apologetic and anti-heretical literary traditions through Church Fathers such as *Justin Martyr*, *Irenaeus of Lyons*, *Tertullian*, and *Origen of Alexandria*.
- Defined orthodox doctrine regarding the Trinity and Christology while combating internal divergent movements such as Gnosticism, Marcionism, and Arianism.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the overarching world religion whose core doctrines, liturgies, and textual canons were formed during this initial era.
- [[judaism]] — the ancestral faith tradition from which Christian messianic theology, ethical monotheism, and sacred scripture originated.
- [[roman-empire]] — the administrative, legal, and communicative infrastructure within which the faith spread and against whose civic cults it clashed.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the philosophical and linguistic substrate whose conceptual tools shaped early theological discourse and scriptural composition.
- [[rome]] — the imperial capital where apostolic leaders were martyred and whose bishopric acquired preeminent ecclesiastical influence.
- [[middle-east]] — the geographic cradle encompassing Jerusalem, Antioch, and Alexandria, where the earliest churches flourished.
- [[ecstasy]] — the visionary and pneumatic religious experience prominent in apostolic worship, prophetic movements, and martyr narratives.
