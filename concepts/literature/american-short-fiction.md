## summary

**American short fiction** is a foundational literary tradition that established the modern short story as an autonomous, artistically rigorous narrative form within [[american-literature]]. Originating in the early nineteenth-century [[united-states]] with the folkloric sketches of *Washington Irving*, the form was critically codified by [[edgar-allan-poe]], who defined its defining aesthetic principle as a unified effect achieved in a single sitting. Developing across nineteenth-century [[romanticism]] and [[gothic-literature]], post-war [[realism]], and twentieth-century [[modernism]], the tradition served as the nation's primary vehicle for stylistic experimentation, regional identity, and concise psychological portraiture.

## you gotta know

- [[edgar-allan-poe]] established the formal critical theory of the modern short story in his 1842 review of *Nathaniel Hawthorne*'s *Twice-Told Tales*, arguing that every word in a tale must contribute directly to a pre-conceived "single effect."
- *Washington Irving* helped invent the national genre with *The Sketch Book of Geoffrey Crayon, Gent.* (1819–1820), adapting European folklore into foundational American myths including *"Rip Van Winkle"* and *"The Legend of Sleepy Hollow"*.
- *O. Henry* popularised the commercially driven short story with concise pacing and signature ironic twist endings, exemplified by *"The Gift of the Magi"* and collected in *The Four Million* (1906).
- *Ernest Hemingway* transformed modernist short fiction through his "iceberg theory," stripping away background exposition to generate tension entirely through dialogue and subtext in collections like *In Our Time* and *Men Without Women*.
- *Nathaniel Hawthorne* and *Herman Melville* infused the genre with moral ambiguity and dark symbolism, creating lasting allegories of Puritan guilt and modern alienation in *"Young Goodman Brown"* and *"Bartleby, the Scrivener"*.
- [[ambrose-bierce]] drew on his battlefield experiences in the [[american-civil-war]] to experiment with distorted subjective time in *"An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge"*, creating a vital precursor to [[stream-of-consciousness]].
- Mid-century writers like *Flannery O'Connor* and *Shirley Jackson* deployed regional gothic tropes and sudden violence to expose social hypocrisy, seen in the ritualistic horror of *"The Lottery"* and the grotesque theology of *"A Good Man Is Hard to Find"*.
- *Raymond Carver* defined late-twentieth-century minimalism, or dirty [[realism]], using spare prose and working-class domestic alienation in collections edited heavily by *Gordon Lish*, such as *What We Talk About When We Talk About Love*.

## connections

- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — formulated the single-effect doctrine and invented modern [[detective-fiction]] within the short format.
- [[american-literature]] — provides the broader national canon in which short stories attained unprecedented critical prestige and commercial reach.
- [[romanticism]] — furnished the folkloric and imaginative foundations that animated early American storytellers.
- [[gothic-literature]] — supplied the psychological dread, haunted landscapes, and moral decay central to nineteenth-century tales.
- [[american-civil-war]] — shattered romantic conventions, prompting a hard-edged realism in the war fiction of *Bierce* and *Stephen Crane*.
- [[realism]] — steered the post-war short story toward local-color sketches, accurate regional dialects, and detailed social observation.
- [[modernism]] — revolutionized twentieth-century short fiction through elliptical plotting, fragmented subjectivity, and the iceberg theory.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — interior psychological technique foreshadowed by subjective nineteenth-century American war stories before modernist adoption.
