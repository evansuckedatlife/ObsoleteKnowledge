# Ambition

## summary

**Ambition** is one of literature's defining human drives—the hunger for power, status, or achievement that propels characters toward their own destruction. In classical and Renaissance literature, ambition often functions as a tragic flaw: it blinds the protagonist to moral consequences, elevates them beyond their natural station, and leads inevitably to their fall. From Marlowe's Faustus to Shakespeare's Macbeth, ambition is the engine of tragedy, the force that turns human aspiration into hubris and catastrophe.

## you gotta know

- In Renaissance drama, ambition embodies *tragic overreach*: the protagonist's desire to transcend their natural limits (social rank, mortality, moral law) sets them on a collision course with cosmic or social order.
- *Christopher Marlowe*'s *Doctor Faustus* (c. 1588) is the archetype: Faustus's ambition for forbidden knowledge leads him to sell his soul; ambition becomes damnation.
- *Shakespeare*'s *Macbeth* and *Lady Macbeth* are driven by ambition—Macbeth's to become king, Lady Macbeth's to unburden herself of moral constraint—and their ambition breeds paranoia and murder.
- *Othello*'s tragedy involves less direct ambition than vulnerability to another's ambition: Iago's resentful ambition to destroy Othello corrodes the general's faith in himself and his love.
- Ambition differs from mere desire: it is *social* (aimed at status or power within a hierarchy) and *future-oriented* (the hunger to become something one is not yet).
- The *psychological weight* of ambition in literature often manifests as insomnia, paranoia, hallucination, and madness—the mind fracturing under the pressure of its own impossible desires.
- In modern literature, ambition often becomes more equivocal: not inherently tragic but revealing of complicity, corruption, and the moral compromises demanded by social success.

## connections

- [[macbeth]] — ambition's tragic prototype: the general who murders for a crown and descends into paranoid tyranny.
- [[christopher-marlowe]] — dramatized ambition as the defining human passion; his overreaching protagonists set the model for Shakespearean tragedy.
- [[othello]] — ambition working through jealousy: the ambition to possess, to be worthy, to prove oneself against social suspicion.
- [[tragedy]] — in classical and Renaissance convention, ambition is the tragic flaw that triggers the protagonist's downfall.
- [[power]] — ambition's object; the desire to reshape the world according to one's will.
- [[king-lear]] — a tragedy of ambition in reverse: Lear's ambition to divide his kingdom among flatterers triggers tragic consequence.
