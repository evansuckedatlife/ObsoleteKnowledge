## summary

**Cuban Independence** denotes the protracted nineteenth-century struggle to liberate the island of *Cuba* from the imperial dominion of the Spanish Crown, culminating in the *Cuban War of Independence* and international intervention. Uniquely intertwined with literary history, the movement was ideologically shaped and led by revolutionary poet *José Martí*, whose essays and verses fused anticolonial nationalism with early literary modernism. The resulting conflict dismantled the final remnants of the Spanish Empire in the Americas and fundamentally reordered hemispheric relations.

## you gotta know

- Commenced with earlier conflicts such as the *Ten Years' War* launched by *Carlos Manuel de Céspedes* at the *Grito de Yara*, followed by the *Little War*.
- Organized diplomatically and philosophically from exile by poet and essayist *José Martí*, who founded the *Cuban Revolutionary Party* in *[[new-york-city]]*.
- Synthesized aesthetic innovation with radical politics through Martí's seminal essay *Our America*, which called for Latin American unity and indigenous self-determination.
- Suffered the devastating battlefield death of Martí himself early in the final war at the *Battle of Dos Ríos*, immediately canonizing him as the national *Apostle of Independence*.
- Led militarily by legendary insurgent generals such as Dominican-born *Máximo Gómez* and the *Bronze Titan*, *Antonio Maceo*, who executed the daring *Invasion of the West*.
- Met with brutal counterinsurgency methods under Spanish Governor-General *Valeriano Weyler*, whose pioneering policy of *reconcentración* forced rural populations into concentration camps where hundreds of thousands died of starvation and disease.
- Prompted armed intervention by the *[[united-states]]* following the mysterious explosion of the battleship *USS Maine* in *Havana Harbor*, precipitating the Spanish-American War of 1898.
- Ended formal Spanish colonial sovereignty through the *Treaty of Paris*, though Cuban self-governance was subsequently constrained by the controversial *Platt Amendment*.

## connections

- [[spain]] — the imperial metropolis whose centuries-long dominion over the Caribbean was shattered by the war.
- [[colonialism]] — the global imperial system whose exploitative plantation economies and overseas subjugation the insurgents fought to abolish.
- [[poetry]] — the artistic medium employed by *José Martí* to articulate national identity and fundraise for the revolutionary cause.
- [[modernism]] — the landmark literary and intellectual movement in Spanish-language literature spearheaded by figures like Martí.
- [[united-states]] — the northern power that intervened in 1898, fundamentally shaping post-war Caribbean geopolitics.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the historical era of initial Iberian colonization that established the centuries of imperial rule ended by this movement.
