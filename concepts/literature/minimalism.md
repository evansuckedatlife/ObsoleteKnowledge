## summary

**Minimalism** is an aesthetic principle across multiple disciplines—literature, visual art, music—that strips away all non-essential elements to achieve maximum emotional or formal impact through reduction. In literature, minimalism favors sparse, precise prose; emotional restraint; and an iceberg effect where what is *omitted* carries as much weight as what is stated. The movement reflects a modernist faith that emptiness and silence can be as eloquent as abundance.

## you gotta know

- Literary minimalism emerged in the 1970s–1980s with American writers *Raymond Carver*, *Amy Hempel*, and *Tobias Wolff*, who stripped away authorial commentary, adjectives, and emotional exposition.
- *Anton Chekhov* is the spiritual ancestor: his late plays (*The Cherry Orchard*) pioneered the idea that drama could consist of mundane domestic moments; the emotional weight lies beneath surface triviality.
- *Raymond Carver* is the master: his short stories present working-class American life in spare, flat prose; dialogue carries emotional truth; the narrator withholds judgment and interpretation, forcing readers to infer meaning.
- The "iceberg principle": like an iceberg, the visible surface (what is written) is only a fraction of the whole; seven-eighths remains submerged (what is *not* written), and the reader must imagine that depth.
- Contrasts with maximalism (abundance) and differs from *realism* in that minimalism is a *formal choice* of reduction, not an attempt to faithfully represent the world; absence is weaponized as technique.
- In *visual art*, minimalism (Mondrian, Malevich) strips painting to geometric form, primary colors, and emptiness; in *music*, it privileges repetition and gradual process (*Philip Glass*, *John Adams*); the principle is consistent: meaning through reduction.
- Minimalism can produce either profound emotional depth (through what is unsaid) or cold emotional distance (through withholding sympathy); the technique cuts both ways.

## connections

- [[anton-chekhov]] — the 19th-century Russian playwright whose sparse, indirect approach to emotion pioneered literary minimalism.
- [[raymond-carver]] — the definitive American minimalist, whose short stories made working-class life eloquent through restraint.
- [[piet-mondrian]] — visual artist whose reduction to geometric form and primary colors exemplifies minimalism across disciplines.
- [[kazimir-malevich]] — suprematist painter whose *Black Square* is minimalism's philosophical statement: reduction to pure form.
- [[canon]] — in music, the minimalist principle of repetition and gradual transformation; Steve Reich's work exemplifies minimalist composition.
- [[modernism]] — the broader aesthetic movement whose faith in formal innovation minimalism inherits and transforms.
