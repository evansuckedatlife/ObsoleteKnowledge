## summary

**Rupi Kaur** is an Indian-born Canadian poet, illustrator, and performer who emerged as the foremost pioneer of the twenty-first-century Instapoetry phenomenon. Rising to prominence through digital platforms, she transformed modern commercial publishing with her minimalist lyricism accompanied by spare, black-and-white line drawings. Her accessible collections explore trauma, immigration, healing, and feminine embodiment, earning global commercial success while sparking widespread debate across contemporary literary criticism regarding mass digital [[poetry]].

## you gotta know

- Broke onto the international literary scene with her self-published 2014 debut collection, *milk and honey*, which was subsequently re-released and spent over three years on *The New York Times* bestseller list.
- Writes exclusively in lowercase letters and uses only the period as punctuation, an intentional stylistic choice honoring the visual conventions of the Gurmukhi script of her native Punjabi heritage.
- Divided *milk and honey* into four thematic chapters—*the hurting*, *the loving*, *the breaking*, and *the healing*—which trace survival through sexual abuse, heartbreak, and self-acceptance.
- Released her second major collection, *the sun and her flowers*, in 2017, using the life cycle of a wilting and blooming sunflower to structure themes of ancestral heritage and diaspora.
- Published *home body* in 2020 during the pandemic lockdowns, addressing battles with clinical depression, burnout, and restorative self-love.
- Gained early viral recognition in 2015 when her visual photography essay depicting menstrual blood challenged social media censorship policies on Instagram.
- Composes alongside line-drawing sketches and stages international spoken-word theatrical tours blending live readings with music and projection.

- [[poetry]] — redefined modern popular poetic distribution by popularizing short, unmetered confessional verse and graphic accompaniment.
- [[margaret-laurence]] — shared focus on female bodily autonomy, social vulnerability, and the immigrant experience in Canada.
- [[robert-munsch]] — fellow Canadian literary figure whose popularity relies heavily on accessible language, direct performance, and visual accompaniment.
- [[romanticism]] — shares thematic parallels with early Romantic verse through an intense emphasis on sincere emotion, vulnerability, and spontaneous feeling.
- [[modernism]] — borrows radical modernist typographics and stripped-back syntactical forms pioneered by twentieth-century avant-garde poets.
- [[realism]] — portrays frank, unvarnished depictions of interpersonal abuse, generational domestic grief, and immigrant displacement.
