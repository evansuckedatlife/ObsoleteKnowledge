## summary

**Expansion**, specifically westward territorial growth across the North American continent, constitutes one of the defining geopolitical processes and mythic frameworks of nineteenth-century *American literature*. Driven by ideological doctrines of continental dominion, this sustained movement transformed national identity by juxtaposing agrarian settlement against an untamed wilderness. Understanding expansion is essential for evaluating how American authors celebrated pioneering self-reliance while simultaneously interrogating imperial displacement, ecological disruption, and the moral costs of conquest.

## you gotta know

- Conceptualized historically in the frontier thesis of *Frederick Jackson Turner*, who argued in 1893 that continual westward movement across free land forged egalitarian American democracy and individualist character.
- Justified ideologically by *Manifest Destiny*, the nineteenth-century belief popularized by editor *John L. O'Sullivan* that Euro-American settlers were providentially chosen to overspread the continent.
- Romanticized early in the national consciousness by *James Fenimore Cooper* through his *Leatherstocking Tales*, where the pioneer scout *Natty Bumppo* navigates the receding boundary between virgin forest and encroaching settlement.
- Symbolized poignantly at the conclusion of *Mark Twain*'s masterpiece *Adventures of Huckleberry Finn*, when the protagonist resolves to escape social conformity by setting off for the unsettled western territory ahead of the rest.
- Chronicled with agrarian empathy in the frontier prairie novels of *Willa Cather*, such as *O Pioneers!* and *My Ántonia*, which dramatize the grueling physical and psychological labor of immigrant homesteaders.
- Deconstructed savagely in revisionist twentieth-century literature, most notably in *Cormac McCarthy*'s *Blood Meridian*, which reimagines southwestern expansion as an apocalyptic nightmare of scalp-hunting and violent imperial subjugation.
- Accelerated by major federal initiatives including the *Homestead Act* of 1862 and the transcontinental railroad, alongside catastrophic federal displacements of Indigenous populations along the *Trail of Tears*.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — the national canon whose foundational mythology, pastoral tropes, and tragic epics are anchored in continental migration.
- [[manifest-destiny]] — the divine and political doctrine that rationalized territorial expansion as an inevitable historical duty.
- [[united-states]] — the sovereign republic shaped culturally, economically, and geographically by its westward march.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader global dynamic of imperial conquest and land dispossession mirrored in internal continental expansion.
- [[wilderness]] — the untamed natural space that served as both an ideological adversary and a spiritual sanctuary during western settlement.
- [[romanticism]] — the philosophical and artistic movement that framed early frontier expansion through sublime landscapes and solitary heroes.
- [[realism]] — the aesthetic development that arose to expose the harsh economic isolation and natural cruelty concealed beneath romantic pioneer myths.
