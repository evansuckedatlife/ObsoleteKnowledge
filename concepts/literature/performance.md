## summary

**Performance** is the live, embodied enactment of a text or script before an audience, where meaning emerges through the bodies, voices, and choices of actors in real time. Unlike narrative fiction, which exists primarily on the page, dramatic literature becomes complete only through performance—the presence of bodies, the contingency of live action, and the irreplaceable moment-to-moment interaction between performer and audience. Performance is both preservation and transformation: it interprets a written text while creating something that cannot be perfectly repeated.

## you gotta know

- Drama as a literary form is written *for* performance; the script is a blueprint, not the final work—the actor's interpretation, blocking, voice, and physical presence complete it.
- Live performance creates an immediate, fragile contract between performer and audience: both are present in the same moment, and the outcome is never guaranteed, making every performance unique.
- The actor's *presence*—a quality of concentrated attention and embodied commitment—becomes the primary communicative vehicle in performance; a missed line or hesitation has immediate impact and cannot be edited out.
- Performance codes include not only dialogue but also gesture, facial expression, spatial blocking, costume, lighting, and music—a layered semiotic system that extends beyond text alone.
- The theatrical *frame*—the convention that the stage is a space where different rules apply—allows audiences to suspend disbelief and accept stylised or impossible actions as meaningful within the dramatic world.
- Performance creates a particular relationship with history: staging a centuries-old play involves interpreting its meanings through contemporary bodies, concerns, and aesthetics, making it a conversation across time.
- Different performance traditions (realism, Kabuki, Commedia dell'arte, physical theatre, Noh) establish radically different conventions for how bodies, voices, and emotions are presented and understood, making performance culturally contingent.

## connections

- [[drama]] — the textual form that performance brings to life; they are inseparable.
- [[theatre]] — the institutional and artistic home of performance; the building, the tradition, the cultural practice.
- [[acting]] — the skilled technique and discipline that transforms a script into lived human action.
- [[broadway-theatre]] — a specific performance tradition shaped by particular economic, aesthetic, and cultural pressures.
- [[shakespeare]] — a writer whose works are defined by performance; his plays reveal their full complexity only when acted.
