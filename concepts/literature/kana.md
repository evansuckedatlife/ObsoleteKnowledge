## summary

**Kana** refers to the two phonetic scripts of Japanese — *hiragana* and *katakana* — derived from Chinese characters but designed for phonetic representation rather than pictographic meaning. Developed around the 9th century, kana enabled the vernacular written expression that had previously been restricted to classical Chinese, democratizing literacy and allowing women authors in particular to forge a literary tradition. Their emergence was foundational to the golden age of Heian court literature.

## you gotta know

- The two scripts are *hiragana*, used for native Japanese words and grammatical particles, and *katakana*, originally reserved for Buddhist priestly annotations but now used for foreign words and emphasis.
- Chinese characters, *kanji*, encode meaning but are phonetically ambiguous in Japanese; kana solved this by providing a fixed sound per symbol, making reading and writing far more accessible to ordinary people.
- *Hiragana* characters are curved and flowing, derived by simplifying kanji radicals, while *katakana* characters are angular and geometric, derived from kanji components in a different way; their visual distinction carries semantic weight.
- Women of the Heian court—including [[murasaki-shikibu]] and [[sei-shonagon]]—gained unprecedented literary voice precisely because they were allowed to write in kana while men were obligated to use the prestige language of Chinese.
- The Tale of Genji and The Pillow Book, cornerstone works of world literature, were written in kana by women who would have been denied access to classical scholarly training in Chinese and its literature.
- The development of kana was not instantaneous but evolved gradually during the 8th and 9th centuries as scribes developed shorthand systems; by the Heian period (794–1185), both scripts had standardized forms.
- The association of kana with women—and thus with vernacular, domestic, and "feminine" literature—persisted even after men began writing in kana; this gendering of scripts shaped literary hierarchies for centuries.

## connections

- [[murasaki-shikibu]] — pioneered the literary novel in kana, establishing *vernacular prose* as an art form.
- [[sei-shonagon]] — used kana to create the essay as a vehicle for wit and observation.
