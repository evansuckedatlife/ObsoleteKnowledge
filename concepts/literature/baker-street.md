## summary

**221B Baker Street** is the iconic London address where *Sherlock Holmes* and *Dr. Watson* live in Arthur Conan Doyle's detective stories. The lodging itself becomes almost a character in the narratives: a refuge where Holmes conducts experiments, broods over cases, and expounds on his methods while Watson narrates. Baker Street has transcended its fictional origins to become one of literature's most recognizable addresses, symbolizing the detective's intellectual sanctuary and the domestic partnership between Holmes and Watson. The street itself, in London's West End, has become a pilgrimage site for Sherlock Holmes enthusiasts and a monument to the power of fictional geography.

## you gotta know

- *221B Baker Street* is where Holmes and Watson take lodgings with their landlady, Mrs. Hudson; the flat becomes the setting for many of Holmes's most famous deductions.
- The address gives the stories an air of plausibility: Baker Street is a real London street, adding verisimilitude to the fictional narratives and allowing readers to imaginatively locate the detective's residence.
- Holmes's sitting room at Baker Street is furnished with the accumulated evidence of his cases: chemical apparatus, scrapbooks, pipes, and the violin he plays when contemplating difficult problems.
- *Dr. Watson*, narrating from 221B, uses the setting as the domestic frame for his tales; many stories begin with Watson and Holmes at breakfast or in the sitting room, establishing the investigative partnership.
- The address has become so embedded in popular culture that tourists visit the real Baker Street in London seeking the fictional address; a small museum celebrates Holmes and Watson's (fictional) residence.
- The lodging represents stability and domesticity within the detective fiction form: 221B is the home base from which investigations launch and to which the detectives return for rest and reflection.
