## summary

**Progress**, often conceptualized historically as the **Idea of Progress**, is the philosophical and cultural conviction that human society, scientific knowledge, and material conditions advance in an upward trajectory toward greater perfection, justice, and prosperity over time. Crystallized during the European [[enlightenment]] and supercharged by the [[industrial-revolution]], it asserted that empirical reason and technological mastery would steadily eradicate poverty, ignorance, and tyranny. In literature, philosophy, and political economy, this conviction underpinned nineteenth-century optimism and catalyzed the birth of [[science-fiction]], while simultaneously provoking intense counter-movements that questioned the environmental, psychological, and moral costs of unconstrained growth.

## you gotta know

- Popularized as a definitive doctrine of the European [[enlightenment]], championed by thinkers such as the *Marquis de Condorcet*, whose posthumous 1795 treatise argued that human faculties and social equity possessed no upper boundary to their future perfection.
- Accelerated exponentially by the [[industrial-revolution]], transforming abstract philosophy into a palpable civic religion that equated steam locomotion, manufacturing capacity, and urban growth with the moral ascent of civilization.
- Served as the fundamental conceptual foundation of [[science-fiction]], providing the speculative engine for early technological visions from *H.G. Wells* to *Arthur C. Clarke*, while also inspiring the dystopian warnings of *Aldous Huxley* and *George Orwell*.
- Provoked a swift and powerful counter-reaction in [[romanticism]], where writers such as *Mary Shelley* in *Frankenstein* and *William Blake* cautioned that mechanistic hubris alienated humanity from the spiritual and natural realms.
- Critiqued sharply within Victorian [[realism]], most visibly in the social novels of [[charles-dickens]], whose depictions of polluted industrial towns and impoverished workhouses exposed the grim human exploitation subsidizing prosperity in [[england]].
- Collapsed as a self-evident historical certainty amid the industrialized slaughter and poison gas of [[world-war-i]], giving rise to the profound spiritual malaise and stylistic alienation characteristic of literary [[modernism]].
- Reconfigured as a central ideological battleground during the [[cold-war]] and the [[space-race]], wherein the [[united-states]] and the [[soviet-union]] competed to prove whether market capitalism or collectivist socialism was the true vehicle of historical destiny.

## connections

- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual epoch that established human reason, secular science, and liberty as the primary catalysts of historical advancement.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the transformation of production and energy that supplied material evidence for doctrines of perpetual economic and societal expansion.
- [[science-fiction]] — the imaginative literary genre centered on exploring the prospective fruits and catastrophic perils of scientific development.
- [[romanticism]] — the artistic movement that challenged cold rationalism and celebrated organic nature against the dehumanizing march of industry.
- [[charles-dickens]] — the Victorian novelist who documented the squalor and cruelty visited upon the working poor in the name of industrial modernization.
- [[world-war-i]] — the catastrophic war whose industrial-scale devastation effectively demolished the nineteenth-century belief in inevitable moral improvement.
- [[space-race]] — the twentieth-century technological sprint celebrated as the ultimate modern fulfillment of human exploratory potential.
