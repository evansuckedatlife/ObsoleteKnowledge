## summary

**Dmitri Karamazov**, often called *Mitya*, is the passionate eldest brother in *Fyodor Dostoevsky*'s 1880 masterpiece *The Brothers Karamazov*. Embodying sensual abandon, erratic honor, and turbulent emotional extremes, he becomes embroiled in a bitter rivalry with his depraved father over money and the love of *Grushenka*. Though innocent of his father's physical murder, Dmitri's wrongful conviction serves as an agonizing vehicle for spiritual resurrection and moral culpability.

## you gotta know

- Eldest son of *Fyodor Pavlovich Karamazov* and his first wife, *Adelaida Ivanovna*, raised largely neglected by his family and serving as an army officer.
- Entangled in an explosive love triangle between his noble betrothed *Katerina Ivanovna* and the alluring, independent *Grushenka* (*Agrafena Alexandrovna Svetlova*).
- Feuds violently with his father over an inheritance of three thousand rubles and their mutual infatuation with *Grushenka*.
- Carries an amulet containing fifteen hundred rubles sewn into a rag, symbolizing his torn moral conscience and lingering shred of personal honor.
- Wrongfully arrested and prosecuted for the parricide of his father after spending lavishly at Mokroye, despite the crime actually being committed by *Pavel Smerdyakov*.
- Experiences a transformative dream of an impoverished, weeping infant—"the wee one"—which inspires him to accept suffering on behalf of all humanity.
- Sentenced to hard labor in Siberia following a sensational court trial marked by rival speeches from the prosecutor and defense attorney *Fetyukovich*.
- Supported tenderly by his pious youngest brother *Alyosha Karamazov*, who helps coordinate an escape plan to the *United States*.

## connections

- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — author who conceived him as the visceral, passionate embodiment of the broad Russian soul.
- [[ivan-karamazov]] — intellectual middle brother whose rationalist doubt forms an ideological foil to Dmitri's tempestuous sensuality.
- [[christianity]] — spiritual tradition underpinning Dmitri's eventual acceptance of vicarious redemption through suffering.
- [[realism]] — literary movement framing the psychological accuracy and legal procedural detail of his trial.
- [[tragedy]] — dramatic structure governing his catastrophic trajectory toward public disgrace and fatal misunderstandings.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical framework anticipating his radical freedom, subjective crisis, and existential anguish.
- [[doppelganger]] — thematic trope reflected in the distorted mirrors and mutual guilt shared between the Karamazov brothers.
