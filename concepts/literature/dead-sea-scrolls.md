## summary

The **Dead Sea Scrolls**, also referred to as the **Qumran Caves Scrolls** or **Qumran Scrolls**, are an ancient archive of thousands of manuscript fragments representing more than 900 distinct Jewish texts discovered between 1946 and 1956 in the [[middle-east]]. Hidden in limestone caves near the ruins of Khirbet Qumran, these writings date between the third century BCE and the first century CE. Their recovery profoundly transformed modern scholarship regarding the Hebrew Bible, sectarian theology in Second Temple [[judaism]], the nascent background of [[christianity]], and ancient Semitic [[linguistics]].

## you gotta know

- Discovered in late 1946 or early 1947 by Bedouin goat-herders of the *Ta'amireh* tribe inside a cliff cave along the northwest shore of the Dead Sea.
- Recovered from eleven distinct caves near *Khirbet Qumran*, with the overwhelming bulk of fragmentary texts retrieved from the artificial chambers of *Cave 4*.
- Preserves the magnificent *Great Isaiah Scroll* (*1QIsa*), a nearly complete twenty-four-foot parchment scroll dating to roughly 125 BCE, containing all sixty-six chapters.
- Contains fragments of every book of the traditional Hebrew Bible with the single exception of the *Book of Esther*, providing the earliest extant manuscript witnesses to Scripture.
- Features prominent non-biblical sectarian works including the *Community Rule* (*Serekh ha-Yahad*), the *War Scroll*, and the *Temple Scroll*, illustrating rigorous purity laws and apocalyptic eschatology.
- Includes the singular *Copper Scroll* found in *Cave 3*, which was inscribed on sheets of metal rather than parchment and catalogs sixty-four hidden caches of gold and temple silver.
- Widely associated with an ascetic, apocalyptic Jewish sect commonly identified with the *Essenes*, organized under a leader termed the *Teacher of Righteousness*.
- Sealed in earthenware storage jars and deposited within caves to shield the sacred library from the Roman legions suppressing the Jewish revolt under the [[roman-empire]].

## connections

- [[judaism]] — the ancestral faith whose Second Temple sectarian movements, legal disputes, and scribal traditions are preserved.
- [[christianity]] — the emerging religious movement whose messianic concepts, communal meals, and apocalyptic expectations share close affinities with Qumran texts.
- [[middle-east]] — the arid desert landscape whose dry atmosphere permitted the miraculous physical preservation of organic parchment over two millennia.
- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean empire whose legions sacked Qumran in 68 CE, prompting the hasty concealment of the scrolls.
- [[linguistics]] — the scholarly field enriched by unprecedented primary evidence of Hellenistic-era Hebrew dialects and Jewish Palestinian Aramaic.
- [[rome]] — the seat of imperial authority that dispatched military forces to subjugate the regional uprising in Judaea.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural world whose language appears in several Greek Septuagint translations recovered alongside Semitic manuscripts.
