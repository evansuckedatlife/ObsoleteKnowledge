## summary

**English nationalism** is a political, cultural, and literary consciousness asserting the distinct sovereignty, language, historical destiny, and communal solidarity of the English people. Emerging forcefully during the *Tudor* era and the *Hundred Years' War*, it consolidated through early modern state-building, Protestant self-definition against continental Catholic powers, and the artistic flowering of the *English Renaissance*. In literature, it provided an enduring imaginative framework for patriotic drama, national chronicle histories, and Romantic civic verse that later fed into the broader ideology of the *British Empire*.

## you gotta know

- Galvanized dramatically by the defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588, which fostered a national myth of divine Protestant protection and insular exceptionalism.
- Immortalized on the early modern stage by *[[william-shakespeare]]*, most famously through the St. Crispin's Day speech in *Henry V* and John of Gaunt's "sceptered isle" speech in *Richard II*.
- Deeply intertwined with the vernacular translation movement, where replacing Latin theological dominance with the English Bible and the Book of Common Prayer created a shared linguistic identity.
- Shaped by early modern historians like Raphael Holinshed, whose *Chronicles* supplied source material for patriotic drama and historical mythmaking.
- Reconceptualized during the English Civil War through republican and Puritan visions of [[england]] as an "Elect Nation" chosen by God.
- Celebrated in *[[english-renaissance-poetry]]*, where poets like Edmund Spenser in *The Faerie Queene* explicitly allegorized the Tudor monarch as an embodiment of national virtue.
- Revived during the Napoleonic Wars by Romantic writers who pitted English insular pastoralism and constitutional liberty against the revolutionary upheavals of [[france]].

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — dramatized English dynastic conflict and codified iconic expressions of martial patriotism.
- [[england]] — the sovereign territorial homeland whose historical consciousness and culture this concept articulates.
- [[france]] — the primary continental geopolitical and cultural rival across the Middle Ages and early modern era.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the cultural epoch that wedded humanist vernacular literature to early modern state identity.
- [[english-renaissance-poetry]] — literary medium celebrating sovereign majesty and mythological foundations of the English realm.
- [[british-empire]] — the global imperial expansion that subsequently subsumed and projected English national self-conception abroad.
- [[romanticism]] — literary movement that re-imagined the English landscape and folklore as bulwarks of national genius.
