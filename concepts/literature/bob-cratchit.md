## summary

**Bob Cratchit** is the impoverished, good-natured clerk employed by the miser *[[ebenezer-scrooge]]* in the 1843 novella *A Christmas Carol* by *[[charles-dickens]]*. Residing in a cramped Camden Town four-room dwelling in Victorian *[[england]]*, he labors tirelessly to sustain a loving family of eight on a meager weekly wage of fifteen shillings during the *[[industrial-revolution]]*. Despite working in a freezing counting-house and enduring chronic economic hardship, he exemplifies unwavering warmth, humility, and festive generosity. His domestic life—most poignantly his tender relationship with his frail, disabled son *[[tiny-tim]]*—forms the emotional core of the story and serves as the ultimate moral impetus for Scrooge's spiritual redemption.

## you gotta know

- Toils as the underpaid, long-suffering clerk at the London counting-house of Scrooge and Marley, confined to a frigid, windowless cell known as "the tank" where he is forbidden to replenish the dying fire from the coal-box.
- Father to the frail, crippled boy *[[tiny-tim]]*, whom he carries through the snowy London streets on his shoulder, treasuring his son's pious cheer and dreading the child's impending demise without medical assistance.
- Demonstrates saintly magnanimity during Christmas dinner by offering a sincere toast to *[[ebenezer-scrooge]]* as "the Founder of the Feast," prompting an impassioned protest from his wife, who denounces the employer as stingy and heartless.
- Receives an unexpected act of benevolence on Boxing Day when his reformed employer pretends to scold him for arriving eighteen minutes late, only to announce an immediate salary raise and promise to help his family.
- Weeps bitterly in the grief-filled vision presented by the *Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come*, which portrays him returning from his son's churchyard burial to an uncharacteristically quiet home before breaking down beside the boy's little bed.
- Presides with heartfelt joy over a modest holiday feast of a small stuffed goose, sage and onions, and a flaming pudding, taking pride in his family's resourcefulness and mutual affection.
- Accompanies his older son *Peter* in seeking employment and celebrates his daughter *Martha* returning from her milliner's apprenticeship, reflecting the real economic struggles of youth during the *[[industrial-revolution]]*.
- Wears a threadbare white comforter that dangles below his waist, lacking a proper winter coat because his fifteen-shilling weekly salary leaves no surplus after paying rent and feeding his household.

## connections

- [[charles-dickens]] — Victorian novelist who created the character in 1843 to expose social inequality and champion the welfare of the poor.
- [[ebenezer-scrooge]] — his grasping employer whose dramatic moral awakening is spurred by witnessing the clerk's humble home.
- [[tiny-tim]] — his vulnerable youngest son whose gentle piety and fragile health symbolize the human cost of unbridled industrial greed.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical transformation whose harsh working conditions and lack of labor protections defined clerical life in London.
- [[realism]] — the literary mode employed by Dickens to portray the authentic domestic details, low wages, and financial anxieties of working families.
- [[christianity]] — the moral ethos embodying charity, forgiveness, and unconditional love that guides his actions throughout the story.
- [[england]] — the Victorian nation whose capital city forms the atmospheric, fog-laden setting for the counting-house and Camden Town.
