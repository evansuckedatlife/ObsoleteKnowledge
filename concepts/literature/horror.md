## summary

**Horror** is a genre and affect in literature and film that aims to induce terror, revulsion, and existential dread through the representation of the grotesque, supernatural, or violently transgressive. Unlike mere fear, horror locates terror in what *should not exist*—the monster, the undead, the psychologically fractured—and forces readers to contemplate the collapse of the natural order or of reason itself. From *Edgar Allan Poe's* interior nightmares to cinematic visions of corruption, horror functions as a space where the repressed, the forbidden, and the annihilating erupts into the everyday.

## you gotta know

- Emerges from *Gothic literature* (18th–19th century) as a mode that transgresses *Enlightenment* rationalism by centring the irrational, the supernatural, and the decadent; *Frankenstein* and *Dracula* define the form.
- *Edgar Allan Poe* transformed horror from gothic spectacle into *psychological terror*: his narrators are often unreliable, paranoid, or mad; the horror is as much internal (guilt, delusion) as external (the supernatural).
- Horror operates through *violation*: the transgression of natural boundaries (the dead rising, the body corrupting, the mind fracturing), which produces both visceral disgust and philosophical dread.
- Differs from *thriller* or *suspense* in its focus on the *ontologically wrong*—the monster is not merely dangerous but an affront to reality itself; fear comes from its very existence, not merely its threat.
- In *cinema*, horror diverged into *psychological horror* (focusing on mind over spectacle: *The Haunting*) and *visceral horror* (prioritizing grotesque imagery); both aim at terror but through different means.
- Horror often carries *social allegory*: monsters embody fears of technology, disease, sexuality, racial otherness, or social decay; the genre allows transgressive social critique through fantastic means.
- Contemporary horror fragments into sub-genres (*cosmic horror*, *body horror*, *folk horror*), each localizing terror in different dimensions of human vulnerability.

## connections

- [[gothic-literature]] — the foundational mode from which horror emerges; the gothic's transgression of reason births horror's monsters.
- [[edgar-allan-poe]] — master of psychological horror who internalized terror, making the narrator's mind itself the site of horror.
- [[the-tell-tale-heart]] — the story that exemplifies horror's power to render guilt and paranoia as auditory hallucination.
- [[psychological-horror]] — the subgenre focusing on internal mental states rather than external monsters.
- [[nosferatu]] — the cinematic horror film that established vampire iconography and set the aesthetic for 20th-century horror cinema.
- [[the-cabinet-of-dr-caligari]] — German Expressionist cinema that weaponized distorted visual form to produce psychological horror without spectacle.
