## summary

**Prospero** is the protagonist of *Shakespeare's* *The Tempest*, a magician-duke who was usurped and exiled to an island where he has spent twelve years mastering occult arts to engineer his enemies' shipwreck and orchestrate their reconciliation. A figure of almost godlike power within his island realm, Prospero embodies the fantasy of total knowledge and control; yet his journey involves learning to surrender that power, freeing his enslaved subjects and acknowledging limits to what magic—or will—can accomplish. Prospero has been read as Shakespeare himself, as a colonial master, and as an archetypal figure of authority wrestling with its own renunciation.

## you gotta know

- The rightful Duke of Milan, Prospero was deposed by his brother *Antonio* and cast adrift with his infant daughter *Miranda*; they washed ashore on an uninhabited island.
- Spent twelve years studying magic and the occult, developing supernatural power over the island's weather, spirits, and inhabitants; his knowledge grants him near-absolute control over events.
- Binds the spirit *Ariel* to his service through magic, extracting labour in exchange for the promise of eventual freedom; their relationship is one of coercion disguised as contractual obligation.
- Enslaves *Caliban*, the island's native inhabitant, subjecting him to servitude and magical punishment; their relationship encodes colonial power and the justification of colonisation through claims of civilising the savage.
- Orchestrates a tempest that shipwrecks his usurper *Antonio* and other enemies on the island, creating conditions for magical theatre and forced reconciliation rather than simple revenge.
- Renounces his magic in the final act, recognising that power—even supernatural power—cannot guarantee human hearts or lasting peace; his surrender of magic parallels Shakespeare's surrender of the playwright's art.
- Often read as a self-portrait of Shakespeare himself, the artist-magician who controls the stage, manipulates audience emotion, and ultimately acknowledges the illusory nature of theatrical power.
