## summary

**Noh** is a form of classical Japanese musical drama that emerged in the 14th century and remains a living tradition. Distinguished by its economy of movement, poetic language, and refined aesthetic principles, Noh represents one of the world's oldest continuously performed theatrical forms. *Zeami*, the great master of the 15th century, codified the form and elevated it to an art of extraordinary psychological and spiritual depth.

## you gotta know

- Emerged during the Muromachi period as an evolution of earlier performance traditions; Zeami systematized and theorized the form in his critical essays (*Kadensho* and others).
- Performances are spare and stylized: minimal sets, elaborate masks, symbolic props, and highly trained actors whose movement is deliberate and weighted with meaning; training takes decades.
- The *shite* (principal actor) often portrays a supernatural being—a ghost, a demon, or a god—revealing truths about human nature or historical trauma through poetic dialogue and dance.
- Noh is structured around the principle of *yohaku no bi*—beauty of emptiness—where what is omitted matters as much as what is performed; silence and stillness are active artistic choices.
- The form draws on Zen Buddhist aesthetics and courtly literary traditions, particularly the narrative sophistication of works like *The Tale of Genji*; literature and performance fuse.
- Language is archaic, poetic, and highly stylized; audiences must follow both the verbal text and the visual/gestural vocabulary to access meaning; comprehension is active work.
- A typical Noh performance features five plays organized by category (god, warrior, woman, madness, demon), with comic interludes (*kyogen*) between them; the program itself becomes a journey.
- Noh's influence on Western modernism (Yeats, Pound) was profound; it offered an alternative to realistic theatre and validated aesthetic principles of restraint and symbolism.

## connections

- [[zeami]] — the master theorist and dramatist who perfected and documented Noh's principles.
- [[murasaki-shikibu]] — whose *The Tale of Genji* provided narrative material and aesthetic ideals that Noh drew upon.
- [[muromachi-period]] — the historical era in which Noh crystallized into classical form.
- [[japan]] — the nation and culture that produced this unique theatrical tradition.
- [[japanese-aesthetics]] — the refined principles of beauty, restraint, and emptiness that Noh embodies.
