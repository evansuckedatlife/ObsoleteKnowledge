## summary

**doublethink** is a psychological and political mechanism coined by *George Orwell* in his dystopian 1949 novel *Nineteen Eighty-Four*. It denotes the cognitive ability to hold two mutually contradictory beliefs in one's mind simultaneously, accepting both as true with absolute conviction. Devised by the ruling Party of *Oceania*, this doctrine serves as the ultimate instrument of totalitarian control, systematically destroying objective reality and intellectual integrity.

## you gotta know

- Defined within the novel's text through the banned treatise *The Theory and Practice of Oligarchical Collectivism*, attributed to *Emmanuel Goldstein*.
- Requires an act of conscious self-hypnosis and willful forgetting termed *crimestop*, which halts dangerous heterodox thoughts before they can form.
- Exemplified by the Party's foundational paradoxes: *War is Peace*, *Freedom is Slavery*, and *Ignorance is Strength*.
- Enforced operationally by the *Ministry of Truth* (*Minitrue*), which perpetually rewrites historical records while workers genuinely believe no alteration has occurred.
- Operates in tandem with *Newspeak*, the state-constructed language engineered to diminish the range of human thought and eliminate nuance.
- Contrasted with simple hypocrisy or deceit; the practitioner must sincerely believe in the falsehood while deliberately forgetting that deception occurred.
- Culminates in the psychological torture of the protagonist *Winston Smith* in *Room 101* by the Party interrogator *O'Brien*, compelling him to love *Big Brother*.
