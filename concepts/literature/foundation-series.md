## summary

The **Foundation series** is a landmark epic of mid-twentieth-century [[science-fiction]] created by Russian-American author Isaac Asimov, originally serialized between 1942 and 1950 during [[world-war-ii]] and its immediate aftermath. Inspired by the decline of the [[roman-empire]], the narrative follows mathematician Hari Seldon, whose statistical science of psychohistory predicts the inevitable collapse of the Galactic Empire and maps a pathway through barbarism. Recognized as a defining milestone of the Golden Age of speculative fiction, the original trilogy earned a special Hugo Award for Best All-Time Series in 1966.

## you gotta know

- Centers on the fictional science of psychohistory, developed by Hari Seldon, which uses statistical sociology and mathematics to predict the broad behavioral trajectories of quadrillions of humans over millennia.
- Originally published as short stories and novellas in John W. Campbell's magazine *Astounding Science Fiction* before being compiled into the classic trilogy: *Foundation* (1951), *Foundation and Empire* (1952), and *Second Foundation* (1953).
- Structured around a series of recurring sociopolitical crises called Seldon Crises, wherein the isolated outpost planet Terminus overcomes external threats through technological monopolies, religious cultism, and economic diplomacy.
- Disrupts Seldon's deterministic historical model through the introduction of the Mule, a powerful mutant telepath capable of emotionally manipulating adversaries and conquering the galaxy outside statistical predictions.
- Features the clandestine Second Foundation, a hidden group of mentalists located at "Star's End" tasked with secretly guiding the Plan back on course and preserving human civilization.
- Drew direct structural and thematic inspiration from Edward Gibbon's classic historical treatise *The History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire*.
- Expanded decades later by Asimov in novels like *Foundation's Edge* (1982) and *Foundation and Earth* (1986), bridging the universe with his Robot mysteries and Galactic Empire sagas.

## connections

- [[science-fiction]] — established the template for galactic empires, sociology-driven speculative worldbuilding, and Golden Age conceptual literature.
- [[world-war-ii]] — drafted during the global conflict, reflecting wartime anxieties over civilizational collapse, industrial mobilization, and ideological totalitarianism.
- [[roman-empire]] — modeled its imperial decay, frontier provinces, and feudal devolution directly on ancient Roman historiography.
- [[cold-war]] — mirrored post-war geopolitical tensions, ideological subterfuge, balance-of-power diplomacy, and covert nuclear deterrence.
- [[united-states]] — embodied mid-century American techno-optimism, celebrating scientific rationality, engineering pragmatism, and merchant capitalism.
- [[form]] — exemplifies the episodic "fix-up" novel structure, assembling discrete magazine novellas into an overarching historical chronicle.
