## summary

The **Ghost of Hamlet's father** appears in *Hamlet* as a spectre demanding vengeance, one of literature's most iconic supernatural figures. The ghost embodies the tragedy's central moral engine: Hamlet's uncertainty whether he is witnessing divine justice, demonic deception, or psychological hallucination, and the subsequent spiral into madness and violence triggered by his encounter with a perhaps-imaginary accuser.

## you gotta know

- Appears first to the guards on the castle rampart, terrifying them with its physical presence and apparent distress, establishing an atmosphere of supernatural dread.
- Reveals to Hamlet that he was murdered by his brother *Claudius* while sleeping—poisoned through the ear—and demands revenge, cursing Claudius to eternal damnation.
- The method of murder (poisoning through the ear) is itself symbolic: a violation of the intimate passage through which one hears truth and receives communion, making it a peculiarly literary and psychological violation.
- Is never unambiguously confirmed as real: Hamlet's doubts about its authenticity drive the plot (hence the "Mousetrap" play to confirm Claudius's guilt), reflecting Renaissance uncertainty about spectral reality and demonic deception.
- Appears again in *Gertrude's* bedroom (the "closet scene"), visible only to Hamlet; his mother cannot see it, deepening audience uncertainty about whether Hamlet is experiencing genuine supernatural contact or descent into madness.
- Represents the weight of paternal authority and obligation: Hamlet's inability to doubt or disobey the ghost traps him in a predetermined revenge plot, raising questions about free will and filial duty.
- Has been interpreted by modern psychology and criticism as a manifestation of Hamlet's grief and ambivalence—projecting his superego as a spectral command, blaming his uncle for what may be his own inner conflict.
- Symbolizes the past's claim on the present: the ghost's refusal to let Hamlet remain passive, insisting on action and violence, drives the tragedy's catastrophic final act.
