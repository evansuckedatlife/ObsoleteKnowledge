## summary

**Alice B. Toklas** was an American writer, salon hostess, and cultural figure who served as the lifelong companion, editor, and confidante of avant-garde author *Gertrude Stein*. Resident in [[france]] for nearly six decades, she managed the celebrated Parisian salon at 27 rue de Fleurus, nurturing the visual and literary talents of early twentieth-century [[modernism]]. Though world-renowned as the eponymous subject and nominal narrator of Stein's commercial bestseller *The Autobiography of Alice B. Toklas*, she was an accomplished author in her own right, remembered especially for her memoir-cookbook and its lasting influence on literary culture.

## you gotta know

- Lent her name and literary persona to *The Autobiography of Alice B. Toklas* (1933), an experimental memoir written by *Gertrude Stein* from Toklas's viewpoint that became Stein's greatest commercial triumph.
- Authored *The Alice B. Toklas Cook Book* (1954), an acclaimed collection of culinary reflections and personal memoirs featuring *Brion Gysin*'s recipe for "Haschich Fudge," which became a counterculture touchstone.
- Organized the salon gatherings at 27 rue de Fleurus in Paris, entertaining prominent expatriates and painters including *Pablo Picasso*, *Henri Matisse*, and *Ernest Hemingway*.
- Formed an intimate personal and working partnership with Stein beginning in 1907, serving as typist, proofreader, and co-publisher through their private venture, the *Plain Edition*.
- Volunteered during [[world-war-i]] by driving an outfitted delivery van nicknamed *Auntie* across rural [[france]] to deliver medical supplies for the American Fund for French Wounded.
- Survived the German occupation of [[france]] during [[world-war-ii]] alongside Stein in rural Bilignin, enduring wartime deprivation as an elderly Jewish-American expatriate under the Vichy regime.
- Published a formal memoir, *What Is Remembered* (1963), recounting her independent recollections of the bohemian artistic circles of the early twentieth century.
- Suffered significant financial hardship late in life after Stein's relatives claimed ownership of the couple's valuable art collection, and was eventually buried next to Stein in Paris's *Père Lachaise Cemetery*.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — acted as an indispensable domestic host and logistical supporter for the central creators of the Parisian avant-garde movement.
- [[american-literature]] — shaped the production and reception of Lost Generation writing, advising Stein and critiquing the work of expatriate authors.
- [[france]] — lived as an expatriate in Paris and the countryside from 1907 until her death, adopting its language, culture, and culinary traditions.
- [[united-states]] — country of her birth and early adulthood in California before her permanent departure following the 1906 San Francisco earthquake.
- [[world-war-i]] — transported humanitarian relief supplies across French military sectors to assist wounded soldiers and civilian refugees.
- [[world-war-ii]] — endured rural isolation and persecution risks while protecting Stein's personal correspondence and literary manuscripts.
- [[poetry]] — assisted in the transcription, typesetting, and independent publication of modernist verse produced within Stein's circle.
