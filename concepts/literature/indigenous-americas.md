## summary

**Indigenous Americas** refers to the native peoples and cultures of North, Central, and South America before, during, and after European colonisation, from the sophisticated civilisations of the *Aztec* and *Inca* to the countless smaller nations across the continent. In literature, the term encompasses both the pre-Columbian oral traditions and written texts, and the later 20th-century recovery and assertion of indigenous voices and histories in the face of centuries of colonial erasure and violence. Indigenous American themes—land, identity, cultural survival, spiritual knowledge—have become central to Latin American and North American literature.

## you gotta know

- The Americas prior to 1492 were home to millions of people across hundreds of distinct cultures, linguistic groups, and political formations, not a "wilderness."
- European colonisation devastated indigenous populations through violence, enslavement, and above all epidemic disease (smallpox, measles) that killed an estimated 90% of native peoples in many regions.
- Indigenous oral traditions and codices (pre-Columbian written records) preserved mythology, history, and knowledge systems fundamentally different from European frameworks.
- *Miguel Asturias* and other 20th-century Latin American writers recovered indigenous narratives and challenged the European colonial narrative of the Americas.
- Indigenous American worldviews—cyclical time, sacred landscapes, spiritual ecology—offered alternatives to Western rationalism and linear progress, influencing modernist and postcolonial literature.
- Contemporary indigenous American literature explicitly reclaims voice and agency, centering indigenous perspectives in their own stories rather than as objects of European observation.
- The literary and historical study of indigenous Americas reveals colonialism not as a "bringing of civilisation" but as a catastrophic interruption and theft.
