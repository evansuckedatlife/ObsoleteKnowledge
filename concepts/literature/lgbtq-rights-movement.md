## summary

The **LGBTQ rights movement** is a social and political struggle for legal recognition, freedom from discrimination, and full civic participation for people of non-heterosexual and non-cisgender identities. Beginning in the 1960s with the Stonewall uprising and accelerating through the decades, the movement has challenged laws criminalizing homosexuality, fought against systemic discrimination, and claimed cultural space through art and literature. Writers and artists have been central to this struggle, articulating experiences of marginalization and envisioning liberated futures.

## you gotta know

- The Stonewall Inn uprising (1969) in New York catalyzed the modern gay liberation movement, though same-sex love and identity have always existed; Stonewall transformed from spontaneous riot into foundational political memory.
- Early movement priorities included decriminalization of homosexuality (still illegal in many jurisdictions), employment protections, and medical recognition of homosexuality as not a disorder; these remain contested issues globally.
- The [[hiv-aids-crisis]] of the 1980s-90s devastated queer communities and galvanized both grassroots activism and artistic production; literature became a vehicle for documenting loss, grief, and community resilience.
- Art and literature became central to the movement—queer writers claimed public voice and visible authorship as a form of resistance and self-assertion; visibility became a political act.
- The movement expanded to include transgender and non-binary identities, broadening beyond a single-axis sexual orientation focus to encompass gender diversity; intersectionality emerged as crucial theoretical framework.
- Contemporary priorities include legal recognition of transgender rights, elimination of conversion "therapy," and global advocacy in countries where homosexuality remains criminalized; the movement remains unfinished.
