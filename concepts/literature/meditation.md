## summary

**Meditation** is a contemplative practice and spiritual discipline common to multiple religious and philosophical traditions, in which the mind is trained to focus inward through sustained attention, breath control, and the quieting of discursive thought. Far more than mere relaxation, meditation is understood in *Buddhism* and *Hinduism* as a path toward enlightenment, self-knowledge, and the transcendence of ego. In literature, meditation becomes both *subject matter* (the meditative experience rendered in text) and *formal technique* (poetry and prose structured to induce meditative states in the reader).

## you gotta know

- Central to *Theravada Buddhism*: a monastic and lay practice in which meditation (*bhavana*) is the primary means of developing mental discipline and insight into the nature of suffering and non-self.
- *Zen Buddhism* emphasizes *zazen* (sitting meditation) as direct access to Buddha-nature; enlightenment (*satori*) arrives through meditation, not intellectual understanding; the paradox is central to Zen.
- In *Hinduism* and *Yoga*, meditation is inseparable from *pranayama* (breath control) and *asana* (posture); the goal is union with *Brahman* (ultimate reality) through systematic mental cultivation.
- In *literature*, meditation becomes a formal property: *haiku* and other Eastern poetic forms structure the reader's attention to produce a meditative state; *silence*, *emptiness*, and *brevity* are techniques that trigger contemplation.
- The meditative tradition values what is *not said*: the space between words, the pause in breath, the emptiness from which meaning emerges; this aesthetic deeply influences minimalist and Zen-influenced literary forms.
- Modern secular meditation divorces the practice from its religious context, treating it primarily as a psychological or physiological technique; this separation obscures meditation's original soteriological (salvation-seeking) purpose.
- Literature *about* meditation often attempts to *induce* a meditative state; the reader's engagement with the text becomes a form of practice.

## connections

- [[zen-buddhism]] — the Buddhist tradition in which meditation is the central path to enlightenment.
- [[theravada-buddhism]] — the oldest Buddhist school, where meditation remains the foundational monastic practice.
- [[yoga]] — the Hindu discipline in which meditation is integrated with physical posture and breath control toward liberation (*moksha*).
- [[haiku]] — the poetic form that uses brevity and emptiness to induce contemplation in the reader.
- [[buddhism]] — the broader religious tradition to which meditation is fundamental; the practice is inseparable from Buddhist philosophy.
- [[enlightenment]] — the goal of meditation in Buddhist and Hindu traditions; the state of awakening or liberation.
