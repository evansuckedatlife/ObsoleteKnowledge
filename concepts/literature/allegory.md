## summary

**Allegory** is a literary device and narrative form in which characters, events, and settings operate on two simultaneous levels of meaning: a surface literal tale and an underlying symbolic framework representing abstract moral, philosophical, or political truths. Originating in classical antiquity through figures like *Plato* in [[ancient-greece]] and flourishing through medieval drama, the form allows storytellers to embody intangible virtues, vices, and spiritual struggles as living participants. From the devotional pilgrimage narratives of [[christianity]] to twentieth-century political [[satire]], allegory derives its lasting artistic power from this sustained dual vision, functioning as engaging fiction and rigorous cultural critique at once.

## you gotta know

- *George Orwell*'s novella *Animal Farm* stands as the quintessential modern political allegory, using a farmyard revolution where mistreated beasts overthrow their human master to map every character, event, and political faction directly onto the rise of the [[soviet-union]] and its descent into totalitarian rule under *Joseph Stalin*.
- *Plato*'s *Allegory of the Cave* from the *Republic* in [[ancient-greece]] remains the most influential philosophical allegory in Western thought, depicting shackled prisoners who mistake flickering wall shadows for true reality until a liberated thinker climbs upward into the blinding sunlight of the *Forms*.
- *John Bunyan*'s 1678 devotional masterwork *The Pilgrim's Progress* established the English prose standard for religious allegory, charting the fraught journey of *Christian* from the *City of Destruction* to the *Celestial City* past personified spiritual hazards like the *Slough of Despond* and *Vanity Fair*.
- Late medieval morality plays such as *Everyman* popularized dramatic allegory on the stage, embodying abstract concepts as speaking characters—including *Fellowship*, *Kindred*, *Goods*, and *Good Deeds*—to dramatize the solitary soul facing mortality and judgment in [[christianity]].
- *Edmund Spenser*'s epic poem *The Faerie Queene*, composed during the [[english-renaissance]], layered intricate moral, theological, and dynastic allegories into chivalric romance, assigning knights to represent virtues like *Holiness* and *Temperance* while celebrating the Protestant rule of Queen *Elizabeth I*.
- *Dante Alighieri*'s fourteenth-century epic poem *Divine Comedy* in medieval [[italy]] uses a literal voyage across the realms of the afterlife to map the spiritual conversion of the soul, guided through the underworld by the Roman poet *Virgil* representing human reason and through paradise by *Beatrice* representing divine grace.
- *Prudentius*'s fourth-century Latin poem *Psychomachia* established the enduring Western template for internal psychological allegory by staging an epic battle between personified Christian virtues and pagan vices contending for the human soul.

## connections

- [[satire]] — provides the primary tonal mechanism for political allegories seeking to expose and ridicule state hypocrisy, authoritarianism, and systemic corruption.
- [[christianity]] — supplied the central theological symbols, scripture, and moral framework that sustained medieval morality plays and religious journey narratives.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the philosophical cradle where thinkers first employed sustained allegorical parables to explore epistemology and the nature of the state.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the flourishing artistic period that produced monumental verse allegories synthesizing classical mythology, Christian theology, and Tudor court politics.
- [[soviet-union]] — the twentieth-century totalitarian state whose historical betrayals and bureaucratic abuses formed the precise allegorical target of *George Orwell*'s major fables.
- [[cold-war]] — the global ideological conflict that heightened the relevance and publication of allegorical narratives addressing surveillance, censorship, and ideological conformity.
- [[poetry]] — the traditional formal medium through which classical and Renaissance writers sustained intricate, multifaceted allegorical schemes over thousands of lines.
