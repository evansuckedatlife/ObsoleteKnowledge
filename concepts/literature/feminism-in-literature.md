## summary

**Feminism in literature** encompasses creative writing and critical inquiry that investigate women's societal conditions, resist patriarchal hegemony, and recover marginalized female perspectives across literary history. Beginning with early tracts advocating educational equity and culminating in twentieth-century theoretical frameworks such as gynocriticism and écriture féminine, it reshaped canons and interpretative methods worldwide. This discipline is essential for analyzing how literary representation constructs gender, encodes systemic subjugation, and provides aesthetic strategies for liberation.

## you gotta know

- *Virginia Woolf* founded modern feminist literary theory in her 1929 essay *A Room of One's Own*, famously arguing that a woman must have money and independent space to write fiction.
- *Mary Wollstonecraft* established early feminist political philosophy in literature with her 1792 treatise *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman*, critiquing domestic disenfranchisement.
- Feminist scholars *Sandra Gilbert* and *Susan Gubar* published the seminal 1979 study *The Madwoman in the Attic*, demonstrating how Victorian women writers encoded rage and rebellion into monstrous doubles.
- Theorist *Elaine Showalter* pioneered gynocriticism, establishing a distinct framework for studying women's writing across three historical phases: Feminine imitation, Feminist protest, and Female self-discovery.
- French theorist *Hélène Cixous* championed écriture féminine in her 1975 manifesto *The Laugh of the Medusa*, urging women to write through bodily experience to shatter phallocentric discourse.
- In *The House of the Spirits*, Chilean author *Isabel Allende* deployed [[magical-realism]] to foreground generational female resilience and political defiance during authoritarian turmoil.
- Black feminist critics and authors such as *Alice Walker*, who coined womanism, and *Toni Morrison* expanded literary analysis to confront the intersection of gender, race, and systemic class oppression.

## connections

- [[virginia-woolf]] — authored foundational modernist essays and experimental novels that revolutionized feminist literary aesthetics and canon critique.
- [[modernism]] — provided formal innovations such as stream-of-consciousness that allowed feminist authors to represent interior female psychology outside patriarchal structures.
- [[female-power]] — serves as a central thematic preoccupation within feminist writing, interrogating how women secure and sustain agency against social suppression.
- [[feminist-speculative-fiction]] — extends feminist literary objectives into non-realist worlds to critique real-world gender and reproductive oppression.
- [[realism]] — provided nineteenth-century women authors a medium to expose marriage economies, domestic claustrophobia, and legal disenfranchisement.
- [[magical-realism]] — utilized by Latin American writers like *Isabel Allende* to weave women's spiritual agency and political resistance into national histories.
