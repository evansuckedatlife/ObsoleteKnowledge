## summary

**Thornton Wilder** was an American playwright and novelist (1897–1975) whose innovative works blended avant-garde theatrical techniques with vernacular American themes. His most famous play, *Our Town*, employed a minimalist stage, a Stage Manager narrator, and meditations on mortality and community, becoming an iconic text of American drama. Simultaneously, his adaptations and original works shaped musical theater, most notably providing the source material for *Hello, Dolly!*, demonstrating his versatility across literary and performance media.

## you gotta know

- Born in Wisconsin, educated at Yale and Princeton; his early career alternated between fiction, drama, and translation work.
- *Our Town* (1938) breaks the fourth wall repeatedly; the Stage Manager addresses the audience directly, creating intimacy and philosophical distance simultaneously.
- *Our Town* won the Pulitzer Prize and radically simplified theatrical aesthetics—no scenery, no curtain, props mimed rather than represented physically.
- Also won the Pulitzer twice: once for *The Bridge of San Luis Rey* (novel) and once for *Our Town*, making him the only writer to win the award three times in two categories.
- *The Matchmaker* (1954), his play about a widowed matchmaker in New York, was adapted into the musical *Hello, Dolly!* (1963), starring *Carol Channing*.
- Served as cultural attaché abroad and was an intellectual liberal voice on American democracy and small-town values during the Cold War.
- *Our Town* focuses on the ordinary lives of residents in Groves Corners, New Hampshire—two neighboring families—and finds profound philosophical significance in their mundane routines.
- The Stage Manager's commentary transforms domestic details into meditations on mortality, love, and the human condition; he functions as both narrator and chorus.
- *Wilder's* theatrical innovations influenced absurdist and meta-theatrical movements later in the twentieth century; his minimalism anticipated modernist theatrical practice.
- He also wrote novels, essays, and translations of classical works; his intellectual breadth and commitment to theater as serious art elevated American drama's cultural status.
