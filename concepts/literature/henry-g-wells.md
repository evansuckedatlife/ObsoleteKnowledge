## summary

**H. G. Wells** (born **Herbert George Wells**, occasionally indexed as **Henry G. Wells**) was an English novelist, social prophet, and public intellectual recognized alongside Jules Verne as a founding architect of modern [[science-fiction]]. Emerging from working-class roots in late Victorian [[england]], he achieved worldwide fame through a rapid series of pioneering "scientific romances" that deployed imaginative premises to interrogate imperialism, class stratification, and biological evolution. In his mature career, he transitioned toward social realism, popular history, and progressive internationalist advocacy, actively campaigning for world governance, human rights, and socialist reform through the cataclysms of two world wars.

## you gotta know

- Popularized the concept of deliberate temporal displacement in *The Time Machine* (1895), wherein an anonymous *Time Traveller* journeys to the distant future and discovers human evolution divided into the aristocratic *Eloi* and cannibalistic *Morlocks*.
- Established the extraterrestrial invasion archetype in *The War of the Worlds* (1898), depicting a ruthless Martian assault on southern England utilizing heat rays, black smoke, and towering tripod war machines.
- Addressed bioethics, animal experimentation, and evolutionary regression in *The Island of Doctor Moreau* (1896), where a stranded gentleman encounters vivisected "Beast Folk" bound by the recitation of the Law.
- Portrayed the moral corruption born of scientific hubris in *The Invisible Man* (1897), chronicling the chemist *Griffin* as he descends into paranoid violence and attempts to institute a "Reign of Terror."
- Accurately predicted twentieth-century military developments such as armored tanks in "The Land Ironclads" (1903) and atomic weapons in *The World Set Free* (1914), a book that directly inspired physicist Leó Szilárd to patent the nuclear chain reaction.
- Wrote widely praised comic realist novels about lower-middle-class Edwardian strivers, including *Kipps* (1905) and *The History of Mr Polly* (1910), drawing upon his youthful draper's apprenticeship.
- Published *The Outline of History* (1919–1920), an immensely popular multi-volume historical survey composed in the wake of [[world-war-i]] to advance universalist education and international harmony.
- Authored the 1940 human rights treatise *The Rights of Man*, which played a foundational role in framing the intellectual consensus for the United Nations Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

## connections

- [[science-fiction]] — genre whose cornerstone conventions of time travel, alien conquest, mad science, and biological dystopia he codified.
- [[henri-bergson]] — French philosopher whose concept of duration as qualitative lived time challenged Wells's fictional treatment of time as a fourth spatial dimension.
- [[england]] — native country whose suburban commuter belts, provincial towns, and imperial anxieties provided the setting for his major romances.
- [[british-empire]] — global imperial power whose colonial brutality he mirrored and satirized by subjecting Victorian London to Martian conquest.
- [[colonialism]] — historical dynamic of conquest and subjugation whose ideological justifications he systematically undermined in his speculative narratives.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — technological transformation whose machinery, urban sprawl, and labor tensions directly inspired his evolutionary speculations.
- [[world-war-i]] — epochal global conflict that galvanized his ardent advocacy for international leagues, global disarmament, and world federation.
- [[modernism]] — contemporary artistic movement whose aesthetic leaders, including [[virginia-woolf]], famously criticized his didactic narrative methods.
