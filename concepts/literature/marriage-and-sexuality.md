## summary

**Marriage and Sexuality** as literary themes explore the emotional and philosophical complexity of intimate relationships—desire, fidelity, possession, and the gap between romantic ideals and lived reality. Writers from Shakespeare to Tolstoy have used marriage as a crucible for examining power, morality, and human nature. These themes are central to tragedy, realism, and modern fiction.

## you gotta know

- Marriage in literature often serves as a testing ground for idealism, revealing how personal desires collide with social structures and moral law.
- Infidelity and jealousy emerge as drivers of tragic action, transforming private emotions into matters of honor and consequence; the cuckolded husband becomes a central figure in both tragedy and comedy.
- Tolstoy's *The Kreutzer Sonata* presents marriage as a battlefield of competing egos and sexual power; his narrator indicts sexuality itself as a corruption of genuine human connection, making sexuality a source of sin rather than love.
- Sexuality in literature is rarely depicted as simple gratification—it entangles with identity, spirituality, violence, and existential meaning; from Eros to guilt, writers use sexual acts as windows into consciousness.
- The conflict between romantic love and social duty animates tragedies across cultures, from Greek drama to Shakespeare to modern psychological fiction; this collision is rarely resolved without death or ruin.
- Feminist literature has reclaimed marriage narratives, centering women's autonomy and the erasure of female desire in patriarchal systems; contemporary novelists rewrite marriage as negotiation rather than subordination.
- Victorian literature developed the "married unhappily" as a dominant theme, exploring the gap between public respectability and private misery; George Eliot and Thomas Hardy dissect this emptiness.
- Sexual transgression—adultery, seduction, conquest—connects to broader themes of power, law, and moral authority; literature uses these acts to interrogate what we truly owe to each other.
