## summary

**Dashiell Hammett** (1894–1961) was an American writer who pioneered the hard-boiled detective novel, transforming crime fiction from genteel puzzle-solving into gritty, morally ambiguous urban narratives. Drawing on his own experience as a Pinkerton detective, Hammett created unforgettable protagonists like *Sam Spade* and the Continental Op, who navigated a world of corruption, violence, and moral compromise. His spare, laconic prose style and cynical worldview established the template for twentieth-century noir fiction and influenced generations of crime writers.

## you gotta know

- Worked as a Pinkerton operative before turning to fiction, giving his work an authenticity of detail and attitude that genteel mysteries lacked.
- Created *Sam Spade* in *The Maltese Falcon* (1930), a detective who is capable, independent, and willing to bend the law to survive in a corrupt world.
- Also wrote *The Big Sleep* (credited to Raymond Chandler), *Red Harvest*, and *The Dain Curse*, establishing the conventions of hard-boiled detective fiction.
- His prose style—stripped of sentiment, ironic, focused on action and dialogue—became the gold standard for crime and noir fiction.
- Adapted to film and television repeatedly; *The Maltese Falcon*, *The Big Sleep*, and adaptations of his work became classics of cinema noir.
- His later life was marked by political persecution during the McCarthy era for his alleged communist sympathies; he refused to name names and spent time in prison for contempt of court.

## connections
