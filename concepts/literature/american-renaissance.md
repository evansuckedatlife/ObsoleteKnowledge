## summary

The **American Renaissance** refers to a flourishing of American arts and letters in the early-to-mid 20th century, particularly centered in *New York City*. During this period, literature, visual art, theater, and music achieved new prominence and innovation, with New York emerging as a rival to European cultural capitals. The movement reflected America's rising economic and political power while fostering experiments in form and expression that defined modern culture.

## you gotta know

- Occurred roughly between the 1910s and 1940s, though its cultural effects persisted for decades.
- *New York City* served as the epicenter: home to publishers, galleries, theaters, cafes, and magazines that fostered artistic collaboration and experimentation.
- The *Harlem Renaissance* was a concurrent and overlapping movement, producing major African American writers, musicians, and visual artists.
- American literature gained international stature through writers like *F. Scott Fitzgerald*, *Ernest Hemingway*, and *Sherwood Anderson*, who adopted modernist techniques while exploring American themes.
- The period saw unprecedented exchange between American and European artists: expatriates, migrants, and traveling performers circulated ideas and forms across the Atlantic.
- Visual artists including Georgia O'Keeffe and the Ashcan School painters developed distinctly American aesthetic vocabularies.
- Theater flourished: *Eugene O'Neill* and others created serious American drama; musicals emerged as a distinctive American art form.
- Jazz, the blues, and vernacular music achieved artistic legitimacy and international influence during this period, often celebrated alongside literature and visual art.
- The period ended symbolically with the Great Depression and World War II, which redirected cultural attention and energy.
