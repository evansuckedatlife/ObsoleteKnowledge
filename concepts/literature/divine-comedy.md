## summary

The **Divine Comedy** is an Italian epic poem composed by *Dante Alighieri* between roughly 1308 and his death in 1321, widely regarded as the preeminent work of Italian literature and a masterpiece of world culture. Structured as an allegorical Christian vision of the afterlife, the poem narrates the author's metaphysical pilgrimage through *Inferno* (Hell), *Purgatorio* (Purgatory), and *Paradiso* (Heaven). Its creation decisively established the Tuscan vernacular as the standardized literary language of Italy and provided the supreme theological synthesis of medieval scholastic thought.

## you gotta know

- Composed in *terza rima*, a challenging hendecasyllabic interlocking three-line rhyme scheme (*aba bcb cdc*) invented by Dante specifically for the work.
- Divided into three distinct *cantiche* containing thirty-three cantos each, with an introductory canto in *Inferno* bringing the grand total to a symbolically perfect one hundred.
- Guided through Hell and Purgatory by the classical Roman poet *Virgil*, who embodies human reason, wisdom, and virtuous paganism prior to the revelation of grace.
- Encounters the lost souls of *Paolo and Francesca* in the second circle of Hell, whose tragic adulterous passion remains one of Western literature's most quoted episodes.
- Depicts the Ninth Circle of Hell, *Cocytus*, as a freezing lake of ice where traitors are punished, anchored by a three-headed *Satan* chewing on *Judas Iscariot*, *Brutus*, and *Cassius*.
- Replaces Virgil with *Beatrice Portinari*, Dante's idealized courtly love and spiritual muse, who guides him through the celestial spheres of *Paradiso*.
- Concludes each of the three cantiche with the exact same final word, *stelle* ("stars"), signifying the upward spiritual orientation of the pilgrim toward the divine.
- Originally titled simply *Commedia* by Dante because it moves from misery to joy; the honorific epithet *Divina* was added by *Giovanni Boccaccio* and first printed in 1555.

## connections

- [[poetry]] — the artistic medium revolutionized by Dante's development and masterful deployment of the *terza rima* form.
- [[christianity]] — the theological and cosmological bedrock supplying the poem's moral architecture, biblical symbolism, and eschatology.
- [[italy]] — the cultural homeland whose linguistic destiny was reshaped when Dante chose vernacular Tuscan over academic Latin.
- [[rome]] — the classical civilization represented by Dante's mentor *Virgil* and celebrated throughout the epic's imperial politics.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural epoch foreshadowed by Dante's intense humanist appreciation of classical pagan poets and philosophers.
- [[t-s-eliot]] — the modernist poet who drew heavily upon the *Inferno* in poems such as *The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock* and *The Waste Land*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the mythological tradition that supplies classical monsters, mythical rivers, and philosophers like *Aristotle* populating Dante's underworld.
