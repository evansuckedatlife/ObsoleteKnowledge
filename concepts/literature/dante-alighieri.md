## summary

**Dante Alighieri** (1265–1321) was an Italian poet and philosopher whose *Divine Comedy* is one of the greatest works of world literature. Writing in the vernacular Florentine Italian rather than Latin, Dante created an epic synthesis of medieval theology, classical learning, and personal allegory that established Italian literature as a major European force and anticipated the *Renaissance* humanist turn.

## you gotta know

- Dante was born and active in *Florence* during a period of intense political and factional strife; his support for the Guelph faction led to his exile in 1302—a trauma that shaped his vision of spiritual pilgrimage and political justice.
- The *Divine Comedy* (completed circa 1320) is an epic poem recounting Dante's journey through Hell (*Inferno*), Purgatory (*Purgatorio*), and Paradise (*Paradiso*), guided by the Roman poet *Virgil* and finally by his beloved *Beatrice*.
- By writing in Italian vernacular rather than Latin, Dante demonstrated that non-Latin languages could express sophisticated theological and philosophical ideas; he thereby opened literature to broader audiences and vindicated the vernacular as a vehicle for high culture.
- The *Divine Comedy* synthesizes medieval theology (especially *Aquinas*), classical literature (*Virgil*, *Ovid*, *Homer*), contemporary politics, and personal experience into a cosmic allegory: the journey parallels both Christian salvation and Dante's own intellectual development.
- Dante's portrayal of Hell and its punishments—suffering proportional to sin—became culturally dominant; his vivid depictions of historical and mythological figures reshaped how Europeans understood those figures.
- The poem anticipates *Renaissance* humanism by integrating pagan classical literature with Christian theology rather than treating them as opposed; it also foregrounds individual human experience and psychology.
- Dante's other works—*La Vita Nuova* (an earlier poetic romance), *Convivio* (philosophical essays), *De Vulgari Eloquentia* (on vernacular language)—were influential in literary theory and the valorization of Italian as a serious language.
