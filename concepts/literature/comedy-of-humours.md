## summary

The **comedy of humours** is a satirical dramatic genre that flourished in late sixteenth- and early seventeenth-century London during the [[english-renaissance]]. Formulated principally by the playwright and poet *Ben Jonson*, the form adapts classical Greek and Roman physiological theories to the comic stage by structuring plots around characters governed by a singular, obsessive psychological inclination. It served as a powerful mechanism for social critique, lampooning metropolitan affectations, vanity, and moral imbalance through starkly individualized caricatures.

## you gotta know

- Popularized on the London stage by *Ben Jonson* with his breakthrough 1598 play *Every Man in His Humour*, an acclaimed production staged by the *Lord Chamberlain's Men* that included *William Shakespeare* in its acting company.
- Derived its central conceit from ancient Galenic medicine, which posited that human health, temperament, and behaviour were dictated by the balance of four bodily fluids: blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile.
- Classifies human conduct according to four predominant humoral types: the courageous and amorous *sanguine*, the indolent and calm *phlegmatic*, the irascible and ambitious *choleric*, and the introspective or despondent *melancholic*.
- Distinguishes itself from contemporary romantic comedies by featuring deliberately rigid, unyielding characters whose monomaniacal traits drive the friction and expose urban foolishness.
- Followed by Jonson's darker and more aggressive 1599 sequel *Every Man out of His Humour*, which formalized the theoretical mechanics of theatrical [[satire]] in extensive metadramatic induction scenes.
- Contrated with the multi-dimensional, evolving personalities found in plays by [[william-shakespeare]], whose characters may exhibit humoral excess but resist static classification, such as the impressionable soldier [[claudio]] in *Much Ado About Nothing*.
- Established structural conventions of eccentric caricature and moral correction that directly influenced seventeenth-century comedy of manners and the satiric comedies of the English Restoration.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — contemporary dramatist and theatrical colleague who acted in Jonson's early humours plays while pursuing a more lyrical, multidimensional comedic aesthetic.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the fertile period of dramatic innovation in London during which commercial playhouses cultivated distinct comedic genres.
- [[satire]] — the guiding literary and moral mode of the humours tradition, aimed at ridiculing civic pretensions and moral failings.
- [[claudio]] — a Shakespearean romantic protagonist whose impulsive, easily swayed temperament provides a fluid contrast to fixed humoral caricatures.
- [[england]] — the nation whose expanding capital city of London provided the social types, legal disputes, and courtly affectations mocked in the plays.
- [[renaissance]] — the wider European intellectual movement that revived classical medical treatises, Roman dramaturgy, and humanist moral philosophy.
