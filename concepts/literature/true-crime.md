## summary

**True crime** is a literary genre and nonfiction subgenre that recounts actual criminal events through a narrative lens, combining journalistic reporting with literary techniques to create compelling accounts of real murders, trials, and investigations. As a distinct genre, true crime emerged in the twentieth century, crystallized by *Truman Capote's* *In Cold Blood*, which applied novelistic structure and psychological depth to factual crime. True crime narratives raise questions about representation, responsibility, and the ethics of aestheticizing real human suffering.

## you gotta know

- Distinguished from detective fiction (invented mysteries) and crime journalism (daily news coverage) by its narrative depth, character development, and literary ambition.
- Emerged as a recognizable genre with *Truman Capote's In Cold Blood* (1966), which sold millions of copies and was adapted into a landmark film, legitimizing the form.
- *In Cold Blood* pioneered immersive reportage: Capote interviewed perpetrators, victims' families, and investigators, then reconstructed real events with fictional techniques.
- The genre necessarily raises ethical questions: Is it voyeuristic? Does it exploit real trauma? Do literary techniques distort or illuminate truth?
- Exploded in popularity in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries with podcast, streaming documentary, and television series, making it a dominant popular genre.
- True crime audiences are often motivated by morbid curiosity, interest in criminal psychology, or fascination with the justice system's failures and inequities.
- Contemporary true crime works include works like *I'll Be Gone in the Dark* (Michelle McNamara), *The Killer Across the Table* (John Douglas), and series like *Serial* and *Making a Murderer*.
- The genre raises questions about representation: whose crimes receive literary treatment? Often true crime amplifies cases involving white perpetrators or privileged victims.
- True crime can serve investigative functions; works like *McNamara's* expose have led to actual arrests and convictions, making the line between literature and activism ambiguous.
- Critics argue the genre can romanticize killers or reduce victims to plot devices; defenders argue it documents systemic injustices and keeps wrongful convictions visible.
