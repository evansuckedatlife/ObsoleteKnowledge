## summary

**Agnes Wickfield** is a principal character in Charles Dickens's novel *David Copperfield*, serving as David's loyal friend, conscience, and eventual wife. Defined by selflessness, moral constancy, and quiet intelligence, Agnes represents the ideal Victorian woman—dutiful, nurturing, and morally steadfast. Her relationship with David anchors the novel's exploration of maturation and the meaning of home.

## you gotta know

- The daughter of a lawyer, *Wickfield*, whom she supports by managing the household while he falls under the influence of the scheming *Uriah Heep*.
- Characterized by her composure, kindness, and almost ethereal virtue; Dickens portrays her as nearly too good to be real.
- Harbors deep affection for David from their first meeting, which David initially fails to recognize or reciprocate.
- Serves as David's confidante and advisor, offering steady moral guidance as he navigates the misadventures and disappointments of his youth.
- A victim of *Uriah Heep's* schemes; she endures his unwanted courtship and manipulation as part of his larger plot to control her father and his business.
- Her constancy stands in sharp contrast to David's youthful confusion and the duplicity of other characters like *Uriah Heep*.
- Represents a strain of idealized womanhood in Victorian literature: the patient, self-sacrificing partner who waits and supports rather than pursuing her own ambitions.
- Her ultimate union with David symbolizes his spiritual and emotional maturation, and her steadiness becomes the foundation of his adult happiness.
- Literary critics have debated whether Agnes represents genuine characterization or an idealized archetype too perfect to be psychologically credible; her passivity contrasts with modern expectations of female agency.
