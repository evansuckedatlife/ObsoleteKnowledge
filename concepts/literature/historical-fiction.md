## summary

**Historical Fiction** is the novel genre in which invented or reimagined narratives are set against real historical events, periods, and figures. The form treats history not as mere backdrop but as a subject of philosophical inquiry: how does individual agency interact with historical forces? How do we know what "really happened"? The genre reaches its apex with *Leo Tolstoy's* *War and Peace*, which transforms the Napoleonic Wars into a meditation on the nature of history itself.

## you gotta know

- Emerged as a distinct form in the *Romantic* era, particularly with *Walter Scott's* historical novels (*Ivanhoe*, *Rob Roy*), which established the template of fictional characters inserted into documented historical crises.
- *Leo Tolstoy's* *War and Peace* (1869) transformed the genre: it interweaves real historical figures (*Napoleon*, *Kutuzov*) and real battles (*Borodino*, *Moscow*) with invented characters and intimate domestic life, making history an epistemological problem rather than mere setting.
- Raises fundamental questions: What is the *relationship between individual and historical event*? If *Napoleon* didn't exist, would history have taken the same course? Can a novel capture the "feel" of a historical period better than history itself?
- Requires *fidelity to period detail* (clothing, language, custom, technology) while inventing compelling characters and plots; the tension between historical accuracy and narrative freedom defines the form.
- In *modernist* and *contemporary* iterations, historical fiction often questions its own relationship to truth—*metahistorical fiction* that foregrounds how history is written and narrated.
- Differs from *historical romance* (which uses history as adventure) and *alternate history* (which imagines counterfactual events); historical fiction aims at fidelity while acknowledging invention.
- The form's central paradox: it uses imagination to understand real events; it uses invented people to illuminate actual history.

## connections

- [[leo-tolstoy]] — the writer who made historical fiction into philosophical inquiry.
- [[war-and-peace]] — the summit of the form; the novel that defined what historical fiction could do.
- [[realism]] — the aesthetic movement that gave historical fiction its commitment to detail and psychological depth.
- [[novel]] — the form within which historical fiction operates; narrative prose is essential to its philosophical reach.
- [[romanticism]] — the movement that birthed historical fiction as a form of imaginative engagement with the past.
- [[adventure-fiction]] — a related form that uses historical settings but emphasizes plot over philosophical questioning.
