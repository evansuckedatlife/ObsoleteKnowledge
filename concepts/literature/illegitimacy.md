## summary

**Illegitimacy** in literature refers to the condition of being born outside wedlock, a social category that carries profound implications for inheritance, legitimacy, and personal identity. In early modern and Victorian literature especially, illegitimacy becomes a narrative driver—a fact that generates plot complications, moral dilemmas, and opportunities for exploring questions of justice, identity, and social hierarchy. The illegitimate child often becomes a figure of symbolic transgression or sympathetic outsider, revealing the arbitrary nature of social categories.

## you gotta know

- In pre-modern and early modern societies, illegitimate birth automatically excluded a person from inheritance and certain social positions, creating material and psychological stakes; the stigma was both legal and social.
- The illegitimate child often harbors resentment toward the father who denied recognition or toward the legitimate heirs who benefit from legal privilege; this resentment becomes a psychological motor for narrative action.
- Literature uses illegitimacy to explore the tension between biological kinship and legal legitimacy—what is "really" yours when law denies your paternity? The category exposes the arbitrariness of social order.
- In [[king-lear]], [[edmund]]'s illegitimacy drives his villainy; he seeks to undo the legitimate claims of his half-brother [[edgar]] and displace the rightful heir through deception and manipulation.
- Illegitimacy can evoke sympathy or complicity depending on how the narrative treats the character's response—whether they accept or resist their exclusion; literature reveals how social structures create criminals.
- The category is gendered: illegitimate daughters face particular sexual and economic vulnerability, while illegitimate sons often pursue status through cunning or force; patriarchal inheritance makes illegitimacy differently consequential.

## connections

- [[edmund]] — the classic literary bastard whose resentment and ambition drive Shakespearean tragedy.
- [[edgar]] — Edmund's legitimate half-brother, whose legitimacy becomes ironically uncertain when Edmund's plots make him appear the traitor.
- [[king-lear]] — the play that makes illegitimacy a central plot engine, exploring how legal status generates moral and political chaos.
- [[inheritance]] — the legal and economic system that makes illegitimacy a consequential category.
- [[social-class]] — illegitimacy marks a permanent exclusion from certain social positions and privileges.
- [[betrayal]] — illegitimate characters often pursue vengeance or deception as means of overcoming their exclusion.
