## summary

**Ecstasy**, derived from the Greek root meaning to stand outside oneself, denotes an intense psychological and metaphysical state marked by trance, emotional transport, and the temporary obliteration of ordinary ego consciousness. From the frenzy of classical Dionysian mystery cults to the devotional fervor of world mysticisms, the phenomenon has served as a central vehicle for divine encounter and artistic inspiration. It is vital in literary and dramatic history because it underpins the cathartic mechanics of classical drama, motivates sacred poetry, and prefigures literary explorations of altered mental states.

## you gotta know

- Embodied in classical Greek religion by *Dionysus*, the god of wine, ritual madness, theatre, and the liberating frenzy celebrated in nocturnal orgia.
- Dramatized supreme tragic dissolution in *Euripides*' masterpiece *The Bacchae*, where the ecstatic fervor of the maenads clashes with political rationalism.
- Analyzed philosophically by *Plato* in the *Phaedrus* as divine madness, identifying inspired poetic and erotic fury as superior to cold human technique.
- Manifested in late antique and medieval mystical traditions, such as *Plotinus*'s visionary henosis and *Teresa of Ávila*'s recorded spiritual raptures.
- Provided a foundational mode of communal and personal prayer in [[early-christianity]], particularly through visionary charisma, glossolalia, and apocalyptic transports.
- Anchored the spiritual renewal of [[eastern-european-jewry]], where Hasidic masters taught joyous rapture, song, and contemplative cleaving to God.
- Reinterpreted by European [[romanticism]] as sublime transport, where poetic imagination dissolves subject-object boundaries before awe-inspiring nature.
- Serves as an essential bridge toward [[early-modern-psychology]], foreshadowing clinical concepts of dissociation, mania, trance states, and subliminal consciousness.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the birthplace of the philosophical and mythological vocabulary connecting ecstatic possession with prophetic and dramatic insight.
- [[tragedy]] — the dramatic genre that developed directly out of ecstatic choral celebrations and structured emotional catharsis.
- [[poetry]] — the verbal art form long understood as flowing from divine inspiration, trance, and altered interior states.
- [[sophocles]] — the Athenian playwright whose tragic contemporaries staged the catastrophic conflicts between human moderation and divinely induced frenzy.
- [[early-christianity]] — the scriptural and historical arena in which charismatic prophecy and visionary transports were cultivated and scrutinized.
- [[eastern-european-jewry]] — the cultural setting where Hasidic piety privileged ecstatic prayer, mystical dance, and spiritual joy over dry scholasticism.
- [[romanticism]] — the literary movement that elevated sublime transport, intuitive rapture, and visionary enthusiasm over rationalist empiricism.
- [[early-modern-psychology]] — the emerging scientific inquiry into extreme emotional passions, visionary delusions, and altered subjective experience.
