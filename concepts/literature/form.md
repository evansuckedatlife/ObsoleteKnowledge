## summary

In literary criticism, **form** denotes the organizing structure, aesthetic architecture, and technical design of a creative work as distinct from its thematic content. Encompassing overarching genres like the novel, sonnet, and dramatic [[tragedy]] as well as internal arrangements of meter, voice, and temporality, it dictates how meaning is constructed and experienced. Writers throughout literary history have treated the manipulation of structural design as essential to artistic expression, with movements from [[realism]] to [[modernism]] defining themselves through radical formal innovations.

## you gotta know

- Distinguishes the "how" of a text—its structural pattern, cadence, narrative vantage, and genre conventions—from the "what" of raw thematic subject matter or plot events.
- Theorized fundamentally by Aristotle in the *Poetics*, which prioritized structural unity of action and dramatic composition over ornamentation or episodic spectacle.
- Revolutionized during nineteenth-century prose fiction by authors like [[flaubert]], who insisted that style, internal cohesion, and the rigorous mechanics of phrasing were inseparable from substantive reality.
- Fractured and reinvented by early twentieth-century [[modernism]], which rejected linear chronology in favour of [[stream-of-consciousness]], collage, and multi-perspective montage.
- Serves as the central focus of Russian Formalism and Anglo-American New Criticism, schools that pioneered close reading to demonstrate how organic unity emerges from textual tensions and ironies.
- Encompasses rigid poetic meters and stanzaic blueprints, such as the Petrarchan and Shakespearean sonnet, the villanelle, and the Japanese haiku, where strict constraint generates artistic resonance.
- Distinguishes between received external genre templates (fixed verse, three-act drama) and organic internal shape, which Romantic critics argued must grow naturally from within the creative impulse.

## connections

- [[flaubert]] — championed absolute devotion to stylistic perfection and the organic architectural unity of prose fiction.
- [[realism]] — utilized carefully hidden narrative structures and objective viewpoints to generate the convincing illusion of unmediated reality.
- [[poetry]] — displays the most concentrated employment of verse architecture, stanzaic rules, rhythm, and metric pattern.
- [[modernism]] — aggressively disrupted traditional Victorian narrative structures to mirror the fractured consciousness of twentieth-century life.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — introduced a specialized narrative technique that reproduces internal cognitive flow by breaking conventional syntactic and chronological boundaries.
- [[tragedy]] — developed stringent dramatic structures around hamartia, peripeteia, and catharsis in ancient and early modern theatre.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — mastered and adapted rigid verse constraints, notably blank verse and dramatic soliloquies, within Renaissance performance.
