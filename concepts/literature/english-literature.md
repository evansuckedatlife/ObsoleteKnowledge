## summary

**English literature** comprises the literary tradition of England and Britain, spanning from medieval verse to contemporary fiction and poetry. Anchored by giants like Shakespeare, Milton, Austen, Dickens, and the Modernists, English literature established many of the formal conventions and genres—the novel, the sonnet, the dramatic soliloquy—that became global literary standards. The tradition is marked by linguistic richness, formal experimentation, and a complex relationship between individual imagination and social critique.

## you gotta know

- The [[english-renaissance]], particularly the Elizabethan era (1558-1603), established England as a major literary power through theater and poetry; Shakespeare's dramatic innovations and sonnets remain unsurpassed.
- The [[romanticism]] movement (late 18th, early 19th century) elevated individual imagination, emotion, and nature as central literary values; Wordsworth, Keats, and Shelley became canonical figures defining lyric intensity.
- The Victorian novel (19th century) became the dominant literary form, with writers like [[charles-dickens]] and George Eliot exploring social conditions and character psychology; the novel became the vehicle for social critique.
- The Modernist movement (early 20th century) saw formal innovation and fragmentation with writers like [[virginia-woolf]] and T.S. Eliot challenging narrative conventions; interior consciousness and fragmented form replaced linear storytelling.
- English literature has historically influenced and been influenced by wider European and global literary traditions, whether through trade, colonialism, or cultural exchange; the tradition remains contested terrain post-empire.
- Regional and national variations exist—Scottish, Welsh, and Irish traditions have their own distinct characteristics while overlapping with English literature; the category "British literature" encompasses diverse linguistic and cultural traditions.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — the foundational figure who established theatrical conventions and linguistic richness central to the tradition.
- [[english-renaissance]] — the period that established England's literary prestige and produced the Elizabethan era masterworks.
- [[romanticism]] — movement that reshaped English literature's values and priorities around individual imagination and emotion.
- [[charles-dickens]] — Victorian novelist who epitomized social critique and psychological depth in the English novel.
- [[virginia-woolf]] — Modernist innovator whose stream-of-consciousness techniques revolutionized narrative form.
- [[jonathan-swift]] — 18th-century satirist who demonstrated literature's power as political and social commentary.
- [[gothic-fiction]] — the dark, atmospheric mode pioneered by English writers that influenced global popular culture.
