## summary

**Social satire** is satire directed at the customs, pretensions, hypocrisies, and injustices of society as a whole or a particular social class. It ridicules not an individual vice but systemic folly: the arbitrary cruelty of class hierarchies, the empty rituals of respectability, the gap between stated ideals and actual practice. *Mark Twain*, *Gogol*, and *Saki* excelled at social satire, using wit and grotesque exaggeration to expose how convention operates as invisible tyranny.

## you gotta know

- Social satire targets not a person but a system or class; it holds up social behavior to the light and asks the reader: do you see now how absurd this is?
- The technique relies on *deadpan delivery*—the satirist presents the injustice or absurdity as if it were normal and sensible, forcing the reader to recognize the gap between appearance and reality.
- *Gogol*'s stories of St. Petersburg bureaucracy and *Twain*'s skewering of American self-importance are canonical because they moved beyond mockery to a kind of horrified pity: society is not just wicked but ridiculous.
- Social satire can be mordant and bitter (condemnation of evil) or light and playful (gentle mockery of human folly); the form accommodates both registers.
- The form depends on the reader's complicity: if you don't share the satirist's values, you may not see the satire as funny or pointed but rather as mere cruelty or perversity.
- Social satire often ages poorly because it relies on shared cultural reference; what was scandalous or recognizable becomes obscure, though the best satire transcends its moment.

## connections

- [[satire]] — the parent form, of which social satire is a crucial species.
- [[mark-twain]] — American master of social satire who exposed racism, imperialism, and greed.
- [[nikolai-gogol]] — Russian virtuoso of grotesque social satire revealing bureaucratic absurdity.
- [[saki]] — Edwardian satirist of social pretense and manners.
- [[jonathan-swift]] — prototype of savage social satire whose *A Modest Proposal* remains unsurpassed.
- [[social-critique]] — the broader project of which social satire is the witty, indirect accomplishment.
