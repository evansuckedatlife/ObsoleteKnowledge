## summary

**Propaganda** is the systematic use of language, imagery, and media to manipulate belief, emotion, and behaviour toward political ends, typically by simplifying complex truths, amplifying grievances, and creating enemies. A central concern of twentieth-century literature (especially dystopian works), propaganda operates by controlling what can be said and thought; totalitarian regimes use it to reshape language itself, as *George Orwell* showed, making dissent literally unthinkable. Modern propaganda extends beyond overt state messaging to shape cultural consensus through repetition, emotional appeal, and the suppression of competing narratives.

## you gotta know

- Propaganda distinguishes itself from persuasion by operating through emotion, repetition, suppression of counterargument, and the deliberate narrowing of available information rather than through reasoned debate; it seeks to bypass critical thought.
- The term gained particular force in the twentieth century as mass media allowed unprecedented reach and as totalitarian regimes weaponised language and image for political control.
- Fascist and communist regimes built propaganda apparatus as essential state machinery, controlling all media to reshape history, manufacture enemies, and enforce ideological conformity across entire populations.
- George Orwell's *1984* depicts propaganda as the tool through which totalitarianism rewrites reality itself; "War is Peace, Freedom is Slavery" exemplifies how propaganda inverts meaning.
- Propaganda operates by creating simplified binaries: good vs. evil, us vs. them, progress vs. decay; nuance and contradiction are eliminated in favour of clarity and emotional intensity.
- Literary responses to propaganda—novels that represent its power and its danger—became ways of resisting it; Orwell and others documented how propaganda corrupts language and truth through deliberate linguistic manipulation.
- Modern advertising and political messaging deploy propaganda techniques (loaded language, false dichotomies, emotional targeting, repetition) widely and often subconsciously, making propaganda a persistent and largely invisible feature of contemporary communication.

## connections

- [[totalitarianism]] — propaganda is the communicative infrastructure of total control; regimes cannot survive without it.
- [[george-orwell]] — the writer who most fully exposed how propaganda rewrites language and thought itself.
- [[language]] — propaganda is fundamentally about seizing control of language and its meanings.
- [[rhetoric]] — the art of persuasion; propaganda is persuasion divorced from truth or consent.
- [[dystopia]] — literary genre that frequently explores propaganda's power and danger.
