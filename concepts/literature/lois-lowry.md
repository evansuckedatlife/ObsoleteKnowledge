## summary

**Lois Lowry** is an American author and illustrator born in 1937, celebrated for writing young adult and children's literature that grapples with profound moral and philosophical questions. A two-time winner of the [[newbery-medal]], she is best known for *The Giver*, a dystopian novel exploring themes of conformity, individuality, and the hidden costs of a seemingly perfect society. Her work combines accessible prose with intellectual rigor, asking young readers to confront difficult concepts—loss, autonomy, death, truth—while maintaining hope and narrative grace.

## you gotta know

- *The Giver* (1993) depicts a society that has eliminated pain, conflict, and choice by suppressing emotion and memory; its protagonist, [[jonas]], is chosen to receive all the suppressed history and color of human experience.
- The novel's ending—ambiguous as to whether [[jonas]] escapes to freedom or walks toward death—has sparked decades of classroom debate and reader interpretation, making it a text that refuses easy answers.
- Lowry won the Newbery Medal twice: for *The Giver* and again for *The View from Saturday*, a multi-perspective narrative about a quiz-bowl team and the teacher who mentors them, demonstrating range in voice and structure.
- Her writing often centers on characters who must navigate loss, secrets, and the gap between surface civility and hidden truth; she treats these themes with seriousness and emotional honesty even in books for young readers.
- Lowry's influence on [[science-fiction]] and [[dystopian-fiction]] for young audiences is immense; *The Giver* became a school curriculum staple and a model for how speculative fiction can pose ethical questions.
- Her other works, including *Number the Stars* and *Gathering Blue*, continue exploring themes of memory, power, and individual agency in constrained societies, establishing her as a major voice in young adult literature.

## connections
