## summary

**Dead Souls** (*Myortvye dushi*) is an epic satirical novel by *Nikolai Gogol*, first published in 1842 under the government-mandated title *The Wanderings of Chichikov*. Structured as an expansive picaresque journey that the author subtitled a "poem in prose," the narrative follows a mysterious traveler touring provincial Russia to purchase ownership rights to deceased serfs. The book is revered as a foundational masterpiece of Russian [[satire]] and critical [[realism]], exposing the absurdities of imperial bureaucracy, serfdom, and moral stagnation.

## you gotta know

- The protagonist *Pavel Ivanovich Chichikov*, an ostensibly respectable collegiate councillor, travels across provincial Russia buying dead male serfs who remain on official census lists until the next revision.
- Chichikov plans to register these phantom serfs on an estate in Kherson province and mortgage them to the state lending board for an enormous sum of money.
- The novel presents an unforgettable rogues' gallery of provincial landowners, beginning with the daydreaming, sentimental *Manilov* and the suspicious, penny-pinching widow *Korobochka*.
- Chichikov is nearly undone by *Nozdryov*, an aggressive drunkard and compulsive gambler who insults him and attempts to expose his strange enterprise at an aristocratic ball.
- Other memorable hosts include *Sobakevich*, a bear-like, cynical landowner who drives a hard bargain while praising his deceased craftsmen, and *Plyushkin*, a pathological hoarder and miser living amidst decaying heaps of junk.
- Gogol originally conceived the work as a three-part Russian counterpart to *Dante Alighieri's* *Divine Comedy*, intending the first volume as an *Inferno*, the second as *Purgatorio*, and the third as *Paradiso*.
- Plagued by deep religious crisis and physical decline under the influence of an ascetic priest, Gogol burned the nearly finished manuscript of the second volume shortly before his death in 1852.
- The first part concludes with a celebrated lyrical digression comparing Russia to a speeding troika carriage, hurtling forward across the plains while other nations look on in awe.

## connections

- [[satire]] — the fundamental comic and critical mode Gogol employs to ridicule the pettiness, vanity, and corruption of Russian provincial society.
- [[realism]] — the literary tradition that adopted Gogol's exacting descriptions of daily life, food, clothing, and institutional decay as its model.
- [[deception]] — the central engine of the plot, driven by Chichikov's fraudulent paperwork, financial imposture, and calculating theatrical manners.
- [[romanticism]] — the literary movement providing the mystical, elevated tone of the narrator's grand lyrical digressions on the Russian soul.
- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — the great Russian psychological novelist whose earliest portraits of social outcasts drew profound inspiration from Gogol's comic grotesques.
- [[leo-tolstoy]] — the panoramic realist master whose sweeping portraits of Russian rural life and moral crisis inherited the ambition of Gogol's unfinished epic.
