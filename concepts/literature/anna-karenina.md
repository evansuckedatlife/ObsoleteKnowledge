## summary

**Anna Karenina**, published in 1877, is *Leo Tolstoy's* masterpiece of 19th-century *Russian literature* and one of the greatest novels in world literature. The novel interweaves multiple narratives exploring love, marriage, death, and the search for meaning in Russian aristocratic society. It opens with the famous line: "All happy families are alike; each unhappy family is unhappy in its own way."—a statement that frames the novel's investigation of human happiness and suffering.

## you gotta know

- The titular protagonist, Anna, is an aristocratic woman who abandons her marriage and social position to pursue a passionate affair with Count Alexei Vronsky.
- Anna's trajectory is tragic: her passion leads to ecstasy, then jealousy, scandal, and ultimately despair, culminating in her suicide by train.
- The novel balances Anna's story with that of Levin, a landowner struggling with faith, meaning, and his identity, who finds redemption through love and family.
- Tolstoy uses the novel to explore the competing claims of passion, duty, faith, and reason—with no single answer declared victorious.
- Themes include the futility of seeking happiness through transient desires, the redemptive power of family and work, and the tension between individual will and social constraint.
- Anna is sympathetically drawn despite her destructive choices; Tolstoy refuses moral simplicity, inviting readers to understand and pity her even as she self-destructs.
- The novel is famous for its psychological depth, complex characterization, and digressive philosophical passages that reflect Tolstoy's own spiritual preoccupations.
- Influenced modernist literature profoundly; the novel's form—multiple plotlines, stream of consciousness, philosophical meditation—became a template for 20th-century fiction.
