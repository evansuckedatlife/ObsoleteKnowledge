## summary

The **Handkerchief** (frequently designated as **Othello's Handkerchief** or the **Strawberry Handkerchief**) is the central dramatic token and symbolic catalyst in *William Shakespeare*'s domestic masterpiece [[othello]]. Embroidered with strawberries and imbued with maternal folklore, this intimate bridal gift is transformed by the manipulative ensign *Iago* into fabricated ocular proof of *Desdemona*'s alleged infidelity with *Cassio*. The textile's loss and circulation illustrate how physical props can be weaponized in [[english-renaissance]] drama to warp perception and trigger psychological destruction.

## you gotta know

- Presented to *Desdemona* by *Othello* as his very first gift and a pledge of everlasting fidelity, functioning on stage as an outward sign of marital chastity.
- Described by *Othello* as having magical provenance, woven by a two-hundred-year-old sibyl from sacred silkworms and dyed in mummy liquid conserved from maiden hearts for his mother to keep his father loyal.
- Dropped inadvertently by *Desdemona* as she attempts to bind *Othello*'s aching brow, after which it is pocketed by her waiting-woman *Emilia* to satisfy *Iago*'s persistent demands for the keepsake.
- Planted surreptitiously by *Iago* in the lodgings of *Cassio*, who finds it without knowing its owner and gives it to the courtesan *Bianca* to replicate its intricate embroidery.
- Substituted as the tangible "ocular proof" demanded by *Othello*, who watches from concealment while an indignant *Bianca* flings the token back at *Cassio*, confirming *Othello*'s mistaken conviction of his wife's guilt.
- Demanded angrily by *Othello* in *Desdemona*'s presence, transforming her inability to produce it on the spot into the definitive confirmation that seals her death warrant.
- Identified publicly by *Emilia* in the play's concluding chamber scene, exposing *Iago*'s treachery and prompting *Othello*'s anguished realization before his suicide.
