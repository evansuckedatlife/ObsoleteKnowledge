## summary

**Psychological realism** is a literary mode that treats the interior life—thoughts, emotions, unconscious impulses—with the same documentary precision that earlier realism applied to external social conditions. Pioneered by Russian novelists like *Dostoevsky* and *Tolstoy*, it positions consciousness itself as the primary subject of narrative. This approach transformed the novel into an instrument for mapping the human mind with unprecedented depth and complexity.

## you gotta know

- Psychological realism emerged in the mid-19th century as a response to the limitations of external realism, which focused on social milieu and plot.
- *Dostoevsky's* novels, especially *Crime and Punishment* and *The Brothers Karamazov*, model the technique of displaying consciousness in its contradictions and spiritual anguish.
- *Tolstoy's* *War and Peace* interweaves historical events with the interior lives of characters, showing how individual consciousness interprets and experiences collective history.
- The technique often employs free indirect discourse—a narrative voice that blurs the boundary between narrator and character consciousness.
- Psychological realism treats irrational and unconscious drives as seriously as rational thought, prefiguring psychoanalytic insights.
- The mode demands intense introspection without sentimentality; characters' interior lives are portrayed with unflinching honesty about contradiction and moral ambiguity.
- It laid the groundwork for modernist innovations in narrative technique, particularly *stream of consciousness* and interior monologue.

## connections

- [[fyodor-dostoevsky]] — defining innovator of psychological realism through the exploration of guilt, faith, and moral crisis.
- [[leo-tolstoy]] — master of psychological realism applied to war, history, and the domestic sphere.
- [[russian-literature]] — the tradition that pioneered and perfected psychological realism.
- [[stream-of-consciousness]] — a modernist extension of psychological realism that renders consciousness more literally.
- [[realism]] — the broader mode from which psychological realism diverges by foregrounding interiority.
- [[modernism]] — psychological realism's techniques become foundational to modernist experimentation.
- [[existentialism]] — philosophical movement aligned with psychological realism's focus on consciousness and choice.
