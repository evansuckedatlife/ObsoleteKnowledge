## summary

**The Castle of Perseverance** is an early fifteenth-century Middle English allegorical morality play, surviving alongside *Wisdom* and *Mankind* in the famed *Macro Manuscript*. Composed roughly between 1400 and 1425 in the East Midlands of [[england]], it constitutes the earliest complete extant morality drama in the English language. The work is widely celebrated in dramatic history both for tracing the complete spiritual pilgrimage of the universal human soul from infancy to the Last Judgment and for its unique surviving stage drawing depicting an elaborate theatre-in-the-round performance.

## you gotta know

- Preserved in the *Macro Manuscript* (Folger Shakespeare Library MS V.a.354), where it forms a foundational triad of English morality plays alongside *Mankind* and *Wisdom*.
- Features a legendary circular stage diagram showing a central moat or ditch surrounding a battle tower, encircled by five distinct scaffold stations for *Mundus* (World), *Caro* (Flesh), *Belial* (Devil), *Coveytyse* (Covetousness), and *Deus* (God).
- Depicts the complete biographical trajectory of the protagonist *Humanum Genus* (Mankind), following him from naked vulnerability at birth through youthful vanity, middle-aged repentance, senile avarice, and bodily death.
- Staged as an epic psychomachia wherein the Seven Cardinal Virtues defend the titular sanctuary against the Seven Deadly Sins, notably pelting the demonic attackers with roses, which serve as physical emblems of the Passion of Christ.
- Mankind is fatally lured out of the spiritual stronghold in his old age by *Coveytyse*, demonstrating the late-medieval conviction that greed is the specific vice that ensnares the elderly before *Mors* (Death) strikes.
- Resolves the fate of the deceased protagonist through the *Parliament of Heaven*, wherein the Four Daughters of God—*Mercy*, *Peace*, *Justice*, and *Truth*—debate divine judgment before the throne of God until divine mercy triumphs over strict retribution.
- Written in ornate, alliterative thirteen-line stanzas with an *ababababcdcdd* rhyming scheme, totalling more than 3,600 lines intended for itinerant vernacular performance.
