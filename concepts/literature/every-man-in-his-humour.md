## summary

**Every Man in His Humour** is *Ben Jonson's* debut comedy (1598), a groundbreaking satire of London life that established the "comedy of humours"—a theatrical genre rooted in Renaissance psychology where characters are driven by a single dominant passion or temperament. The play introduced Jonson's signature style of social critique through exaggerated types, influencing English comedy for centuries.

## you gotta know

- The play's central innovation is the "humours" theory: the idea that human behavior stems from imbalances in bodily fluids (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile), which Jonson adapted to satirize social pretension and folly.
- Features a cast of ridiculous London types: Knowwell, a suspicious father; Brainworm, a witty servant; and Matthew, an aspiring poet—each obsessed with their particular mania or affectation.
- Originally set in Florence in the earliest version but revised by Jonson to be set in London, anchoring the comedy in recognizable contemporary social spaces and follies.
- The linguistic wit of the play is sharp and observant, with dialogue that captures the speech patterns and idioms of London merchants, affected courtiers, and urban tricksters.
- The plot deliberately abandons classical structure in favor of a loose series of intrigue scenes, making it feel more like social observation than tight dramatic architecture.
- The play was enormously successful and established Jonson as a rival to Shakespeare, cementing "comedy of humours" as a distinct theatrical tradition.
- Jonson's method influenced English comedy for centuries: by making characters driven by single dominant traits (vanity, jealousy, greed), he created a template for character-based satire.
- The play's title itself became a proverb in English: the phrase "every man in his humour" entered common usage as shorthand for the idea that everyone has a particular obsession or foolishness.
- The shift from Florence to London was significant politically and culturally: it asserted that contemporary English society, not historical or foreign settings, provided enough material for serious dramatic art.
- Later comedies like *Volpone* and *The Alchemist* refined and deepened Jonson's humours technique, making it the template for his entire dramatic oeuvre.
