## summary

**Jewish identity** in literature encompasses both the exploration of what it means to be Jewish—religious tradition, cultural heritage, historical trauma, belonging and alienation—and the literary articulation of the Jewish self in a world frequently hostile to Jewish presence. From 19th-century *diaspora* poetry to 20th-century American and Israeli fiction, Jewish writers have grappled with integration versus distinctness, assimilation versus maintenance of tradition, and the shadow of *antisemitism* and *Holocaust* on Jewish consciousness and identity.

## you gotta know

- Jewish identity in literature is inseparable from *diaspora*—the historical displacement and scattering of the Jewish people from ancient Palestine.
- *Emma Lazarus* reasserted Jewish identity in American literature during the 19th century, especially in her response to pogroms in Eastern Europe; her *Statue of Liberty* poem invokes Jewish migration.
- American Jewish literature wrestles with assimilation: how to remain Jewish while becoming American? The tension defines much 20th and 21st-century American Jewish writing.
- *Michael Chabon's* work centres Jewish identity while remaining open to universalist and secular concerns; his fiction uses Jewishness as cultural depth rather than limitation.
- The *Holocaust* cast a shadow over post-1945 Jewish identity and literature; how to represent or recover meaning after genocide?
- Jewish identity is often interrogated through *halakha* (Jewish law), *Torah*, and *Talmud* (the textual traditions); literacy and interpretation are central to Jewish culture.
- Contemporary Jewish identity is plural: religious observance ranges from Orthodox to secular; Jewish identity can be ethnic, cultural, political, or spiritual.

## connections

- [[emma-lazarus]] — 19th-century American Jewish poet who explicitly reclaimed and defended Jewish identity.
- [[michael-chabon]] — contemporary American Jewish writer whose work centres Jewish cultural life.
- [[judaism]] — the religious and cultural tradition that grounds Jewish identity.
- [[antisemitism]] — the historical hostility and discrimination that shapes Jewish experience and literature.
- [[diaspora]] — the historical condition of Jewish dispersal and the literary theme of exile and belonging.
- [[american-identity]] — the negotiation of Jewish identity within American culture and citizenship.
- [[philip-roth]] — American Jewish novelist who interrogated Jewish identity through comedy, satire, and philosophical inquiry.
