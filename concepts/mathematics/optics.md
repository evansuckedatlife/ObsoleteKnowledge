## summary

**Optics** is the mathematical and physical study of light, encompassing how light propagates, interacts with matter, and forms images. From Newton's prism experiments revealing the spectrum to Hamilton's wave theory, optics shaped modern physics and mathematics. It bridges geometry (ray optics) and calculus (wave optics), revealing profound connections between different mathematical frameworks.

## you gotta know

- *Geometric optics* treats light as rays traveling in straight lines until reflected or refracted; it explains mirrors, lenses, and basic image formation through simple geometry.
- *Wave optics* describes light as electromagnetic waves, explaining interference, diffraction, and the wave nature of light discovered by Christiaan Huygens; this shifted optics from geometry to partial differential equations.
- Snell's law (sin θ₁ / sin θ₂ = n₂ / n₁) governs refraction—the bending of light at a boundary between media of different refractive indices; it was discovered empirically before being explained via wave theory.
- Newton used prisms to decompose white light into a spectrum, proving that white light is a mixture of colours and challenging older theories that claimed prisms *create* colour.
- The Fermat principle states that light travels along the path that takes least time; this elegant variational principle unifies reflection and refraction and foreshadows the principle of least action in dynamics.
- Hamilton developed quaternions partly motivated by applications to rotations in optics and dynamics; his work on wave surfaces revealed deep connections to algebra and geometry.
- Double refraction (birefringence) occurs in crystals like Iceland spar, creating two refracted rays and revealing light's polarisation—a phenomenon that resisted explanation until wave theory matured.
- The nature of light (particle vs. wave) was hotly debated; quantum mechanics resolved this via wave-particle duality, showing light exhibits both properties depending on the measurement.
- Lens aberrations (spherical, chromatic, etc.) limit optical systems; minimizing these requires sophisticated mathematical optimization and understanding of ray paths.
- The index of refraction (n) varies with wavelength in dispersive media; this dispersion enables prisms to separate light and creates rainbows.

## connections

- [[isaac-newton]] — performed foundational prism experiments and developed corpuscular theories of light.
- [[william-rowan-hamilton]] — contributed mathematical treatments of wave optics and developed quaternion algebra.
- [[light]] — the physical phenomenon optics studies.
- [[reflection]] — a key optical phenomenon governed by the law of reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection).
- [[refraction]] — the bending of light as it passes between media.
- [[calculus]] — the mathematical framework for wave optics and the Fermat principle.
