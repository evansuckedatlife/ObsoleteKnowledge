## summary

**Euclidean geometry** is the mathematical system of plane and solid geometry founded on *Euclid's* five axioms and the parallel postulate, codified in *The Elements* around 300 BCE. It describes the geometry of flat space where familiar rules hold: parallel lines never meet, angles in a triangle sum to 180°, and the Pythagorean theorem governs right triangles. For two millennia, Euclidean geometry was synonymous with geometry itself until the 19th century discovery of non-Euclidean alternatives shattered this monopoly.

## you gotta know

- Built on five axioms: segments can be drawn between any two points, a line can be extended indefinitely, a circle can be drawn from any center and radius, all right angles are equal, and the parallel postulate (the most controversial).
- The *parallel postulate* states that through a point not on a line, exactly one parallel line exists—this single assumption distinguishes Euclidean from non-Euclidean geometries and sparked centuries of mathematical debate.
- *The Elements* (13 books, ~465 propositions) was the dominant geometry textbook for over 2,000 years, establishing the axiomatic method as the standard for mathematical proof and rigor in Western civilization.
- Euclid proved foundational theorems: the Pythagorean theorem, angle sum in triangles (always 180° in Euclidean space), properties of circles and inscribed polygons, and constructions using only compass and straightedge.
- The axiomatic method—starting from simple axioms and deriving theorems via logical deduction—became the gold standard for mathematical rigor and influenced philosophy and science for millennia.
- In the 19th century, Gauss, Lobachevsky, and Bolyai independently discovered *hyperbolic geometry*, where the parallel postulate fails and through a point, infinitely many parallels exist.
- Riemann developed *elliptic geometry* (spherical geometry), where no parallels exist and the angle sum in triangles exceeds 180°, showing that non-Euclidean geometries are logically consistent alternatives.
- The discovery of non-Euclidean geometries revealed Euclid's axioms describe only one possible space; Einstein's relativity theory later demonstrated the universe's actual geometry is non-Euclidean at cosmic scales.
- Euclidean geometry remains the most intuitive and practical for everyday phenomena, engineering, and classical physics, even though it is not the geometry of the universe on large scales.
