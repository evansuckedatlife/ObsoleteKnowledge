## summary

**Additive number theory** is the specialized subfield of [[number-theory]] devoted to analyzing the arithmetic behavior and combinatorial properties of subsets of integers under addition. Unlike multiplicative arithmetic, it focuses on sumsets, asymptotic additive bases, and representation functions that count the ways integers can be decomposed into sums of elements from designated sets, most notably primes and powers. Originating in classical inquiries by *Pierre de Fermat*, *Leonhard Euler*, and *Joseph-Louis Lagrange*, the discipline crystallized during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries around landmark additive puzzles. It remains a core pillar of modern mathematics because resolving its central questions requires synthesizing combinatorics with sophisticated analytic methods such as the Hardy-Littlewood circle method, sieve theory, and harmonic analysis.

## you gotta know

- *Goldbach's conjecture*, famously formulated in a 1742 letter from *Christian Goldbach* to *Leonhard Euler*, asserts that every even integer strictly greater than two can be expressed as the sum of two prime numbers, standing as the most famous unsolved additive problem in history.
- *Lagrange's four-square theorem*, proved by *Joseph-Louis Lagrange* in 1770, settled a classical conjecture originating in the work of *Diophantus* by establishing that every positive integer can be represented as the sum of four integer squares.
- *Waring's problem*, proposed by *Edward Waring* in 1770 and solved affirmatively in the general case by *David Hilbert* in 1909, asks whether for every natural number $k$, there exists an integer $g(k)$ such that every positive integer is the sum of at most $g(k)$ positive $k$-th powers.
- The *Hardy-Littlewood circle method*, developed by *G. H. Hardy* and *John Edensor Littlewood* alongside *Srinivasa Ramanujan* to investigate the partition function, integrates complex analysis and [[fourier-series]] over the unit circle to extract asymptotic representation formulas for additive problems.
- *Schnirelmann density*, introduced by *Lev Schnirelmann* in 1930, formulated a combinatorial density metric on integer sequences that allowed him to prove the groundbreaking theorem that the sequence of primes plus one forms an additive basis of finite order for the integers.
- *Vinogradov's theorem*, established by *Ivan Vinogradov* in 1937 using exponential sum estimates, proved the ternary Goldbach conjecture for all sufficiently large odd integers, a landmark result whose remaining finite cases were completely resolved by *Harald Helfgott* in 2013.
- *Freiman's theorem* provides a cornerstone structural classification in inverse additive problems, proving that any finite subset of integers with a small sumset must be densely embedded within a low-dimensional generalized arithmetic progression.

## connections

- [[number-theory]] — the foundational mathematical discipline investigating integer arithmetic, congruences, and prime distributions.
- [[prime-number-theorem]] — provides the asymptotic density distribution of primes essential for estimating representation functions in prime-sum problems.
- [[twin-prime-conjecture]] — a quintessential unsolved additive problem concerning prime constellations and bounded gaps between consecutive primes.
- [[modular-arithmetic]] — provides cyclic congruence tools and quotient rings necessary for establishing local obstruction criteria against integer representations.
- [[fundamental-theorem-of-arithmetic]] — establishes unique multiplicative prime factorization, against which the non-multiplicative nature of sumsets provides a stark contrast.
- [[fourier-series]] — provides the harmonic foundation and exponential sums utilized directly in the circle method to evaluate representation integrals.
- [[prime-factorization]] — decomposes numbers into multiplicative building blocks whose additive combinations form classical Goldbach-type inquiries.
