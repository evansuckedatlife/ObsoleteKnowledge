## summary

**Notation** refers to the system of symbols and conventions mathematicians use to express ideas precisely and compactly. From Leibniz's integral sign (∫) to Euler's function notation f(x), mathematical notation evolved to make complex concepts tractable and universal. The choice of notation shapes how mathematicians think, communicate, and solve problems.

## you gotta know

- Leibniz invented the integral sign ∫ and the differential notation d/dx, which dominated calculus symbolism across Europe and remain standard today; he also coined the term *function*.
- Euler systematized function notation f(x), introduced the Greek letter Σ for summation, introduced π for the circle ratio, and popularized *e* as the natural exponential base and *i* for the imaginary unit.
- The equals sign (=) was introduced by Robert Recorde in 1557 with the symbolic justification that two quantities are equal when "they are of one length"; earlier mathematicians wrote out equations in words or used alternatives like *aequalis*.
- Binary and positional notation (0–9, place value) emerged from Indian and Arabic mathematics; Roman numerals lack these properties, making multiplication tedious and impossible to systematize.
- Algebraic notation evolved through three historical phases: *rhetorical* (purely verbal), *syncopated* (abbreviated), and *symbolic* (modern); each transition enabled more complex reasoning and swifter calculation.
- The vinculum (overline) was used for grouping and roots in early mathematics before parentheses became standard; clearing such notational clutter was essential for algebra to mature.
- Matrix notation, developed systematically by Cayley and later standardized, enabled linear algebra to flourish; abstract notation allowed mathematicians to manipulate entire systems without ambiguity.
- Standardised vector notation (bold, arrows, or subscripts) transformed multivariable calculus and physics; inconsistent notation obscures elegant relationships and renders proofs illegible.
- Mathematical notation is not universal or unique—different fields and traditions use different symbols—but shared conventions within a field allow rapid communication and build upon prior work.
- The Peano axioms use symbolic notation to formalize natural numbers; this formalization showed how notation enables rigorous logical foundations.

## connections

- [[gottfried-leibniz]] — pioneered integral calculus notation and the differential operator d.
- [[leonhard-euler]] — systematized modern function notation and defined many universal constants.
- [[calculus]] — notation like ∫ and d/dx made calculus teachable and unified Newton and Leibniz's competing notations.
- [[algebra]] — the symbolic language that notation serves.
- [[isaac-newton]] — developed his own notation for calculus (fluxions and fluents), which Leibniz's eventually superseded.
