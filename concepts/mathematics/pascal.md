## summary

**Blaise Pascal** (1623–1662) was a French mathematician, physicist, and religious philosopher whose contributions spanned geometry, probability, fluid mechanics, and the philosophy of faith. Though he lived only 39 years, Pascal made profound discoveries: he invented the hydraulic press, conducted the Torricelli barometer experiment, and—through correspondence with *Fermat*—founded modern probability theory by solving the "problem of points." His *Pascal triangle* (a triangular array of binomial coefficients) remains a cornerstone of combinatorics, and his cycloid studies were central to the calculus wars of the 17th century.

## you gotta know

- *Pascal's triangle:* a triangular arrangement of binomial coefficients where each entry is the sum of the two entries above it; row n contains the coefficients of (x + y)ⁿ, and diagonals reveal figurate numbers (triangular, tetrahedral).
- *Probability theory:* correspondence with *Fermat* on the problem of points (how to divide winnings in an interrupted game) created the foundations of probability; their exchange introduced expected value and the principle of fair division.
- *Cycloid studies:* Pascal conducted rigorous mathematical investigations of the cycloid, including its arc length and area; his work on cycloids was part of the vigorous calculus frontier where *Newton* and *Leibniz* were simultaneously developing the field.
- *Pascal's principle (hydraulic press):* in physics, states that pressure applied to a fluid in a closed system is transmitted uniformly throughout; practical applications led to hydraulic machinery.
- *Barometric experiments:* designed experiments using mercury columns to prove that atmospheric pressure supports a column of liquid, validating that a vacuum can exist (contra Aristotle's horror vacui).
- *Pascal's wager:* a religious argument that it is rational to believe in God because belief has infinite potential reward if God exists, while disbelief has infinite potential cost—influential in philosophy and decision theory.
- *Mathematical style:* known for elegant geometric arguments and an ability to see deep structure; his lost treatise *Traité des sinus* reportedly contained discoveries later rediscovered independently.
- *Limacon investigation:* he studied the limaçon curve (r = a + b cos θ in polar form), and the curve bears his name in some contexts.
