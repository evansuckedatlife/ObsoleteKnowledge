## summary

**Non-Euclidean geometry** refers to geometric systems that do not satisfy *Euclid's parallel postulate*—the claim that through a point not on a line, exactly one parallel line exists. In *hyperbolic geometry*, infinitely many parallels exist; in *elliptic geometry*, none do. These alternative geometries were developed in the 19th century—*Gauss* privately, *Lobachevsky* and *Bolyai* publicly—and later proven consistent by *Poincaré's models*. The discovery shattered the notion that Euclidean geometry was the only "true" geometry and paved the way for Einstein's curved spacetime in relativity.

## you gotta know

- *Parallel postulate:* Euclid's fifth postulate states that given a line and a point not on it, exactly one line through the point is parallel (never intersects) the original line. For 2,000 years, mathematicians tried unsuccessfully to derive this from the other four postulates.
- **Hyperbolic geometry**: assumes that multiple distinct parallel lines exist through a point not on a given line; in this geometry, the sum of angles in a triangle is *less* than 180°, and parallel lines diverge indefinitely.
- **Elliptic geometry**: assumes no parallel lines exist (all lines eventually intersect); the sum of angles in a triangle is *greater* than 180°, and the geometry is bounded (finite total area, like a sphere's surface).
- *Spherical geometry as model:* the surface of a sphere exhibits elliptic properties; geodesics (shortest paths) are great circles, and "parallel" lines always intersect (at the poles).
- *Poincaré's half-plane model:* represents hyperbolic geometry as the upper half-plane {y > 0} with metric ds² = (dx² + dy²)/y², showing hyperbolic geometry is internally consistent.
- *Intrinsic curvature:* non-Euclidean spaces have intrinsic curvature (Gaussian curvature): Euclidean space has curvature 0, hyperbolic space has negative curvature, and elliptic space has positive curvature.
- *Gauss's work:* Carl Friedrich Gauss privately developed non-Euclidean geometry but never published, fearing controversy; his unpublished notes reveal he had explored the subject decades before public breakthroughs.
- *Historical impact:* the existence of consistent non-Euclidean geometries overturned centuries of philosophical certainty, proving that mathematical systems could differ profoundly yet remain internally consistent—a revelation that influenced the foundations of mathematics, physics, and philosophy.

## connections

- [[euclid]] — his parallel postulate is the point of departure for all non-Euclidean systems.
