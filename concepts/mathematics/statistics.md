## summary

**Statistics** is the science of collecting, analyzing, and drawing inferences from data. It bridges probability theory and the real world, providing tools to understand patterns in noisy data and to make decisions under uncertainty. From quality control in manufacturing to epidemiology to social science, statistics is indispensable for turning data into actionable knowledge.

## you gotta know

- *Descriptive statistics*: summarize data via measures—mean (average), median (middle value), mode (most frequent), standard deviation (spread), correlation (linear association).
- *Inferential statistics*: use sample data to estimate unknown population parameters or test hypotheses; quantifies uncertainty via confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.
- *Probability distributions* (normal, binomial, Poisson, exponential, etc.) model random phenomena; the *normal (Gaussian) distribution* is ubiquitous—many real phenomena approximate it.
- *Central limit theorem*: the mean of large random samples is approximately normally distributed, regardless of the underlying distribution; explains why normal distribution is fundamental.
- *Hypothesis testing*: quantifies whether observed data support or contradict a null hypothesis; *p*-values measure evidence strength; significance level (e.g., α = 0.05) controls false positive rate.
- *Confidence intervals*: a 95% confidence interval for a parameter, if the procedure is repeated many times, contains the true value 95% of the time (long-run frequency interpretation).
- *Regression and correlation*: model relationships between variables; least-squares fitting minimizes residuals; Pearson correlation measures linear association strength (−1 to +1).
- *Sampling bias* and *selection effects* distort inference; random sampling and stratified sampling mitigate bias; replication and control groups strengthen causal claims in experiments.
- *Machine learning* and *data mining* apply statistical methods to large datasets; techniques like classification, clustering, and dimensionality reduction extract patterns for prediction and understanding.
- *Bayesian inference* complements frequentist methods; combines prior beliefs with observed data via Bayes' theorem; posterior distributions encode updated uncertainty; gaining importance in modern statistics.
- *Experimental design* (randomization, blocking, factorial designs) reduces confounding and maximizes statistical power; orthogonal contrasts and ANOVA decompose variance to isolate treatment effects.
