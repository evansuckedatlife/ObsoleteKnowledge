## summary

A **recurrence relation** is a mathematical equation that defines each term of a sequence recursively in terms of one or more of its preceding terms, anchored by a set of initial base conditions. Also designated as a **difference equation** in discrete calculus, it plays a vital role across combinatorics, number theory, dynamical systems, and the analysis of algorithms. Understanding these relations allows mathematicians to extract exact closed-form functions, compute asymptotic growth rates, and model discrete physical and computational processes that evolve in discrete time steps.

## you gotta know

- The *Fibonacci sequence*, defined by the relation $F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}$ with initial conditions $F_0 = 0$ and $F_1 = 1$, serves as the canonical example of a second-order linear recurrence, modeling idealized branching and rabbit population growth.
- *Binet's formula* yields an exact closed-form expression for the $n$th Fibonacci number, proving that terms of an integer sequence can be evaluated analytically via irrational powers of the [[golden-ratio]] without iterative summation.
- Constant-coefficient linear homogeneous relations are solved by constructing a characteristic [[polynomial-function]], whose roots form the geometric base terms of the general closed-form solution.
- When a characteristic root exhibits algebraic multiplicity greater than one, linearly independent solutions are constructed by multiplying the geometric sequence by increasing polynomial powers of the sequence index $n$.
- The transition to matrix form expresses linear recurrences as iterative dynamical systems, where evaluating powers of a companion matrix reveals asymptotic growth rates governed by each distinct [[eigenvalue]].
- Method of *generating functions*, developed by *Abraham de Moivre* and *Pierre-Simon Laplace*, translates discrete recurrence relations into continuous algebraic equations using formal power series whose coefficients encode the sequence.
- The *master theorem* in theoretical computer science resolves divide-and-conquer recurrences of the shape $T(n) = a T(n/b) + f(n)$, establishing exact asymptotic time complexity bounds categorized in [[big-o-notation]].
- Non-linear discrete relations like the *logistic map*, $x_{n+1} = r x_n (1 - x_n)$, demonstrate that simple deterministic recurrence rules can yield period-doubling bifurcations and deterministic chaos.

## connections

- [[golden-ratio]] — forms the dominant base of *Binet's formula* and emerges directly as the asymptotic [[limit]] of the ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers.
- [[polynomial-function]] — supplies the characteristic equation whose algebraic roots generate the fundamental eigensolutions of linear constant-coefficient recurrences.
- [[linear-algebra]] — provides the companion matrix framework that transforms high-order scalar difference equations into first-order vector-matrix dynamical systems.
- [[eigenvalue]] — determines the long-term asymptotic behavior and stability of matrix recurrences, matching the roots of the characteristic polynomial.
- [[exponential-function]] — acts as the continuous counterpart to discrete geometric progressions, frequently modeling the continuous envelope of rapidly growing recurrences.
- [[limit]] — defines the asymptotic behavior of recurrence sequences, capturing steady states, attractors, and limiting ratios between successive terms.
- [[big-o-notation]] — serves as the standard classification standard for asymptotic runtime solutions generated by algorithmic divide-and-conquer recurrences.
- [[pascal-triangle]] — embodies the fundamental two-variable additive recurrence for binomial coefficients and encodes Fibonacci numbers along its shallow diagonals.
