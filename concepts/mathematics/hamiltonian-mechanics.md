## summary

**Hamiltonian mechanics** is a powerful mathematical reformulation of classical mechanics introduced by the Irish mathematician *Sir William Rowan Hamilton* in 1833. Originating from optics and Lagrangian mechanics, the formalism replaces second-order differential equations of position with a system of coupled first-order differential equations defined over a geometric arena called phase space. It provides the core structural bridge linking classical dynamics to statistical thermodynamics and modern quantum theory.

## you gotta know

- Formulates physical dynamics on a symplectic manifold known as phase space, parameterized by generalized coordinates and conjugate momenta.
- Relies on the scalar function called the *Hamiltonian*, which corresponds to the total energy of a closed, time-independent system.
- Replaces the Euler-Lagrange equations by applying a *Legendre transformation* to the Lagrangian function.
- Governed by Hamilton's canonical equations of motion, which relate time derivatives of coordinates and momenta to partial derivatives of the Hamiltonian.
- Preserves phase-space volume along dynamical trajectories according to *Liouville's theorem*, maintaining an incompressible probability flow.
- Introduces *Poisson brackets*, an algebraic operation on smooth observables that directly prefigured the commutator algebra of quantum mechanics.
- Features canonical transformations and the *Hamilton-Jacobi equation*, which treats particles as wavefronts and directly inspired *Erwin Schrödinger* to formulate wave mechanics.

## connections

- [[calculus]] — differential machinery used to express canonical rates of change and state trajectories.
- [[derivative]] — mathematical operations used to compute generalized velocities, forces, and canonical equations.
- [[vector-space]] — linear spaces underlying local tangent and cotangent bundles in classical phase space.
- [[linear-algebra]] — structural basis for symplectic transformations, matrix representations, and phase-space invariants.
- [[continuous-functions]] — functional class defining smooth Hamiltonians, conservation laws, and invariant manifolds.
- [[isaac-newton]] — the originator of Newtonian mechanics, which the Hamiltonian system reformulates into generalized coordinates.
