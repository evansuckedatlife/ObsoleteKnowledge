## summary

**Joseph-Louis Lagrange** (1736–1813) was an Italian-born mathematician and astronomer who became one of the most influential figures of the 18th century. Working in Berlin and Paris, he revolutionized calculus through the calculus of variations, reformulated mechanics using abstract mathematical principles, and advanced algebra, number theory, and numerical analysis. His elegant methods—the *Lagrange multipliers*, the *Lagrange interpolation formula*, and the *Euler-Lagrange equations*—remain fundamental tools across mathematics and physics.

## you gotta know

- Born in Turin (1736); worked at the Berlin Academy under *Frederick the Great* for 20 years before moving to Paris, where he flourished during the revolutionary turmoil.
- Developed the *calculus of variations*, generalizing calculus to optimize entire functions (functionals) rather than isolated variables; this unified approach became fundamental to mathematical physics.
- Introduced *Lagrange multipliers* to solve constrained optimization problems; this technique finds extrema of a function subject to equality constraints by introducing auxiliary variables (multipliers).
- Reformulated classical mechanics using the *principle of least action*: a physical system evolves to extremize (usually minimize) the action integral S = ∫ L dt—equivalent to Newton's laws but more elegant and generalizable.
- Formulated the *Lagrangian* function L = T − V (kinetic minus potential energy), leading to the *Euler-Lagrange equations* that govern motion; these differential equations replace Newton's F = ma in Lagrange's formalism.
- Made deep contributions to number theory (theory of quadratic forms, continued fractions, representation by sums of squares) and algebra (resolvents of polynomial equations).
- The *Lagrange interpolation formula* constructs a unique polynomial of degree n−1 passing through n given data points: P(x) = Σ yᵢ ∏((x − xⱼ)/(xᵢ − xⱼ)); indispensable in numerical analysis.
- Developed fundamental results in calculus (mean value theorem, L'Hôpital's rule), number theory, and the theory of differential equations.
- His contributions unified mechanics, calculus, and number theory, establishing Lagrange as arguably the greatest mathematician of the 18th century.
