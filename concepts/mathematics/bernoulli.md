## summary

**Johann Bernoulli** (also known as **Jean Bernoulli**) was a pioneering Swiss mathematician of the early modern era who played a central role in developing and popularizing infinitesimal [[calculus]] across continental Europe. Born in Basel to a famed mathematical dynasty, he was one of the foremost disciples and champions of *Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz* during the bitter priority dispute with [[isaac-newton]]. He is celebrated for posing the famous brachistochrone problem—whose solution is the [[cycloid]], initiating the calculus of variations—as well as solving the shape of the hanging [[catenary]] and discovering the limit theorem later popularized as l'Hôpital's rule.

## you gotta know

- Posed the brachistochrone problem in 1696 in the journal *Acta Eruditorum*, challenging European mathematicians to determine the curve of fastest descent between two points, and proved using an optical analogy with Snell's law that the unique path is a [[cycloid]].
- Discovered the method for calculating indeterminate limits involving 0/0 and entered into a paid contract with *Guillaume de l'Hôpital*, who subsequently published the result under his own name in the landmark first [[calculus]] textbook, *Analyse des Infiniment Petits*.
- Solved the challenge of finding the mathematical curve of a hanging chain alongside *Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz* and *Christiaan Huygens*, demonstrating that the resulting [[catenary]] is expressed via the [[exponential-function]] and hyperbola rather than a parabola.
- Maintained an intensely bitter and public lifelong rivalry with his older brother *Jakob Bernoulli*, which degenerated from joint research on the isochrone into furious mutual accusations and competing mathematical challenges over isoperimetric curves.
- Championed the continental cause of *Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz* during the vitriolic priority dispute over the invention of calculus against [[isaac-newton]], anonymously penning the controversial 1713 pamphlet *Charta Volans* to defend Leibniz against English accusations.
- Succeeded his brother *Jakob* as professor of mathematics at the University of Basel in 1705, where he mentored the young *Leonhard Euler*, offering him private weekly instruction after recognizing his exceptional genius.
- Exhibited profound jealousy toward his own family members, culminating in throwing his son *Daniel Bernoulli* out of their home after they jointly shared the 1734 Grand Prize of the French Academy of Sciences.
- Formulated one of the earliest explicit definitions of a mathematical function in 1718, denoting a quantity composed of variables and constants with Greek letters, thereby advancing the formal analytical language of the [[enlightenment]].

## connections

- [[cycloid]] — the curve of fastest descent that he proved was the solution to his celebrated brachistochrone problem.
- [[catenary]] — the shape assumed by an idealized hanging chain, which he successfully analyzed and solved in 1691.
- [[calculus]] — the mathematical discipline he expanded through differential techniques, integral methods, and active promotion across Europe.
- [[derivative]] — the foundational rate of change concept central to his discovery of the limit evaluation rule sold to l'Hôpital.
- [[isaac-newton]] — the English physicist and mathematician whom he fiercely combated to defend Leibniz's priority in inventing calculus.
- [[exponential-function]] — the mathematical relation underlying the hyperbolic functions that describe the catenary curve he solved.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual era whose scientific revolution was propelled by his continental mathematical correspondence and disputes.
