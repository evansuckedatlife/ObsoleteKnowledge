## summary

In differential geometry, **curvature** quantifies the rate at which a geometric object deviates from being straight or flat within its surrounding space. First investigated for plane curves by *Isaac Newton* and *Christiaan Huygens*, the mathematical study of bending expanded dramatically when *Carl Friedrich Gauss* and *Bernhard Riemann* formalized the intrinsic geometry of surfaces and manifolds. It serves as the fundamental geometric invariant distinguishing non-Euclidean geometries from flat space and provides the mathematical language for general relativity.

## you gotta know

- For a space curve parameterized by arc length, **curvature** measures the magnitude of the rate of change of the unit tangent vector, formalized in the *Frenet*-*Serret* formulas.
- The reciprocal of curve **curvature** yields the radius of the osculating circle, which locally approximates the curve to the second order.
- On a two-dimensional surface embedded in three dimensions, principal curvatures represent the maximal and minimal bending of normal sections.
- Gaussian **curvature** equals the product of the principal curvatures and is an intrinsic invariant, as demonstrated by *Gauss's* celebrated *Theorema Egregium*.
- The *Gauss*-*Bonnet* theorem establishes that integrating Gaussian **curvature** over a compact surface equals two pi times its topological Euler characteristic.
- Constant negative **curvature** characterizes hyperbolic geometry, whereas constant positive **curvature** defines spherical elliptic geometry, both violating *Euclid's* fifth postulate.
- Higher-dimensional spaces are governed by the *Riemann* **curvature** tensor, which measures the failure of parallel transport along closed loops to preserve vector orientation.
- In *Albert Einstein's* general relativity, the stress-energy tensor dictates spacetime **curvature**, directly manifesting physical gravitation.

## connections

- [[euclid]] — the classical geometer whose parallel postulate is violated in spaces exhibiting non-zero intrinsic curvature.
- [[carl-friedrich-gauss]] — formulated the intrinsic theory of surfaces and proved that Gaussian curvature is preserved under local isometries.
- [[henri-poincare]] — developed conformal disk and upper half-plane models that realize constant negative curvature in non-Euclidean geometry.
- [[calculus]] — provides the foundational differential tools required to compute tangents, normals, and rates of turning along manifolds.
- [[derivative]] — difference quotients and derivatives define directional rate-of-change metrics for tangent vectors along curves.
- [[vector-space]] — provides the algebraic structure for tangent spaces and normal bundles where curvature tensors act.
- [[linear-algebra]] — supplies the spectral analysis and eigenvalue techniques used to diagonalize shape operators into principal curvatures.
