## summary

**Complex numbers** are numbers of the form a + bi, where a and b are real numbers and i is the *imaginary unit* (i² = −1). Complex numbers extend the number system beyond real numbers, enabling solutions to polynomial equations that have no real solutions and providing a unified framework for many mathematical and physical phenomena. The *Argand plane* visualizes complex numbers as points in a 2D space, while *Gaussian integers* are complex numbers with integer real and imaginary parts.

## you gotta know

- The imaginary unit *i* is defined by i² = −1; while no real number squares to a negative value, *i* is a useful algebraic construct that enables solving equations like x² + 1 = 0 (solution: x = ±i).
- Every complex number can be written uniquely as a + bi (rectangular form) or in polar form as r(cos θ + i sin θ), where r is the modulus (distance from origin) and θ is the argument (angle from the real axis).
- *Euler's formula* (e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ) connects complex numbers, exponential functions, and trigonometry in one of mathematics' most elegant identities, with applications across engineering, physics, and signal processing.
- The *Argand plane* represents complex numbers as 2D points: the real part is the x-coordinate and the imaginary part the y-coordinate; this geometric interpretation makes complex arithmetic intuitive (addition is vector addition, multiplication rotates and scales).
- *Gaussian integers* (complex numbers with integer real and imaginary parts, like 3 + 4i) have unique factorization properties; they play roles in algebraic number theory and in proving results like Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares.
- The *fundamental theorem of algebra* states that every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has at least one complex root; complex numbers complete the number system so all polynomial equations have solutions.
- Complex numbers are essential in physics and engineering: *quantum mechanics* relies on complex wave functions; *electrical engineering* uses complex impedances to model AC circuits; *signal processing* employs complex Fourier transforms.
