## summary

**Apollonius** was an ancient Greek mathematician (c. 262–190 BCE) whose masterwork *Conics* provided the definitive classical treatment of ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas. He advanced far beyond earlier Greek geometry by deriving these curves from a single geometric principle—the intersection of a plane with a cone—establishing a unified framework that shaped mathematical thought for over a thousand years.

## you gotta know

- Known as "the Great Geometer" for his systematic and rigorous approach to mathematical discovery; his reputation rivaled even [[euclid]]'s among later mathematicians.
- His eight-volume *Conics* was the most advanced mathematical treatise of the ancient world and remained authoritative through the Renaissance, influencing scientists including [[isaac-newton]] centuries later.
- Proved that all conic sections (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola, circle) result from cutting a cone at different angles—a profoundly unifying insight that transformed scattered geometric knowledge into a unified theory.
- Introduced the terminology now standard in conic sections: focus, directrix, and the eccentricity of a curve, allowing precise algebraic description of these shapes.
- Applied conic sections to practical problems in astronomy (planetary orbits), music (acoustic properties), and mechanics, demonstrating their real-world utility beyond pure mathematics.
- His methods anticipated modern analytic geometry by centuries; later mathematicians would use his geometric insights to develop coordinate systems and algebraic representations of curves.
- Little is known of his personal life; most biographical details come from references in other ancient texts, reflecting how much ancient knowledge has been lost to time.
- His work demonstrates the power of unification in mathematics—discovering that seemingly disparate curves (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola) are manifestations of a single geometric principle fundamentally changed mathematical thinking.
- The study of conic sections, revived in Renaissance Europe through [[apollonius]]'s texts, became essential to the emerging field of projective geometry and later enabled the development of analytic geometry.
