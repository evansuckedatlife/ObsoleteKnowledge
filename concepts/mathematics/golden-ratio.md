## summary

The **golden ratio**, frequently denoted by the Greek letter **phi** or called the **divine proportion**, is an irrational mathematical constant approximately equal to 1.6180339887. First rigorously defined in [[ancient-greece]] by the mathematician [[euclid]] as the division of a continuous line segment into extreme and mean ratio, it characterizes a unique geometric relationship where the ratio of the whole to the larger part equals that of the larger part to the smaller. Renowned across [[number-theory]], geometry, and aesthetic philosophy, it represents the limiting ratio of successive terms in the *Fibonacci sequence* and serves as a foundational bridge uniting mathematical rigor with visual harmony in [[renaissance]] art, architecture, and natural morphology.

## you gotta know

- It is an algebraic irrational number whose exact closed form is $(1 + \sqrt{5}) / 2$, which emerges as the [[limit]] of the ratio of consecutive terms in the integer *Fibonacci sequence* governed by a second-order linear [[recurrence-relation]].
- Formally defined in [[euclid]]'s *Elements*, the ratio arises when dividing a line segment such that the ratio of the whole length to the longer segment equals the ratio of the longer segment to the shorter, satisfying the monic quadratic [[polynomial-function]] $x^2 - x - 1 = 0$.
- Celebrated during the [[renaissance]] in [[italy]], the Franciscan mathematician *Fra Luca Pacioli* dedicated his 1509 treatise *De Divina Proportione* to the ratio, featuring sixty intricate geometric perspective drawings rendered by [[leonardo-da-vinci]].
- A golden rectangle can be subdivided into a square and a smaller proportional rectangle ad infinitum, producing a logarithmic [[spiral]] whose radius expands by a factor of the ratio for every quarter turn, approximating patterns found in sunflower heads and nautilus shells.
- The golden angle, measuring approximately $137.5^\circ$, is derived by dividing the circumference of a circle according to the ratio, optimizing geometric packing efficiency in plant leaves and florets to maximize sunlight interception and seed density.
- In analytical [[number-theory]], it is considered the most irrational real number because its canonical continued fraction expansion consists solely of ones, $[1; 1, 1, 1, \dots]$, meaning its rational approximations converge slower than those of any other irrational value.
- The constant directly governs the trigonometry of regular pentagons and pentagrams, appears in the dimensional proportions of the [[dna]] double helix, and forms the fundamental spatial geometry underlying non-periodic *Penrose tilings* that model physical quasicrystals.

## connections

- [[euclid]] — formulated the earliest surviving rigorous definition of the proportion as extreme and mean ratio in his *Elements*.
- [[leonardo-da-vinci]] — illustrated *Luca Pacioli*'s Renaissance treatise on the proportion and investigated geometric harmonic ratios in his anatomical sketches of [[human-anatomy]].
- [[limit]] — expresses the asymptotic convergence of the quotient of consecutive Fibonacci numbers as their index approaches infinity.
- [[spiral]] — generated recursively from nested golden rectangles and mirrors spiral growth morphology across diverse biological structures.
- [[renaissance]] — the intellectual movement in [[italy]] that elevated the proportion to an emblem of cosmic symmetry and aesthetic perfection.
- [[number-theory]] — explores the ratio's algebraic properties as a quadratic unit and its status as the slowest-converging continued fraction.
- [[dna]] — displays structural proportions in the length and width of its major and minor helical grooves that approximate the constant.
- [[recurrence-relation]] — models the discrete recursive sequences whose closed-form expressions, such as *Binet's formula*, explicitly depend on powers of the constant.
