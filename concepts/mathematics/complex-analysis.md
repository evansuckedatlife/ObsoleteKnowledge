## summary

**Complex analysis** is the study of functions that take complex numbers as inputs and produce complex outputs. It extends real calculus to the complex plane, revealing profound structures—holomorphic functions, residues, and conformal mappings—that have applications from engineering to physics to number theory. Many properties of complex functions are far more elegant and rigid than their real counterparts.

## you gotta know

- A *holomorphic* (or analytic) function is one that is complex-differentiable in a neighborhood around each point—far more restrictive than real differentiability, requiring compatibility between horizontal and vertical derivatives.
- The Cauchy integral formula states that the values of a holomorphic function inside a closed curve are completely determined by its values on the boundary, yielding all derivatives via contour integrals.
- *Residue theory* allows computation of real integrals and infinite series through complex function analysis, bypassing direct calculation; the residue theorem is one of mathematics' most powerful computational tools.
- The *Riemann hypothesis*—concerning the distribution of zeros of the Riemann zeta function—is one of mathematics' greatest unsolved problems with million-dollar prize incentive and deep implications for prime distribution.
- Conformal mappings preserve angles and are used to solve boundary-value problems in physics (fluid flow, electrostatics, heat diffusion) by transforming complex domains into simpler ones.
- Power series and Laurent series expansions of holomorphic functions converge with remarkable speed, enabling practical computation and deep theoretical insights unavailable in real analysis.
- Singularities (poles, essential singularities, branch points) carry concentrated information; analyzing their behavior reveals fundamental truths about function structure and global properties.
- The surprising rigidity of holomorphic functions—knowing them on a small region determines them everywhere—contrasts sharply with real functions and reflects the deep constraints imposed by complex differentiability.
