## summary

A **focus** is a distinguished reference point used in geometry to define and construct conic sections along with related curves. Formally studied in *[[ancient-greece]]* by mathematicians such as *Apollonius of Perga* and *Pappus of Alexandria*, the concept describes points from which geometric distances determine the curve's locus. It became foundational to celestial mechanics when *Johannes Kepler* and *[[isaac-newton]]* demonstrated that gravitational orbits follow conic trajectories with an attracting mass located at one focal point.

## you gotta know

- Defines conic sections via the ratio of the distance to a **focus** and the distance to a line called the directrix, a constant ratio known as eccentricity.
- An ellipse possesses two internal foci; the sum of the distances from any point on the curve to the two foci is strictly constant.
- A hyperbola also possesses two foci, with the absolute difference of the distances from any point on the curve to the foci being constant.
- A parabola has a single focal point and a directrix, where every point on the parabola remains equidistant from both.
- Optical reflection properties dictate that rays emanating from one **focus** of an ellipse reflect off the curve directly toward the second focal point.
- Parabolic reflectors redirect incoming parallel rays straight to their single **focus**, forming the operating principle of modern reflecting telescopes, satellite dishes, and radar antennae.
- Governs *Kepler's first law of planetary motion*, which states that every planet moves along an elliptical orbit with the Sun situated at one **focus**.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the civilization where *Apollonius of Perga* composed his foundational treatise *Conics*.
- [[euclid]] — the classical geometer whose geometric axioms and methods established planar loci theory.
- [[isaac-newton]] — the physicist who mathematically derived Keplerian focal orbits using universal gravitation.
- [[calculus]] — mathematical framework required to calculate arc lengths, curvatures, and evolutes of focal conic curves.
- [[polynomial-function]] — algebraic functions that characterize conics as degree-two quadratic curves.
- [[catenary]] — a hanging curve historically distinguished from parabolas by focal properties and tension.
