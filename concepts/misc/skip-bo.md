## summary

**Skip-Bo** is a commercial shedding-type card game originally created by Minnie Hazel Bowman in Texas during the late 1960s. Marketed widely by *International Games* and later by *Mattel*, it is played with a specialized 162-card deck consisting of cards numbered 1 to 12 and wild cards. The primary objective is to be the first player or team to empty a personal stockpile by building sequential stacks from 1 through 12 in a shared center area.

## you gotta know

- Created in 1967 by *Minnie Hazel Bowman* of Brownfield, Texas, who initially packaged and sold the game locally before selling the rights in 1980.
- Derived mechanically from the traditional card game *Spite and Malice*, which itself evolved as a competitive variant of *Russian Bank* using standard playing cards.
- The dedicated deck contains 162 cards in total: twelve cards each of numerals 1 through 12, plus eighteen eponymous wild cards.
- Each player starts with a face-down stockpile of up to thirty cards, with only the top card flipped face up; depleting this pile wins the game.
- Up to four central building piles are constructed in strict ascending order from 1 to 12, after which a completed pile is removed from play and reshuffled.
- Acquired in 1987 by *International Games*, the creator of *Uno*, which was subsequently purchased by corporate toy giant *Mattel* in 1992.
- Players draw up to a hand of five cards each turn and maintain up to four personal discard piles, which require strategic card management.
