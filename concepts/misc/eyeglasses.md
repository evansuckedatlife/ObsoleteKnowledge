## summary

**Eyeglasses** are optical instruments consisting of ground glass or plastic lenses mounted in a frame that rests upon the bridge of the nose and the ears to correct refractive visual errors. Originating in medieval northern *[[italy]]* during the late thirteenth century, they dramatically extended the intellectual and artisan careers of aging scholars. In the history of science and technology, spectacles catalyzed the development of optics, precision manufacturing, and downstream instruments including the compound microscope and the astronomical telescope.

## you gotta know

- Invented in northern *[[italy]]*, most likely between Pisa and Venice, circa 1286; friar *Giordano da Pisa* recorded in a 1306 sermon that the craft had been discovered only twenty years earlier.
- The Dominican friar *Alessandro della Spina* of Pisa is documented in contemporary monastic chronicles as having independently replicated and freely shared the secret of grinding optical glass.
- Murano glassmakers in Venice initially dominated European spectacle production due to their mastery of crystalline glass (*cristallo*) and protective guild regulations.
- Early spectacles consisted of two convex magnifying lenses set in leather, bone, or horn rims pinned together at the bridge, requiring users to clamp them onto the nose or hold them manually.
- Concave lenses for correcting myopia (nearsightedness) appeared in the mid-fifteenth century, famously owned and commissioned by the Medici family of Florence.
- *Benjamin Franklin* is traditionally credited with inventing *bifocals* in the 1780s by slicing convex and concave lenses horizontally and mounting them together in a single frame to eliminate constant swapping.
- British optician *Edward Scarlett* perfected rigid side arms (temples) ending in loops around 1727, allowing frames to balance securely over the ears without slipping.
- The cylindrical lens for correcting astigmatism was designed in 1827 by the British astronomer *George Biddell Airy*, completing the foundational suite of refractive optical corrections.

## connections

- [[italy]] — the birthplace of lens-grinding technology, nurtured in the monastic scriptoria and merchant workshops of Pisa and Venice.
- [[perception]] — the cognitive and physiological process of vision transformed by compensating for optical refraction defects.
- [[five-senses]] — sight, the primary sensory modality augmented and preserved across aging populations by corrective glass.
- [[geometry]] — the mathematical principles of focal lengths, angles of incidence, and curvature governing lens fabrication.
- [[renaissance]] — the era of flourishing scholarship and print culture whose literate class relied directly upon magnifying spectacles.
- [[enlightenment]] — the century of scientific experimentation when figures like *Benjamin Franklin* advanced bifocal optics.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the era of mass mechanization that turned hand-ground bespoke lenses into standardized, interchangeable consumer goods.
