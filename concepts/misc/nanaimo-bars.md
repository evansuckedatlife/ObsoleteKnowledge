## summary

The **Nanaimo bar** is a classic three-layered no-bake dessert square that originated on Vancouver Island in British Columbia, Canada. Named after the coastal harbor city of *Nanaimo*, the confection consists of a coconut graham wafer crumb base, a rich custard-flavored buttercream center, and a glossy melted chocolate ganache top. Celebrated alongside the ceremonial [[mountie-uniform]] as an enduring touchstone of Canadian popular culture, the dessert remains a staple of community bake sales and holiday gatherings throughout North America.

## you gotta know

- Defined strictly by its three distinct unbaked layers: a chewy chocolate-coconut-nut wafer foundation, a dense yellow custard-butter icing midsection, and a smooth hard chocolate canopy.
- The iconic yellow filling owes its characteristic texture and mild vanilla flavor to the specific use of *Bird's Custard Powder*, a classic egg-free starch substitute imported from the *[[united-kingdom]]*.
- The crumbly bottom layer combines crushed graham crackers, cocoa powder, shredded coconut, butter, and finely chopped walnuts or almonds bound with beaten egg.
- First appeared in print under the specific name *Nanaimo bar* in the 1952 edition of the *Nanaimo Hospital Auxiliary Cookbook*, alongside earlier regional monikers such as the *London fog bar* or *smack bar*.
- Gained widespread international fame when Nanaimo mayor Graeme Roberts organized an ultimate recipe competition in 1986, establishing a standardized civic recipe crafted by local baker Joyce Hardcastle.
- Recognized officially as a Canadian cultural culinary staple, featured on federal postage stamps and regularly served at formal diplomatic state events.
