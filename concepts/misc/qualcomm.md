## summary

**Qualcomm** is an American multinational semiconductor and telecommunications equipment corporation headquartered in San Diego, California, that pioneered digital wireless communications. Established in 1985 by *Irwin M. Jacobs* and six co-founders, the firm commercialized Code Division Multiple Access (*CDMA*), transforming global cellular network capacity and setting fundamental standards for mobile connectivity. Through its ubiquitous *Snapdragon* systems-on-chip and an extensive standard-essential patent licensing business, it remains a central architect of modern 4G, 5G, and connected computing infrastructure.

## you gotta know

- Founded in July 1985 in San Diego, California, by *Irwin M. Jacobs*, *Andrew Viterbi* (famed inventor of the error-correcting *Viterbi algorithm*), and five fellow communications engineers under the corporate moniker "Quality Communications."
- Revolutionized mobile cellular networks by championing *CDMA* (Code Division Multiple Access), a spread-spectrum radio transmission standard that allowed multiple transmissions on the same frequency band simultaneously, outperforming rival *TDMA* and early *GSM* technologies.
- Launched its first breakout commercial hardware product called *OmniTRACS*, a two-way satellite data communications and vehicle-tracking system widely installed across commercial trucking fleets in the *[[united-states]]*.
- Developed and maintained the widely popular early desktop email client *Eudora*, acquired in 1991 and named in tribute to the celebrated Southern author *Eudora Welty*.
- Designs the industry-leading *Snapdragon* family of mobile processors and wireless cellular modems, powering a vast proportion of premium Android mobile devices, automotive cockpits, and wearable electronics.
- Operates a highly lucrative and intensely scrutinized licensing division (*QTL*), which collects royalties on nearly every modern cellular handset produced worldwide due to its ownership of foundational wireless patents.
- Defeated a hostile 117-billion-dollar acquisition attempt by Singapore-based rival *Broadcom* in 2018 when the federal government issued an executive order blocking the merger citing national security and technological leadership concerns.
- Held long-term naming rights to San Diego's flagship multi-purpose stadium (*Qualcomm Stadium*), which hosted multiple Super Bowls before the venue was decommissioned and demolished in 2021.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — home country where the company was founded, maintains corporate headquarters in San Diego, and receives regulatory oversight.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical and military research era that spurred federal investments into digital signal processing, satellite arrays, and spread-spectrum radio.
- [[space-age]] — satellite communications infrastructure that enabled the deployment and success of the firm's breakthrough *OmniTRACS* fleet management system.
- [[world-war-ii]] — historical origin point of frequency-hopping spread-spectrum concepts, originally patented by *Hedy Lamarr* and *George Antheil* to guide torpedoes, which prefigured CDMA.
- [[japan]] — vital early international telecommunications partner whose commercial network operators, notably *DDI* and *IDO*, embraced Qualcomm's cdmaOne standard.
- [[modernism]] — industrial and architectural framework characterizing postwar American research campuses and high-technology corporate parks.
