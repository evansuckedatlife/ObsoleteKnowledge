## summary

**Cognac**, officially termed **Cognac brandy**, is a high-grade distilled spirit produced exclusively in a delimited region surrounding the town of *Cognac* in southwestern France. Governed by rigorous French appellation laws, the spirit is crafted through double distillation in copper pot stills from white wine grapes and aged for years in French oak barrels. Renowned worldwide for its complexity and prestige, it represents an essential quiz-bowl subject encompassing viticulture, European trade history, and 20th-century cultural diplomacy.

## you gotta know

- Protected legally by the strict *Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée* (AOC) framework established in 1936, mandating that genuine production occur exclusively within designated zones of the Charente and Charente-Maritime departments of [[france]].
- Distilled overwhelmingly from *Ugni Blanc* white grapes (known in Italy as Trebbiano), prized for their high acidity, low sugar levels, and resistance to disease, producing a thin, sour wine ideal for concentrated distillation.
- Processed by mandatory double distillation (*à repasse*) using traditional copper Charentais pot stills (*alambics charentais*), which run continuously during the winter months and must legally conclude by March 31 following the harvest.
- Matured exclusively in French oak casks crafted from the forests of Limousin or Tronçais, where the spirit extracts tannins, color, and vanillin over a minimum mandatory aging period of two years.
- Classified into official age categories overseen by the *Bureau National Interprofessionnel du Cognac* (BNIC): *V.S.* ("Very Special", aged at least two years), *V.S.O.P.* ("Very Superior Old Pale", aged at least four years), and *X.O.* ("Extra Old", aged at least ten years).
- Originated during the 16th and 17th centuries when Dutch maritime traders traveling during the [[age-of-exploration]] distilled local Charente wines into *brandewijn* ("burnt wine") so they would not spoil during long sea journeys.
- Devastated in the late 19th century by the catastrophic *phylloxera* aphid epidemic, which wiped out local vineyards until growers successfully grafted European scions onto resistant American rootstocks during the [[industrial-revolution]].
- Embraced profoundly by the [[african-diaspora]], initially popularized by Black American soldiers stationed in France during *World War I* and [[world-war-ii]] who found acceptance abroad, turning brands like *Hennessy* and *Courvoisier* into fixtures of mid-century jazz and modern hip-hop culture.

## connections

- [[france]] — nation whose western Charente region holds the exclusive geographic and legal rights to manufacture and label the spirit under international trade agreements.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — historical period of burgeoning maritime commerce during which Dutch and English merchant fleets spurred commercial wine distillation to preserve cargo on oceanic voyages.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era that transformed commercial glass manufacturing and transatlantic shipping, while also witnessing the devastating *phylloxera* blight that overhauled French agricultural science.
- [[belle-epoque]] — golden age of European luxury dining and cafe culture in which fine brandies became premier symbols of international culinary sophistication.
- [[world-war-ii]] — conflict during which French producers hid valuable aged spirit reserves from occupying German authorities and where Allied soldiers developed tastes for French spirits.
- [[african-diaspora]] — cultural sphere where major houses established deep community ties, beginning with Black veterans returning home to the segregated United States and flourishing in urban music culture.
- [[cognate]] — linguistic concept relevant to the commune's name *Cognac*, an ancient toponym that has no etymological connection to the Latin verb *cognoscere* despite phonetic similarity.
