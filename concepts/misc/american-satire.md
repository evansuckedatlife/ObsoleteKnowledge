## summary

**American satire** is a literary, journalistic, and cultural tradition within the [[united-states]] that employs irony, parody, hyperbole, and ridicule to expose social hypocrisy, political corruption, and national mythologies. Originating in colonial broadsides, mock-epic verses, and political tracts during the [[american-revolution]], the genre matured across the nineteenth century through frontier tall tales, vernacular regional dialect, and biting critiques of rapid commercial expansion. From the bitter anti-war absurdism of the twentieth century to modern multimedia commentary, it remains a foundational pillar of American civic discourse and literature, offering an enduring vehicle for dissent, moral interrogation, and democratic self-scrutiny.

## you gotta know

- The author [[mark-twain]] stands as the preeminent master of the tradition, co-authoring the scathing critique *The Gilded Age: A Tale of Today* with *Charles Dudley Warner* to excoriate rampant post-[[american-civil-war]] Washington graft, financial chicanery, and unbridled speculation.
- Post-war twentieth-century masterworks like *Joseph Heller*'s *Catch-22* and *Kurt Vonnegut*'s *Slaughterhouse-Five* synthesized grotesque black humor with [[existentialism]] to lampoon military bureaucracy, senseless violence, and the firebombing atrocities of [[world-war-ii]].
- The genre assumed early institutional weight during the [[american-revolution]] and transatlantic [[enlightenment]], championed by figures such as *Benjamin Franklin*, whose satirical hoaxes like *Rules by Which a Great Empire May Be Reduced to a Small One* exposed British imperial folly.
- Mid-nineteenth-century southwestern humorists and northeastern wits deployed frontier tall tales, comical pseudonyms, and vernacular personae to dismantle aristocratic pretense, directly shaping the dialect-driven storytelling techniques of the [[reconstruction-era]].
- *Ambrose Bierce* channeled bitter personal disillusionment into *The Devil's Dictionary*, recasting conventional civic, religious, and ethical definitions into razor-sharp cynical epigrams shaped by his grim frontline experiences during the [[american-civil-war]].
- Urban literary satire flourished across [[new-york-city]] through Algonquin Round Table members and *The New Yorker*, where wits such as *Dorothy Parker* and *James Thurber* weaponized dry metropolitan irony against middle-class pretension and suburban domesticity.
- Nineteenth-century political print journals, prominently *Puck* and *Judge*, popularized color editorial cartooning, leveraging vicious visual caricatures to target Tammany Hall bosses, political machines, and monopolistic trusts spawned by the [[industrial-revolution]].
- Early colonial roots emerged through mock-heroic verse like *Ebenezer Cooke*'s 1708 poem *The Sotweed Factor*, which parodied promotional travel tracts of *Maryland* with bawdy, burlesque accounts of frontier swindles and corrupt tobacco barter.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — provides the historical, constitutional, and cultural canvas whose democratic ideals, capitalism, and political institutions are relentlessly interrogated.
- [[mark-twain]] — established the quintessential standard of American vernacular realism, western tall humor, and searing anti-imperialist political indictment.
- [[american-civil-war]] — shattered romantic antebellum regional illusions and catalyzed a starker, far more cynical realism and battlefield satire.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — fostered widespread satirical treatments of political carpetbaggers, Southern scalawags, and legislative bribery during tumultuous national reunification.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — fueled ferocious literary and journalistic attacks against the Gilded Age's robber barons, rampant corporate trusts, and urban inequality.
- [[modernism]] — transformed twentieth-century satirical prose through cynical interiority, fragmented syntax, and profound disillusionment with institutional authority.
- [[cold-war]] — prompted a flourish of paranoid, apocalyptic black comedy and political absurdism mocking nuclear brinkmanship and red-scare hysteria.
- [[enlightenment]] — supplied the philosophical skepticism, rationalist wit, and pseudonymity that grounded early American colonial and revolutionary political broadsides.
