## summary

**Euchre** is a trick-taking card game played primarily with a stripped deck of twenty-four playing cards by four players in two fixed partnerships. Originating among nineteenth-century German-American communities in Pennsylvania and the Midwest, it quickly rose to become the premier social card game in nineteenth-century America. It holds lasting historical significance for introducing the Joker card to modern Anglo-American playing decks and remains intensely popular across the Great Lakes region and the American Midwest.

## you gotta know

- Played with a shortened deck of twenty-four standard cards comprising only the nines through aces, divided equally into four five-card hands with four cards left in the kitty.
- The highest trump card is the Jack of the trump suit, termed the *Right Bower*, followed immediately in rank by the Jack of the same-colored off-suit, known as the *Left Bower*.
- Introduced the *Joker* into the modern fifty-two card pack in the 1850s, where it was originally invented as the supreme trump card under names such as the *Imperial Bower* or *Best Bower*.
- Partnerships compete to take at least three of the five available tricks in each round, with failing to make an announced trump contract termed being *euchred*.
- Widely disseminated during the *[[american-civil-war]]*, during which soldiers played it in encampments, helping establish its enduring status as a regional pastime across Michigan, Ohio, Indiana, and Wisconsin.
- Derived from the nineteenth-century German game *Juckerspiel*, which shares its characteristic promotion of Jacks and shortened pack structure.
- Players may declare *going alone*, where one partner plays a hand without teammate participation in pursuit of scoring a four-point sweep.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the primary country where the game developed into a dominant cultural and regional card game.
- [[germany]] — origin of the ancestral card game *Juckerspiel* brought to the Americas by immigrants.
- [[american-civil-war]] — historical conflict whose encampments fueled the widespread national adoption and standardization of the game.
- [[england]] — home to British trick-taking card traditions that merged with continental card designs.
- [[france]] — source of the standard suit symbols (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades) utilized in modern play.
