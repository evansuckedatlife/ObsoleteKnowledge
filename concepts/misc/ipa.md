## summary

The **International Phonetic Alphabet** is an internationally standardized system of phonetic notation devised by linguists to provide a unique, unambiguous graphic symbol for every distinct phone or sound occurring in human spoken language. Established in Paris in 1888 by the *International Phonetic Association* under the leadership of French linguist *Paul Passy*, the notation is anchored in the physiological mechanisms of speech production. It serves as an essential universal framework across descriptive [[linguistics]], speech-language pathology, comparative lexicography, and classical vocal performance.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1888 by the *International Phonetic Association*, which was established two years earlier as *The Phonetic Teachers' Association* by *Paul Passy*, *Daniel Jones*, and *Otto Jespersen*.
- The master chart classifies consonants along two primary physiological axes governed by [[human-anatomy]]: the horizontal axis denotes the *place of articulation* where airflow constriction occurs, while the vertical axis denotes the *manner of articulation* regulating airflow dynamics.
- The chart's vowel quadrilateral arranges vocalic sounds according to the cardinal vowel system developed by *Daniel Jones*, plotting sounds along height, backness, and roundedness dimensions to reflect auditory and articulatory acoustic space.
- Distinct brackets signal crucial theoretical distinctions in transcriptions: square brackets designate narrow phonetic transcription capturing concrete physical acoustic phones, whereas slashes enclose broad phonemic transcription representing abstract cognitive units.
- Non-pulmonic consonants are explicitly cataloged in a dedicated sub-chart encompassing clicks, voiced implosives, and ejectives, which frequently appear in African and indigenous North American tongues like the [[iroquoian-language-family]].
- The mid central unrounded vowel is designated by the *schwa*, an inverted Latin letter derived from Hebrew orthographic conventions that represents the most frequent reduced vowel sound across Germanic languages.
- Suprasegmental notation marks prosodic features, placing high and low vertical ticks before syllables to indicate primary and secondary lexical stress, while specialized combining diacritics represent phonation types like creaky voice and breathy voice.
- Major institutional revisions to the official chart occurred at conferences in *Kiel* in 1989 and *San Francisco* in 1993, with the most recent symbol addition being the labiodental flap in 2005.

## connections

- [[human-anatomy]] — derives symbol categorizations directly from vocal tract biology, including the glottis, larynx, velum, tongue, and lips.

- [[france]] — provided the intellectual birthplace where *Paul Passy* organized European phoneticians to create the earliest published charts.

- [[perception]] — bridges the sensory reception of acoustic frequencies with categorical cognitive representations of spoken language.
