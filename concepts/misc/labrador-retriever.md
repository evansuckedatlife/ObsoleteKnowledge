## summary

The **Labrador Retriever**, commonly known simply as the **Labrador** or **Lab**, is a British breed of retriever gun dog that originated from fishing dogs in Newfoundland and was systematically developed in nineteenth-century [[england]]. Celebrated for its gentle temperament, dense weather-resistant double coat, and distinctive broad "otter tail," it long ranked as the most frequently registered purebred companion animal across both the [[united-kingdom]] and the [[united-states]]. In working disciplines and trivia competitions, the breed is renowned not only as a consummate waterfowl-hunting retriever but also as the quintessential guide dog for the visually impaired, search-and-rescue partner, and contraband-detection specialist.

## you gotta know

- Traces ancestry directly to the extinct *St. John's water dog*, a landrace of working water dog kept by fishermen in Newfoundland to retrieve fishing nets and haul lines in freezing North Atlantic waters.
- Formally imported and preserved by English aristocrats, notably the second *Earl of Malmesbury* at Heron Court and the fifth *Duke of Buccleuch*, who bred them for waterfowl shoots on British estates.
- Exhibits three standard coat colors recognized by kennel clubs: black, yellow, and chocolate, which are governed by epistatic interactions between the *B* and *E* genetic loci.
- Distinctively features an "otter tail" that is exceptionally thick at the base and tapers toward the tip, covered uniformly with dense fur and functioning as a powerful rudder when swimming.
- Possesses webbed paws with skin extending between the digits, aiding in water propulsion and distributing weight across marshy terrain, muddy fields, and packed snow.
- Maintained the historic distinction of holding the record for the most registered dog breed by the American Kennel Club for thirty-one consecutive years from 1991 until 2022.
- Widely selected as guide dogs by organizations such as Guide Dogs for the Blind due to their high trainability, food motivation, biddable personality, and low aggression.
- Prone to specific genetic health vulnerabilities commonly queried in veterinary biology, notably hip and elbow dysplasia, exercise-induced collapse, and progressive retinal atrophy.
