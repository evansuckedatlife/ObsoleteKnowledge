## summary

**Tabasco** is an internationally iconic brand of pepper condiment produced exclusively by the family-operated **McIlhenny Company** on *Avery Island* in southern Louisiana. Created shortly after the American Civil War by former banker *Edmund McIlhenny*, the pungent preparation known universally as **Tabasco sauce** blends ripe red *Capsicum frutescens* peppers with mineral salt and high-grade distilled vinegar. Renowned for its distinctive diamond-shaped label, glass shaker bottles, and multi-year aging process in white oak barrels, the condiment remains one of the longest continuously manufactured commercial food brands in American history.

## you gotta know

- Formulated in 1868 by Maryland native *Edmund McIlhenny*, who planted hot pepper seeds on his family's coastal estate after his New Orleans banking career collapsed.
- Takes its brand name from the Mexican state of *Tabasco*, a coastal territory whose indigenous Nahuatl etymology is generally translated as "place where the soil is humid."
- Undergoes a meticulous maturation process wherein crushed pepper mash is aged for up to three years inside reclaimed white oak bourbon barrels sealed with a thick protective crust of salt.
- Harvests all of its production salt from the expansive subterranean salt dome underlying *Avery Island*, an ancient geological formation surrounded by coastal bayous and marshes.
- Packaged since its inception in small glass bottles modeled after discarded cologne flasks, featuring a green foil neck seal and an aperture restrictor that dispenses the sauce drop by drop.
- Retains exclusive corporate ownership within the direct descendants of the *McIlhenny* family, resisting outside acquisition while operating as a global export empire in over 190 countries.
- Supplied continuously as an official morale-boosting component inside combat field rations, including the *Meal, Ready-to-Eat* (MRE) packets distributed to armed forces worldwide.

## connections

- [[reconstruction-era]] — historical era of southern economic rebuilding during which Edmund McIlhenny patented and commercialized his hot sauce formula.
- [[tex-mex]] — southwestern culinary tradition that frequently integrates hot pepper sauces and fiery salsas into its flavor profiles.
- [[united-states]] — nation where the condiment became a domestic cultural fixture and standard government military ration issue.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — manufacturing era that facilitated automated bottle production, standardized vinegar production, and national rail distribution.
- [[spain]] — European monarchy that once claimed the Louisiana territory and introduced New World peppers into global culinary trade.
