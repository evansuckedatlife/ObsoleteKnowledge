## summary

The **Lychee** (*Litchi chinensis*) is a tropical and subtropical fruit tree in the soapberry family (*Sapindaceae*), native to the southeastern provinces of China. Renowned for its rough, leathery pink-red rind and sweet, floral, translucent white aril, the fruit has been celebrated in East Asian agriculture, gastronomy, and literature for well over two millennia. In cultural history, it is famously associated with the imperial court of the Tang Dynasty, while in modern botanical and medical research, it serves as an important economic crop and a notable subject of metabolic toxicology.

## you gotta know

- Indigenous to the humid rainforests and river valleys of *Guangdong* and *Fujian* provinces, where systematic cultivation dates back to at least the *Han Dynasty* around 200 BCE.
- Central to one of Chinese history's most famous romantic legends involving *Emperor Xuanzong* of the *Tang Dynasty*, who organized a relay of galloping horses over thousands of miles to deliver fresh, unblemished fruit to his favored consort, *Yang Guifei*.
- Celebrated in classic Chinese poetry, most notably by the Tang poet *Du Mu* in his poem *Passing Huaqing Palace*, which critically observes the imperial courier galloping through red dust just to evoke the concubine's smile.
- The Song Dynasty poet *Su Shi* (Dongpo) famously declared during his exile to southern China that he would happily remain a native of *Lingnan* as long as he could feast on three hundred fresh fruits a day.
- Structurally composed of an inedible, brittle, papillate exocarp covering a succulent edible aril, which surrounds a glossy dark brown seed that is botanically considered non-viable in certain prized "chicken-tongue" cultivars.
- Traditional staple of celebrations during *Chinese New Year*, symbolizing romance, family prosperity, and good fortune due to its festive crimson coloration and sweet flavor.
- Medical researchers identified that unripe fruit contains naturally occurring non-proteinogenic amino acids, specifically *hypoglycin A* and *methylene cyclopropyl glycine* (MCPG), which inhibit gluconeogenesis and caused lethal seasonal encephalopathy outbreaks among malnourished children in regions like *Muzaffarpur*, India.
- Widely exported globally either canned in heavy syrup, fresh via refrigerated cold chains, or in dehydrated form known as "lychee nuts," which feature a firm, raisin-like consistency and concentrated sweetness.

- [[colonialism]] — shaped the nineteenth-century plantation agriculture and commercial trading routes that established subtropical fruit orchards across the tropical world.
