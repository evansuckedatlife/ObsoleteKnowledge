## summary

**Clove** is a pungent, highly aromatic spice derived from the dried, unopened flower buds of the evergreen tree *Syzygium aromaticum*, native to the Maluku Islands of eastern Indonesia. Valued across Eurasia since antiquity as a culinary seasoning, perfume, and topical dental anesthetic, cloves formed one of the most lucrative prizes of global commerce. Monopolization of the clove trade stood at the center of violent colonial conflicts during the *[[age-of-exploration]]* and the rise of European mercantilist empires.

## you gotta know

- Harvested while the flower buds are still immature and green, which turn dark reddish-brown upon drying and resemble small round-headed nails—a resemblance reflected in the Latin root *clavus*.
- Contains a remarkably high concentration of the phenylpropanoid compound eugenol, which accounts for its distinctive sharp aroma, numbing properties, and antiseptic qualities.
- Originally endemic exclusively to a tiny chain of volcanic islands in the Maluku archipelago (known historically as the Spice Islands), including Ternate and Tidore.
- Used in ancient imperial China during the Han dynasty, where court officials were required to hold cloves in their mouths to sweeten their breath before addressing the emperor.
- Served as a central objective for Ferdinand Magellan's Spanish expedition, whose surviving vessel, the *Victoria*, returned to *[[spain]]* in 1522 laden with cloves to yield an immense financial profit.
- Became the subject of a brutal commercial monopoly maintained by the Dutch East India Company (VOC), which destroyed native trees outside designated islands and massacred local populations to control global output.
- Smuggled out of the East Indies in the 18th century by French agronomist Pierre Poivre, enabling successful commercial cultivation across Zanzibar and Madagascar.
