## summary

**Quebec Sign Language** (*Langue des signes québécoise*, commonly abbreviated as *LSQ*) is the primary visual-gestural language utilized by Deaf communities in Francophone regions of Canada, especially across the province of *Quebec*. Originating in mid-nineteenth-century residential schools founded by Catholic religious orders, it evolved into an autonomous natural language with its own intricate morphology and syntax. Distinct from both spoken French and neighboring sign languages, it remains an indispensable symbol of cultural heritage, linguistic autonomy, and Deaf empowerment.

## you gotta know

- Established through the pedagogical initiatives of Roman Catholic religious congregations in the 1850s, principally the *Clerics of Saint Viator* for deaf boys and the *Sisters of Providence* for deaf girls in *Montreal*.
- Descends historically from the French signing lineage introduced by educators trained in *French Sign Language* (*LSF*), while incorporating significant structural borrowings from *American Sign Language* (*ASL*) due to close geographic contact.
- Operates with an independent visual-spatial grammar entirely distinct from the grammar of [[quebec-french]], utilizing three-dimensional signing space, classifier constructions, and vital non-manual markers produced by facial expressions and torso postures.
- Employs a one-handed manual alphabet derived from the European continental tradition to fingerspell specialized loanwords, proper names, and terms lacking a dedicated lexical sign.
- Endured severe institutional suppression following the 1880 *Milan Congress*, an international conference that enforced oralist instruction and banned manual signing in classrooms for nearly a century.
- Achieved milestone legal recognition in 2022 under provincial language reforms (*Bill 96*), formally recognizing its status as an official visual language of Quebec's Deaf population.
- Maintains vibrant signer communities beyond provincial borders, including Francophone Deaf associations and educational enclaves in parts of *Ontario* and *New Brunswick*.
