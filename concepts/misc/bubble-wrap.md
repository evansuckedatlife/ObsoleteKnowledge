## summary

**Bubble Wrap** is a pliable, transparent plastic packaging material characterized by regularly spaced, air-filled hemispheres designed to cushion fragile goods from shock and vibration during transit. Invented in 1957 by engineers *Alfred Fielding* and *Marc Chavannes* in Hawthorne, New Jersey, the material was initially conceived as a tactile, textured wallpaper and later marketed as greenhouse insulation before finding its transformative role in industrial shipping. Today a registered trademark of the *Sealed Air Corporation*, the material has transcended commercial logistics to become an international pop-cultural phenomenon renowned for the sensory satisfaction of popping its individual bubbles.

## you gotta know

- Invented accidentally in 1957 by *Alfred Fielding* and Swiss inventor *Marc Chavannes*, who sealed two plastic shower curtains together with trapped air pockets.
- Pitched originally as modern, easy-to-clean textured wallpaper, an avant-garde design venture that proved an absolute commercial failure.
- Marketed subsequently as insulating greenhouse film before its developers recognized its unmatched shock-absorbing qualities for delicate industrial equipment.
- Secured its legendary commercial breakthrough in 1960 when *IBM* adopted the protective film to ship their sensitive *1401* mainframe computers safely nationwide.
- Manufactured and guarded under trademark by the *Sealed Air Corporation*, co-founded by Fielding and Chavannes following their design patent in 1960.
- Celebrated culturally for the compulsive stress-relieving pleasure derived from bursting the pressurized bubbles, prompting stress-relief consumer novelties.
- Evolved into newer commercial varieties such as un-poppable connected-bubble sheets (*iBubble Wrap*), designed to ship uninflated and conserve warehouse space.
