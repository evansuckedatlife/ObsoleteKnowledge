## summary

**Uno** is an American shed-type card game played with a custom-printed deck, developed to simplify and standardize the varying rules of the traditional game *Crazy Eights*. Invented in 1971 by Ohio barber *Merle Robbins* and later marketed globally by *International Games* and *Mattel*, it has become one of the best-selling branded card games in history. In quiz bowl, it frequently appears in trivia concerning its barbershop origin, card distribution mechanics, the famous penalty for failing to declare one's final card, and official clarifications surrounding penalty card stacking.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1971 by *Merle Robbins*, a barbershop owner in Reading, Ohio, who designed the prototype deck on his kitchen table to resolve family disputes over the household rules of *Crazy Eights*.
- Funded initially through an eight-thousand-dollar family loan to manufacture five thousand decks sold out of Robbins's barbershop, before he sold the global rights in 1972 to funeral home director *Robert Tezak* for fifty thousand dollars plus royalties.
- Features a standard 108-card deck consisting of four primary color suits—red, yellow, green, and blue—with ranks zero through nine, alongside *Skip*, *Reverse*, and *Draw Two* action cards, and eight uncolored *Wild* cards.
- Obligates a player with only one card remaining in hand to audibly proclaim **Uno** before another participant can call attention to it, under penalty of drawing two cards from the central deck.
- Scores points at the conclusion of each round based on the unplayed cards remaining in opponents' hands, awarding face value for numerical cards, twenty points for action cards, and fifty points for *Wild* cards, toward a standard target of five hundred points.
- Sparked widespread viral controversy in 2019 when *Mattel* publicly verified via social media that official tournament rules strictly forbid "stacking" *Draw Two* or *Wild Draw Four* cards to pass penalties to subsequent players.
- Incorporates a formal challenge mechanism governing *Wild Draw Four* cards, permitting an affected opponent to challenge whether the player possessed any cards matching the active color in hand, penalizing an illegal player with four cards or an incorrect challenger with six.
- Produced numerous commercial offshoots and licensed editions, including the motorized card-launching variant *Uno Attack* and the 2018 numerical sequel game *Dos*.
