## summary

**Dub** is an electronic music genre and studio recording methodology that grew out of reggae in late-1960s Jamaica. Pioneered by innovative audio engineers who treated the mixing console as a musical instrument, it involves stripping existing multitrack recordings down to their rhythm section and manipulating them with echo, reverb, and tape delays. The genre fundamentally reshaped modern popular music production, serving as a primary precursor to remix culture, hip-hop, ambient music, and modern electronic dance styles across the globe.

## you gotta know

- Originated in Kingston, Jamaica, during the late 1960s when audio engineers began pressing instrumental versions of popular rocksteady and reggae singles onto acetate dubplates for sound systems.
- Emphasizes the *riddim*—specifically stripping away melodic vocal lines to isolate a heavily amplified drum and bass framework often termed the drum-and-bass core.
- Elevated the studio engineer to the status of primary artist and auteur, utilizing the mixing board to creatively drop instruments in and out of the arrangement.
- Relies on studio manipulation hardware, particularly analog space echo units, spring reverbs, sweeping phase shifters, filter sweeps, and dramatic tape delay feedback loops.
- *Osbourne Ruddock*, professionally known as *King Tubby*, is widely recognized as the preeminent pioneer of the sound alongside *Lee "Scratch" Perry* and *Errol Thompson*.
- *Lee "Scratch" Perry* established the iconic *Black Ark* studio in Kingston, producing landmark records such as *Super Ape* that defined dub's mystical, textured sonic architecture.
- Migrated heavily to the [[united-kingdom]] via Caribbean immigration, inspiring British sound systems, dub poetry, and genres like dubstep, drum and bass, and trip-hop through figures like *Adrian Sherwood* and *Mad Professor*.

## connections

- [[african-diaspora]] — cultural and geographic dispersion of Afro-Caribbean communities whose migration patterns carried sound system culture to global metropolitan centers.
- [[united-kingdom]] — primary international center for sound system evolution, where immigrant communities adapted Jamaican dub into UK roots, post-punk, and UK garage.
- [[british-empire]] — colonial administration of Jamaica whose historical labor and cultural migrations shaped the post-colonial musical exchanges between London and Kingston.
- [[colonialism]] — historical system of exploitation in the Caribbean that fueled Rastafari resistance and political themes prominently voiced in dub production.
- [[modernism]] — avant-garde artistic and technological movement whose deconstructionist aesthetic mirrors dub's fragmentation of pre-recorded audio sources.
