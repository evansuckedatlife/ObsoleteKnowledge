## summary

**KFC**, originally known as **Kentucky Fried Chicken**, is an American multinational fast-food restaurant chain specializing in pressure-fried, seasoned chicken pieces. Founded during the Great Depression by Harland Sanders, it pioneered the concept of restaurant franchising and grew into one of the largest global restaurant conglomerates. It remains a hallmark of modern industrial food distribution, American cultural export, and distinctive corporate branding across dozens of international markets.

## you gotta know

- Founded by Harland Sanders in Corbin, Kentucky, during the 1930s, where he perfected his signature seasoning blend known as the secret recipe of 11 herbs and spices.
- Revolutionized commercial kitchen frying by adopting early commercial pressure fryers in 1939, substantially shortening cooking times while locking in moisture compared to traditional skillet frying.
- Introduced the iconic red-and-white paper bucket packaging in 1957 through franchisee Pete Harman in Salt Lake City, establishing a standard family-sized catering format widely copied throughout the fast-food industry.
- Sanders sold the growing enterprise to investors John Y. Brown Jr. and Jack C. Massey in 1964, though he retained trademark control over his likeness and served as the brand's ubiquitous goodwill ambassador dressed in a white linen suit and string tie.
- Achieved extraordinary cultural integration in *Japan* [[japan]] following the 1974 "Kentucky for Christmas" marketing campaign, making fried chicken buckets the standard national holiday meal for millions of Japanese households on Christmas Eve.
- Became one of the earliest Western fast-food franchises to breach international markets across the *Cold War* [[cold-war]] era, famously opening a massive multi-story branch overlooking Tiananmen Square in Beijing in 1987.
- Officially shortened its brand name to the initialism *KFC* in 1991 to de-emphasize the unhealthy dietary connotations of the word "fried" and accommodate expanded non-chicken menu items.
- Sanders notoriously feuded with corporate management during the 1970s, publicly disparaging the company's reformulated gravy as tasting like "wallpaper paste" and suing parent company Heublein over quality standards.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — home country where Sanders developed the core franchise model and recipes during the twentieth century.
- [[japan]] — prominent foreign market where the brand became permanently associated with secular Christmas celebrations.
- [[kimchi]] — fermented Korean staple frequently served alongside fried chicken across East Asia in modern fusion dining.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical era during which American fast-food corporations expanded internationally as commercial emblems of capitalism.
- [[united-kingdom]] — first European nation to establish franchise locations, opening its initial shop in Preston in 1965.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — technological transformation that enabled industrialized poultry processing, mechanical refrigeration, and automated pressure cooking.
- [[modernism]] — cultural and design movement that influenced mid-century architectural roadside franchise prototypes.
