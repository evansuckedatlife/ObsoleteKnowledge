## summary

*Lord Byron's* **Don Juan** is an expansive, satirical epic poem in *ottava rima* published in sixteen cantos between 1819 and 1824, left unfinished at the author's death. Reversing the legendary Spanish libertine myth, *Byron* casts his hero not as a ruthless seducer, but as an innocent, charming youth who is repeatedly seduced by formidable women across continental Europe and the Ottoman Empire. Celebrated as *Byron's* definitive masterpiece, the work blends blistering social commentary, biting digressions, and romantic irony, making it a cornerstone of English [[romanticism]] and a frequent subject in literary quiz-bowl competitions.

## you gotta know

- Written entirely in *ottava rima* stanzas rhyming *abababcc*, a verse form borrowed from Italian mock-heroic poetry that enabled rapid shifts from lyric pathos to witty, deflating couplets.
- Subverts the traditional Spanish legend dramatized by *Tirso de Molina* and *Mozart*, portraying the young *Juan* as a passive, well-intentioned wanderer whom women actively pursue.
- Begins in Seville with *Donna Inez*, modeled closely on *Byron's* estranged wife *Annabella Milbanke*, whose pedantic moralizing drives the young hero into an illicit affair with *Donna Julia*.
- Follows *Juan's* shipwreck in the Aegean, where he falls into an idyllic romance with the Greek pirate's daughter *Haidée*, a sequence renowned for the lyrical interpolation "The Isles of Greece."
- Depicts *Juan's* sale into slavery in Constantinople, where he is disguised as a woman named *Juche* inside the harem of Sultana *Gulbeyaz* before escaping to the Russian army.
- Satirizes militarism and imperial conquest through *Juan's* valor under *Alexander Suvorov* at the brutal Siege of Ismail, leading to his role as a favorite courtier to *Catherine the Great* in Saint Petersburg.
- Concludes in Cantos XI through XVII with *Juan's* diplomatic mission to Regency [[england]], where *Byron* turns his razor-sharp satirical lens on high-society hypocrisies, matchmaking, and the mysterious "Black Friar" ghost at Norman Abbey.
- Dedicated with scathing contempt to Poet Laureate *Robert Southey*, attacking the "Lake Poets"—including *William Wordsworth* and *Samuel Taylor Coleridge*—as political apostates and pompous hypocrites.

## connections

- [[percy-bysshe-shelley]] — close friend and fellow radical poet who lived alongside *Byron* in [[italy]] while *Byron* was composing the early cantos, offering vital critical feedback.
- [[mary-shelley]] — transcribed several cantos of the poem from *Byron's* manuscripts while residing in the Pisan circle.
- [[england]] — the target of *Byron's* devastating social satire in the later cantos, depicting the moral duplicity of London's aristocratic drawing rooms.
- [[spain]] — the homeland of the titular protagonist and the setting of the opening scandals with *Donna Inez* and *Donna Julia*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural cradle celebrated in the famous lyric "The Isles of Greece," where *Byron* eventually died fighting during the War of Independence.
- [[design-principles]] — reflected in the meticulous architectural metrics of *Byron's* ottava rima stanzas, balance of comic antitheses, and formal stanzaic symmetry.
- [[realism]] — anticipating later nineteenth-century prose fiction through cynical observation, historical verisimilitude, and rejection of sentimental romantic idealism.
