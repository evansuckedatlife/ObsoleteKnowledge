## summary

A **skewer** is a fundamental tactical motif in chess where an attacking long-range piece threatens two enemy pieces positioned along a shared geometric line, with the more valuable piece placed in front. When the foremost piece is compelled to move to escape capture, it exposes the less valuable or undefended piece behind it to immediate seizure. Frequently characterized as the inverse of a pin, this tactic serves as an essential endgame and middlegame instrument for forcing material gain.

## you gotta know

- Distinguished fundamentally from a pin because the higher-value piece stands in the line of direct fire, forcing its retreat and sacrificing the subordinate piece behind it.
- Executed exclusively by long-range line pieces—queens, rooks, and bishops—which leverage ranks, files, or diagonals to traverse open space across the board.
- Absolute skewers occur when the attacked front piece is the king, creating an inescapable check that legally mandates the king to step aside and concede the target behind.
- Relative skewers involve a non-royal front piece, such as a queen shielding an undefended rook or minor piece, where moving the queen remains legally optional but practically necessary.
- Theoretical rook endgames, notably practical applications of the *Lucena position* and *Philidor defense*, frequently hinge on executing or preventing skewers along open files.
- The motif shares conceptual overlap with the broader tactical category known as an *X-ray attack*, in which a piece's geometric influence penetrates an enemy shield to strike distal targets.
- Soviet school training manuals during the [[cold-war]] emphasized tactical calculation of skewers to exploit weaknesses in the games of grandmasters across the [[soviet-union]] and [[united-states]].

## connections

- [[geometry]] — provides the mathematical principles of collinearity, angles, and linear axes that govern rook files, bishop diagonals, and rank-based threats.
- [[cold-war]] — historical period that catalyzed intense international chess rivalry and competitive development of master-level tactical repertoires.
- [[soviet-union]] — state that developed a dominant state-sponsored chess academy systematically drilling tactical motifs like skewers and pins.
- [[united-states]] — nation represented by individual grandmasters like *Bobby Fischer*, whose precise tactical vision famously dismantled opposing defensive structures.
- [[france]] — historical epicenter of early European chess theory, immortalized by master *François-André Danican Philidor* at the *Café de la Régence*.
- [[spain]] — country that published early Renaissance chess treatises, including the foundational tactical manuals composed by *Ruy López de Segura*.
