## summary

**Parmesan**, strictly known under its European protected designation of origin as *Parmigiano Reggiano*, is an unpasteurized, hard, granular cow's milk cheese originating in the Emilia-Romagna and Lombardy regions of northern Italy. Renowned for its complex, savory umami depth, crystalline texture, and extended aging potential, the cheese has been produced using virtually identical artisan methods since the medieval era. It represents one of the world's most famous culinary staples, celebrated both as a standalone table cheese and as a quintessential grating cheese across Italian gastronomy.

## you gotta know

- Originated in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries through the agricultural innovations of Benedictine and Cistercian monks draining the Po River plains.
- Protected under strict European Union Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) laws, limiting authentic production to the Italian provinces of Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, and sections of Mantua and Bologna.
- Produced exclusively from the raw milk of cows fed on locally sourced forage, using natural whey cultures, calf rennet, and Mediterranean sea salt with no additives.
- Requires aging for a mandatory minimum of 12 months, with premium varieties categorized as *vecchio* (18–24 months), *stravecchio* (24–36 months), and *stravecchione* (over 36 months).
- Features tiny crunchy white crystals throughout its paste, which are harmless calcified crystals of the amino acid tyrosine formed as milk proteins break down during aging.
- Mentioned famously in fourteenth-century Italian literature, including *Giovanni Boccaccio's* *Decameron*, which describes a mythical mountain of grated Parmesan where cooks roll macaroni down into capon broth.
- Subject to stringent acoustic quality testing by master testers (*battitori*), who tap the wheels with a small silver-plated hammer to detect internal cracks or air pockets.
