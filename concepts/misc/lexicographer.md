## summary

A **lexicographer** is a scholar, author, or linguistic editor who compiles, writes, and maintains dictionaries, recording the semantic nuances, etymologies, and spellings of words. The scholarly pursuit of **lexicography** expanded dramatically during the European [[enlightenment]], evolving from prescriptive efforts to standardize vernacular tongues into the rigorous, descriptive study of living language. Celebrated in literary lore and humanities curricula, the profession is remembered both for monumental solitary undertakings and for massive crowdsourced lexical archives that document human culture.

## you gotta know

- Defined famously by *Samuel Johnson* in his 1755 dictionary as "a writer of dictionaries; a harmless drudge that busies himself in tracing the original, and detailing the signification of words."
- Undertaken single-handedly by *Samuel Johnson* over nearly nine years to produce *A Dictionary of the English Language*, celebrated for witty definitions such as oats being food for horses in [[england]] but people in Scotland.
- Spearheaded in early America by *Noah Webster*, whose 1828 *An American Dictionary of the English Language* introduced deliberate orthographic reforms like dropping the "u" in *color* and transposing "re" to "er" in *center*.
- Revolutionized through the crowdsourced editorial effort of the *Oxford English Dictionary*, initiated under the philologist *James Murray*, who worked out of a corrugated-iron outbuilding known as the Scriptorium.
- Involved unusual contributors to the *Oxford English Dictionary*, most famously *Dr. William Chester Minor*, an American surgeon incarcerated for murder at Broadmoor Criminal Lunatic Asylum who submitted thousands of quotation slips.
- Distinguishes between *prescriptivism*, which seeks to establish rules for correct language usage, and modern *descriptivism*, which documents how language is actually employed by native speakers.
- Traces early English roots to *Robert Cawdrey*'s 1604 work *A Table Alphabeticall*, widely considered the first monolingual English dictionary, compiled to explain difficult "hard usual English words."
- Employs corpus linguistics in contemporary practice, using automated supercomputing algorithms to scan petabytes of digital texts to identify neologisms and shifts in lexical frequency.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — parent academic discipline that studies the structural, historical, and semantic properties of language.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual movement whose passion for categorization and reason spurred monumental dictionaries.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — dramatist whose vocabulary provided countless citation slips for early historical dictionaries.
- [[england]] — birthplace of landmark vernacular lexicographical works and the foundational traditions of English scholarship.
- [[united-kingdom]] — kingdom where nineteenth-century philological societies created the *Oxford English Dictionary*.
- [[united-states]] — nation where spelling reforms and national identity were codified by lexicographical pioneers.
- [[renaissance]] — era of humanism and classical scholarship that revived interest in comprehensive vernacular vocabularies.
- [[linux-kernel]] — digital project whose structural terminology and collaborative mailing lists mimic open lexical slip submissions.
