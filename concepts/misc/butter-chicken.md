## summary

**Butter Chicken**, known natively as *murgh makhani*, is an internationally renowned curry originating in Delhi, India during the mid-twentieth century. Created by restaurant cooks seeking to repurpose leftover skewered tandoori chicken, the dish pairs tender poultry with a velvety, mildly spiced gravy composed of tomatoes, butter, cream, and aromatic spices. Its global popularity mirrors the post-independence Indian culinary diaspora, making it a ubiquitous centerpiece in South Asian dining traditions worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Invented in the 1950s at the famous *Moti Mahal* restaurant in the Daryaganj neighborhood of Old Delhi by Punjabi culinary entrepreneurs *Kundan Lal Gujral*, *Kundan Lal Jaggi*, and *Thakur Dass*.
- Conceived as an economical solution to prevent unsold, cooked tandoori chicken from drying out, tossing the roasted meat into a simmering tomato-and-butter gravy.
- Characterized by a rich, mildly sweet, and tangy sauce (*makhani gravy*) seasoned with *kashmiri mirch*, dried fenugreek leaves (*kasuri methi*), garam masala, ginger, garlic, and heavy dairy cream.
- Historically linked to the widespread migration and culinary adaptation that occurred in the wake of the 1947 Partition of India, as refugees from Peshawar relocated to Delhi and popularized tandoor-based cooking.
- Spawned related global comfort-food offshoots, particularly influencing the British creation of *chicken tikka masala* in curry houses across England and Scotland.
- Sparked high-profile twentieth-first-century intellectual property litigation in the Delhi High Court between the descendant families of *Moti Mahal* and *Daryaganj* restaurant chains over the title of inventor.
