## summary

**Greek architecture**, encompassing the built legacy of the Hellenic world from the Archaic through the Hellenistic periods, established the canonical vocabulary of Western classical building. Characterized by post-and-lintel (*trabeated*) construction, mathematical proportion, and harmonized structural logic, its forms were codified around distinct architectural orders. Flourishing prominently in civic and religious monuments across [[ancient-greece]], southern [[italy]], and Asia Minor, its temples, open-air theaters, and colonnaded agoras provided timeless archetypes that directly shaped Roman monumentality and subsequent neoclassical revivals.

## you gotta know

- Originated the three fundamental classical orders of architecture: the sturdy, unadorned **Doric order**; the graceful, scroll-capitaled **Ionic order**; and the elaborate, acanthus-leaf **Corinthian order**.
- Relied upon the trabeated system of vertical columns (*peristyle*) supporting a horizontal entablature composed of the architrave, frieze, and projecting cornice beneath a triangular pediment.
- Reached its artistic and technical zenith in the High Classical monuments of the Athenian Acropolis, spearheaded by the statesman *Pericles* and designed by master architects *Ictinus* and *Callicrates* alongside the sculptor *Phidias*.
- Incorporated deliberate optical refinements (*entasis*) in buildings like the *Parthenon*, curving stylobate platforms upward and angling columns inward to compensate for visual illusions of sagging and distortion.
- Utilized the Doric frieze scheme of alternating grooved tablets (*triglyphs*) and sculpted narrative relief panels (*metopes*), which evolved from primitive timber joinery details into marble masterpieces.
- Developed the monumental public theater form carved directly into natural hillsides, as exemplified by the theater at *Epidaurus*, renowned for its semicircular *orchestra* and extraordinary acoustics.
- Advanced urban town planning under *Hippodamus of Miletus*, introducing rectilinear grid street layouts and surrounding public civic squares with extended sheltered colonnades known as *stoas*.

## connections

- [[architecture]] — broader discipline of building design and spatial composition for which Hellenic orders established enduring structural rules.
- [[ancient-greece]] — historical civilization whose religious rituals, civic democracy, and mythology dictated temple form and urban planning.
- [[geometry]] — mathematical science of proportions, ratios, and symmetries used by Hellenic builders to achieve harmonious optical balance.
- [[rome]] — imperial successor culture that adopted, modified, and combined the Greek orders into arched and vaulted monumental complexes.
- [[roman-empire]] — expansive empire through which Hellenistic architectural templates spread throughout the Mediterranean basin and Western Europe.
- [[renaissance]] — European intellectual and cultural rebirth that rediscovered Hellenic and Vitruvian architectural proportions.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical and archaeological era that sparked the Neoclassical movement through direct rediscovery of Greek ruins.
