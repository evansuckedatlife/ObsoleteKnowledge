## summary

**Haida** (autonym *X̱aayda* or *X̱aadas*) refers to the Indigenous nation and culture native to the Pacific Northwest archipelago of *Haida Gwaii* in British Columbia, Canada, as well as the southern portion of Prince of Wales Island in Alaska. Renowned for their masterful monumental totem poles, oceanic redcedar dugout war canoes, and matrilineal clan structure, they historically dominated regional coastal commerce and warfare. In anthropology, art history, and Indigenous sovereignty movements, they represent a preeminent Northwest Coast society that preserved its distinct artistic lineage and territorial stewardship through fierce resistance against European imperial expansion.

## you gotta know

- Inhabits ancestral homelands centered on the mist-shrouded archipelago of *Haida Gwaii* off the coast of British Columbia, alongside the *Kaigani Haida* communities located in southern Alaska across the border with the [[united-states]].
- Famed across global ethnography and art history for monumental architecture and woodcarving, specifically heraldic cedar totem poles (*gyáa'aang*) displaying crest figures such as the Raven (*Yáahl*) and the Eagle (*Ts'uts'x*).
- Structured socially around a strict matrilineal moiety system dividing the entire population into Raven and Eagle clans, governing property ownership, chieftainship lineages, ceremonial prerogatives, and exogamous marriages.
- Practised the ceremonial *potlatch* (*waahlgahl*), an elaborate gathering involving competitive gift-giving, theatrical performances, and public validations of hereditary titles and ancestral crests that was criminally banned by the Canadian government under the Indian Act from 1884 to 1951.
- Developed exceptional maritime seamanship and seafaring warfare, constructing massive ocean-going canoes carved from single trunks of western redcedar that could exceed sixty feet in length and transport dozens of warriors and trade goods.
- Maintained a rich coastal subsistence economy reliant on eulachon candlefish oil, halibut fishing, sea otter pelts, and the culinary practice of open-fire [[grilling]] and smoke-curing salmon on aromatic cedar stakes over seaside fire pits.
- Suffered a catastrophic demographic collapse during the nineteenth century due to virgin-soil epidemics of smallpox and measles introduced through contact with the [[british-empire]] and maritime fur traders, reducing their population from over ten thousand to fewer than six hundred by 1900.
- Speaks a linguistic isolate known as the *Haida language* (*X̱aat Kíl* or *X̱aadas Kíl*), which was once hypothesised to belong to the Na-Dené phylum alongside Dene languages but is now widely classified by scholars in [[linguistics]] as an unproven isolate.

## connections

- [[grilling]] — culinary technique central to traditional Haida foodways, particularly roasting fresh sockeye and king salmon over open cedar fires.
- [[colonialism]] — historical force of European settlement, disease, resource extraction, and assimilationist residential schools that devastated coastal Indigenous populations.
- [[linguistics]] — discipline that analyzes the disputed genealogy of the endangered *Haida language*, preserving it as an isolated linguistic treasury.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial power whose naval explorers, Hudson's Bay Company traders, and colonial officials claimed dominion over the Pacific Northwest.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — historical era when European navigators like *Juan Pérez* and *George Dixon* first made contact with Haida island settlements in the late eighteenth century.
- [[united-states]] — nation encompassing southern Alaska, home to the northern diaspora known as the *Kaigani Haida* on Prince of Wales Island.
