## summary

The **Caesar cocktail**, ubiquitously known across Canada simply as a **Caesar** or **Bloody Caesar**, is a savory mixed drink made with vodka, a seasoned tomato and clam broth blend (*clamato*), hot pepper sauce, and Worcestershire sauce. Formulated in Calgary, Alberta, in 1969 to commemorate the opening of an upscale Italian dining establishment, the cocktail is traditionally served in a glass rimmed with celery salt and garnished with a crisp stalk of celery and a lime wedge. As an entrenched cultural institution, it serves as Canada's official national cocktail, consuming hundreds of millions of servings annually despite enjoying remarkably little popularity outside Canadian borders.

## you gotta know

- Created in 1969 by food and beverage manager *Walter Chell* at the Calgary Inn (now operating as the Westin Calgary) to celebrate the debut of Marco's Italian Restaurant.
- Chell drew inspiration from his native Italian background and the culinary profile of *spaghetti alle vongole* (spaghetti with clams and tomato), painstakingly hand-crushing fresh clams to blend with tomato puree during initial testing.
- Distinct from the American *Bloody Mary* primarily due to its thinner, more savory, and umami-rich base of clam-infused tomato juice rather than viscous, pure tomato juice.
- The commercial launch of the cocktail coincided fortuitously with the 1969 release of mass-produced canned *Mott's Clamato* juice, which rapidly forged a symbiotic commercial relationship with Canadian taverns and hospitality venues.
- The drink earned its famous moniker when an English patron at the Calgary Inn sampled Chell's pungent concoction and remarked approvingly that it was a "bloody good Caesar."
- In 2009, Member of Parliament *Colin Mayes* introduced a petition in the Canadian House of Commons to formally designate the drink as Canada's official national cocktail, leading to the creation of National Caesar Day celebrated before Victoria Day.
- Modern Canadian pub and brunch culture has transformed the cocktail's garnish into competitive high-concept culinary theatre, expanding beyond simple celery to skewer bacon, hamburgers, fried chicken, onion rings, oysters, or lobster tails atop the glass.
- Often featured in pub trivia and national celebrations as a quintessential pairing with iconic Canadian comfort sweets such as [[butter-tarts]].
