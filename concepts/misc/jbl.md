## summary

**JBL**, originally established as **James B. Lansing Sound**, is an influential American audio electronics enterprise founded in 1946 by pioneering audio engineer *James Bullough Lansing*. Globally renowned for developing groundbreaking acoustic transducers, rugged concert public-address horns, and standard-setting reference monitors, the company profoundly shaped the sound of twentieth-century music production and public performance. From high-fidelity domestic living rooms and Hollywood film dubbing stages to the mammoth amplification rigs of major open-air festivals, its engineering innovations created enduring international benchmarks for acoustic fidelity, frequency separation, and industrial durability.

## you gotta know

- Founded in Los Angeles by audio engineer *James Bullough Lansing* shortly after he departed Altec Lansing, which he had helped create following the acquisition of his earlier Lansing Manufacturing Company.
- Built the landmark D130 fifteen-inch loudspeaker transducer, which incorporated a four-inch flat-wire voice coil and an Alnico V magnet, becoming the speaker of choice for *Leo Fender* in early electric guitar amplifiers.
- Developed the 4310 and 4311 professional studio monitors in the late 1960s, which became ubiquitous throughout the recording industry and cemented the company's dominant reputation among commercial sound engineers.
- Created the legendary L100 consumer bookshelf loudspeaker with its distinctive sculpted *Quadrex* foam grille, which went on to become the best-selling residential loudspeaker of the 1970s.
- Provided the massive sound reinforcement loudspeakers and transducer arrays that famously powered the 1969 Woodstock Music and Art Fair in upstate *New York*.
- Introduced the *Paragon* integrated stereophonic loudspeaker system, designed by *Arnold Wolf*, which housed mid-century curved wooden acoustics that remain one of the most celebrated achievements in industrial audio cabinetry.
- Maintained enduring industry influence after acquisition by *Sidney Harman* and Harman International, ultimately evolving into a major modern producer of professional line arrays and portable consumer Bluetooth speakers.

## connections

- [[perception]] — provides the psychoacoustic auditory framework governing how human listeners evaluate frequency response, dynamic range, and spatial fidelity in high-end audio engineering.
- [[five-senses]] — encompasses human hearing, the sensory foundation that high-performance transducers and electroacoustic components are designed to stimulate.
- [[united-states]] — served as the birthplace and commercial incubator for postwar American audio pioneers, manufacturing giants, and rock-and-roll amplification cultures.
- [[cold-war]] — spanned the postwar consumer boom and technological expansion in which American domestic high-fidelity audio equipment proliferated rapidly.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — laid the mass-manufacturing principles, precision coil-winding techniques, and magnetic metallurgy essential for twentieth-century loudspeaker mass production.
- [[modernism]] — influenced the sleek functionalist aesthetics and sculptural mid-century industrial design exemplified by the *Paragon* and *Quadrex* foam grilles.
