## summary

**Space Shuttle Discovery** (designated **OV-103**) was a crewed orbital spacecraft operated by NASA and the operational workhorse of the American Space Shuttle program. First launched in August 1984, **Discovery** flew 39 successful missions over twenty-seven years, accumulating more spaceflights and days in orbit than any other reusable orbiter in history. Its storied career included deploying the *Hubble Space Telescope*, executing both post-disaster "Return to Flight" missions, and facilitating vital assembly flights to the *International Space Station*.

## you gotta know

- Carried the *Hubble Space Telescope* into low Earth orbit during mission *STS-31* in April 1990, followed by subsequent servicing mission *STS-82* in 1997.
- Designated NASA's dedicated "Return to Flight" orbiter after both catastrophic shuttle losses, restoring American human spaceflight on *STS-26* in 1988 following *Challenger* and on *STS-114* in 2005 following *Columbia*.
- Flew pioneering American astronaut *John Glenn* back into orbit on mission *STS-95* in October 1998, making the 77-year-old senator the oldest person to fly in space at that time.
- Conducted the historic *STS-63* mission in 1995 piloted by *Eileen Collins*, who became the first female shuttle pilot during a close rendezvous with the Russian space station *Mir*.
- Retired in March 2011 after completing mission *STS-133*, whereupon NASA preserved it for public display inside the *Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center* of the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum.
- Transported Soviet cosmonaut *Sergei Krikalev* on *STS-60* in 1994, marking the first joint flight of a Russian space traveler aboard an American spacecraft since the 1975 *Apollo-Soyuz* project.
- Hauled to Launch Complex 39 at the *Kennedy Space Center* atop massive crawler-transporters powered by dual locomotive engines fueled by heavy petroleum oil.
