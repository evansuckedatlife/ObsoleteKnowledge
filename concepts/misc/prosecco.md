## summary

**Prosecco** is an Italian sparkling white wine produced primarily in the Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia regions, made predominantly from the aromatic *Glera* grape variety. Renowned for its crisp, floral, and orchard-fruit profile, it is typically vinified using the *Charmat-Martinotti* method, where secondary fermentation occurs in pressurized stainless-steel autoclaves rather than inside individual bottles. In recent decades, it has grown into one of the most widely exported and consumed sparkling wines on earth, serving as the essential base for classic aperitifs like the *Bellini* and *Aperol Spritz*.

## you gotta know

- Governed by strict European geographical appellations, primarily designated as *DOC* (*Denominazione di Origine Controllata*) across nine provinces, with premier hillside vineyards recognized under *DOCG* status around *Conegliano Valdobbiadene* and *Asolo*.
- Made from at least eighty-five percent of the thin-skinned, high-acid white grape formerly called prosecco, which was officially renamed *Glera* in 2009 to prevent international producers outside northern [[italy]] from usurping the regional name.
- Produced via the *Charmat* or *Martinotti* tank method, an engineering innovation that preserves fresh primary fruit aromatics of green apple, pear, honeysuckle, and melon at lower production costs than the traditional champagne method of [[france]].
- Categorised by residual sugar levels ranging from the driest *Extra Brut* and *Brut* to *Extra Dry* (the historical classic style) and *Dry*, which actually indicates a noticeably sweet wine.
- Serves as the indispensable wine component in famous cocktails such as the *Bellini*, invented by *Giuseppe Cipriani* at *Harry's Bar* in Venice with white peach purée, and the ubiquitous *Spritz Veneziano*.
- Gained immense market dominance through mass retail channels and supermarket private-label lines such as [[presidents-choice]], outpacing champagne sales by volume globally in the early twenty-first century.
- Traces its ancestral heritage back to antiquity in the village of *Prosecco* near Trieste, where Roman writers celebrated a local vintage known as *Pucinum* praised by *Pliny the Elder*.

## connections

- [[italy]] — Mediterranean nation whose northeastern regions of Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia hold exclusive legal rights to produce the wine.
- [[rome]] — capital city whose ancient imperial trade networks spread viticulture throughout the northern Adriatic coastline.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical civilization whose naturalists and agronomists, such as *Pliny the Elder*, documented the ancestral vines of the Istrian and Venetian hills.
- [[france]] — European viticultural peer renowned for champagne, whose labor-intensive bottle-fermentation stands in contrast to the Italian autoclave process.
- [[renaissance]] — cultural epoch that revitalized Venetian agriculture, landscape painting, and civic dining traditions centered around northeastern Italian wines.
- [[presidents-choice]] — prominent retail brand whose upscale store-brand grocery offerings helped popularize affordable sparkling wines for international home consumers.
