## summary

The **Mountie uniform**, most famously embodied by the formal *Red Serge*, is the ceremonial dress attire of the *Royal Canadian Mounted Police* (RCMP). Developed in the late nineteenth century from British imperial cavalry patterns, the garment was chosen to signal British royal authority and distinguish early federal riders from blue-coated soldiers of the *[[united-states]]*. Today, the ensemble stands alongside symbols like [[nanaimo-bars]] as an internationally recognizable emblem of Canadian national identity and pageantry.

## you gotta know

- Features the distinctive scarlet tunic known worldwide as the *Red Serge*, tailored with high stand collars, brass buttons, and deep blue shoulder straps and sleeve cuffs.
- Headwear standard for ceremonial dress is the iconic wide-brimmed felt *stetson* campaign hat, complete with a distinctive flat Montana peak crease and narrow leather hatband.
- Lower attire consists of midnight blue riding breeches known as *jodhpurs*, featuring a thick yellow cavalry stripe running down the outside seam of each leg.
- Worn alongside high-gloss brown leather Strathcona boots that extend nearly to the knee, secured over the calves and fastened with nickel spurs.
- Paired with the traditional brown leather Sam Browne belt, diagonally slung across the chest to carry a formal sidearm holster and ceremonial ammunition pouches.
- Originating in 1873 with the creation of the *North-West Mounted Police*, scarlet wool coats were explicitly selected to evoke the prestige of the *[[british-empire]]* and reassure Indigenous nations familiar with red-jacketed treaties rather than blue cavalry coats.
- First officially codified with standard Stetson hats during the late Victorian and Edwardian eras, replacing traditional white pillbox caps and pith helmets following service in the Second Boer War.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — provided the scarlet-coated cavalry aesthetic and institutional tradition that directly shaped the tunic.
- [[united-kingdom]] — supplied early cloth materials and cavalry equipment designs for Canadian federal forces.
- [[colonialism]] — historical context in which the early frontier constabulary was mobilized across western territories.
- [[world-war-i]] — era during which cavalry units wearing the uniform saw service and cemented international recognition.
- [[nanaimo-bars]] — fellow cornerstone of Canadian civic iconography and regional folklore.
