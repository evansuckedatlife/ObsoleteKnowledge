## summary

**Limburger** is a notorious cow's milk cheese historically originating in the historical Duchy of Limburg and popularized across central Europe. It is universally famous for its pungent, intensely pungent aroma caused by surface smears of the bacterium *Brevibacterium linens*. Despite its overpowering, sulfuric scent, the cheese develops a creamy, mild, and distinctly savory interior favored in traditional rustic sandwiches.

## you gotta know

- Originated during the nineteenth century in the historic Duchy of Limburg, a region spanning modern-day Belgium, the Netherlands, and western *Germany*.
- Characterized by its wash-rind curing process, where wheels are periodically washed in saltwater brine to encourage bacterial growth.
- Smear-ripened predominantly by *Brevibacterium linens*, the exact species of bacterium partially responsible for human foot and body odor.
- Scientifically notable for attracting the malaria mosquito *Anopheles gambiae* due to shared chemical odorants with human skin, an observation that won an *Ig Nobel Prize* in 2006.
- Traditional American pairing, prominent in nineteenth-century tavern culture, features slices of onion, brown mustard, and rye bread alongside dark beer.
- Production in the United States became concentrated in Green County, Wisconsin, notably within the town of *Monroe*, the nation's sole remaining domestic producer.
- Enjoys European Union *Protected Designation of Origin* (PDO) status under the traditional appellation *Fromage de Herve*.
