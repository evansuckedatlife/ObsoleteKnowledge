## summary

**Fresh water** is naturally occurring water containing minimal concentrations of dissolved salts and other total dissolved solids. Constituting less than three percent of all water on Earth, the vast majority is locked within glaciers, ice sheets, and deep underground aquifers. It is an indispensable resource for terrestrial ecosystems, human physiology, agriculture, and industrial society, serving as a primary driver of historical settlement patterns and modern environmental geopolitics.

## you gotta know

- Defined chemically and limnologically as water with salinity below 1,000 parts per million, distinguishing it from brackish and saline water.
- Accounts for approximately 2.5 to 2.75 percent of the global water supply, with roughly two-thirds trapped in polar ice and glaciers.
- Replenished constantly across the globe by the hydrologic cycle through evaporation, atmospheric condensation, and precipitation.
- Held primarily in surface form within large lake systems, most notably *Lake Baikal* and the *Great Lakes* shared by the [[united-states]] and [[canada]].
- Stored abundantly underground in porous rock formations known as aquifers, such as the *Ogallala Aquifer* of North America.
- Threatened globally by industrial runoff, agricultural eutrophication, salinization, and over-extraction for irrigation.
- Managed and divided by major international legal accords such as the *Boundary Waters Treaty* of 1909.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — co-custodian of the Great Lakes basin and manager of major continental river systems.
- [[canada]] — contains a substantial fraction of global surface liquid reserves within its glacial lakes and rivers.
- [[geometry]] — utilized in hydrology, watershed surveying, fluid dynamics modeling, and canal engineering.
- [[astronomy]] — guides the search for habitable exoplanets and subsurface liquid reserves on icy extraterrestrial moons.
- [[enlightenment]] — era whose naturalists pioneered modern hydrological studies, quantitative chemistry, and classification of waters.
- [[cold-war]] — period during which large-scale irrigation and strategic water diversion projects reshaped continental environments.
