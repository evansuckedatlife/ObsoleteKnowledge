## summary

**Etiquette** refers to the conventional code of polite behavior governing social interactions, dining, diplomacy, and written correspondence. Originating from the formal protocols and instructional placards of early modern European royal courts, it evolved into an elaborate system of class signaling and civic conduct across the Western world. In cultural history and trivia competitions, it is celebrated through foundational manuals written by figures ranging from Renaissance courtiers to twentieth-century authorities like *Emily Post*.

## you gotta know

- Etymologically derived from the Old French term for a ticket or label, originating from court placards at the court of King *Louis XIV* of *[[france]]* at *Versailles* that instructed nobles where to walk and how to stand.
- *Baldassare Castiglione* established the Renaissance ideal of gentlemanly demeanor in *The Book of the Courtier* (1528), introducing *sprezzatura*, the art of effortless grace and nonchalance.
- *Desiderius Erasmus* penned one of the earliest bestselling handbooks for youth in 1530, *De Civilitate Morum Puerilium* (*On Good Manners for Boys*), establishing rules for posture, spitting, and table hygiene across Europe during the *[[renaissance]]*.
- *Emily Post* redefined twentieth-century social expectations in the *[[united-states]]* with her landmark 1922 compendium *Etiquette in Society, in Business, in Politics, and at Home*, popularizing democratic courtesy over aristocratic pretension.
- *Amy Vanderbilt* published the rival mid-century authority *Complete Book of Etiquette* (1952), advising post-war suburban households on formal entertaining, weddings, and modern social transitions.
- The *duelling code* or *code duello*, formalised in eighteenth-century Ireland and Britain, operated as an extreme subset of gentlemanly etiquette designed to regulate affairs of honour and combat.
- Victorian dining etiquette reached peak material complexity during the *[[industrial-revolution]]*, requiring distinct cutlery implements such as fish knives, asparagus tongs, and marrow spoons to enforce social gatekeeping.
- Modern electronic correspondence developed *netiquette* in the early 1980s, which established conventions such as avoiding full capitalization and limiting vulgar typographical marks like the *[[exclamation-point]]*.

## connections

- [[france]] — the origin of royal court placards at *Versailles* that gave the practice its name and formalization.
- [[enlightenment]] — the philosophical era that shifted manners from arbitrary feudal display toward universal civic sociability and reason.
- [[renaissance]] — the period when humanist educators like *Erasmus* codified dining hygiene and courtly posture.
- [[england]] — home to Victorian class rituals, afternoon tea traditions, and *Lord Chesterfield's* letters to his son on worldly polish.
- [[belle-epoque]] — the late nineteenth-century European zenith of rigid aristocratic social calling cards and seasonal salon protocol.
- [[united-states]] — the commercial market where *Emily Post* transformed manners into accessible guides for ordinary citizens.
- [[exclamation-point]] — the punctuation mark widely discouraged in classical epistolary guides and modern professional correspondence codes.
