## summary

The **feminine** is a foundational category denoting womanhood, female qualities, and in *[[linguistics]]*, the grammatical gender inflection assigned to female-referencing or morphologically categorized nouns in inflected languages. Across anthropology, aesthetics, and cultural theory, the concept encompasses socially constructed roles, aesthetic ideals, and expressive behaviors contrasted with or complementary to the masculine. In the history of literature, philosophy, and fashion, evolving definitions of the term have driven major debates concerning gender performance, linguistic classification, and political emancipation.

## you gotta know

- In descriptive and historical *[[linguistics]]*, refers to one of the major grammatical genders inherited from Proto-Indo-European, contrasting with masculine and neuter categories across Indo-European tongues.
- Grammatical gender does not inherently correlate with biological sex, as many inanimate objects, abstract nouns, and collective entities are assigned feminine morphological markers in Romance, Slavic, and Germanic languages.
- In French grammar, failing to match adjectives and articles to feminine nouns is a classic grammatical *[[faux-pas]]* that immediately reveals a non-native speaker.
- In nineteenth-century theater and fashion, boundary-pushing actresses like *Sarah Bernhardt* radically redefined feminine public presentation by wearing cross-gender costumes and popularizing headwear that became the *[[fedora]]*.
- Second-wave feminist philosophy, notably articulated by *Simone de Beauvoir* in *The Second Sex*, famously argued that one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman, dissecting societal enforcement of traditional roles.
- Psychoanalytic theories, notably developed by *Carl Jung*, posited the *anima* as the inner archetype of womanhood present within the collective unconscious of men.
- Sociological scholarship distinguishes essentialist biological accounts from performative gender identity, framing social expression as an iterative, culturally conditioned performance rather than an immutable essence.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — primary academic discipline examining grammatical gender classifications, morphosyntax, and gendered language dynamics.
- [[fedora]] — iconic accessory originally debuted by a celebrated actress in stage costume before transitioning from feminine fashion to masculine menswear.
- [[faux-pas]] — social and communicative stumble frequently exemplified by grammatical agreement mistakes or violations of gendered etiquette codes.
- [[ancient-greece]] — classical civilization whose grammatical theorists like *Protagoras* first systematically classified nouns into masculine, feminine, and neuter genders.
- [[roman-empire]] — society whose Latin grammatical structures and gendered legal statuses directly shaped modern Western European terminology.
- [[modernism]] — cultural and artistic movement that deconstructed nineteenth-century domestic norms and embraced the New Woman ideal.
- [[france]] — birthplace of groundbreaking feminist theorists and the literary salons that systematically contested traditional social roles.
