## summary

**Cyrillic** is an alphabetic writing system developed in the ninth century in the *First Bulgarian Empire* to write *Old Church Slavonic*, largely derived from the *Greek uncial script* with additional letters borrowed or adapted from *Glagolitic*. Named in honor of the Byzantine missionary Saint *Cyril*, who alongside his brother Saint *Methodius* first devised the Glagolitic precursor, Cyrillic became the primary sacred and literary script of Eastern Orthodox Slavic cultures. Today, it serves as the official script for over 250 million people across Eastern Europe, the Balkans, Central Asia, and northern Eurasia.

## you gotta know

- Developed in the late ninth and early tenth centuries at the *Preslav Literary School* under the patronage of Bulgarian rulers *Boris I* and *Simeon the Great*.
- Named after Saint *Cyril*, although modern scholarship attributes the direct creation of the script primarily to his disciples, such as Saint *Clement of Ohrid*, who streamlined Saint Cyril's earlier, more intricate *Glagolitic* alphabet.
- Base letterforms were adopted directly from the Byzantine *Greek uncial script*, supplemented with novel characters (such as *Ш*, *Ж*, and *Ч*) designed to accurately represent complex Slavic phonemes.
- Underwent massive orthographic modernization in 1708 under Russian Tsar *Peter the Great*, who introduced the secular *Civil Script* (*grazhdansky shrift*) to mimic Western European typography.
- Standardised across the *Soviet Union* during the twentieth century, where it was systematically adapted to write dozens of non-Slavic Caucasian, Turkic, Uralic, and Siberian Indigenous languages.
- Remains one of the official writing scripts of the *European Union* following the accession of Bulgaria in 2007, alongside the Latin and Greek alphabets.
