## summary

**Skirt steak** is a long, flat, ribbon-like cut of beef prized for its rich, beefy flavor and distinct, coarse muscle grain. Sourced from the diaphragm muscle of cattle in the plate and abdominal sections, it is subdivided into the more tender inside skirt and outside skirt cuts. It is widely celebrated across global culinary traditions, serving as the definitive meat for Mexican *fajitas* and *arrachera*, as well as classic Romanian Jewish steakhouse preparations.

## you gotta know

- Anatomically derived from the diaphragm of the steer, consisting of the transversus abdominis muscle (inside skirt) and the costal diaphragm (outside skirt).
- The outside skirt is thicker, more uniform, and prized for restaurant dining, but is frequently stripped of its protective membrane for commercial sale.
- Characterized by very long, thick muscle fibers running crosswise, requiring the cooked meat to be sliced strictly against the grain to ensure tenderness.
- High in connective tissue and rich in intramuscular fat, making it particularly well-suited to high-heat grilling, broiling, or searing after wet marinades.
- Serves as the traditional and canonical cut used for Mexican *fajitas*, developed by Tejano ranch workers in South *Texas* who were given discarded butchery cuts.
- Known in traditional Jewish-American butcher shops and New York delis as *Romanian tenderloin* or *roumanian steak*, traditionally rubbed with garlic and broiled.
- In French butchery, it corresponds roughly to the bistro cut known as *hampe*, famously served alongside french fries in *steak frites*.
