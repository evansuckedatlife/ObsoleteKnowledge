## summary

**Saskatoon Berries** are the sweet, edible purplish-blue pome fruits of the deciduous shrub *Amelanchier alnifolia*, native to western North America from Alaska through the Canadian prairies to the northern United States. Belonging to the rose family (*Rosaceae*), they closely resemble blueberries in external appearance but possess a distinct, sweet-nutty flavor reminiscent of apples and almonds due to the presence of soft, edible seeds. Long harvested as a vital staple food and medicinal resource by Indigenous peoples of the Great Plains, they famously formed a core ingredient of *pemmican*.

## you gotta know

- Takes its common name from the Cree word *misâskwatômina*, which also provided the name for the city of Saskatoon in Saskatchewan, Canada.
- Botanically classified not as true berries, but as small pomes, placing them in the same botanical family (*Rosaceae*) and subfamily as apples, pears, and hawthorns.
- Served as a cornerstone of traditional Indigenous diets across the Canadian Plains and northern forests, particularly among the Cree, Blackfoot, and Métis nations, who dried them for winter sustenance.
- Essential component of *pemmican*, a calorie-dense survival ration made by pounding dried lean meat (bison or elk) with rendered fat and dried fruit, sustaining fur traders throughout the subarctic.
- Possesses an almond-like undertone caused by tiny concentrations of benzaldehyde inside the soft, chewable seeds that soften during baking and stewing.
- The shrub, often called *serviceberry*, *juneberry*, or *prairie berry*, is remarkably cold-hardy, tolerating winter temperatures dropping below minus forty degrees Celsius.
- Cultivated commercially throughout the Canadian prairie provinces and the upper Midwest of the United States for use in traditional regional desserts like pies, jams, wines, and syrups.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — fur trading companies such as the *Hudson's Bay Company* and the *North West Company* relied critically on preserved fruits supplied by Indigenous hunters for polar expeditions and frontier trade routes.
- [[british-empire]] — British botanical explorers and naturalists systematically cataloged the flora of the Canadian Northwest during nineteenth-century crown mapping surveys.
- [[united-states]] — grows widely across the northern Great Plains and Pacific Northwest, where it was documented extensively by the *Lewis and Clark Expedition*.
- [[five-senses]] — notable for its distinctive flavor profile combining tart berry notes with a nutty, marzipan-like aromatic finish detected by retronasal olfaction.
- [[sauteing]] — freshly harvested pomes can be quickly cooked in melted butter and sugar over high heat to prepare warm dessert sauces and pancake compotes.
- [[human-anatomy]] — provides high levels of dietary fiber, polyphenolic antioxidants, anthocyanins, vitamin C, and manganese beneficial for human digestive and metabolic health.
