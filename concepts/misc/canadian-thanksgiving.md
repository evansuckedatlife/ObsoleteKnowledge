## summary

**Canadian Thanksgiving** (*Action de grâce*) is an annual statutory harvest holiday celebrated in Canada on the second Monday in October. Distinct from its American counterpart, the observance blends European harvest festivals, early Protestant days of thanksgiving, and historical commemorations dating back to the Arctic voyages of English explorer *Martin Frobisher*. Celebrated across most provinces and territories, the occasion is marked by family gatherings, communal prayers of gratitude, and traditional autumnal feasts featuring turkey, squash, and cranberries.

## you gotta know

- Celebrated on the second Monday of October, falling earlier in the calendar year than the American holiday because of Canada's more northerly geography and earlier harvest season.
- Traces its earliest attributed European observance in Canada to 1578, when English mariner *Martin Frobisher* held a formal service of communion and gratitude in present-day Nunavut during the *[[age-of-exploration]]* after surviving his hazardous Arctic search for the Northwest Passage.
- Influenced heavily in culinary tradition by United Empire Loyalists who fled the *[[united-states]]* during the American Revolution, introducing roasted turkey, pumpkin, squash, and stuffing to the Canadian celebratory menu.
- First declared an official civic holiday by the Parliament of Canada in 1879, initially dedicated to thanking God for an abundant harvest and the recovery of the Prince of Wales from severe illness.
- Confirmed permanently in its modern calendar position on January 31, 1957, by Governor General *Vincent Massey*, who proclaimed it as a day of general thanksgiving to Almighty God for the bountiful harvest with which Canada has been blessed.
- An official statutory paid holiday in most Canadian provinces and territories, though it remains optional in Prince Edward Island, New Brunswick, and Nova Scotia, and has lower cultural prominence in Quebec.
- Culturally associated with sports traditions, most notably the *Thanksgiving Day Classic* doubleheader played annually by the Canadian Football League (CFL).
