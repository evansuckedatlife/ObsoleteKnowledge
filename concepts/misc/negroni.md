## summary

The **Negroni** is an iconic Italian cocktail made from equal parts gin, sweet red vermouth, and Campari bitter liqueur, stirred over ice and served with an orange garnish. Conceived in Florence around 1919 at *Caffè Casoni*, the drink was created when Count *Camillo Negroni* requested that bartender *Fosco Scarselli* stiffen his usual Americano cocktail by swapping soda water for gin. Celebrated for its bright ruby hue, aromatic balance of herbal sweetness, and intense bitterness, it remains an official International Bartenders Association selection and the definitive standard of the classic European aperitivo tradition.

## you gotta know

- Defined by an exact equal-parts formula of one part dry gin, one part sweet red vermouth (vermouth rosso), and one part Campari, typically stirred with ice and served in an old fashioned glass with an orange peel or wheel.
- Originated in Florence, [[italy]], in approximately 1919 when the eccentric traveler Count *Camillo Negroni* requested a bolder version of the Americano cocktail at the fashionable *Caffè Casoni*.
- Bartender *Fosco Scarselli* marked the change from an Americano by using an orange peel garnish instead of the traditional lemon twist, visually distinguishing the new drink for café patrons.
- Prompted the Negroni family to establish the *Negroni Distillerie* in Treviso shortly after its invention to produce a pre-mixed, bottled version marketed as *Antico Negroni*.
- Described famously in 1947 by filmmaker *Orson Welles* in [[rome]], who noted in a dispatch that the bitters are excellent for the liver and the gin is bad, making the drink perfectly balanced.
- Gave rise to several prominent variations, most notably the *Negroni Sbagliato* (created at Milan's *Bar Basso* by accidentally using sparkling wine instead of gin) and the *Boulevardier* (which substitutes American whiskey for gin).
- Enjoyed a major global resurgence during the twenty-first-century cocktail renaissance, anchored by international charity promotions like the annual *Negroni Week*.
