## summary

**Curing** is a food preservation and flavoring practice that draws moisture out of meats, fish, and vegetables through the controlled application of salts, nitrates, sugars, and spices. Developed during antiquity to guard against microbial decay and spoilage, the technique allowed human societies to store seasonal surpluses and sustain maritime exploration. Beyond simple preservation, it fundamentally transforms the texture, color, and flavor of foods, giving rise to celebrated culinary traditions such as charcuterie, prosciutto, and corned beef.

## you gotta know

- Operates primarily through *osmosis*, where high external concentrations of salt draw water out of microbial and animal cells, drastically lowering water activity.
- Relies on sodium chloride along with sodium nitrite and sodium nitrate, which inhibit *Clostridium botulinum* spores and preserve the characteristic pink color of preserved meat.
- Divided into two fundamental technical methods: dry curing, where surface rubs are applied directly, and wet curing, which submerges products in liquid brine solutions.
- Frequently combines desiccation with smoking, fermentation, and the topical application of antimicrobial spices such as [[cumin]] and black pepper.
- Practiced extensively throughout the [[ancient-greece]] and [[roman-empire]] civilizations, which relied on salted pork and garum fish pastes for military logistics.
- Vital to maritime survival during the [[age-of-exploration]], when vessels stocked barrels of salt pork and salt beef to prevent scurvy and starvation.
- Transformed by the [[industrial-revolution]], which introduced automated brine injection, chemical refrigeration, and precise scientific understanding of nitrogen chemistry.

## connections

- [[cumin]] — warm, antimicrobial seed spice frequently included in regional dry rubs to season and protect preserved cuts.
- [[ancient-greece]] — early Mediterranean society that refined techniques for salting seafood and pork for long-term storage.
- [[roman-empire]] — empire whose legions and urban centers relied heavily on cured meats, sausage-making traditions, and salted provisions.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical shift that mechanized food production, revolutionizing meatpacking plants and refrigeration.
- [[middle-east]] — geographic region where prehistoric populations pioneered salting, sun-drying, and preserving meat in arid climates.
- [[spain]] — country renowned for its artisanal traditions of cured ham, including *jamón ibérico* and *jamón serrano*.
