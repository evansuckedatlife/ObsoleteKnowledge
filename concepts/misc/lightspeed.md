## summary

The concept of **lightspeed**, denoted by the physical constant *c*, represents the universal speed at which all massless particles and electromagnetic waves travel through a vacuum. Exactly defined in the International System of Units as 299,792,458 meters per second, it establishes the ultimate upper speed limit for information, matter, and causal propagation across spacetime. First measured quantitatively by Danish astronomer *Ole Rømer*, its constancy serves as the foundational postulate of modern relativistic physics.

## you gotta know

- Defined precisely in SI units as exactly 299,792,458 m/s, fixing the length of the standard meter as the distance light travels in 1/299,792,458 of a second.
- First calculated quantitatively in 1676 by *Ole Rømer* by timing the apparent orbital eclipses of Jupiter's moon *Io* across Earth's changing orbital positions.
- Demonstrated to be invariant regardless of the motion of the emitting source or observer by the 1887 *Michelson-Morley experiment*, disproving the luminiferous aether hypothesis.
- Functions as the core universal invariant in *Albert Einstein's* 1905 theory of *Special Relativity*, underpinning the mass-energy equivalence formula *E = mc²*.
- Establishes the boundaries of causality via spacetime light cones, dividing events into timelike, spacelike, and lightlike intervals.
- Generates *Cherenkov radiation*, a faint bluish glow, when charged particles travel through a dielectric medium faster than the phase velocity of light in that specific medium.
- Traversed by photons from the Sun to the Earth in approximately eight minutes and twenty seconds.
