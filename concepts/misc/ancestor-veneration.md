## summary

**Ancestor veneration**, also widely termed **ancestor worship**, is the ritualized practice of honoring, remembering, and spiritually sustaining deceased forebears across generations. It is especially prominent throughout East and Southeast Asia, where domestic altars, incense offerings, and ceremonial meals form the spiritual heartbeat of major holidays like [[tet]] and [[chinese-new-year]]. In anthropology and comparative religion, the tradition represents a fundamental framework for expressing filial piety, reinforcing kinship bonds, and maintaining continuity between the living and the spirit world.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the primary spiritual focus of [[tet]], the Vietnamese celebration of the [[lunar-calendar]] new year, where families thoroughly clean ancestral graves (*tảo mộ*) and formally invite departed ancestors home to partake in holiday feasts.
- Maintained domestically through dedicated household altars, where families arrange fresh fruit on a five-fruit tray (*mâm ngũ quả*), burn incense (*nhang*), and display ancestral tablets (*bài vị*) or framed portraits of the deceased.
- Commemorated on individual death anniversaries, known in Vietnamese culture as *giỗ*, which bring extended families together to prepare elaborate banquets, execute ritual bows (*lạy*), and burn votive paper (*vàng mã*) for the spirit's use.
- Grounded philosophically in Confucian principles of filial piety (*hiếu* or *xiao*), forming a syncretic cultural foundation that comfortably intersects with Daoist cosmology and local expressions of [[theravada-buddhism]].
- Parallel traditions in [[japan]] structure domestic rites around the Buddhist *butsudan* altar and Shinto *kamidana*, culminating in New Year observances during [[shogatsu]] and the midsummer homecoming festival of *Obon*.
- Practiced across diverse global traditions beyond Asia, notably within the [[african-diaspora]], Indigenous nations, and classical antiquity throughout [[ancient-greece]] and the [[roman-empire]].
- Precipitated the historic Chinese Rites Controversy of the 17th and 18th centuries, in which European missionaries and theologians within [[christianity]] clashed over whether these customs constituted civil familial respect or prohibited religious idolatry.

## connections

- [[tet]] — the Vietnamese Lunar New Year celebration where welcoming ancestral spirits to the family altar constitutes the central sacred ritual.
- [[chinese-new-year]] — the major seasonal festival marked by grave sweeping, family reunion feasts, and ancestral offerings across the Sinosphere.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — the traditional reckoning system that determines the timing of tomb-sweeping dates, annual death anniversaries, and seasonal rites.
- [[shogatsu]] — the Japanese New Year celebration featuring household purification, grave visitation, and hospitality for ancestral and tutelary spirits.
- [[japan]] — the country where ancestor veneration persists through *butsudan* altars, household tablets, and the national *Obon* festival.
- [[theravada-buddhism]] — the Southeast Asian Buddhist tradition that frequently coexists with domestic ancestral rites and merit-transference ceremonies.
- [[african-diaspora]] — global cultural sphere where traditional ancestral devotions were preserved, adapted, and syncretized into new religious practices.
