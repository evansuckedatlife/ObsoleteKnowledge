## summary

**Checkers**, widely designated outside North America as **Draughts**, and formally known in competitive circles as **English draughts** or **American checkers**, is a traditional two-player board game of diagonal movement and mandatory capture. Opponents maneuver identical circular counters across the dark squares of a gridded surface, leaping over opposing men to remove them from play until one player captures or traps the opposing army. Tracing its lineage to medieval board games and ancient ancestors, it represents both an accessible domestic pastime and a legendary benchmark in algorithmic game theory.

## you gotta know

- Contested on the identical 64-square dark-and-light board employed by [[chess]], though play is restricted exclusively to the 32 dark squares across which pieces advance diagonally.
- Commences with each contestant directing twelve flat disk pieces, traditionally rendered in red and white or black and red, stationed upon the three rows closest to the player's edge.
- Enforces a mandatory jumping rule under standard Anglo-American regulations, requiring a player to capture whenever an opposing piece is vulnerably situated with an open square behind it.
- Promotes an ordinary man to a king when it reaches the opponent's farthest row (the "kings row"), granting it the vital capability of moving and leaping backward as well as forward.
- Developed in twelfth-century [[france]] and [[england]] as *fierges* or *dames*, which mapped the jumping captures of the Middle Eastern game *alquerque* onto an eight-by-eight checkered grid.
- Achieved historic mathematical resolution in 2007 when computer scientist *Jonathan Schaeffer* and his team announced that their program *Chinook* had solved the game, proving that optimal play always results in a draw.
- Entered twentieth-century political rhetoric via *Richard Nixon*'s televised 1952 "Checkers speech," wherein the vice-presidential candidate defended personal campaign contributions by discussing his family's cocker spaniel named *Checkers*.
- Encompasses several major global variants, most notably *International draughts*, which unfolds on a wider ten-by-ten board with twenty pieces per side and features "flying kings."

## connections

- [[chess]] — sister two-player strategy game that shares the eight-by-eight checkered board and alternating square layout.
- [[geometry]] — the principles of diagonal vectors, Cartesian grid symmetry, and planar angles directing piece captures.
- [[england]] — the historical nation where the rules of draughts were standardized in early rulebooks and mathematical treatises.
- [[united-states]] — the country where American checkers developed major tournament circuits and gained renown via political speechcraft.
- [[france]] — the medieval realm where *alquerque* mechanics were merged onto a chess board to form the game *dames*.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical Mediterranean civilization whose strategic grid game *ludus latrunculorum* operated as a mechanical predecessor.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the ancient culture whose pebble board game *petteia* established early precedents for tactical capture games.
