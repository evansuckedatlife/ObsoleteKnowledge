## summary

**Chicken Tikka Masala** (frequently referred to simply as **Tikka Masala**) is a renowned curry dish consisting of marinated, roasted chicken chunks served in a rich, spiced, and creamy tomato gravy. Developed in the mid-twentieth century within the South Asian diaspora in the *United Kingdom*, the dish creatively adapted the dry clay-oven cooking of Mughal and Punjabi tandoori culinary traditions to suit British tastes for meat with rich sauces. Its overwhelming popularity led British Foreign Secretary *Robin Cook* to famously proclaim it "a true British national dish" in 2001, symbolizing post-imperial multiculturalism and culinary migration.

## you gotta know

- Originated from traditional South Asian *chicken tikka*, which consists of boneless poultry pieces marinated in yogurt and spices before being baked on skewers inside a clay *tandoor*.
- Popular folklore most frequently attributes the creation of the sauce to *Ali Ahmed Aslam*, a Pakistani-Scottish chef who founded the *Shish Mahal* restaurant in Glasgow in the 1970s.
- Legend recounts that *Aslam* improvised the velvety sauce by combining a tin of Campbell's condensed tomato soup, cream, and mild spices after a customer complained that the roasted tandoori chicken was too dry.
- Prepared using a sauce flavored with garam masala, ginger, garlic, tomatoes, fenugreek (*kasuri methi*), and double cream or yogurt, often tinted bright orange with paprika, turmeric, or food dye.
- Proclaimed by British Foreign Secretary *Robin Cook* in a landmark 2001 speech as "a true British national dish" representing modern multicultural Britain absorbing and adapting external influences.
- Prompted a 2009 campaign by Glasgow Member of Parliament *Mohammad Sarwar* to secure European Union *Protected Designation of Origin* status for the city as the dish's birthplace, which remained unratified.
- Differentiated from the authentic North Indian specialty *butter chicken* (*murgh makhani*), which was invented in Delhi at *Moti Mahal* by *Kundan Lal Gujral* using a tandoori-dripping tomato and butter gravy.
- Circulated widely across modern digital culture through viral home-cooking tutorials and restaurant reviews on video platforms like [[tiktok]], introducing regional variations to a global youth audience.
