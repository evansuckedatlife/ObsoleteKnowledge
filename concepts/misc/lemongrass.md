## summary

**Lemongrass**, classified botanically within the genus **Cymbopogon** and often referred to as **citronella grass**, is a genus of tall, aromatic perennial grasses native to tropical and subtropical Asia, Africa, and Australia. Known for its distinct citrus-like fragrance and culinary versatility, the plant plays a central role in Southeast Asian gastronomy, herbal pharmacology, and the international fragrance trade. Its essential oils contain aromatic compounds that serve as traditional therapeutics, commercial perfume constituents, and natural insect repellents.

## you gotta know

- Forms dense clumping mounds of fibrous, sharp-edged blue-green foliage, with thick, layered basal stalks that are crushed, bruised, or minced to release aromatic volatile oils.
- The dominant chemical component responsible for its citrus bouquet is *citral*, an isomeric aldehyde blend of *geranial* and *neral* that strongly engages human *[[perception]]* through both olfactory and gustatory pathways.
- Serves as a signature flavoring base throughout Southeast Asian countries rooted in *[[theravada-buddhism]]*, providing essential aromatic balance in Thai *tom yum* and *tom kha*, Cambodian *kroeung* pastes, and Vietnamese stocks.
- Cultivars such as *Cymbopogon nardus* and *Cymbopogon winterianus* are cultivated to produce citronella oil, an essential oil utilized globally to deter mosquitoes and other pests.
- Propagated across maritime trade routes and commercial plantations across Central America, Africa, and the Caribbean under European *[[colonialism]]* as a lucrative essential-oil crop.
- Employed in traditional Ayurvedic medicine and folk traditions across South Asia and the *[[middle-east]]* as a fever reducer, digestive carminative, and calming infusion.
- Concentrated distillates are processed into aromatherapy products and topical rubs that interact with *[[human-anatomy]]* by soothing muscle tension and exhibiting mild antimicrobial properties.
