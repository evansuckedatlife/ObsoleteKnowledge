## summary

A **preposition** is an adpositional word that precedes its nominal complement to establish spatial, temporal, or logical relations with other sentence elements. Functioning as a closed class of grammatical particles across numerous natural languages, it connects nouns, pronouns, and noun phrases to matrix clauses. In formal grammar and typological study, its placement relative to the noun correlates strongly with head-directionality and broad syntactic structure.

## you gotta know

- Member of the broader linguistic category of adpositions; while prepositions precede their complement noun phrase, postpositions follow it, a parameter tightly tied to word-order typology like Subject-Verb-Object versus Subject-Object-Verb.
- Classical grammarians in [[ancient-greece]] and the [[roman-empire]], such as *Dionysius Thrax* and *Priscian*, categorised the part of speech under the Greek term *prothesis* and Latin *praepositio*, denoting "placing before".
- Typically forms a prepositional phrase that functions syntactically as an adverbial or adjectival modifier, establishing location, direction, duration, instrument, or possession.
- Preposition stranding (*P-stranding*), where a preposition is left without a following noun phrase in interrogative or relative clauses (such as "What are you looking at?"), is common in Germanic languages like English but prohibited or rare in Romance languages.
- Prescriptive grammar historically discouraged stranding and ending sentences with prepositions in English, an artificial rule popularised by eighteenth-century figures like *Robert Lowth* based on Latin syntax.
- Historical grammaticalization often derives prepositions from body-part nouns or dynamic spatial verbs, such as "ahead of" or "concerning".
- Often closely related to the morphological **[[prefix]]**, as Indo-European preverbs historically fused with verb stems to alter aspect and valency.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — parent discipline that classifies word classes, syntactic projections, and adpositional phrases.
- [[predicate]] — major clausal constituent often modified or complemented by prepositional phrases to specify adverbial conditions.
- [[prefix]] — closely related morphological element that frequently shares etymological origins with spatial prepositions through grammaticalization.
- [[ancient-greece]] — origin of early grammatical analysis where the term *prothesis* was first canonised in Stoic and Alexandrian treatises.
- [[roman-empire]] — established Latin grammatical treatises that coined *praepositio* and profoundly influenced European grammars.
- [[england]] — home to early modern English grammarians whose prescriptive rules artificially targeted preposition stranding.
