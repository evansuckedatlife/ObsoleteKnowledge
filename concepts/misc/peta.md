## summary

**Peta** (symbol **P**) is a metric unit prefix denoting a factor of $10^{15}$, or one quadrillion in the short scale system. Adopted in 1975 by the *General Conference on Weights and Measures* (CGPM) during the modern expansion of the International System of Units (SI), it applies across physical science, data storage, and telecommunications. It is essential in scientific contexts ranging from astronomical distances measured in light-travel times to high-performance computing performance measured in floating-point operations.

## you gotta know

- Represents $10^{15}$, or 1,000,000,000,000,000, situated directly between *tera-* ($10^{12}$) and *exa-* ($10^{18}$).
- Etymology derives from the Greek word *pente* meaning "five", referencing the fifth power of one thousand ($1000^5$), with the letter *n* omitted by convention in parallel with *tera-*.
- Adopted by the 15th CGPM in 1975 alongside *exa-*, expanding SI capabilities as digital computation and advanced physics demanded larger scalings.
- In supercomputing, high-performance systems benchmark processing speeds in petaFLOPS, with the milestone of one petaFLOP first broken in 2008 by the *IBM Roadrunner*.
- In data storage, a petabyte (PB) comprises $10^{15}$ bytes, whereas the binary counterpart pebibyte (PiB) established by the IEC represents $2^{50}$ ($1,125,899,906,842,624$) bytes.
- In particle physics and laser optics, petawatts (PW) measure peak pulses of extreme ultra-short lasers focused onto microscopic plasma targets.
- In [[astronomy]], a petametre ($10^{15}\text{ m}$) spans approximately 0.1057 light-years, roughly the scale of the outer reaches of the Solar System and inner Oort cloud.

## connections

- [[pico]] — its exact reciprocal prefix counterpart representing $10^{-12}$, often paired when discussing extreme metric prefixes.
