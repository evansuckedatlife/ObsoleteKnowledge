## summary

**Blanching** is a foundational culinary and food-preservation technique in which vegetables, fruits, or nuts are briefly immersed in boiling water or steam and subsequently plunged into ice water in a step known as **shocking**. Formalized within classical French cooking traditions and later scaled during nineteenth-century industrial processing, the process halts enzymatic breakdown, fixes bright pigmentation, and loosens tough skins. In gastronomic science, it represents the indispensable preliminary measure that protects color, texture, and nutritional integrity before freezing, canning, or secondary cooking.

## you gotta know

- The central biochemical purpose of the process is the rapid thermal inactivation of native plant enzymes, particularly *polyphenol oxidase*, *peroxidase*, and *lipoxygenase*, which otherwise induce unpalatable browning, softening, and rancid flavors during storage.
- The immediate secondary step of submerging the scalded food into an ice bath, termed **shocking** or refreshing, stops residual thermal transfer instantly to maintain crisp cellular structure.
- Thermal scalding causes trapped air within plant tissues to expand and escape, setting the brilliant green color of *chlorophyll* before prolonged acid cooking can transform it into dull olive-toned *pheophytin*.
- In pastry and confectionery, the technique is routinely applied to loosen papery skins from tree nuts, producing bare kernels suitable for almond paste, marzipan, or inclusion in dry goods like [[biscotti]].
- Commercial fruit canners and pastry chefs use brief scalding to remove peels cleanly from stone fruits and cherries, such as those processed for desserts like [[black-forest-cake]], without cooking the internal fruit flesh.
- Classical culinary technique developed in [[france]] distinguishes between immersing green vegetables into already boiling water and placing root vegetables or bitter meats into cold water brought slowly to a boil.
- The rise of commercial freezing and canning during the [[industrial-revolution]] established precise scalding time charts calibrated to vegetable diameter to prevent both nutrient loss and under-processing.
