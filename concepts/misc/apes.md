## summary

**Apes** (*Hominoidea*) are a tailless evolutionary superfamily of catarrhine primates comprising lesser apes—the hylobatids or gibbons—and the **great apes** (family **Hominidae**), the taxonomic group encompassing chimpanzees, bonobos, gorillas, orangutans, and human beings. Diverging from ancestral Old World monkeys during the Oligocene and early Miocene epochs across Afro-Eurasia, they evolved upright torso postures, mobile shoulder joints, and substantially expanded cranial capacities. Within [[evolutionary-biology]], studying these hominoid lineages provides critical empirical evidence for understanding human evolutionary origins, complex social dynamics, tool manufacture, and the cognitive emergence of culture and symbolic reasoning.

## you gotta know

- Anatomical absence of an external tail, upright trunk posture, and highly mobile shoulder joints adapted for suspensory brachiation distinguish them from monkeys, establishing structural foundations shared in [[human-anatomy]].
- Humans (*Homo sapiens*) are cladistically nested within the great apes (*Hominidae*), sharing more than ninety-eight percent of their genomic [[dna]] sequence identity with common chimpanzees (*Pan troglodytes*) and pygmy chimpanzees or bonobos (*Pan paniscus*).
- Pioneering long-term field studies directed by anthropologist *Louis Leakey* and carried out by *Jane Goodall*, *Dian Fossey*, and *Birutė Galdikas* revolutionized primatology by revealing sophisticated tool use, organized group warfare, cooperative hunting, and profound bereavement rituals among wild populations.
- Controversial cross-fostering and cognitive communication experiments demonstrated that captive individuals—such as the chimpanzee *Washoe*, the gorilla *Koko*, and the bonobo *Kanzi*—could master hundreds of American Sign Language signs or manipulate symbolic lexigram keyboards to convey intentions.
- Pronounced encephalization and complex [[neurology]] foster self-awareness, famously validated through the *mirror self-recognition test* developed by psychologist *Gordon Gallup Jr.*, an experimental benchmark routinely passed by chimpanzees, bonobos, and orangutans.
- Social structures vary extensively across genera, ranging from the semi-solitary, arboreal foraging of Bornean and Sumatran orangutans to cohesive silverback-led gorilla harems, female-dominated egalitarian bonobo groups, and patriarchal, fission-fusion chimpanzee societies.
- All extant non-human species face immediate threats of extinction across equatorial Africa and Southeast Asia due to commercial deforestation, agricultural palm oil expansion, civil conflict, illegal bushmeat poaching, and high susceptibility to human pathogens.
- The Miocene fossil record features diverse extinct hominoid genera such as *Proconsul*, *Dryopithecus*, *Sivapithecus*, and the Pleistocene giant *Gigantopithecus blacki*, illustrating wide geographic radiations across Eurasia and Africa before hominin speciation.

## connections

- [[human-anatomy]] — shares fundamental homologous skeletal morphology, including broad thoracic ribcages, absent caudal vertebrae, and flexible ball-and-socket shoulder joints.
- [[neurology]] — underpins remarkable cerebral cortical expansion, advanced spindle neurons, and the neural substrates driving empathy, deceptive strategy, and theory of mind.
- [[linguistics]] — frames contentious scholarly inquiries regarding syntactic recursion, symbolic abstraction, and whether ape gesture systems represent evolutionary precursors to spoken human language.
- [[five-senses]] — highlights an evolutionary shift toward acute trichromatic binocular vision and heightened tactile manipulation at the expense of olfactory receptor specialization.
- [[perception]] — tests comparative perceptual mechanisms examining how primates navigate complex three-dimensional canopies, recognize facial expressions, and interpret causal physics during tool manipulation.
- [[united-kingdom]] — intellectual home of Victorian naturalists *Charles Darwin* and *Thomas Henry Huxley*, whose foundational debates established human biological descent from ancestral primates.
- [[colonialism]] — spurred European imperial expeditions that mapped tropical habitats, collected museum specimens, and initiated extensive resource exploitation that still threatens wild hominoid sanctuaries.
