## summary

The **Autumn Equinox**, or **Autumnal Equinox**, is a celestial milestone and worldwide seasonal holiday occurring annually around September 22 to 24 when the sun crosses the celestial equator going south, yielding nearly equal parts day and night across the globe. Celebrated across world cultures as a harvest festival, ancestral remembrance day, and calendar anchor, it marks the astronomical beginning of autumn in the Northern Hemisphere and spring in the Southern Hemisphere. Its integration into civic calendars—most famously establishing the epoch of the *French Republican Calendar*—and global religious observances makes it a vital nexus of [[astronomy]], cultural mythology, and timekeeping.

## you gotta know

- Marks the astronomical moment when the subsolar point crosses the celestial equator moving southward, creating roughly twelve hours of daylight and twelve hours of darkness worldwide and launching the northern harvest cycle in [[astronomy]].
- Celebrated throughout [[japan]] as the official national holiday *Shūbun no Hi*, an occasion dedicated to ancestral veneration during the week of *Higan*, when Buddhist practitioners visit familial graves, clear tombs, and prepare sweet bean paste confections called *ohagi*.
- Served as the astronomical epoch for the decimalized *French Republican Calendar* during the [[french-revolution]], which decreed that the proclamation of the First Republic on September 22, 1792, would permanently define the start of each civil year on the day of the true equinox in [[france]].
- Associated in [[ancient-greece]] with the sacred rites of the *Eleusinian Mysteries*, which commemorated the earth goddess *Demeter* and her daughter *Persephone*, whose annual descent to the underworld brought about the barren chill of autumn and winter.
- Anchors the autumn liturgical season in [[judaism]], closely preceding the High Holy Days in the month of *Tishrei*, which encompasses the celebration of *Rosh Hashanah*, the solemn fast of *Yom Kippur*, and the harvest huts constructed for *Sukkot*.
- Observed in neopagan and Wiccan traditions as the sabbat of *Mabon*, the second seasonal harvest on the *Wheel of the Year*, accompanied by widespread modern folklore asserting that raw eggs can uniquely be balanced upright on their ends only during the equinox.
- Influenced monumental sacred [[architecture]] worldwide, exemplified by the Mayan pyramid *El Castillo* at *Chichen Itza*, where late afternoon equinoctial sunlight creates a undulating shadow across the northern balustrade that mimics the serpent deity *Kukulkan* descending to earth.

## connections

- [[nowruz]] — the Iranian New Year holiday that celebrates the opposing spring equinox as a festival of rebirth and agricultural renewal.
- [[tet]] — the Vietnamese holiday marking the lunar new year and the traditional arrival of spring agricultural labor.
- [[shogatsu]] — the Japanese New Year holiday featuring ancestral veneration and seasonal customs closely parallel to equinox traditions.
- [[french-revolution]] — the political transformation that chose the autumnal equinox as the foundational start of its revolutionary calendar.
- [[japan]] — the nation that designates the equinox as the public holiday *Shūbun no Hi* for family cemetery visits.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical culture that framed seasonal shifts through the *Eleusinian Mysteries* and the myth of *Demeter*.
- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition whose autumn calendar coordinates the High Holy Days and *Sukkot* around the equinox.
