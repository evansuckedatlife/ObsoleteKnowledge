## summary

The **peanut**, also known taxonomically as *Arachis hypogaea*, is an annual legume native to South America cultivated primarily for its edible seeds and rich oil. Uniquely maturing subterraneanly via geocarpy, the plant became a foundational global agricultural staple through maritime exchange and chattel slavery across the Atlantic world. In quiz bowl, it frequently appears in questions concerning the agricultural innovations of *George Washington Carver*, dietary staple foods, and cultural economies of the American South.

## you gotta know

- Classified botanically as a legume rather than a true nut, developing its pods underground after flowering through elongated floral stems termed pegs.
- Agricultural chemist *George Washington Carver* developed over three hundred distinct uses for the crop, including dyes, plastics, cosmetics, and paints, promoting it as a soil-enriching alternative to cotton in the *reconstruction-era* South.
- Native to tropical South America, where indigenous cultures in modern Bolivia and Peru domesticated the allotetraploid species thousands of years prior to European contact.
- Dispersed worldwide via transatlantic *colonialism*, becoming an indispensable subsistence crop across West Africa before enslaved peoples transported cultivation knowledge to North America under the name *goober*.
- Major global producers today include India, China, Nigeria, and the *united-states*, particularly concentrated in Georgia, Alabama, and North Carolina.
- Severe Type I hypersensitivity allergic reactions triggered by storage vicilin and legumin seed proteins make it a central topic in immunology and public health epidemiology.
- Susceptible to fungal infection by *Aspergillus flavus*, which synthesizes highly carcinogenic aflatoxins when pods are stored in warm, humid conditions.

## connections

- [[african-diaspora]] — transported the crop and its linguistic namesake across the Atlantic middle passage into plantation cuisine.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — period during which agricultural reformers sought crop diversification away from soil-depleting cotton monoculture.
- [[united-states]] — home to southern agricultural hubs that industrialized cultivation, processing, and confectionery production.
- [[colonialism]] — facilitated the global transfer of the crop between the Americas, Africa, and European mercantile trade hubs.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict during which boiled varieties served as an essential emergency dietary ration for soldiers.
- [[pepper]] — fellow New World botanical domesticate that transformed global culinary traditions following transatlantic trade.
