## summary

**Robert Louis Stevenson** was a Scottish author and adventurer whose imaginative tales of mystery, adventure, and psychological terror defined Victorian literature. Born in 1850 and dying of tuberculosis in Samoa in 1894, he produced works of enduring cultural force, including *Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde* and *Treasure Island*, that explore dual natures and the romantic allure of transgression.

## you gotta know

- Wrote *Treasure Island*, one of the first novels to define the modern pirate adventure genre, serialized initially in a boys' magazine and featuring iconic figures like Long John Silver and young Jim Hawkins.
- Created *Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde*, a novella about a doctor's chemical experiment that unleashes his repressed evil self, becoming a touchstone for psychological horror and the duality of human nature.
- Wrote *Kidnapped*, a novel of adventure and identity set in post-Jacobite Scotland, featuring the memorable character Alan Breck Stewart and exploring themes of loyalty, exile, and the Highland clearances.
- Suffered from chronic tuberculosis throughout his life, which forced periods of travel and confinement that shaped both his work and his wanderlust; illness becomes a recurring theme in his fiction.
- Settled in Samoa at age 40, where he lived among the local population and wrote essays and stories that revealed deep respect for Pacific cultures; the Samoans called him *Tusitala*, "the teller of tales," honoring his storytelling gifts.
- Pioneered a prose style that balanced Victorian propriety with gothic sensibility, blending adventure narrative with philosophical introspection and moral ambiguity that influenced modernist writers.
- Wrote *The Strange Case of Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde* as a novella of fewer than 30,000 words yet created one of literature's most enduring explorations of morality, addiction, and split personality.
