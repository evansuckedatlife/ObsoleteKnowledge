## summary

The **T-bone steak** is a premium subprimal cut of beef butchered from the short loin of cattle, immediately identifiable by its distinct cross-cut lumbar vertebra resembling the letter T. The bone physically separates two prized, highly tender muscle sections: the larger longissimus dorsi (*strip loin*) and the smaller psoas major (*tenderloin*). Widely considered a premier showpiece of modern steakhouse culinary traditions, the cut requires careful cooking methods to reconcile the disparate cooking rates and fat profiles of its two constituent meats.

## you gotta know

- Anatomically derived from the anterior section of the subprimal beef short loin, cut across the lumbar vertebrae to include sections of both the transverse process and the vertebral body.
- The bone functions as an insulator during dry-heat cooking, reducing conductive heat transfer to the surrounding flesh while imparting collagen and marrow flavors.
- According to official United States Department of Agriculture specifications, a standard cut must feature a tenderloin section measuring at least 0.5 inches in width across its center.
- When the tenderloin portion measures 1.25 inches or wider, the cut is officially graded and sold as a *porterhouse steak*, butchered from the posterior end of the short loin.
- The strip side corresponds to the New York strip or Kansas City strip, exhibiting rich marbling and robust intramuscular fat, whereas the tenderloin portion corresponds to the exceptionally soft *filet mignon*.
- Culinary execution presents a classic thermodynamic challenge: the lean tenderloin cooks more rapidly than the marbled strip, often requiring angled placement away from direct heat sources.
- Italian culinary heritage celebrates a renowned regional variant known as *bistecca alla fiorentina*, cut thick from prized Chianina cattle and grilled over red-hot glowing wood embers.
