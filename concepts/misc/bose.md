## summary

**Satyendra Nath Bose**, universally remembered in physics as **Bose**, was an Indian mathematician and theoretical physicist whose pioneering statistical treatment of light quanta laid the theoretical groundwork for quantum statistics. Working in Calcutta during the tenure of the [[british-empire]], he derived Planck's radiation law without classical assumptions by treating photons as indistinguishable particles, capturing the attention of *Albert Einstein* who championed and extended his findings. In tribute to his fundamental contributions to physical science, theoretical physicist *Paul Dirac* named the entire class of integer-spin subatomic particles *bosons* in his honor.

## you gotta know

- Authored the landmark 1924 paper *Planck's Law and the Light Quantum Hypothesis*, which discarded classical electrodynamics to derive Planck's blackbody law purely through statistical mechanics of indistinguishable light quanta.
- When British journals initially rejected his manuscript, he mailed the paper directly to *Albert Einstein* in [[germany]], who translated the work into German and personally submitted it to *Zeitschrift für Physik*.
- Collaborated intellectually with Einstein to extend these statistical principles to ideal monoatomic gases, developing *Bose-Einstein statistics* and predicting the fifth state of matter known as the *Bose-Einstein condensate* (BEC).
- Particles that possess integer values of quantum spin (such as photons, gluons, and the Higgs boson) are formally designated *bosons*, contrasting with half-integer spin *fermions* named for *Enrico Fermi*.
- The Bose-Einstein condensate was experimentally verified in 1995 using magnetically trapped rubidium-87 atoms by *Eric Cornell* and *Carl Wieman* at JILA, and with sodium atoms by *Wolfgang Ketterle* at MIT, earning them the 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics.
- Educated at Presidency College in Calcutta alongside celebrated astrophysicist *Meghnad Saha*, he served on the faculty at the University of Dacca and later occupied the prestigious Khaira Chair of Physics at Calcutta University.
- Contributed broadly beyond quantum mechanics to x-ray crystallography, unified field theory, mineral chemistry, and the popularization of vernacular science education in Bengal.
