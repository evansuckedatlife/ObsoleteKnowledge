## summary

A **funicular**, also designated as a **funicular railway** or **inclined plane railway**, is a cable-driven transit system designed to ascend and descend steep grades that conventional adhesion rail vehicles cannot navigate. The technology relies on a pair of counterbalanced carriages permanently attached to opposite ends of a central cable looped around a haulage pulley at the summit of the incline. Developed into widespread public transit during the nineteenth century, funiculars transformed transportation across mountainous terrain and steeply graded cities throughout Europe, the Americas, and Australasia.

## you gotta know

- Operates on a simple physics principle where the weight of the descending car acts as a natural counterweight to lift the ascending car, drastically minimizing the external energy required to power the system.
- The cars typically travel along a single track that branches into a central passing loop using specialized Abt switch turnouts, which utilize wheels with double flanges on the outer rail and flat rollers on the inner rail.
- Water-balance propulsion powered the earliest commercial funiculars, pumping water into a tank aboard the descending upper carriage to weigh it down and drain it upon arrival at the bottom station.
- The oldest working funicular in the world is widely considered to be the *Reisszug*, a private freight hoist serving the Hohensalzburg Fortress in Austria that dates back in primitive wooden form to 1504.
- The *Funiculaire de la Croix-Rousse* in Lyon, [[france]], opened in 1862 as the first urban passenger funicular railway, carrying commuters up the city's steep hillside.
- The famous Italian novelty song *Funiculì, Funiculà* was composed in 1880 by Luigi Denza with lyrics by Peppino Turco to celebrate the opening of the first funicular on Mount Vesuvius in [[italy]].
- The *Angels Flight* funicular in Los Angeles, California, built in 1901, became one of the most famous landmarks in American urban transit, appearing in numerous films and literary works.

## connections

- [[ford]] — primitive topographical transit solution that navigates natural contours, contrasting with the mechanical engineering of funicular railways.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical era whose innovations in steel cable manufacturing, steam engines, and metallurgy made steep incline railways feasible.
- [[architecture]] — discipline that designed ornamental summit stations, viaducts, and integrated transit terminals for urban cliff railways.
- [[france]] — nation where the first passenger funicular operated in Lyon, pioneering urban mass transit across severe topographical elevation changes.
- [[italy]] — home to iconic incline railways, including the Mount Vesuvius line immortalized in popular European song.
- [[germany]] — country where advanced funicular engineering, including the *Nerobergbahn* water-ballast railway in Wiesbaden, was perfected.
- [[united-kingdom]] — nation featuring extensive Victorian seaside cliff lifts in coastal resort towns such as Scarborough and Hastings.
