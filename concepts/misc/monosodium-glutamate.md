## summary

**Monosodium glutamate**, widely abbreviated as **MSG**, is the sodium salt of the non-essential amino acid glutamic acid, renowned as the archetypal compound responsible for eliciting the savory taste sensation known as umami. Discovered and patented in the early twentieth century by chemist *Kikunae Ikeda* in Tokyo, the white crystalline powder became an industrial staple under the brand name *Ajinomoto*. While scientifically recognized as safe and physiologically ubiquitous in protein digestion, it remains culturally famous for triggering mid-century public controversies and dietary anxieties regarding systemic food additives.

## you gotta know

- First isolated in 1908 by imperial chemist *Kikunae Ikeda* from the kelp seaweed *Saccharina japonica* (*kombu*), whose dashi broth possessed a rich savoriness distinct from sweet, sour, salty, and bitter profiles.
- Commercialized globally by the *Ajinomoto* corporation, which shifted manufacturing from wheat gluten hydrolysis to bacterial fermentation using *Corynebacterium glutamicum*.
- Triggers the physiological sensation of umami by binding to heterodimeric G-protein coupled taste receptors, specifically *T1R1* and *T1R3*, across the papillae of the human tongue.
- Naturally concentrated in ripe tomatoes, aged cheeses such as parmesan, cured meats, fermented soy sauce, and hydrolyzed yeast extracts, explaining their cross-cultural culinary importance.
- Sparked the panic known as *Chinese restaurant syndrome* following an unverified 1968 letter by *Robert Ho Man Kwok* to the *New England Journal of Medicine*, alleging headaches, palpitations, and numbness.
- Discredited scientifically by double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials, proving that typical dietary intake does not produce neurological toxicity or systemic acute illness in normal populations.
- Formally classified under European food labeling regulations as additive *E621*, frequently paired synergistically with inosinate and guanylate disodium ribonucleotides to amplify perceived savoriness.
