## summary

**Inuktitut**, specifically referring to **Eastern Canadian Inuktitut**, is an indigenous language of North America belonging to the Eskimo-Aleut language family and spoken across the Arctic regions of northern Canada. Renowned in structural [[linguistics]] for its polysynthetic and agglutinative typology, it constructs intricate, sentence-length words through extensive suffixation attached to base roots. The language holds official institutional status in the Canadian territory of Nunavut and the Northwest Territories, representing a resilient symbol of Arctic cultural identity against historical pressures of European [[colonialism]].

## you gotta know

- Typologically celebrated as a classic polysynthetic language, it strings together dozens of distinct morphemes—termed *postbases*—to generate single complex words that express complete propositional thoughts.
- Written in two standard orthographic systems: an indigenous syllabary adapted in the nineteenth century from James Evans's Cree syllabics, and a Roman orthography known as *Qaliujaaqpait*.
- Demonstrates an ergative-absolutive morphosyntactic alignment, grouping the intransitive grammatical subject with the direct object of a transitive verb while marking the transitive agent separately.
- Features grammatical dual number in addition to singular and plural across its nouns and verbs, a morphological trait shared across the broader Inuit dialect continuum.
- Utilizes purely concatenative suffixation to modify verbal and nominal stems, markedly avoiding internal morphological alterations like an [[infix]] in favor of linear chaining.
- Lacks grammatical gender distinctions in personal pronouns and noun phrases, relying instead on nominal noun-incorporation strategies to embed objects directly into complex predicate stems.
- Subjected to severe institutional disruption under the [[british-empire]] and Canadian federal policies, including forced relocation and residential schooling during the [[cold-war]], prompting twenty-first-century language revitalization initiatives.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — the geopolitical expansion and assimilation policies that marginalized indigenous circumpolar communities.
