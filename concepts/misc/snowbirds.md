## summary

**Snowbirds** refers to seasonal migrants—predominantly retirees and older adults—who temporarily relocate from colder northern climates to warmer southern destinations during the winter months. Popularized in North America during the mid-twentieth century, the term most commonly describes residents from the northern [[united-states]] and Canada who winter in warm-weather regions like Florida, Arizona, Texas, and Southern California. The phenomenon plays a substantial role in regional economies, seasonal housing markets, and transportation infrastructure across the American Sun Belt.

## you gotta know

- Refers primarily to retirees who maintain dual residences or travel via recreational vehicles to avoid harsh winter weather.
- Coined in North America in the early twentieth century, initially referring to migrant laborers who moved south for winter employment before shifting to seasonal leisure travelers and retirees.
- Concentrates demographically in southern destinations such as Florida, the desert regions of Arizona, the Gulf Coast of Texas, and the Coachella Valley in California.
- Drives seasonal economic booms in destination communities, impacting local hospitality, retail, healthcare systems, and seasonal rental housing.
- Canadian seasonal travelers in the United States represent a major sub-demographic, subject to specific visa limitations allowing stays of up to six months without tax residency implications.
- Inspired the title of Canadian singer Anne Murray's breakout 1969 hit song *"Snowbird"*, written by Gene MacLellan, which achieved international acclaim.
- Contrasts with the term "sunbirds," which occasionally describes desert dwellers who travel north during extreme summer heatwaves.
