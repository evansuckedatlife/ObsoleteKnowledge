## summary

**Wool** is a natural protein textile fiber obtained from the fleece of sheep and other animals such as cashmere goats, alpacas, and muskoxen. Composed predominantly of the fibrous protein keratin, its natural crimp, elasticity, and lanolin content made it one of humanity's earliest and most valuable domestic commodities. In quiz bowl, wool appears in questions on medieval European guild commerce, the English Enclosure Movement, the mechanization of textiles during the Industrial Revolution, and pastoral economies of the Southern Hemisphere.

## you gotta know

- Composed of the protein keratin, featuring microscopic overlapping scales and natural crimp that trap air to provide exceptional thermal insulation and natural flame resistance.
- Coated in raw form with lanolin, a natural water-repellent wax that must be removed through industrial scouring before the fiber is carded and spun.
- Formed the primary foundation of medieval English wealth, commemorated by the *Woolsack*, the historic seat of the Lord Speaker in the House of Lords in the [[united-kingdom]].
- The expansion of sheep pasture drove the *Enclosure Acts* in [[england]], transforming rural tenant farming and sparking bitter social upheavals described by Thomas More in *Utopia*.
- Industrial mechanization began largely in the textile sector during the [[industrial-revolution]], with inventions like James Hargreaves's spinning jenny and Richard Arkwright's water frame revolutionizing fiber processing.
- The Merino sheep breed, developed in medieval Spain under strict royal export bans, became the global gold standard for ultra-fine apparel fleece after spreading to Australia and New Zealand.
- Distinguished in garment production between *woolen* yarns, which are carded to retain fluffy air pockets, and *worsted* yarns, which are combed straight for smooth tailoring.

## connections

- [[england]] — kingdom whose medieval treasury and early modern parliament were fundamentally built upon the export of raw fleece.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — sparked the transition from manual cottage spinning to automated textile mills in northern Britain.
- [[british-empire]] — fostered extensive nineteenth-century pastoral colonies in Australia and New Zealand to feed domestic textile mills.
- [[roman-empire]] — utilized specialized fulling workshops (*fullonicae*) across Mediterranean cities to clean and thicken woven cloth.
- [[middle-east]] — ancient heartland of sheep domestication and the origin of specialized pastoral nomadic cultures.
