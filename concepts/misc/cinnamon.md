## summary

**Cinnamon** is an aromatic spice obtained from the inner bark of several tree species belonging to the genus *Cinnamomum*. Prized since antiquity across the *[[middle-east]]*, Egypt, and classical Europe for culinary, medicinal, and embalming purposes, it became one of the primary commercial drivers of global commerce during the *[[age-of-exploration]]*. Control over its cultivation and maritime trade routes fueled intense rivalries and early *[[colonialism]]* among European powers in South Asia.

## you gotta know

- Obtained by peeling and drying the inner bark of evergreen trees, which naturally curls into distinctive rolled quills during desiccation.
- Divided primarily between "true" Ceylon cinnamon (*Cinnamomum verum*, native to Sri Lanka) and the harsher, more common cassia varieties (*Cinnamomum cassia*) cultivated across China and Indonesia.
- Contains the organic compound cinnamaldehyde, which gives the bark its characteristic pungent aroma and sweet flavor, stimulating human *[[perception]]* via the chemical senses.
- Mentioned extensively in ancient texts, including the Hebrew Bible as an ingredient in holy anointing oil, and utilized by ancient Egyptians in mummification rites.
- Kept as a closely guarded trade secret by Arab merchants for centuries, who fabricated myths of giant raptors nesting in cinnamon trees to conceal Asian supply routes from European buyers.
- Fueled European territorial conquests in Ceylon (modern Sri Lanka), where the Portuguese seized the trade in the early 16th century before being ousted by the Dutch East India Company, which was later displaced by the *[[british-empire]]*.
- Often combined in classical confectionery and savory spice blends alongside other maritime commodities such as nutmeg and *[[clove]]*.

## connections

- [[clove]] — complementary global spice traded alongside cinnamon during early European maritime expansion.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — global era driven in substantial part by European competition to monopolize maritime spice routes.
- [[colonialism]] — imperial economic system that established direct colonial control over spice-producing islands in South and Southeast Asia.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial power that annexed Ceylon in 1796 to secure the world's most lucrative cinnamon plantations.
- [[middle-east]] — historical hub where overland merchants controlled and obscured cinnamon supply lines to the Mediterranean.
- [[five-senses]] — biological sensory systems engaged by the olfactory and gustatory stimulation of aromatic spice compounds.
