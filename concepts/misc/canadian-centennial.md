## summary

The **Canadian Centennial** was the nationwide celebration throughout 1967 commemorating the 100th anniversary of the formation of the Canadian Confederation under the *British North America Act* of 1867. Centered on the monumental *Expo 67* international exposition in Montreal, the anniversary catalyzed an unprecedented surge in domestic cultural infrastructure, public art, architectural modernism, and emerging Canadian national identity. It remains a hallmark milestone in twentieth-century Canadian civic history, symbolizing post-war optimism and national coming-of-age.

## you gotta know

- Marked the 100th anniversary of Confederation, when the colonies of Canada, New Brunswick, and Nova Scotia united into a self-governing dominion of the *[[british-empire]]* on July 1, 1867.
- Anchored by the 1967 International and Universal Exposition (*Expo 67*) held in Montreal, which welcomed over fifty million visits and became one of the most successful world's fairs in history.
- Prominently featured the *USA Pavilion* designed by *[[buckminster-fuller]]*, a landmark geodesic dome that captured the technological optimism of the era.
- Introduced Moshe Safdie's pioneering prefabricated modular housing complex *Habitat 67*, illustrating high-density architectural innovation under *[[modernism]]*.
- Commissioned the Centennial symbol designed by Stuart Ash, an iconic stylized maple leaf composed of eleven interlocking equilateral triangles representing the ten provinces and the territories.
- Initiated hundreds of federally subsidized "Centennial Projects" across the country, financing local libraries, performing arts centres, arenas, parks, and museums.
- Sparked the composition of the bilingual celebration anthem "A Place to Stand, a Place to Grow" (*Une place pour se tenir*) and Bobby Gimby's hit parade march "CA-NA-DA".
- Preceded major constitutional and cultural developments during the *[[cold-war]]*, including the formal adoption of the national flag in 1965 and the Official Languages Act of 1969.
