## summary

**Visual art** encompasses creative works made primarily for aesthetic contemplation and to be perceived through the eye: painting, sculpture, drawing, printmaking, photography, and other media. As a category distinct from performing or literary arts, visual art has flourished across all human cultures as a means of expression, ritual, documentation, and beauty-making, from cave paintings to contemporary installation art.

## you gotta know

- Broadly divided into traditional media (painting, sculpture, drawing, printmaking) and contemporary practices (photography, video, digital art, performance art, installation, conceptual art), continuously expanding with new technologies.
- Distinguishable from craft and decorative arts historically, though modern art theory has blurred these boundaries; the distinction between "fine art" and "applied art" emerged as an eighteenth-century Western concept tied to aesthetic value systems.
- Fundamental to the Renaissance—a cultural revolution centered in Italian cities like Florence that rediscovered perspective, anatomy, and classical ideals through visual media, revolutionizing how humans represented the world.
- Central to Modernism's radical experiments with abstraction, color theory, fragmentation, and the rejection of representational realism in movements like Cubism, Surrealism, and Abstract Expressionism, fundamentally challenging what art could be.
- Analyzed and theorized through formal properties (composition, color, form, line) and through meaning and iconography, making it a rich site for both creative practice and scholarly interpretation across disciplines.
- A vehicle for cultural memory, identity, and power; visual art reflects and shapes how societies understand themselves, their values, and their place in history, from temple reliefs to contemporary installations.
- Requires active interpretation and experience; unlike literature or music, visual art engages the eye directly and invites embodied presence, making the viewer's position in front of the work part of its meaning.

## connections

- [[painting]] — the medium of applied pigment on a surface; perhaps the most recognizable visual art form.
- [[sculpture]] — three-dimensional visual art working in stone, metal, clay, or other materials.
- [[drawing]] — foundational to training and spontaneous visual expression across cultures and eras.
- [[renaissance]] — a period of extraordinary visual innovation and cultural transformation centered on art.
- [[modernism]] — the aesthetic revolution that transformed visual art in the twentieth century.
- [[aesthetics]] — the philosophical framework for thinking about beauty, taste, and the visual.
