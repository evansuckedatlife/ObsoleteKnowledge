## summary

The **daiquiri** is a classic cocktail composed of white rum, fresh lime juice, and simple syrup, shaken vigorously with ice and served neat in a chilled coupe. Originating in southeastern Cuba near the turn of the twentieth century, it became one of the foundational six basic drinks codified in David Embury's *The Fine Art of Mixing Drinks*. Celebrated for its elegant balance of sweet and sour profiles, the drink became a cultural icon through its strong association with American expatriates and prominent literary figures.

## you gotta know

- Formulated with three essential ingredients following a strict sour ratio: white or light rum, freshly squeezed lime juice, and simple syrup or superfine sugar.
- Invented around 1898 in the Cuban mining town of Daiquirí near Santiago de Cuba, traditionally credited to American mining engineer *Jennings Cox*.
- Popularized in the [[united-states]] navy by medical officer *Lucius W. Johnson*, who introduced the recipe to the Army and Navy Club in Washington, D.C.
- Closely associated with legendary author *Ernest Hemingway*, who frequented the *El Floridita* bar in Havana and inspired a frozen, sugar-free variation featuring maraschino liqueur and grapefruit juice.
- Codified by the International Bartenders Association as an Official Cocktail within the "Contemporary Classics" category.
- Often degraded in mid-twentieth-century mass commercialization into blended frozen fruit slushies made with artificial strawberry syrup, diverging sharply from the original sour.
- Rose to massive domestic popularity in America during the administration of Franklin D. Roosevelt, aided by the Good Neighbor policy and wartime whiskey rationing.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — nation where naval officers, tourists, and literary expatriates promoted the cocktail into a mainstay.
- [[american-literature]] — artistic realm featuring iconic authors like *Ernest Hemingway* and *F. Scott Fitzgerald* who memorialized the beverage.
- [[spain]] — colonial power whose rum-distilling heritage in Cuba laid the structural foundation for the spirit used in the cocktail.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical era during which the Cuban embargo altered international rum commerce and transformed Caribbean tourism.
