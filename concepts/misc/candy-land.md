## summary

**Candy Land** is a classic American children's racing board game designed by retired schoolteacher *Eleanor Abbott* in 1948 while recovering in a polio hospital ward. Published by the *Milton Bradley Company* in 1949, the game requires no reading or numerical literacy, using colored cards to navigate players along a whimsical winding path toward the *Candy Castle*. It stands as an enduring cultural staple of mid-century tabletop gaming, early childhood socialization, and vivid nostalgic marketing.

## you gotta know

- Created by *Eleanor Abbott* in San Diego, California, specifically to entertain and comfort children quarantined during the devastating mid-century polio epidemics.
- Purchased and published in 1949 by *Milton Bradley*, rapidly becoming the company's best-selling board game and an institutional fixture of post-war nursery rooms.
- Operates entirely on pure chance with zero tactical decision-making: players draw colored cards from a deck and advance their pawns to the corresponding nearest colored square.
- Features prominent thematic waypoints along the board, including *Peppermint Forest*, *Gumdrop Pass*, *Lollipop Woods*, *Molasses Swamp*, and the culminating *Candy Castle*.
- Populated by colorful fairy-tale characters added or revised across successive decades, including *King Kandy*, *Lord Licorice*, *Queen Frostine*, *Princess Lolly*, *Gramma Nutt*, and *Gloppy the Molasses Monster*.
- Served as the subject of significant early Internet trademark jurisprudence in *Hasbro, Inc. v. Internet Entertainment Group, Ltd.* (1996), where a federal court protected the game's wholesome mark against adult web domain dilution.
- Inducted into the *National Toy Hall of Fame* at *The Strong National Museum of Play* in Rochester, New York, in 2005.
