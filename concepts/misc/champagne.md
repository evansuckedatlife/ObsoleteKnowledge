## summary

**Champagne** is a sparkling wine produced exclusively within the designated Champagne viticultural region of northeastern *[[france]]*, strictly regulated under the rules of the *Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée* (AOC). Crafted primarily from *Chardonnay*, *Pinot Noir*, and *Pinot Meunier* grapes, it undergoes a secondary fermentation in the bottle to produce carbonation, known as the *méthode champenoise* or traditional method. Worldwide, it serves as the archetypal luxury beverage synonymous with festivity, victory, state banquets, ship christenings, and New Year's toasts.

## you gotta know

- Legally protected under geographic indication laws and the 1919 Treaty of Versailles, prohibiting sparkling wines made outside the historic Champagne province from using the protected designation.
- Produced via the *méthode traditionnelle*, where still base wine undergoes a secondary fermentation inside the bottle initiated by adding a mixture of yeast and sugar (*liqueur de tirage*).
- Popular myth attributes its invention to the Benedictine monk Dom Pierre Pérignon of Hautvillers Abbey, who actually pioneered viticultural blending and thick-glass cellar management rather than carbonation.
- Developed into a clear, sediment-free sparkling drink through the invention of the riddling table (*pupitre*) and disgorgement by Madame Barbe-Nicole Clicquot (*Veuve Clicquot*).
- Classified into sweetness designations determined by the final sugar dosage (*dosage*), ranging from bone-dry *Brut Nature* and ubiquitous *Brut* to sweeter *Demi-Sec*.
- Served widely in flutes, tulips, and wide-rimmed coupes, the latter mythologized as having been modeled on the anatomy of Marie Antoinette or Madame de Pompadour.
- Integral to major holiday rites, especially midnight toasts during the *[[times-square-ball-drop]]* and festive gatherings across the globe.
