## summary

**Coup d'état**, frequently abbreviated as a **coup**, is the sudden, unlawful seizure or overthrow of an established government by a faction within the state apparatus, typically drawn from the military, security services, or political elites. Unlike broad popular revolutions or prolonged civil wars, it is executed through swift, calculated coercion targeted at command centres, telecommunication hubs, and heads of state. The phenomenon has served as a central mechanism of regime change throughout modern political history, fundamentally shaping post-colonial governance and geopolitical rivalry.

## you gotta know

- Translates literally from French as "blow of state" or "stroke of state," denoting a decisive action striking directly at the sovereign political authority.
- Exemplified historically by the *Coup of 18 Brumaire* in 1799, when *Napoleon Bonaparte* toppled the French Directory to establish the French Consulate, setting a modern precedent for military seizure of power.
- Codified into political theory by *Edward Luttwak* in his influential 1968 manual *Coup d'État: A Practical Handbook*, which analysed logistical imperatives like neutralising broadcasting studios and blocking key transit choke points.
- Termed a *Putsch* in German political vocabulary, immortalised by events such as the 1920 *Kapp Putsch* and *Adolf Hitler*'s failed 1923 *Beer Hall Putsch* in Munich.
- Distinguished in comparative politics as a *self-coup* or *autogolpe* when an incumbent, legally elected executive illegally dissolves the legislature and suspends judicial independence, as seen with *Alberto Fujimori* in Peru in 1992.
- Proliferated during the twentieth century across Latin America, Africa, and Asia, often exacerbated by proxy conflicts between superpowers supporting military juntas or orchestrating clandestine overthrows.
- Occurred prominently in events such as the 1953 Iranian coup d'état overthrowing *Mohammad Mosaddegh* and the 1973 Chilean coup d'état wherein General *Augusto Pinochet* toppled President *Salvador Allende*.

## connections

- [[france]] — historical origin of the term and setting for landmark overthrows, including *18 Brumaire* and the 1851 seizure of power by *Louis-Napoléon*.
- [[cold-war]] — global ideological confrontation that prompted frequent superpower-backed overthrows across the Global South.
- [[soviet-union]] — target of the failed August 1991 putsch by hardline communist officials that accelerated the collapse of the state.
- [[spain]] — site of frequent nineteenth-century *pronunciamientos* and the dramatic failed *23-F* parliamentary putsch attempt in 1981.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical civilization plagued by Praetorian Guard assassinations and military seizures of the imperial purple.
- [[ancient-greece]] — birthplace of philosophical treatises by *Aristotle* and *Plato* classifying factional coups and the rise of tyrannies.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — legal framework specifically structured with separation of powers and civilian command of the military to avert domestic executive usurpation.
