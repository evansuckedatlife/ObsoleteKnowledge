## summary

**Pico** (symbol **p**) is a metric unit prefix denoting a factor of $10^{-12}$, or one trillionth in the short scale system. Formally incorporated into the International System of Units (SI) at the 11th *General Conference on Weights and Measures* in 1960, it standardizes sub-microscopic measurements in atomic physics, electrical engineering, and chemistry. It is universally applied to quantities such as picofarads in circuit design, picoseconds in ultrafast laser spectroscopy, and picometres in molecular bond lengths.

## you gotta know

- Represents $10^{-12}$, or $0.000000000001$, positioned directly between *nano-* ($10^{-9}$) and *femto-* ($10^{-15}$).
- Etymology derives from the Italian word *piccolo*, meaning "small" or "tiny", reflecting its role in designating sub-atomic and microphysical scales.
- Established officially in 1960 by the 11th CGPM during the formal resolution establishing the SI metric framework.
- In chemistry and condensed matter physics, atomic bond lengths and crystal lattice constants are frequently expressed in picometres (pm), where 100 pm equals 1 ångström.
- In electrical engineering, circuit capacitance is predominantly quantified in microfarads, nanofarads, or picofarads (pF), historically dubbed "puffs" in colloquial laboratory slang.
- In chemical kinetics and biochemistry, picomolar (pM) concentrations measure ultra-potent hormones, neurotransmitters, and receptor affinities.
- Ultrafast chemistry, such as the transition-state dynamics pioneered by *Ahmed Zewail*, operates on picosecond ($10^{-12}\text{ s}$) and femtosecond scales.
