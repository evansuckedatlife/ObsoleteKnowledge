## summary

**Edam** is a semi-hard cow's milk cheese originating from the northern town of Edam in the Netherlands. Recognised worldwide by its distinctive spherical shape and bright red paraffin wax coating, it has historically served as one of Europe's most durable and widely exported food commodities. Its firm texture, mild nutty profile, and remarkable shelf life made it an indispensable staple during maritime trade and naval expeditions throughout the Age of Discovery.

## you gotta know

- Encased in a bright red paraffin wax rind for export, whereas local consumption within the Netherlands traditionally features an unwaxed natural yellow rind.
- Made predominantly from pasteurised semi-skimmed cow's milk, yielding a notably lower fat content and firmer body compared to its regional neighbour Gouda.
- Acquired widespread global fame during the fourteenth through eighteenth centuries because its round, durable balls cured thoroughly without spoiling on long sea voyages.
- Highly favored as standard sea rations across naval fleets during the era of European overseas expansion, trading companies, and worldwide [[colonialism]].
- Produced traditionally in spherical molds that give each cheese its characteristic cannonball appearance, allowing them to be rolled easily along loading ramps and ship holds.
- Legendarily employed as emergency ammunition by naval gunners when cannonball supplies ran out during seventeenth-century maritime skirmishes in South America.
- Matures with age from a mild, slightly salty, springy cheese into a dry, crumbly, and sharp delicacy prized in international culinary traditions.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — served as durable cargo and vital seafaring provisions across global Dutch and European trading networks.
- [[renaissance]] — expanded rapidly in production technique and municipal market prominence across northern Europe during this era.
- [[spain]] — frequently imported Dutch dairy during Habsburg rule and disseminated spherical cheeses to colonies throughout the Caribbean and the Philippines.
- [[france]] — inspired French cheesemakers to develop mimolette during seventeenth-century trade embargoes to emulate Dutch spherical imports.
- [[germany]] — maintained long-standing contiguous overland trade routes importing northern dairy staples across the Rhine valley.
- [[united-kingdom]] — competed maritime trade routes where preserved round cheeses supplied prolonged sea voyages across the Atlantic.
