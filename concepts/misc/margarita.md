## summary

The **Margarita** is a world-renowned cocktail composed of tequila, orange liqueur, and freshly squeezed lime juice, traditionally served in a distinctive stepped-diameter stem glass with a salted rim. Developing across Mexican resort towns and the southwestern borderlands of the [[united-states]] during the 1930s and 1940s, the drink evolved from earlier nineteenth-century sour cocktails. Today, it stands as the global benchmark for agave spirits and one of the most widely consumed mixed drinks in international hospitality.

## you gotta know

- Classic formulation adheres strictly to the traditional sour blueprint: three parts blanco or reposado tequila, two parts triple sec or premium orange liqueur such as Cointreau, and one part fresh lime juice, vigorously shaken with ice and strained.
- The salted rim serves an essential sensory function beyond presentation, as sodium ions suppress bitterness while enhancing perceived sweetness and bright citrus acidity across the palate.
- Several competing origin legends exist, most famously attributing the creation to Tijuana restaurateur Carlos *Danny Herrera* in 1938 for dancer Marjorie King, or to Dallas socialite *Margarita Sames*, who allegedly concocted it for guests at her Acapulco vacation estate in 1948.
- Etymologically, *margarita* is the Spanish word for "daisy," indicating that the drink almost certainly evolved as a regional Mexican adaptation of the classic American Daisy cocktail family, which combined brandy, citrus, and cordial syrup before tequila was substituted.
- The frozen variation was revolutionized in 1971 by Dallas restaurateur *Mariano Martinez*, who adapted a surplus soft-serve ice cream machine to continuously freeze and dispense blended margaritas without melting, an invention now preserved in the Smithsonian Institution.
- A prominent contemporary variation is *Tommy's Margarita*, conceived in San Francisco during the late 1980s by *Julio Bermejo*, which omits orange liqueur entirely in favor of local agave nectar to highlight the pure agave character of 100% blue agave tequila.
- While commonly presented in an eponymous broad-rimmed, stepped glass derived from the champagne coupe, bar professionals frequently serve it over fresh ice in an old-fashioned rocks glass.

## connections

- [[martini]] — peer classic cocktail of the twentieth century that relies on an austere spirit base balanced by an aromatic modifier.
- [[united-states]] — primary commercial market where post-Prohibition tourism and marketing transformed regional Mexican tequila drinks into an international phenomenon.
- [[spain]] — origin of the Spanish language from which the cocktail takes its floral name, as well as the distillation equipment introduced to the New World during colonization.
- [[france]] — source of classic triple sec and orange liqueurs like Cointreau and Grand Marnier, which are standard ingredients in the cocktail.
- [[perception]] — human sensory mechanics demonstrated by the interaction between the sodium rim and the tart citric acid of lime juice.
- [[new-york-city]] — major culinary and hospitality capital whose mid-century cocktail revival popularized standardized recipes for Mexican spirits.
