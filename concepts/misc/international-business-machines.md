## summary

**International Business Machines**, universally known as *IBM* or nicknamed *Big Blue*, is an American multinational technology corporation that played a defining role in the rise of the digital computer age. Founded in the early twentieth century from Herman Hollerith's tabulating machine enterprise, the company shaped twentieth-century computing, corporate enterprise systems, and scientific engineering. Its pioneering innovations span foundational mainframes, programming languages, and advanced artificial intelligence systems that reshaped modern commerce, industry, and governance.

## you gotta know

- Formed in 1911 as the *Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company* (*CTR*) through a merger engineered by Charles Ranlett Flint, which brought together Herman Hollerith's *Tabulating Machine Company* with manufacturers of computing scales and time recorders.
- Guided to global prominence by long-serving chief executive *Thomas J. Watson Sr.*, who established the company's famous motto *"THINK"*, cultivated an iconic corporate sales culture of crisp dark suits and white shirts, and formally renamed the enterprise *International Business Machines* in 1924.
- Revolutionized commercial computing in 1964 by launching the *System/360*, a family of mainframe computers that introduced interchangeable peripheral equipment and upward-compatible software architectures, cementing enterprise dominance.
- Spearheaded landmark contributions to software engineering and computer science, including John Backus's development of the *FORTRAN* programming language, Edgar F. Codd's creation of the relational database model, and the standard *SQL* query language.
- Standardized personal computing in 1981 with the launch of the *IBM Personal Computer* (*IBM 5150*), establishing the open hardware platform that defined the PC compatible era using Intel processors and Microsoft DOS.
- Demonstrated breakthroughs in artificial intelligence and supercomputing through *Deep Blue*, which defeated world chess champion Garry Kasparov in 1997, and *Watson*, which defeated champion human contestants on the game show *Jeopardy!* in 2011.
- Maintained a world-renowned corporate research division whose scientists garnered multiple Nobel Prizes in Physics for milestones such as the invention of the scanning tunneling microscope and high-temperature superconductivity.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — corporate headquarters founded and anchored in New York, spearheading twentieth-century American technological hegemony.
- [[cold-war]] — supplied advanced military radar computing systems, such as the *AN/FSQ-7* developed for the Semi-Automatic Ground Environment (*SAGE*) air defense network.
- [[space-age]] — engineered guidance computers and trajectory data processing systems for NASA's Saturn V rocket and Apollo missions.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — evolved directly from mechanized business tabulating equipment designed to catalog nineteenth-century census and industrial labor metrics.
- [[modernism]] — adopted a sleek corporate design aesthetic championed by industrial designer Eliot Noyes and graphic designer Paul Rand, creator of its iconic striped logo.
- [[world-war-ii]] — contributed massive manufacturing capacity to Allied wartime efforts, producing rifles and bombsights alongside military logistics tabulators.
