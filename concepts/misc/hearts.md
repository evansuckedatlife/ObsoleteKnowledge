## summary

**Hearts** (often known colloquially by variants such as **Black Lady** or **Black Maria**) is an evasion-style trick-taking card game played by four players using a standard fifty-two-card deck. Developed in the [[united-states]] during the late nineteenth century as an evolution of European ancestors, the game requires competitors to avoid capturing tricks laden with penalty points. It achieved global ubiquity and enduring cultural resonance in the late twentieth century after being bundled across desktop computers worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Aims principally at evasion, where each captured card of the heart suit adds one penalty point and the dreaded queen of spades adds thirteen penalty points.
- Features the dramatic alternative gambit known as *shooting the moon*, wherein a player boldly captures all twenty-six penalty points to award zero to themselves and twenty-six points to every opponent.
- Begins each hand with a strategic three-card pass across the table, cycling sequentially to the left, to the right, directly across, and concluding with a *keeper* hand where no cards are passed.
- Requires the player holding the two of clubs to lead the opening trick of the round, strictly forbidding the play of penalty cards on that initial trick unless no other suit can be followed.
- Enforces the *breaking hearts* convention, which dictates that players cannot lead a heart card until a heart or the queen of spades has already been discarded on an earlier trick.
- Evolved in the post-war era of the [[american-civil-war]] from the eighteenth-century French parlor game *reversis*, gradually adopting modern penalty structures and scoring caps.
- Attained immense mainstream popularity after being packaged directly with graphical operating systems during the [[cold-war]] tech boom, turning casual office workers into avid tacticians.
