## summary

**BeaverTails** (in French, *queues de castor*) are a style of Canadian fried-dough pastry hand-stretched to resemble the flat tail of a beaver, a national emblem of Canada. Typically made from a yeast dough, deep-fried in vegetable oil, and served hot with varied sweet or savory toppings, the pastry was popularized by Grant and Pam Hooker in Killaloe, Ontario, in 1978. It has evolved from a rural community festival snack into an internationally recognized symbol of Canadian popular culture and tourism.

## you gotta know

- Created commercially by *Grant and Pam Hooker*, who commercialized an old family recipe passed down from German-Canadian homestead baking traditions.
- Hand-stretched before frying into an elongated, paddle-shaped oval designed to mirror the tail of the North American beaver.
- The classic and most traditional presentation, known as the "Killaloe Sunrise," is dusted with cinnamon sugar and served with a freshly squeezed lemon wedge.
- Became an iconic fixture of winter recreation in Ottawa after opening a permanent kiosk along the frozen Rideau Canal Skateway in 1980.
- Attracted international diplomatic press coverage in 2009 when United States President *Barack Obama* stopped at an Ottawa market stand during his first foreign presidential visit, leading to the creation of the chocolate-hazelnut and maple "ObamaTail."
- Traces culinary heritage to traditional Canadian voyageur and indigenous camp breads cooked over open fires, such as fried bannock and dough fried on hot axe blades.
- The company BeaverTails Canada Inc. operates franchise stands in multiple countries, including ski resorts and tourist destinations across the *[[united-states]]*, Japan, and France.
