## summary

A **sacrifice** in chess is a tactical or positional deliberate surrender of material, typically a piece or pawn, in exchange for a non-material compensation such as tempo, space, king exposure, or checkmate. Regarded as the aesthetic pinnacle of chess artistry, sacrifices distinguish routine calculation from profound strategic foresight. In quiz bowl, sacrifices are frequently clued via legendary historical games, named opening gambits, and the distinctive tactical paradigms of world champions such as *Mikhail Tal* and *Garry Kasparov*.

## you gotta know

- Chess theoreticians divide sacrifices into *tactical* (or real/sham) sacrifices, which yield a concrete, calculable forcing sequence like immediate checkmate or net material recovery, and *positional* (or speculative) sacrifices, which furnish prolonged pressure, spatial control, or initiative without immediate material retrieval.
- The *Greek gift sacrifice* is a classical tactical motif involving a bishop sacrificing itself on the h7 (or h2) square to strip away the defensive pawn cover of a castled enemy king, opening the file for an assault by a knight and queen.
- In the *Immortal Game* of 1851 played in London between *Adolf Anderssen* and *Lionel Kieseritzky*, Anderssen sacrificed a bishop, both rooks, and his queen to deliver a picturesque checkmate using only three minor pieces.
- *Paul Morphy* elevated the sacrifice to a fundamental principle of open games, famously surrendering pieces to accelerate development and blast open center lines against stalled kings, epitomized by the 1858 *Opera Game* in Paris.
- The eighth World Chess Champion, *Mikhail Tal*, the *Magician from Riga*, achieved worldwide acclaim in the mid-twentieth century for unleashing intuitive, volatile sacrifices that threw opponents into profound defensive complications.
- The modern *Evans Gambit* and the *King's Gambit* represent systematic sacrifices of pawns during the opening phase to seize control of central squares and accelerate piece development.
- A distinctive defensive or drawing sacrifice is the *desperado*, where an already doomed piece sacrifices itself on the highest possible target or forces a stalemate by checking the enemy king relentlessly.
