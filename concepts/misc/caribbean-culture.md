## summary

**Caribbean culture** is the distinctive fusion of African, European, Indigenous, and Asian traditions forged in the islands of the Caribbean through centuries of colonialism, slavery, and exchange. Born from violent displacement and creative resistance, it encompasses music, language, ritual, and cuisine that reflect the region's complex legacy and continues to shape global popular culture.

## you gotta know

- Emerged from the collision of West African, European colonial, and Indigenous Caribbean societies, with significant later migration from Asia (indentured labour).
- The sugar plantation system and the transatlantic slave trade were the engine: millions of Africans were forcibly brought to the Caribbean, structuring society around racial hierarchy and labour.
- Language is creolized: English-, French-, and Spanish-based creoles fuse European grammar with African phonology and vocabulary, reflecting the linguistic reality of multilingual colonialism.
- Music and dance traditions (calypso, reggae, salsa, merengue) blend African rhythms, European instruments, and Caribbean innovation—exported globally as markers of Caribbean identity.
- Food reflects the layering of cultures: African preparations meet European and Asian ingredients, indigenous crops, and colonial trade goods, creating distinct regional cuisines.
- Religious practice often melds Catholicism or Protestantism with African ancestral veneration and Indigenous spirituality—exemplified in Haitian Vodou, Trinidadian Orisha, and other syncretic traditions.
- Carnival and related festivals (Trinidadian Carnival, Jamaica's Jonkonnu) have roots in enslaved peoples' limited freedom during holidays, transformed into explosive celebrations of cultural pride.
- Literary and artistic renaissance: figures like Derek Walcott, Kamau Brathwaite, and reggae musicians transformed Caribbean culture into global influence, especially during late 20th-century decolonization.
- Island heterogeneity: despite shared histories, each island or archipelago (Jamaica, Trinidad, Haiti, Barbados, Puerto Rico) has its own dialect, musical idiom, and historical trajectory.
- Caribbean culture's global impact is immense: reggae, dancehall, and other musical forms; Caribbean writers in English diaspora literature; culinary traditions spreading worldwide.
