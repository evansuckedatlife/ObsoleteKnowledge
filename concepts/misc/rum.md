## summary

**Rum** is a distilled alcoholic beverage made from sugarcane byproducts, such as molasses or direct sugarcane juice, via fermentation and distillation. Originating extensively in seventeenth-century Caribbean plantations, the spirit became a central commodity in maritime trade, oceanic naval traditions, and early Atlantic commerce. Its production and consumption shaped economic systems across European empires and fuelled pivotal political disputes throughout the colonial Americas.

## you gotta know

- Traditional Caribbean production developed during the seventeenth century as enslaved laborers on sugarcane plantations discovered that molasses, a byproduct of the sugar refining process, could be fermented into alcohol.
- The beverage formed a vital leg of the historical triangular trade route, connecting West African slave trading, Caribbean sugar production, and distilleries located in colonial New England in the *[[united-states]]*.
- The *British Royal Navy* famously maintained a daily ration of rum for sailors, known as the *tot*, which was commonly diluted with water and lime juice to create *grog* to combat scurvy.
- The British Parliament's passage of the *Sugar Act* of 1764 substantially disrupted the importation of molasses, inciting widespread smuggling and fueling colonial unrest leading to the *American Revolution*.
- Distinct styles include English-style dark rums distilled in pot stills, Spanish-style light rums pioneered by producers in *Cuba* and *[[spain]]*, and French *rhum agricole* distilled directly from fresh cane juice in overseas territories of *[[france]]*.
- In *Australia*, disputes surrounding the illicit distribution of the spirit led to the 1808 *Rum Rebellion*, the only successful armed overthrow of an Australian government, deposing Governor *William Bligh*.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — plantation economies and colonial resource extraction drove global production of the liquor.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — maritime trade networks established the oceanic routes used to transport spirits and sugar.
- [[british-empire]] — naval fleets made daily rations of the spirit an iconic military tradition for centuries.
- [[united-states]] — colonial New England operated major commercial distilleries whose trade taxation spurred political revolt.
- [[spain]] — colonial enterprises introduced sugarcane cultivation across Caribbean territories and developed light distillation styles.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — mechanized sugar processing and continuous column stills transformed industrial distillation capacity.
