## summary

**Bentley**, officially registered as **Bentley Motors** and historically styled as **Bentley Motors Limited**, is an iconic British automotive manufacturer celebrated for high-performance grand tourers, bespoke saloons, and an illustrious endurance racing heritage. Founded in London in 1919 by engineer *Walter Owen Bentley*, the marque rose to international acclaim by dominating the grueling *24 Hours of Le Mans* throughout the 1920s. Characterised by robust mechanical engineering, lavish interior leather and veneer craftsmanship, and the patronage of charismatic British sportsmen, the company remains a premier symbol of industrial luxury in the [[united-kingdom]].

## you gotta know

- Founded in Cricklewood, North London, in 1919 by engineer *W. O. Bentley*, whose early career pioneered aluminium pistons for rotary aero engines such as the *BR1* during [[world-war-i]].
- Captured five historic victories at the *24 Hours of Le Mans* in [[france]] during 1924, 1927, 1928, 1929, and 1930, demonstrating unprecedented endurance with flagship machines such as the *Speed Six* and *4½ Litre*.
- Famously driven and championed by the "Bentley Boys," a daring cadre of wealthy amateur racers, aristocrats, and aviators led by South African mining heir *Woolf Barnato*, who personally financed the marque through financial crises.
- Rival carmaker *Ettore Bugatti* famously derided the immense physical weight and industrial scale of Bentley's green racing machines by dubbing them "the fastest lorries in the world."
- Characterised visually by its famous "Winged B" bonnet mascot, designed by artist *F. Gordon Crosby*, alongside massive circular headlamps and a classic wire-mesh radiator matrix designed to deflect track gravel.
- Sir Henry "Tim" Birkin famously defied the founder's preference for naturally aspirated displacement by developing the supercharged *Blower Bentley*, an audacious though mechanically fragile racing icon.
- Absorbed by rival *Rolls-Royce* during the Great Depression in 1931, leading production to relocate to Crewe, Cheshire, after [[world-war-ii]] where it was eventually acquired by [[germany]]'s *Volkswagen Group* in 1998.

## connections

- [[united-kingdom]] — the sovereign nation where the manufacturer was founded, established its sporting lore, and crafted its luxury grand tourers.
- [[england]] — home to both the marque's initial workshops in Cricklewood and its historic, continuous post-war manufacturing center in Crewe.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the sweeping transformation of British manufacturing, metallurgy, and mechanical precision that made luxury automotive production possible.
- [[world-war-i]] — the military conflict in which founder *W. O. Bentley* designed military aero engines, perfecting engine tolerances applied to post-war sports cars.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global war during which the Crewe facility was originally erected as an underground shadow factory for *Merlin* aircraft engines before automobile assembly began.
- [[france]] — the setting of the Circuit de la Sarthe, where Bentley's team established its worldwide racing legend through five early endurance victories.
- [[germany]] — origin of corporate parent *Volkswagen Group*, which modernised the marque and financed its 2003 Le Mans victory with the *Speed 8*.
