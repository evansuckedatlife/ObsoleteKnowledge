## summary

**AMD**, legally known as **Advanced Micro Devices**, is an American multinational semiconductor corporation based in Santa Clara, California. Founded in 1969 by *Jerry Sanders* and alumni of Fairchild Semiconductor, the firm emerged as the chief architect of competition in the microchip industry against *Intel*. It transformed modern high-performance computing through the development of the 64-bit extension to the x86 architecture and the innovative Zen microarchitecture.

## you gotta know

- Co-founded in Sunnyvale, California, in May 1969 by former Fairchild Semiconductor executive *Jerry Sanders* and seven colleagues, entering the burgeoning Silicon Valley market.
- Signed an indispensable technology cross-licensing agreement with *Intel* in 1982 to act as a second-source manufacturer of 8086 processors for the *IBM Personal Computer*.
- Pioneered the backwards-compatible 64-bit extension known as *AMD64* (or x86-64) with the Opteron and Athlon 64 processors in 2003, outmaneuvering Intel's competing Itanium architecture and establishing the dominant modern desktop computing standard.
- Produced the celebrated *Athlon* processor in 1999, which broke the historic one-gigahertz CPU frequency barrier ahead of all competitors in March 2000.
- Completed the landmark acquisition of Canadian graphics processor manufacturer *ATI Technologies* in 2006, creating the unified Radeon graphics processing unit line.
- Spun off its capital-intensive chip manufacturing foundries in 2009 into an independent commercial foundry named *GlobalFoundries*, adopting a fabless corporate design model.
- Appointed electrical engineer *Lisa Su* as chief executive officer in 2014, leading an extraordinary corporate turnaround centered on high-core-count *Zen* processors and Epyc data-center chips.
