## summary

A **DDoS attack**, or *distributed denial-of-service attack*, is a malicious cyber offensive aimed at disrupting the normal traffic and availability of a targeted server, network, or service by overwhelming it with a flood of illegitimate Internet traffic. By coordinating distributed botnets of compromised computers, servers, and *Internet of Things* (*IoT*) devices, attackers saturate the bandwidth and compute capacity of their target without needing to breach its internal data storage. It represents one of the most persistent and disruptive challenges to contemporary global digital infrastructure, telecommunications, and state security.

## you gotta know

- Operates by distributing the generation of attack traffic across thousands or millions of globally dispersed, compromised host devices known collectively as a *botnet*.
- Classified broadly into three primary categories: volumetric attacks that saturate bandwidth, protocol attacks that exhaust stateful server resources, and application-layer attacks that exhaust web server computation.
- Often leverages *amplification* and *reflection* vectors, using unauthenticated public protocols such as *DNS*, *NTP*, or *SSDP* to send forged requests that return disproportionately massive responses to the victim's spoofed IP address.
- Historical milestone include the February 2000 attacks orchestrated by the teenage Canadian hacker *Mafiaboy* (*Michael Calce*), which crippled prominent websites including *Yahoo!*, *eBay*, *CNN*, and *Amazon*.
- The 2016 *Mirai* botnet marked a major paradigm shift by weaponising hundreds of thousands of default-credentialed consumer *IoT* devices to launch historic attacks exceeding one terabit per second against the *Dyn* DNS provider.
- Regularly deployed in modern geopolitical conflicts, state-sponsored cyber warfare, extortion campaigns, and activist disruptions to silence critical public infrastructure or news outlets.
