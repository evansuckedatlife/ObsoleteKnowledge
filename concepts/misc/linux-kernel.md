## summary

The **Linux kernel** is a free, open-source monolithic Unix-like operating system kernel created in 1991 by Finnish computer science student *Linus Torvalds*. When integrated with userland utilities from *Richard Stallman*'s GNU Project, it forms the foundational architecture powering the modern Internet, cloud computing servers, scientific supercomputers, and mobile devices running Android. In computing history, it stands as the most prominent triumph of decentralized collaborative engineering and the defining touchstone of the copyleft software philosophy.

## you gotta know

- Announced on August 25, 1991, in a famous Usenet post to `comp.os.minix` where *Linus Torvalds* described it as "just a hobby, won't be big and professional like gnu."
- Sparked the famous *Tanenbaum–Torvalds debate* in 1992, in which computer science professor *Andrew Tanenbaum* declared microkernels the modern design and dismissed monolithic architecture as obsolete.
- Adopted the GNU General Public License version 2 in 1992, a pivotal legal decision ensuring source code reciprocity that Torvalds later described as the best decision he ever made.
- Takes as its official mascot *Tux*, a chubby and contented penguin designed by *Larry Ewing* after Torvalds was reportedly nipped by a fairy penguin during a visit to a zoo in Canberra.
- Prompted the development of the distributed version control system *Git*, which Torvalds created in 2005 specifically to manage kernel patch collaboration after proprietary tool BitKeeper revoked free access.
- Operates under a monolithic design, meaning the entire operating system kernel executes in privileged supervisor mode, though it utilizes loadable kernel modules for hardware drivers.
- Powers all five hundred of the world's fastest supercomputers on the TOP500 list, representing complete dominance in high-performance scientific computing.
- Serves as the modified underpinning of Google's *Android* mobile platform, establishing it as the most widely deployed operating system kernel by volume on consumer devices.
