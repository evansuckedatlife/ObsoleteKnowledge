## summary

**Gouda** is a renowned sweet, creamy Dutch cheese manufactured from cow's milk, characterized by its traditional paraffin wax rind and springy to crystalline texture depending on maturity. Originating in the medieval agricultural lowlands of the Netherlands, it ranks among the oldest and most commercially significant cheeses in global commerce. For academic quiz competitions, it is recognized for its unique "curd washing" manufacturing technique, its historical market monopoly in South Holland, and its maturation into intense caramel flavors rich in crunchy calcium lactate crystals.

## you gotta know

- Named not because it was manufactured within the municipal boundary of *Gouda*, but because the city historically held exclusive feudal market rights (*waag*) in the province of *South Holland*, forcing regional dairy farmers to weigh and trade their produce at its central marketplace.
- Relies upon the distinctive technique of "washing the curd", wherein a portion of the whey is drained and replaced with warm water, rinsing away residual lactose to inhibit lactic acid fermentation and produce a characteristically sweet, mild profile.
- Distinguishes age classifications from young (*jong*), matured for around four weeks, up to very old (*overjarig*), aged for well over a year into a brittle, crumbly paste packed with tyrosine and calcium lactate crystals.
- Traditional artisanal farmhouse versions made from unpasteurized raw milk are legally distinguished under European protection as *Boerenkaas* ("farmer's cheese").
- While the generic term *Gouda* has become widespread globally, authenticity is legally defended under European Union law via the Protected Geographical Indication (*PGI*) designation *Gouda Holland*.
- Encased commonly in yellow or red paraffin wax coatings for younger varieties, whereas long-aged wheels typically bear a deep black wax rind to signify maturity.
- Documented in Dutch civil records as far back as 1184 CE, making it one of the oldest recorded cheeses still prepared according to historical principles.
