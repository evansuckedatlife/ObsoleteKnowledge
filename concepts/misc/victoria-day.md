## summary

**Victoria Day** is a Canadian federal statutory holiday celebrated annually on the penultimate Monday preceding May 25 in honour of the birthday of *Queen Victoria*. Established initially in the Province of Canada in 1845, the observance evolved from royal imperial celebrations under the [[british-empire]] into the unofficial marker of the beginning of the summer season across Canada. It remains a uniquely Canadian civic holiday that simultaneously commemorates the reigning Canadian monarch's official birthday.

## you gotta know

- Celebrated on the last Monday preceding May 25, ensuring a perpetual three-day weekend often marked by camping, fireworks displays, and cottage openings.
- Originally enacted by the legislature of the Province of Canada in 1845 to commemorate the May 24 birthday of *Queen Victoria*, who reigned over the expanding [[british-empire]] and signed the British North America Act of 1867.
- Known colloquially in Canadian English as *May Two-Four*, a double pun referring to the monarch's birthdate of May 24 and the popular Canadian term for a twenty-four pack of beer.
- Observed in the province of Quebec officially as *National Patriots' Day* (*Journée nationale des patriotes*) to honour the 1837 Lower Canada Rebellion against British rule, having previously been known there as *Fête de la Reine*.
- Serves as the official birthday in Canada for the reigning monarch regardless of their actual date of birth, continuing an imperial royal tradition imported from the [[united-kingdom]].
- Governed federally by the Holidays Act, though provincial statutory holiday regulations vary, with some Atlantic provinces treating it as an optional or non-statutory civic day.
- Signals heavy domestic seasonal travel across Canada, frequently driving peak spring holiday passenger traffic for major domestic carriers like [[westjet]].
