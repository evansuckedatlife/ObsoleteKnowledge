## summary

**Mille Bornes** is a classic French card game designed in 1954 by Arthur Dujardin that simulates an endurance automobile road race across the national highway network. Players or teams compete to travel a cumulative distance of exactly 1,000 kilometers by playing numerical distance cards while simultaneously impeding their rivals with vehicular hazard cards. Celebrated for its unique graphic iconography and tactical cutthroat interactions, it remains one of the most commercially successful tabletop games ever exported from *[[france]]*.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1954 by French game designer Arthur Dujardin, deriving its underlying mechanics from the earlier 1906 American automotive card game *Touring*.
- Takes its name from the cylindrical stone distance markers, or *bornes*, historically positioned along the arterial highways of *[[france]]*.
- Centers on accumulating distance cards representing 25, 50, 75, 100, and 200 kilometers until a player reaches the target distance of 1,000 kilometers.
- Features a suite of malicious hazard cards—*Out of Gas*, *Flat Tire*, *Accident*, *Speed Limit*, and *Stop*—which can only be resolved by their corresponding remedy cards.
- Includes four powerful "Safety" cards: *Extra Tank*, *Puncture-Proof*, *Driving Ace*, and *Right of Way*, which grant permanent immunity against specific categories of disruption.
- Famous for the *Coup-fourré* maneuver, a sudden counterstroke executed when an attacked player immediately plays the matching safety card directly out of turn for substantial bonus points.
- Bridges the opening deployment of vehicles and the endgame sprint with a tactical phase mirroring a classic tabletop **[[middle-game]]**, where hand management and hazard distribution dictate momentum.
- Reflects the post-World War II popularization of private automobile ownership and consumer leisure travel across Western Europe during the early *[[cold-war]]*.
