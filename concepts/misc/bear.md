## summary

A **bear** is a large carnivorous or omnivorous mammal of the family *Ursidae*, found widespread across the Americas, Europe, and Asia. In world folklore, heraldry, and modern international athletics, bears serve as formidable symbols of raw physical power, maternal guardianship, and rugged wilderness. For quiz bowl competitors, bears frequently anchor clues spanning mammalian biology, indigenous mythologies, astronomical constellations, and political iconography across Eurasia and North America.

## you gotta know

- Classified scientifically in the family *Ursidae*, containing eight living species ranging from the herbivorous giant panda to the carnivorous polar bear of the Arctic.
- Possesses a non-retractile claw structure and plantigrade locomotion, walking flat on the soles of their feet in a manner similar to human gait.
- Serves as the namesake for prominent northern celestial constellations, notably *Ursa Major* and *Ursa Minor*, crucial in [[astronomy]] for locating the North Star.
- Associated with the linguistic taboo deformation phenomenon in Indo-European languages, where words like the Germanic *bear* (meaning *the brown one*) replaced the sacred ancestral Proto-Indo-European root.
- Embraced as an enduring national and geopolitical mascot, famously personifying the [[soviet-union]] and modern Russia in centuries of satirical and propaganda cartoons.
- Adopted by major sporting organizations for international branding, including polar bear mascots *Hidy and Howdy* created for the 1988 Calgary Winter Games overseen by the [[canadian-olympic-association]].
- Indigenous peoples of northern [[japan]], the Ainu, historically practiced the ritual sacrifice and spiritual worship of brown bears in the elaborate *iomante* ceremony.
- Cultural ubiquity extends into modern digital communication, where standard pictograms represent the mammal across platforms as a prominent [[emoji]].

## connections

- [[canadian-olympic-association]] — oversaw the 1988 Calgary Winter Games, whose official mascots were the celebrated twin polar bears *Hidy and Howdy*.
- [[astronomy]] — references the species directly in the classic circumpolar asterisms *Ursa Major* and *Ursa Minor*.
- [[perception]] — relates to biological sensory specializations, as *Ursidae* rely primarily on an extraordinary olfactory sense superior to canine tracking.
- [[soviet-union]] — utilized the animal extensively in political satire and official iconography, exemplified by the bear mascot *Misha* at the 1980 Moscow Olympics.
- [[japan]] — forms the setting for the indigenous Ainu bear-sending ritual and the habitat of the Ussuri brown bear in Hokkaido.
- [[united-states]] — features the grizzly on the California state flag and inspired the ubiquitous stuffed teddy bear named after President Theodore Roosevelt.
- [[emoji]] — encodes multiple official Unicode pictographs celebrating the animal, including the standard bear face and polar bear icons.
