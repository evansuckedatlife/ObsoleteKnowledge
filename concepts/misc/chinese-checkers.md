## summary

**Chinese checkers**, originally patented under the name **Sternhalma**, is a tabletop strategy board game for two to six players played upon a six-pointed star grid. Despite its modern English moniker, the game did not originate in China nor did it evolve from traditional checkers, but was developed in Germany in 1892 as a hexagonal geometric refinement of the earlier American game *Halma*. Celebrated in recreational history for its intuitive mechanics, spatial elegance, and lack of player elimination, it became a runaway commercial sensation in the interwar United States through exoticized marketing campaigns.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1892 in [[germany]] by toy designer *Robert Freese*, who secured a patent for the game under the German name *Sternhalma*, combining the German word for star (*Stern*) with the name of its game predecessor.
- Directly adapted from *Halma*, an 1883 board game created on a square 16-by-16 checkerboard by *George Howard Monks*, an American thoracic surgeon and Harvard Medical School professor who derived the title from the classical Greek term for a jump.
- Rebranded with its famous Western misnomer "Chinese Checkers" in 1928 by the American toy company *Pressman Toy Corporation*, which sought to market the product by exploiting widespread public fascination with Orientalist aesthetics and Asian parlor mystique.
- Utilizes a distinctive board configured as a symmetrical hexagram containing 121 individual holes or indented recesses, representing an elegant practical exercise in discrete [[geometry]] and graph traversal.
- Starts with players arranging sets of ten matching marbles (or wooden pegs) in their respective triangular star points, with the universal objective of racing all ten pieces across the board to occupy the opposite destination star point.
- Features dynamic jumping mechanics allowing players to execute consecutive hopping sequences across single occupied adjacent spaces in any direction, enabling rapid traversal without capturing or removing opponent pieces from play.
- Popularized the strategic phenomenon of building "ladders" or cooperative hopping chains across the central board, while simultaneously requiring players to block opposing players from lingering in home territories to delay an opponent's victory.
- Translated into East Asian languages retrospectively under the name *Tiaoqi* ("jump chess"), completing an ironic full-circle cultural journey as an American-marketed German game was adopted across Asian households.

- [[germany]] — European nation where the game was originally created and patented in 1892 as *Sternhalma*.
- [[united-states]] — nation where interwar toy manufacturers successfully rebranded the game with its famous Orientalist title.
- [[geometry]] — branch of mathematics embodied in the game's symmetrical hexagram star grid and coordinate network pathways.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical era whose advances in pressed tin lithography, injection molding, and glass marble manufacturing enabled cheap mass distribution.

- [[japan]] — East Asian country that integrated *Sternhalma* into family board gaming traditions alongside *Go* and *Shogi*.
