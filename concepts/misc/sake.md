## summary

**Sake**, known natively in [[japan]] as *nihonshu*, is a traditional brewed alcoholic beverage produced by fermenting polished rice. Distinct from both distilled spirits and simple fruit wines, its manufacturing process relies on multiple parallel fermentation mediated by Aspergillus mold and specialized brewing yeasts. Revered in Shinto ritual, royal court ceremonies, and daily culinary culture, it is a staple topic in cultural history, chemistry of brewing, and Japanese literature.

## you gotta know

- Brewed from polished rice using a unique process called multiple parallel fermentation (*heiko fukuhakko*), in which the mold *koji-kin* (*Aspergillus oryzae*) converts starches into fermentable glucose simultaneously while yeast ferments that sugar into ethanol.
- Classified into official premium grades according to the milling ratio (*seimaibuai*), which measures the percentage of the rice grain remaining after polishing off outer proteins and fats; *ginjo* requires polishing to at least sixty percent, while ultra-premium *daiginjo* requires polishing down to fifty percent or lower.
- Distinguished between *junmai* varieties, which are brewed purely from rice, water, yeast, and *koji* without any additives, and non-*junmai* styles such as *honjozo*, to which small quantities of pure distilled brewer's alcohol are introduced to extract aromatic compounds.
- Traditionally decanted from a ceramic carafe called a *tokkuri* and consumed from small cups called *ochoko* or flat ceremonial dishes called *sakazuki*, or served overflowing into a square cedar box called a *masu*.
- Central to Shinto religious traditions as *o-miki*, where it is offered to the *kami*, used in wedding vows through the three-by-three cup-sharing ritual *san-san-kudo*, and ritually celebrated during [[shogatsu]] via the spiced medicinal drink *toso*.
- Features in classical Japanese mythology within the *Kojiki*, where the storm god *Susanoo* slays the eight-headed serpent *Yamata no Orochi* by intoxicating each of its heads with eight-fold refined sake before dismembering it.
- Supervised by a master brewer known as a *toji*, who directs seasonal brewery artisans (*kurabito*) affiliated with historic regional guilds such as the *Tanba*, *Nanbu*, and *Echigo* schools.

## connections

- [[japan]] — national beverage deeply woven into its mythology, religious rituals, and gastronomic history.
- [[shogatsu]] — featured prominently during Japanese New Year celebrations through the ceremonial spiced draught *toso*.
- [[five-senses]] — evaluated by professional tasters on balance across visual clarity, aroma profile (*ginjo-ka*), acidity, sweetness, and umami mouthfeel.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — production historically aligned with cold winter months following the autumn rice harvest under traditional seasonal calendars.
- [[world-war-ii]] — wartime shortages of rice led to the widespread government-mandated introduction of glucose and distilled alcohol stretching, giving rise to modern *sanzo-shu* regulations.
