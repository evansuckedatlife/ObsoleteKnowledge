## summary

**Small World** is a fantasy-themed designer board game created by Belgian designer *Philippe Keyaerts* and published by *Days of Wonder* in 2009. A reimplementation of Keyaerts's 1999 title *Vinci*, it features players deploying whimsical combinations of fantasy races and special powers across a cramped map to conquer territories. The game is celebrated for its dynamic decline mechanic, in which players must abandon overextended civilisations to select fresh combinations before their scoring stalls.

## you gotta know

- Designed by Belgian game designer *Philippe Keyaerts* and published in 2009 by *Days of Wonder*, a publisher based in *France* and the *United States*.
- Mechanically refines Keyaerts's earlier historical civilization game *Vinci*, substituting European tribes with modular fantasy races and powers.
- The central mechanism pairs race banners with special power badges randomly, generating unique combinations such as *Flying Sorcerers* or *Commando Ratmen*.
- The game board is intentionally too small to accommodate all tokens comfortably, ensuring constant conflict and territory turnover.
- Players score victory coins at the end of each turn based on the number of regions occupied, kept face-down to obscure precise scores.
- Recognised for its graceful decline mechanism, where players place their active race into decline and forgo normal actions to draft a new combination.
- Won the prestigious *Games Magazine Game of the Year* award in 2010 and spawned numerous expansions, digital ports, and spinoffs like *Small World Underground*.

## connections

- [[france]] — home nation of publisher *Days of Wonder*, which developed and released the game.
- [[ancient-greece]] — mythological origin point for several archetype fantasy races featured on the race banners, such as Amazons and Tritons.
- [[middle-east]] — ancient cradle of imperial warfare and territorial conquests simulated abstractly by the game's map mechanics.
- [[geometry]] — underlying mathematical discipline governing map tessellation, area adjacency, and board space partitioning.
- [[tragedy]] — dramatic literary concept mirrored thematically when once-mighty civilisations overextend and fall into irreversible decline.
