## summary

**Cheddar** is a relatively hard, natural cow's milk cheese that originated around the twelfth century in the village of Cheddar in Somerset, *England*. Distinguished by the eponymous manufacturing step of "cheddaring"—in which warm curds are repeatedly cut, stacked, and turned to expel whey—it matures into a firm, crumbly texture with a sharp, savory flavor profile. Today it ranks as the most popular and commercially produced cheese variety across the English-speaking world.

## you gotta know

- Takes its name from *Cheddar Gorge* and the village of Cheddar in Somerset, where limestone caves historically provided cool, humid environments for aging wheels.
- Undergoes the unique milling technique known as *cheddaring*, where blocks of curd are stacked and turned repeatedly to drain remaining whey and knead the protein matrix.
- Standardized into modern industrial dairying during the nineteenth century by Somerset dairyman *Joseph Harding*, often styled the "father of cheddar cheese."
- Possesses a natural pale yellow or off-white hue, but is frequently dyed deep orange using the tropical seed extract *annatto*, particularly in regional traditions.
- Ranges widely in sharpness, texture, and moisture based on aging, developing crunchy calcium lactate crystals over extended curing times.
- Protected within the *European Union* and *United Kingdom* under the name *West Country Farmhouse Cheddar*, which requires traditional sourcing in southwestern English counties.
- Disseminated globally throughout North America, Australia, and New Zealand via the colonial trading routes and agricultural outposts of the *British Empire*.

## connections

- [[england]] — historic nation of origin where Somerset farmers developed the traditional cave-aging and artisanal curd manipulation techniques.
- [[united-kingdom]] — modern sovereign state maintaining legal geographical protections for authentic farmhouse production in the West Country.
- [[british-empire]] — worldwide maritime empire that transplanted dairy herds, cheese-making methods, and consumption habits across colonies.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of nineteenth-century scientific agriculture and mass factory processing pioneered by figures like *Joseph Harding*.
- [[united-states]] — major global producer where industrial cheddar became the cornerstone of commercial dairy markets and processed food products.
- [[chick-fil-a]] — American restaurant chain that utilizes melted cheese varieties, including cheddar slices, on breakfast chicken biscuits.
