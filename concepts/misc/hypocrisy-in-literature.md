## summary

**Hypocrisy in literature** is a perennial thematic trope and structural device that scrutinizes the chasm between professed moral virtue and corrupt private conduct. Across drama, satire, and the novel, authors have mobilized the hypocrite to expose institutional piety, bourgeois respectability, and social deceit. Analyzing literary hypocrisy illuminates how narrative forms critique societal power structures, enforce ethical accountability, and reveal the fractures inherent in moral posturing.

## you gotta know

- *Molière* crafted the archetypal religious charlatan in his seventeenth-century comedy *Tartuffe*, presenting a deceitful zealot whose feigned piety manipulates a gullible patriarch until royal justice intervenes.
- In *[[william-shakespeare]]*'s *Measure for Measure*, the puritanical magistrate *Angelo* strictly enforces severe moral edicts while secretly demanding sexual favors from the novice nun *Isabella*, epitomizing judicial and personal duplicity.
- *Charles Dickens* anatomized Victorian sanctimony through grotesque caricatures such as *Mr. Pecksniff* in *Martin Chuzzlewit* and the pious tyrant *Mr. Bumble* in *Oliver Twist*, satirizing social institutions in the *[[united-kingdom]]*.
- Nineteenth-century *[[realism]]* turned hypocrisy into an indispensable mechanism for dissecting the moral rot of polite society, as seen in *Honoré de Balzac*'s *La Comédie humaine* and *Leo Tolstoy*'s *The Death of Ivan Ilyich*.
- In *[[france]]*, *Stendhal*'s *The Red and the Black* tracks the ambitious provincial youth *Julien Sorel*, who intentionally adopts religious hypocrisy as a calculated weapon to navigate the rigid social stratification of the Bourbon Restoration.
- In *[[american-literature]]*, *Nathaniel Hawthorne*'s *The Scarlet Letter* contrasts the public ostracization of *Hester Prynne* with the excruciating internal torment of the revered minister *Arthur Dimmesdale*, whose hidden sin corrodes his soul.
- Twentieth-century modernist and dystopian satires, notably *George Orwell*'s *Animal Farm*, transformed hypocrisy into a systemic political critique where revolutionary slogans of absolute equality cloak totalitarian domination and privilege.

## connections

- [[william-shakespeare]] — dramatized the perils of unbending puritanical righteousness through corrupt officials like *Angelo*.
- [[realism]] — embraced meticulous scrutiny of bourgeois manners to expose the gap between public decorum and private selfishness.
- [[france]] — nurtured seminal satires of religious and political deceit through the classic works of *Molière* and *Stendhal*.
- [[united-kingdom]] — provided the rigid Victorian class architecture targeted by *Charles Dickens* and *William Makepeace Thackeray*.
- [[american-literature]] — explored Puritan guilt, public piety, and secret sin through the masterworks of *Nathaniel Hawthorne* and *Mark Twain*.
- [[enlightenment]] — championed rationalist philosophical critiques of clerical corruption and dogmatic duplicity across Europe.
- [[human-expression]] — supplies the aesthetic and moral vehicle through which storytellers expose hidden realities and challenge pretension.
