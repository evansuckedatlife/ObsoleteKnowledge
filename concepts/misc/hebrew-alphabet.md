## summary

The **Hebrew alphabet**, traditionally referred to as the **alefbet** or **ktav ashuri**, is a twenty-two-letter consonantal writing system that originated across the ancient [[middle-east]] to record the Hebrew language and later Jewish vernaculars including Yiddish and Ladino. Classified typologically as an abjad, it is written continuously from right to left and preserves semantic roots primarily through consonants, relying on contextual literacy or secondary diacritics to convey vowel quality. Its evolution from earlier Northwest Semitic scripts and its meticulous preservation in liturgical scrolls make it a cornerstone of comparative [[linguistics]] and biblical scholarship.

## you gotta know

- Consists of twenty-two graphemic characters representing consonant phonemes, completely lacking dedicated independent letters for vowels in its primary script.
- Written horizontally from right to left, a directional orientation shared with neighboring Semitic scripts like Arabic and Syriac that diverged from left-to-right systems.
- Employs five distinct final letterforms, known collectively as *sofit*, which replace the standard glyphs of *kaf*, *mem*, *nun*, *pe*, and *tsadi* whenever those characters appear at the terminal end of a word.
- Utilizes the *niqqud* system of diacritical points, sublinear dashes, and supralinear dots developed by the Masoretes of Tiberias during the early Middle Ages to standardize liturgical recitation and resolve phonetic ambiguities.
- Features the six *begadkepat* letters (*bet*, *gimel*, *dalet*, *kaf*, *pe*, and *tav*), which historically alternated between plosive stops and fricative continuants depending on the presence or absence of a central point termed a *dagesh*.
- Relies on *matres lectionis*, or *mothers of reading*, an orthographic convention wherein weak consonants such as *aleph*, *he*, *vav*, and *yod* serve dual functions to indicate long vowel sounds without requiring formal *niqqud*.
- Supplies the structural mechanics for *gematria*, an esoteric interpretive discipline within Jewish mysticism and biblical exegesis wherein each character is assigned an exact numerical value.
- Replaced the older paleo-Hebrew script with the current square Aramaic-derived block script following the Babylonian exile, contrasting fundamentally with South Asian syllabic writing systems like the script of [[hindi]].

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — serves as the classical reference model for an abjad writing system utilizing secondary diacritical vowel pointing.
- [[middle-east]] — arose within the Levant alongside Phoenician and Aramaic scripts during the first millennium BCE.
- [[christianity]] — preserved the textual transmission of the Old Testament Scriptures in original biblical Hebrew manuscripts.
- [[roman-empire]] — suppressed in monumental public use throughout Judea following the suppression of the Bar Kokhba revolt.
- [[ancient-greece]] — shares common ancestral roots with the Greek alphabet through their shared derivation from the Phoenician script.
- [[hindi]] — provides an illustrative typological contrast between West Asian consonantal abjads and South Asian abugidas.
