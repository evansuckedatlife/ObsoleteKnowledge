## summary

A **tourtière** (frequently rendered in English as **tourtiere**) is a traditional French-Canadian spiced meat pie originating in the province of Quebec and enjoyed throughout Acadia and French North America. Baked in a flaky pastry crust, it features seasoned minced pork, veal, or beef delicately fragrant with warm winter spices such as clove, cinnamon, and savory. It represents the quintessential culinary highlight of the festive winter season, deeply tied to communal celebrations marking the conclusion of the Catholic Advent fast.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the crowning centerpiece of the *réveillon*, the lavish midnight feast celebrated by French-Canadian families immediately following Christmas Eve Midnight Mass.
- Ground pork forms the classic base of the filling, frequently combined with minced veal or beef and bound with diced potatoes or breadcrumbs to absorb the aromatic cooking juices.
- Spiced uniquely with warm aromatics including ground cloves, cinnamon, nutmeg, allspice, onion, and savory (*sarriette*), differentiating it from savory English meat pasties.
- The regional version from the *Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean* area differs markedly, prepared as a massive deep-dish stew pie layered with cubed wild game, pork, and potatoes rather than minced meat.
- Etymologically named after the ceramic baking dish (*tourtière*) in which it was prepared, though folklore frequently connects it to the extinct passenger pigeon (*tourte*), which early New France settlers once used in game pies.
- Historically dates back to seventeenth-century French settlers in New France, drawing lineage from medieval European pasties documented in *The Forme of Cury*.
- Commonly served with sweet and tangy homemade fruit ketchup, pickled green tomatoes, or cranberry relish, alongside hearty French-Canadian winter staples.

## connections

- [[france]] — draws on medieval French pastry and game-pie traditions transported to the Saint Lawrence River valley during the seventeenth century.
- [[christianity]] — rooted in Roman Catholic liturgical rhythms, served specifically to break the solemn fast following Christmas Eve Midnight Mass.
- [[colonialism]] — reflects the culinary survival, localized ingredient adaptations, and rustic domestic heritage of New France.
- [[quebec]] — stands as a primary culinary symbol of Québécois cultural identity and ancestral hearth traditions.
- [[toonie]] — shares its place as an instantly recognizable emblem of broader Canadian national material culture and heritage.
- [[christmas]] — marks the holiday season across Franco-American and Canadian households as an annual ceremonial feast.
- [[réveillon]] — anchors the traditional French-Canadian midnight gathering celebrated on both Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve.
