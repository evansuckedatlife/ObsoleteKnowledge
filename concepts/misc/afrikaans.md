## summary

**Afrikaans** is a Low Franconian West Germanic language that evolved during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries in the Dutch Cape Colony of southern Africa. Historically labeled **Cape Dutch** or *Kitchen Dutch*, it emerged from the contact between seventeenth-century Dutch settlers, indigenous Khoisan populations, enslaved peoples from Southeast Asia and Madagascar, and French Huguenot refugees. It became an official language of South Africa alongside English in 1925, was later weaponized by the apartheid regime, and remains a major literary and communal tongue spoken by millions of white, Coloured, and Black South Africans today.

## you gotta know

- Developed primarily from the South Holland dialects brought by the *Dutch East India Company* (*Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie*) when *Jan van Riebeeck* founded a supply station at Table Bay in 1652.
- Historically known disparagingly as **Cape Dutch** or *kombuistaal* (*kitchen language*) before achieving standardized literary status and formal institutional recognition in the early twentieth century.
- Exhibited severe grammatical simplification compared to European Dutch, completely eliminating grammatical gender, verbal conjugation for person and number, and the simple past tense in favor of a periphrastic perfect using *het*.
- Features obligatory syntactic double negation (*ontkenning*), where a negative sentence marks negation with an auxiliary particle followed by a sentence-final *nie*, a structure heavily influenced by contact with indigenous and creolized tongues.
- Written initially in Arabic script by enslaved Muslims and political exiles in the Bo-Kaap district of Cape Town, producing the earliest known sustained texts of the language (*Arabic Afrikaans*) before Latin orthography took root.
- Propagated through the *Genootskap van Regte Afrikaners* (*Society of True Afrikaners*) founded at Paarl in 1875, which published the first newspaper, *Die Afrikaanse Patriot*, and translated Christian scripture.
- Served as the linguistic centerpiece of Afrikaner nationalism and the legal machinery of apartheid, triggering the fatal 1976 *Soweto Uprising* when Black schoolchildren protested the compulsory use of the language as an instructional medium.
- Produced world-renowned twentieth-century literature through modernist movements such as the *Dertigers* and the anti-apartheid *Sestigers*, including prominent dissident writers *Breyten Breytenbach*, *Ingrid Jonker*, and *André Brink*.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — serves as a textbook example of language contact, regularizing inflectional morphology while retaining core Germanic syntax.
- [[colonialism]] — born directly out of European imperial trade outposts and the displacement of indigenous Khoekhoe pastoralists.
- [[british-empire]] — shaped by resistance to nineteenth-century imperial Anglicization policies following British annexation of the Cape.
- [[african-diaspora]] — shaped lexically and syntactically by enslaved persons trafficked from the Indonesian archipelago, South Asia, and East Africa.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — intersected globally with anti-apartheid campaigns after the 1976 student resistance against forced language edicts.
- [[cold-war]] — politicized during regional southern African conflicts where the Afrikaner-led government cast itself as a Western anticommunist bulwark.
