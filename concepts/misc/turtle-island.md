## summary

**Turtle Island** is an Indigenous cosmological name for the continent of North America and, more broadly, the earthly sphere, originating in the creation narratives of several First Nations, most prominently the *Haudenosaunee* (Iroquois Confederacy), *Anishinaabeg*, and *Lenape*. Rooted in an ancient Earth-Diver mythos, the narrative tells of pregnant Sky Woman falling into a primordial water world, where compassionate aquatic creatures help place ocean mud onto the shell of a giant turtle, which miraculously expands into living land. In modern political, literary, and environmental discourse, the term serves as a powerful decolonial alternative to Eurocentric colonial place names across the continent.

## you gotta know

- In the foundational *Haudenosaunee* creation story, *Sky Woman* (*Atsī'tsik[an]* or *Atahensic*) falls from the celestial Sky World through a hole beneath the roots of the celestial Tree of Life.
- Flocks of waterbirds, frequently identified as wild geese or swans, catch Sky Woman in mid-air on their wings and safely lower her toward the endless primordial ocean below.
- Aquatic animals attempt heroic deep dives to retrieve soil from the ocean floor, an endeavor in which larger animals fail before a humble *muskrat* successfully surfaces with a mouthful of sacred mud.
- The retrieved mud is placed upon the back of a giant marine turtle, traditionally interpreted as a snapping turtle, which grows and multiplies exponentially under Sky Woman's dance to form the continent.
- Sky Woman gives birth to a daughter, who later dies giving birth to divine twin brothers—often known as the *Good Twin* and the *Bad Twin*—who shape the continent's topography, flora, and fauna.
- American Beat Generation poet *Gary Snyder* popularized the phrase among environmentalists and non-Native readers with his 1974 poetry collection *Turtle Island*, which was awarded the Pulitzer Prize for Poetry.
- Modern Indigenous sovereignty movements, environmental justice organizers, and cultural revivalists frequently use the name to challenge the geopolitical borders established by European settler states.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — historical process of European imperial expansion that renamed Indigenous geographies and displaced native populations.
- [[indian-removal]] — nineteenth-century federal policy that forcibly displaced Eastern Woodlands tribes who preserved the Sky Woman and turtle traditions.
- [[american-literature]] — national literary tradition encompassing both oral Indigenous storytelling and modern adaptations like *Gary Snyder's* poetry.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical era whose political theories often clashed with Indigenous communal understandings of geography and the cosmos.
- [[astronomy]] — observational tradition linked to Native sky lore, the celestial Sky World, and cosmic origins in oral mythologies.
