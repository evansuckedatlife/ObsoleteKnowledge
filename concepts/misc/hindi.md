## summary

**Hindi**, specifically standardized as **Modern Standard Hindi** or **Manak Hindi**, is an Indo-Aryan language spoken primarily across northern India that functions alongside English as an official language of the central government. Descended from Sanskrit via Middle Indo-Aryan Prakrits and medieval Apabhramsha, it forms a pluricentric dialect continuum with Urdu through their shared spoken base of Hindustani. Boasting hundreds of millions of speakers worldwide, it represents a major global language and a central subject in comparative historical [[linguistics]].

## you gotta know

- Written in the Devanagari script, an abugida where each consonant glyph possesses an inherent vowel sound that is modified or suppressed by diacritical vowel marks known as *matras*.
- Shares its core grammar, syntax, morphological system, and basic colloquial lexicon with Urdu, diverging primarily through literary register, script, and high-vocabulary borrowings.
- Belongs to the Indo-Aryan branch of the Indo-European family, demonstrating systematic sound correspondences recognized by philologist *Sir William Jones* during the late eighteenth century.
- Preserves classical phonetic distinctions formalised in ancient India by the grammarian *Panini*, including four-way contrasts between voiced, unvoiced, aspirated, and unaspirated stops, alongside retroflex consonants.
- Exhibits split ergativity in its nominal syntax, marking subjects of transitive verbs with the postposition *ne* in perfective aspects and triggering verbal agreement with the direct object.
- Standardized during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries by literary reformers such as *Bharatendu Harishchandra*, who championed the Delhi-based Khariboli dialect over regional literary standards like Braj Bhasha and Awadhi.
- Differs structurally in its orthographic mechanics from consonantal abjads like the [[hebrew-alphabet]], utilizing a Brahmic script that visually unites consonant-vowel combinations into composite syllabic blocks.
