## summary

A **bombardier** (historically designated as a **bomb aimer** within Commonwealth military services) is an aircrew specialist tasked with the optical targeting, ballistic calculation, and manual or automated release of airborne ordnance. The specialization attained crucial tactical and technological prominence during [[world-war-ii]], when air forces fielded intricate analog mechanical computers to execute high-altitude daylight precision bombing. In cultural memory, the position remains famous as the military role of the conflicted antihero in mid-century American war literature.

## you gotta know

- Originated historically as an early modern European artillery designation, derived from the French military term for cannoneers who operated heavy mortar siege engines known as *bombards*.
- Preserved as a distinct non-commissioned rank in the British *Royal Artillery*, where a bombardier ranks alongside an army corporal and a lance-bombardier equates to a lance-corporal.
- Stationed within the transparent plexiglass nose compartment of multi-engine combat aircraft such as the *B-17 Flying Fortress*, *B-24 Liberator*, and *B-25 Mitchell* during [[world-war-ii]].
- Operated the top-secret *Norden bombsight*, a gyrostabilized analog computer engineered by *Carl Norden* that took control of the aircraft autopilot during the final bomb run to calculate drift and groundspeed.
- Required airmen to take an official security oath promising to defend the secret components of their sighting mechanisms with their lives, keeping the device shrouded in canvas until takeoff.
- Immortalized in *Joseph Heller*'s landmark 1961 satirical novel *Catch-22*, whose protagonist *John Yossarian* serves as an anxious B-25 bombardier flying combat sorties over Italy from the island of *Pianosa*.
- Carried out the atomic bombing missions of August 1945, executed by Major *Thomas Ferebee* aboard the *Enola Gay* over Hiroshima and Captain *Kermit Beahan* aboard *Bockscar* over Nagasaki.
- Shares its name with the Canadian industrial enterprise founded by inventor *Joseph-Armand Bombardier*, who created the modern snowmobile before his firm grew into a major aerospace and railway manufacturer.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict that established strategic daylight aerial doctrine and propelled specialized bomber aircrews to central tactical importance.
- [[american-literature]] — post-war literary movement shaped by veteran alienation and bureaucratic satire, exemplified by novels chronicling Mediterranean bombing campaigns.
- [[blackberry]] — Canadian technological peer whose national manufacturing and engineering sectors developed alongside major transportation firms bearing the same family name.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical standoff during which strategic bomber crews stood on permanent runway alert before intercontinental ballistic missiles supplanted heavy bombers.
- [[world-war-i]] — armed conflict that witnessed the earliest primitive hand-dropped aerial bombardment experiments and the birth of military aviation corps.
- [[france]] — source of the historic etymological root *bombarde*, reflecting centuries of artillery terminology shared across European military institutions.
