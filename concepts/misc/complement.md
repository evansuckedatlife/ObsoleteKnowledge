## summary

In mathematics, formal logic, and grammar, a **complement** represents that which completes a whole or supplies what is missing from a given structure. Across set theory, the **set complement** denotes all elements within a universal set not contained in a specified subset, governed by Boolean algebra and De Morgan's laws alongside [[conjunction]] and disjunction. In Euclidean geometry, complementary angles sum to ninety degrees, while in modern linguistics, a complement is a clause or phrase required by a head word to complete its syntactic meaning.

## you gotta know

- In set theory, the absolute complement of a set *A* within a universe *U*, written *A* complement or *U* difference *A*, consists of every element belonging to *U* that does not belong to *A*.
- De Morgan's laws establish fundamental dualities between set operations: the complement of a [[conjunction]] is the union of the individual complements, formulated algebraically as the complement of the intersection equaling the union of the complements.
- In Euclidean [[geometry]], complementary angles are pairs of angles whose measures sum precisely to a right angle or ninety degrees, contrasting with supplementary angles that sum to one hundred eighty degrees.
- In formal [[linguistics]], a syntactic complement is an argument selected directly by a lexical head, such as a direct object completing a transitive verb or an embedded clause completing a propositional verb.
- In immunology and [[human-anatomy]], the complement system is an ancient biochemical cascade of over thirty plasma proteins that opsonizes pathogens, recruits inflammatory cells, and forms the membrane attack complex.
- In color theory dating to the [[enlightenment]], complementary colors sit directly opposite one another on the color wheel—such as red and green or blue and orange—producing strong chromatic contrast when juxtaposed.
- In digital arithmetic and computer science, two's complement notation provides an ingenious binary system for representing signed integers and performing subtraction through straightforward addition circuits.

## connections

- [[conjunction]] — set intersection and logical conjunction map to disjunction under negation via complementation in De Morgan's laws.
- [[converse]] — both concepts represent formal structural transformations in deductive logic, where negations and complements interact to define inverses and contrapositives.
- [[geometry]] — complementary angles form a foundational concept in Euclidean axiomatic geometry and right-triangle trigonometry.
- [[linguistics]] — complementizers and complement phrases serve as core structural building blocks in generative syntax and phrase-structure grammar.
- [[human-anatomy]] — the physiological complement cascade functions as an indispensable arm of the innate immune response in the human body.
- [[ancient-greece]] — early Greek mathematicians from Euclid to Apollonius defined complementary geometric figures based on right-angle partitions.
