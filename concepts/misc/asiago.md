## summary

**Asiago** is a historic alpine plateau and town located in the Veneto region of northeastern [[italy]], internationally celebrated both for its eponymous cow's milk cheese and for its profound military history. The plateau, traditionally known as the *Altopiano dei Sette Comuni*, developed a unique cultural heritage as an autonomous confederation and linguistic enclave before becoming a pivotal battleground during *World War I*. As a culinary export, **Asiago cheese** is protected under strict European Union agricultural regulations, representing one of the quintessential regional traditions of northern Italian dairy craft.

## you gotta know

- The cheese is protected by a European *Denominazione di Origine Protetta* (DOP) certification, meaning authentic production is legally confined to designated provinces including *Vicenza*, *Trento*, and parts of *Padua* and *Treviso*.
- It is manufactured in two primary culinary categories: *Asiago Pressato*, a fresh, semi-soft variety made from whole milk and aged for roughly a month, and *Asiago d'Allevo*, an aged cheese crafted from partially skimmed milk.
- The aged *d'Allevo* style is further classified by maturation time into *Mezzano* (four to ten months), *Vecchio* (ten to fifteen months), and *Stravecchio* (aged beyond fifteen months to produce a hard, crumbly, savory grating cheese).
- During [[world-war-i]], the plateau was the focal point of the massive Austro-Hungarian *Strafexpedition* (Punitive Expedition) in May 1916, where Italian armies fought desperate defensive actions to prevent an imperial breakthrough into the Venetian plains.
- The brutal mountain warfare on the plateau was immortalized by Italian soldier and author *Emilio Lussu* in his celebrated wartime memoir *Un anno sull'Altipiano* (*A Year on the Plateau*).
- For centuries, the plateau functioned as the *Federation of the Seven Communities*, a semi-independent rural league whose inhabitants spoke *Cimbrian*, an ancient Germanic dialect distinct from standard Italian.
- The *Military Shrine of Asiago* (*Sacrario Militare del Leiten*), constructed in the 1930s, stands as a monumental marble ossuary containing the remains of over fifty thousand Italian and Austro-Hungarian soldiers killed in local mountain fighting.
- The plateau is also home to the *Asiago Astrophysical Observatory*, founded during [[world-war-ii]] by the University of Padua, which houses significant optical telescopes taking advantage of clear alpine altitudes.
