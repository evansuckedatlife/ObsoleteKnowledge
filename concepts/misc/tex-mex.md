## summary

**Tex-Mex** is an acclaimed regional culinary tradition rooted in the traditional home cooking of *Tejano* populations inhabiting the southwestern borderlands of the [[united-states]]. Characterized by a generous use of shredded yellow cheddar, melted cheese dips, cumin, beef skirt steaks, and large wheat flour tortillas, the tradition represents a dynamic fusion of indigenous Mesoamerican foodways with Old World Iberian ranching staples. Originally referred to simply as domestic northern Mexican food, this distinct regional style, also celebrated as **Tejano cuisine**, gained formal international recognition through twentieth-century food writing.

## you gotta know

- Distinguished from traditional interior Mexican cookery by its fundamental reliance on yellow cheeses, heavy quantities of powdered cumin (*comino*), canned tomatoes, pinto beans, and beef cuts.
- Popularized the sizzling tabletop skillet service of *fajitas*, a dish created from skirt steak cuts traditionally butchered and marinated by Mexican ranch hands (*vaqueros*) along the Rio Grande valley.
- Codified as a legitimate, distinct culinary tradition by British cookbook author *Diana Kennedy* in her groundbreaking 1972 volume *The Cuisines of Mexico*, which separated it from authentic regional dishes of interior states.
- Traces its commercial origin to the late nineteenth century in San Antonio, where entrepreneurial street vendors dubbed the "Chili Queens" served spicy meat stews to travelers in public squares like *Plaza de Armas*.
- Derived its name from the nineteenth-century abbreviation for the *Texas Mexican Railway*, an infrastructure acronym that local journalists subsequently adapted to describe the area's hybrid culinary culture.
- Revolutionized the modern cocktail industry in 1971 when Dallas restaurateur *Mariano Martinez* modified a soft-serve ice cream machine to invent the world's first automated frozen margarita dispenser.
- Relies centrally on *chili con queso*, a rich molten dip often concocted from processed American cheese and chopped green chiles that serves as a universal appetizer across southwestern dining establishments.

## connections

- [[tabasco]] — Louisiana pepper sauce frequently featured in southwestern pantries to elevate piquant table condiments and marinades.
- [[spain]] — colonial empire that introduced domesticated cattle, wheat flour, and Old World seasonings into northern New Spain.
- [[united-states]] — country where the regional cuisine developed, commercialized, and expanded into a multi-billion-dollar restaurant industry.
- [[colonialism]] — historical exchange that blended indigenous corn, bean, and chili agriculture with European livestock and culinary practices.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — period of rapid southwestern railway construction and urbanization during which San Antonio street vendors popularized chili dishes.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict that disrupted southern agriculture and subsequently catalyzed large-scale cattle ranching across the western plains.
