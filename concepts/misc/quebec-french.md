## summary

**Quebec French** (*français québécois*) is the predominant variety of the French language spoken in Canada, tracing its ancestral lineage directly to seventeenth- and eighteenth-century settlers from northern and western *France*. Distinct from European French in its historical phonology, rich vocabulary, and subtle morphosyntax, it serves as the official and cultural voice of the province of *Quebec*. Its preservation across centuries of *British Empire* dominion and Anglo-American linguistic hegemony makes it a central pillar of Francophone identity and modern sociolinguistic scholarship.

## you gotta know

- Recognized as the primary mother tongue of the majority of *Quebec* residents, where provincial legislation such as *Bill 101* (*Charter of the French Language*) strictly protects its primacy in public education, signage, and workplaces.
- Famous for its distinctive religious profanities, known colloquially as *sacres*, which repurpose Catholic ecclesiastical terms like *tabarnak* (tabernacle), *câlice* (chalice), *ostie* (host), and *sacrament* into emotionally charged expletives.
- Features prominent phonological markers such as high vowel assibilation, where dental stops /t/ and /d/ become affricates [ts] and [dz] before high front vowels /i/ and /y/, as well as widespread vowel lengthening and diphthongization.
- Retains classical vocabulary and phonetic patterns dating to seventeenth-century *Paris* and northwestern French maritime provinces, preserving archaic idioms that disappeared from mainland standard French following the French Revolution.
- Diverged sharply from metropolitan speech after the 1763 Treaty of Paris, which ceded New France to Great Britain and isolated Canadian Francophones from the sociolinguistic changes sweeping mainland *France* during the *Enlightenment*.
- Absorbed significant lexical contributions from Indigenous languages for local topography, flora, and fauna, alongside historically borrowed English technical idioms that modern language boards actively seek to Frenchify.
- Contrasts with other North American Francophone varieties such as *Acadian French* and *Franco-Ontarian*, maintaining its own prestigious urban standard centered around *Montreal* and *Quebec City*.
