## summary

**United Airlines**, commonly known simply as **United**, is a major American legacy air carrier headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, operating an expansive domestic and transcontinental route network. Tracing its lineage back to *Walter Varney*'s airmail service and the early industrial aviation empire of *William Boeing*, the company played a foundational role in establishing modern commercial aviation standards. In quiz bowl, it frequently appears in questions concerning antitrust actions under the *Air Mail Act of 1934*, corporate advertising featuring *George Gershwin*, historic aviation disasters, and its prominent role in twentieth-century airline deregulation.

## you gotta know

- Hired the commercial airline industry's first female flight attendant, registered nurse *Ellen Church*, who operated her inaugural flight aboard a Boeing 80A trimotor between Oakland and Chicago in 1930.
- Adopted *George Gershwin*'s celebrated orchestral composition *Rhapsody in Blue* for its major marketing campaigns starting in 1987, pairing the music with its long-running advertising slogan, "Fly the Friendly Skies."
- Executed a major corporate merger with *Continental Airlines* in 2010 under chief executive *Jeff Smisek*, adopting Continental's blue-and-gold globe tail livery while retaining the historic name **United**.
- Formed through the forced breakup of the *United Aircraft and Transport Corporation*, a sprawling vertical monopoly dismantled by the *Air Mail Act of 1934* into Boeing, United Aircraft (later United Technologies), and the independent passenger airline.
- Introduced the *Boeing 247* into service in 1933, an all-metal cantilever monoplane widely recognized as the first truly modern airliner, whose exclusive initial order forced rival *TWA* to sponsor the *Douglas DC-2*.
- Suffered the hijackings of two airliners during the September 11 attacks: *Flight 175*, which struck the South Tower of the World Trade Center in [[new-york-city]], and *Flight 93*, whose passengers fought back before crashing in Shanksville, Pennsylvania.
- Experienced the historic crash-landing of *Flight 232* in Sioux City, Iowa, in 1989, where captain *Al Haynes* and an auxiliary crew steered a disabled McDonnell Douglas DC-10 without hydraulic controls by modulating engine throttles.
- Involved alongside a TWA Super Constellation in the deadly 1955 *Grand Canyon mid-air collision*, a disaster that spurred the [[united-states]] Congress to enact the Federal Aviation Act of 1958 and establish the modern *Federal Aviation Administration*.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — home country where the carrier maintains massive primary connecting hubs in Chicago, Denver, Houston, Newark, and San Francisco.
- [[new-york-city]] — major transatlantic market anchored by the airline's major East Coast international gateway at Newark Liberty International Airport.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict during which the airline modified military bombers, operated transpacific supply routes, and trained military flight crews.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical era encompassing the rapid expansion of international jetliner routes, the 1978 deregulation act, and aggressive global competition.
- [[space-age]] — technological epoch characterized by commercial jet adoption, inaugurating high-speed service aboard the *Douglas DC-8* and *Boeing 747*.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — foundational era of powered transport and mechanical manufacturing that enabled twentieth-century commercial aviation infrastructure.
