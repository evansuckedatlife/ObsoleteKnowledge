## summary

**Répondez s'il vous plaît**, universally abbreviated as **RSVP** or **R.S.V.P.**, is a French formal etiquette phrase translating literally into English as *please reply*. Originating within the courtly protocol of early modern *[[france]]*, the expression was adopted by British and American high society to solicit confirmations of attendance for formal social gatherings. In trivia and quiz bowl contexts, it appears frequently in questions addressing loanwords, aristocratic correspondence conventions, diplomatic protocol, and modern invitation etiquette.

## you gotta know

- Derives directly from the formal French imperative phrase meaning *reply if it pleases you*, serving as an explicit request for an invited party to confirm whether they will attend an event.
- Adopted into English social correspondence during the late 18th and 19th centuries as the British aristocracy adopted Francophone court etiquette and diplomatic conventions.
- The phrase is often modified in contemporary social usage to *RSVP, regrets only*, dictating that recipients need contact the host solely if they are unable to attend.
- Modern etiquette authorities, including *Emily Post* and *Amy Vanderbilt*, historically admonished guests who failed to reply promptly, noting that proper hosts required exact headcounts for seated dining.
- Though pervasive on formal printed stationery throughout the Anglosphere, modern French speakers often favor more direct contemporary phrasing such as *veuillez répondre* or informal expressions.
- The acronym spawned a common English back-formation verb phrase, prompting grammar purists to criticize expressions like *please RSVP* as redundant due to the inclusion of *s'il vous plaît*.
