## summary

**Roquefort** is a celebrated sheep milk blue cheese originating from the southern commune of Roquefort-sur-Soulzon in *[[france]]*. Aged exclusively within the natural, humid caves of Mount Combalou, it is distinguished by blue-green veins of the mold *Penicillium roqueforti* that confer an intensely pungent aroma and crumbly texture. Known historically as the *king of cheeses*, it became the benchmark for cheese craftsmanship and early geographical food protection laws.

## you gotta know

- The cheese is crafted exclusively from raw, whole milk produced by the *Lacaune* breed of sheep, which graze on the plateaus of southern *[[france]]*.
- According to local legend, a young shepherd seeking shelter in the caves abandoned his lunch of bread and ewe's milk cheese to pursue a maiden, returning months later to find the cheese miraculously veined with mold.
- Unique geological conditions in the limestone caves of Mount Combalou provide natural ventilation through vertical rock fissures called *fleurines*, creating optimal moisture and temperature for mold growth.
- In 1411, King *Charles VI* granted the local inhabitants a royal monopoly on the ripening of the product, establishing one of the earliest predecessors to modern appellation laws.
- In 1925, it became the very first cheese awarded France's prestigious *Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée* (AOC) designation, legally restricting production techniques and regional ripening facilities.
- The characteristic blue veining is produced by *Penicillium roqueforti*, a mold traditionally cultured by leaving loaves of rye bread in the caves until thoroughly colonized.
- Culinary philosopher *Denis Diderot* famously lauded the cheese during the *[[enlightenment]]*, giving rise to its enduring epithet as the *king of cheeses*.
