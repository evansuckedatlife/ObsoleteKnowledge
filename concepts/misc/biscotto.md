## summary

**Biscotto** is an Italian gastronomic and material concept originating in Mediterranean antiquity that denotes twice-cooked bread, encompassing common cookies as well as artisanal refractory clay baking tiles. Tracing directly to the Latin **panis biscoctus** produced across the [[roman-empire]] as an unspoilable ration for soldiers and sailors, the term served as the linguistic ancestor for dry baked goods throughout Western Europe. In contemporary culinary engineering, the word also designates **biscotto di Sorrento**, a specialized volcanic clay oven floor indispensable for baking Neapolitan pizza at extreme heat without burning the crust.

## you gotta know

- Etymologically constructed from the Latin *bis* ("twice") and *coctus* ("cooked"), the name originally designated loaves baked first to set the crumb and returned to the oven to drive off remaining water.
- In everyday contemporary Italian, the singular noun functions as the generic term for any sweet cookie, biscuit, or dry cracker, unlike international parlance where the plural [[biscotti]] refers strictly to Tuscan almond cantucci.
- In the military logistics of [[rome]], *panis biscoctus* constituted the core non-perishable marching ration for legions, providing durable calories that resisted dampness and mould on distant frontiers.
- Maritime republics such as Venice and Genoa supplied their naval fleets with hard maritime biscuits known as *biscotto di mare*, which sustained rowers and merchants across months of sea transit.
- In authentic Neapolitan pizza baking, the term describes a porous terracotta tile made from volcanic clay mixed with volcanic pozzolana or alluvial sand, historically handcrafted on the Sorrento peninsula.
- The high porosity and air pockets of the clay tile lower its thermal conductivity, allowing pizza ovens to operate at temperatures exceeding 450 degrees Celsius without incinerating the base of the dough.
- The mechanization of flour milling and biscuit stamping during the [[industrial-revolution]] shifted biscuit production from manual bakeries into massive industrial commercial enterprises.
