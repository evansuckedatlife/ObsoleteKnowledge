## summary

**Spam** is a brand of canned, cooked meat product composed primarily of minced pork shoulder and ham, salt, water, modified potato starch, sugar, and sodium nitrite. Introduced by the American meatpacking firm *Hormel Foods Corporation* in 1937, it achieved global distribution and cultural prominence as a vital non-perishable military ration during [[world-war-ii]]. In subsequent decades, it became a beloved staple in various regional cuisines and famously gave its name to unsolicited electronic messaging.

## you gotta know

- Introduced in 1937 in Austin, Minnesota, by Hormel Foods, named via a promotional naming contest won by Kenneth Daigneau.
- Formed the core animal protein ration for Allied forces during *World War II*, with over one hundred million pounds shipped overseas to American, British, and Soviet troops.
- Entrenched deeply into post-war Pacific and Asian culinary cultures, becoming a foundational ingredient in Hawaiian *Spam musubi*, South Korean *budae jjigae* (army base stew), and Okinawan *champuru*.
- Parodied in a famous 1970 sketch on the British television comedy show *Monty Python's Flying Circus*, in which patrons in a café are endlessly drowned out by the repetition of the word.
- Lent its name directly to unsolicited bulk digital messages ("spam mail") due to early internet culture referencing the relentless repetition from the Monty Python sketch.
- Featured at the Spam Museum located at Hormel's corporate headquarters in Austin, Minnesota, which documents the product's social history.
- Relies on sodium nitrite both to cure the meat and preserve its signature pink coloration and ambient shelf stability.
