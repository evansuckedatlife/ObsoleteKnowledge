## summary

In linguistics, **conjugation** is the systematic inflection of a verb to convey grammatical categories such as tense, aspect, mood, voice, person, and number. As a foundational principle of morphology, conjugation allows synthetic and inflected languages to pack rich syntactic information into a single verbal word form. Outside of language, the word designates parallel processes of coupling, union, or orbital alignment in microbiology, organic chemistry, and mathematics.

## you gotta know

- Grammars classically categorize verbs into distinct conjugation groups or paradigms based on their thematic vowels and inflectional endings, such as the four traditional conjugations of Latin.
- Synthetic languages with rich verbal inflection, such as *Spanish*, *Italian*, and *Ancient Greek*, frequently permit pro-drop or null-subject constructions because person and number are unambiguous from the verb ending alone.
- While regular verbs adhere to uniform inflectional rules, irregular verbs frequently preserve archaic phonological shifts, such as Germanic vowel gradation or ablaut illustrated by the sequence *sing*, *sang*, *sung*.
- Grammatical theory divides verbal forms into finite forms that are conjugated for grammatical person and tense, and non-finite forms such as infinitives, gerunds, and participles that remain uninflected for person.
- In microbiology, bacterial conjugation is a mode of horizontal gene transfer whereby a donor bacterium transmits genetic material, often plasmids carrying antibiotic resistance, directly to a recipient cell across a mating pilus.
- In physical organic chemistry, a conjugated system involves alternating single and multiple bonds with overlapping p-orbitals, allowing delocalized electrons to impart special stability and shift light absorption spectra.
- In mathematics, complex conjugation flips the sign of the imaginary component of a number, while abstract algebra examines conjugation actions to partition elements into conjugacy classes.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — the overarching academic field studying morphology, syntax, and verbal inflection paradigms across natural languages.
- [[ancient-greece]] — origin of early grammatical analysis where scholars formalized the moods, voices, and synthetic conjugations of the Hellenic verb.
- [[rome]] — society whose grammarians developed the classical four-conjugation pedagogical model that organized Latin verb instruction.
- [[spain]] — Romance language whose verbal system preserves extensive indicative, subjunctive, and imperative conjugations directly inherited from Latin.
- [[france]] — language characterized by elaborate written verbal inflection tables distinguishing compound tenses, subjunctive moods, and participle agreements.
- [[germany]] — language whose verb morphology prominently contrasts weak regular inflections with strong ablaut-based conjugations.
- [[renaissance]] — period that sparked renewed humanist study of classical Latin and Greek grammar, codifying inflectional conjugation tables.
