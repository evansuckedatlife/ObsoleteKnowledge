## summary

**United States History** encompasses the rise of a colonial rebellion into a continental republic and world superpower over 250 years. From the [[american-revolution]] through westward expansion, civil conflict, industrialization, and global intervention, American history shaped the modern world. Its narrative includes ideals of democracy and freedom intertwined with slavery, displacement of [[native-americans]], and imperial ambition.

## you gotta know

- The thirteen colonies rebelled against [[great-britain]] in 1776, enshrining principles of individual rights, popular sovereignty, and democratic governance in the [[united-states-constitution]] (1787) and its [[bill-of-rights]].
- The Constitution's *three-fifths compromise* (counting enslaved people as 3/5 of a person for representation and taxation) institutionalized slavery and inflated Southern political power, delaying but intensifying the coming conflict.
- Westward expansion under *Manifest Destiny* (ideology of inevitable continental dominion) displaced millions of [[native-americans]]; the [[indian-removal]] Act of 1830 forcibly relocated the [[five-civilized-tribes]] along the [[trail-of-tears]], a death march of thousands.
- The [[american-civil-war]] (1861-1865) erupted over states' rights, slavery, and union; the North's victory ended slavery but Reconstruction (1865-1877) failed to secure lasting racial equality, leading to Jim Crow segregation.
- Industrialization and the rise of monopolies (Standard Oil, Carnegie Steel) transformed the U.S. into an economic powerhouse; mass immigration (Ellis Island, 1890s-1920s) reshaped cities and sparked nativist backlash and restrictive immigration laws.
- The U.S. entered [[world-war-ii]] reluctantly (after Pearl Harbor, 1941) and emerged as a superpower rivaling the [[soviet-union]] in the [[cold-war]], shaping global geopolitics for half a century.
- Post-WWII America grappled with racial injustice through the [[civil-rights-movement]] (1950s-60s), urban unrest, and the Vietnam War, fundamentally reshaping American society and values.
