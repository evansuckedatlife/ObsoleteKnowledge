## summary

The **Latin alphabet**, also known as the **Roman alphabet**, is the world's most widely adopted phonemic writing script and the official orthographic system for languages across the Americas, Western and Central Europe, sub-Saharan Africa, and Oceania. Originating in central *[[italy]]* during the seventh century BCE, it was adapted from the Etruscan alphabet, which derived from the Western Greek script of *[[ancient-greece]]*. Its global ubiquity was driven by the administrative spread of the *[[roman-empire]]*, the liturgical dominance of Western *[[christianity]]*, and European colonial expansion.

## you gotta know

- Developed around the seventh century BCE in the region of Latium, adapting twenty-one letters from the archaic Etruscan script to write the Old Latin language.
- Derived from the Cumaean alphabet of Magna Graecia, reflecting phonetic adaptations categorized in *[[linguistics]]* where Greek aspirate letters were discarded or repurposed as numeral symbols.
- Monumental capital lettering (*capitalis monumentalis*) was standardized under the *[[roman-empire]]*, achieving its classical artistic peak on public architecture in the forum of *[[rome]]*.
- The script developed cursive forms for commercial correspondence, which eventually evolved into the Carolingian minuscule championed under Charlemagne to standardize monastic manuscripts.
- The expansion of Western *[[christianity]]* established Latin as the universal sacred and diplomatic language of medieval Europe, entrenching its script across previously non-literate societies.
- Humanist scholars and typographers of the *[[renaissance]]* finalized the modern twenty-six-letter inventory by establishing orthographic distinctions between *I* and *J*, and between *U*, *V*, and *W*.
- Widely employed during the *[[enlightenment]]* to publish encyclopedic compendia, and later imposed on overseas territories through European *[[colonialism]]* using auxiliary diacritics.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — ancient Mediterranean power that carried the script as its official administrative, legal, and military writing system.
- [[rome]] — founding city-state whose stone carvers and scribes established the proportions of classical lapidary majuscule letters.
- [[linguistics]] — academic study of speech sounds, writing systems, and orthographic mutations across the world's language families.
- [[ancient-greece]] — origin of the Cumae alphabet that Italic peoples adapted to create their earliest epigraphic texts.
- [[christianity]] — religious institution whose Latin liturgy and scriptoria preserved, copied, and standardized the script throughout the Middle Ages.
- [[renaissance]] — intellectual era that revived Roman classical capitals and designed movable metal typefaces that define modern typography.
- [[colonialism]] — historical imperial system that carried Roman-derived alphabets across the Americas, Africa, and Southeast Asia.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical movement whose scientific treatises, legal reforms, and dictionaries were circulated in standardized Latin type.
