## summary

**BlackBerry** was a pioneering brand of mobile smartphones and wireless data devices developed by the Canadian telecommunications firm **Research In Motion** (**RIM**), based in Waterloo, Ontario. Founded by *Mike Lazaridis* and *Jim Balsillie*, the company revolutionized global enterprise communication by introducing secure, real-time push email, full *QWERTY* thumb keyboards, and proprietary encrypted messaging. At its peak in the mid-2000s, the platform enjoyed unprecedented corporate and governmental ubiquity before succumbing to modern capacitive touchscreen rivals.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1984 as *Research In Motion* (*RIM*) by Canadian engineering students *Mike Lazaridis* and *Douglas Fregin*, who were later joined by business executive *Jim Balsillie*.
- Rose to global prominence with early two-way alphanumeric pagers like the *Inter@ctive Pager 950* in 1998, followed by the monochrome *BlackBerry 850* in 1999 operating over the Mobitex network.
- Named by the branding consultancy *Lexicon Branding*, whose linguists observed that the device's small, angled keyboard buttons resembled the seed drupelets of a fruit.
- Captured enterprise and government markets via *BlackBerry Enterprise Server* (*BES*), which routed corporate email seamlessly to handsets using centralized end-to-end encryption.
- Fostered a cultural sensation through *BlackBerry Messenger* (*BBM*), a proprietary instant messaging service that connected users via unique alphanumeric personal identification numbers (*PINs*).
- Earned the widespread satirical nickname *CrackBerry* due to its addictive influence on corporate executives, lawyers, and politicians who checked messages compulsively.
- Sparked national attention during the 2008 presidential transition when *Barack Obama* successfully negotiated with intelligence agencies to retain a customized, NSA-encrypted handset in office.
- Survived a massive $612.5 million patent infringement settlement with *NTP Inc.* in 2006, avoiding a court-ordered shutdown of its American infrastructure before losing market dominance to the iPhone and Android.
