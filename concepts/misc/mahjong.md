## summary

**Mahjong** is a traditional tile-based tabletop game that originated in nineteenth-century China during the late Qing dynasty. Typically contested by four players, the game revolves around drawing and discarding tiles to assemble valid melds and complete a winning fourteen-tile hand. As a cornerstone of social recreation across East Asia, it evolved into numerous international variants, including Japanese *riichi mahjong* and American tournament forms.

## you gotta know

- Played traditionally with a standard set of 144 tiles featuring three basic suits—Dots (*tong*), Bamboo (*tiao*), and Characters (*wan*)—alongside honor tiles representing the four winds and three dragons.
- Standard gameplay mechanics require players to draw and discard in turns, racing to build combinations known as *pungs* (three identical tiles), *chows* (three consecutive numbers in suit), and *kongs* (four identical tiles), finished by a single matching pair.
- The three dragon tiles consist of *Zhong* (Red Dragon), *Fa* (Green Dragon), and *Bai* (White Dragon), which carry specific scoring values and symbolic cultural meanings.
- Introduced heavily into the [[united-states]] in the 1920s by *Joseph Park Babcock*, leading to the establishment of the *National Mah Jongg League*, which issues an updated annual card of legal hands.
- In [[japan]], the game underwent significant strategic evolution into *riichi mahjong*, distinguished by a declared betting mechanic (*riichi*), the presence of *dora* bonus indicator tiles, and complex defensive strategies.
- Widely played as a festive communal pastime during [[chinese-new-year]] and other family gatherings, serving as an enduring social custom and competitive sport worldwide.

## connections

- [[chinese-new-year]] — traditional holiday during which families gathered together frequently play the game.
- [[japan]] — country where the distinctive, highly tactical *riichi* ruleset was developed and popularized.
- [[united-states]] — destination where early twentieth-century crazes led to unique rules and national leagues.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — calendar governing traditional East Asian festivals where tile games feature prominently.
- [[shogatsu]] — Japanese New Year celebrations where tabletop parlor games are standard recreation.
