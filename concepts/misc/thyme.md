## summary

**Thyme** is an aromatic perennial evergreen herb in the mint family *Lamiaceae*, historically native to the Mediterranean basin. Celebrated since classical antiquity for both culinary enhancement and therapeutic properties, it is prized for its pungent essential oil dominated by the antimicrobial phenol thymol. It constitutes an indispensable foundation of traditional Mediterranean cookery, forming the backbone of iconic herb mixtures such as the French *bouquet garni* and the Levantine seasoning *za'atar*.

## you gotta know

- Derived botanically most commonly from *Thymus vulgaris*, a low-growing woody subshrub with tiny oval leaves and pale purple to white flowers.
- Associated in [[ancient-greece]] with physical bravery and moral courage, where the phrase "to smell of thyme" was considered high praise and sprigs were burned as incense in temples.
- Utilized by the ancient Egyptians as an ingredient in embalming fluids and mummification preparations due to its potent preservative qualities.
- Adopted by the [[roman-empire]] to flavor cheeses and alcoholic spirits, and consumed by soldiers who bathed in infused water before battle to instill vigor.
- Features as a central component of classical seasoning blends such as the French *herbes de Provence*, *bouquet garni*, and Middle Eastern *za'atar*.
- Contains the monoterpene phenol thymol, which exhibits strong natural antiseptic and antifungal properties, historically utilized in surgical dressings and modern mouthwashes.
- Symbolic during the European Middle Ages of chivalry, often embroidered onto knights' scarves and tunics by noblewomen before jousting tournaments.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — civilization that burned the herb as incense and considered it an emblem of divine bravery.
- [[roman-empire]] — empire that spread its cultivation across Europe for medicinal baths and culinary flavoring.
- [[middle-east]] — native regional homeland where the herb remains central to staple mixtures like *za'atar*.
- [[france]] — culinary tradition where it serves as an indispensable pillar of classic stocks and roasted dishes.
- [[human-anatomy]] — target of historical herbal pharmacology, particularly for soothing the respiratory and digestive systems.
