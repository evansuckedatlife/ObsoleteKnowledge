## summary

**Sony** (formally the **Sony Group Corporation**, originally founded as **Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo**) is a multinational conglomerate headquartered in *Tokyo*, *Japan*, that became a defining pillar of global consumer electronics, digital entertainment, and mass media. Founded in 1946 by engineer *Masaru Ibuka* and physicist *Akio Morita* in the devastation following *World War II*, the enterprise pioneered the miniaturization of transistor radios, personal audio cassette players, and high-fidelity video devices. In quiz bowl, Sony appears extensively in questions concerning postwar industrial recovery, legendary corporate rivalries such as the *Betamax* format war, groundbreaking audio standards like the *Compact Disc*, and the revolutionary debut of the *PlayStation* video game console.

## you gotta know

- Created the iconic *Walkman* portable cassette player (model TPS-L2) in 1979, fundamentally reshaping global youth culture and personal music consumption by enabling users to carry high-fidelity stereo sound in their pockets.
- Revolutionized the digital home gaming industry in 1994 with the launch of the original *PlayStation*, a CD-ROM gaming console conceived after a collaborative project with *Nintendo* collapsed into acrimony.
- Founded in May 1946 by *Masaru Ibuka* and *Akio Morita* in a fire-damaged Tokyo department store under the name *Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo* (Tokyo Telecommunications Engineering Corporation), with an initial capital of roughly five hundred dollars.
- Lost the legendary videotape format war of the late 1970s and 1980s, in which its technically sharper *Betamax* standard was commercially overwhelmed by JVC's longer-playing and open-licensed *VHS* format.
- Engineered the *Trinitron* television system in 1968, which utilized a single electron gun and a patented vertically striped aperture grille to provide picture brightness and clarity far superior to conventional shadow-mask screens.
- Partnered with Dutch electronics giant Philips in the late 1970s and early 1980s to co-develop the *Compact Disc* (CD) format, establishing the Red Book standard and famously setting the disc capacity to 74 minutes at the insistence of corporate president *Norio Ohga* to accommodate Beethoven's Ninth Symphony.
- Entered the American film industry through its landmark 1989 acquisition of *Columbia Pictures Entertainment* for 3.4 billion dollars, expanding its media portfolio alongside its major recorded music publishing arm.
- Introduced the first commercially successful all-transistor radio in Japan, the TR-55, in 1955, simultaneously adopting the brand name Sony, derived from the Latin *sonus* (sound) and the mid-century American colloquialism *sonny boy*.

## connections

- [[japan]] — home nation where the corporation was founded in Tokyo and drove postwar economic resurgence.
- [[world-war-ii]] — destructive conflict whose aftermath left Tokyo devastated, motivating the founders to rebuild domestic industry.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical era during which the corporation expanded exports across Western markets and established global dominance.
- [[united-states]] — crucial export market and jurisdiction where the firm acquired major entertainment entities such as Columbia Pictures.
- [[modernism]] — design philosophy whose functional minimalism and clean lines heavily influenced Japanese industrial product aesthetics.
- [[soy-sauce]] — fellow prominent culinary export that emerged alongside consumer goods as a global symbol of Japanese craftsmanship.
- [[southwest-airlines]] — American commercial airline whose fleets transported generations of business travelers carrying portable personal electronics.
