## summary

The **mojito** is a traditional Cuban highball cocktail composed of white rum, sugar, lime juice, sparkling soda water, and fresh spearmint. Originating in Havana, the drink balances sweet citrus and refreshing herbaceous notes to mitigate the heat of the Caribbean climate. Celebrated across world cocktail culture, it represents an iconic culinary confluence of Caribbean sugarcane cultivation, indigenous herbal traditions, and transatlantic history.

## you gotta know

- Traditional preparation requires gently muddling fresh mint leaves with sugar and fresh lime juice in a highball glass before adding ice, white rum, and club soda.
- Traced historically to a sixteenth-century medicinal concoction called *El Draque*, named after English privateer *Sir Francis Drake*, who mixed cane spirit with lime and mint to treat scurvy.
- Evolved into its modern refined form during the mid-nineteenth century with the founding of the Bacardi rum distillery in Santiago de Cuba, which popularized crisp white rum.
- Famously associated in popular literary lore with American author *Ernest Hemingway*, who purportedly enjoyed them at Havana's storied tavern *La Bodeguita del Medio*.
- Etymologically linked either to the Cuban citrus-garlic seasoning *mojo* or derived from the Spanish word *mojadito*, meaning *a little wet*.
- Requires delicate muddling of the mint to bruise the leaves and express aromatic essential oils without shredding the plant matter or releasing bitter chlorophyll compounds.
- Became one of the defining cocktails of the international cocktail renaissance in the late 1990s and 2000s, praised for its aromatic refreshment.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — historical system establishing the Caribbean plantation economy, sugarcane cash-crop production, and rum distillation.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — seafaring era that introduced sugarcane, citrus fruits, and European maritime figures like *Sir Francis Drake* to the West Indies.
- [[african-diaspora]] — enslaved African laborers whose labor and knowledge formed the backbone of Caribbean sugarcane and agricultural systems.
- [[spain]] — colonial power that governed Cuba for centuries, introducing European distillation technologies and culinary influences.
- [[american-literature]] — literary tradition prominently featuring *Ernest Hemingway*, whose Caribbean travels popularized the drink worldwide.
- [[united-states]] — northern neighbor where Prohibition drove wealthy tourists to Havana bars, helping cement the cocktail's international renown.
