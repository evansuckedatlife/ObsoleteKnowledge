## summary

The **Canadian Flag**, formally known as the *National Flag of Canada* and commonly referred to as the *Maple Leaf* (*l'Unifolié*), is Canada's primary national symbol, consisting of a red field featuring a white central square charged with a stylized, eleven-pointed red maple leaf. Adopted in 1965 following the contentious Great Flag Debate, it replaced the *Canadian Red Ensign* as the official banner of the nation. The flag serves as an enduring visual hallmark of Canadian sovereignty, constitutional autonomy from the *[[british-empire]]*, and national unity.

## you gotta know

- Officially inaugurated on February 15, 1965, on Parliament Hill in Ottawa, a date commemorated annually across the country as National Flag of Canada Day.
- Replaced the *Canadian Red Ensign*, which bore the Union Jack in the canton and the Canadian coat of arms in the fly, sparking intense national debate over British heritage and post-colonial identity.
- Adopted following the fierce *Great Flag Debate* of 1963–1964, championed by Liberal Prime Minister *Lester B. Pearson*, who encountered vehement opposition from Conservative leader *John Diefenbaker*.
- Designed primarily by historian and academic *George Stanley*, whose concept was inspired by the flag of the *Royal Military College of Canada* in Kingston, Ontario.
- Features a unique heraldic layout called a *Canadian pale*, where the central white stripe occupies exactly half the width of the flag rather than the standard one-third of a conventional triband.
- Employs an eleven-pointed stylized maple leaf refined by graphic designer *Jacques Saint-Cyr*, selected after wind-tunnel tests demonstrated that eleven points remained crisp and legible in strong wind conditions.
- Uses the official national colors of red and white, which were proclaimed for Canada by King George V in the 1921 Royal Arms of Canada, representing historic French and British royal traditions.
