## summary

**Fratricide** is the act of killing one's brother, or in an extended legal and institutional sense, the murder of a countryman, comrade, or sister. Deeply embedded in the mythologies, sacred texts, and royal succession laws of world civilizations, the deed represents one of humanity's most primal taboos against shattering sacred familial bonds. In modern military doctrine, the term is also used technically to designate friendly fire, wherein combat forces inadvertently fire upon or destroy allied troops during military engagements.

## you gotta know

- The Book of Genesis records the world's first biblical murder as Cain slaying his brother Abel in a fit of jealousy after God favored Abel's pastoral sacrifice over Cain's agricultural offering in [[christianity]].
- The founding myth of [[rome]] centers on Romulus murdering his twin brother Remus after Remus mockingly leaped over the freshly dug furrow marking the sacred walls of the new city on the Palatine Hill.
- In ancient Greek drama and myth, the sons of Oedipus, Polynices and Eteocles, slaughter each other in mutual combat before the gates of Thebes in Aeschylus's classic [[tragedy]] *Seven Against Thebes*.
- The Ottoman Empire formalized dynastic brother-killing under Sultan Mehmed II's legal code, the *Kanunname*, which legally permitted newly crowned sultans to execute their brothers to eliminate succession civil wars.
- William Shakespeare dramatized the catastrophic consequences of brother murder in *Hamlet*, where the ghost reveals that his brother Claudius poisoned him while he slept in his orchard in [[william-shakespeare]].
- In Spanish history, King Peter of Castile was killed by his illegitimate half-brother Henry of Trastámara in 1369 during the Castilian Civil War at the Castle of Montiel.
- Fyodor Dostoevsky explored the psychological and moral boundaries of kin murder in *The Brothers Karamazov*, where the trial of Dmitri Karamazov interrogates both parricide and moral complicity among brothers.
- Modern military protocols apply the term specifically to friendly fire incidents, deploying laser target interrogators and digital tracking systems to prevent battlefield fratricide among allied formations.

## connections

- [[galentines-day]] — cultural celebration of mutual loyalty and non-destructive voluntary kinship, offering an antithesis to dynastic fratricidal betrayal.
- [[christianity]] — faith tradition whose foundational Genesis narrative introduces moral culpability and wandering exile through Cain's murder of Abel.
- [[rome]] — ancient civilization whose imperial origin narrative was sanctified through Romulus's foundational slaying of his twin brother Remus.
- [[ancient-greece]] — culture whose tragic theatrical tradition repeatedly interrogated the civic and divine curses unleashed by kin-slaying.
- [[tragedy]] — dramatic genre that frequently employs the slaughter of brothers as the ultimate emotional catalyst for societal collapse.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — playwright who explored the spiritual horror of brother-slaying in masterworks like *Hamlet* and *Richard III*.
- [[roman-empire]] — empire plagued by bloody dynastic rivalries, including Emperor Caracalla ordering the assassination of his younger brother Geta.
