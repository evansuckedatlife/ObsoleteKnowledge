## summary

**Za'atar** (also transcribed as **Zaatar** or **Zahtar**) refers both to a specific genus of wild culinary herbs native to the Levant and to an ancient, omnipresent Middle Eastern condiment blend. The herbal component typically centers on wild Syrian oregano (*Origanum syriacum*), which is dried and blended with ground sumac berries, toasted sesame seeds, and salt. An indispensable culinary emblem across Levantine, Palestinian, Lebanese, Syrian, and Jordanian foodways, it is praised both for its distinctive aromatic profile and its deep roots in regional folk medicine.

## you gotta know

- Blends crushed wild oregano, thyme, or marjoram with acidic ground sumac (*Rhus coriaria*) for tart fruitiness and nutty toasted sesame seeds (*Sesamum indicum*) for textural richness.
- Traditionally mixed with olive oil to form a paste and spread across dough to bake *man'ousheh*, the quintessential Levantine morning flatbread cooked upon a convex metal griddle called a *saj* or inside a stone hearth oven.
- The base plant is historically and botanically identified with the biblical plant *ezov*, translated into English as hyssop, which was used for ritual sprinkling and purification ceremonies in the Book of Leviticus and during the first Passover.
- Twelfth-century medieval philosopher and physician *Maimonides* praised its health-restoring properties and prescribed its medicinal decoctions to relieve respiratory complaints, digestive ailments, and bodily melancholia.
- Widely celebrated in Levantine folklore as a mental stimulant; parents routinely feed flatbread coated with the mixture to children before school examinations to sharpen memory and concentration.
- In 1977, the Israeli government declared wild *Origanum syriacum* a protected endangered plant species, prohibiting public foraging and penalizing harvesting, which sparked significant political and cultural debate among Arab foraging communities.
- Variations across the Levant include Aleppo varieties tinted red by heavier portions of sumac, Jordanian blends rich in roasted wheat or chickpeas, and herbal Palestinian green mixes.
