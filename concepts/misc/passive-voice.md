## summary

The **passive voice** (or **passive construction**) is a grammatical voice wherein the grammatical subject of a clause undergoes or receives the action expressed by the verb rather than performing it. In English, it is characteristically formed using a conjugated form of the auxiliary verb *to be* alongside a past participle, optionally appending the original agent within a prepositional *by*-phrase. Widely employed across academic, legal, and scientific registers to emphasize results or deliberately obscure agency, it is also a frequent target of stylistic critique in prescriptive grammar.

## you gotta know

- Contrasts directly with the active voice, in which the semantic agent serves as the grammatical subject performing the action (e.g., "The chef cooked the meal" versus "The meal was cooked by the chef").
- The semantic agent may be omitted entirely in what is known as a "short passive" or "agentless passive" (e.g., "Mistakes were made"), a device frequently scrutinized in political rhetoric for deflecting accountability.
- In English syntax, forming the passive involves "promoting" the direct object of a transitive active sentence to the grammatical subject position, while "demoting" the active subject to an oblique prepositional phrase or dropping it altogether.
- English also possesses a colloquial "get-passive" (e.g., "He got arrested"), which often carries an informal tone or implies adversity or personal involvement in the event.
- Classical languages such as Latin and Ancient Greek feature dedicated synthetic passive and middle-voice inflectional paradigms rather than relying solely on periphrastic auxiliary verbs.
- Scientific writing traditionally favored the agentless passive voice to project empirical objectivity and depersonalize research methodology, although modern style guides increasingly encourage active constructions.
- Famous style manuals, notably Strunk and White's *The Elements of Style* and George Orwell's essay *Politics and the English Language*, heavily criticized the passive voice as weak, evasive, and needlessly wordy.
