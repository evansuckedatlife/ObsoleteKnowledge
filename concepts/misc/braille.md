## summary

**Braille** is a tactile writing and reading system composed of raised dots arranged in rectangular cells, designed to enable visually impaired and blind individuals to read and write through touch. Developed in *France* during the 1820s by *Louis Braille*, who was blinded in a childhood accident, the system revolutionized accessibility, literacy, and independence for millions of people worldwide. Its structured mathematical simplicity and adaptability across global languages, music, and science have cemented its status as one of the most transformative communication innovations of modern history.

## you gotta know

- Standard literary cells use a six-dot matrix arranged in two parallel vertical columns of three dots each, allowing sixty-four distinct configurations including the empty space.
- Invented by fifteen-year-old French student *Louis Braille* at the *Institution Royale des Jeunes Aveugles* in Paris, refining an earlier military tactile cipher.
- Derived from *night writing* (*écriture nocturne*), a twelve-dot tactile cipher invented by French artillery officer *Charles Barbier* to allow soldiers to read orders silently in darkness without lanterns.
- *Grade 1 Braille* transcribes text letter-by-letter, while *Grade 2 Braille* uses an extensive inventory of contractions, abbreviations, and ligatures to increase reading speed and reduce document bulk.
- *Nemeth Code*, formulated by mathematician *Abraham Nemeth*, extends the tactile system to transcribe complex arithmetic, algebraic notation, and advanced calculus.
- Specialized variants include tactile musical notation, computer eight-dot codes, and localized adaptations for non-alphabetic writing systems like Chinese and Japanese.
- The *Louis Braille* bicentennial in 2009 was marked globally by commemorative coins, postage stamps, and inclusion in the *World Braille Day* observance established on January 4 by the United Nations.
