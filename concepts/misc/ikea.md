## summary

**IKEA** is a multinational home furnishings conglomerate founded in 1943 by seventeen-year-old Swedish entrepreneur *Ingvar Kamprad*. Renowned worldwide for its flat-pack furniture, self-assembly model, and distinctive warehouse retail layouts, it popularized democratic design and functionalist Scandinavian aesthetics across the globe. It revolutionized global retail supply chains and consumer interior design in the post-war era.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1943 in Älmhult, Småland, Sweden; its acronym derives from founder *Ingvar Kamprad*, his family farm *Elmtaryd*, and the parish village *Agunnaryd*.
- Pioneered flat-pack ready-to-assemble furniture in 1956 after employee *Gillis Lundgren* unbolted the legs of a *Lövet* leaf-shaped side table to fit it inside a car trunk.
- Employs a trademark one-way winding store layout known as the "long natural path," designed to guide consumers past curated showroom vignettes into a self-service warehouse.
- Relies on a systematic Nordic naming convention for products: bathroom items named after lakes and bodies of water, rugs named after Danish towns, bookcases after professions or Scandinavian male names like *Billy*, and bedroom textiles after flowers and plants.
- Famous for its in-store cafeterias serving Swedish meatballs with lingonberry jam, introduced intentionally by Kamprad under the philosophy that hungry shoppers buy fewer furnishings.
- Operates under a complex corporate structure historically anchored by the Dutch *Stichting INGKA Foundation* and the Inter IKEA Group to minimize taxation and prevent hostile takeovers.
- Published an annual print catalogue from 1951 until 2020, which at its peak had a larger annual print circulation than the Bible.

## connections

- [[modernism]] — heavily adopted mid-century Scandinavian functionalist design principles emphasizing simplicity and utility.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformed mass production, standardized modular components, and globalized distribution logistics.
- [[architecture]] — popularized minimalist interior spaces and democratic furniture planning in modern domestic architecture.
- [[world-war-ii]] — established in neutral Sweden during the conflict before expanding rapidly across post-war Europe.
- [[cold-war]] — strategically sourced low-cost manufacturing and timber supplies behind the Iron Curtain in Poland during the 1960s.
- [[united-states]] — opened its first American store in Plymouth Meeting, Pennsylvania in 1985, reshaping suburban consumer culture.
- [[germany]] — established its largest and most lucrative European retail market, where it was nicknamed "das Möbelhaus."
