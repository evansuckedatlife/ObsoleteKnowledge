## summary

**en passant** (French for "in passing") is a special pawn-capturing rule in the game of chess. It permits a pawn that attacks a square skipped by an opposing pawn's initial two-square advance to capture that opposing pawn as if it had advanced only a single square. Standardised across Europe between the late fifteenth and nineteenth centuries, the rule maintains tactical balance and prevents pawns from evading capture by leaping past closed ranks.

## you gotta know

- Dictates that a pawn can capture an opponent's pawn that moves two squares forward from its starting square, exactly as if that pawn had only moved one square.
- Must be executed immediately on the turn directly following the opponent's double-step advance; the right to capture is permanently forfeited if another move is made.
- Requires the capturing pawn to stand on its fifth rank (for White) or fourth rank (for Black) adjacent along the rank to the enemy pawn that just moved.
- Leaves the capturing pawn on the square the opposing pawn bypassed, rather than on the final square the enemy pawn landed upon.
- Formulated alongside the introduction of the pawn's initial double-step move during the late Middle Ages and early modern era to preserve the defensive integrity of pawn structures.
- Solves an evasion exploit where advancing two squares would otherwise allow pawns to slip past enemy control zones without engaging in combat.
- Frequently features in chess endgame studies, tactical puzzles, and modern internet culture as an unexpectedly missed or misunderstood legal maneuver.

## connections

- [[france]] — provided the international French terminology for the maneuver and hosted the leading chess salons of the eighteenth century that helped codify universal tournament rules.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural era in southern and western Europe during which classic chess underwent fundamental rule reforms, speeding up play through pawn double-steps and queen mobility.
- [[spain]] — played a central early role through treatises by *Luis Ramírez de Lucena* and *Ruy López de Segura*, which documented evolving pawn mechanics.
- [[italy]] — retained an alternative traditional rule known as *passar battaglia* ("passing battle") for centuries before eventually harmonising with international standards.
- [[enlightenment]] — saw chess transformed into an analytical intellectual discipline across European cafés, cementing uniform standard rules across regional variations.
