## summary

The term **Québécois** (frequently rendered in English as **Quebecois**) designates the predominantly French-speaking inhabitants, nation, and culture of the Canadian province of Quebec, or specifically the distinct historical lineage of **French Canadians of Quebec**. Rooted in the seventeenth-century settlement of New France and fundamentally transformed by the secularizing *Quiet Revolution* of the 1960s, the identity transitioned from a confessional identity into an assertive modern civic nation. The population is globally recognized for its impassioned defense of Francophone linguistic heritage against continental Anglophone assimilation and its twice-attempted constitutional referendums on political sovereignty.

## you gotta know

- Traces ancestral origins to the seventeenth-century colony of New France founded by *Samuel de Champlain*, which passed to British imperial governance under the 1763 *Treaty of Paris* following the French and Indian War.
- Underwent the transformative *Quiet Revolution* (*Révolution tranquille*) during the 1960s under Premier *Jean Lesage*, breaking the historic dominance of the Roman Catholic Church in favor of a secular, state-led welfare economy and modernized educational infrastructure.
- Enacted *Bill 101* (the *Charter of the French Language*) in 1977 under the *Parti Québécois* government of *René Lévesque*, establishing French as the exclusive official language of public administration, commerce, court proceedings, and primary education.
- Held two major democratic referendums on national sovereignty and constitutional separation from Canada, being defeated first in 1980 by a 60 to 40 percent vote, and narrowly falling short in 1995 when the federalist side prevailed with 50.58 percent of ballots cast.
- Quebec French exhibits distinctive dialectal features studied within [[linguistics]], including seventeenth-century lexical archaisms, vowel diphthongization, phonetic affrication of dental stops before high front vowels, and a celebrated lexicon of sacred profanities derived from Catholic liturgical implements.
- Governed linguistically by the prescriptive authority of the *Office québécois de la langue française*, which actively coins technical terminology, regulates commercial signage, and mediates controversies surrounding gender-neutral [[pronouns]] and inclusive grammar.
- Received official federal recognition within Canada in 2006 following a parliamentary motion introduced by Prime Minister *Stephen Harper*, which formally acknowledged that the Québécois form a nation within a united Canada.

## connections

- [[france]] — the European mother country whose civil legal code, language, and cultural traditions established the bedrock of Quebec society.
- [[colonialism]] — the transatlantic imperial expansion that established New France as a mercantile fur-trading and settlement territory in North America.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power that conquered New France in 1759 on the Plains of Abraham and governed through the landmark Quebec Act of 1774.
- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline analyzing Quebec French's distinctive historical phonology, morphosyntax, and dialectology.
- [[pronouns]] — grammatical elements subject to specialized sociolinguistic dynamics in French, including the *tu* versus *vous* distinction and contemporary nonbinary neologisms.
- [[enlightenment]] — philosophical movement whose concepts of constitutional rights and popular sovereignty influenced Lower Canada's 1837 Patriote rebellion.
