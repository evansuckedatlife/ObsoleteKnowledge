## summary

**Hydro-Québec** is a major Canadian public utility established in 1944 to manage the generation, transmission, and distribution of electricity throughout the province of Quebec. Built upon vast northern river systems, it operates one of the world's largest hydroelectric networks and serves as a cornerstone of Quebec's economic and political identity. The corporation's engineering triumphs and massive renewable power output have also made it a key exporter of clean electricity to northeastern markets in the *United States*.

## you gotta know

- Created during the Second World War under Premier *Adélard Godbout* through the provincial expropriation and nationalization of the private utility *Montreal Light, Heat and Power*.
- Served as the economic catalyst of the *Quiet Revolution* (*Révolution tranquille*) in 1962, when Natural Resources Minister *René Lévesque* championed total nationalization of private electricity providers under the nationalist campaign slogan *"Maîtres chez nous"* ("Masters in our own house").
- Constructed the massive *Manicouagan-Outardes* project along the Saint Lawrence North Shore, featuring the *Daniel-Johnson Dam* (*Manic-5*), one of the world's largest multiple-arch buttress dams.
- Pioneered ultra-high-voltage transmission technology in 1965 by commissioning the world's first commercial 735-kilovolt alternating-current transmission line, designed by engineer *Jean-Jacques Archambault*.
- Launched the colossal *James Bay Project* (*Projet de la Baie-James*) under Premier *Robert Bourassa* in the 1970s, damming the La Grande River to produce tens of thousands of megawatts of power.
- Prompted historic legal and territorial negotiations with Indigenous nations, culminating in the 1975 *James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement* signed with the *Grand Council of the Crees* and northern Inuit communities.
- Suffered widespread electrical grid collapse during the catastrophic North American *Ice Storm of 1998*, leaving millions without power for weeks amidst freezing winter temperatures.
