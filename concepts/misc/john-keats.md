## summary

**John Keats** (1795–1821) was an English Romantic poet whose lyrical genius earned him rapid fame and has only grown since his death from tuberculosis at age 25. Though his career lasted merely five years, his odes and narrative poems rank among the finest in English literature, celebrated for their sensory richness and philosophical depth.

## you gotta know

- Born in London to a middle-class family; trained as an apothecary but abandoned medicine to pursue poetry full-time—a romantic but precarious choice.
- Published three volumes of poetry between 1817 and 1820, gaining recognition quickly within literary circles despite harsh reviews from major periodicals.
- Wrote his most celebrated works in 1819: the *Ode to a Nightingale*, *Ode on a Grecian Urn*, *To Autumn*, and *La Belle Dame sans Merci*—works of extraordinary emotional and sensory power.
- Coined the phrase "negative capability"—the capacity to exist in doubt and uncertainty without reaching for easy answers—a cornerstone of his poetic philosophy.
- Contracted tuberculosis (then called consumption) and moved to Rome seeking warmer climate, but died there in 1821 at 25, largely unknown outside literary circles.
- His sonnet "On First Looking Into Chapman's Homer" celebrates the emotional impact of discovering an old translation, demonstrating his capacity for sublime aesthetic response.
- His love for *Fanny Brawne*, whom he met in 1818, became a consuming passion; their relationship and his illness are intertwined in his late poetry and letters.
- Modern literary criticism views Keats as having achieved in five years what many poets never accomplish—a complete poetic vision combining sensory precision with philosophical depth.
- His letters, considered among the finest in English literature, reveal his aesthetic thinking and his struggle between love, ambition, and disease.
