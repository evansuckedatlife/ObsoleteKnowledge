## summary

The **katana**, an iconic form of traditional Japanese sword or **nihonto**, is a curved, single-edged weapon historically carried by the samurai class of feudal [[japan]]. Developing during the Muromachi period as cavalry tactics gave way to close-quarters infantry combat, the weapon is characterized by its slender curved blade, circular or squared handguard (*tsuba*), and elongated two-handed hilt (*tsuka*). Renowned for its differential clay hardening and folded-steel metallurgy, the blade stands as both a lethal instrument of martial arts and a supreme masterpiece of traditional craftsmanship.

## you gotta know

- Carried thrust through the sash (*obi*) with the sharpened edge facing upward, allowing the warrior to draw the sword and deliver a lethal strike in a single continuous movement known as *iaijutsu* or *battojutsu*.
- Paired with the shorter companion blade known as the *wakizashi* to form the *daisho*, an aristocratic two-sword ensemble that legally demarcated the elite social status and bearing of the samurai during the Edo period.
- Forged through a labor-intensive metallurgical process utilizing *tamahagane*, a high-carbon bloomery steel produced in traditional clay smelting furnaces called *tatara* from black iron sand.
- Features a distinctive undulating temper line known as the *hamon*, created by coating the spine with an insulating clay mixture prior to quenching, which leaves the edge as brittle martensite while preserving tough pearlite in the body.
- Distinguished structurally and historically from the earlier *tachi*, which was longer, more dramatically curved, slung from belt cords with the cutting edge facing downward, and inscribed on the opposite side of the tang (*nakago*).
- Classified by sword scholars into distinct historical eras: *koto* (ancient blades made prior to 1596), *shinto* (new swords crafted up to 1781), *shinshinto* (revival period blades), and *gendaito* (modern hand-forged blades).
- Stripped of its battlefield role following the 1876 Haitorei Edict that prohibited the public carrying of swords, yet preserved across modern society through ceremonial veneration, cultural heritage preservation laws, and martial disciplines like *kendo*.

## connections

- [[japan]] — the island nation where the samurai warrior caste, metallurgical traditions, and martial philosophies flourished.
- [[kirpan]] — another culturally sacred edged blade central to religious identity, martial duty, and ethical self-defense.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the modern conflict in which mass-produced military swords (*gunto*) were manufactured for imperial army officers.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the global transition to mechanized warfare and mass production that ended the military dominance of hand-forged bladed weapons.
- [[art-nouveau]] — the Western design movement whose curvilinear motifs and metalwork drew heavy inspiration from imported Japanese decorative sword guards (*tsuba*).
- [[shogatsu]] — the Japanese New Year celebration during which sacred armaments, blades, and ancestral heirlooms are ritually displayed.
