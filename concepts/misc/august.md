## summary

**August** is the eighth month of the modern Gregorian calendar and the sixth month in the ancient Roman calendar, in which it was initially designated as **Sextilis**. Renamed by the Roman Senate in 8 BCE to honour *Augustus Caesar*, the month commemorates the foundational triumphs that cemented the transition from the Roman Republic to the *[[roman-empire]]*. In the northern hemisphere, it represents late summer and the peak of the *Perseids* meteor shower, serving as an enduring subject of historical literature and astronomical observation.

## you gotta know

- Named in honour of *Augustus Caesar*, the first emperor of the *[[roman-empire]]*, who selected this specific month because his major triumphs—including the conquest of Alexandria and the submission of Egypt—took place during this interval.
- Originally named **Sextilis** in the early ten-month calendar of *[[rome]]*, reflecting its status as the sixth month following the vernal equinox that marked the start of the year in March.
- Maintained a length of twenty-nine days under the pre-Julian republican system until *Julius Caesar* instituted the Julian calendar reform in 45 BCE, expanding the month permanently to thirty-one days.
- Debunked the persistent historical myth claiming *Augustus* stole a day from February so that his namesake month would not be shorter than July, which honored *Julius Caesar*; records confirm that **Sextilis** had already received its thirty-first day under the Julian reform.
- Associated with the *Perseids*, one of the most prominent annual meteor showers studied in *[[astronomy]]*, caused by debris from the comet *Swift-Tuttle* and peaking between the ninth and fourteenth days of the month.
- Featured in the title of *Barbara Tuchman*'s Pulitzer Prize-winning history *The Guns of August*, which chronicles the diplomatic failures and military mobilizations that opened *[[world-war-i]]*.
- Marked historically by fateful twentieth-century milestones in *[[world-war-ii]]*, including the atomic detonations over Hiroshima and Nagasaki on the sixth and ninth days of the month in 1945.

## connections

- [[roman-empire]] — governed by *Augustus Caesar*, whose senate decreed the renaming of the month to mark his political ascendancy.
- [[rome]] — origin of the archaic republican calendar that first classified the month numerically.
- [[astronomy]] — discipline that tracks the annual celestial events of the month, most notably the *Perseids* meteor display.
- [[italy]] — geographic heartland where the ancient civic celebrations known as the *Feriae Augusti* originated.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflagration triggered by the rapid military mobilizations across Europe during this month in 1914.
- [[world-war-ii]] — worldwide conflict concluded following the decisive atomic strikes and imperial surrender that occurred in this month in 1945.
- [[ancient-greece]] — cultural civilization whose mythological hero *Perseus* gave his name to the major meteor radiant active during this period.
