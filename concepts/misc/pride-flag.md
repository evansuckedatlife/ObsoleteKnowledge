## summary

The **pride flag**, most prominently realized as the **rainbow flag** or **LGBT pride flag**, is an international vexillological emblem representing lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, and queer social movements. Conceived in San Francisco in 1978 by artist and activist *Gilbert Baker*, the design originated as a community symbol of liberation, celebration, and diversity intended to replace the reclaimed pink triangle from World War II. Across decades of civil agitation, its evolving iterations—ranging from the original hand-dyed eight-stripe banner to modern variants honoring transgender people and racial minorities—have become universal signifiers of solidarity and human rights across the [[united-states]] and the globe.

## you gotta know

- Designed in 1978 by *Gilbert Baker* at the urging of city supervisor *Harvey Milk* for the San Francisco Gay Freedom Day Parade, where the first two prototypes were hand-stitched and dyed by a volunteer collective including *Lynn Segerblom* and *James McNamara*.
- The original version contained eight distinct horizontal stripes, with each color assigned a specific symbolic attribute: hot pink for sex, red for life, orange for healing, yellow for sunlight, green for nature, turquoise for magic and art, indigo for serenity, and violet for spirit.
- Modified to seven stripes and subsequently six stripes when commercial mass production faced shortages of hot pink fabric, with turquoise and indigo later consolidated into royal blue to facilitate split-street display banners following the assassination of *Harvey Milk*.
- Replaced the downward-pointing pink triangle, a Nazi concentration camp badge reclaimed by activists during the post-Stonewall era, providing an affirming and life-affirming rather than trauma-derived badge of identity for the modern [[civil-rights-movement]].
- The *Progress Pride flag*, designed in 2018 by *Daniel Quasar*, incorporated a five-color chevron containing white, pink, light blue, brown, and black along the hoist to visibly center transgender people and communities of color within the broader coalition.
- The South African gay pride banner and the *Philadelphia Pride flag* of 2017 similarly added black and brown stripes to address systemic racism, while related heraldry like the bisexual and transgender banners (the latter created by *Monica Helms* in 1999) expanded autonomous community representation.
- Associated sociopolitically with the linguistic declaration of personal [[pronouns]] and institutional public displays, frequently becoming a prominent focal point of diplomatic diplomacy, corporate marketing debates, and municipal flagpole litigation in metropolitan centers such as [[new-york-city]].
