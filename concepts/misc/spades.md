## summary

**Spades** is a popular four-player partnership trick-taking card game of the *whist* family that originated in the *United States* during the late 1930s. Played using a standard 52-card deck, the game is characterized by spades serving as the permanent, non-negotiable trump suit and partners bidding their exact trick expectations before gameplay begins. It holds an enduring and celebrated cultural stature within the *African-diaspora* and *African American* communities, while also serving as a classic social pastime across the American armed forces.

## you gotta know

- Uses the spade suit as the permanent trump suit throughout every hand, distinguishing it from related trick-taking games like *bridge* where players actively bid for trump suit supremacy.
- Requires players to bid the number of tricks their partnership expects to take, with a *nil* bid denoting a player's commitment to winning zero tricks, yielding a large bonus if achieved but a harsh penalty if failed.
- Penalizes partnerships that fail to make their bid by subtracting ten points for every trick bid, while assigning a single point for each extra trick taken, known colloquially as *overtricks*, *bags*, or *sandbags*.
- Enforces an accumulation penalty for bags, typically docking a partnership one hundred points whenever it accrues ten total bags, discouraging players from underbidding to secure safe contracts.
- Originated during the late 1930s, likely among college students in *Cincinnati*, *Ohio*, who sought a brisk, accessible alternative to the complex conventions of contract bridge.
- Became an iconic social institution within *African American* family reunions, HBCU dormitories, and community events, where table etiquette, swift card-slapping, and unspoken partner synergy are deeply venerated.
- Spread extensively through the United States military during *World War II*, the *Cold War*, and the *Vietnam War*, favored by deployed troops due to its simple deck requirements and fast-paced competitive balance.
- Concludes when one partnership reaches a predetermined winning total, traditionally set at 500 points, or when a partnership falls below negative 200 points.
