## summary

The **Buddhist calendar** is a family of lunisolar timekeeping systems used across Southeast Asia and Sri Lanka to regulate agricultural rhythms and religious observances in [[theravada-buddhism]]. Marked formally by the **Buddhist Era**, its chronological numbering tracks the *parinirvana* of *Gautama Buddha* in the sixth century BCE. In nations like Thailand, Myanmar, and Cambodia, traditional mathematical tables govern both lunar monastic feast days and the sidereal calculation of the solar new year festival *Songkran*.

## you gotta know

- Anchored by the **Buddhist Era** (B.E.), which in Thailand and Cambodia is counted as starting 543 years before the Gregorian Common Era, making 2024 CE equivalent to 2567 B.E.
- Calculates the timing of *Songkran* and related regional new year festivals such as *Thingyan* in Myanmar, based on the sidereal [[astronomy]] of the sun entering the constellation of Aries (*Mesha*).
- Structured as a lunisolar system that intersperses alternating months of 29 and 30 days with periodic intercalary leap months (*Adhikamasa*) and occasional leap days (*Adhika-vara*) to synchronize the lunar year with seasonal cycles, sharing core principles with the broader [[lunar-calendar]] tradition.
- Fixes pivotal festival dates across the Theravada world, notably *Vesak* on the full moon of the sixth lunar month, marking the birth, enlightenment, and passing of the *Buddha*.
- Determines the monastic calendar and ascetic calendar milestones, including *Vassa* (the three-month Rains Retreat) beginning in the eighth lunar month and ending with the celebratory *Pavarana* and gift-giving *Kathina* ceremonies.
- Inherited its mathematical foundations and astronomical tables from ancient Indian treatises, most prominently the classic text *Surya Siddhanta*.
- Features slight regional divergence in year reckoning: while Southeast Asian traditions treat the year of the *Buddha*'s death as year zero (yielding a 543-year offset), the Sri Lankan and Burmese traditions count it as year one (yielding a 544-year offset).
- Modernized under King *Chulalongkorn* (*Rama V*) of Siam in 1888 as the official national calendar, which Thailand later harmonized with the international civil calendar by adopting January 1 as the civil New Year while retaining Buddhist Era numbering.

## connections

- [[theravada-buddhism]] — the monastic tradition whose holy days, ordination periods, and ritual fasts are dictated by the calendar.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — the foundational timekeeping model governing its synodic lunar months, phase tracking, and intercalary leap periods.
- [[astronomy]] — the celestial science that provides the sidereal solar calculations required to fix the entry of the sun into Aries.
- [[islamic-calendar]] — an alternative religious timekeeping system that maintains a strictly lunar cycle without seasonal intercalary adjustments.
- [[chinese-new-year]] — a prominent neighboring lunisolar festival whose calculation similarly relies on solar terms to prevent seasonal drift.
- [[tet]] — the Vietnamese new year festival celebrated according to East Asian lunisolar cycles alongside regional spring observances.
- [[shogatsu]] — the Japanese new year observance historically coordinated with lunisolar months prior to modern solar calendar reforms.
