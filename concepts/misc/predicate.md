## summary

A **predicate** is the fundamental constituent of a sentence or proposition that expresses an action, state, or property attributed to the grammatical subject. Originating in ancient philosophical logic and codified across structural and generative grammar, it encompasses the finite verb along with its objects, complements, and adverbial modifiers. In formal semantics and modern logic, the term denotes a function that maps arguments onto truth values.

## you gotta know

- Traditional grammar defines the bipartite structure of a simple declarative clause as consisting of a subject and a predicate, where the subject represents the topic and the predicate asserts something about it.
- In modern formal logic established by *Gottlob Frege*, the concept was generalised into a function taking one or more arguments, replacing subject-predicate analysis with mathematical function-argument notation.
- In generative syntax and phrase-structure grammar, the predicate is formally identified with the Verb Phrase (VP) or extended verbal projections that take internal arguments.
- Distinction between primary predication (the main clausal assertion) and secondary predication, such as depictives ("He drank the tea *cold*") or resultatives ("They painted the fence *white*").
- Aristotle introduced the philosophical foundation of predication in the *Categories* and *On Interpretation*, examining how substances receive universal attributes.
- Predicate valency denotes the number of syntactic arguments a verbal head requires, categorising clauses as intransitive (monadic), transitive (dyadic), or ditransitive (triadic).
- Often works in conjunction with a **[[preposition]]** to head prepositional phrases that satisfy subcategorised oblique argument slots or provide optional adjunct modifiers.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — primary discipline investigating the syntactic configurations, argument structures, and semantic interpretations of clausal predicates.
- [[preposition]] — heads prepositional phrases that often serve as oblique arguments or adjuncts within the wider predicate phrase.
- [[prefix]] — morphological affix attached to verbs to alter verbal aspect, valency, and the resulting argument structure of the predicate.
- [[ancient-greece]] — birthplace of classical logic and grammar, where *Aristotle* pioneered philosophical theories of predication and categories.
- [[enlightenment]] — era whose logicians and rationalist grammarians examined the universal structure of propositions and predication.
- [[geometry]] — formal mathematical field whose axiomatic and relational proofs inspired Frege's modern logic of relational predicates.
