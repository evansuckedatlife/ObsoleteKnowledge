## summary

**Galangal** refers to the aromatic rhizome of several perennial plant species in the ginger family (*Zingiberaceae*), particularly *Alpinia galanga* and *Alpinia officinarum*, native to Southeast Asia. Distinct from common ginger in its sharp, citrusy, pine-like flavor and denser, woody texture, it serves as an indispensable culinary and medicinal ingredient throughout maritime and mainland Southeast Asian cuisine. Traded internationally since antiquity, it played a prominent role in historic maritime spice routes, medieval European pharmacology, and traditional Asian medicine.

## you gotta know

- Primarily distinguished into greater galangal (*Alpinia galanga*), widely cultivated across Indonesia and Thailand, and lesser galangal (*Alpinia officinarum*), indigenous to southern China.
- Functions as an essential culinary base in Southeast Asian cooking, providing the foundational flavor profile for Thai dishes like *tom yum* and *tom kha gai*, as well as Indonesian spice pastes (*bumbu*).
- Known in Indonesian and Malaysian culinary traditions as *lengkuas* or *laos*, forming a critical flavor component in slow-cooked dishes such as *rendang*.
- Differentiates sharply from common culinary ginger (*Zingiber officinale*) by possessing a firmer, paler exterior, a woody flesh that resists easy grating, and prominent floral, camphoraceous notes.
- Mentioned by medieval European herbalists such as the German mystic *Hildegard of Bingen*, who praised the root as a restorative spice of life.
- Traded along ancient maritime routes by Arab and Persian merchants, entering medieval European pharmacopoeias under forms of the Arabic name *khalanjan*.
- Extensively utilized in traditional Ayurvedic, Unani, and Chinese medical systems to treat digestive ailments, respiratory inflammation, and abdominal pain.
