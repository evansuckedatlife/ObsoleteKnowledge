## summary

**White Trillium**, scientifically termed *Trillium grandiflorum*, is a perennial herbaceous woodland wildflower native to eastern North America. Renowned for its conspicuous three-petaled white flowers that turn pink with age, it serves as an iconic spring ephemeral across temperate deciduous forests. It holds significant cultural prominence as the official provincial floral emblem of Ontario and the state wildflower of Ohio.

## you gotta know

- Classic spring ephemeral woodland wildflower displaying three distinct white petals that gradually fade to light pink as the blossom ages and approaches pollination.
- Characterized botanically by a strict 3-part whorled structural symmetry, bearing three petals, three green sepals, and three broad leaf-like bracts atop a solitary unbranched stem.
- Designated officially as the provincial floral emblem of Ontario in 1937 and serves as the state wildflower of Ohio.
- Seeds possess specialized lipid-rich appendages called *elaiosomes*, facilitating dispersal by ants through a mutualistic biological process known as *myrmecochory*.
- Highly sensitive forest indicator species that suffers severe population declines from excessive white-tailed deer browsing and canopy disruption.
- Protected by regional conservation statutes because harvesting the single flower can destroy the underground rhizome and prevent plant survival.
- Native geographic distribution spans the rich deciduous woodlands of eastern North America from Quebec and Ontario south into the Appalachian mountain range.
