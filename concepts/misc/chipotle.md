## summary

**Chipotle** is a smoke-dried, fully ripened jalapeño chili pepper (*Capsicum annuum*) that serves as a cornerstone seasoning across Mexican and Southwestern American cuisines. Originating from pre-Columbian Mesoamerican agricultural practices, the pepper undergoes an extensive dehydration and hot-smoking process over aromatic hardwoods that imparts an earthy sweetness, mahogany color, and complex wood-smoke pungency. Widely utilized either in its whole dried form or reconstituted in a rich, vinegar-infused *adobo* sauce, it provides indispensable depth to traditional marinades, moles, salsas, and braised meat stews.

## you gotta know

- Produced by allowing green jalapeño peppers to remain on the vine until the end of the growing season, where they ripen into deep red fruits with high natural sugar content before undergoing slow smoking in covered earthen pits or kilns.
- Derives its name from the Classical Nahuatl term *chilpoctli* (or *chilpotle*), an agglutinative compound of *chilli* ("chili pepper") and *poctli* ("smoke"), reflecting indigenous food preservation technologies developed prior to Spanish contact.
- Two distinct commercial varieties dominate Mexican markets: the smaller, reddish-purple **morita** (predominantly processed in Chihuahua), which is smoked for a shorter period, and the larger, tan **meco** (also called *chile ahumado* or *chile navideño*), which is smoked for days until completely desiccated and intensely aromatic.
- Commonly packaged and sold canned in **adobo**, a tangy, savory sauce of pureed tomatoes, vinegar, garlic, onions, and herbs that rehydrates the leathery peppers and yields a versatile cooking paste.
- Registers moderate heat on the Scoville Heat Scale, typically rating between 2,500 and 8,000 Scoville Heat Units (SHU), matching the intrinsic capsaicin range of mature jalapeños.
- Commonly balanced in classic Mexican sauces and street food tacos by cooling, fresh herbal garnishes such as chopped [[cilantro]], especially during holidays like [[cinco-de-mayo]].
- Often cross-utilized or paired with South American grilling marinades like [[chimichurri]] in contemporary pan-Latin American barbecue culture.
- Achieved widespread international brand recognition in the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries as the namesake for the fast-casual dining chain *Chipotle Mexican Grill*, established by *Steve Ells* in Colorado in 1993.

## connections

- [[cilantro]] — serves as the primary fresh herbal contrast to balance chipotle's deep, wood-smoked capsaicin heat in Mexican cooking.
- [[cinco-de-mayo]] — integral ingredient in festive braises, salsas, and marinades prepared for holiday banquets across North America.
- [[chimichurri]] — paired or compared in contemporary Latin American grilling traditions as distinct flavor strategies for roasted meats.
- [[spain]] — integrated New World chili peppers into broader Mediterranean trade and introduced the vinegar *adobo* preservation method.
- [[colonialism]] — spurred the global dispersal of the genus *Capsicum* while blending indigenous Mesoamerican preservation techniques with European ingredients.
- [[five-senses]] — stimulates chemical thermal receptors through capsaicin while activating olfaction via volatile phenolic smoke compounds like guaiacol.
