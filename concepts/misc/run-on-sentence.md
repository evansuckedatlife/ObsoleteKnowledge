## summary

A **run-on sentence** is a grammatical construction in which two or more independent clauses are joined together without appropriate punctuation or coordinating conjunctions. Contrary to a common popular misconception, a run-on is defined not by excessive physical length, but strictly by syntactical fault, encompassing both *fused sentences* and *comma splices*. In academic trivia and literary history, run-on sentences serve as central touchstones for discussing formal grammar pedagogy, modernist stream-of-consciousness prose, and avant-garde stylistic experimentation.

## you gotta know

- A run-on occurs strictly when two independent clauses (clauses capable of standing alone as complete sentences) are connected without a coordinating conjunction, semicolon, or period; length alone does not make a sentence a run-on.
- The two principal varieties are the *fused sentence* (where clauses collide with no intervening punctuation whatsoever) and the *comma splice* (where independent clauses are improperly joined with only a comma).
- Standard prescriptive grammar offers four basic remedies: separating the clauses with a period into distinct sentences, connecting them with a semicolon, joining them with a comma paired with a coordinating conjunction (*FANBOYS*), or converting one clause into a subordinate dependent clause.
- In literature, deliberate run-on sentences and unpunctuated syntax are employed as artistic devices known as *polysyndeton* or as vehicles for stream-of-consciousness narration.
- *James Joyce* concluded his monumental modernist novel *Ulysses* with *Molly Bloom's soliloquy*, an iconic episode consisting of eight massive, largely unpunctuated sentences spanning thousands of continuous words.
- *William Faulkner* frequently deployed sweeping, syntactically complex sentences bordering on or embracing run-on structures in works like *Absalom, Absalom!*, reflecting thematic obsessions with the unpunctuated, inescapable continuity of historical memory.
- In formal generative syntax and linguistics, sentence boundary constraints are analyzed through parse trees and complementizers rather than prescriptive mechanical punctuation rules.
