## summary

**Nikola Tesla** (widely commemorated simply as **Tesla**) was a Serbian-American inventor, electrical engineer, and mechanical engineer whose visionary discoveries revolutionized modern electrical power systems. Emigrating to the [[united-states]] during the high tide of the [[industrial-revolution]], he developed polyphase alternating current (AC) machinery that ultimately displaced direct current networks in the War of the Currents. In recognition of his foundational contributions to electromagnetism, the 1960 General Conference on Weights and Measures permanently named the SI derived unit of magnetic flux density in his honor.

## you gotta know

- Alternating current and the War of the Currents: Partnering with industrialist *George Westinghouse*, he championed polyphase alternating current systems against the direct current infrastructure of *Thomas Edison*, achieving public triumph by illuminating the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago.
- The AC induction motor: Patented the rotating magnetic field principle and the brushless alternating current induction motor in 1888, eliminating mechanical commutators and enabling reliable long-distance power distribution.
- Niagara Falls hydroelectric power: Designed the core polyphase generation system installed at the Adams Power Plant at Niagara Falls in 1895, creating the first large-scale commercial AC hydroelectric project in the world.
- The Tesla coil: Invented the resonant air-core transformer circuit in 1891, producing high-voltage, low-current, high-frequency electrical discharges essential for early wireless telegraphy, spark-gap transmitters, and gas-discharge lighting.
- Wardenclyffe Tower and wireless power: Constructed a massive telecommunications facility on Long Island designed by architect *Stanford White* and financed by *J. Pierpont Morgan*, intending to broadcast global wireless information and electrical power before bankruptcy halted the project.
- Colorado Springs experiments: Conducted pioneering high-altitude electrical experiments in 1899, discovering terrestrial stationary electrical waves, producing artificial lightning bolts over one hundred feet in length, and tracking high-frequency atmospheric resonance.
- The SI derived unit: In 1960, the Conférence Générale des Poids et Mesures officially designated the derived unit of magnetic flux density as the tesla (symbol T), defined mathematically as one weber per square meter.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the country to which he emigrated in 1884, where he acquired citizenship and patented his most significant inventions.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — provided the manufacturing infrastructure, urban growth, and industrial demand that made his alternating current systems essential.
- [[new-york-city]] — center of his early laboratory experiments, high-society demonstrations, and his final years residing at the Hotel New Yorker.
- [[space-age]] — relied extensively on the high-frequency electronics, telemetry concepts, and radio remote-control principles that he pioneered.
- [[world-war-i]] — era during which military engineers investigated his patents and he advocated for early radio detection and directed-energy defensive weapons.
- [[american-civil-war]] — the conflict whose aftermath spurred the rapid transcontinental telegraphic and electrical expansion into which he arrived.
