## summary

The **Constitutional Convention** (historically documented as the **Philadelphia Convention** or the **Federal Convention of 1787**) convened at the Pennsylvania State House from May 25 to September 17, 1787, to construct a new governmental framework for the fledgling American republic. Spurred by national insolvency and the violent civil unrest of *Shays' Rebellion*, fifty-five delegates discarded their narrow mandate to revise the Articles of Confederation and instead authored the *United States Constitution*. Through prolonged summer debates, the assembly devised foundational compromises governing congressional representation, federal sovereignty, and executive administration that established modern federal governance.

## you gotta know

- Presided over by *George Washington*, whose unanimous election as convention president and dignified silence bestowed indispensable national legitimacy upon the gathering.
- Initiated through *James Madison's* fifteen-point Virginia Plan, presented by *Edmund Randolph*, which boldly proposed scrapping the confederation in favor of a powerful bicameral national legislature.
- Polarized between large states favoring proportional representation and small states backing *William Paterson's* New Jersey Plan, which sought to maintain equal voting power for each state.
- Brokered the Great Compromise (or Connecticut Compromise) through *Roger Sherman*, establishing a dual legislative structure featuring a proportional House of Representatives and an equal-representation Senate.
- Sanctioned the Three-Fifths Compromise, which counted three-fifths of enslaved persons toward congressional apportionments and federal taxation while scrupulously avoiding the explicit word *slave*.
- Formulated the indirect *Electoral College* mechanism for selecting the chief executive, resolving fierce disputes over whether the presidency should be chosen by Congress or popular vote.
- Concluded with thirty-nine delegates signing the parchment document, while prominent delegates *George Mason*, *Elbridge Gerry*, and *Edmund Randolph* refused their signatures over the omission of a Bill of Rights.
- Conducted deliberations behind closed doors in strict secrecy during an oppressively humid Philadelphia summer, with surviving records heavily reliant on *James Madison's* meticulous private journals.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — formed the enduring organic supreme law formulated and transcribed during the four months of deliberations.
- [[united-states]] — transformed thirteen loosely confederated, disputatious former colonies into a cohesive sovereign federal nation.
- [[american-revolution]] — reunited prominent patriot figures, Continental Army veterans, and colonial statesmen striving to stabilize the newly won independence.
- [[enlightenment]] — operationalized political theories of checks and balances, popular sovereignty, and separation of powers derived from *Montesquieu* and *John Locke*.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — rectified structural protections granted to chattel slavery at the convention through the adoption of the Thirteenth, Fourteenth, and Fifteenth Amendments.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — challenged centuries of racial disenfranchisement and institutional inequality directly rooted in the convention's constitutional compromises.
- [[british-empire]] — sought to eliminate parliamentary tyranny while consciously preserving cherished English common law traditions and procedural liberties.
