## summary

A **Montreal-style bagel**, often historically spelled *beigel*, is a distinctive handmade, wood-fired variety of boiled ring bread originating among Jewish immigrant bakers in *Montreal*, *Quebec*. Characterized by a significantly larger hole, a sweeter profile from honey water, and a denser, chewier texture than its cousin in [[new-york-city]], it has become one of Canada's most celebrated culinary traditions. It remains a fixture of competitive food culture and regional identity, anchored in the historic bakeries of the city's *Mile End* neighborhood.

## you gotta know

- Boiled in honey-sweetened water before baking, which imparts a subtle sweetness to the crust that distinguishes it from malt-boiled American styles.
- Baked exclusively in wood-burning ovens fueled by hardwood such as maple, yielding an unevenly charred crust, patchily caramelized surface, and subtle woodsmoke aroma.
- The dough is formulated without salt in traditional recipes, producing a smaller, flatter, and substantially denser crumb with an elongated central hole.
- Rolled, shaped, and seeded entirely by hand on long wooden planks known as boards before being flipped directly onto the stone hearth of the oven.
- Almost universally ordered in one of two canonical varieties: "black seed" topped with poppy seeds or "white seed" coated densely in sesame seeds.
- The historic rivalry between *Fairmount Bagel*, founded in 1919 by *Isadore Shlafman*, and *St-Viateur Bagel*, opened in 1957 by *Myer Lewkowicz*, defines the pastry's local folklore.
- Carried into orbit aboard the International Space Station in 2008 by astronaut *Gregory Chamitoff*, a relative of the owner of *Fairmount Bagel*.

## connections

- [[montreal-style-smoked-meat]] — fellow cornerstone of Jewish-Canadian immigrant food culture anchored in the *Mile End* and *The Main*.
- [[new-york-city]] — origin of the larger, saltier, steam-or-gas-baked bagel standard with which Montreal carries on an enduring culinary rivalry.
- [[france]] — cultural influence on the francophone culinary landscape of *Quebec* where Yiddish baking traditions firmly took root.
- [[united-states]] — destination for bagel export culture and comparative food debates across North American urban centers.
- [[world-war-ii]] — historical era whose displacement brought Eastern European refugees who expanded Montreal's Jewish bakery enclaves.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of mass-mechanization that modern Montreal bagel-makers consciously rejected in favor of hand-rolling and artisanal wood kilns.
