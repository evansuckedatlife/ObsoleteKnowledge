## summary

The **Niger-Congo language family**, classified in earlier mid-twentieth-century scholarship as **Niger-Kordofanian**, is the preeminent language phylum of sub-Saharan Africa and ranks as the largest language family in the world by total number of distinct languages, comprising over 1,500 tongues spoken by hundreds of millions of people. Spanning westward from Senegal across West Africa and sweeping southward through Central, Eastern, and Southern Africa, the family encompasses major linguistic groupings such as Volta-Congo, Mande, Adamawa-Ubangi, and the geographically expansive Bantu branch. In descriptive and comparative [[linguistics]], the phylum is world-renowned for its elaborate morphological systems, pervasive tonality, and characteristic noun classification schemes.

## you gotta know

- The modern genealogical grouping of **Niger-Congo** was established by American linguist *Joseph Greenberg* in his landmark 1963 volume *The Languages of Africa*, which synthesized previously fragmented African classifications into four continental phyla.
- The defining typological trademark of the family is its extensive system of [[noun]] classes, in which words are assigned to semantic and morphological categories via paired singular and plural affixes that trigger alliterative concord across phrases.
- Swahili (*Kiswahili*), a Bantu tongue belonging to the Sabaki subgroup, developed into the primary lingua franca across the African Great Lakes region through historical Indian Ocean maritime trade, absorbing extensive Arabic vocabulary.
- The vast majority of member languages exhibit complex lexical and grammatical tone systems, where contrasting pitch contours alter lexical meaning or conjugate verbal tense and aspect, with non-tonal exceptions such as Swahili being relatively rare.
- The prehistoric Bantu Expansion, initiating around 3000 to 1500 BCE from the Nigeria-Cameroon borderlands, disseminated agriculture, iron metallurgy, and closely related languages across central, eastern, and southern regions of the continent.
- British scholar *Malcolm Guthrie* provided foundational comparative frameworks for the family's largest subgroup by compiling comparative vocabularies, reconstructing Proto-Bantu, and formulating an alphanumeric geographical coding system.
- Enforced transatlantic migrations during the European slave trade carried speakers of numerous West African languages across the Atlantic, heavily contributing grammatical substrate structures and lexical items to Atlantic creoles throughout the [[african-diaspora]].

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — scientific discipline in which historical-comparative methodology established the genetic unity and internal reconstruction of the phylum.
- [[noun]] — grammatical part of speech whose morphological class prefixes and concord agreements form the defining structural feature of the family.
- [[african-diaspora]] — global dispersal of African peoples through which Niger-Congo linguistic features influenced Caribbean and North American creoles.
- [[colonialism]] — historical phenomenon during which European powers divided African speech communities and imposed foreign administrative languages.
- [[british-empire]] — colonial empire that governed extensive territories in West, East, and Southern Africa containing millions of Niger-Congo language speakers.
- [[france]] — European state whose colonial assimilationist policies established French as an official administrative language alongside native Niger-Congo languages.
