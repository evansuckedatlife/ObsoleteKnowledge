## summary

**National Aboriginal Day** was an official annual observance in Canada established in 1996 by Governor General *Roméo LeBlanc* through a royal proclamation to honour the heritage, cultures, and contributions of First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples. Celebrated annually on June 21 to coincide with the summer solstice, the date was chosen for its profound spiritual and cultural significance to many Indigenous communities as the longest day of the year. The observance served as a key federal acknowledgment of Indigenous rights and cultural persistence under the historical framework of [[colonialism]] until its official redesignation in 2017 as [[national-indigenous-peoples-day]].

## you gotta know

- Established on June 13, 1996, by Governor General *Roméo LeBlanc* through a formal proclamation issued after nationwide consultations, fulfilling recommendations made by the *Royal Commission on Aboriginal Peoples* and the national Sacred Assembly chaired by Cree leader *Elijah Harper* in 1995.
- Celebrated annually on June 21 to align with the summer solstice, reflecting traditional ceremonies across numerous Indigenous societies that honoured the sun's highest celestial path and astronomical renewal through [[astronomy]].
- Recognized the distinct cultures and heritages of the three constitutional groupings of Indigenous populations identified in Section 35 of the Canadian Constitution Act, 1982: First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples.
- Emerged from decades of grassroots activism, including the 1982 declaration by the *National Indian Brotherhood* (predecessor to the *Assembly of First Nations*) that proclaimed June 21 as National Aboriginal Solidarity Day.
- Made a statutory paid public holiday in the Northwest Territories in 2001, making it the first Canadian jurisdiction to grant workers paid leave for the celebration.
- Integrated into the federal Celebrate Canada campaign, an eleven-day civic cycle connecting Indigenous heritage to Saint-Jean-Baptiste Day, Canadian Multiculturalism Day, and Canada Day.
- Succeeded in June 2017 when the Canadian government announced that the official title of the event would be changed to [[national-indigenous-peoples-day]] to modernize statutory terminology.
