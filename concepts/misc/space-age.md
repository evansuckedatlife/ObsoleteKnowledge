## summary

The **Space Age** was a cultural and design movement spanning roughly the 1950s through 1970s, born from humanity's ventures into space exploration and Cold War technological competition. Characterized by a sleek, futuristic aesthetic celebrating curves, metallic materials, and streamlined forms, the Space Age influenced everything from architecture and furniture to fashion and automobiles. World's Fairs of the 1960s became temples to this optimistic vision of technological progress, embodying faith in science, automation, and humanity's boundless future.

## you gotta know

- Emerged from the space race between the [[soviet-union]] and [[united-states]], symbolized by the 1957 Sputnik launch and the 1969 moon landing.
- Distinguished by a futuristic aesthetic: sleek, geometric forms; bold primary colors; chrome, plastic, and fiberglass materials; and a sense of upward mobility and progress.
- Manifested across industrial design—furniture like Eero Saarinen's Tulip chairs, cars like the Chevrolet Corvette Stingray, and architecture featuring swooping curves and cantilevered structures.
- Featured prominently in the Seattle World's Fair (1962), New York World's Fair (1964), and Osaka Expo (1970), which showcased visions of automated, technologically advanced futures.
- Celebrated optimism about technology and science, contrasting sharply with Cold War tensions and existential anxieties that swirled beneath the surface.
- Faded by the 1970s as economic recession, environmental concerns, and disillusionment with technological utopianism tempered the era's unbounded optimism.
- Left an enduring design legacy: retrofuturism continues to reference and reimagine Space Age aesthetics in contemporary design, fashion, and popular culture.
