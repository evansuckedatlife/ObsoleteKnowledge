## summary

**Tuning fork** denotes an acoustic resonator consisting of a two-pronged U-shaped metal bar, engineered to emit an exceptionally pure sinusoidal musical pitch when set into mechanical vibration. Invented in 1711 by British royal court musician *John Shore*, the device revolutionized music theory, acoustic physics, and diagnostic medicine by providing an immutable, portable frequency standard that is largely independent of ambient temperature. Its unique geometry causes the two tines to vibrate in antiphase, canceling translational forces at the central handle and allowing the instrument to transmit clear vibrational energy to acoustic sounding boards or human bone.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1711 by *John Shore*, who served as sergeant trumpeter to Queen Anne and lutenist to King George I, the instrument was playfully nicknamed "Shore's pitchfork" by composer *George Frideric Handel*.
- The mechanical design vibrates in a fundamental transverse mode where the two prongs move in exact opposition, ensuring net force at the base stem is zero so hand grip does not dampen oscillation.
- Because its vibrational energy generates almost no high-order overtones, the instrument produces an acoustic waveform that closely approximates a pure sinusoidal wave.
- In medicine, the device is fundamental to audiometric diagnostics through the *Rinne test* (comparing air conduction to bone conduction) and the *Weber test* (detecting lateralization to evaluate conductive versus sensorineural hearing loss).
- French physicist *Jules Antoine Lissajous* used vibrating forks equipped with tiny mirrors in 1855 to generate the complex optical figures now bearing his name (*Lissajous figures*).
- Swiss watchmaker *Max Hetzel* utilized a miniaturized 360-hertz electronic fork to drive the movement of the revolutionary *Bulova Accutron* wristwatch, launched in 1960.
- Modern police radar systems utilize calibrated forks struck against non-metallic objects to simulate precise Doppler shifts and verify radar gun calibration in traffic enforcement.
