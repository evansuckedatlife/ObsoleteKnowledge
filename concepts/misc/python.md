## summary

In classical Greek mythology, **Python** (frequently called **Pytho** in archaic poetry, and sometimes identified with the serpentine drakaina **Delphyne**) was the monstrous chthonic dragon that dwelled at the base of Mount Parnassus and guarded the sacred sanctuary of Delphi. Born from the primordial earth goddess *Gaia*, the great beast guarded the primordial omphalos stone and gave prophetic counsel before being slain by the Olympian archer god *Apollo*. The death of the monster sanctified the holy site, inaugurating the Pythian Games and establishing the institutional seat of the Pythia, the most influential oracle of antiquity.

## you gotta know

- Inhabited the sacred springs of Castalia on the southern slopes of Mount Parnassus, guarding the chthonic sanctuary that was originally consecrated to *Gaia* and subsequently maintained by *Themis*.
- Slain by *Apollo* with a barrage of hundred-fold golden arrows shortly after the young deity's birth on the floating island of Delos, avenging the serpent's earlier pursuit of Apollo's mother *Leto* on orders from the jealous queen *Hera*.
- According to classical etymology preserved in the Homeric Hymns, the monster's rotting carcass gave the site its archaic name of *Pytho*, derived from the Greek verb *pythein*, meaning "to rot" or "to putrefy" under the heat of the sun.
- Required *Apollo* to undergo eight or nine years of ritual servitude in Thessaly under King *Admetus* to expiate the ritual pollution (*miasma*) incurred by slaughtering a primordial chthonic entity, establishing the foundational Greek rites of blood purification.
- Commemorated by the institution of the Panhellenic *Pythian Games*, held every four years at Delphi to celebrate Apollo's triumph through athletic contests and prestigious musical, poetic, and theatrical competitions.
- Lent its name directly to the *Pythia*, the virgin peasant priestess who inhaled subterranean vapors while perched on a bronze tripod within Apollo's inner adyton to deliver hexameter pronouncements for the Delphic [[oracle]].
- The sanctuary built over the dragon's burial ground functioned as the diplomatic and financial treasury overseen by the [[amphictyonic-league]], a sacred confederation of neighboring Greek tribes sworn to protect the shrine.

## connections

- [[tragedy]] — the dramatic genre exemplified by *Aeschylus* and *Euripides*, whose plots frequently center on Delphic oracles and ritual purification.
- [[apollo]] — the Olympian deity of prophecy, music, and healing who slew the serpent and claimed Delphi as his sacred center.
- [[oracle]] — the sacred prophetic institution at Delphi whose priestesses took their title and mystic authority from the slain serpent.
- [[amphictyonic-league]] — the ancient religious council of Greek city-states organized to protect and administer Apollo's sanctuary at Delphi.
- [[delphi]] — the panhellenic sanctuary on Mount Parnassus considered the navel of the ancient world, sanctified by the creature's demise.
