## summary

**Gin Rummy** (often called simply **Gin**) is a two-player card game belonging to the matching-card rummy family, created in 1909 by *Elwood T. Baker* and his son *C. Graham Baker* in *New York City*. Designed as a faster, more dynamic variant of traditional *Conquian* and standard rummy, it became a nationwide cultural phenomenon in the *United States* during the 1930s and 1940s. Its delicate balance of deduction, memory, probability, and risk calculation made it an iconic pastime of mid-century Broadway, Hollywood studios, and soldiers during *World War II*.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1909 at the *Knickerbocker Whist Club* in *New York City* by teacher *Elwood T. Baker*, who named it after the spirit [[gin]] to play upon the word "rummy".
- Played with a standard 52-card deck where cards rank consecutively from *Ace* (valued at 1 point) through *King* (valued at 10 points).
- Requires players to group cards into valid *melds*, consisting of either *sets* (three or four of the same rank) or *runs* (three or more consecutive cards in the same suit).
- Permits a player to *knock* when their unmatched cards, known as *deadwood*, total 10 points or fewer, risking being *undercut* if the opponent holds equal or fewer deadwood points.
- Features *going gin*, which occurs when a player melds all ten cards with zero deadwood, earning a bonus payment and barring the defender from laying off deadwood cards.
- Achieved massive popularity during the Great Depression and *World War II*, becoming the quintessential backstage and filming-lot game of Golden Age Hollywood actors.

## connections

- [[gin]] — alcoholic liquor that inspired the game's playful nomenclature, complementing the existing card game *rummy*.
- [[new-york-city]] — metropolitan birthplace where the game was crafted inside the *Knickerbocker Whist Club*.
- [[united-states]] — nation where the game developed into a preeminent domestic social recreation and competitive tournament sport.
- [[world-war-ii]] — conflict during which portable decks of cards made the game ubiquitous among servicemen in mess halls and transit ships.
- [[american-literature]] — frequent mid-century cultural motif depicted in twentieth-century short fiction, detective novels, and theater.
- [[cold-war]] — era of suburban social gaming in which competitive gin rummy tournaments flourished across North America.
