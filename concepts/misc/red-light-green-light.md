## summary

**Red light, green light** is a traditional children's street and playground game played worldwide under numerous cultural variations and regional names. One chosen player, acting as the *traffic light* or *caller*, stands facing away from a field of runners and shouts verbal signals to govern their forward movement toward a designated finish line. In quiz bowl and general academic competitions, the game is referenced in studies of motor inhibition and child development, cross-cultural comparative folklore, and modern global pop culture via the South Korean television drama *Squid Game*.

## you gotta know

- In the standard American version, the caller faces a wall or tree and shouts *Green light!* to allow participants to run forward, then shouts *Red light!* and spins around to catch anyone moving.
- Players caught moving after the caller turns around are penalized, either by being eliminated from the round or sent back to the starting line.
- The psychological mechanism relies heavily on executive function, vestibular control, and *motor response inhibition*, testing a child's neurological ability to suppress momentum instantly.
- In the *[[united-kingdom]]* and Commonwealth nations, traditional variants of the game include *Grandmother's Footsteps* and *Fairy Footsteps*, where the caller turns without speaking.
- In *[[japan]]*, a near-identical folk game is called *Daruma-san ga koronda* (*The Daruma doll fell over*), where the caller chants the ten-syllable phrase at varying speeds before turning.
- The Korean variant, known as *Mugunghwa kkochi pieot-seumnida* (*The hibiscus flower has bloomed*), gained massive international recognition as the deadly opening contest in the 2021 drama *Squid Game*.
- Variants in non-English cultures include *Un, deux, trois, soleil* in French-speaking countries and *Un, due, tre, stella!* across Italy.
