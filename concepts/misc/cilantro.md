## summary

**Cilantro** is the culinary term applied to the fresh vegetative leaves and stems of *Coriandrum sativum*, a fast-growing annual herb belonging to the family Apiaceae. Native to regions spanning Southern Europe, Northern Africa, and Southwestern Asia, the plant produces both pungent foliage and dried, citrus-scented spherical fruits, which are globally known as **coriander**. In sensory biology and quiz-bowl trivia, cilantro is celebrated as a classic textbook example of genetically determined taste aversion, wherein single nucleotide polymorphisms in human olfactory receptor genes cause a subset of the population to experience its flavor as violently soapy or metallic.

## you gotta know

- In North American culinary usage, **cilantro** refers specifically to the fresh leafy foliage, whereas the dried seeds are designated as coriander; Commonwealth English generally uses the term coriander for all stages of the plant.
- Belongs to the carrot family *Apiaceae*, sharing botanical lineage and umbelliferous flowering structures with parsley, cumin, fennel, anise, dill, and celery.
- Serves as the prime exemplar of genetic sensory variation, where variations in the *OR6A2* olfactory receptor gene cluster heighten sensitivity to specific aliphatic aldehydes such as *(E)-2-alkenals* and *n-alkanals*, causing four to fourteen percent of people to perceive a flavor reminiscent of dish soap or bath cleaner.
- Essential component of Mexican gastronomy, chopped fresh into *pico de gallo*, guacamole, ceviches, and tacos, where its citrus brightness balances the earthy heat of smoked peppers like [[chipotle]], particularly during feasts such as [[cinco-de-mayo]].
- Also known historically as **Chinese parsley** or **Mexican parsley**, forming a bedrock herb in South Asian chutneys and curries, Middle Eastern salads, and Southeast Asian noodle soups such as Vietnamese *pho*.
- Ranks among humanity's oldest cultivated herbs, with archaeological recovery of seeds dating back over 8,000 years in the Pre-Pottery Neolithic site of Nahal Hemar in Israel and ancient Egyptian specimens preserved inside the tomb of *Tutankhamun*.
- The word *coriander* derives through Latin from the Ancient Greek *koriannon*, related to *koris*, meaning a bedbug, alluding to the pungent odor released by the bruised leaves of the plant.
- Frequently contrasted with flat-leaf parsley in South American cuisine, where authentic Argentine [[chimichurri]] strictly excludes it in favor of parsley, despite modern international confusion.
