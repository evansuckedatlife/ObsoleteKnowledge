## summary

**Poutine** is a classic Canadian dish consisting of crispy French fries and fresh cheese curds smothered in hot brown gravy. Originating in rural *Centre-du-Québec* snack bars during the late 1950s, it transitioned from a mocked regional comfort food to a celebrated national emblem of Quebec and broader Canadian identity. The dish's appeal relies on the textural contrast between hot fries, savoury sauce, and squeaky, semi-melted cheddar curds.

## you gotta know

- Traditional preparation requires three distinct components: freshly deep-fried potatoes, fresh cheddar cheese curds (*fromage en grains*), and a light chicken, veal, or beef gravy seasoned with pepper and vinegar.
- Multiple dairy-farming communities in *Centre-du-Québec* claim its invention, most prominently *Warwick*, where restaurateur *Fernand Lachance* allegedly combined curds and fries at a customer's request in 1957, exclaiming that it would make "an awful mess" (*une maudite poutine*).
- *Jean-Paul Roy* of *Drummondville* claims to have introduced the essential hot gravy component in 1964 at his drive-in restaurant *Le Roy Jucep*, initially registering a trademark for the dish.
- Freshness of the cheese curds is critical; unpasteurised cheddar curds must retain their high moisture content to produce the characteristic squeak (*couic-couic*) against the teeth when bitten.
- Long stigmatised by Anglophone media as low-class junk food used to caricature Francophone culture, it underwent a rapid culinary rehabilitation in the 1990s and 2000s, appearing on high-end bistro menus with foie gras and duck confit.
- Numerous variants have emerged across North America, including Italian poutine (*poutine italienne*) topped with Bolognese sauce, disco fries in the [[united-states]], and festive celebrations such as Montreal's *La Poutine Week*.

## connections

- [[france]] — source of the French culinary heritage, mother sauces, and Francophone linguistic roots of Quebec where the dish evolved.
- [[colonialism]] — shaped the settlement, agricultural trade, and distinctive Francophone culture in the Saint Lawrence River valley.
- [[british-empire]] — governed colonial Canada and established the dairy farming and cheddar production traditions that supply Quebec cheese curds.
- [[united-states]] — neighbor whose diner culture adopted related variations such as New Jersey disco fries and gravy-covered chips.
- [[five-senses]] — directly stimulated by the dish's distinct multisensory profile, from the auditory squeak of curds to the olfactory richness of hot pepper gravy.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformed dairy processing and restaurant machinery, enabling commercial fryers and rapid curd distribution.
