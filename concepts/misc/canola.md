## summary

**Canola** refers to a group of genetically bred cultivars of rapeseed developed in Canada during the 1970s to produce edible, neutral-flavored vegetable oil and protein meal. Engineered through traditional selective breeding to drastically minimize bitter *erucic acid* and anti-nutritional *glucosinolates*, it turned an industrial marine lubricant into one of the world's most widely consumed culinary oils. The crop remains a cornerstone of North American agribusiness, modern biofuel development, and international commodity trade.

## you gotta know

- Bred by Canadian agricultural scientists *Keith Downey* and *Baldur Stefansson* at the *University of Manitoba* and *Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada* using *Brassica napus* and *Brassica rapa*.
- Derived its name as a portmanteau of *Canada* and *ola* (signifying oil low acid), trademarked originally by the *Rapeseed Association of Canada*.
- Historically utilized as a vital heavy-duty lubricant for steamships and naval engines during [[world-war-ii]] due to its unique property of adhering tenaciously to wet metal surfaces.
- Required substantial reduction of *erucic acid* below two percent to prevent myocardial lipidosis in mammals, earning official Generally Recognized as Safe (*GRAS*) status from the United States Food and Drug Administration in 1985.
- Reduced sulfurous *glucosinolates* in the processed seed meal, transforming a toxic byproduct into a nutritious, high-protein feed supplement for domestic livestock.
- Became the focal point of agricultural biotechnology disputes and intellectual property law, notably the landmark Canadian Supreme Court case *Monsanto Canada Inc. v. Schmeiser* concerning patented *Roundup Ready* herbicide-resistant genes.
- Distinguishes itself nutritionally with exceptionally low saturated fat content, high monounsaturated oleic acid levels, and an ideal ratio of omega-6 to omega-3 essential fatty acids.

## connections

- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict that caused Allied shortages of naval lubricants and catalyzed domestic Canadian rapeseed cultivation.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the rise of mechanized processing, chemical extraction, and industrial-scale commodity farming that powered mass oilseed distribution.
- [[canadian-jetlines]] — a Canadian enterprise linked to transportation infrastructure that intersects with modern aviation biofuel initiatives utilizing oilseed crops.
- [[cold-war]] — the mid-century geopolitical era during which strategic food supply security and Green Revolution breeding techniques accelerated.
- [[united-kingdom]] — the historic Commonwealth partner where early steam propulsion technology relied heavily on rapeseed-derived mechanical lubricants.
- [[united-states]] — the major export destination and trading partner whose regulatory approval opened vast global commercial consumer markets.
