## summary

**Potato chips**, known throughout the *United Kingdom* and Commonwealth nations as **potato crisps** or simply **crisps**, are thin slices of potato that are deep-fried, baked, or air-fried until crunchy and seasoned with salt or flavorings. Popular legend attributes their invention in 1853 to African-Native American chef *George Crum* at Moon's Lake House in Saratoga Springs, New York, following a patron's complaint about overly thick fried potatoes. Over the twentieth century, mechanical batch fryers, protective wax-paper packaging, and synthetic seasoning applications transformed the snack from an upscale restaurant specialty into one of the world's most ubiquitous mass-market industrial foods.

## you gotta know

- Popularly credited to chef *George Crum* (*George Speck*) at Moon's Lake House in Saratoga Springs, New York, in August 1853, where they were initially dubbed *Saratoga Chips*.
- Historical recipes for thin fried potatoes existed earlier in European cookery books, including an 1817 recipe for potatoes fried in slices or shavings in *William Kitchiner*'s *The Cook's Oracle*.
- In 1926, entrepreneur *Laura Scudder* pioneered packaging the snack in sealed, ironed wax-paper bags, preserving freshness and revolutionizing commercial distribution by preventing sogginess.
- Industrial manufacturer *Herman Lay* founded *Lay's* in Nashville, Tennessee, during the 1930s, establishing the first coast-to-coast snack-food brand using automotive distribution fleets.
- Dublin native *Joe "Spud" Murphy*, founder of the Irish crisp company *Tayto*, invented the world's first seasoned crisps in 1954, introducing cheese and onion flavor and barbecue flavor via chemical flavor bonding.
- In *Great Britain*, the product is exclusively called **crisps**, while the term *chips* refers instead to thick-cut deep-fried potatoes akin to French fries.
- The physics of their auditory appeal relies on high-frequency sound waves generated during mastication, making acoustic crispness an essential component of sensory perception.
- Grew in domestic popularity alongside other nineteenth-century commercial novelties like pictorial [[postcards]] distributed widely throughout resort towns and leisure destinations.
