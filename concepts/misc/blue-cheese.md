## summary

**Blue cheese** (frequently spelled **bleu cheese**) encompasses a celebrated international category of cheeses distinguished by blue, green, or grayish mold marbling running through a white or pale ivory paste. Inoculated with specific fungal cultures such as *Penicillium roqueforti* or *Penicillium glaucum*, these cheeses develop assertive, salty, and pungent sensory profiles through the biochemical breakdown of dairy fatty acids. Originating in medieval European agricultural regions, major varieties rank among the earliest culinary delicacies to receive strict geographic appellation protections.

## you gotta know

- Relies on internal mold cultures, predominantly *Penicillium roqueforti*, whose enzymes metabolize milk lipids into aromatic methyl ketones like *2-heptanone* and *2-nonanone* to produce characteristic sharp flavors.
- Involves the cheesemaking technique of *needling*, in which maturing wheels are pierced with stainless steel rods to open air channels that supply oxygen for internal mold germination.
- Exemplified by *Roquefort*, a historic French raw sheep's milk cheese aged exclusively in the damp limestone caves of Mount Combalou within *Roquefort-sur-Soulzon*.
- Features the Italian cow's milk classic *Gorgonzola*, crafted in Lombardy and Piedmont since the early Middle Ages, available as either youthful, creamy *dolce* or firm, aged *piccante*.
- Anchored in English culinary history by *Stilton*, an unpressed cow's milk cheese legally restricted to Derbyshire, Leicestershire, and Nottinghamshire, traditionally consumed with fortified port wine.
- Produced in northern Spain as *Cabrales*, an intensely sharp artisanal wheel cured in limestone mountain caves within the *Picos de Europa* using cow, goat, and sheep milk.
- Cultivated domestically in the United States through *Maytag Blue*, created during the 1930s by researchers at Iowa State University who adapted homogenized cow's milk to standard ripening protocols.
- Paired frequently in haute cuisine with sweet dessert wines like Sauternes, crusty breads, walnuts, and dark fruits such as the [[blackberry]] to balance pungent mold acidity with natural sweetness.

## connections

- [[france]] — historic home of Roquefort, which earned the nation's inaugural registered *appellation d'origine contrôlée* designation in 1925.
- [[five-senses]] — biological faculties engaged by the cheese's strong olfactory pungency, crumbly mouthfeel, and bold gustatory notes of sodium and methyl ketones.
- [[italy]] — cradle of Gorgonzola production, where medieval alpine dairy practices fostered regional mold-cured cheese varieties.
- [[england]] — home to Stilton cheesemakers, whose regulated creameries established an iconic staple of traditional winter holiday banquets.
- [[blackberry]] — companion agricultural produce whose natural fruit sugars and tart tannins provide a balanced culinary contrast to rich blue curds.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical power whose encyclopedists, including *Pliny the Elder*, documented ancient curd-curing practices across southern Gaul.
- [[ancient-greece]] — Mediterranean culture whose foundational dairy pastoralism and curdling methods influenced subsequent European cheesemaking traditions.
