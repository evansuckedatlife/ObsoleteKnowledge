## summary

The **spring equinox** (or vernal equinox) marks the astronomical moment when the sun crosses the celestial equator, making day and night nearly equal length across the globe. Occurring around March 20 in the Northern Hemisphere, this transitional point has anchored human calendars, mythologies, and renewal celebrations for millennia, signaling the return of growth and fertility after winter dormancy.

## you gotta know

- Occurs when the sun is directly above the equator, creating a moment when the plane of Earth's equator and the plane of its orbit around the sun align; the exact date varies slightly year to year based on Earth's orbital mechanics, but centers around March 20–21 in the Northern Hemisphere.
- Called the *vernal equinox* because "vernal" derives from the Latin *vernus* for spring, distinguishing it from the autumnal equinox in September, which is essentially its opposite in Earth's orbit.
- In the Northern Hemisphere, marks the astronomical beginning of spring; in the Southern Hemisphere, it coincides with the autumn equinox (approximately September 22–23), creating a mirror-image seasonal rhythm.
- The name *equinox* derives from Latin meaning "equal night," because day and night are nearly equal in length (approximately 12 hours each) at this moment worldwide.
- Pivotal for ancient agricultural societies, whose planting calendars and crop prayers were synchronized to this moment of solar alignment; farming communities depended on precise knowledge of this astronomical event.
- Encoded into countless cultural and religious observances: Nowruz in the Persian tradition, Songkran in Southeast Asia, and many pre-Christian fertility rites across Europe, the Mediterranean, and Asia.
- Creates a moment of cosmic symmetry that has resonated symbolically across human cultures as a gateway between seasons, metaphorically representing rebirth and renewal, much like the autumn equinox marks transition into dormancy.
