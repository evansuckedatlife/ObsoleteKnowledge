## summary

The **Seven Wonders**, historically commemorated as the **Seven Wonders of the Ancient World**, was a canonical catalog of extraordinary architectural and sculptural triumphs scattered around the eastern Mediterranean basin during classical antiquity. Compiled by Hellenistic poets, scholars, and travelers such as *Antipater of Sidon* and *Philo of Byzantium*, the list celebrated human ingenuity, engineering majesty, and monumentality. Of the seven classic monuments, only the monumental *Great Pyramid of Giza* survives to the present day, with the remaining six destroyed by earthquake, fire, or warfare.

## you gotta know

- First compiled in the Hellenistic era as *theamata*, meaning sights or marvels to be seen, functioning as travel itineraries for ancient Greek travelers.
- Includes the *Great Pyramid of Giza*, built for the Fourth Dynasty Pharaoh *Khufu*, which stood as the tallest artificial structure in the world for over three millennia and is the only surviving wonder.
- Features the legendary *Hanging Gardens of Babylon*, supposedly built by King *Nebuchadnezzar II* for his homesick wife *Amytis*, though no definitive archaeological proof of their physical existence has been discovered.
- Encompasses the *Statue of Zeus at Olympia*, a colossal chryselephantine ivory-and-gold sculpture crafted by the master Athenian sculptor *Phidias*.
- Includes the *Temple of Artemis at Ephesus*, a magnificent Ionian sanctuary repeatedly rebuilt before being famously torched by the fame-seeking arsonist *Herostratus*.
- Commemorates the *Mausoleum at Halicarnassus*, an ornate tomb constructed in Caria for the satrap *Mausolus*, whose name became the root word for grand monumental tombs.
- Celebrates the *Colossus of Rhodes*, a giant bronze statue of the sun god *Helios* erected by *Chares of Lindos* to celebrate a military siege victory, and the *Lighthouse of Alexandria* on Pharos island.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the cultural, literary, and aesthetic framework within which travelers, poets, and historians codified the canonical list of wonders.
- [[architecture]] — structural engineering feats across classical masonry, load-bearing stonework, and colossal statuary established historical standards for architectural ambition.
- [[middle-east]] — geographical cradle of several wonders, spanning the lands of ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, Caria, and the Aegean coast of Asia Minor.
- [[roman-empire]] — inherited, preserved, and popularized Hellenistic wonder literature, with authors like *Pliny the Elder* recording detailed architectural descriptions.
- [[geometry]] — mathematical principles pioneered by ancient Euclidean and Egyptian surveyors enabled the precise construction of monumental proportions.
- [[percy-bysshe-shelley]] — romantic poetry concerning the inevitable ruin of colossal ancient monuments drew profound philosophical inspiration from classical ruins.
