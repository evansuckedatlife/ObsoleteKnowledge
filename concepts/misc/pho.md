## summary

**Pho** (Vietnamese: *phở*) is a traditional Vietnamese noodle soup consisting of fragrant bone broth, flat rice noodles (*bánh phở*), aromatic herbs, and thinly sliced meat, typically beef or chicken. Developing in northern Vietnam during the early twentieth century around Hanoi and Nam Dinh province, it reflects an intercultural culinary synthesis shaped by French colonial presence and indigenous traditions. Following the mid-century partition of Vietnam and the postwar diaspora, it transformed into a world-famous culinary symbol of Vietnamese national identity.

## you gotta know

- Broth is traditionally simmered for many hours using charred ginger, charred onions, star anise, cinnamon (cassia bark), black cardamom, cloves, and coriander seeds.
- Divided into two primary geographic traditions: northern *phở bắc*, known for its clear, delicate, savory broth and austere garnishes; and southern *phở nam*, which is sweeter, richer, and served with bean sprouts, hoisin sauce, Thai basil, and culantro (*ngò gai*).
- Emerged in the early 1900s in northern Vietnam, heavily influenced by French colonial demand for beef slaughter, which provided abundant marrow bones for soup stock.
- Etymology is commonly hypothesized to derive either from the French beef stew *pot-au-feu* or from Cantonese *ngau yuk phan* (cow-meat flat noodles).
- Main variants are differentiated by protein: *phở bò* (beef, prepared with rare steak, flank, brisket, or tendon) and *phở gà* (poached chicken, popularized during wartime beef shortages).
- The 1954 partition of Vietnam triggered an exodus of nearly a million northern refugees to the south, spreading the dish southward where southern herbs and condiments altered its profile.
- Spread internationally after 1975 when refugees fleeing the fall of Saigon established diasporic communities in North America, Europe, and Australia.

## connections

- [[france]] — colonial power whose cultural and culinary contact in Indochina gave rise to the beef-broth techniques underpinning the dish.
- [[colonialism]] — imperialist system in French Indochina that restructured local cattle usage and fostered modern urbanization.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical struggle resulting in the 1954 partition and 1975 conclusion of the Vietnam War, dispersing the soup worldwide.
- [[united-states]] — destination of the post-1975 diaspora, where large immigrant enclaves popularized the dish across Western culture.
- [[five-senses]] — sensory apparatus engaged by the distinct aroma of star anise, fish sauce, herbs, and textural contrasts of rice noodles.
