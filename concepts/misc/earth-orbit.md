## summary

**Earth's orbit** is the elliptical trajectory by which Earth revolves around the Sun at an average distance of approximately one astronomical unit, or roughly 149.6 million kilometers. This revolution completes a full cycle in roughly 365.25 solar days, governed by gravitational attraction and Keplerian orbital mechanics. Understanding the physical dynamics and cyclical variations of this motion forms the baseline for modern [[astronomy]], celestial navigation, solar timekeeping, and planetary climate modelling.

## you gotta know

- Takes approximately 365.256 days for a complete sidereal year and 365.242 days for a tropical year, necessitating regular leap years in civic timekeeping.
- Governed by *Johannes Kepler*'s laws of planetary motion, moving along an ellipse with an eccentricity of approximately 0.0167 rather than a true circle.
- Reaches *perihelion*, the point of closest approach to the Sun at about 147 million kilometers, during early January in the modern epoch.
- Reaches *aphelion*, the point of greatest distance from the Sun at roughly 152 million kilometers, in early July.
- Features axial tilt, or obliquity, of roughly 23.44 degrees relative to the orbital plane, which serves as the direct physical driver of terrestrial seasons rather than distance from the Sun.
- Undergoes long-term cyclical oscillations in orbital eccentricity, axial tilt, and precession known as *Milankovitch cycles*, which drive geological ice ages over tens of thousands of years.
- Defines the fundamental astronomical baseline known as the *astronomical unit* (*AU*), historically measured by observing transits of *Venus*.
- Constrains the calculation of solar and lunar cycles that underpin historic calendrical systems, seasonal equinoxes, and movable celebrations such as [[easter]].

## connections

- [[astronomy]] — the overarching scientific discipline studying celestial trajectories, stellar parallax, and planetary mechanics.
- [[geometry]] — the mathematical field providing the conic sections, eccentricities, and angular measurements necessary to calculate orbital paths.
- [[easter]] — the Christian movable feast determined by lunar phases relative to the vernal equinox produced by orbital axial orientation.
- [[eid-al-fitr]] — the Islamic holiday celebrating the conclusion of fasting, timed by the lunar sighting cycle intersecting Earth's annual movement.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — reckoning systems based on the Moon's synodic months while accounting for or drifting across Earth's annual solar period.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual epoch during which *Isaac Newton* and contemporaries mathematically formalized universal gravitation to explain Keplerian planetary tracks.
- [[space-age]] — the era of human history marked by artificial satellite launches and interplanetary probes breaking free of terrestrial gravity wells.
