## summary

**Apples to Apples** is an influential American party card game designed by *Matthew Kirby* and *Mark Alan Osterhaus*, first published in 1999 by *Out of the Box Publishing* and later acquired by toy manufacturer *Mattel*. The game challenges participants to form clever, funny, or persuasive word associations by playing red noun cards from their hands to match a green adjective card revealed by a rotating judge. Recognized for pioneering the subjective fill-in-the-blank comparison format that transformed contemporary casual tabletop gaming, it received the *Mensa Select* award in 1999 and the *Origins Award* for Best Party Game in 2000.

## you gotta know

- Designed by *Matthew Kirby* and *Mark Alan Osterhaus*, who conceived the matching concept in the late 1990s and debuted it with *Out of the Box Publishing* at the 1999 *Origins Game Fair*.
- Features two distinct card decks: green cards printed with qualifying adjectives and red cards printed with nouns, notable personalities, historical figures, places, or cultural concepts.
- Uses a rotating judge mechanic where each active player takes turns judging submissions while all other players submit one concealed red card that best pairs with the active green card.
- Awards the contested green card as a victory token to the player who submitted the red card selected by that round's judge, with victory attained by collecting a predetermined quota of cards.
- Revolutionized the party game industry by establishing a template directly borrowed and satirized by later adult games, most famously *Cards Against Humanity*.
- Spawned multiple licensed iterations and alternate editions, including *Apples to Apples Junior*, *Bible Edition*, *Disney Edition*, and extensive *Party Box* expansions containing thousands of cards.
- Acquired in 2007 by *Mattel*, which bought the game rights from *Out of the Box Publishing* to distribute the title globally throughout mass-market retail outlets.
- Exhibits linguistic interest because playing a red noun phrase alongside a descriptive green card often mirrors the grammatical function of an [[appositive]], testing players' intuitive grasp of semantics and connotation.
