## summary

**Al Jazeera** is an international state-funded news conglomerate based in Doha, Qatar, operating under the umbrella of the **Al Jazeera Media Network** (**AJMN**). Founded in 1996 by decree of the Emir of Qatar, Sheikh *Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani*, the network revolutionized journalism across the Arab world by introducing aggressive open debate, investigative reporting, and uncensored coverage of regional autocracies. Broadcasting worldwide in Arabic, English, and digital streaming formats, the broadcaster has drawn immense praise for democratizing Middle Eastern media while attracting intense criticism from regional and Western governments over its political alignments and editorial decisions.

## you gotta know

- Established in November 1996 with a startup loan of 500 million Qatari riyals from the Emir following the collapse of the *BBC Arabic Television* joint venture with Saudi firm *Orbit*.
- Absorbed dozens of seasoned television journalists and producers from the defunct *BBC* venture, implementing professional investigative standards under the Arabic slogan *al-ra'y wa-l-ra'y al-akhar* (*the opinion and the other opinion*).
- Gained global prominence following the September 11 terrorist attacks as the sole international broadcaster operating inside Taliban-controlled Afghanistan, airing exclusive video communiqués from *Osama bin Laden* and *al-Qaeda* leadership.
- Sparked repeated diplomatic crises across the Arab world by inviting Israeli officials, political dissidents, and Islamist opposition figures onto combative talk programs like *The Opposite Direction* (*Al-Ittijah al-Mu'akis*) hosted by *Faisal al-Qassem*.
- Launched *Al Jazeera English* in 2006, expanding its geopolitical footprint to compete directly with *CNN International* and the *BBC World News*, prioritizing coverage of the Global South.
- Played an instrumental role in televising the 2011 *Arab Spring* uprisings in Tunisia, Egypt, Libya, and Syria, providing continuous live coverage that galvanized public demonstrations and unseated long-ruling dictators.
- Prompted severe retaliation from neighboring regimes, including the shuttering of its regional offices, the arrest of journalists such as *Peter Greste* and *Mahmoud Hussein* in Egypt, and the fatal shooting of correspondent *Shireen Abu Akleh* in the West Bank.
- Formed a central point of contention during the 2017–2021 Qatar diplomatic blockade, where Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and Egypt explicitly demanded the total closure of the network as a condition for restoring diplomatic ties.
