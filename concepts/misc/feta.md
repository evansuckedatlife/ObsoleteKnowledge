## summary

**Feta** is a protected-designation-of-origin (PDO) brined curd cheese historically associated with Greece and pastoral dairy traditions across the eastern Mediterranean. Crafted traditionally from sheep's milk or a blend containing up to thirty percent goat's milk, it is aged in wooden barrels or tin containers within a salty brine solution that imparts its signature crumbly texture and tangy profile. It serves as a cornerstone of Mediterranean cuisine and appears in trivia as a premier case study in international geographical indication law and culinary history.

## you gotta know

- Granted Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) status by the European Union in 2002, restricting the name exclusively to brined white cheeses produced traditionally in mainland Greece and Lesbos.
- Requires by regulatory mandate at least seventy percent sheep's milk, with goat's milk comprising no more than thirty percent of the dairy blend.
- Stored and matured for a minimum of two months in heavy brine, developing high salinity, sharp acidity, and a compact, crumbly texture without an exterior rind.
- Believed to have ancient forebears referenced in classical antiquity, famously connected to the cheese-making episode of the Cyclops *Polyphemus* in Homer's *Odyssey*.
- Forms the quintessential centerpiece of the traditional Greek village salad, known regionally as *horiatiki*.
- Served as the subject of prolonged legal disputes within European commerce, prompting rival dairy producers in [[france]], Denmark, and Germany to rebrand cow-milk variants as white salad cheese.
