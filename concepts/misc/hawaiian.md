## summary

The **Hawaiian** language (known endonymously as **ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi**) is a Polynesian member of the Austronesian language family, indigenous to the Hawaiian archipelago. Characterized by one of the world's most compact phonological inventories, it served as the administrative language of the nineteenth-century Hawaiian Kingdom before suffering rapid institutional decline following American annexation. In modern times, it stands as an internationally celebrated model for indigenous language revitalization through groundbreaking community immersion schooling.

## you gotta know

- Features an exceptionally small phonemic inventory consisting of only eight consonants (*p*, *k*, *h*, *m*, *n*, *w*, *l*, and the glottal stop) and ten distinct vowel sounds.
- The glottal stop is an official phonemic consonant written with an apostrophe-like letter known as the *ʻokina*, whose presence or absence distinguishes minimal pairs such as *aliʻi* (hereditary noble) and *alii*.
- Vowel length is strictly phonemic and orthographically marked by a macron termed a *kahakō*, which alters grammatical meaning as observed in *pau* (finished) versus *pāʻū* (ritual skirt).
- Classified within the Eastern Polynesian branch of the Oceanic language subfamily, demonstrating close genetic kinship to *Tahitian*, *Cook Islands Māori*, and New Zealand *Māori*.
- Following the introduction of the Latin alphabet by Protestant missionaries in the 1820s, the Kingdom of Hawaii achieved widespread literacy, producing dozens of independent native-language newspapers.
- Banned as the primary medium of instruction in public and private schools by the Republic of Hawaii in 1896, precipitating steep intergenerational decline under American [[colonialism]].
- Revitalized beginning in 1984 through the pioneering *Pūnana Leo* ("language nest") preschool movement, which subsequently inspired global endangered language recovery efforts from [[welsh]] to Native American communities.
- Syllable structure is strictly open and conforms to a (C)V template, forbidding consonant clusters and final codas, which forces phonological reshaping for loanwords such as *Kalikimaka* from Christmas.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — serves as a classical case study in reduced consonant inventories, vowel phonology, and open syllable typology.
- [[colonialism]] — suffered institutional marginalization following the 1893 overthrow of Queen *Liliʻuokalani* and subsequent territorial education policies.
- [[united-states]] — annexed the islands in 1898 and eventually recognized the speech as co-official alongside English in the 1978 state constitution.
- [[welsh]] — provides contemporary revitalization parallels and exchanges pedagogical strategies for community-based immersion education.
- [[kannada]] — contrasts sharply as a classical Dravidian language characterized by an elaborate consonant system, retroflexion, and extensive agglutination.
- [[christianity]] — Protestant missionaries formalized the standard orthography, introduced printing presses, and published the first complete Bible translation (*Ka Baibala Hemolele*).
