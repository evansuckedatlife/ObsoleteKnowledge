## summary

An **adjective** is a lexical part of speech whose primary grammatical function is to modify, qualify, or describe a noun or nominal phrase. Across world languages, adjectives denote qualities such as size, age, shape, color, provenance, and material composition, appearing either attributively within a noun phrase or predicatively via a copula. In formal [[linguistics]], adjectives constitute an open lexical class in many language families, although some languages express adjectival concepts primarily through stative verbs or nouns.

## you gotta know

- Traditional Western grammar defines an **adjective** as a word that modifies a noun or pronoun to specify properties such as quality, quantity, or extent.
- Can appear in *attributive* position directly modifying a noun head (e.g., *a green door*) or in *predicative* position following a linking verb (e.g., *the door is green*).
- In English syntax, multiple attributive adjectives generally follow a rigid natural order of categories: opinion, size, physical quality, shape, age, color, origin, material, type, and purpose.
- Often inflect for degree in Indo-European traditions, exhibiting positive, comparative (e.g., *-er* or *more*), and superlative forms (e.g., *-est* or *most*).
- Exhibit concord or grammatical agreement with their governing head nouns in gender, number, and case across inflected tongues like Latin, Russian, and Arabic.
- Ancient grammarians in [[ancient-greece]] such as *Dionysius Thrax* originally categorized adjectives as a subclass of nouns called *noun adjective* (*onoma epitheton*), a taxonomy inherited by Roman grammarians in [[rome]].
- Distinct from an [[adverb]], which typically modifies verbs, adjectives, or entire clauses rather than nominal entities.
- Non-Indo-European languages, including members of the [[afro-asiatic-language-family]] and many Sino-Tibetan tongues, exhibit distinct morphological strategies such as bound construct forms or stative verbal roots to encode adjectival meanings.

## connections

- [[adverb]] — a related modifier class that qualifies verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs rather than nominal heads.
- [[linguistics]] — the overarching scientific study of language systems, syntax, morphology, and parts of speech.
- [[ancient-greece]] — origin of early syntactic classifications where grammarians first defined descriptive attributes.
- [[rome]] — classical scholars like *Varro* and *Priscian* systematized the morphological properties of nominal modifiers.
- [[france]] — standard French places most descriptive adjectives postpositively, with specific exceptions following the BANGS mnemonic rule.
- [[spain]] — Romance syntax in Spanish places descriptive adjectives postnominally while using prenominal placement for figurative emphasis.
- [[afro-asiatic-language-family]] — language family wherein adjectives exhibit strict gender-number concord with their governing nouns.
