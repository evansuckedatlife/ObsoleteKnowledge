## summary

**Parliament Hill**, colloquially known as *The Hill*, is a prominent promontory on the southern banks of the Ottawa River in downtown Ottawa, Ontario, serving as the political and ceremonial heart of Canada. Originally a military base known as Barrack Hill during the construction of the Rideau Canal, it became the seat of the Canadian federal government after Queen Victoria chose Ottawa as the capital in 1857. Renowned for its distinctive High Victorian Gothic Revival complex of public buildings, it remains one of the defining symbols of Canadian federalism, parliamentary democracy, and national assembly.

## you gotta know

- Selected in 1857 by Queen Victoria as the permanent capital of the Province of Canada, superseding rotational sittings between Toronto, Montreal, and Quebec City.
- Anchored by the Centre Block, which features the iconic Peace Tower, dedicated to Canadian service members who lost their lives during *[[world-war-i]]*.
- Suffered a catastrophic fire on February 3, 1916, which destroyed the original Centre Block, sparing only the domed Library of Parliament due to its iron fire doors.
- Designed primarily in the Gothic Revival style by architects *Thomas Fuller* and *Chilion Jones* for the Centre Block, with *Thomas Stent* and *Augustus Laver* responsible for the East and West Blocks.
- Houses the Centennial Flame, an eternal flame fueled by natural gas unveiled on January 1, 1967, by Prime Minister *Lester B. Pearson* to mark the centennial of Canadian Confederation.
- Built atop an imposing limestone bluff overlooking the Ottawa River that originally housed military fortifications constructed by the Royal Engineers under Lieutenant Colonel *John By*.
- Commenced the massive, multi-decade Centre Block Rehabilitation project in 2019, temporarily relocating the House of Commons to the West Block courtyard and the Senate to Ottawa's historic Government Conference Centre.
