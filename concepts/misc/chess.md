## summary

**Chess**, frequently termed **Western chess** or **International chess**, is a two-player abstract strategy board game played on an eight-by-eight checkered grid that simulates tactical battle between two sovereign armies. Descended from the sixth-century Indian game *chaturanga* and transmitted through medieval Persia and Islam, it reached its modern rules in late-fifteenth-century Europe. As an intellectual sport demanding profound spatial foresight and tactical calculation, it has exerted a monumental influence on literature, cognitive psychology, computer science, and international statecraft.

## you gotta know

- Played on an alternating 64-square board shared directly with [[checkers]], where each contestant commands sixteen pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights, and eight pawns.
- Achieves victory through checkmate (*shah mat*, a Persian phrase meaning "the king is helpless"), trapping the opponent's monarch beneath unavoidable attack, while indeterminate struggles conclude in stalemate or draw.
- Acquired modern movement conventions in southern Europe during the [[renaissance]] around 1475, when the queen and bishop received sweeping powers in a dynamic reform termed *mad queen chess* (*alla rabiosa*).
- Formed a paramount cultural arena during the [[cold-war]], epitomized by the 1972 World Chess Championship in Reykjavik, Iceland, where American *Bobby Fischer* defeated Soviet titleholder *Boris Spassky*.
- Dominated across the mid-to-late twentieth century by grandmasters from the [[soviet-union]], including legendary world champions *Mikhail Botvinnik*, *Vasily Smyslov*, *Mikhail Tal*, *Tigran Petrosian*, *Anatoly Karpov*, and *Garry Kasparov*.
- Marked an epochal milestone for computing in 1997 when IBM's chess-playing supercomputer *Deep Blue* defeated reigning world champion *Garry Kasparov* in a six-game match under tournament conditions.
- Governed universally by the *Fédération Internationale des Échecs* (FIDE), which regulates grandmaster titles, Elo rating metrics, classical world championships, and the biennial international Chess Olympiad.
- Utilizes an extensive canon of recorded opening theory systematically cataloged in the *Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings*, containing time-tested schemes such as the *Ruy Lopez*, *Sicilian Defence*, and *Queen's Gambit*.

## connections

- [[checkers]] — fellow tabletop abstract strategy game contested across the identical 64-square alternating grid.
- [[geometry]] — the formal study of spatial coordinates, perpendicular lines, diagonals, and geometric vectors governing piece mobility.
- [[middle-east]] — the historical crossroad where the ancestral game *shatranj* flourished under Islamic rule before travelling into medieval Europe.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical standoff between rival superpowers that turned elite championship matches into proxy ideological contests.
- [[soviet-union]] — the socialist state that financed state-sponsored chess academies, dominating world titles for nearly half a century.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural epoch in Italy and Spain that codified modern movement rules, especially the expanded powers of the queen.
- [[enlightenment]] — the eighteenth-century philosophical era whose Parisian luminaries, including *Denis Diderot* and *Voltaire*, congregated at the *Café de la Régence* to study the game.
