## summary

**Easter**, also known in many liturgical traditions as *Pascha*, is the preeminent feast of the Christian calendar celebrating the resurrection of *Jesus Christ* from the dead on the third day following his crucifixion. Originating in Judea under Roman rule, it marks the triumphant climax of *Holy Week* and terminates the forty-day penitential season of *Lent*. As a movable feast computed by complex astronomical calculations linking solar equinoxes and lunar phases, it establishes the liturgical cadence for church calendars across the world.

## you gotta know

- Commemorates the central theological tenet of the Christian faith: the bodily resurrection of *Jesus Christ* following his execution at Golgotha.
- Preceded by the preparatory fasting period of *Lent*, Holy Week observances, the *Last Supper* of Maundy Thursday, and the solemnity of Good Friday.
- Categorized as a movable feast because its date changes annually, calculated as the first Sunday following the ecclesiastical full moon on or after March 21.
- Standardized in method by the First Council of Nicaea in 325 CE, which sought to establish an autonomous Christian calculation independent of the contemporary Jewish calendar.
- Derives its English name from the Germanic month *Eosturmonath*, whereas Romance and Slavic languages retain derivatives of the Greek and Latin *Pascha*, rooted in the Hebrew *Pesach*.
- Celebrated on divergent dates by Western and Eastern churches due to Western adoption of the Gregorian reckoning and Eastern adherence to the Julian calendar.
- Features liturgical highlights including the lighting of the *Paschal candle*, joyful vigil baptisms, chanting of the *Exsultet*, and shouts of the Paschal greeting.
- Associated with traditional folk customs such as decorated eggs, dawn sunrise services, festive lamb or ham dinners, and seasonal Easter parades.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the world religion centered upon the life, teachings, crucifixion, and resurrection celebrated throughout the festival.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — the astronomical lunar reckoning that dictates the ecclesiastical full moon determining the annual holiday date.
- [[judaism]] — the biblical religious tradition whose festival of *Passover* provides the typological, chronological, and theological backdrop for the holiday.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial political authority under whose provincial administration *Pontius Pilate* ordered the crucifixion of Jesus in Roman Judea.
- [[holy-roman-empire]] — the medieval central European realm where imperial coronation cycles, diets, and liturgical calendars strictly centered on major church feasts.
- [[earth-orbit]] — the annual planetary trajectory around the Sun that dictates the astronomical vernal equinox serving as the computation boundary for the feast.
- [[eid-al-fitr]] — the major Islamic festival that similarly marks the joyous conclusion of a multi-week penitential fast through communal dining and prayer.
