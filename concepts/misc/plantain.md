## summary

A **plantain** is a starchy cultivar of the genus *Musa* that functions as a major staple crop across Central and West Africa, the Caribbean, and Latin America. Often called the **cooking banana**, it differs from sweeter dessert cultivars because of its high starch content, thick fibrous peel, and requirement for thermal processing before consumption. Dispersed across ocean trade routes by early Austronesian voyagers and later disseminated globally under European [[colonialism]], it remains a vital economic commodity and a cultural touchstone for culinary identity throughout the [[african-diaspora]].

## you gotta know

- Eaten primarily cooked rather than raw because its high starch content and low sugar levels give green fruit an astringent, potato-like consistency that sweetens only when fully ripe and blackened.
- Widely prepared across the Caribbean and Hispanic America as *tostones* or *patacones*, which are twice-fried, flattened savory rounds typically seasoned with garlic and salt.
- Served in Puerto Rico as the signature dish *mofongo*, consisting of fried green plantains mashed in a wooden mortar with garlic, broth, and crispy pork cracklings called *chicharrón*.
- Processed into *fufu* across Ghana, Nigeria, and neighboring West African nations, where boiled plantains are vigorously pounded with cassava into a smooth, dense paste meant to accompany savory soups and stews.
- Introduced across the Atlantic Basin during early modern maritime voyages initiated by [[portuguese]] and Spanish mariners, quickly becoming an essential subsistence food on plantation complexes.
- Belongs botanically to the hybrid triploid group *Musa acuminata* crossed with *Musa balbisiana*, historically designated as the AAB genomic group.
- Ripe preparations known as *maduros* or *tajadas* caramelize exterior natural sugars when pan-fried, yielding a sweet interior that accompanies rice and beans.

## connections

- [[african-diaspora]] — served as a foundational staple crop sustaining enslaved laborers and remains central to Afro-descendant foodways.
- [[colonialism]] — transcontinental dispersal occurred along mercantile and imperial trade corridors established during early modern expansion.
- [[portuguese]] — Iberian explorers transported suckers from West African coastal enclaves into the Canary Islands and across the Atlantic Ocean.
- [[spain]] — colonial trade networks integrated cooking cultivars into the daily diet and regional cuisine of the Caribbean and Central America.
- [[british-empire]] — plantation economies in Jamaica and other crown colonies relied heavily upon provision grounds planted with hardy cultivars.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — global transport innovations and refrigerated shipping enabled broad commercialization of tropical agricultural produce.
