## summary

A **lunar calendar** is a timekeeping system based on the cycles of the moon, marking months by lunar phases (typically 29–30 days per month) rather than Earth's orbit around the sun. Used by cultures worldwide—including Islamic, Hebrew, Hindu, Buddhist, and traditional East Asian societies—lunar and lunisolar calendars (which incorporate lunar months into solar years) organize religious observances, agricultural cycles, and cultural celebrations. Though increasingly superseded by the Gregorian calendar in global commerce, lunar calendars remain central to traditional cultural practices and determine the dates of major festivals including *Tet*, *Chinese New Year*, *Shogatsu*, and *Ramadan*.

## you gotta know

- The lunar month derives from the 29.53-day cycle of lunar phases (new moon to new moon); a twelve-month lunar year spans approximately 354 days, roughly 11 days shorter than the solar year, causing lunar calendar dates to drift gradually through seasons if uncorrected.
- Many cultures adopted lunisolar calendars, which integrate lunar months into a solar framework by intercalating extra months periodically; this system keeps months aligned with lunar phases while ensuring the calendar cycles through seasons consistently (e.g., Hebrew, Hindu, Buddhist, Traditional Chinese calendars).
- Ancient civilizations developed lunar calendars independently: Mesopotamians, Egyptians, Greeks, Romans, Hebrews, and Chinese all used moon-based or lunisolar systems before adopting or being influenced by solar calendars.
- The Islamic calendar (*Hijri*) remains purely lunar (12 months of 29–30 days), causing Islamic holidays to shift through all seasons over a 33-year cycle; *Ramadan* progresses from summer to winter across years as a result.
- East Asian lunisolar calendars govern *Lunar New Year* celebrations (Chinese New Year, Vietnamese *Tet*, Korean *Seollal*, Japanese *Shogatsu*); these festivals typically fall between January 21 and February 20 by the Gregorian calendar.
- Lunar calendars synchronized human activities with observable celestial events, making them more intuitive than abstract solar calculations; each month's phases provided visible monthly markers and guided agricultural and ritual timing.
- The adoption of the Gregorian solar calendar by most of the world reflects both scientific precision and colonial/imperial imposition; lunar calendar traditions persist because they embed cultural and religious significance beyond mere timekeeping.
- Modern astronomy revealed that lunar months vary slightly (29–30 days depending on orbital mechanics), requiring rules for intercalation; traditional calendar systems encoded these adjustments through empirical observation and mathematical refinement over centuries.
