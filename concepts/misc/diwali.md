## summary

**Diwali**, also known as **Deepavali**, is the five-day Hindu "Festival of Lights" celebrated across South Asia and the global diaspora during the autumn lunisolar month of *Kartika*. Signifying the spiritual triumph of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance, the festival is observed with clay oil lamps (*diyas*), fireworks, festive sweets, and family gatherings. As one of the world's most widely recognized religious celebrations, it holds profound cross-cultural significance for Hindus, Jains, Sikhs, and Newar Buddhists, frequently appearing in competitive quiz bowl.

## you gotta know

- Anchored chronologically to the darkest night of the Hindu [[lunar-calendar]], falling on the new moon (*Amavasya*) between mid-October and mid-November according to astronomical cycles.
- Celebrates *Rama's* return to *Ayodhya* with his wife *Sita* and brother *Lakshmana* in the *Ramayana*, following their fourteen-year exile and victory over the demon king *Ravana*.
- Centers on *Lakshmi*, the goddess of wealth and prosperity, who is worshipped during the main evening *puja* alongside *Ganesha*, the remover of obstacles, to invite auspicious blessings into clean homes.
- Features the creation of elaborate *rangoli* floor patterns at domestic thresholds, utilizing colored powders, rice flour, and flower petals to welcome deities and guests.
- Encompasses five distinct festive days: *Dhanteras* (buying precious metals and honoring *Dhanvantari*), *Naraka Chaturdashi* or *Choti Diwali* (commemorating the defeat of the demon *Narakasura*), *Lakshmi Puja*, *Govardhan Puja* (or *Annakut*), and *Bhai Dooj* (celebrating sibling bonds).
- Commemorated by Jains as the anniversary of *Mahavira's* attainment of ultimate liberation (*moksha* or *nirvana*) in 527 BCE, marking the conclusion of his earthly teachings.
- Celebrated by Sikhs as *Bandi Chhor Divas* ("Day of Liberation"), marking the historical release of the sixth guru, *Guru Hargobind*, and fifty-two rajputs from Gwalior Fort by Mughal Emperor *Jahangir*.
- Culminates in community fireworks displays, the exchange of *mithai* (traditional confectioneries), and charitable giving, serving as a civil new year for merchant communities across western India.

## connections

- [[lunar-calendar]] — the astronomical dating framework whose lunisolar months and dark new-moon night determine the festival's timing.
- [[astronomy]] — the celestial observation of lunar phases and autumn constellations that fixes the date of *Amavasya* in the month of *Kartika*.
- [[chinese-new-year]] — another major Asian cultural festival marking new beginnings, familial reunion, and renewal with lanterns and festive fireworks.
- [[nowruz]] — Persian new year festival that similarly celebrates the arrival of light, spring renewal, and domestic purification across Asian traditions.
- [[tet]] — Vietnamese lunar festival celebrating ancestral reverence, domestic renewal, and new annual cycles.
- [[shogatsu]] — Japanese new year observance marked by domestic purification, symbolic decorations, and family gatherings.
- [[theravada-buddhism]] — related Asian spiritual tradition that shares historical roots and calendar systems with Indian autumn festival cycles.
- [[design-principles]] — embodied in the radial symmetry, compositional balance, scale, and vibrant color harmonies of ceremonial *rangoli* designs.
