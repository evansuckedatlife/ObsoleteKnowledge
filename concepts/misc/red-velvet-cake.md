## summary

**Red velvet cake** is a classic layer cake known for its signature crimson or reddish-brown crumb, mild cocoa flavor, and rich topping of cream cheese or cooked roux frosting. Emerging in the *[[united-states]]* during the late 19th and early 20th centuries, its smooth, velvety crumb was historically produced by a chemical reaction between natural non-alkalized cocoa powder and acidic ingredients like buttermilk and vinegar. In quiz bowl, it frequently clues through culinary chemistry, its commercial promotion by the Texas flavor extract firm *Adams Extract*, and urban legends linking its luxury revival to *The Waldorf-Astoria* in *[[new-york-city]]*.

## you gotta know

- The characteristic reddish hue originally occurred through a natural chemical reaction between the anthocyanin pigments in unprocessed, natural cocoa powder and acidic buttermilk or vinegar.
- The term *velvet cake* originated in the Victorian era as a culinary descriptor denoting a tender, soft crumb created by adding cornstarch or cake flour to break down gluten.
- During *[[world-war-ii]]*, rationing of butter and sugar led bakers to incorporate boiled beet juices and pureed beets to retain moisture, naturally intensifying the red coloration.
- The Austin, Texas-based flavor and dye company *Adams Extract* popularized the modern vibrant red version during the Great Depression by packaging red food dye with tear-off recipe cards.
- A pervasive American culinary urban legend claims that a customer at *[[new-york-city]]*'s *Waldorf-Astoria Hotel* requested the chef's secret cake recipe, only to be billed hundreds of dollars, retaliating by circulating the recipe to the public.
- Traditional historical recipes called for a cooked flour buttercream known as *ermine frosting* or roux icing, whereas mid-to-late-20th-century Southern adaptations made cream cheese frosting standard.
- The cake enjoyed a major modern cultural resurgence following its appearance as an armadillo-shaped groom's cake in the 1989 American film *Steel Magnolias*.
