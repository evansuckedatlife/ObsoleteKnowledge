## summary

The **loonie**, officially designated as the **Canadian one-dollar coin** (or **Canadian dollar coin**), was introduced in 1987 by the Royal Canadian Mint to replace the green one-dollar paper banknote. Its widely recognized colloquial name derives from the solitary common loon (*Gavia immer*) depicted on its reverse side, created by Ontario wildlife artist *Robert-Ralph Carmichael*. Renowned for its distinctive golden hue, its eleven-sided curved polygonal edge, and its durability, the coin quickly established itself as an essential emblem of modern Canadian national identity and daily commerce.

## you gotta know

- Features the striking image of a common loon floating on water with a solitary conifer-lined island in the background on its reverse, which instantly prompted Canadians to christen the coin with its avian nickname.
- Entered general circulation across Canada on June 30, 1987, serving as a cost-saving measure by the federal government because metal coinage lasts decades longer than paper currency.
- Constructed as an eleven-sided curve-edged polygon known in applied mathematics and [[geometry]] as a Reuleaux-like curvilinear hendecagon, engineered intentionally to produce a constant diameter across all orientations so that automated vending machines could accurately process it.
- Replaced the historic paper one-dollar bill featuring Queen *Elizabeth II*, with the green banknotes being officially phased out of circulation by 1989 and stripped of legal tender status decades later.
- The original intended reverse design was the classic voyageur motif used on previous silver dollars, but the master casting dies mysteriously disappeared or were stolen while in transit between Ottawa and the Winnipeg minting facility in November 1986, forcing the emergency commissioning of Carmichael's backup design.
- The obverse has borne the royal effigies of the reigning monarch of the [[british-empire]] and Commonwealth, displaying portraits of Queen *Elizabeth II* created by *Arnold Machin*, *Dora de Pédery-Hunt*, and *Susanna Blunt*, before transitioning to King *Charles III* in 2023.
- Gained legendary status at the 2002 Winter Olympic Games in Salt Lake City when Canadian ice technician *Trent Evans* secretly embedded a "lucky loonie" beneath the center ice surface, after which both the Canadian men's and women's ice hockey squads captured Olympic gold.
- Its widespread commercial acceptance cleared the logistical path for the subsequent introduction of the bimetallic two-dollar coin in 1996, which Canadians naturally dubbed the "toonie" in playful rhyming homage.
