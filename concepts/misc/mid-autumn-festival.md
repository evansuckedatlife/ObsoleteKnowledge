## summary

The **Mid-Autumn Festival**, also celebrated as the **Moon Festival**, **Mooncake Festival**, or **Zhongqiu Jie**, is a major harvest holiday celebrated across East and Southeast Asia on the fifteenth day of the eighth month of the traditional [[lunar-calendar]]. Rooted in ancient Chinese lunar worship, agricultural thanksgiving, and mythological devotion to the moon goddess *Chang'e*, the festival honors autumn abundance and family unity beneath the harvest full moon. Celebrants observe the occasion by sharing elaborate pastries called mooncakes, carrying illuminated lanterns, reciting classical poetry, and performing ceremonial fire dragon dances.

## you gotta know

- Falls annually on the fifteenth day of the eighth month of the Chinese [[lunar-calendar]], coinciding with the autumn equinox's bright harvest full moon when the celestial body is traditionally believed to be at its fullest and most luminous.
- Centered on the mythological tale of *Chang'e*, who ingested the elixir of immortality granted to her husband, the heroic archer *Houyi*—famed for shooting down nine surplus scorching suns—and ascended to live forever in the lunar Palace of Vast Cold.
- Accompanied on the moon by the Jade Rabbit (*Yutu*), who ceaselessly pounds herbs with a mortar and pestle beneath a self-healing sweet osmanthus tree to formulate the elixir of life.
- Symbolized by the consumption and gifting of mooncakes (*yuebing*), rich round pastries imprinted with auspicious characters and packed with fillings such as sweet red bean paste, lotus seed paste, mixed nuts, and whole salted duck egg yolks representing the full moon.
- Anchored in a legendary historical folk tale in which future *Ming* dynasty emperor *Zhu Yuanzhang* and his strategist *Liu Bowen* coordinated the Red Turban Rebellion against the Mongol *Yuan* dynasty by hiding secret rebellion notes inside mooncakes.
- Celebrated through family reunions, skyward moon-gazing (*shangyue*), sipping fragrant osmanthus-infused wine, and eating round pomelos, whose Chinese name homophonically evokes prayers for maternal and domestic protection.
- Marked in *Hong Kong* by the celebrated Tai Hang Fire Dragon Dance, an acrobatic heritage ritual featuring a massive hemp-and-straw dragon studded with tens of thousands of burning incense sticks to ward off misfortune.
- Celebrated across neighboring cultures, notably in Vietnam as *Tết Trung Thu* with vibrant children's lion dances and carp lanterns, in Korea as the harvest festival *Chuseok*, and in Japan as the autumnal moon-viewing celebration *Tsukimi*.
