## summary

**IBM**, officially known as the **International Business Machines** Corporation and colloquially nicknamed **Big Blue**, is an American multinational technology and consulting corporation headquartered in Armonk, New York. Tracing its lineage to the late nineteenth-century punched-card tabulating machinery engineered by *Herman Hollerith*, it was incorporated in 1911 as the *Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company* (*C-T-R*) before being renamed in 1924 under long-serving executive *Thomas J. Watson*. The firm dominated twentieth-century enterprise data processing, pioneering fundamental landmarks in hardware architecture, mainframe systems, silicon fabrication, programming languages, and networked enterprise systems.

## you gotta know

- Traces its technical origins to the 1890 United States Census, for which *Herman Hollerith* patented electromechanical punched card tabulating machines, leading to the formation of the *Tabulating Machine Company*.
- Consolidated in 1911 by financier *Charles Ranlett Flint* as *C-T-R*, combining Hollerith's firm with the *Computing Scale Company of America* and the *International Time Recording Company*.
- Led through unprecedented twentieth-century commercial dominance by *Thomas J. Watson Sr.* and subsequently *Thomas J. Watson Jr.*, establishing iconic corporate traditions including the single-word company motto *"THINK"*.
- Built massive electromechanical computers for scientific and military applications during [[world-war-ii]], such as the *Harvard Mark I* (*Automatic Sequence Controlled Calculator*), designed by *Howard Aiken*.
- Revolutionized enterprise computing in 1964 by launching the *System/360* family of mainframe computers, introducing standard peripheral interfaces, microcode architecture, and software compatibility across price points.
- Developed foundational software and computing standards throughout the mid-twentieth century, including the pioneering programming language *FORTRAN*, the relational database model codified by *Edgar F. Codd*, and the standard query syntax *SQL*.
- Introduced the *IBM Personal Computer* (*Model 5150*) in 1981, which established the de facto open standard for desktop microcomputers powered by Intel microprocessors and Microsoft's *MS-DOS*, creating the modern PC ecosystem.
- Enabled modern internet architectures by deploying commercial application servers, database engines, and hardware routers optimized to exchange traffic using standardized protocols such as [[http]].
