## summary

The **cayenne pepper** is a pungent cultivar of the chili pepper species *Capsicum annuum* within the nightshade family *Solanaceae*. Named after the city of Cayenne in French Guiana, this slender, tapering red fruit is most frequently dried and pulverized into a fine, fiery culinary powder. Its assertive, clean heat has established it as an essential seasoning across global culinary traditions, commercial condiment industries, and traditional pharmacological therapies.

## you gotta know

- Registers between 30,000 and 50,000 *Scoville Heat Units* on the Scoville scale, placing it significantly above mild jalapenos while remaining milder than fiery habaneros.
- O reformulates sensory experience via the chemical compound *capsaicin*, an alkaloid that activates heat-sensing vanilloid receptors to produce a burning sensation without actual thermal tissue damage.
- Named after the coastal capital of French Guiana, though the ancestor plants were originally domesticated across tropical regions of the Americas over thousands of years by Indigenous peoples.
- Dispersed worldwide as a direct outcome of sixteenth-century [[colonialism]] following voyages sponsored by [[spain]], swiftly integrating into the diets of South Asia, Africa, and East Asia.
- Forms the indispensable spicy foundation of American Creole and Cajun cooking, acting as the primary pungent ingredient in vinegar-based Louisiana hot sauces in the [[united-states]].
- Extensively employed in topical medical preparations and creams designed to deplete *Substance P*, relieving chronic joint ache and neuropathic pain.
- Shares botanical membership in the family *Solanaceae* with tomatoes, potatoes, and eggplants, linking it genetically to key Mediterranean components like the tomato in [[caprese-salad]].
- Commonly paired with mild cruciferous vegetables such as [[cauliflower]] in spiced roasted dishes, vegetable medleys, and spicy Indian curries.

- [[human-anatomy]] — the physiological neural network containing peripheral sensory nerves and TRPV1 receptors sensitive to chemical capsaicin stimulation.

- [[cauliflower]] — an Old World brassica vegetable frequently seasoned with cayenne pepper to enhance its earthy, neutral flavor profile.
