## summary

**Haft-Sin**, also transcribed as **Haft-Seen** or the **Sofreh-ye Haft-Sin**, is the ceremonial table arrangement prepared to celebrate [[nowruz]], the Persian and Iranian New Year that marks the vernal equinox. Rooted in ancient Zoroastrian cosmological traditions and refined across millennia in Greater Iran, the setting features seven symbolic items whose names in the Persian language begin with the letter *sin* (س). Each element embodies renewal, vitality, prosperity, and cosmic balance, serving as the cultural and domestic centerpiece of the spring celebration throughout the [[middle-east]] and Central Asia.

## you gotta know

- Centered on seven primary symbolic items starting with the Persian letter *sin*, with each representing a cardinal Zoroastrian Amesha Spenta or natural blessing.
- *Sabzeh* consists of sprouted wheat, barley, lentil, or mung bean shoots growing in a dish, symbolizing rebirth, spring renewal, and the lush vigor of nature.
- *Samanu* is a sweet, dense pudding made from germinated wheat germ cooked slowly in large cauldrons, embodying sweetness, affluence, and inner strength.
- *Senjed*, the dried fruit of the oleaster or wild lotus tree, represents romantic love, affection, and wisdom.
- *Sir* (garlic) serves as a potent emblem of health, medicine, disease prevention, and personal sanctuary.
- *Sib* (apple) is placed upon the spread to symbolize beauty, radiant health, and bountiful harvest.
- *Somagh* (crushed sumac berries) evokes the vibrant red crimson of sunrise, heralding the victory of light over seasonal darkness.
- *Serkeh* (aged vinegar) symbolizes patience, philosophical maturity, and the stoic acceptance of aging and time.
- Accompanied by supplementary non-*sin* items including a mirror (*a’ineh*) for honest self-reflection, decorated eggs for fertility, lit candles for enlightenment, and a book of wisdom such as the *Shahnameh* or the poetry of *Hafez*.

- [[islamic-calendar]] — regional religious calendar operating concurrently with the ancient solar celebrations in the Iranian cultural sphere.
- [[chinese-new-year]] — parallel major spring celebration featuring household tables adorned with symbolic auspicious foods and red decorations.
