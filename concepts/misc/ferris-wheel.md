## summary

The **Ferris Wheel**, originally known as the *Chicago Wheel*, is a monumental rotating observation wheel engineered by *George Washington Gale Ferris Jr.* for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago. Designed specifically to rival the engineering triumph of the *Eiffel Tower* from the 1889 exposition in Paris, it stood 264 feet tall and carried over a thousand passengers at a time. Its debut established a global archetype for amusement rides and became an enduring symbol of American industrial capability during the late nineteenth century.

## you gotta know

- Conceived as America's architectural answer to Gustave Eiffel's tower erected at the [[1889-paris-worlds-fair]].
- Designed and constructed by civil engineer *George Washington Gale Ferris Jr.*, a bridge builder from Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.
- Debuted at the 1893 *World's Columbian Exposition* in Chicago, whose construction and layout were directed by [[daniel-burnham]].
- Stood 264 feet tall and was powered by two 1,000-horsepower steam engines, carrying thirty-six wooden cars each capable of holding sixty passengers.
- Faced initial skepticism from exposition organizers who considered Ferris's structural calculations unsafe before granting approval.
- Operated along the Midway Plaisance, serving as the commercial centerpiece and primary financial triumph of the Chicago fair.
- Rebuilt at the 1904 *Louisiana Purchase Exposition* in St. Louis before being demolished by dynamite in 1906.
- Spawned modern successors worldwide, including giant observation wheels such as the *London Eye* and the *High Roller*.
