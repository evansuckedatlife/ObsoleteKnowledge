## summary

**Mozzarella** is a traditional southern Italian fresh curd cheese made primarily using the specialized *pasta filata* (stretched-curd) technique. Originally crafted from the rich milk of water buffaloes in the marshy lowlands of Campania and Lazio, it has become one of the most widely consumed cheeses in global culinary culture. Recognized historically in monastic records south of *[[rome]]*, it is prized for its soft elasticity, milky sweetness, and unique melting qualities celebrated worldwide on Neapolitan pizza.

## you gotta know

- Produced through the ancient *pasta filata* method, in which warm whey-drained curds are steeped in boiling water, kneaded, pulled, and stretched until supple and glossy.
- The name derives directly from the Neapolitan Italian verb *mozzare*, meaning "to sever" or "to cut off," referencing the manual pinching of the stretched curd mass into individual spheres using the thumb and index finger.
- Authentic *Mozzarella di Bufala Campana* holds European Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) status and must be manufactured exclusively from whole water buffalo milk.
- Cow milk versions are historically termed *fior di latte*, possessing a somewhat milder flavor and slightly firmer moisture balance ideal for industrial grating and high-heat baking.
- Earliest preserved documentary mention dates back to twelfth-century records from the Benedictine monastery of *San Lorenzo in Capua*, where monks offered loaves of bread alongside a cheese termed *mozza* to pilgrims.
- High-moisture fresh balls are traditionally packaged suspended in lightly salted water, brine, or whey to maintain springy tenderness and prevent drying.
