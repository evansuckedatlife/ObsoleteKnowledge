## summary

**Namaste** is an ancient Sanskrit salutation, greeting, and gesture of reverence widely observed across the Indian subcontinent and within Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist communities worldwide. Formed from the grammatical fusion of the noun *namas*, signifying a bow or humble adoration, and the dative enclitic pronoun *te*, it translates literally to "I bow to you." Accompanied by the physical gesture of *añjali mudrā*—palms pressed together before the heart with a gentle bow—it philosophically acknowledges the divine presence and inherent consciousness dwelling within the recipient.

## you gotta know

- Composed etymologically from the Proto-Indo-European root *nem-*, meaning to bend or distribute, which yielded the Vedic Sanskrit noun *namas* ("obeisance") paired with the enclitic personal pronoun *te*.
- Executed traditionally through the somatic gesture known as *añjali mudrā* or *pranamasana*, where the palms are joined flat against one another at the chest level over the *anahata* or heart center.
- Manifested across modern South Asian vernacular languages in widespread cognate forms, such as **Namaskar** in Hindi and Marathi, **Namaskaram** in Telugu and Malayalam, and *Nomoshkar* in Bengali.
- Recorded extensively in foundational Hindu scriptures, appearing throughout Vedic hymns, the *Upanishads*, and the epic *Mahabharata* as a formula for honoring deities, preceptors, and revered guests.
- Shared across multiple Dharmic traditions, featuring prominently in [[theravada-buddhism]] as a devotional posture toward the monastic Sangha, venerable teachers, and representations of the Buddha.
- Integrated into Western culture during the twentieth century through the international diffusion of modern postural yoga, where it serves as a concluding invocation between instructor and students.
- Adopted globally as a hygienic, touchless diplomatic and social greeting during respiratory pandemics, replacing physical contact like the handshake.
- Transcribed over international aviation and naval communication networks by spelling its initial letter with the NATO phonetic code word [[november]].
