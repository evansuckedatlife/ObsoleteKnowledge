## summary

**Kimchi** is a traditional Korean side dish of salted and fermented vegetables seasoned with chili pepper, garlic, ginger, and fermented seafood. Anchored by napa cabbage and radishes, it is a quintessential cornerstone of Korean gastronomic identity and nutritional culture. Its preparation through communal seasonal labor reflects longstanding agrarian traditions attuned to the climate and preservation needs of the Korean peninsula.

## you gotta know

- Consists primarily of napa cabbage (*baechu*) or Korean radish (*mu*), salted to draw out excess moisture before being coated in a seasoned paste of chili flakes (*gochugaru*), minced garlic, ginger, scallions, and salted fish sauce (*jeotgal*).
- Did not originally include hot red peppers; early historical varieties were salted, unspiced white vegetable preserves until New World chili peppers were introduced to East Asia following the *Age of Exploration* [[age-of-exploration]].
- Driven by wild lactic acid bacteria—predominantly species of the genera *Leuconostoc*, *Lactobacillus*, and *Weissella*—which ferment sugars under anaerobic conditions into lactic acid and carbon dioxide.
- Inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2013 under the cultural practice of *kimjang*, the communal preparation and sharing of massive vegetable batches ahead of winter.
- Traditionally fermented and stored in large porous earthenware crocks called *onggi*, which were partially buried in the ground to insulate the vegetables against freezing winter temperatures.
- Scheduled traditionally according to the agricultural cycles of the *lunar calendar* [[lunar-calendar]], taking place during the solar term of *Ipdong* (the onset of winter) in November or early December.
- Supported in contemporary urban South Korea by specialized computerized kimchi refrigerators, which maintain precise cold microclimates to control fermentation rates in high-rise apartments.
- Widely popularized in global gastronomy alongside Korean fried chicken and fast-food chains like [[kfc]], forming a celebrated pairing that balances fried richness with pungent acidity.

## connections

- [[lunar-calendar]] — traditional calendar system that historically determined the agricultural rhythm and annual timing of winter *kimjang*.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — era of transoceanic commerce that facilitated the Columbian Exchange, introducing chili peppers to Korean cuisine.
- [[kfc]] — American fast-food chain whose global presence and fried chicken menus are frequently paired with kimchi in contemporary fusion dining.
- [[japan]] — neighboring nation with substantial culinary exchange, producing its own commercialized variation called *kimuchi*.
- [[five-senses]] — physiological framework reflected in kimchi's sensory profile spanning sourness, salinity, spiciness, pungency, and crunch.
- [[cold-war]] — historical period during which the South Korean government supplied canned kimchi as essential field rations to troops serving in the Vietnam War.
- [[colonialism]] — global historical phenomenon that reshaped maritime crop distributions and regional food preservation strategies.
