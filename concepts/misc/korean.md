## summary

**Korean**, known natively as **Hangul** in South Korea and *Chosongul* in North Korea, is an East Asian language spoken natively by more than eighty million people across the Korean Peninsula and the global diaspora. Often classified genealogically as a language isolate or a member of the proposed macro-Altaic or Koreanic families, it possesses an agglutinative morphology and a strict subject-object-verb word order. Its brilliant indigenous featural alphabet was deliberately promulgated in the fifteenth century by monarch *Sejong the Great* to replace complex Chinese logographs and achieve universal literacy.

## you gotta know

- Commissioned and promulgated in 1446 by *King Sejong the Great* of the Joseon Dynasty through the treatise *Hunminjeongeum* ("The Correct Sounds for the Instruction of the People") to liberate commoners from reliance on elite *Hanja* characters.
- Script letters are grouped into distinct syllabic blocks rather than written in a linear stream, with initial consonants shaped to depict the biomechanical profile of the tongue, lips, and throat during articulation.
- Characterized by an extensive grammatical hierarchy of speech levels and honorific markers, demanding that speakers calibrate verb suffixes according to the precise social seniority, age, and intimacy of the listener.
- Morphologically agglutinative with robust subject-object-verb (SOV) syntax and postpositional case particles that mark subjects, topics, direct objects, and directional movement.
- Phonology features a three-way distinction among voiceless stops and affricates—plain, heavily aspirated, and tense or glottalized—without relying on phonemic voicing contrasts between voiced and unvoiced consonants.
- Divided by the geopolitical cleavage of the post-1945 era into the southern standard known as *Pyojun-eo* centered on Seoul and the northern *Munhwa'o* ("Cultural Language") centered on Pyongyang, which systematically purges foreign loanwords.
- The dialect of *Jeju Island* preserves archaic medieval phonetic vowels and grammatical particles absent in standard speech, leading modern linguists to classify it as a distinct sister language within the Koreanic family.
- Influenced lexical registers heavily through historical interaction with Classical Chinese, giving modern vocabulary a dual layer of native words and Sino-Korean roots alongside twentieth-century borrowings from English.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — primary field analyzing Korean as an iconic test case for agglutination, featural writing systems, and the debate surrounding linguistic isolates.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical ideological division that bisected the speech community along the 38th parallel, yielding distinct institutional standards, orthographies, and lexical policies.
- [[japan]] — historical neighbor whose language shares remarkable syntactic, morphological, and sociolinguistic parallels with Korean, prompting ongoing debates regarding deep typological contact.
- [[world-war-ii]] — conclusion of Japanese colonial rule in 1945 initiated the restoration and institutionalization of standard national language education across the peninsula.
- [[lunar-calendar]] — traditional calendar system deeply embedded in the vocabulary of regional festivals, agrarian idioms, and ancestral ceremonies like *Chuseok*.
- [[chinese-new-year]] — celebrated in Korea as *Seollal*, forming a foundational cultural anchor that preserves ancestral greetings, food terminology, and seasonal rites.
