## summary

**Bourbon**, or **bourbon whiskey**, is a distinctive distilled American whiskey crafted primarily from corn and aged inside new charred oak barrels. The liquor takes its name from Bourbon County, Kentucky, which was established and christened in honor of the royal House of Bourbon in [[france]] to commemorate French assistance during the American Revolutionary War. Recognized by an act of the United States Congress in 1964 as a distinctive domestic product of the [[united-states]], it stands as a cornerstone of Southern agrarian heritage and American mixology.

## you gotta know

- United States federal regulations stipulate that the grain mash bill must consist of at least 51 percent corn, distilled to a strength not exceeding 160 proof (80 percent alcohol by volume).
- Must enter the aging barrel at no more than 125 proof (62.5 percent alcohol) and mature strictly in newly charred oak containers, without the addition of any artificial coloring or flavoring agents.
- Although culturally and historically synonymous with the Bluegrass region of Kentucky—favored for its high corn yields and calcium-rich, iron-free limestone spring water—it may legally be distilled anywhere in the [[united-states]].
- To qualify for the legal classification *straight bourbon*, the spirit must mature in charred oak for a minimum of two consecutive years, requiring an explicit age statement on the label if aged under four years.
- The consumer purity standard was firmly established by the *Bottled-in-Bond Act of 1897*, spearheaded by distiller *E. H. Taylor Jr.* and Treasury Secretary *John G. Carlisle* during the rapid industrial growth of the [[industrial-revolution]].
- Tennessee whiskey follows identical grain and aging standards but distinguishes itself by undergoing the *Lincoln County Process*, filtering the unaged distillate through sugar maple charcoal before barreling.
- Regional legend frequently credits Baptist preacher *Elijah Craig* with inventing the charred-barrel aging method in 1789, though historical evidence shows the process developed gradually among frontier distillers.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — country of origin that codified the whiskey as an officially protected distinctive domestic product in 1964.
- [[france]] — kingdom ruled by the House of Bourbon, whose wartime alliance inspired the naming of Bourbon County in Kentucky.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of steam transport and commercial distillation that spurred regulatory statutes like the Bottled-in-Bond Act.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict that disrupted regional supply chains while heavily taxing distilleries to fund federal military operations.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — postwar period that saw the consolidation, commercial modernization, and national distribution of Kentucky spirits.
- [[new-york-city]] — metropolitan bar capital that elevated the liquor through enduring classic cocktails like the *Manhattan* and the *Old Fashioned*.
