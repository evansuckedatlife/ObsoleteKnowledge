## summary

**Patient H.M.**, whose identity was revealed posthumously as **Henry Molaison** (full name **Henry Gustav Molaison**, often cited in medical literature simply as **H.M.**), was an American surgical patient whose tragic medical history revolutionized cognitive [[neurology]]. In 1953, he underwent an experimental bilateral medial temporal lobectomy to alleviate debilitating epileptic seizures, leaving him with profound anterograde amnesia. Studied across five decades primarily by Canadian neuropsychologist *Brenda Milner*, his condition provided definitive clinical evidence that human memory is compartmentalized into anatomically distinct neural systems.

## you gotta know

- Underwent an experimental bilateral medial temporal lobectomy in 1953 performed by neurosurgeon *William Beecher Scoville* at Hartford Hospital to control intractable post-traumatic epilepsy.
- Lost the anterior two-thirds of his hippocampi, parahippocampal cortices, entorhinal cortices, and amygdalae bilaterally, producing a severe and permanent deficit in forming new conscious long-term memories.
- Retained normal working memory, language capabilities, sensory faculties, and above-average intelligence, holding conversations effortlessly while forgetting interlocutors the moment they left his line of sight.
- Maintained largely intact retrograde memory for life events and general knowledge acquired prior to his surgery, demonstrating that long-term memories are not permanently stored in the medial temporal lobes.
- Provided landmark evidence for the distinction between declarative memory and procedural memory through *Brenda Milner*'s mirror-tracing star experiment, steadily improving his drawing motor skills despite having no conscious memory of practicing.
- Kept strictly anonymous throughout his life to protect his personal privacy, living under scientific observation until his death in a Connecticut nursing facility in December 2008.
- His brain was preserved postmortem and transported to the University of California, San Diego, where it was frozen and cut into over two thousand histological microtome slices broadcast via live digital webstream.

## connections

- [[neurology]] — supplied foundational clinical evidence establishing that memory consolidation relies on localized structures in the temporal lobe.
- [[human-anatomy]] — clarified the precise functional roles of the hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, and amygdala in human cognition.
- [[perception]] — proved that immediate conscious perceptual experience can function independently from long-term memory encoding.
- [[five-senses]] — demonstrated intact sensory processing across vision, audition, and somatic touch despite severe hippocampal amnesia.
- [[united-states]] — lived and participated in continuous scientific research across Connecticut and Massachusetts throughout the late twentieth century.
