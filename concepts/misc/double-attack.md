## summary

A **double attack** is a fundamental tactical motif in board games, most notably chess, where a single move creates two simultaneous threats against opposing targets. Because an opponent can typically address only one threat on their subsequent turn, the tactic frequently results in the winning of material or decisive strategic advantage. While commonly referred to as a **fork** when executed by a knight or pawn, the broader concept encompasses attacks by any piece as well as compound tactical maneuvers like discovered checks combined with piece captures.

## you gotta know

- Occurs whenever a single maneuver creates two distinct offensive threats, making complete defense virtually impossible on the subsequent move.
- The knight is the most famous executor of this tactic—widely termed a royal fork when simultaneously threatening the enemy king and queen—owing to its unique ability to leap over intervening pieces and attack along asymmetric lines of [[geometry]].
- Queens, rooks, and bishops frequently execute double attacks along intersecting ranks, files, and diagonals, exploiting loose or undefended enemy pieces.
- A discovered attack represents a specific compound form of double attack where moving one piece unmasks a line of sight for another piece, simultaneously unleashing two distinct threats from two different units.
- Pawn forks commonly punish premature piece development in opening systems, such as the famous fork trick occurring in openings like the Vienna Game or Petroff Defense.
- Defensive resources against a double attack are rare but include counter-threats of equal or greater value, such as an interposing check or creating a more lethal threat against the attacker's king.
- Strategic play, such as creating structural weaknesses or forcing opponent pieces onto poorly coordinated squares, is often aimed specifically at generating tactical double attacks.
