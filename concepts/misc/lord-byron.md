## summary

**Lord Byron** (George Gordon, 1788–1824) was an English Romantic poet whose outrageous personal life and stirring verse made him one of the most famous writers of his age. Notorious for scandal, seduction, and political passion, he embodied the "Byronic hero"—dark, brooding, and defiant—and died in Greece fighting for its independence.

## you gotta know

- Inherited a title and fortune; his beauty and wit made him a sensation in London literary society, but his affairs and rumours (including an alleged relationship with his half-sister) scandalized the public.
- Published *Childe Harold's Pilgrimage* (1812), which made him instantly famous; its brooding, introspective protagonist became the template for the Byronic hero.
- His masterwork is *Don Juan* (1819–1824), a sprawling, irreverent narrative poem that combines comedy, eroticism, social satire, and philosophical reflection—utterly unlike the serious poetry of his contemporaries.
- Actively involved in radical politics: championed Greek independence and died in Missolonghi in 1824 while organizing military support for Greek revolutionaries against the Ottoman Empire.
- His personal scandal (real and imagined) was inseparable from his fame; he was celebrated and condemned in equal measure—the celebrity poet par excellence of the early 19th century.
- Guest at Villa Diodati with *Shelley*, *Mary Shelley*, and John Polidori in 1816, where *Frankenstein* was conceived; his cynical personality contrasted with Shelley's idealism.
- The "Byronic hero"—dark, defiant, haunted by a mysterious past—became a cultural archetype, influencing centuries of Romantic and Gothic literature thereafter.
- Despite his wealth and title, Byron lived much of his adult life in exile, traveling through Italy, Greece, and the Levant, both escaping scandal and pursuing adventure.
- His verse is technically brilliant: he mastered multiple forms (Spenserian stanza, ottava rima), used irony and comedy to undercut Romantic sentimentality, and influenced poetry for generations.

- [[percy-bysshe-shelley]] — contemporary Romantic; they were friends and occasional rivals, united by radical politics.

- [[poetry]] — his primary art, from the brooding to the comic.
- [[don-juan]] — his satirical masterpiece, a monument to wit and irreverence.
- [[greece]] — the nation he died fighting for, cementing his legend as a liberator-poet.
- [[england]] — the country whose propriety he gleefully violated.
