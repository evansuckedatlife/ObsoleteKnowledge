## summary

**pawn promotion** is a fundamental chess rule dictating that when a pawn reaches the eighth rank for White or the first rank for Black, it must immediately transform into a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same color. Because players overwhelmingly choose the most powerful piece, the process is commonly referred to as queening, though choosing an alternate piece—known as underpromotion—is occasionally necessary to deliver checkmate or avoid stalemate. Promotion represents the culmination of successful pawn play and serves as the primary decisive mechanism in most competitive endgames.

## you gotta know

- Occurs on the eighth rank on the very turn the pawn arrives, requiring the piece choice to be declared immediately before the move is considered complete.
- Allows players to promote to a queen regardless of whether their original queen is still on the board, making multiple queens on one side entirely legal.
- Employs *underpromotion* strategically, most commonly to a knight to execute a tactical fork with check, or to a rook or bishop specifically to avoid an accidental stalemate.
- Traces back to ancient ancestors of chess such as *chaturanga* and *shatranj*, where pawns originally promoted into the weak *fers* or counselor.
- Evolved dramatically during the late fifteenth century alongside the emergence of the modern overpowered queen, revolutionizing endgame strategy.
- Relies critically on healthy [[pawn-structure]] and the escort of the friendly king, particularly in pure king-and-pawn endgames where passed pawns race forward.
- Features classical geometric rules in endgame study, such as the *rule of the square*, which visually calculates if an enemy king can catch a running passed pawn.
