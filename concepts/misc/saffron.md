## summary

**Saffron** is an exceptionally prized spice derived from the three vivid crimson stigmas and styles of the autumn-blooming purple flowering plant *Crocus sativus*. Originating in the Mediterranean basin and the [[middle-east]], it is universally celebrated as the most expensive culinary spice by weight due to the labor-intensive requirement of manual harvesting. In academic competitions, it regularly appears across antiquity, art history, classical mythology, and gastronomy questions due to its legendary role in dyeing royal vestments, scenting ancient halls, and anchoring iconic national dishes.

## you gotta know

- By weight, it is the most expensive agricultural spice in the world, requiring approximately 75,000 blossoms and extensive hand harvesting to yield a single pound of dried spice.
- The distinctive golden-yellow hue that it imparts to water and broth is primarily caused by the water-soluble carotenoid pigment *crocin*, while its bitter taste comes from *picrocrocin*.
- Its distinctive aroma, frequently described as reminiscent of sweet hay or metallic honey, arises from the volatile terpene compound *safranal*, produced by the degradation of picrocrocin during post-harvest drying.
- Minoan frescoes found at the Bronze Age site of *Akrotiri* on *Santorini* depict women known as the *Saffron Gatherers* harvesting blossoms to present to a seated goddess, highlighting its religious and medicinal antiquity in [[ancient-greece]].
- *Homer* repeatedly evokes the plant in the *Iliad* through the formulaic epithet *crocus-cloaked* or *saffron-robed Dawn* (*Krokopeplos Eos*), illustrating its symbolic connection to sacred radiance.
- Iran is the dominant modern producer of the global supply, while European production remains famous in the *La Mancha* region of [[spain]], where it is an indispensable component of authentic *paella*.
- The *Crocus sativus* cultivar is a triploid that is completely male-sterile, meaning it cannot reproduce sexually through seed set and depends entirely on vegetative propagation through underground corms divided by humans.
