## summary

**Stroganoff**, most famously prepared as **Beef Stroganoff** or *bœuf Stroganoff*, is a celebrated Russian dish composed of sautéed strips of tender beef simmered in a tangy sauce enriched with *smetana* (sour cream), mustard, and broth. Originating in mid-nineteenth-century imperial Saint Petersburg, the dish represents an influential synthesis of classic Russian pantry ingredients and sophisticated French culinary technique. Following the Russian Revolution, it traveled across East Asia and Western Europe, eventually transforming into an iconic mid-century comfort staple in the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Named after the aristocratic Russian *Stroganov* family, with popular attribution honoring either Count *Pavel Stroganov* or the diplomat Count *Alexander Grigorievich Stroganov*.
- First documented in print in *Elena Molokhovets*'s classic 1871 Russian culinary manual *A Gift to Young Housewives*, where it appeared as *goviadina po-strogonovski, s gorchitseiu* (beef à la Stroganov, with mustard).
- The original imperial recipe relied on cubed meat dredged in flour, sautéed in butter, and dressed with a simple pan reduction of mustard, bouillon, and a dollop of fresh sour cream, completely omitting mushrooms and onions.
- French chefs working for aristocratic Russian households, such as *Charles Brière*, popularized the dish internationally, notably entering a variation into a Parisian culinary competition in 1891.
- In North American mid-century home cooking, the dish evolved into sliced beef, button mushrooms, and onions simmered in sour cream and canned mushroom soup, universally ladled over broad egg noodles.
- In Brazil and other South American nations, the dish is known as *estrogonofe*, commonly made with beef, chicken, or shrimp, spiked with tomato sauce and heavy cream, and served alongside white rice and matchstick potato chips.
- Traditional accompaniments in pre-revolutionary Russia favored crisp straw potato fries rather than noodles, balancing the rich, acidic profile of the emulsified dairy sauce.
