## summary

**Thai**, historically known as *Siamese*, is the national and official language of Thailand and the native tongue of the Central Thai people. Belonging to the Kra-Dai language family, it is an analytic, tonal language characterized by five distinct lexical tones and complex registers reflecting social hierarchy. Written in its own abugida script derived from ancient Brahmic writing, the language incorporates deep lexical layers of Sanskrit, Pali, and Old Khmer alongside an influential national literature.

## you gotta know

- Member of the Southwestern Tai branch of the Kra-Dai language family, distinct from Sino-Tibetan despite historical tonal similarities.
- Operates with five distinct phonemic tones: low, mid, high, falling, and rising, which alter the lexical meaning of identical consonant-vowel combinations.
- Uses an abugida script traditionally attributed to King *Ram Khamhaeng* the Great of the Sukhothai Kingdom in the late thirteenth century.
- Relies on an uninflected, analytic grammar with Subject-Verb-Object word order, extensive numeral classifiers, and an absence of verb conjugations or grammatical gender.
- Integrates extensive religious and philosophical loanwords from Sanskrit and Pali, largely absorbed through the profound cultural presence of [[theravada-buddhism]].
- Maintains distinct linguistic registers depending on social deference, including *Rachasap*, an elaborate royal vocabulary reserved exclusively for the monarch and senior clergy.
- Functioned as the sovereign administrative tongue of the only Southeast Asian nation to successfully evade direct European conquest during nineteenth-century [[colonialism]].
