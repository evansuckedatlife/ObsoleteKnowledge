## summary

**Exchange chess**, commonly called *Bughouse chess* or *Siamese chess*, is a fast-paced chess variant played on two adjacent boards by two teams of two players. Captured pieces from one board are immediately passed to one's teammate, who can drop them onto any vacant square on their own board on any subsequent turn instead of making a regular piece movement. Characterized by rapid tactical calculation, mutual piece-flow dynamics, and intense verbal communication between partners, it is one of the most widely played cooperative variants in competitive chess circles.

## you gotta know

- Played on two parallel chessboards with four players, where partners sit adjacent to each other and play opposite colors against an opposing team.
- Whenever a player captures an opponent's piece, that piece is transferred to the player's partner's reserve bank to be dropped onto an empty square on their board.
- Pawn drops are generally subject to specific restrictions, such as prohibition from being placed on the first or eighth ranks, preventing instant unearned promotions.
- When a promoted pawn is captured, it reverts to a standard pawn rather than the promoted officer piece when handed to the partner's reserve.
- The game terminates instantly across both boards the moment checkmate is delivered on either individual board, or when a player forfeits on time.
- Employs the mechanic of piece drops directly reminiscent of the traditional Japanese strategy game *shogi*, combined with Western chess piece movements.
- Fast time controls such as blitz or bullet are standard, requiring continuous tactical alerts and verbal coordination between partners regarding desired piece drops.
