## summary

**Farsi**, commonly known in English scholarship as Persian, is an Iranian tongue belonging to the Indo-Iranian branch of the Indo-European language family. Spoken predominantly in Iran, Afghanistan (as Dari), and Tajikistan (as Tajik), it boasts an unbroken literary tradition spanning over a millennium, renowned for monumental poetic masterpieces and courtly prestige across Central and South Asia. In academic and quiz bowl domains, it is celebrated for its historical evolution from Old and Middle Persian, its adoption of the Perso-Arabic script, and its extensive linguistic legacy.

## you gotta know

- Belongs to the Western Iranian subfamily of the Indo-European languages, sharing ancient grammatical and lexical cognates with Sanskrit, Latin, and Greek.
- Written since the Islamic conquest using the Perso-Arabic script, incorporating four additional modified characters (pe, che, zhe, and gaf) to transcribe native phonemes.
- Classically divided by historical linguists into Old Persian (inscribed in cuneiform by the Achaemenid Empire), Middle Persian or Pahlavi (Sasanian era), and New Persian.
- Retains significant mutual intelligibility across its standard dialect continuum, encompassing *Farsi* in Iran, *Dari* in Afghanistan, and Cyrillic-written *Tajik* in Tajikistan.
- Famed for a monumental corpus of medieval poetry, immortalized by master writers including *Ferdowsi* (author of the national epic *Shahnameh*), *Hafez*, *Rumi*, and *Saadi*.
- Features extensive prestige loanwords adopted throughout neighboring Ottoman Turkish, Urdu, and Central Asian languages through centuries of courtly dominance.
