## summary

**Reduplication** is an extensive morphological and phonological process across human languages wherein all or part of a word or stem is repeated to alter grammatical or semantic meaning. Commonly employed to indicate grammatical aspects such as plurals, frequentatives, intensification, diminutives, or tense inflection, the mechanism appears across diverse global language families. Its systematic study remains a central topic in theoretical grammar, comparative historical philology, and linguistic typology.

## you gotta know

- Classified broadly into *total reduplication*, where an entire base or root is repeated, and *partial reduplication*, where only an affix, syllable, or single segment is doubled.
- Operates in Indo-European historical grammar to mark the perfect tense, as famously seen in Greek verb conjugations like *leloipa* from *leipō* and Latin forms like *pepigi*.
- Functions extensively in Austronesian languages, such as Tagalog and Indonesian, to indicate nominal plurality, collective groupings, or progressive verbal aspect.
- Appears in Japanese onomatopoeia and mimetics, where doubled sound sequences like *doki-doki* express a pounding heart or psychological states.
- Utilized in informal English in the form of contrastive focus reduplication, often called the *salad-salad* phenomenon, to denote a prototypical or genuine exemplar.
- Encompasses rhyming and ablaut-motivated compounding, producing colloquial expressions and binomial pairs like *chit-chat*, *mumbo-jumbo*, or *super-duper*.
- Serves as a vital developmental tool in early infant language acquisition and babbling across human linguistic cultures.
