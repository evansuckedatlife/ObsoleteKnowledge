## summary

**Stratego** is a classic two-player board game of imperfect information and battlefield tactics that simulates military command on a 10-by-10 grid. Players maneuver armies of forty rank-bearing pieces hidden from the opponent's view, attempting to locate and capture the enemy Flag while safeguarding their own. Drawing direct descent from early twentieth-century ancestral games like *L'Attaque* and traditional Chinese military chess (*Luzhanqi*), the game was trademarked in post-war Europe and became a cornerstone of mass-market strategy gaming throughout the [[cold-war]].

## you gotta know

- The ultimate victory condition is capturing the opponent's immobile Flag piece or leaving the opposing player with no legal moves remaining.
- Incorporates hidden information: pieces are printed with ranks on one side facing only the controlling player, concealing their identities until an opposing piece initiates a combat challenge.
- Combat follows a strict hierarchical ranking system where higher-ranking military officers defeat lower ones upon occupying the same square, with both pieces removed in the event of an equal rank tie.
- Special piece powers defy the raw numerical hierarchy: the low-ranking Spy can eliminate the supreme Marshal if the Spy attacks first, and Miners are uniquely equipped to defuse stationary Bombs.
- Two 2-by-2 impassable lake regions occupy the center of the grid, funneling maneuvers into narrow chokepoints and dividing the board into distinct tactical flanks.
- Derived directly from *L'Attaque*, a game patented in France in 1909 by *Hermance Edan*, before being commercialized as *Stratego* in 1946 by Dutch manufacturer *Jacques Johan Mogendorff* and popularized globally by *Milton Bradley*.
- European and American editions historically adopted inverted ranking conventions: American releases traditionally designated the Marshal as 1 and the Spy as S, whereas contemporary modern editions align with the European standard numbering the Marshal as 10.

## connections

- [[study-chess]] — shares strict square-grid movement, piece valuation, and tactical sacrificial bluffs, though chess possesses complete rather than imperfect information.
- [[world-war-ii]] — shaped the mid-century tactical board game industry and the public appetite for mechanized and infantry military command simulations.
- [[cold-war]] — coincided with the golden era of board game distribution, mirroring espionage tensions, concealed intelligence, and nuclear stalemate fears.
- [[geometry]] — dictates pathing across the 10-by-10 grid, flank maneuvers, and movement constraints imposed by central lake chokepoints.
- [[perception]] — crucial for reading opponent bluffing patterns, deducing hidden piece values, and tracking revealed piece movements across the board.
- [[united-states]] — served as a major commercial market where *Milton Bradley* turned the European import into a household board game staple.
- [[modernism]] — reflected mid-twentieth-century design sensibilities in stylized plastic game tokens, modular cardboard boards, and streamlined symbolic rulesets.
