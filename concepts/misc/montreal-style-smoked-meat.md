## summary

**Montreal-style smoked meat**, known locally in French as *viande fumée*, is a kosher-style cured and smoked beef brisket specialty native to *Montreal*, *Quebec*. Originating in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries among Central and Eastern European Jewish immigrants, it involves dry-curing the entire brisket with coarse peppercorn, coriander, and garlic before smoking, steaming, and slicing it warm by hand. Renowned worldwide alongside pastrami, it stands as an emblematic pillar of Canadian urban gastronomy centered on *Saint Laurent Boulevard*.

## you gotta know

- Utilizes the entire brisket, encompassing both the lean "flat" and the fatty "point", whereas traditional American pastrami often favors the navel cut or plate.
- Dry-cured with cracked black peppercorns, coriander seeds, mustard seed, garlic, and curing salts, featuring considerably less sugar and a sharper spice profile than pastrami.
- Undergoes a multi-day curing process followed by warm smoking in a smokehouse and subsequent gentle steaming to render the connective collagen tender.
- Always sliced warm by hand with a long knife directly upon ordering, as automated deli slicers tend to tear the delicate, steaming meat fibers.
- Customers traditionally specify their preferred fat ratio when ordering at the counter: "lean", "medium", "medium-fat", or "fat".
- Served customarily stacked high on seedless rye bread smeared with yellow ballpark mustard, accompanied by a sour dill pickle, French fries, and black cherry soda.
- Synonymous with *Schwartz's*, formally known as the *Montreal Hebrew Delicatessen*, founded in 1928 by *Reuben Schwartz* on *The Main*.
- Frequently cross-pollinated with other regional staples, serving as a signature topping on Quebecois poutine and sharing culinary territory with the city's wood-fired bakers.
