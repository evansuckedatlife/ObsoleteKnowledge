## summary

**Falafel** is a deep-fried ball or patty made from seasoned ground chickpeas, fava beans, or a combination of both, native to the culinary traditions of Egypt and the Levant. Widely consumed as a staple street food wrapped in flatbread with salads and tahini, it occupies an iconic position in Middle Eastern cuisine and global vegetarian dining. The dish carries deep cultural significance across competing national cuisines and plays an important role in religious dietary observances throughout the Mediterranean basin.

## you gotta know

- Prepared by soaking dried pulses without boiling, grinding them coarsely with fresh herbs such as parsley and coriander, seasoning with cumin and coriander seed, and deep-frying the mixture.
- Known in Egypt and Sudan as *ta'amiya*, where it is traditionally prepared using exclusively broad fava beans rather than chickpeas.
- Often served tucked inside pita or wrapped in flatbread, accompanied by pickled vegetables, chopped salads, and sauces based on tahini or amba.
- Frequently cited as originating among Coptic Christians in Egypt as a nourishing, meatless substitute for consumption during prolonged fasts such as Lent.
- Forms a quintessential food for breaking the daily fast during *Ramadan*, aligning with communal evening meals throughout the *[[middle-east]]*.
- Center of passionate culinary diplomacy and cultural ownership debates between Egyptian, Palestinian, Lebanese, Syrian, and Israeli cuisines.
- Became a foundational dish of international vegetarian and vegan street food culture during the late twentieth century.
