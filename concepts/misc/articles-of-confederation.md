## summary

The **Articles of Confederation**, formally the **Articles of Confederation and Perpetual Union**, served as the first governing charter of the [[united-states]] following its break from the [[british-empire]]. Drafted by the *Second Continental Congress* in 1777 and formally ratified in 1781, the charter established a decentralized confederacy of thirteen sovereign states under a single legislative body. Designed specifically to prevent centralized tyranny, the government operated without an independent chief executive, a national judiciary, or the power to collect federal taxes. Although the charter successfully saw the infant nation through the conclusion of the [[american-revolution]], its severe structural deficiencies, fiscal insolvency, and inability to resolve interstate crises led directly to the drafting of the [[united-states-constitution]].

## you gotta know

- Established a unicameral legislature where each state received exactly one vote, requiring a supermajority of nine states to pass major legislation and unanimous consent of all thirteen states to enact amendments.
- Intentionally created neither an independent executive branch to enforce federal laws nor a national judiciary to resolve interstate disputes, leaving governance dependent on voluntary state compliance.
- Deprived the national congress of the power to levy taxes directly or regulate foreign and interstate commerce, leaving the confederation dependent on voluntary financial requisitions that states routinely ignored.
- Exposed its fatal administrative and military vulnerability during *Shays' Rebellion* in Massachusetts, where the national government proved completely powerless to intervene or dispatch troops to restore order.
- Achieved its most enduring legislative success through the *Land Ordinance of 1785* and the *Northwest Ordinance of 1787*, establishing rectangular township surveys, funding public education, and barring slavery north of the Ohio River.
- Originated from an early draft prepared by *John Dickinson*, whose initial vision for a strong central administration was drastically curtailed by delegates determined to guard state sovereignty.
- Ratification was delayed for over three years until 1781 because Maryland refused to sign until land-rich states like Virginia surrendered their western territory to create a shared public domain.
- Prompted the *Annapolis Convention* of 1786 to resolve interstate trade barriers, an effort that failed to reach quorum but directly yielded Alexander Hamilton's call for the Philadelphia Convention.

## connections

- [[united-states-constitution]] — drafted in 1787 to replace the Articles by instituting a federal executive, a bicameral legislature, and national taxation.
- [[american-revolution]] — the wartime crisis that forced thirteen disparate colonies to formalize an alliance and establish a legitimate continental government.
- [[enlightenment]] — provided the republican theories of Montesquieu and John Locke that made delegates distrust centralized power and favor localized sovereignty.
- [[united-states]] — established the legal foundation, institutional sovereignty, and enduring name for the newly declared republic.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial monarchy whose arbitrary taxation and administrative overreach prompted the framers to avoid creating a strong central executive.
- [[france]] — the essential wartime ally and creditor whose loans could not be serviced because the confederation lacked any power to generate tax revenue.
- [[ancient-greece]] — offered historical models of decentralized confederacies, such as the Amphictyonic League, studied by James Madison to diagnose the charter's systemic weaknesses.
- [[massachusetts-history]] — witnessed *Shays' Rebellion*, the armed uprising that alarmed George Washington and crystallized elite support for constitutional reform.
