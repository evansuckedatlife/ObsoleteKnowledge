## summary

A **postcard** is an open rectangular piece of cardstock designed for writing and mailing without an envelope, introduced in the mid-nineteenth century to simplify inexpensive postal communications. Popularized across the Austro-Hungarian Empire, *Great Britain*, and the [[united-states]], the medium evolved dramatically during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries into the ubiquitous **picture postcard**, sparking a global craze for collecting known as **deltiology**. These mass-produced visual documents captured urban streetscapes, international exhibitions, and wartime messages, fundamentally transforming visual culture and everyday communication.

## you gotta know

- Emerged internationally following the 1869 adoption of the *Correspondenz-Karte* by the Austro-Hungarian postal administration, designed to facilitate short, affordable messages.
- The Golden Age of **postcards** spanned roughly from 1898 to the outbreak of [[world-war-i]] in 1914, driven by advancements in photolithography, chromolithography, and reduced mailing tariffs.
- The study and collection of these cards is formally designated **deltiology**, derived from the Greek *deltion*, meaning small writing tablet.
- International postal regulations initially forbade writing personal correspondence on the address side; in 1902 *Britain* and in 1907 the Universal Postal Union approved the divided back, allowing message and address to share one face while leaving the reverse entirely for photography or art.
- The *Universal Postal Union* standardized dimensions and international postage rates, allowing millions of tourist cards, souvenir greetings, and propaganda cards to cross borders cheaply.
- Popularized widely at international expos, including the *1889 Exposition Universelle* in [[france]], where views of the newly erected Eiffel Tower were mailed directly from the monument.
- Crucial documentary medium during wartime, where field service cards permitted soldiers to transmit pre-printed assurances of survival or brief personal updates under strict military censorship.
- Often carried very brief notations or a hasty [[postscript]] due to strict spatial constraints on the printed cardstock.

## connections

- [[belle-epoque]] — the cultural era in Europe that coincided with the golden age of publishing and mailing illustrated cards.
- [[art-nouveau]] — the prevailing decorative style utilized by artists like *Alphonse Mucha* who created collectible commercial cards.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — provided the mechanized high-speed printing presses and expanding railroad networks that enabled cheap, rapid distribution.
- [[united-states]] — home to early private mailing cards authorized by Congress in 1898 and publishers like the *Curt Teich Company* of Chicago.
- [[postscript]] — the epistolary convention frequently deployed in the margins of restricted card layouts to convey final thoughts.
- [[world-war-i]] — catalyzed massive production of patriotic, satirical, and standardized field service cards sent between trenches and the home front.
- [[germany]] — led the global lithographic printing industry, producing the highest-quality colored cards exported throughout the Western world.
