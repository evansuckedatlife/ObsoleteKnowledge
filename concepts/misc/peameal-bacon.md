## summary

**peameal bacon** is an iconic Canadian cured pork specialty made from lean, boneless pork loin trimmed of fat, wet-cured in a salty brine, and rolled in ground yellow cornmeal. Developed during the nineteenth century in Toronto by pork packer *William Davies*, the style originally utilized ground dried yellow peas as a protective crust to preserve meat shipped across the Atlantic. Today it is celebrated as an emblematic culinary staple of Ontario and the defining ingredient of Toronto's famous St. Lawrence Market breakfast sandwiches.

## you gotta know

- Developed in the late nineteenth century by English immigrant *William Davies*, whose massive meatpacking empire earned Toronto the enduring moniker *Hogtown*.
- Originally rolled in crushed, dried yellow field peas to retard spoilage, absorb exterior moisture, and deter pests during lengthy overseas shipping to Victorian Britain.
- Transitioned after [[world-war-i]] to yellow cornmeal rather than crushed peas due to easier availability, longer shelf life, and resistance to rancidity.
- Differs sharply from standard American streaky bacon by utilizing the lean loin rather than fatty pork belly, and differs from traditional ham by remaining unsmoked.
- Served typically griddled or sliced thick in round cutlets rather than fried crisp, remaining juicy and tender inside a toasted kaiser roll.
- Anchored at the historic *St. Lawrence Market* in Toronto, where vendors like the *Carousel Bakery* popularized it internationally for food travelers.
- Often confused internationally with generic *Canadian bacon*, which in American parlance typically denotes fully smoked, pre-cooked cylindrical pork loin.
