## summary

The **Tlingit** (autonym **Lingít**) are an Indigenous people of the Pacific Northwest Coast of North America whose ancestral homeland spans the coastal temperate rainforests, islands, and fjords of Southeast Alaska as well as adjacent parts of British Columbia and Yukon. Renowned for their sophisticated matrilineal clan governance, monumental red cedar architecture, and intricate ceremonial arts, they developed complex marine-based economies long before maritime fur traders arrived. Their enduring cultural institutions and legal triumphs in defending land and civil rights remain foundational to the modern history of Alaska.

## you gotta know

- Traditional society is structured around a strict matrilineal moiety system divided into *Raven* (*Yéil*) and *Eagle* or *Wolf* (*Ch'aak'* or *Gooch*), requiring individuals to marry members of the opposite moiety.
- Monumental woodcarving is a celebrated artistic hallmark, expressed through heraldic crests carved on cedar [[totem-pole]] monuments, mortuary poles, ceremonial clan house posts, and sleek ocean-going dugout canoes.
- The ceremonial [[potlatch]] (*koo.éex'*) serves as the core legal, political, and spiritual assembly, where hosts distribute wealth, transfer ancestral names, mourn deceased clan leaders, and publicly validate hereditary crests and territorial rights.
- Textile masterworks include the prestigious *Chilkat* blanket, woven from mountain goat wool and shredded yellow cedar bark using twining techniques to display stylized formline clan crests, and the later button blanket robes.
- Major oral literature and cosmologies center on the culture hero and trickster [[raven]], who stole the celestial light from an old chief's bentwood box to release the sun, moon, and stars into the world.
- Armed resistance against Russian imperial expansion culminated in the 1802 destruction of *Saint Michael* redoubt and the decisive 1804 Battle of Sitka, where the Kiks.ádi clan led by *K'alyáan* defended Shis'kí Noow against *Alexander Baranov*.
- Civil rights pioneer *Elizabeth Peratrovich*, a member of the Lukaax̱.ádi clan, delivered an impassioned 1945 speech before the territorial senate that spurred passage of Alaska's pioneering Anti-Discrimination Act.

## connections

- [[colonialism]] — endured maritime fur trade exploitation, missionary suppression of rituals, and direct warfare against the Russian-American Company.
- [[united-states]] — saw sovereignty compromised after the 1867 Alaska Purchase, later achieving landmark recognition in the 1971 Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act.
- [[linguistics]] — speaks an endangered branch of the Na-Dene language family, noted for its complex consonant inventory and rich lateral fricatives.
- [[potlatch]] — practiced this vital redistributive ceremony to honor the dead, validate clan titles, and reaffirm social bonds.
- [[haida]] — shared maritime borders, trade networks, artistic formline conventions, and reciprocal ceremonial visits across the Alexander Archipelago.
- [[totem-pole]] — carved monumental cedar pillars to record genealogies, memorialize chiefs, and proclaim lineage heraldry outside clan houses.
- [[raven]] — reveres this culture hero and mythic trickster who brought light, freshwater, and salmon to humanity.
