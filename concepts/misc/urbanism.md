## summary

**Urbanism** is the discipline of designing and organizing cities and urban spaces—neighborhoods, streets, parks, transit systems, and civic buildings. It treats the city as a coherent whole, considering how people move, gather, live, and interact within built environments. Modern urbanism emerged as cities exploded in the Industrial Revolution.

## you gotta know

- Distinct from architecture (individual buildings) and from sociology (studying cities); urbanism is the design practice that shapes urban form and social life through spatial planning.
- The field formalized in the 19th century: *Daniel Burnham* and others developed comprehensive master plans for cities (the "City Beautiful" movement), emphasizing grand avenues, parks, and civic centers.
- Zoning—dividing cities into residential, commercial, and industrial districts—became a major planning tool in early 20th-century cities but often reinforced racial and economic segregation.
- Public space is central: streets, plazas, parks, and transit hubs determine how strangers interact, whether neighbourhoods feel safe and lively, and how effectively cities function.
- Transportation shapes urbanism profoundly: streetcars, subways, and automobiles have determined sprawl, density, and walkability; modern urbanism grapples with car-dependence.
- Contemporary urbanism debates include gentrification, mixed-income housing, transit-oriented development, pedestrian-friendly streets, and environmental sustainability.
- The "New Urbanism" movement (1980s–present) argues for walkable neighborhoods, diverse housing types, and mixed-use districts—a reaction against post-war suburban sprawl and car-dependent planning.
- Jane Jacobs revolutionized urbanism by arguing that vibrant, diverse neighborhoods (not top-down master plans) create healthy cities—her critique of modernist urban renewal remains influential.
- Smart cities and digital urbanism are reshaping how planners design infrastructure, optimize transit, and respond to climate change; data and IoT sensors inform real-time urban management.
- Urban parks (Central Park, Parc des Buttes-aux-Cailles, Singapore's nature-integrated design) are essential to urban quality of life, providing recreation, air quality, and social gathering.
- Affordable housing remains urbanism's central challenge: market-driven development often prices out longtime residents, displacing communities and eroding the diversity that makes cities vibrant.
