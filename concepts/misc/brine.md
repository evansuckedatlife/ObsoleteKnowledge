## summary

**Brine** is a concentrated aqueous solution of sodium chloride or other mineral salts, occurring naturally in salt lakes and ocean depths or manufactured for industrial and culinary use. Historically foundational to global trade through food preservation, pickling, and meat curing, the solution dehydrates harmful microorganisms through high osmotic pressure. In modern industrial chemistry and municipal civil engineering, concentrated salt solutions play indispensable roles in the chlor-alkali process, industrial refrigeration, cheese aging, hydraulic fracturing, and roadway de-icing.

## you gotta know

- Possesses an elevated salinity typically exceeding 50 parts per thousand, placing it well above typical seawater concentrations of around 35 parts per thousand.
- Prevents microbial spoilage in culinary pickling and corning by exerting strong osmotic pressure, which draws water out of bacterial cells to halt cellular metabolism.
- Lowers the freezing point of liquid water via colligative freezing-point depression, making sprayed liquid solutions essential for modern highway winter de-icing operations.
- Serves as the fundamental raw feedstock in the industrial *chlor-alkali process*, undergoing membrane electrolysis to yield elemental chlorine, sodium hydroxide, and hydrogen.
- Acts as a crucial chemical intermediary in the nineteenth-century *Solvay process*, reacting with ammonia and carbon dioxide to generate industrial sodium carbonate.
- Forms lethal natural underwater reservoirs called *brine pools* on the ocean floor, where high fluid density prevents dense saline fluids from mixing into surrounding seawater.
- Circulates through commercial ice production, meatpacking refrigeration networks, and industrial cooling loops as an efficient secondary cooling liquid.
