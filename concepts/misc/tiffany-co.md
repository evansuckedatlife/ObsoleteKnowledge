## summary

**Tiffany & Co.** (popularly known as **Tiffany's** or **Tiffany and Company**) is an American luxury jewelry and specialty design house founded in *New York City* in 1837 by *Charles Lewis Tiffany* and *John B. Young*. Renowned globally for its diamond craftsmanship, robin's-egg blue packaging, and flagship boutique on Fifth Avenue, the maison became a quintessential symbol of high-society glamour and decorative artistry during the *Gilded Age* and the *Belle Époque*. Its pioneering standards in sterling silver, collaborations with master decorative artists like *Louis Comfort Tiffany*, and cultural immortalization in mid-century literature and cinema solidified its place in international decorative arts and commerce.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1837 as a stationery and fancy goods emporium named *Tiffany, Young and Ellis* at 259 Broadway in [[new-york-city]], funded by a modest thousand-dollar loan from the founder's father.
- Introduced the iconic trademarked robin's-egg color known as *Tiffany Blue* in 1845 for the cover of the *Blue Book*, the first direct-mail luxury retail catalogue printed in the [[united-states]].
- Established the 925/1000 sterling silver purity standard in the [[united-states]] in 1851, a benchmark later adopted nationally by the federal government for coinage and industrial hallmarking.
- Gained international prestige at the [[1889-paris-worlds-fair]] and the [[1851-great-exhibition-london]], where decorative designer *Edward C. Moore* earned global accolades for silverware exhibiting Japanese and naturalistic design influences.
- Introduced the celebrated six-prong *Tiffany Setting* for diamond solitaire engagement rings in 1886, engineered to elevate the gemstone above the band to maximize light refraction and brilliance.
- Acquired the extraordinary 287.42-carat yellow rough diamond discovered in South Africa in 1877, cut into the famous 128.54-carat *Tiffany Diamond*, worn in public by only a few figures including *Audrey Hepburn* and *Lady Gaga*.
- Associated with *Louis Comfort Tiffany*, son of the founder, who became the firm's first design director in 1902 and pioneered influential [[art-nouveau]] leaded favrile glassware and stained glass lamps.
- Immortalized in popular literature and cinema through *Truman Capote*'s 1958 novella and *Blake Edwards*'s 1961 film adaptation starring *Audrey Hepburn*, cementing its flagship at Fifth Avenue and 57th Street as an enduring monument of Manhattan elegance that later sought revitalization among younger generations via platforms like [[tiktok]].

## connections

- [[new-york-city]] — the urban birthplace and home of the maison's legendary Fifth Avenue flagship boutique.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose commercial silver standards and luxury consumer culture the firm fundamentally shaped.
- [[art-nouveau]] — the decorative arts movement championed by the firm's first design director, *Louis Comfort Tiffany*.
- [[belle-epoque]] — the historic era of cosmopolitan luxury and decorative refinement in which the brand gained international renown.
- [[1889-paris-worlds-fair]] — the world exposition where the company triumphed on the international stage with its silverware and jewelry.
- [[france]] — the European center of haute joaillerie where the house purchased royal crown jewels and opened Parisian ateliers.
- [[tiktok]] — the short-form video platform leveraged in modern viral rebranding campaigns targeting contemporary luxury consumers.
