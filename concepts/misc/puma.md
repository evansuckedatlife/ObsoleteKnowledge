## summary

**Puma** (*Puma concolor*), commonly referred to across the Western Hemisphere as the *cougar*, *mountain lion*, or *panther*, is an adaptable large felid native to the Americas. Spanning an unparalleled natural range from the Canadian Yukon down to the southern tip of Chile, it ranks as the second-heaviest cat in the New World after the jaguar. Classified within the subfamily Felinae rather than Pantherinae, it cannot roar, relying instead on purrs, hisses, and vocal screams while reigning as a solitary apex ambush predator of crucial ecological significance.

## you gotta know

- Holds the official *Guinness World Record* for the animal with the greatest number of common names, boasting more than forty distinct appellations in English alone, including *cougar*, *mountain lion*, *panther*, *catamount*, and *painter*.
- Belongs anatomically to the subfamily *Felinae* (small cats) rather than *Pantherinae* (big roaring cats); possesses a completely ossified hyoid bone that allows continuous purring while breathing in and out but prevents genuine roaring.
- Possesses the broadest latitudinal geographic distribution of any native terrestrial land mammal in the Western Hemisphere, occupying habitats across more than 110 degrees of latitude from boreal forests to tropical wetlands and arid deserts.
- Operates as a solitary, stealthy ambush predator, using massive hind legs to execute vertical leaps reaching 18 feet (5.5 meters) into the air and horizontal bounds spanning over 40 feet (12 meters).
- Preys principally upon large ungulates including mule deer, white-tailed deer, and elk, regularly caching uneaten portions of carcasses beneath leaf litter, brush, or snow to feed over consecutive days.
- Includes vulnerable sub-populations of urgent conservation concern, most notably the *Florida panther* (*Puma concolor coryi*), which endured extreme genetic inbreeding depression alleviated by the strategic introduction of Texas cougars in 1995.
- Demonstrates close evolutionary affinities with the African cheetah (*Acinonyx jubatus*) and the American jaguarundi (*Herpailurus yagouaroundi*), having descended from a shared felid lineage that inhabited North America in the Pliocene epoch.
- Occupies a venerated position in Indigenous American cosmology and art, celebrated by the Inca Empire—whose imperial capital of Cusco was intentionally planned in the architectural silhouette of a puma—as well as Cherokee and Navajo traditions.
