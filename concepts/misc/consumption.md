## summary

**Consumption**, identified clinically as **pulmonary tuberculosis** and traditionally termed **phthisis**, is a debilitating infectious disease caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* that predominantly ravages the human lungs. Marked by relentless weight loss, chronic fever, night sweats, and blood-streaked sputum, the pestilence devastated urban centers across Europe and North America during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Beyond its staggering clinical mortality, the condition generated a complex cultural mythology that associated physical wasting with heightened artistic genius, spiritual purity, and tragic romantic allure.

## you gotta know

- Acquired the colloquial moniker "the White Plague" throughout nineteenth-century cities due to the extreme pallor and emaciation exhibited by chronically infected patients.
- Mythologized heavily by the aesthetic movement of *Romanticism*, which interpreted the disease's physical decline as an outward emblem of emotional sensitivity and delicate intellectual refinement.
- Claimed the life of the English poet *John Keats* in 1821 at age twenty-five in *Rome*, an event famously prefigured when Keats examined his own coughed arterial blood and pronounced it his death warrant.
- Identified bacteriologically in 1882 by German physician *Robert Koch*, who isolated the tubercle bacillus using carbol-fuchsin staining and formulated his famous postulates proving microbial causation.
- Gave rise to the international sanatorium movement pioneered by *Hermann Brehmer* and *Edward Livingston Trudeau*, which isolated patients in clean alpine or rural environments to promote pulmonary rest.
- Framed the central dramatic setting of *Thomas Mann's* seminal modernist novel *The Magic Mountain*, which explored European ideological decay within a Swiss tuberculosis sanatorium.
- Yielded to pharmaceutical treatment only in the mid-twentieth century with the discovery of the aminoglycoside antibiotic *streptomycin* by *Selman Waksman* and *Albert Schatz* in 1943.

## connections

- [[percy-bysshe-shelley]] — commemorated fellow poet *John Keats* in the pastoral elegy *Adonais* following Keats's fatal decline from pulmonary infection.
- [[england]] — experienced staggering industrial mortality rates where damp housing and unventilated factories catalyzed lethal urban transmission.
- [[human-anatomy]] — destroys pulmonary alveoli through granulomatous inflammation and caseous necrosis before potentially disseminating to bones and lymph nodes.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — facilitated widespread epidemic outbreaks through crowded tenement dwellings, child labor, and impoverished factory working conditions.
- [[romanticism]] — embraced an idealized aesthetic standard characterized by translucent skin, flushed cheeks, and fragile melancholy.
- [[realism]] — stripped away sentimental Victorian mythology in late nineteenth-century literature to expose the grim, impoverished reality of terminal infection.
- [[united-kingdom]] — enacted foundational sanatorium regimens and municipal public health initiatives to curb devastating workplace morbidity.
