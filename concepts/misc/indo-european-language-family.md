## summary

The **Indo-European language family** is the world's most widely spoken language family, encompassing hundreds of related languages across Europe, the Iranian plateau, and the Indian subcontinent. Originating from a reconstructed common ancestor known as *Proto-Indo-European* (PIE), it includes major branches such as Indo-Iranian, Italic, Germanic, Celtic, Slavic, Baltic, Hellenic, Armenian, and Albanian. Its systematic identification in the late eighteenth century laid the foundation for modern comparative linguistics.

## you gotta know

- Identified systematically by British philologist *Sir William Jones* in his famous 1786 Third Anniversary Discourse to the *Asiatic Society* in Calcutta, where he observed common structural roots linking Sanskrit, Greek, Latin, Gothic, and Celtic.
- Reconstructed as *Proto-Indo-European* through the comparative method, posited by the dominant *Kurgan hypothesis* of *Marija Gimbutas* to have originated among pastoralists in the Pontic-Caspian steppe during the Chalcolithic and Early Bronze Age.
- Explains systematic consonant shifts across branches, most notably codified by *Grimm's Law* and *Verner's Law*, showing regular phonetic correspondences between Germanic consonants and their classical equivalents (e.g., Latin *pater* to English *father*).
- Characterized historically by the *Centum-Satem* isogloss division, distinguishing western branches that merged palatovelar stops with plain velars from eastern branches that affricated or spirantized them.
- Analyzed structurally using the *laryngeal theory* proposed by Swiss linguist *Ferdinand de Saussure* in 1878 to account for vowel alternations (*ablaut*), later vindicated when *Jerzy Kuryłowicz* discovered the phoneme *ḫ* in deciphered Hittite texts.
- Includes extinct historical branches such as *Anatolian* (the earliest attested branch, preserving *Hittite* cuneiform tablets) and *Tocharian* (spoken in the Tarim Basin of western China).

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — served as the foundational testing ground for historical phonology, language tree modeling, and the comparative method.
- [[indonesian]] — contributed vast lexical strata to non-Indo-European languages through prolonged contact, trade, and colonisation.
- [[ancient-greece]] — provides some of the oldest alphabetic literature in the family through the Homeric epics and Mycenaean Linear B.
- [[roman-empire]] — disseminated the Italic branch across western Europe through Latin, which subsequently diversified into the modern Romance languages.
- [[enlightenment]] — stimulated philological inquiry into human antiquity, comparative grammar, and ancient texts.
- [[british-empire]] — enabled the comparative study of Sanskrit and Celtic while globally spreading the English language.
- [[germany]] — nurtured pioneer nineteenth-century philologists such as *Franz Bopp*, *August Schleicher*, and the *Brothers Grimm*.
