## summary

**Eid al-Fitr** is an Islamic religious holiday celebrated worldwide to mark the conclusion of the month of *Ramadan*, the month-long dawn-to-sunset fast. Observed on the first day of the lunar month of *Shawwal*, it provides a communal occasion for spiritual gratitude, charitable giving, festive feasting, and family reunification. It is one of the two major canonical celebrations in the Muslim world, standing alongside *Eid al-Adha*.

## you gotta know

- Marks the end of *Sawm*, the mandatory month of daytime fasting that constitutes one of the five core obligations of Islamic practice.
- Commences only upon the verified physical or calculated sighting of the waxing crescent moon, or *hilal*, signaling the entry of the tenth lunar month.
- Requires the distribution of *Zakat al-Fitr*, a compulsory charitable donation of food staples or money given to the poor prior to the morning festival prayer.
- Centers on the performance of a communal morning prayer, known as *Salat al-Eid*, performed in congregation within open fields or large mosques.
- Traditionally opens with a small ritual meal, frequently odd numbers of dates, before departing for morning prayers to signify the deliberate end of fasting.
- Commonly referred to informally as the *Lesser Eid* in comparison to *Eid al-Adha*, despite its widespread communal festivities, gift-giving, and public celebrations.
- Prompts greetings of *Eid Mubarak*, meaning blessed festival, along with regional customs such as preparing traditional sweets like *ma'amoul*, *sheer khurma*, or *kahk*.
- Shifts backward through the solar year by approximately ten to eleven days annually because the observational calendar contains no intercalary leap months.
