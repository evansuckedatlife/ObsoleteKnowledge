## summary

**Mechanics** (often designated as **classical mechanics**) is the primary branch of physical science and applied mathematics concerned with the behavior of macroscopic physical bodies subjected to forces, displacements, and their subsequent environment interactions. Originating in [[ancient-greece]] through early practical and mathematical investigations into levers, pulleys, and hydrostatic balance, the discipline attained rigorous theoretical maturity during the European [[enlightenment]]. It established the deterministic paradigm of natural philosophy, demonstrating that the motions of terrestrial falling bodies and distant celestial orbits follow identical, mathematically quantifiable laws. By unifying geometric proofs with observational calculus, the field became the enduring operational foundation for civil engineering, navigation, ballistics, and the steam-powered innovations that catalyzed the [[industrial-revolution]].

## you gotta know

- *Isaac Newton* established the mathematical foundation of classical dynamics in his 1687 masterwork *Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica*, articulating the three universal laws of motion alongside universal gravitation to unify terrestrial kinematics and planetary orbits.
- Theoretical statics began in antiquity when *Archimedes* mathematically formalized the law of the lever and center of gravity, famously declaring that given a suitable fulcrum and a sufficiently long lever, he could move the entire Earth.
- Classical simple machines—traditionally enumerated as the lever, wheel and axle, pulley, inclined plane, wedge, and screw—produce mechanical advantage by trading the distance through which an input effort moves for an amplified output load force.
- Fundamental conservation laws govern all isolated physical systems, dictating that total linear momentum, angular momentum, and mechanical energy remain strictly constant over time during conservative interactions in the absence of external net forces.
- *Galileo Galilei* dismantled Aristotelian mechanics by demonstrating through inclined plane experiments that gravitational acceleration is independent of mass, while establishing the core concept of classical inertia and inertial frames of reference.
- The discipline divides fundamentally into kinematics, which describes trajectories, velocities, and accelerations geometrically without considering the forces causing them, and dynamics, which analyzes how applied forces and torques dictate the motions of bodies.
- Analytical mechanics reformulated Newtonian vector equations into scalar energy frameworks, advanced when *Joseph-Louis Lagrange* introduced generalized coordinates to eliminate constraint forces and *William Rowan Hamilton* reformulated mechanics using phase-space canonical coordinates.
- *Emmy Noether* proved in 1915 that every continuous differentiable symmetry of a physical action corresponds to an exact conservation law, establishing that time-translation invariance produces energy conservation and rotational symmetry produces angular momentum conservation.

## connections

- [[geometry]] — provides the spatial coordinate frames, vector algebra, and differential manifolds necessary to model trajectories, rigid body rotations, and mechanical constraints.
- [[astronomy]] — served as the empirical crucible for gravitational mechanics through *Johannes Kepler*'s planetary laws and precision orbital calculations of moons and planets.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — mechanized human production by applying kinematic linkages, gears, and thermodynamics to steam engines, automated looms, and railway locomotives.
- [[architecture]] — depends directly on static equilibrium, vector resolution of loads, and shear stress calculations to guarantee structural stability against gravity and wind.
- [[enlightenment]] — celebrated Newtonian deterministic mechanics as the supreme intellectual framework of natural order, reshaping philosophy, political theory, and scientific methodology.
- [[ancient-greece]] — produced early mechanical treatises and practical machines through *Archimedes* and *Hero of Alexandria*, who analyzed simple machines, catapults, and hydrostatics.
- [[space-race]] — required orbital mechanics, multi-stage ballistic rocketry calculations, and gravitational assist trajectories to maneuver spacecraft between celestial bodies.
