## summary

**Texas hold 'em** is a widely played community card variant of poker that originated in Robstown, Texas, in the early twentieth century. Each player is dealt two private hole cards, followed by five face-up community cards dealt across three betting rounds known as the flop, turn, and river. Renowned as the premier contest of chance, psychology, and probability, it serves as the main event format for the *World Series of Poker* and remains a ubiquitous cultural pastime across the [[united-states]].

## you gotta know

- Officially recognized by the Texas Legislature as originating in Robstown, Texas, dating back to the early 1900s before spreading through the state.
- Introduced to Las Vegas in 1967 by a group of legendary gamblers including *Doyle Brunson*, *Amarillo Slim*, and *Crandell Addington*.
- Formatted around four betting rounds: pre-flop, the flop of three cards, the single turn card, and the concluding river card.
- Skyrocketed in global television and online popularity following accountant *Chris Moneymaker*'s victory in the 2003 *World Series of Poker* Main Event after qualifying via an online satellite tournament.
- Played in fixed-limit, pot-limit, and most famously no-limit formats, the latter described by *Doyle Brunson* in his seminal strategy manual *Super/System* as the "Cadillac of Poker."
- Analyzed heavily via game theory and probability, leading to the development of sophisticated artificial intelligence solvers such as *Libratus* and *Pluribus* to achieve game-theoretic equilibrium.
- Involves standard hand rankings where players construct the strongest five-card hand using any combination of their two personal hole cards and the five community cards.
