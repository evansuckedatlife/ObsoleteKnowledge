## summary

**Soy sauce** (known natively in various forms including Japanese **shoyu** and Chinese **jiangyou**) is a dark, salty, and savory fermented liquid condiment prepared from soybeans, roasted grain, brine, and specialized mold cultures. First created in ancient *China* more than two millennia ago as a method for preserving proteins, the seasoning spread across East Asia, inspiring distinctive national traditions and brewing methods. In sensory science and gastronomy, the condiment is celebrated as the defining historical foundation of the *umami* flavor profile due to its exceptionally high concentration of free glutamates.

## you gotta know

- Supplies an intense savory taste driven by naturally occurring free glutamic acid and amino acids released during enzymatic proteolysis, serving as the universal benchmark for the taste sensation known as *umami*.
- Traces its ancestral lineage to ancient *China* during the *Western Han* dynasty, where it evolved from meat and grain ferments called *jiang* into a purely vegetarian, soy-based liquid seasoning.
- Produced traditionally through a two-stage fermentation process, beginning with an aerobic solid-state culture where soybeans and cracked wheat are inoculated with *Aspergillus oryzae* or *Aspergillus sojae* molds to create *koji*.
- Undergoes a prolonged anaerobic secondary fermentation lasting months or years after the koji is submerged in saltwater brine to form a mash termed *moromi*, in which lactic acid bacteria and yeasts develop rich flavor compounds.
- Diverged in *Japan* during the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries through the introduction of roasted wheat in equal proportion to soybeans, yielding the five classic *shoyu* varieties: *koikuchi* (all-purpose dark), *usukuchi* (pale and saltier), *tamari* (thick and wheat-free), *shiro* (light wheat-dominant), and *saishikomi* (double-brewed).
- Contrasts with cheap industrial imitations made via chemical acid-hydrolysis, which rapidly breaks down defatted soy meal with hydrochloric acid but lacks the delicate esters, alcohols, and organic acids produced by genuine microbial aging.
- Commercialized globally by centuries-old brewing dynasties such as *Kikkoman*, which originated in Noda, *Japan*, and exported barrel-aged condiments through maritime trade routes to European courts as early as the seventeenth century.
