## summary

The **outboard motor** is a self-contained marine propulsion and steering mechanism engineered to be attached to the exterior transom of a boat. Comprising an internal combustion engine or electric drive, a transmission gearbox, and a submerged propeller in a single pivotable assembly, it revolutionized small watercraft design across commercial fishing, recreational boating, and military tactics. Developed in the early twentieth century, it granted boaters portable, efficient, and easily maintained mechanical power without requiring inboard engine compartments.

## you gotta know

- Popularized commercially by Norwegian-American machinist *Ole Evinrude*, who founded *Evinrude Motors* in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, in 1909 after allegedly rowing across a hot Wisconsin lake to fetch melting ice cream for his future wife, *Bess Cary*.
- Preceded by early mechanical designs like the gasoline-powered "Porto-Motor," developed and patented in 1907 by American inventor *Cameron Waterman*.
- Combines powerhead, driveshaft, and lower gearcase into an integrated unit that pivots on transom clamps, allowing the pilot to steer the watercraft by directing propeller thrust without needing a separate rudder.
- Deployed widely as vital tactical equipment during *World War II*, where high-powered military outboards propelled combat engineer assault boats, pontoon-building rafts, and inflatable reconnaissance dinghies across European rivers and Pacific beaches.
- Incorporates an anti-ventilation plate (frequently referred to as an anti-cavitation plate) situated directly above the propeller to prevent surface air from being pulled into the spinning blades, which would cause sudden loss of thrust.
- Features a mechanical tilt-and-trim system that allows the lower unit to be swung upward, enabling boats to navigate shallow water safely and preventing propeller damage when beaching or trailering.
- Fueled the growth of major twentieth-century manufacturing enterprises, most notably the Outboard Marine Corporation (OMC, makers of *Evinrude* and *Johnson*) and *Carl Kiekhaefer*'s Mercury Marine, which pioneered lightweight die-cast aluminum alloys.
- Shifted in recent decades from smoky, carbureted two-stroke motors toward direct-injection two-strokes and electronically managed four-stroke engines to comply with stringent marine emissions regulations.

- [[united-states]] — center of commercial outboard innovation and home to the pioneering industrial machine shops of the American Upper Midwest.
