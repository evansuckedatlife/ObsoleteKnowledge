## summary

**Kanji** are the adopted logographic Chinese characters that constitute one of the three primary scripts employed in modern Japanese writing, alongside the native phonographic syllabaries *hiragana* and *katakana*. First arriving in the Japanese archipelago via imported diplomatic gifts, coins, and Buddhist sutras during the first millennium, they were gradually adapted to transcribe a native spoken language with completely different grammatical and phonetic structures. Today, these complex characters remain fundamental to literacy, literature, administrative record-keeping, and visual arts across *Japan*.

## you gotta know

- Employs two primary systems of pronunciation for individual characters: *on'yomi*, which represents Sino-Japanese readings derived from historical Chinese phonology, and *kun'yomi*, which represents native Japanese words assigned to characters based on their semantic meaning.
- Regulated in education and official publishing by the Japanese Ministry of Education through the *jōyō kanji* list, a baseline curriculum specifying 2,136 characters required for regular literacy in secondary schooling and public media.
- Classified traditionally through the *Rikushsho*, or Six Principles of character formation, which divides characters into pictographs, simple ideographs, compound ideographs, phonetic loan graphs, derivative characters, and ubiquitous phono-semantic compounds.
- Supplemented for legal documentation and birth registries by the *jinmeiyō kanji*, an additional official schedule of characters approved specifically for personal naming.
- Organized in dictionaries and index tables primarily by a standard set of 214 traditional semantic classifiers known as *radicals* or *bushu*, categorized further by stroke count and stroke order (*hitsujun*).
- Underwent significant postwar script reform in 1946 that simplified traditional, complex glyphs (*kyūjitai*) into modernized forms known as *shinjitai*, paralleling similar twentieth-century orthographic simplifications across East Asia.
- Works in tandem with *okurigana*, phonographic *hiragana* suffixes written alongside character roots to inflect Japanese verbs and adjectives for tense, mood, and politeness.

## connections

- [[japan]] — represents the island nation where these continental characters were adopted, transformed, and codified into an integral national writing system.
- [[linguistics]] — provides the theoretical framework for analyzing logographic writing systems, phonological adaptation, morphemic representation, and cross-linguistic borrowing.
- [[shogatsu]] — celebrates the traditional Japanese New Year festival, where decorative scrolls featuring auspicious characters are prominently inscribed and displayed.
- [[chinese-new-year]] — shares the historical continental cultural and epigraphic heritage that originally produced the ideographic characters and annual seasonal greetings.
- [[art-nouveau]] — drew immense aesthetic inspiration from Japanese woodblock prints and dynamic calligraphic stroke aesthetics during the late nineteenth century.
- [[modernism]] — incorporated concepts of ideogrammatic economy and non-alphabetic typography into early twentieth-century poetic and visual avant-garde movements.
