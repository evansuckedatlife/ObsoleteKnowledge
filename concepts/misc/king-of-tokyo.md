## summary

**King of Tokyo** is a competitive tabletop board game designed by Richard Garfield and published in 2011 by the French publisher IELLO. Players assume the roles of towering mutant monsters, destructive mechas, and extraterrestrial invaders fighting for territorial dominance over Tokyo, *Japan* [[japan]]. Combining accessible push-your-luck dice-rolling with card-drafting and player elimination, it stands as one of the most commercially successful modern gateway board games.

## you gotta know

- Created by American game designer Richard Garfield, who is best known in board gaming history as the creator of *Magic: The Gathering*.
- Centers on a push-your-luck mechanic derived from Yahtzee, where players roll six large black engraved dice up to three times per turn, keeping desired results while re-rolling the rest.
- Features custom dice faces displaying claws for attacking rivals, hearts for healing lost health, lightning bolts for generating energy cubes, and numbers for scoring victory points.
- Features a tight "king-of-the-hill" spatial board layout representing Tokyo City and Tokyo Bay, where occupying the city enables attacks against all players outside, but renders the occupant vulnerable to counterattacks while barring self-healing.
- Offers two independent victory conditions: achieving an economic win by accumulating 20 victory points through survival and dice sets, or achieving a military win by eliminating all other monster rivals to zero health.
- Employs an evolving marketplace of purchasable power cards funded by green energy cubes, introducing tactical special abilities, extra heads, armor plating, and surprise attacks.
- Pays direct homage to classic Japanese *kaiju* pop culture and cinematic tropes, featuring stylized monster archetypes such as Gigazaur (*Godzilla*), The King (*King Kong*), and Cyber Bunny (*mecha*).
- Expanded by Garfield in 2014 with the standalone sequel *King of New York*, which shifted the monster brawl across the five boroughs of *New York City* [[new-york-city]] with destructible infantry and building tiles.

- [[perception]] — psychological factor governing bluffing, risk assessment, and table talk during dice re-rolls.
