## summary

**Twister** is an iconic American parlor game produced by the *Milton Bradley Company*, played on a large white vinyl floor mat adorned with four rows of colored circles. Invented in 1964 by *Chuck Foley* and *Neil Rabens* under the direction of design consultant *Reyn Guyer*, it broke radical ground in tabletop entertainment by transforming the players themselves into the physical playing pieces. The game achieved sensational nationwide fame in 1966 after a live broadcast demonstration on *The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson*, surviving early puritanical accusations of being "sex in a box" to become an enduring fixture of post-war American popular culture.

## you gotta know

- Players interact with a large vinyl mat containing twenty-four colored circles—six each of red, blue, yellow, and green—guided by an external cardboard spinner.
- The game's spinner board is divided into four distinct quadrants corresponding to human limbs: Left Foot, Right Foot, Left Hand, and Right Hand, each containing sectors for all four mat colors.
- Originally conceived by *Reyn Guyer* while designing an in-store shoe polish promotion, the prototype was initially developed under the working title *Pretzel* before being purchased and rebranded by *Milton Bradley*.
- The game faced widespread skepticism and moral panic upon its 1966 debut, as leading competitors and retailers initially refused to stock it, deeming competitive physical tangling "sex in a box."
- Sales exploded nationwide following the May 3, 1966 episode of *The Tonight Show*, in which host *Johnny Carson* played the game on air with Hungarian-American actress *Eva Gabor*.
- The game shares its evocative moniker with the colloquial term for a violent atmospheric vortex or tornado, a thematic connection later mirrored in popular media including the 1996 disaster film *Twister*.
- The game was inducted into the *National Toy Hall of Fame* in Rochester, New York, in 2015 alongside the water gun and the puppet.
