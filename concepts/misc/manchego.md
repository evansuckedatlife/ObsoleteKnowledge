## summary

**Manchego** is a celebrated Spanish cheese crafted exclusively from the whole milk of Manchega sheep in the arid high-plateau region of *La Mancha*. Recognized across the world by its distinctive herringbone-patterned rind and firm, ivory-to-pale-yellow paste, it holds strict Protected Designation of Origin status within the European Union. Its complex, savory flavor evolves from buttery and mildly herbal when young into deeply piquant, crumbly, and nutty as it ages.

## you gotta know

- Produced exclusively in the historical *La Mancha* region of [[spain]], covering designated provinces including *Albacete*, *Ciudad Real*, *Cuenca*, and *Toledo*.
- Made solely from the unpasteurized or pasteurized milk of registered *Manchega* sheep (*Ovis aries ligeriensis*), a hardy breed adapted to the dry Iberian Meseta.
- Bears an inedible rind etched with a distinctive zigzag or herringbone pattern, historically left by woven esparto grass cheese-press mats (*pleitas*).
- Classified into three standard aging tiers: *semicurado* (aged between one and three months), *curado* (aged from three to six months), and *viejo* (aged up to one or two years).
- Developed a dense interior texture with small, unevenly dispersed eyes, developing crystalline pockets of calcium lactate in long-aged wheels.
- Often traditionally served as tapas alongside Spanish quince paste (*membrillo*), Marcona almonds, or cured Ibérico ham.
- Mentioned historically in classical Spanish literature, notably associated with the windmills and pastures traversed in *Miguel de Cervantes*'s [[renaissance]] masterpiece *Don Quixote*.
