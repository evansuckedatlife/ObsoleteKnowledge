## summary

**Coriander** (*Coriandrum sativum*), widely known as **cilantro** in the [[united-states]] and **Chinese parsley** across East Asia, is an annual herb in the family Apiaceae native to the Mediterranean and the [[middle-east]]. Both its pungent green leaves and aromatic dried globular seeds have been cultivated and prized across global culinary traditions since the Neolithic era. In sensory science and genetics, it serves as the textbook case study of human olfactory variation, where specific receptor polymorphisms cause its foliage to register as soapy and unpleasant to a distinct minority of the population.

## you gotta know

- A well-documented genetic variant in the olfactory receptor gene *OR6A2* leaves roughly ten to twenty percent of humans perceiving the foliage as tasting of bath soap or metallic dampness, an effect driven by sensitivity to unsaturated aldehydes such as *(E)-2-alkenals*.
- Distinct terminology characterizes the plant in American English, which uses the Spanish loanword **cilantro** strictly for the fresh vegetative leaves and reserves **coriander** for the dried, citrus-scented globular seeds.
- Belongs to the botanical family Apiaceae (the umbellifers), sharing close kinship, pinnate leaf structure, and hollow stems with cumin, caraway, fennel, dill, and hemlock.
- One of the oldest cultivated herbs in human history, with fifteen desiccated mericarps recovered from the Pre-Pottery Neolithic B layer of Nahal Hemar cave in Israel, dating back more than eight thousand years.
- Excavated from the royal tomb of Tutankhamun and documented in ancient Egyptian medical papyri as a digestive aid, cooling carminative, and component of funerary garlands.
- Mentioned twice in the Hebrew Bible, where the falling celestial food manna is described in the books of Exodus and Numbers as resembling white coriander seed.
- Its dried seeds yield essential oils dominated by the terpene alcohol *linalool*, making them an indispensable botanical for flavoring traditional Belgian witbiers, London dry gins, and Indian spice blends such as *garam masala*.
