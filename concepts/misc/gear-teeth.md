## summary

**Gear teeth**, also referred to as **cogs** or the **gear tooth profile**, are the precisely engineered ridges along the rim of a rotating wheel or cylinder designed to engage with matching projections on an adjacent mechanical component. By ensuring constant velocity ratios and minimizing mechanical wear through rolling rather than sliding contact, their design represents a foundational triumph of applied [[geometry]] and kinematics. They are indispensable components of clockwork, mechanical automobiles, industrial machinery, and historical horological instruments.

## you gotta know

- The fundamental law of gearing dictates that the common normal to the tooth profiles at all points of contact must pass through a fixed point along the line connecting the centers of rotation, known as the pitch point.
- Most modern power-transmission gears utilize the *involute* profile, a curve generated by unwinding an imaginary taut string from a base circle, first analyzed mathematically for mechanics by Swiss polymath *Leonhard Euler*.
- *Cycloidal* profiles, generated by rolling a generating circle along an internal or external pitch circle, were championed by French mathematician *Philippe de La Hire* and remain widely used in mechanical clocks and horology due to minimal friction at low tooth counts.
- The earliest known complex geared mechanism is the ancient Greek *Antikythera mechanism*, an astronomical calculator discovered in an Aegean shipwreck dating to circa 100 BCE that featured hand-cut triangular teeth.
- *Interference* occurs when the tip of a larger gear tooth attempts to contact the non-involute flank of a smaller mating gear, requiring *undercutting* of the root during manufacturing if the tooth count falls below a critical threshold (commonly seventeen teeth for a standard twenty-degree pressure angle).
- Key geometric parameters governing gear design include the *addendum* (height above the pitch circle), *dedendum* (depth below the pitch circle), *backlash* (clearance between mating teeth to prevent binding), and *module* (ratio of pitch diameter to tooth count in metric standards).
- Helical, bevel, and hypoid gears modify tooth geometry along curved or angled trajectories to achieve smoother tooth engagement, higher load capacities, and non-parallel axis transmission in automotive differentials.

## connections

- [[geometry]] — mathematical branch that provides the involute, cycloid, and epicycloid equations necessary for smooth conjugate action.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era that replaced wooden mill cogs with standardized, interchangeable machine-cut iron and steel gears.
- [[ancient-greece]] — civilization that produced the bronze triangular-toothed gear trains of the Antikythera mechanism.
- [[renaissance]] — era when engineers such as Leonardo da Vinci sketched advanced worm gears, crown wheels, and kinematic gear transmissions.
- [[united-kingdom]] — cradle of mechanized tool cutting where innovators like Sir Joseph Whitworth standardized machine components and gear tolerances.
- [[france]] — home of Philippe de La Hire, whose 1694 treatise laid the scientific foundations of cycloidal tooth kinematics.
