## summary

The **Gilded Age** was the period of rapid industrialization and economic growth in the United States, roughly from the 1870s through the 1900s, marked by the accumulation of vast wealth alongside stark inequality and labor exploitation. Named after Mark Twain and Charles Dudley Warner's 1873 satirical novel, the era saw the rise of industrial magnates and monopolies that displayed their fortunes through ostentatious architecture and consumption. Though glittering on the surface, the age concealed the harsh realities faced by industrial workers and immigrant populations.

## you gotta know

- Named by Mark Twain's satirical novel *The Gilded Age*, which critiqued the era's corruption and greed lurking beneath superficial splendor.
- Characterized by robber barons—industrialists like Carnegie, Rockefeller, and J. P. Morgan who accumulated unprecedented wealth through railroads, steel, and oil.
- Witnessed the rise of massive monopolies that controlled entire industries, prompting anti-trust legislation and regulatory reform.
- Coincided with massive immigration waves and brutal working conditions in factories, mills, and mines that fueled industrial expansion.
- Displayed opulence through architectural marvels like palatial mansions, grand hotels, and monumental public buildings celebrating industrial achievement.
- Provoked the Progressive Era reform movement, which sought to curb corporate power, improve labor conditions, and regulate industry through antitrust laws and social reform.
- Represented in grand World's Fairs—the 1893 Chicago Columbian Exposition and 1904 St. Louis Fair—as monuments to American industrial and technological prowess.
