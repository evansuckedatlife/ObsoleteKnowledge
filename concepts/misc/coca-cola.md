## summary

**Coca-Cola**, popularly known as **Coke**, is a carbonated soft drink formulated in 1886 by pharmacist *John Stith Pemberton* in Atlanta, Georgia. Originally patented as a patent medicine to relieve headache pain and morphine dependency, it grew into an ubiquitous symbol of global commercial capitalism and 20th-century consumer culture. It is a cornerstone quiz-bowl topic for its intricate corporate history, legendary marketing campaigns, and wartime logistical feats.

## you gotta know

- Formulated in May 1886 by *John Stith Pemberton*, an *American Civil War* veteran wounded at Columbus who sought a cure for his morphine addiction, initially creating *Pemberton's French Wine Coca* based on the European tonic *Vin Mariani*.
- Adapted into a non-alcoholic fountain syrup when Atlanta and Fulton County passed temperance legislation in 1886, blending coca leaf extract and caffeine-rich African kola nuts at *Jacob's Pharmacy*.
- Named by Pemberton's bookkeeper and partner *Frank Mason Robinson*, who suggested the alliterative pairing and rendered the distinctive flowing Spencerian script trademark still featured on every bottle.
- Acquired in the late 1880s by Atlanta entrepreneur *Asa Griggs Candler*, who consolidated ownership, founded *The Coca-Cola Company* in 1892, and introduced innovative marketing practices including paper coupons for free drinks and branded clocks.
- Distributed nationwide through an innovative franchised bottling system established in 1899 when Chattanooga lawyers *Benjamin Thomas* and *Joseph Whitehead* secured exclusive bottling rights across the [[united-states]] for just one dollar.
- Packaged in the iconic 1915 contoured "hobbleskirt" glass bottle created by the *Root Glass Company* of Indiana, whose design team mistakenly modeled the vessel's ridges on a cacao pod rather than a coca leaf, confusing two names that are frequent false [[cognate]] pairs.
- Expanded internationally under longtime president *Robert Woodruff*, who pledged during [[world-war-ii]] that American soldiers would receive a bottle for five cents anywhere, leading directly to the establishment of overseas plants and the creation of *Fanta* in [[germany]].
- Replaced temporarily in April 1985 by *New Coke*, an infamous reformulated sweeter beverage whose public backlash forced the company to restore the original recipe as *Coca-Cola Classic* only seventy-nine days later.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — home country where the soft drink became an indelible emblem of industrial mass production and popular culture.
- [[american-civil-war]] — conflict in which founder *John Stith Pemberton* suffered saber and gunshot wounds, sparking the chronic pain that drove his pharmaceutical experiments.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of mechanized manufacturing and rapid urban growth that enabled the creation of mass bottling networks and national soda fountain distribution.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict during which the company established dozens of mobile military bottling plants, cementing international brand loyalty.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical standoff during which the soda served as an ideological symbol of Western market capitalism, famously prompting Soviet Marshal *Georgy Zhukov* to request a secret uncolored version dubbed *White Coke*.
- [[germany]] — European market cut off from American syrup imports during wartime, prompting local manager *Max Keith* to formulate *Fanta* from cheese whey and apple pomace.
- [[cognate]] — linguistic concept relevant to the botanical confusion of *coca* and *cacao*, whose superficial phonetic resemblance led bottle designers to copy cacao pod contours.
