## summary

**City planning** is the technical, political, and spatial discipline devoted to guiding the design, land use, infrastructure, and socioeconomic growth of urban environments. Tracing back to ancient orthogonal grids in *[[classical-antiquity]]*, it evolved into a formal modern profession in response to the squalor, congestion, and epidemics unleashed by the *[[industrial-revolution]]*. Through regulatory zoning, public open spaces, and comprehensive transportation networks, planning shapes the physical form and livability of human settlements.

## you gotta know

- Early historical precedents date to ancient civilizations, including the orthogonal street grids of Mohenjo-daro, the Hippodamian grid plan of *[[ancient-greece]]*, and the standard castra military layouts of the *[[roman-empire]]*.
- Radical nineteenth-century modernization of Paris under *Georges-Eugène Haussmann* and Napoleon III demolished medieval quarters in *[[france]]* to create wide boulevards, public parks, and uniform sewer systems.
- Catalyst for the American City Beautiful movement led by *[[daniel-burnham]]*, who helped craft the monumental 1893 World's Columbian Exposition and the influential 1909 Plan of Chicago.
- Responded to urban industrial blight through *Ebenezer Howard's* Garden City model in *[[england]]*, which advocated self-contained planned communities ringed by greenbelts.
- Institutionalized in the *[[united-states]]* through zoning laws, landmarked by the 1916 New York Zoning Resolution and the 1926 Supreme Court case *Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty Co.*
- Transformed in the mid-twentieth century by *[[modernism]]* and car-centric designs, exemplified by *Le Corbusier's* Radiant City concept and *Robert Moses* in *[[new-york-city]]*.
- Challenged fundamentally by theorist *Jane Jacobs* in her 1961 work *The Death and Life of Great American Cities*, which promoted mixed-use neighborhoods, short blocks, and pedestrian-oriented street vitality.

## connections

- [[architecture]] — the parent design discipline focused on individual buildings and aesthetic structures within planned urban fabrics.
- [[daniel-burnham]] — leading architect and planner who championed the City Beautiful movement and authored the Plan of Chicago.
- [[classical-antiquity]] — source of early grid planning doctrines, monumental public forums, and municipal civil engineering.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of rapid urbanization and factory pollution that made systematic urban hygiene and planning urgent.
- [[new-york-city]] — major urban laboratory that pioneered early skyscraper zoning laws, park systems, and highway networks.
- [[modernism]] — architectural and social movement that advocated high-rise towers, functional zoning, and separation of vehicular traffic.
- [[france]] — nation where Haussmann's dramatic Parisian renovation set international standards for modern urban redesign.
