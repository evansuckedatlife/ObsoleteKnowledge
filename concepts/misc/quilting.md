## summary

**Quilting** is a textile craft and decorative needlework tradition defined by stitching together three layers: a fabric top, an insulating layer of batting, and a fabric backing. Practiced globally across centuries for thermal protection and domestic utility, it evolved into an expressive folk and fine art movement, most notably within regional communities of the *[[united-states]]*. In quiz bowl, questions frequently test historic communal sewing bees, the symbolic and geometric patterns of American patchwork, and celebrated artistic enclaves such as the African American quilters of *Gee's Bend*.

## you gotta know

- Traditional construction unites three distinct structural layers—top, filler fleece or batting, and backing—fastened with decorative running stitches either by hand or sewing machine.
- Historic communal gatherings known as quilting bees provided vital social, domestic, and political organizing spaces for women throughout 19th-century rural America.
- Celebrated African American craft traditions, especially the isolated community of *Gee's Bend* (*Boykin, Alabama*), produced improvisational, abstract textile works featuring bold *[[geometry]]* that later entered major modern art museums.
- Popular folkloric theories link specific patchwork patterns—such as the *Log Cabin*, *Wagon Wheel*, and *Flying Geese*—to secret directional signaling along the Underground Railroad prior to the *[[american-civil-war]]*, though historians debate the extent of documented evidence.
- The *NAMES Project AIDS Memorial Quilt*, founded in 1987 by activist *Cleve Jones*, became a monumental piece of participatory public art commemorating lives lost to the epidemic and is recognized as the largest ongoing community arts project in history.
- The *Amish* communities of Pennsylvania and the Midwest established a distinct minimalist school characterized by saturated, solid jewel-toned fabrics, unprinted surfaces, and intricate concentric diamond or square stitching.
- The *[[industrial-revolution]]* radically transformed the practice through the commercial invention of mechanical sewing machines, affordable mass-produced calico cottons, and synthetic dyes.

## connections

- [[african-diaspora]] — cultural heritage preserved in improvisational textile traditions like the celebrated quilts of Gee's Bend, Alabama.
- [[american-civil-war]] — era associated with wartime fundraising quilts, relief supplies for soldiers, and Underground Railroad signaling folklore.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — historical backdrop during which rural cooperative sewing initiatives gained economic independence and artistic prominence.
- [[geometry]] — structural foundation for traditional quilt blocks utilizing repeating squares, triangles, hexagons, and symmetrical tessellations.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era that introduced mechanized loom textiles, mass-market thread, and domestic sewing machines.
- [[modernism]] — 20th-century aesthetic movement whose critics and curators drew direct parallels to the asymmetric visual rhythms of American patchwork.
- [[united-states]] — country where regional variations, pioneer quilting bees, and national memorials established major folk art forms.
