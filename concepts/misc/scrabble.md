## summary

**Scrabble** is a globally renowned word game for two to four players, designed during the Great Depression by American architect *Alfred Mosher Butts*. Players score points by forming interlocking, crossword-style words on a fifteen-by-fifteen grid using individual wooden or plastic letter tiles of varying point values. Combining statistical letter frequency with tactical spatial control, it has evolved from an informal domestic parlor amusement into a rigorous international competitive discipline.

## you gotta know

- Invented in 1938 in [[new-york-city]] by out-of-work architect *Alfred Mosher Butts*, who systematically calculated letter point values by analyzing the front page of *The New York Times*.
- Originally named *Lexiko* and later *Criss-Cross Words*, the game was refined with a specialized board and trademarked under its iconic title in 1948 by entrepreneur *James Brunot*.
- Played on a square grid consisting of two hundred and twenty-five cells, strategically featuring premium squares including double and triple word scores as well as double and triple letter scores.
- Standard English sets contain exactly one hundred tiles, featuring high-frequency letters like *E* and *A* worth one point, contrasted with rare letters like *Q* and *Z* worth ten points.
- Awarding a fifty-point bonus known as a *bingo* when a player successfully uses all seven rack tiles in a single turn, which remains the cornerstone of advanced competitive play.
- Governed internationally by official competitive lexicons, such as the *Official Tournament and Club Word List* and *Collins Scrabble Catchphrases and Words*, which standardize accepted anagrams and obscure stems.
- Popularized in mid-century mainstream retail after *Jack Straus*, president of *Macy's*, played it on vacation and subsequently ordered massive commercial stock for his flagship Manhattan store.
