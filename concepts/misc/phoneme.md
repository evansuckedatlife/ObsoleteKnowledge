## summary

A **phoneme** is the smallest abstract unit of sound in a language capable of distinguishing one word or morpheme from another. First conceptualized in the late nineteenth century by scholars such as *Jan Baudouin de Courtenay* and systematically developed by the *Prague School*, it serves as the cornerstone of modern structural phonology. Rather than representing an exact acoustic sound wave, it constitutes a cognitive category encompassing several phonetic variants known as allophones.

## you gotta know

- Contrastive function is demonstrated through minimal pairs, such as the English words "pat" and "bat", which prove that /p/ and /b/ are distinct units.
- Distinct from a phone, which is any concrete, physical speech sound produced; a phoneme is the psychological or structural abstraction behind those sounds.
- Conditioned variants of a single sound category that do not alter meaning are called allophones, such as the aspirated [pʰ] in "pin" versus the unaspirated [p] in "spin".
- Conventionally transcribed between slashes (e.g., /t/), contrasting with narrow phonetic transcriptions written within square brackets (e.g., [tʰ]).
- Developed by the structuralist linguistics movement, notably through *Nikolai Trubetzkoy* and *Roman Jakobson* in the *Prague Linguistic Circle*.
- Formulated as bundles of binary distinctive features—such as [±voice], [±nasal], or [±continuant]—by generative linguists like *Noam Chomsky* and *Morris Halle*.
- The inventory of sound categories varies radically across human languages, ranging from barely a dozen in *Rotokas* and *Pirahã* to over one hundred in certain *!Xóõ* (Khoisan) dialects.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — discipline for which phonemic analysis provides the baseline framework for studying sound systems and grammars.
- [[perception]] — cognitive mechanism by which listeners categorize continuous auditory signals into discrete categorical sound representations.
- [[five-senses]] — physiological auditory faculty that receives acoustic input before higher cognitive processing decodes it into language.
- [[neurology]] — branch of medicine and neuroscience mapping the cerebral processing of speech sounds in *Wernicke's area* and *Broca's area*.
- [[ancient-greece]] — origin of the root noun *phōnēma*, meaning "sound made" or "utterance", derived from *phōneō* ("to speak").
