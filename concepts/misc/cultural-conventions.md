## summary

**Cultural conventions** are the unwritten rules, shared expectations, communicative protocols, and social rituals that regulate behavior and artistic expression within a given community. Ranging from everyday etiquette, kinship obligations, and diplomatic decorum to established genre rules in theater, literature, and visual arts, these tacit agreements maintain collective cohesion and mutual legibility. Over time, conventions undergo intense contestation, rupture, and reformulation when societies encounter new philosophical currents or the disruptive pressures of [[creolization]].

## you gotta know

- Regulate interpersonal behavior, bodily etiquette, modes of address, gift-giving practices, and linguistic registers across distinct human societies.
- Governed ancient civic and dramatic life in [[ancient-greece]], dictating everything from strict codes of hospitality (*xenia*) to the formal structures of tragic poetry and the tragic chorus.
- Codified into legalism, familial hierarchy (*patria potestas*), and public ceremonial prestige across the civic sphere of the [[roman-empire]].
- Governed artistic representation through established dramatic formulas, such as soliloquies, asides, and comic resolutions utilized by [[william-shakespeare]].
- Analyzed critically by sociologists and philosophers from the [[enlightenment]] onward, examining how arbitrary social habits construct perceived natural laws.
- Challenged systematically in literature and painting by movements like [[realism]] and modernism, which sought to strip away romanticized artificiality.
- Transformed profoundly in pluralistic contact zones, where intercultural contact, migration, and imperial expansion destabilize traditional customs and forge hybrid practices.

## connections

- [[creolization]] — the transformative contact process wherein rigid cultural conventions are disassembled, remixed, and rebuilt into dynamic syncretic forms.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization that formalized enduring western literary, philosophical, and social conventions.
- [[roman-empire]] — the Mediterranean empire whose civic rituals, legal traditions, and administrative conventions structured European institutions for centuries.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual era that subjected traditional religious, monarchical, and social conventions to rigorous rational skepticism.
- [[william-shakespeare]] — the English playwright who masterfully manipulated, fulfilled, and subverted early modern theatrical conventions.
- [[realism]] — the artistic movement that rejected melodramatic conventions in order to represent the unvarnished realities of daily life.
- [[colonialism]] — the imperial enterprise that violently imposed European social manners, naming systems, and legal codes upon foreign populations.
