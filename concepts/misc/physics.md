## summary

**Physics** is the fundamental natural science that seeks to understand how matter, energy, and forces interact at all scales—from subatomic particles to galaxies. It is both the most mathematically rigorous and most broadly applicable science, explaining everything from falling apples to nuclear reactions. Modern physics revealed that the universe is far stranger than classical intuition suggests.

## you gotta know

- Classical mechanics ([[isaac-newton]]'s laws of motion and gravitation) governed physics for 300 years and remains practical for everyday scales; it assumes determinism and instantaneous action at a distance.
- Thermodynamics quantifies heat, energy transfer, and entropy through laws: energy is conserved (first law), disorder increases in isolated systems (second law), absolute zero is unreachable (third law).
- *Electromagnetism* (light, magnetism, electricity) was unified as a single phenomenon by [[james-clerk-maxwell]], revealing that light is an electromagnetic wave; this unified theory revolutionized technology and laid groundwork for relativity.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] in the early 20th century revealed that at atomic scales, particles behave probabilistically — observation affects outcome, particles exist in superposition until measured, and action is quantized (energy comes in packets called quanta).
- [[relativity]] ([[albert-einstein]]) showed that space and time are relative and intertwined, that energy and mass are equivalent (E=mc²), that nothing travels faster than light, and that gravity is the curvature of spacetime itself.
- Physics distinguishes between *real forces* ([[gravity]], friction, electromagnetic) and *fictitious forces* ([[centrifugal-force]], Coriolis force) that appear only in rotating or accelerating reference frames.
- Quantum field theory reconciles quantum mechanics with relativity for electromagnetic, weak, and strong nuclear forces, but *gravity* remains stubbornly unquantized; unifying all four forces remains an open problem (string theory, loop quantum gravity are candidates).

## connections

- [[mechanics]] — the subdiscipline studying motion and forces.
- [[thermodynamics]] — energy and heat; explains why time only flows forward.
- [[electromagnetism]] — light and charged particles unified.
- [[quantum-mechanics]] — the rules governing atomic-scale reality.
- [[relativity]] — Einstein's revolution in space, time, and gravity.
- [[isaac-newton]] — founder of classical physics.
- [[symmetry]] — fundamental principle underlying physical laws.
