## summary

**Calamine lotion** is an over-the-counter topical medication composed primarily of zinc oxide mixed with a small concentration of ferric oxide to produce its characteristic pale pink hue. Used worldwide as an antipruritic and mild astringent, the suspension relieves the intense itching and discomfort caused by insect bites, sunburn, measles, chickenpox, and contact dermatitis from plants such as poison ivy. The preparation acts by creating a localized soothing sensation through evaporative cooling while precipitating surface proteins to protect irritated dermal tissue.

## you gotta know

- Formulated chemically from a blend of basic zinc oxide with roughly half a percent of iron(III) oxide (*ferric oxide*), which imparts its recognizable pink color.
- Included on the Model List of Essential Medicines compiled by the World Health Organization as an effective, safe topical antipruritic.
- Treats the severe blistering and pruritus of urushiol-induced contact dermatitis caused by toxic encounters with poison ivy, poison oak, and poison sumac.
- Applied historically as a staple domestic remedy for soothing the itchy vesicular rashes of pediatric varicella infections (*chickenpox*).
- Operates pharmacologically as a mild protective astringent that cools inflamed skin by aqueous evaporation while gently coagulating tissue exudates.
- Historically designated the naturally occurring zinc carbonate mineral smithsonite, which was originally mined under the classical pharmaceutical label **calamine**.
- Frequently combined in modern pharmaceutical suspensions with liquid phenol, pramoxine, or diphenhydramine to add mild local anesthetic or antihistamine properties.
