## summary

**Neurology** is the branch of medicine and biology that studies the structure and function of the nervous system, including the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves. It encompasses how neurons transmit signals, how the brain processes information, and how neural dysfunction causes disease. Modern neurology bridges anatomy, chemistry, and behavior to explain everything from reflexes to consciousness.

## you gotta know

- The nervous system comprises the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and the peripheral nervous system (nerves extending throughout the body).
- Neurons communicate via electrochemical signals: an electrical impulse (action potential) down the axon triggers chemical neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin, acetylcholine) across the synapse to the next neuron.
- The brain's *neuroplasticity* — its ability to rewire and adapt throughout life — explains learning, recovery from injury, memory formation, and skill mastery even in adulthood.
- Different brain regions specialize: the *prefrontal cortex* for reasoning and impulse control, the *cerebellum* for coordination and motor precision, the *limbic system* (amygdala, hippocampus) for emotion and memory.
- The [[human-anatomy|spinal cord]] contains neural circuits for reflexes, allowing rapid responses (like pulling your hand from a flame) without waiting for the brain to process.
- Neurology explains [[sensation]] at the mechanism level: how sensory receptors (photoreceptors in the eye, mechanoreceptors in skin) detect stimuli and relay signals via sensory neurons to the spinal cord and brain.
- [[perception]] — the conscious interpretation of sensory data — emerges from complex neural processing involving multiple brain regions working in concert, not just passive reception.
- Neurological diseases (stroke, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Multiple Sclerosis) reveal how disruptions to specific neural systems cascade into loss of function, speech, movement, or memory.
