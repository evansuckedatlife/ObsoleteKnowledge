## summary

The **Martini** (frequently ordered in its archetypal form as the **Dry Martini**) is an iconic American cocktail traditionally made with dry gin and white vermouth, chilled thoroughly and garnished with either an olive or a lemon twist. Originating in the [[united-states]] during the late nineteenth century, the drink became a defining cultural artifact of twentieth-century cosmopolitan life. Celebrated by writers, statesmen, and filmmakers, it is widely regarded as the ultimate expression of mid-century hospitality and minimalist mixology.

## you gotta know

- Core formula pairs London dry gin with dry French vermouth, chilled through stirring with ice and served up in a chilled, V-shaped stem glass that prevents body heat from warming the liquid.
- Cocktail historians widely trace its lineage to the *Martinez*, an 1880s drink combining Old Tom gin, sweet Italian vermouth, maraschino liqueur, and aromatic bitters, popular in San Francisco and northern California.
- The name was also reinforced by the Italian vermouth manufacturer *Martini & Rossi*, whose commercial advertising across late nineteenth-century North America made vermouth cocktails ubiquitous.
- Over the course of the twentieth century, consumer preference shifted drastically toward an increasingly dry drink, reducing the proportion of vermouth from an equal one-to-one ratio down to a mere rinse or drop.
- Traditional bartending technique mandates stirring rather than shaking spirit-forward cocktails to preserve a clear, silky texture without introducing air bubbles or fractured ice chips, in direct contrast to *James Bond*'s famous preference for "shaken, not stirred."
- Author *Ian Fleming* introduced a specific high-proof variant named the *Vesper* in his 1953 novel *Casino Royale*, combining three measures of gin, one measure of vodka, and half a measure of Kina Lillet.
- Major standard variations include the *Dirty Martini* (incorporating a splash of olive brine), the *Gibson* (garnished with a pickled cocktail onion), and the *Vodka Martini* (substituting vodka for the traditional gin base).

- [[modernism]] — cultural and artistic movement whose clean lines and functional minimalism were reflected in the austere clarity of the drink.
