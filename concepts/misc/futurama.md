## summary

**Futurama** was an influential large-scale model exhibit and ride created by industrial designer *Norman Bel Geddes* for the General Motors pavilion at the 1939 New York World's Fair. Presenting a dramatic vision of the American landscape twenty years into the future, it depicted an automated nation organized around expansive multi-lane highway networks and planned urban centers. The attraction captivated millions of visitors, profoundly shaping post-war suburban development and setting the conceptual blueprint for the American interstate highway system.

## you gotta know

- Designed by industrial designer and theatrical visionary *Norman Bel Geddes* for *General Motors* at the 1939 New York World's Fair.
- Moved seated spectators in conveyor-mounted sound-equipped armchairs over an enormous 35,738-square-foot miniature diorama representing the year 1960.
- Imagined a sprawling network of radio-controlled expressways allowing automated vehicular travel at speeds up to 100 miles per hour.
- Distributed iconic souvenir pins to exiting fairgoers bearing the slogan *"I Have Seen the Future"*.
- Directly influenced subsequent national infrastructure planning, anticipating the *Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1956*.
- Reflected Streamline Moderne aesthetics, functionalist zoning, and the car-centric urban design philosophies championed by [[modernism]].
- Updated for the 1964 New York World's Fair as *Futurama II*, which depicted lunar colonies, undersea resorts, and deep jungle excavation.
