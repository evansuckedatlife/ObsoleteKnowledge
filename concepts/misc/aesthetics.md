## summary

**Aesthetics** is the philosophical and critical study of beauty, taste, and the nature of the artistic experience. It asks fundamental questions: What makes something beautiful? Is beauty objective or subjective? What distinguishes art from non-art? This inquiry has shaped how cultures understand and value visual, musical, and literary expression across centuries.

## you gotta know

- Originates as a formal discipline in 18th-century Europe, though questions about beauty date to [[plato]] and [[aristotle]].
- [[immanuel-kant]] famously argued that aesthetic judgment is disinterested — we appreciate beauty not for personal gain but for its own sake — yet universal in appeal.
- Explores whether beauty is inherent in objects or exists only in the perception of the observer; empiricism and idealism offer competing answers.
- Distinguishes between *utility* (function) and *beauty* (form), asking why we value the decorative alongside the practical.
- The *sublime* — experiences of awe and terror before vast or overwhelming phenomena — became central to Romantic-era aesthetics; it differs from beauty as a mix of fear and wonder.
- Addresses *kitsch* and mass production: what happens to artistic value when art is mass-manufactured and commodified for profit rather than expression?
- Connects intimately to [[symmetry]] and [[proportion]], which humans find intuitively pleasing across cultures; these principles underlie classical architecture and visual harmony.
- The *readymade* movement ([[marcel-duchamp]]) challenged aesthetics itself by framing everyday objects (urinals, bicycle wheels) as art, questioning whether beauty or originality even matters.
- Modern aesthetics grapples with whether non-Western, avant-garde, and popular forms deserve equal standing with "high art," rejecting Eurocentric hierarchies.

## connections

- [[symmetry]] — the mathematical and perceptual basis of much visual beauty.
- [[visual-art]] — the primary domain where aesthetic principles are tested and felt.
- [[philosophy]] — the broader discipline housing aesthetics as a subfield.
- [[plato]] — early thinker on beauty as an eternal Form.
- [[kantian-ethics]] — [[immanuel-kant]]'s aesthetics emphasizing disinterested judgment.
- [[romanticism]] — a movement defining beauty through emotion and the sublime.
