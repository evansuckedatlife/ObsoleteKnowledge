## summary

**Balderdash** is a classic bluffing parlor board game created by *Laura Robinson* and *Paul Toyne*, originally released in 1984. Based on the traditional public-domain game *Fictionary* (or *The Dictionary Game*), it challenges players to fabricate plausible-sounding definitions for obscure English words to deceive their opponents. Its blend of linguistic inventiveness, psychological deceit, and trivia has made it an enduring worldwide favorite in party gaming.

## you gotta know

- Invented in Canada by actress *Laura Robinson* and *Paul Toyne* in 1984, before becoming a commercial hit licensed internationally by games giant *Milton Bradley* and later *Mattel*.
- Derived directly from the traditional parlor game *Fictionary*, where players write fictional definitions for bizarre real dictionary entries.
- In each round, the designated "dasher" reads an obscure headword aloud, after which each player writes down a bluff definition while the dasher copies the actual definition.
- Points are earned both by successfully guessing the genuine definition and by duping fellow players into voting for one's own fabricated answer.
- Expanded in later editions—such as *Beyond Balderdash* and *Absolute Balderdash*—to include categories like obscure movie titles, weird laws, unfamiliar initials, and strange dates.
- Highlights unusual curiosities in historical *[[linguistics]]*, archaic jargon, and bizarre lexical oddities of the English language.
- Adapted into short-lived television game shows in both the *[[united-states]]* and the *[[united-kingdom]]*.
