## summary

The **Greek alphabet** is a twenty-four letter writing system developed in classical antiquity that has been used continuously to write the Greek language since the late ninth or early eighth century BCE. Recognized by epigraphers and philologists as the first true phonemic alphabet to provide explicit, dedicated letters for both vowels and consonants, it derives from the earlier *Phoenician* abjad. Beyond its linguistic vitality, the script serves as the direct ancestor of the Latin and Cyrillic scripts and provides foundational nomenclature across modern mathematics, theoretical physics, astronomy, and fraternity culture.

## you gotta know

- Emerged when Hellenic scribes adapted the *Phoenician* consonantal alphabet, innovatively reassigning Phoenician letters representing Semitic guttural consonants that had no Greek equivalent—such as *aleph*, *he*, *ayin*, and *yodh*—to represent the vowels *alpha*, *epsilon*, *omicron*, and *iota*.
- Spans twenty-four standard canonical letters in the *Ionic* alphabet adopted by *Athens* in 403 BCE under the archonship of *Eucleides*, opening with *alpha* ($\alpha$) and culminating in *omega* ($\omega$).
- Archaic variants preserved obsolete letters such as *digamma* (representing the voiced labial-velar approximant /w/), *qoppa*, *san*, and *sampi*, which were later retained in the classical numeral system as alphabetic numbers.
- Early archaic inscriptions were frequently inscribed in *boustrophedon* style—meaning "as the ox turns" while plowing—alternating reading directions from right-to-left and left-to-right across successive lines before settling on the standard left-to-right direction.
- Gave rise through its Western Euboean variant to the *Cumae* alphabet in southern *Magna Graecia*, which the *Etruscans* adapted and passed along to form the *Latin alphabet*.
- The *Septuagint* and the *New Testament* were recorded in *Koine* Greek using this script, ensuring its canonical place in Biblical scholarship, patristic theology, and early manuscript paleography.
- Pervades modern science and quantitative analysis, denoting angles and coefficients (*theta*, *phi*), infinite cardinalities, Dirac delta functions, summation signs (*sigma*), and stellar luminosity designations in the *Bayer designation* catalog.

## connections

- [[ancient-greece]] — the Mediterranean civilization that invented, standardized, and diffused the script across its city-states and colonies.
- [[linguistics]] — academic discipline studying phonemic evolution, orthographic borrowing, and morphological structures encoded by alphabetic systems.
- [[roman-empire]] — imperial state that adopted the Western Greek-derived Latin alphabet, spreading alphabetic literacy throughout Western Europe.
- [[geometry]] — mathematical branch founded by figures like *Euclid* that relies upon Greek letterings to label vertices, angles, and proofs.
- [[astronomy]] — natural science utilizing Greek letters in the *Bayer designation* system to rank the apparent brightness of stars in constellations.
- [[tragedy]] — dramatic genre flourishing in classical *Athens* whose play scripts by *Aeschylus*, *Sophocles*, and *Euripides* were recorded in the script.
- [[christianity]] — world religion whose foundational scriptures were codified in *Koine* Greek script and theological slogans like *Alpha and Omega*.
