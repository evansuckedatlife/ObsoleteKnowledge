## summary

**Béchamel** is a classical white sauce made by whisking heated milk into a cooked white roux of butter and all-purpose flour. Originating in western European court cookery, it was standardized by French culinary master *Auguste Escoffier* as one of the fundamental five "mother sauces" of classical French cuisine. Renowned for its silky texture, gentle seasoning of nutmeg, and culinary versatility, it serves as the foundational base for countless secondary preparations across global gastronomy.

## you gotta know

- Formally categorized by chef *Auguste Escoffier* in *Le Guide Culinaire* as one of the primary mother sauces alongside velouté, espagnole, hollandaise, and sauce tomate.
- Named in honor of the French financier and courtier *Louis de Béchameil*, Marquis de Nointel, who served as chief steward to King Louis XIV of *[[france]]*.
- Derived daughter sauces created by enriching béchamel include Mornay (incorporating Gruyère and Parmesan cheeses), Soubise (pureed cooked onions), and Nantua (crayfish butter and cream).
- An alternate Italian culinary legend attributes its origin to *Catherine de' Medici*, asserting that Italian pastry chefs carried *salsa besciamella* to Paris in the sixteenth century during the *[[renaissance]]*.
- First documented in French literature under its modern designation in the seminal 1651 cookbook *Le Cuisinier François* by *François Pierre de la Varenne*.
- Essential structural building block for celebrated baked pasta and vegetable dishes, most notably Italian *lasagne alla bolognese*, Greek *moussaka*, and French *croque monsieur*.
