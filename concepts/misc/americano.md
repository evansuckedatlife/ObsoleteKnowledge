## summary

A **Caffè Americano** (commonly shortened simply to an **Americano**) is a classic hot espresso beverage prepared by diluting freshly pulled espresso shots with hot water, producing a brew of similar strength to drip coffee while retaining espresso's aromatic complexity. Widely believed to have originated in [[italy]] during [[world-war-ii]], the beverage was concocted to accommodate the palate of American military personnel accustomed to lighter filter-brewed coffee. It remains a ubiquitous staple in modern international café culture, bridging traditional Southern European extraction methods with twentieth-century global coffee drinking habits.

## you gotta know

- Traditionally attributed to [[world-war-ii]] American soldiers stationed in [[italy]] who found concentrated Italian espresso excessively bitter and intense, prompting local baristas to add hot water.
- Distinct from the Australian and New Zealander *long black*, which pulls espresso directly over hot water to preserve a thick layer of golden *crema*, whereas an Americano often pours hot water into espresso.
- Differs from a *lungo*, where extra water is forced entirely through the compressed coffee grounds inside the portafilter during an extended extraction cycle, yielding higher bitterness.
- Differs from a *red eye*, *black eye*, or *dead eye*, which fortify standard drip-brewed coffee with one, two, or three shots of espresso rather than using hot water alone.
- Served in large ceramic cups or tumblers, typically prepared using a standard ratio of one part espresso to two or three parts hot water, tailored to customer preference.
- Commonly adapted into an *iced Americano* by combining freshly extracted espresso with chilled water and ice cubes, a staple item popularized in contemporary East Asian and American café chains.
- Preserves volatile aromatics and subtle acidity from espresso beans while reducing the heavy body and viscosity inherent to undiluted single or double shots.

## connections

- [[italy]] — nation where the beverage was first crafted in local cafés to cater to foreign Allied soldiers seeking familiar tastes.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict whose Allied Mediterranean campaign brought American service members into prolonged contact with Italian café culture.
- [[united-states]] — country whose domestic soldiers and widespread preference for mild drip coffee led directly to the beverage's invention and naming.
- [[rome]] — major liberated Italian cultural hub where Allied troops frequented espresso bars in the months following liberation in 1944.
- [[angel-food-cake]] — companion culinary creation demonstrating the historical American tendency to adapt European recipes toward lighter, milder, and aerated forms.
