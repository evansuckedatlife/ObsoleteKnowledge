## summary

**Spanakopita** is a traditional Greek savory pastry made of flaky phyllo dough layers filled with chopped spinach, feta cheese, onions or scallions, eggs, and herbs such as dill. Originating in Greece, this classic dish has become an internationally celebrated emblem of Hellenic and Mediterranean culinary tradition. It is commonly served across Greek households and bakeries either as a large casserole sliced into squares or rolled into individual triangular hand pies.

## you gotta know

- Prepared using paper-thin sheets of *phyllo* pastry, which are brushed with melted butter or olive oil to achieve a crisp, laminated texture upon baking.
- Features a signature filling dominated by chopped spinach combined with brined *feta* cheese, curd cheese such as *myzithra*, beaten eggs as a binder, and fresh dill.
- Traceable to centuries of culinary interaction across the Balkans and the Levant, sharing structural lineage with regional layered pastries like Turkish *börek*.
- Prepared in a fasting or Lenten variation known as *nistisimi*, which omits dairy cheese and eggs, relying strictly on greens, onions, and olive oil.
- Formed either into large sheet-pan trays (*tapsi*) sliced into rectangular portions or folded into individual triangular envelopes known as *spanakopitakia*.
- Widely referenced in popular diaspora culture, appearing frequently in Mediterranean festivals and global casual dining.
