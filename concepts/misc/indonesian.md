## summary

**Indonesian**, or **Bahasa Indonesia**, is an Austronesian language that serves as the official and national language of the Republic of Indonesia. Standardized from a literary prestige dialect of Classical Malay, it served for centuries as the maritime trading lingua franca of the Malay Archipelago. Established as a symbol of anti-colonial unity in 1928, it functions as one of the most successful examples of modern language engineering in the post-colonial world.

## you gotta know

- Formally proclaimed as the unifying national language during the second Indonesian Youth Congress on October 28, 1928, via the *Sumpah Pemuda* (Youth Pledge).
- Standardized from the Riau-Johor variety of *Malay*, an Austronesian tongue chosen deliberately over the numerically dominant *Javanese* to maintain ethnic neutrality and promote egalitarian civic identity.
- Displays rich linguistic stratification featuring extensive loanwords from Sanskrit, Arabic, Persian, Portuguese, Dutch, Hokkien Chinese, and English, tracing centuries of archipelago trade.
- Functions as an agglutinative language relying on extensive prefixation, suffixation, and circumfixation (such as *ke-...-an* and *pe-...-an*) attached to base root words.
- Features complete absence of grammatical gender, grammatical tense, and noun inflection, utilizing reduplication (e.g., *anak-anak* for "children") to express plurality, variety, or habituality.
- Adopted the Latin alphabet (*Tulisan Rumi*) under colonial administration, replacing the historic Arabic-derived *Jawi* script.
- Standardized spelling mutually with Malaysia in 1972 through the *Ejaan Yang Disempurnakan* (Perfected Spelling System).

## connections

- [[indo-european-language-family]] — deeply influenced through vocabulary borrowed from Indo-European languages including Sanskrit, Dutch, Portuguese, and English.
- [[colonialism]] — developed in opposition to Dutch colonial linguistic hegemony in the Dutch East Indies.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — served as the maritime commercial lingua franca encountered by European spice traders in the Moluccas.
- [[middle-east]] — absorbed extensive religious, legal, and scholarly vocabulary from classical Arabic following the spread of Islam.
- [[world-war-ii]] — promoted rapidly when the occupying Japanese military administration banned the use of Dutch in civil administration and education.
- [[cold-war]] — utilized as the primary vehicle of state consolidation, public education, and political rhetoric under *Sukarno* and *Suharto*.
- [[linguistics]] — studied extensively as a prime sociolinguistic example of planned language engineering and bilingual diglossia.
