## summary

The **Arabic script** is a cursive writing system that originated in the fourth century CE from the *Nabataean* alphabet in the Arabian Peninsula. Primarily structured as an abjad, it is written continuously from right to left, with the visual form of most individual letters changing depending on their position within a word. As the sacred medium used to record the *Quran*, the script expanded across Africa, the Middle East, and Central and South Asia, becoming adapted for numerous non-Semitic languages including Persian, Urdu, Pashto, and Ottoman Turkish.

## you gotta know

- Operates primarily as an abjad consisting of twenty-eight consonant letters, with short vowels marked through optional diacritical signs known as *tashkil* or *harakat*.
- Written entirely from right to left in an inherently cursive style, meaning that letters within a word generally connect with adjacent letters in both handwriting and typography.
- Employs contextual letterforms, wherein nearly every character exhibits up to four distinct graphical variations based on whether it appears in an isolated, initial, medial, or final position.
- Distinguishes phonetic consonants that share an identical skeletal stroke (*rasm*) through the crucial placement of dots (*i'jam*) placed above or below the base letter.
- Cultivated celebrated calligraphic traditions across Islamic civilization, including geometric *Kufic*, flowing *Naskh*, monumental *Thuluth*, and the slanted Persian *Nastaliq*.
- Adapted to write a vast spectrum of non-Arabic languages across Afro-Eurasia, giving rise to writing traditions such as the *Perso-Arabic* alphabet and West African *Ajami*.
- Rendered on architecture and commemorative textiles, such as the elaborate Egyptian *Khayamiya* hangings decorated with intricate calligraphic passages using needlework [[applique]].
- Replaced by Latin orthographies in several modern secularization programs, most prominently through the 1928 language reforms instituted in Turkey by *Mustafa Kemal Atatürk*.

## connections

- [[middle-east]] — geographic region where the script emerged, evolved from ancient Northwest Semitic scripts, and achieved administrative dominance.
- [[five-pillars-of-islam]] — core religious practices inextricably linked to the script through mandatory recitations and copies of the holy text.
- [[islamic-calendar]] — lunar calendar system whose historic chronicles, religious documents, and royal decrees were transcribed in the script.
- [[applique]] — textile technique utilized in traditional Egyptian tentmaking to stitch ornamental calligraphic panels and verses onto canvas.
- [[linguistics]] — academic field that analyzes the writing system's phonology, abjad orthography, and historical cross-linguistic adaptations.
- [[geometry]] — mathematical branch that informed the strict proportional canons developed by classical calligraphers such as *Ibn Muqla*.
