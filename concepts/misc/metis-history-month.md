## summary

**Métis History Month**, observed alongside **Métis Week** across Canada every November, is an annual commemoration dedicated to celebrating the culture, heritage, and political struggles of the Métis Nation. The observance focuses on the anniversary of November 16, 1885, when political leader and provincial founder *Louis Riel* was executed for treason in Regina following the North-West Resistance. Educational forums, artistic gatherings, and communal ceremonies highlight Métis self-determination, traditional fiddling, distinct languages such as [[metis-french]], and the enduring legal struggle for land rights against North American [[colonialism]].

## you gotta know

- Observed throughout November across Canadian provinces, with celebrations heavily concentrated during the designated week surrounding November 16 to commemorate the sacrifice of national leader *Louis Riel*.
- Commemorates *Louis Riel*, the intellectual and political leader who organized the Red River Resistance of 1869–1870, established a provisional government that brought Manitoba into Canadian Confederation via the Manitoba Act of 1870, and led the 1885 North-West Resistance.
- Commemorates the tragic culmination of the 1885 North-West Resistance, which ended in military defeat at the Battle of Batoche under military commander *Gabriel Dumont* and led to *Louis Riel*'s controversial hanging for high treason under Prime Minister *John A. Macdonald*.
- Prominently showcases the Métis Infinity Flag, featuring a horizontal white mathematical lemniscate on either a blue background (symbolizing historic alliances with the North West Company) or a red background (tied to the Hudson's Bay Company), symbolizing the eternal survival of a distinct people born of Indigenous and European unions.
- Celebrates the ceremonial wearing of the finger-woven woolen sash (*l'assomption sash* or *ceinture fléchée*), a multifunctional garment historically employed as a tumpline, coat tie, and emergency rope during western fur trade expeditions.
- Highlights traditional Métis fiddle music and dance, especially the fast-paced, rhythmic *Red River Jig*, an energetic blend of Scottish reel, French-Canadian step-dancing, and Plains Indigenous footwork.
- Promotes the revitalization of endangered ancestral languages, specifically the French-Cree mixed language *Michif* and the distinct regional dialect of [[metis-french]].
- Educates the public on constitutional rights enshrined under Section 35 of the Canadian Constitution Act of 1982, alongside the landmark 2003 Supreme Court case *R. v. Powley*, which legally affirmed Métis hunting and harvesting rights.

## connections

- [[metis-french]] — represents the historic French dialect whose preservation and oral traditions are highlighted during the month.
- [[colonialism]] — sparked the territorial dispossessions, scrip speculation, and resistance movements that define Métis history.
- [[british-empire]] — exercised colonial sovereignty and deployed military troops to suppress prairie self-determination.
- [[france]] — supplied the French-Canadian voyageur ancestry and linguistic foundation that intermingled with First Nations cultures.
- [[indian-removal]] — parallels the forced displacements, military confrontations, and fraudulent scrip systems experienced on the northern plains.
- [[world-war-i]] — saw disproportionately high volunteer enlistment among Métis soldiers despite continuous domestic disenfranchisement.
