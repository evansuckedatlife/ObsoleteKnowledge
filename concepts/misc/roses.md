## summary

The **roses** encompass a renowned genus (*Rosa*) of prickly, woody perennial shrubs in the family Rosaceae, widely cultivated across temperate regions for their ornamental blossoms, fragrant essential oils, and profound cultural resonance. Cultivated for millennia across Europe, Asia, and the Mediterranean, the flower stands as one of the world's most enduring botanical symbols, embodying romantic love, clandestine discretion, religious veneration, and royal dynasty. In quiz bowl, questions frequently test its classical mythological origins, its role in English dynastic civil wars, its presence in religious iconography, and its adoption by 20th-century political resistance movements.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the central heraldic motif of the 15th-century English civil wars known as the *Wars of the Roses*, contested between the *House of Lancaster* (a red rose) and the *House of York* (a white rose), reconciled when *Henry VII* created the unified *Tudor rose*.
- Associated in classical myth with the love goddess *Aphrodite* (or *Venus*), whose tears and the blood of *Adonis* produced red roses, while *Eros* bribed the god of silence *Harpocrates* with a rose, originating the Latin phrase *sub rosa* for secrecy.
- Honored in Roman ritual during the spring festival of *Rosalia*, when citizens adorned graves with fresh blossoms and scattered petals at banquets as emblems of rebirth and seasonal celebration.
- Revered in Christian tradition as a symbol of the Virgin Mary, invoked as the *Mystic Rose* (*Rosa Mystica*), which inspired the etymology of the Catholic *Rosary* prayer beads from Latin *rosarium*.
- Celebrated across world literature, inspiring Juliet's famous reflection on names in *William Shakespeare*'s *Romeo and Juliet*, Gertrude Stein's aphorism "Rose is a rose is a rose is a rose," and Umberto Eco's *The Name of the Rose*.
- Adopted globally as an emblem of democratic socialism and labor rights, commemorating the landmark 1912 Lawrence textile strike remembered by the feminist phrase "Bread and Roses."
- Named the *White Rose* (*Die Weiße Rose*), the courageous student resistance circle led by *Hans Scholl* and *Sophie Scholl* that distributed anti-Nazi leaflets at the University of Munich in 1943.
- Yields valuable commercial extracts such as attar of roses and rose water, distilled primarily from *Rosa damascena* for high-end perfumery, traditional confectioneries, and medicinal preparations.
