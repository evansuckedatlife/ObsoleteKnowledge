## summary

**Samsung** is South Korea's largest multinational conglomerate, or *chaebol*, headquartered in Samsung Town, Seoul. Founded in 1938 by *Lee Byung-chul* as a modest trading firm dealing in dried fish, noodles, and local produce, it diversified into textiles, insurance, securities, and retail before launching Samsung Electronics in the late 1960s. Today, it stands as a cornerstone of the South Korean national economy and a global titan in consumer electronics, semiconductor manufacturing, display panels, and telecommunications.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1938 in Daegu by *Lee Byung-chul*, the company takes its name from the Korean word for *three stars*, representing a wish to become big, numerous, and powerful like stars in the sky.
- Under Lee's son *Lee Kun-hee*, who took over in 1987, the company underwent a radical transformation marked by the famous 1993 *Frankfurt Declaration*, where Lee instructed executives to "change everything except your wife and children."
- In 1995, *Lee Kun-hee* demonstrated an uncompromising commitment to quality by ordering the destruction and burning of 150,000 defective mobile phones in front of 2,000 employees at the Gumi factory.
- Its flagship consumer smartphone brand, the *Galaxy* series, runs on Google's Android operating system and competes directly with Apple's iPhone in the global market.
- Samsung Electronics is one of the world's largest producers of memory chips, including DRAM and NAND flash memory, supplying crucial components to rival tech giants and personal computing markets worldwide.
- The conglomerate encompasses diverse divisions beyond consumer tech, including Samsung Heavy Industries (one of the globe's largest shipbuilders) and Samsung C&T, which served as primary contractor for the construction of the *Burj Khalifa* in Dubai.
- The company was embroiled in major political scandals, notably the 2016 South Korean political scandal involving President *Park Geun-hye*, which led to the arrest and conviction of de facto chief *Lee Jae-yong*.

## connections

- [[cold-war]] — the postwar geopolitical environment and rapid industrialization of South Korea enabled state-supported *chaebols* like Samsung to expand rapidly.
- [[japan]] — early Korean technological development frequently adapted manufacturing practices and electronic component designs pioneered by Japanese conglomerates.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — reflects the modern iteration of mass manufacturing, heavy industrial production, and assembly-line technology.
- [[space-age]] — advanced semiconductor manufacturing and microelectronics from Samsung support modern aerospace instrumentation and digital satellites.
- [[modernism]] — sleek, minimalist product design and high-tech aesthetics characterize its global corporate branding and consumer lines.
- [[united-states]] — remains one of Samsung's primary consumer export markets and key patent battlegrounds against domestic competitors like Apple.
