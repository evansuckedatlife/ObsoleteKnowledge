## summary

**Domino's**, legally operating as **Domino's Pizza**, is an American multinational restaurant corporation founded in 1960 in *Ypsilanti*, *Michigan*, that stands as the largest pizza company in the world by global retail sales. Established by brothers *Tom Monaghan* and *James Monaghan* under the original name *DomiNick's*, the chain revolutionized the quick-service food sector by subordinating dine-in seating to streamlined delivery logistics, custom corrugated cardboard packaging, and mechanized assembly-line food preparation. Recognized for pioneering supply-chain efficiency and aggressive digital franchising, the enterprise transformed how commercial food delivery interfaces with modern consumer telecommunications and app-based commerce.

## you gotta know

- Founded in December 1960 when *Tom Monaghan* and his brother purchased an existing pizzeria named *DomiNick's* near *Eastern Michigan University*, with Tom later trading his brother a Volkswagen Beetle for full ownership of the business.
- Adopted its famous domino logo with three dots to represent the three franchise locations open in 1965, abandoning plans to add a dot for each new store as expansion exploded across the continent.
- Rose to national prominence through its controversial "30 minutes or it's free" delivery guarantee launched in 1973, which was discontinued in the *[[united-states]]* in 1993 following multi-million-dollar lawsuits regarding dangerous driving accidents.
- Redesigned the modern corrugated cardboard pizza box by deploying the rigid "die-cut fold" design, preventing delivery boxes from sagging in the center and adhering to melted cheese during transport.
- Executed an audacious self-deprecating advertising overhaul in 2009 known as the "Pizza Turnaround," publicly acknowledging that customer surveys compared its crust to cardboard and completely reformulating its sauce, cheese, and dough recipes.
- Repositioned itself as an e-commerce and technology company, developing digital platforms like the proprietary *Domino's Tracker* and automated mobile ordering systems that drove phenomenal stock market returns throughout the 2010s.
- Competes directly with regional takeaway staples and culinary hybrids, prompting international and regional franchises to adapt localized items such as donair pizza alongside *[[donair]]* spots in Canada.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — corporate birthplace in Midwestern post-war suburbia where automotive convenience and fast-food franchising converged.
- [[italy]] — distant cultural origin of flatbread and Neapolitan pizza tradition, drastically re-engineered for industrial American mass production.
- [[donair]] — regional street-food phenomenon whose flavors are adapted into specialized regional toppings by competing delivery franchises.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — foundational mechanization of food prep, centralized commissary dough distribution, and logistical time-and-motion optimization.
- [[dollarama]] — peer in aggressive value-oriented franchise retail and low-overhead unit economics catering to cost-conscious suburban consumers.
- [[new-york-city]] — historical contrasting epicenter of artisanal, thin-crust, slice-counter pizza culture that resisted centralized delivery chains.
