## summary

**Cotton** is a soft, protective staple fiber that surrounds the seeds of plants in the genus **Gossypium**, belonging to the mallow family Malvaceae. Cultivated independently for thousands of years in both the Old World and the Americas, it emerged in the late eighteenth century as the premier raw material driving the global [[industrial-revolution]]. Its intensive production anchored the plantation economy of the American South, deepened the horrors of chattel slavery, fueled the geopolitical crisis of the [[american-civil-war]], and restructured textile manufacturing across the [[british-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Commercially cultivated worldwide through four domesticated species: *Gossypium hirsutum* (upland cotton, providing over ninety percent of global supply), *Gossypium barbadense* (extra-long staple Pima and Egyptian varieties), *Gossypium arboreum* (tree cotton of South Asia), and *Gossypium herbaceum* (native to southern Africa and the Levant).
- Eli Whitney's 1793 invention of the mechanical cotton gin permitted rapid separation of seeds from short-staple green-seed fibers, revitalizing the institutional cruelty of plantation slavery across the [[united-states]].
- Propelled the antebellum political ideology of *King Cotton*, articulated by South Carolina senator James Henry Hammond in 1858 under the mistaken conviction that British textile dependence would compel foreign intervention during the [[american-civil-war]].
- Served as the economic foundation of the British [[industrial-revolution]], wherein mechanization in Lancashire mills via the spinning jenny, water frame, and power loom created a voracious global demand that reshaped trade networks under [[colonialism]].
- Consists biologically of single elongated epidermal cells on the seed coat composed of over ninety percent pure cellulose, exhibiting natural spiral twists called convolutions that allow it to be spun into durable thread.
- Decimated in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries by the Mexican boll weevil (*Anthonomus grandis*), an agricultural catastrophe that precipitated rural poverty, spurred the Great Migration, and inspired a famous tribute monument in Enterprise, Alabama.
- Transformed Soviet Central Asian geography when water was diverted from the rivers Amu Darya and Syr Darya to irrigate monoculture fields, causing the environmental desiccation of the Aral Sea.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — provided the physical fiber that stimulated mechanized factory systems and textile export markets in Manchester and New England.
- [[american-civil-war]] — constituted the primary cash crop of the Confederate States and the basis of their failed wartime diplomatic leverage.
- [[colonialism]] — motivated British imperial restructuring of rural Indian agriculture and the forceful suppression of indigenous handloom weaving.
- [[british-empire]] — sustained the global maritime textile supply chain connecting colonial plantations to industrial processing centers.
- [[reconstruction-era]] — shaped the postwar Southern agricultural economy through the widespread rise of sharecropping and tenant farming.
- [[united-states]] — served as the country's dominant antebellum export and a defining driver of territorial expansion into the Deep South.
- [[costco]] — sold in bulk volume as apparel, bed linens, and personal care products sourced across transnational supply chains.
