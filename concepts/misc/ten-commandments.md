## summary

The **Ten Commandments** are the foundational moral and religious laws given by God to *Moses* on Mount Sinai, according to Hebrew scripture. Presented as inscribed on two stone tablets, these commandments form the ethical core of Judeo-Christian tradition, encompassing duties to God and to one's neighbor. They remain one of the most recognizable and culturally influential texts in Western civilization.

## you gotta know

- Divided into two categories: the first four address duties to God (worshipping no other gods, not making idols, not misusing God's name, remembering the Sabbath as holy); the remaining six concern interpersonal ethics (honoring parents, prohibiting murder, theft, false witness, adultery, and coveting).
- Appear twice in the Hebrew Bible—Exodus 20:1–17 and Deuteronomy 5:4–21—with minor textual variations between the two accounts, reflecting different traditions of transmission through Jewish history.
- Known as the *Decalogue* from the Greek for "ten words" or "ten sayings," emphasizing their role as foundational divine utterances, the core of divine law revealed at Sinai.
- Placed in the *Ark of the Covenant*, a sacred container held in the inner sanctum of the Jewish Temple, making them the holiest object in Judaism and the central relic of Israelite worship.
- Interpreted and amplified through centuries of rabbinic commentary, each commandment spawning hundreds of subsidiary laws and debates about proper observance; the Talmud develops vast jurisprudential frameworks from these ten principles.
- Adopted into Christian moral theology and embedded in Western legal systems, from Puritan New England to modern constitutions referencing natural law; many Western codes trace their ethical foundations to this text.
- Historically displayed prominently in American courthouses and public spaces, making them a flashpoint in modern debates over secularism, religious freedom, and the role of faith in law.

## connections

- [[moses]] — the lawgiver who received the commandments directly from God.
- [[torah]] — the Torah, of which Exodus and Deuteronomy are parts.
- [[mount-sinai]] — the sacred mountain where the revelation occurred.
- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition for which the commandments are absolutely central.
- [[christianity]] — inherited and reinterpreted the commandments as part of its moral framework.
- [[law]] — both as a concept and as a historical influence on Western legal codes.
