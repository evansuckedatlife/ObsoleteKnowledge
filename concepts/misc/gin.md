## summary

**Gin** is a distilled alcoholic spirit that derives its predominant, characteristic aroma and flavor profile from the essential oils of *juniper berries* (*Juniperus communis*). Evolving out of medieval medicinal distillations and the traditional Dutch spirit **Genever**, it achieved widespread notoriety in *England* during the eighteenth-century "Gin Craze" following the Glorious Revolution. Today, gin stands as one of the world's most versatile cocktail spirits, encompassing strict traditional styles such as *London Dry*, *Plymouth*, and *Old Tom*, as well as botanical-forward contemporary expressions.

## you gotta know

- Rooted historically in the Low Countries as the malt-based, juniper-flavored liquor *genever*, originally marketed by physicians as an herbal diuretic and restorative remedy.
- Introduced en masse to *England* during the seventeenth-century Anglo-Dutch Wars—giving rise to the phrase "Dutch courage"—and popularized when Dutch prince *William of Orange* assumed the English crown.
- Precipitated the notorious eighteenth-century *Gin Craze* in London, where unregulated distilling caused widespread urban social dislocation, famously satirized by *William Hogarth* in his print *Gin Lane*.
- Standardized during the *Industrial Revolution* by the invention of the *Aeneas Coffey* continuous column still, enabling the production of crisp, clean *London Dry Gin*.
- Gave rise to the *Gin and Tonic* in the *British Empire*, where colonial officers blended bitter medicinal quinine tonic water with gin to stave off malaria.
- Serves as the quintessential foundation spirit of premier classic cocktails, including the *Dry Martini*, *Negroni*, *Gimlet*, *Aviation*, and *Tom Collins*.

## connections

- [[england]] — historic European nation that experienced the socio-cultural upheaval of the eighteenth-century Gin Craze.
- [[united-kingdom]] — country whose legal statutes and distilleries standardized internationally recognized categories like *London Dry Gin*.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial administration whose soldiers in India popularized mixing tonic water and citrus with gin to combat malaria.
- [[ginger]] — aromatic spice used in ginger beer and ginger ale to create classic gin cocktails like the *Gin Buck* and *London Mule*.
- [[gin-rummy]] — popular two-player card game named after the liquor as a pun on *rummy*.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of technological innovation that produced the continuous column still, revolutionizing mass spirit rectification.
- [[colonialism]] — global trade and territorial expansion that dispersed European distillation and botanical mixology worldwide.
