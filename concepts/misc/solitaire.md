## summary

**Solitaire**, historically designated across much of the Anglophone sphere as **patience**, encompasses a broad family of single-player card games centered around arranging a shuffled deck into predetermined sequences or foundations. Emerging in continental Europe during the late eighteenth century, the genre flourished in [[france]] and nineteenth-century [[england]] before evolving into a worldwide cultural staple. In modern popular culture, the game became synonymous with the standard seven-column variant **Klondike**, popularized during the late-nineteenth-century Gold Rush and later ubiquitously distributed on millions of personal computers throughout the [[united-states]] and the world.

## you gotta know

- Earliest documented literary mentions appear in the 1788 German gaming compendium *Das neue Königliche L'Hombre-Spiel*, designating the format as *Patiencespiel* and suggesting origins in Baltic fortune-telling or solitary diversion.
- Romantic lore often claimed French emperor *Napoleon Bonaparte* spent his lonely exile on *Saint Helena* devising variations, though British aristocrats popularized dedicated guidebooks during the Victorian era through authors like *Lady Adelaide Cadogan*.
- The *Klondike* variant arranges twenty-eight cards across seven tableau columns with building downward in alternating red and black colors, alongside four suit foundations constructed from ace upward to king.
- Modern global familiarity was cemented in 1990 when *Microsoft* included a graphical version programmed by intern *Wes Cherry* in *Windows 3.0*, designed specifically to teach computer novices how to operate the graphical mouse interface and practice drag-and-drop mechanics.
- Notable competitive and historic variants include *FreeCell*, *Spider*, *Canfield* (invented by Saratoga casino owner *Richard Canfield*), *Yukon*, and *Forty Thieves*.
- Mathematically classified within combinatorial game theory, where computing the solvability of an arbitrary shuffled layout with hidden cards presents complex non-deterministic algorithmic challenges.
- In French parlance, the term *solitaire* originally denoted a solitary peg-jumping board game, whereas card-laying varieties were exclusively termed *la patience*.

## connections

- [[france]] — the continental birthplace where aristocratic salons formalized rules, nomenclature, and early literature for *la patience*.
- [[england]] — the Victorian society whose leisure culture and parlor publications popularized the game across the British Empire.
- [[united-kingdom]] — the home domain where nineteenth-century card manuals standardized tournament structures and card etiquette.
- [[united-states]] — the country where gambling saloons during the Yukon gold strike created *Klondike* and software developers popularized digital editions.
- [[geometry]] — the spatial arrangements, column symmetry, and grid-based foundations essential to manual layout structures.
- [[perception]] — the visual scanning, spatial reasoning, and probabilistic estimation required to navigate concealed tableau columns.
