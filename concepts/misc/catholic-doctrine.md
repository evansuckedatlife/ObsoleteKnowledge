## summary

**Catholic doctrine** represents the body of theological teachings, authoritative dogmas, and moral principles upheld by the Catholic Church as revealed truth handed down through Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition. Interpreted and preserved by the Magisterium under the leadership of the Bishop of *[[rome]]*, it synthesizes early Christian conciliar creeds with scholastic philosophy and patristic theology. The doctrine governs the Church's sacramental life, ecclesiology, and distinct dogmatic teachings regarding salvation, grace, the Virgin Mary, and the authority of the papacy.

## you gotta know

- Identifies Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition as forming a single sacred deposit of the Word of God, entrusted to the authentic interpretation of the living Magisterium.
- Affirms the core Trinitarian and Christological definitions formulated at the early ecumenical councils, including Nicaea, Constantinople, and Chalcedon.
- Recognizes seven sacraments instituted by Christ—Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony—as efficacious signs of grace.
- Dogmatically distinguishes the universal Christian consensus on the *Virgin Birth* of Christ from the *Immaculate Conception*, a distinct Catholic dogma defined in 1854 by Pope Pius IX stating that the Virgin Mary was preserved free from original sin from her first moment of conception.
- Teaches the real presence of Christ in the Eucharist through *transubstantiation*, where the substance of bread and wine is converted into the Body and Blood of Christ while retaining their outward accidents.
- Defined the dogma of Papal Infallibility at the First Vatican Council in 1870, asserting that the Pope is preserved from error when solemnly defining a doctrine concerning faith or morals *ex cathedra*.
- Emphasizes the communion of saints, the efficacy of prayer for the deceased in Purgatory, and the veneration (*hyperdulia*) due uniquely to Mary as Mother of God.

## connections

- [[christianity]] — the broad monotheistic world faith of which the Catholic Church constitutes the largest communion.
- [[rome]] — the historic seat of the papacy, the Apostolic See, and the central administrative curia of the Church.
- [[roman-empire]] — the ancient political sphere in which early Catholic structures, dioceses, and conciliar assemblies first evolved.
- [[holy-roman-empire]] — the medieval and early modern European imperial confederation whose constitutional order was closely intertwined with the Catholic Church.
- [[renaissance]] — the cultural and intellectual movement in Europe deeply patronized by the Roman papacy, producing monumental sacred art and humanist scholarship.
- [[celebrations]] — the liturgical calendar of holy days, feasts, solemnities, and jubilees central to Catholic parish life.
