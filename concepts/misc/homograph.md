## summary

A **homograph** is a linguistic term designating two or more words that share an identical written spelling but carry different meanings, origins, and frequently different pronunciations. Formed from the Greek *homos* (same) and *grapho* (to write), homographs present unique challenges in visual reading comprehension and text-to-speech technology. When homographs differ in acoustic vocalization—such as *lead* the metal versus *lead* the verb—they are specifically categorized as heteronyms, serving as staple puzzles in orthographic and phonetic education.

## you gotta know

- Originates etymologically from the Greek *homos* meaning *same* and *graphein* meaning *to write*, highlighting visual orthographic unity.
- Subdivided into *heteronyms* when the shared spelling corresponds to distinct pronunciations, as in *bass* (the fish) and *bass* (the low musical register).
- Functions as a true homonym when identical spelling coincides with identical spoken pronunciation, as in *bat* (flying mammal) and *bat* (wooden sporting club).
- Involves stress shifts between syllables in English verbs and nouns, such as *re-CORD* (action) versus *REC-ord* (physical vinyl or transcript).
- Arises frequently through independent morphological evolution where divergent Old English or foreign loanwords converged upon the identical Roman spelling.
- Creates unique pronunciation hurdles in early childhood reading instruction, necessitating readers to infer syntax and syntactic category prior to vocalization.
- Complicates digital optical character recognition and text-to-speech algorithmic synthesis, which rely on contextual natural language parsing to select correct phonemes.
- Forms the basis of literary sight puns, typographical riddles, and visual wordplay enjoyed across printed anthologies and comedic literature.
