## summary

**Hummus**, formally known as *hummus bi tahina*, is a traditional Levantine dip and spread prepared from mashed cooked chickpeas, sesame paste (*tahini*), lemon juice, and garlic. Originating in the medieval *Middle East*, the dish has formed a dietary staple across Arab, Mediterranean, and Jewish culinary traditions for centuries. In the contemporary era, it has transitioned from a regional cornerstone of meze dining into a globally celebrated health food and plant-based protein staple.

## you gotta know

- Takes its fundamental name directly from the Arabic word for the chickpea (*Cicer arietinum*), an ancient legume cultivated in the Fertile Crescent since the dawn of agriculture.
- Earliest documented culinary predecessors incorporating pureed chickpeas with sesame paste, vinegar, and spices appear in medieval Arabic cookbooks from thirteenth-century Cairo and Damascus.
- Prepared canonically by emulsifying cooked and mashed chickpeas with *tahini*, freshly squeezed lemon juice, crushed garlic, salt, and generous drizzles of extra virgin olive oil.
- Traditionally served warm or at room temperature as part of a *meze* platter, commonly garnished with whole chickpeas, ground sumac, paprika, or chopped flat-leaf parsley and scooped with flatbread.
- Regional variations feature toppings such as spiced ground lamb or beef, sautéed pine nuts, whole fava beans (*ful*), or fiery chili pastes like *shatta* and *harissa*.
- Became the centerpiece of cultural ownership and culinary copyright disputes colloquially dubbed the "Hummus Wars," where nations like Lebanon and Israel competed for *Guinness World Records* for the largest prepared dish.
- Enjoyed widespread international adoption during the late twentieth century as vegetarian and vegan diets gained popularity in Western markets.
