## summary

**Nvidia** is an American multinational technology company that invented the graphics processing unit and pioneered the modern paradigm of accelerated parallel computing. Established in California in 1993, the enterprise initially focused on engineering specialized microchips for rendering complex 3D visual environments in consumer video games before expanding into scientific computing and artificial intelligence. Today, its specialized silicon architectures and proprietary software frameworks form the vital backbone of global data centers and cutting-edge machine learning research.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1993 at a Denny's diner in San Jose, California, by electrical engineers *Jensen Huang*, *Chris Malachowsky*, and *Curtis Priem*, with Huang serving continuously as its leather-jacket-wearing chief executive.
- Coined the official industry term *graphics processing unit* (*GPU*) in 1999 with the commercial debut of the *GeForce 256*, which integrated hardware transform, lighting, and rendering on a single chip.
- Released *CUDA* (*Compute Unified Device Architecture*) in 2006, an influential parallel programming model that allowed general-purpose software algorithms to execute directly on the massive core arrays of graphics hardware.
- Ignited the modern deep learning revolution in 2012 when *Alex Krizhevsky*, *Ilya Sutskever*, and *Geoffrey Hinton* utilized two *GeForce GTX 580* graphics cards to train the breakthrough neural network *AlexNet*.
- Designed leading-edge enterprise tensor-core architectures—including the *Hopper* and *Blackwell* series—which provide the foundational computing clusters powering contemporary frontier artificial intelligence models.
- Reached historic financial milestones in 2024 by crossing a three-trillion-dollar market capitalization, reflecting intense global demand for advanced accelerated hardware.
- Powers massive computational clusters utilized in astrophysics, aerospace simulation, and mathematical calculations where researchers model complex variables represented by symbols such as [[omega]].
