## summary

**Washington, D.C.** is the capital of the United States, founded in 1790 as a purpose-built city on the Potomac River serving as the seat of federal government. Named after *George Washington*, the city was designed by French architect Pierre L'Enfant as a symbol of Enlightenment ideals and republican virtue, with grand avenues, neoclassical monuments, and the great *Capitol* building as its centerpiece. It represents American power, democracy, and aspirations, while being a living document of the nation's history and contradictions.

## you gotta know

- Established by the *Compromise of 1790* between Alexander Hamilton and Thomas Jefferson, placed on the Potomac River as a geographic compromise between North and South.
- Built on swampland from scratch, making it a planned capital—unlike older European capitals that grew organically.
- Designed by Pierre L'Enfant with radiating avenues, grand vistas, and neoclassical architecture inspired by Enlightenment ideals.
- The *Capitol Building* is the symbolic heart, housing the U.S. Congress and serving as the visual anchor of the city's design.
- Home to the *White House*, the presidential residence since 1800 and one of the world's most recognizable buildings.
- Houses the *Smithsonian Institution*, museums, and libraries—making it a center of American intellectual and cultural life.
- Built partially by enslaved labor and stood in a city where slavery was legal until 1862, embodying America's foundational contradiction.
- Located on the *Potomac River*, which defines its geography and connected it to colonial trade networks.
- The *National Mall* stretches from the Capitol to the Washington Monument, lined with museums, monuments, and memorials to American history.
