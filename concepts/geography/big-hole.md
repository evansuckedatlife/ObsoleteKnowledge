## summary

The **Big Hole**, historically known as the **Kimberley Mine** or the **Open Mine**, is a massive open-pit and underground diamond excavation located in Kimberley, South Africa. Hand-dug by thousands of miners using picks and shovels between 1871 and 1914, it became the epicentre of the southern African mineral revolution and reshaped the global diamond trade. The site fueled the corporate ascension of *De Beers* under *Cecil Rhodes*, drove aggressive British imperial expansion in the region, and established modern industrial diamond mining.

## you gotta know

- Excavated entirely by human labor without heavy mechanical excavators, yielding an estimated 2,722 kilograms (roughly 14.5 million carats) of diamonds from 22 million tons of excavated earth.
- Sparked the intense New Rush of 1871 following the discovery of diamonds on the *Colesberg Kopje* on the farm *Vooruitzigt*, originally owned by the Boer brothers *Johannes Nicolaas de Beer* and *Diederik Arnoldus de Beer*.
- Gave its name to kimberlite, the volatile potassium-bearing ultramafic igneous rock formed deep in the Earth's mantle that serves as the primary volcanic pipe matrix for gem-quality diamonds.
- Formed the economic foundation of *De Beers Consolidated Mines*, created through the 1888 merger orchestrated by imperialist financier *Cecil Rhodes* alongside his primary rival *Barney Barnato*.
- Sustained deep underground shaft mining under the guidance of American mining engineer *Gardner Williams* after open-pit excavation grew dangerously prone to catastrophic rockfalls and wall collapses in 1889.
- Endured the four-month *Siege of Kimberley* by Boer commandos during the Second Boer War, during which thousands of civilians sheltered inside the deep tunnels of the mine while *Cecil Rhodes* directed local defense efforts.
- Stands today as a major museum complex featuring a preserved historic mining town and a massive viewing platform overlooking the water-filled abyss, which descends over two hundred meters to the surface of the subterranean water.

## connections

- [[bloemfontein]] — neighboring capital of the Orange Free State whose strategic rivalry with the Kimberley diamond fields shaped regional railway construction and nineteenth-century imperial diplomacy.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power that annexed the Griqualand West territory around the mine following the disputed *Keate Award* to secure the mineral wealth.
- [[colonialism]] — the overarching political system under which migrant African laborers were subjected to strict pass laws and enclosed living compounds that prefigured apartheid policies.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the global economic transformation that supplied steam winding engines and steel cables to extract diamonds while demanding vast industrial output.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the maritime transit route through Cape Town along which extracted gems were shipped to European cutting centers and global gem exchanges.
- [[world-war-i]] — the global conflict that halted diamond production at the site in August 1914, bringing commercial mining operations at the pit to a permanent close.
