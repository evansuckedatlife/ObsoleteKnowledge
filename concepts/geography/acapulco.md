## summary

**Acapulco**, officially designated *Acapulco de Juárez*, is a major coastal city and deepwater seaport situated on the Pacific coast of *Guerrero*, Mexico. During the early modern era, it operated as the vital American terminus of the transpacific *Manila Galleon* trade under the global empire of [[spain]]. In the twentieth century, the city underwent a dramatic transformation into an international playground for mid-century Hollywood celebrities, renowned worldwide for the dramatic cliff-diving performances at *La Quebrada*.

## you gotta know

- Served for two and a half centuries (1565–1815) as the exclusive western terminal for the *Manila Galleons*, trading Mexican silver for Asian silk, porcelain, and spices during the [[age-of-exploration]].
- Defended by the star-shaped fortress *Fuerte de San Diego*, constructed by Spanish authorities to protect valuable cargo fleets from privateers such as *Francis Drake*.
- Gained world fame for the daring cliff divers (*clavadistas*) of *La Quebrada*, who plunge over one hundred feet into a narrow ocean cove timed precisely to incoming swells.
- Emerged during the mid-twentieth century and the [[cold-war]] as a premier luxury resort for jet-set figures, politicians, and Hollywood stars from the [[united-states]].
- Hosted the honeymoon retreats of prominent international figures, including *John F. Kennedy* and *Jacqueline Bouvier*, as well as *Elizabeth Taylor*.
- Served as a beloved retreat and promotional backdrop for cinema personalities since the era of [[silent-film]], later immortalized in popular culture by figures like *Elvis Presley* in *Fun in Acapulco*.
- Situated around a vast, natural semicircular bay backdropped by the rugged peaks of the *Sierra Madre del Sur*, making it one of the most naturally sheltered anchorages on the Pacific coast of the Americas.

## connections

- [[spain]] — the imperial power that established the port as the gateway for its transpacific maritime empire.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the historical era of transoceanic navigation that inaugurated global commerce through the port.
- [[colonialism]] — the political and economic system through which the city linked *New Spain* with the *Philippines*.
- [[cold-war]] — the mid-twentieth-century period when the city reached its peak as a glamorous cosmopolitan resort.
- [[united-states]] — the northern neighbor whose tourists, entertainers, and investors fueled the city's golden age of hospitality.
- [[silent-film]] — the early cinematic era whose pioneering actors were among the first international celebrities to patronize the bay.
