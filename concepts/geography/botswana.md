## summary

**Botswana** is a large, landlocked Southern African nation characterized by the *Kalahari Desert*, the *Okavango Delta*, and vast wildlife reserves, with a relatively small but ethnically diverse population including the *San* people and *Tswana* majority. Since independence from Britain in 1966, Botswana has achieved political stability, democratic governance, and economic growth based initially on diamonds and later diversified development—making it one of Africa's most successful post-colonial states. Its conservation efforts, wildlife heritage, and geopolitical role make it significant in African and global contexts.

## you gotta know

- The *Kalahari Desert* dominates central Botswana; it is a vast, semi-arid landscape of sparse vegetation, salt pans, and wildlife adapted to scarce water; historically, the San people thrived as hunter-gatherers in this ecosystem.
- The *Okavango Delta*, in northern Botswana, is one of Africa's most biodiverse ecosystems—a massive inland delta where the Okavango River spreads into channels and lagoons creating a wetland oasis in the surrounding desert; it supports exceptional wildlife including elephants, lions, hippos, and countless bird species.
- Botswana is one of the world's largest diamond producers; diamond wealth funded the nation's development, though revenues have been managed relatively responsibly compared to other resource-rich African nations (avoiding the "resource curse" of corruption and inequality).
- The country borders *South Africa* to the south and east, *Zimbabwe* to the north and east, and *Zambia* to the north; its geographic position made it strategically important during the Cold War and in anti-apartheid struggles in Southern Africa.
- Independence from Britain in 1966 was achieved peacefully; unlike many African colonies, Botswana inherited functioning institutions and has maintained democratic governance and regular, competitive elections for six decades.
- The *Tswana* people are the majority ethnicity, though Botswana encompasses other groups including the *San*, *Kalanga*, and *Kgalagadi*; the San, traditionally hunter-gatherers, face social marginalization and land dispossession despite constitutional protections.
- Botswana pursues wildlife conservation; it has established national parks and game reserves protecting elephants, wild dogs, and other megafauna; conservation tourism generates significant revenue and has created international recognition for Botswana's environmental commitment.
- The capital, Gaborone, grew from a small settlement in the 1960s into a modern city; the nation has achieved economic diversification, investing in education, infrastructure, and financial services while remaining vulnerable to commodity price fluctuations and climate variability.
