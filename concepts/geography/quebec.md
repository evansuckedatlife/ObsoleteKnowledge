## summary

**Quebec**, officially written in French as **Québec**, is the largest province by land area in [[canada]] and the second-most populous after Ontario. Distinguished as Canada's only predominantly French-speaking province, its political, legal, and linguistic identity is safeguarded by the civil law tradition and strict protective language statutes. Bordered by the Saint Lawrence River and James Bay, the province has exerted an outsized influence on Canadian politics through nationalist movements, hydroelectric development, and its vibrant athletic and literary culture.

## you gotta know

- Largest province by land area in [[canada]], encompassing vast stretches of the Canadian Shield, the Saint Lawrence Lowlands, and subarctic tundra extending north to Hudson Strait.
- Capital city is [[quebec-city]] on the Saint Lawrence River, while *Montreal* stands as its largest metropolis and the second-largest French-speaking city in the Western world.
- Distinguishes itself from all other Canadian provinces by retaining a continental *Civil Code* system of private law based on French legal traditions rather than British common law.
- Underwent the transformative *Quiet Revolution* (Révolution tranquille) during the 1960s under Premier *Jean Lesage*, secularizing social institutions, modernizing education, and nationalizing private electric utilities under the public giant *Hydro-Québec*.
- Subjected to intense constitutional crises and two sovereignty referendums: the first in 1980 under *Parti Québécois* founder *René Lévesque*, and the second in 1995 under *Jacques Parizeau*, which narrowly failed by less than one percentage point.
- Home to the legendary *Montreal Canadiens* ice hockey franchise of the NHL, whose storied heroes like *Maurice Richard*, *Jean Béliveau*, and *Ken Dryden* became emblematic symbols of Québécois cultural pride.
- Governed linguistically by the 1977 *Charter of the French Language* (popularly known as *Bill 101*), which established French as the sole official language of government, court proceedings, commercial signage, and public education.
- Produces over seventy percent of the global supply of maple syrup, heavily centered in the Eastern Townships and the Chaudière-Appalaches agricultural regions.

## connections

- [[quebec-city]] — provincial capital city, housing the unicameral National Assembly and historic fortifications overlooking the Saint Lawrence River.
- [[canada]] — confederation partner that emerged out of the 1867 British North America Act, joining Quebec, Ontario, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick into the original dominion.
- [[france]] — original colonial sovereign that colonized New France from the early seventeenth century until formal cession under the 1763 Treaty of Paris.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial ruler that accommodated the French populace through the landmark *Quebec Act of 1774*, guaranteeing freedom of Catholic worship and French civil jurisprudence.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — primary oceanic basin connected to the province's economic heartland via the Gulf of Saint Lawrence and the Saint Lawrence Seaway.
- [[saskatchewan]] — fellow Canadian province that joined confederation later in 1905, representing the agrarian, English-speaking prairie counterweight to central Canadian industrial power.
- [[prince-edward-island]] — maritime neighbor whose political leaders convened the 1864 meetings that defined Quebec's provincial status within the nascent federation.
- [[colonialism]] — broader European expansion that reshaped indigenous ancestral territories of the Innu, Cree, Algonquin, and Mohawk peoples into an urbanized commercial dominion.
