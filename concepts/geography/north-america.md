## summary

**North America** is the northern continent of the Americas, encompassing the United States, Canada, Mexico, and numerous Caribbean island nations, stretching from the Arctic Ocean to the tropical region of Central America. Inhabited for thousands of years by diverse Indigenous peoples, it became a focal point of European colonization beginning in the late 15th century, leading to the rise of powerful nation-states and eventually becoming the dominant geopolitical force of the modern era. Its vast natural resources, geographic diversity, and role in shaping world history make it central to contemporary global affairs.

## you gotta know

- North America is bounded by the Atlantic Ocean to the east, the Pacific Ocean to the west, the Arctic Ocean to the north, and merges with Central America and the Caribbean to the south; it encompasses vast mountain ranges, great plains, deserts, and ancient forests.
- The *Mississippi River*, one of the world's longest rivers, drains much of central North America and was crucial to Indigenous trade networks and later European-American expansion and commerce.
- Indigenous peoples inhabited North America for at least 15,000 years before European contact, developing diverse civilizations—from the *Ancestral Puebloans* of the Southwest to the *Haudenosaunee Confederacy* in the Northeast—with sophisticated governance, agriculture, and trade systems.
- European colonization intensified in the 16th–18th centuries with Spanish, French, English, and Dutch expeditions; competition among these powers for colonial control shaped North American political boundaries and international conflicts.
- The *United States* declared independence from Britain in 1776 and established itself as a republic; its westward expansion, codified in the concept of "Manifest Destiny," displaced Indigenous peoples and extended territorial control to the Pacific Ocean.
- Canada emerged as a British dominion in 1867, gradually acquiring sovereignty; it represents a different colonial trajectory—less revolutionary than the U.S. and maintaining stronger ties to Britain and the Commonwealth.
- The *American Civil War* (1861–1865) determined that the United States would remain a unified nation and abolished slavery, fundamentally reshaping North American society and establishing the U.S. as an ascendant power.
- North America became the dominant global economic and military superpower in the 20th century, especially after World War II; its technological innovation, resource wealth, and cultural influence shape world affairs.
