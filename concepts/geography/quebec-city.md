## summary

**Quebec City**, officially designated as **Ville de Québec** and commonly rendered as **Québec City**, is the provincial capital of [[quebec]] and one of the oldest permanent European settlements in North America. Located on Cap Diamant overlooking the Saint Lawrence River where the channel dramatically narrows, the city preserves the only fortified ramparts north of Mexico, designated a UNESCO World Heritage site. It serves as an enduring heart of French-speaking culture and administrative governance in [[canada]], dominated visually by the iconic *Château Frontenac* hotel.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1608 by French explorer *Samuel de Champlain*, who established a fortified trading post named *L'Habitation* near the indigenous site of *Stadacona*.
- Famous for its historic *Old Quebec* (Vieux-Québec), which remains the only walled urban center in North America north of Mexico, featuring stone ramparts, bastions, and preserved entry gates.
- Dominated by the opulent *Château Frontenac*, a grand railway hotel designed by architect *Bruce Price* for the Canadian Pacific Railway, perched dramatically atop Cap Diamant.
- Site of the decisive 1759 *Battle of the Plains of Abraham* during the French and Indian War, in which British general *James Wolfe* defeated French general *Louis-Joseph de Montcalm*, with both commanders suffering mortal wounds.
- Hosted the historic 1864 *Quebec Conference*, where thirty-three delegates known as the Fathers of Confederation drafted the *Seventy-Two Resolutions* that formed the constitutional foundation of modern [[canada]].
- Features the historic *Place Royale* in the Lower Town (Basse-Ville) and the monumental *Citadelle of Quebec*, a star-shaped fortress constructed by the British that serves as official residence for the Governor General of Canada.
- Celebrates the massive winter festival *Carnaval de Québec*, featuring elaborate ice palaces, snow sculpture competitions, canoe races across the icy Saint Lawrence, and the snowman mascot *Bonhomme*.
- Name derives from the Algonquin word *kébec*, meaning "where the river narrows," describing the Saint Lawrence River channel constricting between Cap Diamant and the city of Lévis.

## connections

- [[quebec]] — provincial capital, serving as the seat of the National Assembly at the Parliament Building and the administrative core of the province.
- [[canada]] — foundational colonial settlement that served as the capital of New France, Lower Canada, and the Province of Canada prior to national confederation.
- [[france]] — mother country whose explorers established the settlement in 1608 and maintained governance until the British conquest of 1759–1760.
- [[british-empire]] — victorious imperial power that fortified the citadel, governed the colony through the Quebec Act of 1774, and repelled American attacks in 1775.
- [[prince-edward-island]] — location of the earlier 1864 *Charlottetown Conference*, which directly precipitated the crucial *Quebec Conference* held in Quebec City later that autumn.
- [[colonialism]] — transatlantic expansion that transformed the native Saint Lawrence Iroquoian lands into the administrative hub of French and British fur trading empires.
