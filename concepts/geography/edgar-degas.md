## summary

**Edgar Degas** (1834–1917) was a French painter and sculptor central to Impressionism, though he resisted the label throughout his career. Unlike other Impressionists' focus on landscape and light, Degas fixated on urban life—especially ballet dancers, racehorses, and female nudes—rendered with scientific precision and unusual compositional angles borrowed from photography. His innovative approach to perspective and subject matter profoundly influenced modern art and continues to define how twentieth-century artists approached representation and composition.

## you gotta know

- Born into a wealthy Parisian banking family, granting him financial independence to pursue art outside market pressures; this wealth allowed him to experiment without selling work.
- Initially trained in historical painting but redirected toward contemporary urban scenes, particularly dancers at the Paris Opéra—a subject that consumed his artistic output.
- Never exhibited with official Salon juries; participated in all eight independent Impressionist exhibitions (1874–1886), though he distanced himself from Impressionism's theoretical claims.
- Pioneered asymmetrical composition, cropping, and extreme angles inspired by Japanese prints and photography—techniques that prefigured film and modern art conventions.
- Obsessed with capturing movement—his dancers and racehorses freeze action in unstudied, anatomically complex poses that emphasize musculature and effort rather than grace.
- Worked across media: paintings, pastels, printmaking, and bronze sculpture, demonstrating technical mastery in each; his pastels are particularly prized for their luminosity and handling.
- Severely criticized by conservative critics for depicting ballet dancers (associated with loose morality) and working women at leisure; his subject choices were radical and controversial.
- Suffered progressive vision loss later in life, driving him toward abstraction and increasingly bold use of color and form; he continued working despite near-blindness.

## connections

- [[claude-monet]] — fellow Impressionist pioneer, though they disagreed on Impressionism's definition and methods.
- [[pierre-auguste-renoir]] — Impressionist colleague, though Degas' harsh line-work contrasted with Renoir's soft dissolution of form.
- [[impressionism]] — the movement he co-founded while deliberately distancing himself from its theoretical framework.
- [[paris]] — the city whose theaters, cafés, and streets provided endless subjects for his work.
- [[modernism]] — his compositional innovations directly prefigured 20th-century abstraction and experimentation.
- [[photography]] — influenced his off-kilter framing and cropped perspectives.
