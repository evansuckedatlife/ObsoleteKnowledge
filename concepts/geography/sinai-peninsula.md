## summary

**The Sinai Peninsula** is a triangular landmass connecting Africa to Asia, bordered by the Mediterranean Sea to the north, the Red Sea to the east and south, and Egypt to the west. It is one of the world's most strategically consequential geographies, linking three continents and two religious traditions while hosting one of the planet's most vital shipping lanes. The region's possession has been contested repeatedly, defining modern Middle Eastern conflict.

## you gotta know

- Bridges Africa and Asia; its control determines access between Mediterranean and Indian Ocean via the *Suez Canal*.
- Predominantly desert (the *Negev Desert* extends into it), supporting sparse nomadic populations historically.
- Biblically, Mount Sinai is where *Moses* received the Ten Commandments — making it sacred to Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.
- Occupied by Israel during the 1967 Six-Day War; returned to Egypt in stages (1973–1982) following the Yom Kippur War.
- Remains a flashpoint of Israeli-Palestinian tensions and a route for smuggling between Egypt and Gaza.
- Home to Saint Catherine's Monastery, one of Christianity's oldest surviving monastic communities, nestled at the mountain's base.
- The *Red Sea* separates it from Saudi Arabia, making it a crossroads of three continents' trade and geopolitics.
- Its control has shaped empires from Pharaonic Egypt through Ottoman rule to modern nation-states.

- [[egypt]] — owns and controls the peninsula; central to Egyptian sovereignty and national security.

- [[red-sea]] — the southern waterway that frames the peninsula's maritime significance.

- [[middle-east]] — the peninsula exemplifies the region's complex geopolitical and religious layering.
