# Pakistan

## summary

**Pakistan** is a South Asian nation formed in 1947 after the partition of British India, located northwest of India with coastlines on the *Arabian Sea* and bordered by *Afghanistan*, *Iran*, *China*, and *India*. The *Indus River*, flowing from the *Himalayas* southward through the country to the sea, is Pakistan's lifeblood—source of water for agriculture, drinking, and power generation in a region dominated by monsoon climate and arid terrain.

## you gotta know

- Partitioned from British India in 1947 following Indian independence; created as a homeland for Muslims of the Indian subcontinent, displacing millions and triggering religious violence that killed up to a million people.
- The *Indus River* is Pakistan's primary water source and economic artery; the Indus Valley civilization (c. 3300–1300 BCE) was one of the world's earliest urban centers, centered on cities like *Harappa* and *Mohenjo-daro*.
- Named after *Punjab* and *Sindh* (the 'P' and 'S' in an acronym also including *Khan*, *Afghanistan*, and *Balochistan*); the capital was initially *Karachi*, later moved to the purpose-built city of *Islamabad* in 1967.
- Partition resulted in two separate nations: *Pakistan* (Muslim-majority) and *India* (Hindu-plurality, Muslim minority), with the border—the Radcliffe Line, drawn by a British lawyer with minimal knowledge of South Asia—leaving millions on the wrong side of communal lines.
- Home to major cities *Lahore*, *Karachi*, and *Peshawar*; dominated by the *Punjab* region in the north and the arid *Sindh* region in the south; the *Balochistan* plateau dominates the west.
- Borders the *Himalayas* and *Karakoram* mountains to the north, including *K2*, the world's second-highest peak; the northern regions are ethnically and linguistically diverse (Pashtun, Baloch, Kashmiri, Sindhi).
- Shares a complex, often adversarial relationship with neighboring *India* over the disputed region of *Kashmir*, which both nations claim; the relationship is complicated by three major wars (1947, 1965, 1971) and ongoing border tensions.
