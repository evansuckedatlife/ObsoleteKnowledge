## summary

**Juno Beach** was the Allied operational codename for the six-mile coastal landing sector in Normandy, *France*, assaulted primarily by Canadian forces on D-Day, June 6, 1944. Situated between the British invasion sectors of *Gold Beach* to the west and *Sword Beach* to the east, the landing spanned the coastal resort communities of *Courseulles-sur-Mer*, *Bernières-sur-Mer*, and *Saint-Aubin-sur-Mer*. The successful breakthrough by the *3rd Canadian Infantry Division* remains a defining operational milestone in the liberation of Western *Europe* during *World War II*.

## you gotta know

- Assigned primarily to the *3rd Canadian Infantry Division* and the *2nd Canadian Armoured Brigade* under Major-General *Rod Keller*, supported by commandos of the British *Royal Marines*.
- Bookended geographically by the British assault zones of *Gold Beach* to the west and *Sword Beach* to the east along the *Calvados* coast of Lower Normandy.
- Delayed landing times caused by rough chop and sea reefs forced assault landing craft to arrive behind schedule at high tide, stranding boats directly upon concealed German submerged obstacles and Teller mines.
- Defended by the German *716th Static Infantry Division*, which had reinforced concrete seawalls, pillboxes, machine-gun nests, and 75 mm and 88 mm coastal artillery emplacements within the *Atlantic Wall*.
- The *Queen's Own Rifles of Canada*, *The Royal Winnipeg Rifles*, *The North Shore (New Brunswick) Regiment*, and *Le Régiment de la Chaudière* took heavy casualties in the initial assault waves at *Nan Green* and *Mike Red* sectors.
- Canadian armour from *The Fort Garry Horse* and *1st Hussars* deployed amphibious duplex-drive (*DD*) Sherman tanks that swam ashore to knock out fortified German beach exits.
- Advanced farther inland on June 6 than any other Allied landing force, cutting the primary coastal highway between *Caen* and *Bayeux* and reaching intermediate railway objectives near *Carpiquet*.
- Modern visitors encounter the *Centre Juno Beach*, a museum and cultural memorial opened in 2003 at *Courseulles-sur-Mer* to commemorate Canadian military contributions throughout *World War II*.
