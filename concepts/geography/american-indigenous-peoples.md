## summary

**American Indigenous peoples**, encompassing **Native Americans** and **First Nations**, are the culturally diverse original inhabitants of North, Central, and [[south-america]] whose sovereign societies flourished for millennia prior to European [[colonialism]]. Across varied biomes spanning from the subarctic to the [[andes]], these independent nations engineered extensive agricultural complexes, transcontinental trade networks, and sophisticated political confederacies that delineated the physical frontiers of early settlement. In historical geography, anthropology, and [[linguistics]], the study of Indigenous territoriality is vital for understanding ecological resource stewardship, treaty law, and enduring cultural sovereignty across the modern landscapes of the [[united-states]] and [[canada]].

## you gotta know

- The *Trail of Tears* forced relocation policy enacted under the *Indian Removal Act* of 1830 expelled tens of thousands of Cherokee, Muscogee, Seminole, Chickasaw, and Choctaw people from their ancestral homelands in the southeastern [[united-states]] to designated Indian Territory west of the [[mississippi-river]].
- The *Iroquois Confederacy* (*Haudenosaunee*), established under the *Great Law of Peace*, united the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, and later Tuscarora nations into a powerful diplomatic and military alliance that commanded strategic trade corridors between the [[british-empire]] and French colonies before fracturing during the [[american-revolution]].
- Major mound-building Mississippian cultures flourished across eastern river valleys, exemplified by the metropolitan center of *Cahokia* near modern St. Louis, which featured massive earthen platform mounds like *Monks Mound* and sustained an urban trade hub rivaling contemporary European cities in population.
- The arrival of European explorers during the [[age-of-exploration]] introduced virgin-soil epidemics such as smallpox, influenza, and measles, resulting in catastrophic mortality rates that decimated between 80 to 90 percent of Indigenous populations and fundamentally disrupted regional political landscapes.
- The Shawnee military leader *Tecumseh* and his religious brother *Tenskwatawa* (*The Prophet*) forged a formidable pan-tribal confederacy headquartered at *Prophetstown* along the Wabash River, resisting western American expansionism before allying with Great Britain in the War of 1812.
- In anthropology and [[linguistics]], intensive field documentation of indigenous languages across the Pacific Northwest and Great Plains by *Franz Boas* and *Edward Sapir* directly challenged racialist hierarchies and spurred the development of structural linguistic theory and cultural relativism.
- Sophisticated agricultural techniques such as the *Three Sisters* companion planting system—intercropping maize, climbing beans, and winter squash—demonstrated advanced agronomic knowledge by maximizing soil nitrogen fixation, physical crop support, and ground moisture retention without draft animals or metal plows.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — sovereign tribal nations retain distinct domestic dependent status, governance rights, and reserved territories through federal treaties.
- [[colonialism]] — systematic European settlement, military incursions, and assimilation policies drove widespread land dispossession and demographic catastrophe.
- [[mississippi-river]] — vital interior waterway that sustained complex pre-Columbian riverine trade routes and Mississippian urban centers.
- [[american-revolution]] — continental military struggle that forced Indigenous confederacies to negotiate competing diplomatic alliances with opposing colonial powers.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — initial transoceanic voyages by European powers that permanently transformed global ecology, settlement boundaries, and Indigenous sovereignty.
- [[linguistics]] — hundreds of distinct indigenous language families provided essential empirical evidence for theories of linguistic relativity and phonological typology.
- [[canada]] — home to recognized *First Nations*, *Inuit*, and *Métis* peoples whose aboriginal title and territorial rights are enshrined in federal constitutional law.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial crown authority that negotiated early territorial boundaries, including the *Proclamation of 1763*, before ceding frontier lands.
