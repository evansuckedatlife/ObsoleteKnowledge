## summary

**Lisbon** is the capital and largest city of Portugal, strategically positioned on the Tagus River estuary where it meets the Atlantic Ocean. As a major medieval and early modern port, it became the launching point for Portuguese maritime expeditions that connected Europe to Asia, Africa, and the Americas. Its geography, culture, and historical significance make it an essential landmark for understanding European exploration and Atlantic commerce.

## you gotta know

- Located on the Tagus (Tejo) estuary, which provided a natural harbor and protected anchorage for oceangoing vessels; positioned perfectly at the convergence of river and sea.
- Rose to prominence in the 14th and 15th centuries as the center of Portuguese maritime expansion and colonial ambition; became Europe's gateway to the world.
- Home to the Portuguese monarchy and government; the palaces and archives reflect centuries of imperial power and cultural achievement.
- The Point of Departure for Vasco da Gama's voyage to India (1497), which established the sea route to Asia and transformed global trade forever.
- Devastated by a catastrophic earthquake in 1755 that destroyed much of the medieval city; rebuilt in a rational, grid-based plan by Marquês de Pombal.
- Connected the Atlantic world with Indian Ocean trade, making it one of the most important hubs of the Age of Exploration and a symbol of European ambition.
- Fado music, a melancholic national tradition, originated in Lisbon's working-class neighborhoods and remains central to Portuguese identity and cultural expression worldwide.
