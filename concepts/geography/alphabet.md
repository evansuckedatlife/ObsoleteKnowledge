## summary

An **alphabet** is a standardized set of basic written graphemes, traditionally called letters, each of which roughly represents a single phoneme of a spoken [[language]]. Originating in the *Bronze Age* [[middle-east]] with the Proto-Sinaitic script derived from Egyptian [[hieroglyphics]], it evolved into the Phoenician script, which ultimately gave rise to the Greek, Latin, Cyrillic, Aramaic, Hebrew, and Arabic writing systems. Unlike logographic scripts or syllabaries, the alphabet dramatically lowered the barrier to widespread literacy by requiring learners to master only a few dozen distinct characters rather than thousands of complex symbols.

## you gotta know

- The *Latin* script, originating in ancient [[rome]], is the most widely used phonemic writing system in the world today, having spread across [[europe]] and the globe through the expansion of the [[roman-empire]], Western [[christianity]], and modern European [[colonialism]].
- The ancient *Phoenicians* developed an influential twenty-two-letter consonantal script by the eleventh century BCE, utilizing a streamlined phonetic inventory that became the common ancestor of Greek, Latin, Aramaic, Hebrew, and Arabic writing.
- In [[ancient-greece]] during the eighth century BCE, scribes adapted Phoenician letters to systematically represent vowel sounds such as *alpha*, *epsilon*, and *omicron*, creating the first writing system in history to assign dedicated, independent letters to vowels.
- The word itself derives from the first two characters of the Greek script, *alpha* and *beta*, which themselves originated from the Semitic terms *aleph* (meaning *ox*) and *beth* (meaning *house*), reflecting the original pictographic shapes of the signs.
- In the classification of [[linguistics]], scholars distinguish true phonemic alphabets from *abjads*, which omit vowels, and *abugidas*, where vowels are indicated through diacritical marks attached to consonant letters, as seen across [[india]] and [[southeast-asia]].
- The earliest known alphabetic inscriptions are the *Proto-Sinaitic* carvings found at *Serabit el-Khadim* near [[egypt]], created around 1800 BCE by Canaanite miners who repurposed Egyptian [[hieroglyphics]] according to the acrophonic principle.
- The fifteenth-century Korean script *Hangul*, created under King *Sejong the Great*, is classified as a featural alphabet because the geometric shapes of its consonant letters intentionally depict the physical position of speech organs during articulation.

## connections

- [[linguistics]] — the academic discipline that classifies writing systems into phonetic alphabets, abjads, abugidas, and syllabaries based on phonological representation.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the Mediterranean civilization that adapted Phoenician consonantal signs to invent full vowel notation, creating the direct prototype for European alphabets.
- [[egypt]] — the civilization whose monumental hieroglyphs were reinterpreted by Semitic laborers to produce the initial Proto-Sinaitic consonantal letterforms.
- [[middle-east]] — the geographic region where Bronze Age Canaanite and Phoenician merchant communities pioneered simplified consonantal scripts to facilitate commerce.
- [[rome]] — the ancient Italian civilization that adapted the Etruscan alphabet into the Latin script, which became the standard orthography of the Western world.
- [[hieroglyphics]] — the ancient Egyptian writing system whose phonetic uniliteral signs directly inspired the acrophonic design of the earliest Semitic letters.
- [[kana]] — the Japanese moraic syllabic scripts that contrast with alphabetic systems by encoding whole syllables rather than individual segmental consonants and vowels.
- [[india]] — the birthplace of the ancient Brahmi script, which gave rise to the extensive family of South Asian abugidas that spread throughout [[southeast-asia]].
