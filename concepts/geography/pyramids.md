## summary

**The pyramids** are monumental stone tombs built by ancient Egyptian pharaohs, primarily during the Old Kingdom (2686–2181 BCE). The most iconic structures—the three pyramids of Giza (including the Great Pyramid of Khufu)—stand as humanity's oldest surviving major monuments and testify to extraordinary organizational, mathematical, and engineering achievement. Built to house pharaohs in the afterlife, the pyramids represent the apex of ancient Egyptian architecture and remain unparalleled in their scale and precision.

## you gotta know

- The Great Pyramid of Khufu (circa 2560 BCE) was originally 481 feet tall and contained 2.3 million limestone blocks, each weighing 2–15 tons; it remained the world's tallest structure for 3,800 years.
- Pyramids evolved over centuries: the Step Pyramid of Djoser (circa 2670 BCE) was the first monumental stone structure; the Bent Pyramid shows transitional design; finally the true geometric pyramid form emerged.
- The pyramids were built as tombs for pharaohs and their families, equipped with chambers, passages, and burial goods to sustain them in the afterlife according to Egyptian religious belief.
- Giza's three major pyramids (Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure) were aligned with precision to cardinal directions and celestial bodies, reflecting sophisticated astronomical knowledge.
- Construction required tens of thousands of workers (contrary to popular myth, not slaves) laboring over decades, mobilizing Egypt's entire economy and manpower.
- The pyramids originally had smooth white limestone casings that reflected the sun; interior passages and chambers contained hieroglyphic texts and artwork describing the pharaoh's journey to the afterlife.
- Over 130 pyramids exist along the Nile, though most are now ruined; the Giza pyramid complex remains the most magnificent and mysterious.
