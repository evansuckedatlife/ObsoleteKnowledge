## summary

**Greece** (officially the Hellenic Republic) is the modern nation-state occupying the eastern Mediterranean peninsula and its islands, occupying much of the same territory as *ancient Greece* but with radically different political structures and international status. From the fall of Constantinople in 1453 until independence in 1821, Greece was fragmented under Ottoman rule; the modern state emerged during the Greek War of Independence and has remained a crossroads between Eastern and Western political, religious, and cultural influences. Its strategic position in the eastern Mediterranean, the Balkans, and its legacy as the birthplace of Western philosophy and democracy make it geopolitically and culturally significant.

## you gotta know

- The modern Greek state gained independence from Ottoman rule in 1821–1829, established as a kingdom with European great-power intervention, marking one of the first successful anti-colonial national movements.
- Geography comprises the Peloponnesian peninsula, the Attica region (including Athens), and thousands of Aegean and Ionian islands, making maritime trade and defense historically central.
- The capital, *Athens*, was revived from Ottoman decline in the 19th century and became the symbolic anchor of modern Greek identity, celebrating its classical heritage as the birthplace of democracy and philosophy.
- Divided between Greek Orthodox Christianity (dominant) and Muslim Turkish minorities (especially after the 1920s population exchanges with Turkey), making religious identity a geo-political marker.
- The Greco-Turkish War (1919–1923) and subsequent population exchanges redrew Greece's ethnic and religious geography, displacing hundreds of thousands and cementing the "national" population.
- Strategically located at the Mediterranean's northeastern approach, with control over the Dardanelles (shared with Turkey) and access to the Black Sea, making it vital to NATO Mediterranean strategy.
