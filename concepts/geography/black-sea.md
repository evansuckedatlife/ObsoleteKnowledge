## summary

The **Black Sea** is a major body of water bordered by *Turkey*, *Bulgaria*, *Romania*, *Georgia*, and *Russia*, connected to the *Mediterranean Sea* through the narrow *Strait of Bosphorus*. Despite being surrounded by significant civilizations for millennia, the Black Sea remained economically and politically distinct from Mediterranean commerce, becoming a zone of imperial competition between the *Ottoman Empire*, Russian expansion, and European powers. Today it remains geopolitically volatile and economically important.

## you gotta know

- Named "Black" by ancient Greeks, possibly for the dark appearance of its waters during storms or perhaps due to early confusion with the Euxine (hospitable) sea.
- Surrounded entirely by land, making it a bounded sea rather than an ocean; the only external connection is the Bosphorus Strait, controlled historically by whoever held Istanbul.
- The *Danube River* (Europe's second-longest) drains into the Black Sea, creating a major economic corridor and the largest European river delta.
- Ancient Greek colonies dotted the Black Sea coast; control of the region's trade routes (particularly grain from the steppes) was strategically vital.
- For centuries the Ottoman Empire controlled the Black Sea effectively as an internal lake, blocking Russian access to Mediterranean commerce.
- Russian imperial expansion southward was motivated largely by competition for Black Sea access; the *Russo-Turkish Wars* (18th–19th centuries) reflected this struggle.
- The region's resources (oil, natural gas, fisheries) and strategic location continue to make it contested; the Crimea annexation (2014) and ongoing Ukraine conflict reflect these tensions.
- Mediterranean exchange and development long outpaced the Black Sea; it remained less economically developed despite substantial economic and strategic potential, even into the modern era.
