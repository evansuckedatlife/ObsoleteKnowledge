## summary

**Niagara Falls** is a cascade of three massive waterfalls on the Niagara River, which forms the border between New York and Ontario. Straddling the Canada-U.S. boundary, the falls represent one of North America's most recognizable natural wonders and a crucial source of hydroelectric power. The thundering cataract has inspired awe and daring feats for centuries, symbolizing both sublime nature and human ingenuity.

## you gotta know

- Niagara Falls drops approximately 188 feet (57 meters) from Lake Erie into the Niagara River, which flows into Lake Ontario.
- The falls consist of three separate cascades: Horseshoe Falls (Canadian side), American Falls, and Bridal Veil Falls, with the Horseshoe Falls being the most powerful.
- Over 750,000 gallons of water per second flow over the falls, making it one of the fastest-moving sections of the Niagara River.
- The falls were formed about 10,000 years ago at the end of the last ice age when meltwater carved the Niagara Gorge.
- First noted by European explorers in the 1600s, the falls quickly became famous as a natural wonder; daredevils have attempted stunts over the falls since the 1800s.
- Hydroelectric power stations harness the falls' energy; today the falls supply electricity to millions in both Canada and the United States.
- Niagara Falls remains a major tourist destination attracting millions annually, with illuminated nighttime displays adding to its spectacle.

## connections

- [[five-great-lakes]] — Niagara Falls connects Lake Erie to Lake Ontario, joining two of the Great Lakes.
- [[st-lawrence-river]] — the Niagara River flows from the falls into Lake Ontario, which drains via the St. Lawrence to the Atlantic.
- [[canada]] — the Canadian side hosts the iconic Horseshoe Falls and the major tourist infrastructure.
- [[united-states]] — New York's border with Canada runs through the falls; American Falls are on the U.S. side.
- [[new-york-city]] — downriver from Niagara, New York City benefits from the water system and trade routes created by the falls.
