## summary

**Angola**, officially the **Republic of Angola**, is a southwest African nation along the [[atlantic-ocean]], bordered by the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Zambia, and Namibia, with the oil-rich exclave of *Cabinda* situated to the north. Gaining independence in 1975 after centuries of Portuguese rule shaped by [[colonialism]], the country immediately plunged into a protracted civil war that evolved into a prominent proxy battleground of the [[cold-war]]. Today, the country is known for its immense petroleum and diamond wealth, its historic Atlantic port capital of *Luanda*, and its distinct Luso-African cultural heritage.

## you gotta know

- The capital and largest city is *Luanda*, a historic Atlantic port founded in 1576 by *Paulo Dias de Novais*, which grew into an administrative center of the transatlantic slave trade and later emerged as an expensive epicenter for international petroleum business.
- Gained independence from Portugal in November 1975 following the Carnation Revolution in Lisbon, concluding the Angolan War of Independence under the leadership of the *People's Movement for the Liberation of Angola* (*MPLA*) led by poet-statesman *Agostinho Neto*.
- The subsequent Angolan Civil War (1975–2002) became a defining [[cold-war]] proxy conflict, aligning the [[soviet-union]] and tens of thousands of Cuban expeditionary troops behind the *MPLA* against *Jonas Savimbi*'s *UNITA*, which received clandestine military backing from the [[united-states]] and apartheid-era [[south-africa]].
- The exclave of *Cabinda*, isolated from the mainland by the Democratic Republic of the Congo and the mouth of the *Congo River*, accounts for the majority of the nation's offshore crude oil output and has experienced prolonged separatist tensions led by the *Front for the Liberation of the Enclave of Cabinda* (*FLEC*).
- In the seventeenth century, Queen *Nzinga Mbande* of the *Ndongo* and *Matamba* kingdoms mounted legendary resistance against Portuguese colonial subjugation and human trafficking, employing sophisticated diplomatic maneuvering and military alliances.
- Southern coastal regions encompass the arid northern reaches of the [[namib-desert]] in the province of *Namibe*, notable for endemic desert wildlife and the ancient, two-leaved gymnosperm *Welwitschia mirabilis*.
- The high central elevation of the *Bié Plateau* (*Planalto Central*) forms an essential hydrological heartland, giving rise to major rivers such as the *Kwanza* and the *Cubango*, the latter flowing southeast to feed the inland *Okavango Delta*.
- The national currency is the *kwanza*, named after the *Kwanza River*, which also lent its name to two north-central provinces and is harnessed for hydroelectric power at the *Capanda Dam*.

## connections

- [[atlantic-ocean]] — bounds the country's entire western perimeter, anchoring key coastal settlements and extensive deepwater oil extraction platforms.
- [[colonialism]] — Portuguese rule established Portuguese as the official language, transformed the coastal economy around the slave trade, and left deep institutional legacies.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical rivalry between superpowers that dramatically prolonged and militarized the domestic civil war between rival liberation factions.
- [[soviet-union]] — supplied fighter aircraft, heavy artillery, and strategic logistics to the ruling *MPLA* alongside massive troop deployments from Cuba.
- [[united-states]] — covertly funded and equipped the anti-communist *UNITA* movement to counter Soviet and Cuban influence in southern Africa.
- [[south-africa]] — launched extensive military interventions into southern Angola during the Border War, clashing directly with Angolan and Cuban forces at the *Battle of Cuito Cuanavale*.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — brought Portuguese navigator *Diogo Cão* to the Angolan coast in 1482, initiating early European contact with the historic *Kingdom of Kongo*.
