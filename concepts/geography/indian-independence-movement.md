## summary

The **Indian Independence Movement** was a centuries-long struggle by Indians against British colonial rule, culminating in independence in 1947. Led by political organizations like the Indian National Congress and figures like Mohandas *Gandhi*, the movement employed both constitutional reform and mass civil disobedience, ultimately forcing the British Empire to relinquish control. India's independence became a model for decolonization movements worldwide and demonstrated the power of nonviolent resistance against imperial power.

## you gotta know

- British colonial rule over India began in the 18th century through the East India Company's military conquest and commercial monopoly, and was formalized under Crown rule after the Indian Rebellion (1857–1858) established the British Raj as direct imperial governance.
- The Indian National Congress, founded in 1885 by educated Indian elites, began as a constitutional reform movement seeking gradual Indian participation in government but evolved into an independence movement after World War I when British promises remained unfulfilled.
- Mohandas Gandhi emerged as the movement's foremost leader during the 1920s, championing Satyagraha (nonviolent resistance based on truth-force), boycotts of British goods, and mass civil disobedience campaigns that mobilized millions and changed anti-colonial strategy globally.
- Major campaigns included the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922), the Salt March (1930) protesting the British salt monopoly, and the Quit India Movement (1942) demanding immediate British withdrawal; each campaign faced British repression but grew in scope and popular participation.
- The independence struggle was not monolithic; Hindu-Muslim tensions and communal violence intensified during the 1940s, leading to the traumatic Partition of India in 1947 that created Pakistan, displacing millions and killing hundreds of thousands in communal riots.
- Indian independence in 1947 inspired and accelerated decolonization movements across Africa, Asia, and the Caribbean, establishing a template for anti-imperial resistance through nonviolence and popular mobilization, and establishing India as a major postcolonial power and leader of the Non-Aligned Movement.
- Post-independence India faced immense challenges: nation-building, Hindu-Muslim coexistence after Partition, integration of princely states, and transformation of a feudal agrarian society into a modern nation-state and democracy.
