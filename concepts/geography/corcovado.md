## summary

**Corcovado** is a granite peak rising 710 metres above the coastal city of Rio de Janeiro in southeastern Brazil. Located within the protected Tijuca National Park, the peak is celebrated worldwide for hosting the monumental Art Deco statue *Christ the Redeemer* upon its summit. Looming over Guanabara Bay and the South Atlantic Ocean, it stands as an internationally recognized symbol of Brazilian geography and cultural identity.

## you gotta know

- Takes its name from the Portuguese word for "hunchback", describing the distinctive curved profile of the narrow granite monolith when viewed from sea level.
- Crowning the peak is the thirty-metre-tall statue *Christ the Redeemer* (*Cristo Redentor*), designed by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa and sculpted by French-Polish artist Paul Landowski.
- Located within Tijuca National Park, considered one of the largest urban rainforests in the world, reforested in the nineteenth century to protect municipal water supplies.
- Accessed by the historic Corcovado Rack Railway (*Trem do Corcovado*), opened in 1884 by Emperor Pedro II, which utilized an electric cog-wheel system pre-dating the monument itself.
- Built using reinforced concrete surfaced with thousands of mosaic soapstone tiles chosen for their durability, elegance, and resistance to harsh maritime weather.
- Formed as an ancient coastal inselberg comprising Neoproterozoic granite and gneiss that resisted regional erosion along the Brazilian Atlantic Shield.
- Provides sweeping panoramic vistas of iconic landmarks including Sugarloaf Mountain, Copacabana and Ipanema beaches, the Maracanã Stadium, and Guanabara Bay.
