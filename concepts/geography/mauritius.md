## summary

**Mauritius**, officially known as the **Republic of Mauritius**, is a volcanic island nation situated in the southwestern Indian Ocean approximately five hundred miles east of Madagascar, forming part of the Mascarene Archipelago alongside Réunion and Rodrigues. Celebrated for its unique biodiversity, thriving multicultural society, and sustained political stability, the island is world-famous as the only recorded habitat of the extinct *dodo*. Ruled successively by the Dutch, the French under the name **Isle de France**, and the British, Mauritius functioned for centuries as a vital oceanic crossroads linking European trade between the *[[atlantic-ocean]]* and the Asian subcontinent.

## you gotta know

- Celebrated as the exclusive native habitat of the *dodo* (*Raphus cucullatus*), a large flightless pigeon species driven to rapid extinction in the late seventeenth century due to hunting, deforestation, and introduced invasive predators.
- Named *Mauritius* in 1598 by Dutch sailors under Admiral *Wybrand van Warwyck* in tribute to Prince *Maurice of Nassau*, the Stadtholder of the Dutch Republic.
- Colonized by France in 1715 as *Isle de France*, flourishing under Governor *Bertrand-François Mahé de Labourdonnais*, who established the port city of *Port Louis* as an essential naval outpost and sugar-producing center.
- Served as a notorious haven for French privateers, notably *Robert Surcouf*, who raided British East India Company vessels until a British amphibious expedition captured the island in 1810 during the Napoleonic Wars.
- Ceded formally to the *[[british-empire]]* under the Treaty of Paris in 1814, with Britain pledging to respect the French language, the legal system based on the *Napoleonic Code*, and the Roman Catholic religion.
- Transformed into the testing ground for the British empire's *"Great Experiment"* after the abolition of slavery in 1835, bringing nearly half a million indentured laborers from *[[india]]* through the historic immigration depot at *Aapravasi Ghat*.
- Frequented by American merchantmen sailing from ports in *[[massachusetts-bay]]*, who made Port Louis a key provisioning and transshipment port while trading spices, textiles, and porcelain throughout the Indian Ocean.
- Achieved national independence from Britain in 1968 under the leadership of Sir *Seewoosagur Ramgoolam*, later transitioning peacefully to a republic in 1992 while continuing to press sovereignty claims over the *Chagos Archipelago*.

## connections

- `[[colonialism]]` — the international system of European conquest, plantation monoculture, and coerced migration that formed the island's historical trajectory.
- `[[british-empire]]` — the global imperial power that governed Mauritius from 1810 to 1968 and introduced the massive Indian indentured labor program.
- `[[france]]` — the colonial power that developed the island's sugar infrastructure and established its enduring French linguistic and cultural character.
- `[[india]]` — the subcontinent from which the majority of indentured workers migrated, giving Mauritius its distinctive Indo-Mauritian cultural and demographic majority.
- `[[atlantic-ocean]]` — the oceanic domain through which ships rounded the Cape of Good Hope before crossing into the Mauritian sea lanes.
- `[[massachusetts-bay]]` — the New England maritime hub whose merchant vessels called regularly at Port Louis during long voyages to East Asian markets.
