## summary

**Alaska** is the largest state in the *United States* by area, located at the northwestern tip of North America, adjoining Canada and separated from Russia by the narrow Bering Strait. Acquired by the U.S. in 1867 from *Russian America*, it encompasses arctic tundra, subarctic forests, and some of the continent's most dramatic peaks. Alaska's vast wilderness and strategic position have made it economically and geopolitically significant since American settlement.

## you gotta know

- The largest U.S. state by area (665,000 square miles) but among the least densely populated, with most residents concentrated in Anchorage and coastal towns.
- Purchased from Russia in 1867 for $7.2 million in a deal secretary of state William Seward championed as visionary, though it was initially derided as "Seward's Folly."
- Contains *Denali*, North America's tallest peak at 20,310 feet, a defining geographic symbol of American wilderness.
- Home to the Yukon River, one of North America's longest rivers, flowing through the interior to the Bering Sea.
- Granted statehood in 1959, making it the 49th state; strategically positioned for Cold War oversight of Soviet activities across the Bering Strait.
- Discovered massive oil reserves at Prudhoe Bay in 1968, leading to the construction of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline and economic transformation.
- Contains vast permafrost regions and is warming faster than most continental U.S. areas, making it a focal point for climate change research.
- Named from the Aleut word "Alaxsxaq," meaning "the great land."
- The northernmost and westernmost state; the Aleutian Islands extend toward Russian territory; some islands are among the westernmost U.S. points.
