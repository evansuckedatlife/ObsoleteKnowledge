## summary

**Catholicism** is the largest branch of *Christianity*, centered on the authority of the *Pope* and the traditions of the *Roman* Church. Comprising over a billion adherents worldwide, it emphasizes sacramental worship, apostolic succession, and the communion of saints. Its institutions—monasteries, cathedrals, universities—shaped European civilization for two millennia, and its teachings remain foundational to Western philosophy and ethics.

## you gotta know

- The term derives from the Greek word for "universal"; Catholicism claims to be the oldest continuous Christian tradition, tracing authority back to Peter and the Apostles.
- The *Pope* serves as the head of the Church, residing in the *Vatican*, an independent city-state within *Rome*.
- *Papal authority* is asserted over all Catholics worldwide, a point of major contention with *Protestant* churches and Eastern Orthodox Christianity.
- Central practices include the seven sacraments (Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, Matrimony) and veneration of Mary and saints.
- The *Counter-Reformation* (16th–17th centuries) was the Catholic Church's response to Protestant critique, emphasizing renewed piety and institutional reform.
- Catholic teaching informed medieval scholasticism, the philosophy of *Thomas Aquinas*, and the moral frameworks underlying Western law and governance.
- The Church owns vast land, art collections, and educational institutions; *Michelangelo*'s *Sistine Chapel* and *St. Peter's Basilica* are among its supreme cultural achievements.
- Modern Catholicism grapples with declining membership in Europe, scandals within hierarchy, and evolving stances on contraception, divorce, and LGBTQ+ inclusion.
- The cult of saints—the veneration and invocation of canonized figures as intercessors—is central to Catholic devotion and distinguishes it sharply from Protestantism, which rejects such intermediaries.
