## summary

**Carnival** is an exuberant festive season celebrated annually across Catholic and Western Christian communities immediately prior to the liturgical season of *Lent*. Historically evolving from medieval European street masquerades into lavish public spectacles, it is characterized by elaborate costumed parades, dancing, satirical street theater, and communal indulgence. While rooted in ancient rituals of temporary status inversion, its most internationally renowned expressions developed in the Americas, particularly throughout [[south-america]], through dynamic synthesis with African diaspora traditions.

## you gotta know

- Climax occurs on *Shrove Tuesday*—widely celebrated in Francophone and American traditions as **Mardi Gras** ("Fat Tuesday")—the final day of feasting before *Ash Wednesday*.
- Etymologically derived from the late Latin *carne vale*, traditionally interpreted as "farewell to meat," signifying the impending forty-day dietary abstinence of Lent.
- The most celebrated global spectacle takes place in *Rio de Janeiro*, Brazil, where elite samba schools compete in the purpose-built *Sambadrome Marquês de Sapucaí*.
- Italian renaissance centers like Venice popularized the tradition of elegant, porcelain or leather Venetian masks, allowing citizens to disguise identity, gender, and social rank in playful subversion.
- In the Caribbean, notably *Trinidad and Tobago*, celebrations incorporate *Canboulay* traditions, calypso competitions, and steelband percussion forged out of resistance to enslavement.
- Russian and Eastern Slavic cultures observe a related pre-Lenten folk holiday known as *Maslenitsa* ("Butter Week"), centered on eating round crepes (*blini*) symbolizing the return of the sun.
- Sociologist *Mikhail Bakhtin* famously theorized the festival in *Rabelais and His World*, developing the concept of the *carnivalesque* to describe licensed, joyous subversions of social hierarchy.

## connections

- [[south-america]] — host to the planet's largest and most famous annual celebrations, most notably in Brazil and Colombia.
- [[christianity]] — religious calendar framework establishing the penitential season of Lent that necessitates the pre-Lenten festival.
- [[italy]] — cradle of Renaissance masquerade traditions, home to the iconic masked balls and gondola pageants of Venice.
- [[colonialism]] — Iberian imperial conquest transported Mediterranean pre-Lenten festivals into colonies across the Atlantic basin.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — triangular slave trade transported millions of enslaved Africans whose music transformed American festive celebrations.
- [[france]] — source of regional festive customs that seeded major North American celebrations like New Orleans Mardi Gras.
- [[rome]] — ancient predecessor festivals like *Saturnalia* and *Lupercalia* established classical precedents for costumed status reversals.
