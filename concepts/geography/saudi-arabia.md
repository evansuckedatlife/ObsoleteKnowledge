# Saudi Arabia

## summary

**Saudi Arabia** is a vast, desert-dominated kingdom occupying most of the *Arabian Peninsula* in western Asia, home to Islam's two holiest cities (*Mecca* and *Medina*) and the planet's second-largest proven oil reserves. Founded in 1932 through the consolidation of tribal territories by *Abd al-Aziz ibn Saud*, the state derives its legitimacy from Wahhabist Islam and its wealth from petroleum exports, making it a dominant regional power and vital global actor despite its modest population. The kingdom's influence on Islamic theology, regional geopolitics, and world energy markets far exceeds its size.

## you gotta know

- Saudi Arabia occupies the central and eastern portions of the Arabian Peninsula, bordered by *Jordan*, *Iraq*, and *Kuwait* to the north, the *Persian Gulf* and *Qatar* to the east, the *United Arab Emirates* and *Oman* to the southeast, and the *Red Sea* to the west.
- The vast *Rub' al-Khali* (Empty Quarter), one of the world's largest sand deserts, covers much of the southern interior; the landscape is predominantly arid, with mountainous regions along the Red Sea coast.
- *Mecca*, in western Saudi Arabia, is the birthplace of the *Prophet Muhammad* (c. 570 CE) and Islam's holiest site; Muslims are religiously obligated to perform the *Hajj* (pilgrimage) to Mecca at least once in their lifetime.
- *Medina*, Saudi Arabia's second-holiest city, is where Muhammad established the first Islamic community and mosque; it became the spiritual and administrative center of early Islam.
- Saudi Arabia's oil reserves were discovered in 1938; the kingdom became the world's leading petroleum exporter in the mid-20th century and founded the *OPEC* (Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries) in 1960 to control global oil prices.
- *King Abdulaziz Ibn Saud* (r. 1932–1953) unified the Arabian Peninsula through military conquest and political alliances, establishing the absolute monarchy that persists today under the *House of Saud*.
- The kingdom's official religious ideology is *Wahhabism*, a puritanical Islamic reformation originating in 18th-century Arabia; it has influenced Islamic movements globally and defines Saudi Arabia's domestic and foreign policy.
- Saudi Arabia remains a critical U.S. ally in the Middle East, though its human-rights record, strict religious governance, and regional interventions (especially in Yemen and against Iran) remain internationally controversial.
