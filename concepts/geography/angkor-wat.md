## summary

**Angkor Wat** is a vast twelfth-century temple complex located in northwestern Cambodia within [[southeast-asia]], originally commissioned by King *Suryavarman II* as the state temple and eventual funerary monument of the *Khmer Empire*. First dedicated to the Hindu preserver deity *Vishnu*, the sanctuary represented an intentional shift from traditional royal devotion to *Shiva* before gradually transitioning into an enduring Theravada Buddhist pilgrimage center over subsequent centuries. Celebrated for its harmonious classical Khmer architecture, soaring lotus-bud towers, and miles of intricate narrative bas-reliefs, it stands as the largest religious monument in the world and remains the preeminent national symbol of the Cambodian people.

## you gotta know

- It appears prominently on the national flag of Cambodia, making the country one of the few sovereign nations in the world to feature an identifiable architectural monument on its official state banner.
- Built as a physical microcosm of Hindu cosmology, the central temple mountain replicates the sacred *Mount Meru*, enclosed by an expansive rectangular moat representing the cosmic ocean and crowned by five towers shaped like lotus blossoms.
- Unlike the overwhelming majority of classical Khmer temples that face east toward the morning sun, this complex uniquely opens toward the west, an orientation associated with sunset, funerary ceremonies, and the patron god *Vishnu*.
- The inner gallery walls house extensive stone bas-relief friezes, most famously illustrating the *Churning of the Ocean of Milk* (*Samudra Manthan*), in which dozens of *asuras* and *devas* pull the serpent king *Vasuki* around *Mount Mandara* to produce the nectar of immortality.
- Commissioned by King *Suryavarman II* during his reign from 1113 to roughly 1150 in the capital of *Yasodharapura*, the vast complex required millions of sandstone blocks transported via extensive canal networks from the distant quarries of *Mount Kulen*.
- Following regional conflicts with the *Chams* and the rise of King *Jayavarman VII*, who constructed the nearby Buddhist capital of *Angkor Thom*, the site adapted into an active Theravada Buddhist sanctuary, ensuring continuous maintenance that preserved it from total overgrowth.
- Although local monks and regional pilgrims never completely abandoned the sacred grounds, the travel writings of nineteenth-century French naturalist *Henri Mouhot* popularized the ruins across the Western world, prompting extensive excavation and restoration led by [[france]].
- Modern airborne LiDAR surveys demonstrate that the temple served as the epicenter of a massive, interconnected hydraulic settlement whose sophisticated network of reservoirs (*barays*), canals, and embankments ultimately suffered systemic collapse due to devastating megadroughts and monsoonal flooding.

## connections

- [[southeast-asia]] — geographic and cultural zone where the Khmer civilization flourished alongside neighboring maritime and riverine kingdoms.
- [[india]] — cultural source of the Sanskrit literary traditions, architectural treatises, and Hindu-Buddhist cosmologies adapted by Khmer builders.
- [[france]] — European power that established a colonial protectorate over Cambodia and organized long-term archaeological restorations through the *École française d'Extrême-Orient*.
- [[china]] — origin of essential contemporary historical accounts of Khmer court life, society, and religious ritual recorded by thirteenth-century envoy *Zhou Daguan*.
- [[mekong-river]] — vital regional waterway whose annual hydrological cycle feeds the *Tonlé Sap* system, providing the agricultural wealth that sustained Angkor.
- [[colonialism]] — historical phenomenon under which Western empires documented, managed, and extracted heritage artifacts from indigenous Southeast Asian archaeological sites.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict whose Japanese occupation of Indochina catalyzed political upheavals that permanently reshaped twentieth-century Cambodia.
