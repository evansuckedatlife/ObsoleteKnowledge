## summary

**Greenock** is a historic port town and administrative centre of Inverclyde in western [[scotland]], situated on the south bank of the Firth of Clyde where the river widens into the sea. Best known worldwide as the birthplace of mechanical engineer and inventor *James Watt*, the town evolved from a modest fishing village into an industrial titan during the *Scottish Enlightenment* and subsequent manufacturing expansion. As a vital transatlantic maritime hub, it spearheaded Scotland's shipbuilding, sugar refining, and merchant trade across the wider networks of the *British Empire*.

## you gotta know

- Birthplace of inventor *James Watt* in 1736, whose revolutionary steam engine improvements propelled the global [[industrial-revolution]].
- Situated on the Firth of Clyde west of Glasgow, serving historically as deep-water port access for large ocean-going vessels unable to navigate upriver.
- Rose to prominence as a major centre for marine engineering and naval shipbuilding, constructing merchant steamers and warships at yards such as *Scotts Shipbuilding and Engineering Company*.
- Known as the sugar capital of [[scotland]] in the nineteenth century, operating numerous refineries processing raw cane imported from Caribbean plantations.
- Served as a strategic naval base for the Royal Navy and Allied forces during [[world-war-ii]], serving as an escort base for the Western Approaches command.
- Endured severe damage during the *Greenock Blitz* of May 1941, when the German Luftwaffe launched devastating bombing runs against the Clyde shipyards.
- Commemorates its most famous son through local landmarks, including the *Watt Library*, *James Watt College*, and a prominent statue on Cathcart Square.
