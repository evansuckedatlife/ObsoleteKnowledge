## summary

The **Confederation Bridge** is a curved, eight-mile post-tensioned concrete box girder bridge that spans the ice-covered waters of the Abegweit Passage in the Northumberland Strait. Opened in 1997, it connects the Canadian provinces of Prince Edward Island and New Brunswick, fulfilling an enduring constitutional pledge made during Canadian Confederation. It stands as one of the world's longest bridges constructed over water that regularly freezes in winter, representing an iconic feat of modern cold-ocean engineering.

## you gotta know

- Spans twelve point nine kilometres across the Abegweit Passage of the Northumberland Strait, directly linking Borden-Carleton, Prince Edward Island, to Cape Jourimain, New Brunswick.
- Fulfilled the historic terms under which Prince Edward Island joined [[canada]] in 1873, which constitutionally guaranteed "efficient steam service" for continuous communication and transport with the mainland.
- Constructed between 1993 and 1997 as a public-private partnership by the Strait Crossing Development Inc. consortium, replacing the year-round marine ferry service operated by Marine Atlantic.
- Engineered specifically to withstand severe sea ice loads using specialized conical ice-shields positioned around its concrete piers that fracture encroaching drifting ice sheets in flexure rather than crushing.
- Nicknamed the "Fixed Link" by residents and planners throughout decades of intense political debate, plebiscites, and environmental impact assessments prior to its official christening.
- Carries two lanes of the Trans-Canada Highway across forty-four main marine spans, incorporating a high navigation span that provides a sixty-metre vertical clearance for commercial maritime traffic.
- Collects a toll exclusively from traffic departing Prince Edward Island, allowing free passage onto the island while funding private debt amortization before returning to federal ownership.
