## summary

The **White House** (originally called the **Executive Mansion**) is the official residence and principal workplace of the President of the United States, located in Washington, DC at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue. Constructed between 1792 and 1800 as the residence of President *George Washington*, it has served as home to every subsequent president and has evolved into one of the world's most recognizable symbols of executive power and democratic governance. Beyond its ceremonial role, the White House houses the president's administrative offices, the Oval Office, and complex state functions—making it both a residence and the operational center of American government.

## you gotta know

- The White House was designed by Irish-born architect James Hoban in the neoclassical style; its cornerstone was laid in 1792, making it contemporary with the early republic's founding institutions and inspired by Irish classical architecture.
- The building's distinctive white exterior came later—originally it was unpainted or cream-colored sandstone, but it was painted white to protect the porous stone from weather deterioration after the British burned it during the War of 1812.
- President Harry Truman undertook a massive reconstruction and modernization (1949–1952) after the structure was deemed unsafe and unstable; the renovation preserved the iconic exterior walls while completely rebuilding the interior with modern infrastructure and structural supports.
- The White House contains 132 rooms (6 floors including basement levels), 35 bathrooms, 412 doors, 147 windows, and 28 fireplaces; it functions simultaneously as a historical museum, a working office building, and a residence housing the president's family.
- State dinners, press conferences, diplomatic receptions, and ceremonial events held in the White House serve critical functions in American foreign policy and domestic politics; the building is both stage and symbol of American democratic and executive authority.
- The White House grounds include the Oval Office (the president's personal office), the Situation Room (for national security briefings), the Rose Garden, the South Lawn, and adjacent government buildings housing executive staff, reflecting the concentration of presidential power and administration.
- The White House has been a site of historic moments, controversies, and scandals throughout American history, from Lincoln's assassination to Watergate to modern political crises, making it both a symbol of democratic ideals and a stage for their violation.
