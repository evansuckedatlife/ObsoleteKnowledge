## summary

**Modernity** is the defining historical and geographical epoch characterized by the widespread transition from traditional agrarian feudalism to an industrialized, urbanized global society governed by secular institutions and market capitalism. Emerging across post-Renaissance [[europe]] and accelerated by the intellectual shifts of the [[enlightenment]] and the technological power of the [[industrial-revolution]], this condition radically reorganized spatial landscapes into dense metropolitan centers, international shipping corridors, and interconnected railway networks. Beyond its material structures, it encompasses a profound transformation in human consciousness, marked by the rise of mass media, perpetual technological acceleration, and the ongoing struggle between rational progress and social alienation. Understanding this epoch is essential for tracing how contemporary landscapes, political structures, and artistic traditions took shape over the past two centuries.

## you gotta know

- Driven by the technological innovations of the [[industrial-revolution]] in [[england]], replacing seasonal agrarian labor with mechanized factory production, wage labor, and synchronized clock-time schedules.
- Precipitated massive rural-to-urban migrations that established sprawling metropolises like [[new-york-city]], *London*, and *Paris*, which reshaped human geography through electric streetlights, underground transit systems, and towering corporate skyscrapers.
- Cultivated a dynamic consumer culture across the [[united-states]] during the 1920s, where mass production, radio broadcasting, advertising, and the syncopated energy of [[jazz]] established commercial entertainment as a central facet of modern social life.
- Reconfigured global geography by linking imperial centers to distant resource frontiers, utilizing steamship lines across the [[atlantic-ocean]] and telegraph cables to bind territories of the [[british-empire]] into unified international commodity markets.
- Sparked major counter-cultural and artistic movements, beginning with the fleeting outdoor urban scenes of [[impressionism]] and culminating in the radical formal experimentation of [[modernism]], both striving to capture the sensory overload of rapid change.
- Reached a devastating philosophical and geopolitical crisis during the mechanized trench warfare of [[world-war-i]] and [[world-war-ii]], shattering nineteenth-century beliefs in rational progress through industrial-scale destruction.
- Theorized by sociologists and geographers through *Max Weber*'s critique of bureaucratic *disenchantment* and *David Harvey*'s spatial model of *time-space compression*, which explains how modern transit and telecommunications drastically shrink physical distance and alter perceptions of space.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — the mechanical and economic transformation that shifted humanity from agrarian subsistence to urbanized, factory-driven manufacturing.
- [[new-york-city]] — the preeminent global metropolis whose vertical skyline, extensive transit systems, and financial power embody the spatial triumph of modern capitalism.
- [[modernism]] — the interdisciplinary artistic and literary movement that discarded classical forms to reflect the speed, anxiety, and psychological fragmentation of the era.
- [[enlightenment]] — the foundational intellectual movement that championed scientific empiricism, individual liberties, and secular governance as the ideological bedrock of modern society.
- [[jazz]] — the innovative, African American-born musical genre whose fast tempos and improvisation became the defining sound of 1920s urban modernity.
- [[world-war-i]] — the catastrophic total war that deployed modern industrial technology to shatter imperial Europe and undermine faith in human progress.
- [[impressionism]] — the groundbreaking nineteenth-century French painting style that documented the new leisure spaces, train stations, and boulevards of transformed urban environments.
- [[existentialism]] — the postwar philosophical school that probed human alienation, responsibility, and meaning within an increasingly secular and mechanistic world.
