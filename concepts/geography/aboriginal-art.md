# Aboriginal art

## summary

**Aboriginal art** encompasses the visual traditions of Australia's Indigenous peoples, ranging from ancient rock art and ground paintings to contemporary dot painting on canvas. Rooted in *Dreamtime* narratives and sacred geography, these works encode creation stories, songlines, and spiritual knowledge in symbolic form. Modern Aboriginal art, especially dot painting pioneered in the 1970s, has achieved international recognition while preserving ancient cosmological knowledge.

## you gotta know

- Rock art sites across *Australia* demonstrate continuous cultural expression spanning tens of thousands of years, making them among the world's oldest artistic traditions.
- *Dot painting* emerged in Papunya in the 1970s as a way for Aboriginal artists to represent Dreamtime narratives and sacred sites using acrylic dots on canvas, gaining global prominence.
- Songlines (or Dreaming tracks) are invisible pathways crisscrossing the continent; Aboriginal art visually encodes these routes and the stories they carry.
- Aboriginal artists traditionally used natural pigments (ochre, charcoal, clay) applied to rock, ground, and bodies; canvas and acrylic were adopted strategically to preserve and sell sacred knowledge.
- Each design and color holds specific meanings tied to place, family, and spiritual authority; abstract-looking patterns actually constitute a precise cartography and genealogy.
- Contemporary Aboriginal artists like *Emily Kame Kngwarreye* and *Rover Thomas* have become celebrated internationally, commanding high prices while confronting issues of cultural appropriation and repatriation.
- Aboriginal art is inseparable from land rights: artistic expression is simultaneously a claim to territory and custodianship.
