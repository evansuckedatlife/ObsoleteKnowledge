## summary

**New Brunswick** (French: **Nouveau-Brunswick**) is one of the three Maritime provinces of eastern *[[canada]]* and the only province in the Canadian federation that is constitutionally bilingual in English and French. Bordering the *[[united-states]]* state of Maine to the west, Quebec to the north, and Nova Scotia across the Chignecto Isthmus to the southeast, the province is renowned for the extraordinary marine dynamics of the Bay of Fundy, which records the highest tidal range on Earth. Anchored by the cultural heritage of the Acadians and the historic settlements along the Saint John River, the province played a foundational role in northern North American history as one of the four original partners entering Confederation in 1867.

## you gotta know

- The Bay of Fundy, forming the southern coast of the province, experiences the highest tides in the world, with water levels fluctuating up to sixteen metres twice daily; famous landmarks shaped by these dramatic waters include the sea-carved sandstone formations of the Hopewell Rocks (popularly called the "Flowerpots") and the reversing tidal bore on the Petitcodiac River near Moncton.
- It is the only Canadian province with official constitutional bilingualism, guaranteed under Section 16 of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, recognizing equal institutional status for the Anglophone majority and the Francophone Acadian minority who predominantly inhabit the northern and eastern shores.
- Fredericton serves as the provincial capital and legislative center on the Saint John River, while Moncton stands as the province's largest metropolitan area and bilingual commercial crossroads, and Saint John is the oldest incorporated city in *[[canada]]*, noted for the Reversing Falls where tidal forces push seawater backwards upriver.
- Entered Canadian Confederation on July 1, 1867, under the leadership of Premier Samuel Leonard Tilley—who famously suggested the name "Dominion of *[[canada]]*" from Psalm 72:8—joining Nova Scotia, Ontario, and Quebec as the four founding provinces of the new nation under the *[[british-empire]]*.
- Originally inhabited by the Algonquian-speaking Mi'kmaq and Wolastoqiyik (Maliseet) nations, the territory became part of French Acadia before British conquest during the Seven Years' War precipitated the tragic Great Expulsion (*Grand Dérangement*) in 1755, after which thousands of United Empire Loyalists fled here following the *[[american-revolution]]*.
- Connected to neighbouring Prince Edward Island across the Northumberland Strait by the thirteen-kilometre Confederation Bridge, an engineering marvel completed in 1997 that ranks as one of the longest continuous marine-span bridges globally.
- Dominated economically and industrially by the sprawling conglomerate of the billionaire Irving family (J.D. Irving), whose extensive private holdings span forestry, commercial shipping, news media, and the Saint John Oil Refinery, the largest refinery in *[[canada]]*.

## connections

- [[canada]] — one of the four original provinces that formed the dominion at Confederation in 1867.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — bordered by major tidal waters including the Bay of Fundy and the Gulf of Saint Lawrence.
- [[newfoundland-and-labrador]] — fellow Atlantic Canadian province situated across the Gulf of Saint Lawrence.
- [[british-empire]] — former British crown colony settled heavily by loyalists fleeing the thirteen American colonies.
- [[colonialism]] — site of intense Anglo-French imperial rivalry and the forced expulsion of the Acadian population.
- [[france]] — European power that established early seventeenth-century settlements under Samuel de Champlain in Acadia.
- [[linguistics]] — legally protected home of distinct regional dialects such as Chiac, an Acadian French-English contact variety.
