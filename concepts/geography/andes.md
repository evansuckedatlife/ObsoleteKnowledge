## summary

**The Andes** is the world's longest continental mountain range, spanning approximately 7,000 kilometers along the western edge of South America through seven countries—Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Argentina, and Chile. Formed by tectonic plate collision and volcanic activity, the Andes have shaped human civilization for millennia, hosting advanced pre-Columbian empires including the *Inca Empire* and serving as a crucial geographic and climatic divide for South American societies. The range remains geologically active, economically vital, and culturally central to Andean peoples.

## you gotta know

- The Andes contain some of the world's highest peaks, including *Mount Aconcagua* (6,961 m), the highest mountain in the Americas; the range is geologically young and still forming through ongoing subduction of the Nazca and South American tectonic plates.
- The range encompasses multiple climate zones, from tropical rainforest on its eastern slopes to arid deserts on its western side; this climatic diversity supported the development of distinct ecological zones that sustained pre-Columbian civilizations.
- The *Inca Empire* (1438–1533) dominated much of the Andes, creating an advanced administrative state with sophisticated agriculture, architecture, and engineering; its legacy remains deeply embedded in Andean culture and society.
- The *Quechua* people, descendants of the Inca, inhabit the Andes and maintain their language and cultural traditions despite centuries of Spanish colonization, economic exploitation, and political marginalization; they represent the largest indigenous population in South America.
- The Andes are extraordinarily biodiverse, containing unique species adapted to high altitude including llamas, alpacas, condors, and viscachas; the eastern slopes of the range form part of the Amazon basin and support tropical biodiversity.
- The range is rich in mineral resources—copper, silver, tin, and lithium—that have been extracted since pre-Columbian times; colonial and modern mining has been central to South American economies but has also caused environmental degradation and social conflict.
- The Andes remain geologically active with numerous volcanoes, some actively erupting; earthquakes are frequent and can be devastating to the settlements and infrastructure of the region.
- The high-altitude plateaus, particularly the *altiplano* shared by Peru and Bolivia, have hosted human settlements for thousands of years; Lake Titicaca, on this plateau, was sacred to Andean civilizations and remains culturally significant.
