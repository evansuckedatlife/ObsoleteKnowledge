## summary

**Great Slave Lake** is the second-largest lake in the Northwest Territories of [[canada]], trailing only Great Bear Lake, and ranks as the deepest lake in North America at over six hundred metres. Situated in the subarctic boreal forest and Canadian Shield, it serves as the principal headwater reservoir for the massive Mackenzie River system, which drains northward into the Arctic Ocean. Historically central to the indigenous Dene peoples and the inland fur trade routes of northern North America, the lake remains an economic and transport artery anchored by the territorial capital of *Yellowknife* on its northern arm.

## you gotta know

- Deepest lake in North America, plunging to a maximum recorded depth of approximately 614 metres in its rugged Christie Bay.
- Principal source of the *Mackenzie River*, the longest river system in [[canada]], which flows out of the lake's western basin toward the Arctic Ocean.
- Territorial capital *Yellowknife* sits along the north arm of the lake, founded following the discovery of gold in the region during the 1930s.
- Named after the indigenous *Slavey* or *Dene Tha'* people, an Athabaskan-speaking First Nations group native to the surrounding subarctic region.
- Explored by British fur trader *Samuel Hearne* in 1771 while travelling overland on behalf of the [[hudson-bay-company]], followed soon after by *Peter Pond*.
- Major tributary inflows include the *Slave River*, which delivers sediment from Lake Athabasca and the Peace-Athabasca Delta into the southern shore.
- Remained frozen for over half the year, historically giving rise to essential winter ice roads that connect remote mining outposts to mainland highways.

## connections
