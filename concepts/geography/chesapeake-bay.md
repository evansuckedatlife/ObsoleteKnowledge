## summary

**Chesapeake Bay** is the largest estuary in the [[united-states]], situated in the Mid-Atlantic region between *Maryland* and *Virginia*. Formed by the drowned river valley of the *Susquehanna River*, the bay receives freshwater from more than one hundred and fifty rivers and streams, notably the *Potomac River*, the *James River*, and the *Rappahannock River*. Renowned for its rich maritime heritage and historic seafood harvests of oysters and blue crabs, the bay served as an indispensable artery for early colonial settlements, decisive naval warfare, and regional commerce.

## you gotta know

- Spanning an area of roughly four thousand four hundred square miles, its expansive drainage basin encompasses portions of six states and the entirety of *Washington, D.C.*
- Formed during the Holocene epoch as glacial retreat raised global sea levels to flood the lower basin of the *Susquehanna River*, while its lower topography was shaped by a thirty-five-million-year-old bolide impact crater near Cape Charles.
- Served as the theater for the critical *Battle of the Capes* in 1781 during the [[american-revolution]], where the French fleet under *François Joseph Paul, Comte de Grasse* turned back the British Navy, sealing the entrapment of *Lord Cornwallis* at Yorktown.
- Supported a traditional harvesting culture led by *watermen*, who navigated shallow oyster grounds on traditional wooden sailing vessels known as *skipjacks* and *bugeyes* to dredge the eastern oyster (*Crassostrea virginica*).
- Formed the maritime gateway for the historic English settlement of *Jamestown* founded in 1607 on the *James River*, which was subsequently explored and mapped by Captain *John Smith*.
- Traversed by major civil engineering landmarks, including the dual-span *Chesapeake Bay Bridge* connecting Sandy Point to Kent Island and the seventeen-mile-long *Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel* across its southern ocean mouth.
- Experienced acute ecological degradation throughout the late twentieth century due to agricultural nitrogen and phosphorus runoff, creating expansive seasonal hypoxic "dead zones" and prompting large-scale conservation agreements.
