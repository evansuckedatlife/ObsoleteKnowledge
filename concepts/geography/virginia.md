# Virginia

## summary

**Virginia** is a state on the Atlantic coast of the eastern United States, historically among America's most influential and consequential regions. The site of early English colonization, the birthplace of the American Revolution and the nation's founding, and a crucial battleground of the American Civil War, Virginia has shaped American politics, slavery, and democracy. Home to multiple U.S. presidents, iconic historical sites, and the seat of federal power nearby in Washington, D.C., Virginia remains central to American identity and governance.

## you gotta know

- Virginia lies on the Atlantic coast of the southeastern United States, bordered by *North Carolina*, *West Virginia*, *Kentucky*, *Tennessee*, *Maryland*, and the *District of Columbia*; the *Potomac River* forms its northern and eastern boundary.
- *Jamestown*, founded in 1607 by English settlers, was the first permanent English settlement in North America; it established the first representative assembly (House of Burgesses) in 1619 and pioneered the plantation economy based on African slave labor.
- Virginia was one of the thirteen original colonies and a powerhouse in the independence movement: it hosted the *Continental Congress*, produced revolutionary leaders (*George Washington*, *Thomas Jefferson*, *Patrick Henry*), and witnessed the British surrender at *Yorktown* (1781).
- *Thomas Jefferson*, Virginia-born author of the *Declaration of Independence* and third U.S. president, exemplified Virginia's intellectual influence; other Virginia-born presidents include *George Washington*, *James Madison*, and *James Monroe*.
- Virginia was a slave state throughout the antebellum era; enslaved Africans made up a large portion of Virginia's population and labor force, producing immense wealth for planter elites while creating the moral contradictions of a slave-owning republic.
- Virginia was a primary battleground of the *American Civil War* (1861–1865): cities including *Richmond* (capital of the Confederacy), *Petersburg*, and *Shenandoah Valley* saw repeated, devastating campaigns; *Robert E. Lee* and the *Confederate Army of Northern Virginia* fought some of the war's bloodiest battles there.
- *Richmond*, Virginia's capital, sits on the *James River* and served as the Confederate capital during the Civil War; it was burned during Union General *William Sherman*'s campaign and rebuilt into a modern city.
- Modern Virginia is economically and politically significant: it includes the Washington, D.C. suburbs (a booming region), major military installations (*Pentagon*, naval bases), and is a key swing state in U.S. presidential elections.
