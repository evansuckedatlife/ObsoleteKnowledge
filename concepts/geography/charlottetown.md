## summary

**Charlottetown** is the capital and largest city of *Prince Edward Island*, situated on the southern shore of the island along *Hillsborough Bay*. Renowned across the country as the **Birthplace of Confederation**, the settlement served as the host city for the landmark 1864 *Charlottetown Conference*, which initiated the political unification that formed modern *Canada*. The city remains an enduring cultural, governmental, and tourist destination within the maritime region, recognized for its Victorian architecture, rich political legacy, and agricultural heritage.

## you gotta know

- Hosted the historic 1864 *Charlottetown Conference* within the sandstone chambers of *Province House*, where colonial delegates from across British North America met to negotiate the initial framework of Canadian federalism.
- Holds the honorific title **Birthplace of Confederation**, a legacy celebrated throughout the city and honored at the civic institution of the *Confederation Centre of the Arts*.
- Serves as the provincial capital, seat of government, and primary commercial center of *Prince Edward Island*, the smallest and most densely populated province in the nation.
- Founded as an English settlement in 1764 and named in tribute to Queen *Charlotte of Mecklenburg-Strelitz*, the royal consort of King *George III* of the *British Empire*.
- Sits at the confluence of three tidal rivers—the *Hillsborough River*, the *York River*, and the *Eliot River*—which flow together to empty into the sheltered harbor opening onto the *Northumberland Strait*.
- Raided in 1775 during the *American Revolution* by privateers from Massachusetts who ransacked the town and kidnapped colonial administrators, an incident that prompted General *George Washington* to rebuke the privateers and order the prisoners returned.
- Centered politically upon *Province House*, an imposing neoclassical building opened in 1847 that remains the second-oldest active legislative seat in the country.
- Serves as the urban gateway for visitors exploring the pastoral red-soil landscapes and beaches immortalized in *Lucy Maud Montgomery's* classic maritime novel *Anne of Green Gables*.
