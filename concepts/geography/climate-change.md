## summary

**Climate change** refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns, primarily driven by human activities since the onset of industrialization. The phenomenon accelerates ice loss at the poles, reshapes weather systems, and threatens ecosystems and human societies worldwide. It has become central to both scientific inquiry and political discourse, crystallizing in the late 20th century as the defining environmental challenge.

## you gotta know

- Temperature rise of approximately 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels, with the majority of warming occurring in recent decades; projected to reach 1.5–2°C by 2050 under current trajectories.
- Primarily caused by greenhouse gas emissions from fossil fuel combustion, deforestation, and industrial agriculture; carbon dioxide concentration has risen from 280 ppm to over 420 ppm.
- Observable effects include melting glaciers, rising sea levels (approximately 3–4mm per year), ocean acidification, and intensifying extreme weather events like hurricanes and droughts.
- Antarctica and Greenland ice sheets are leading indicators of warming trends and potential irreversible tipping points; their collapse could raise sea levels catastrophically.
- Glaciers on peaks like Mount Kilimanjaro have retreated dramatically over the past century, visible markers of planetary warming accessible to scientific measurement.
- Emerged as a major political and cultural force in the 1990s, with the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (1992) and later the Paris Agreement (2015).
- Al Gore became the public face of climate activism after his tenure as Vice President, making it his signature cause; his documentary amplified scientific consensus to mass audiences.
