## summary

**Alexander Mackenzie** was a Scottish explorer and fur trader for the *North West Company* who completed the first recorded overland crossing of North America north of Mexico to the Pacific Ocean in 1793. Four years earlier, in 1789, he navigated the vast northern river system in [[canada]] that now bears his name, hoping it would lead to Cook Inlet but discovering instead that it emptied into the Arctic Ocean. His expeditions mapped enormous swaths of uncharted territory across the western interior, challenged Spanish and Russian claims along the Pacific Northwest, and established him as a premier continental surveyor during the [[age-of-exploration]].

## you gotta know

- Completed the first recorded transcontinental crossing of North America north of Mexico in July 1793, reaching the Pacific coast twelve years before the American *Lewis and Clark* expedition.
- Inscribed a famous marker on a rock by the Dean Channel using vermilion and bear grease: *"Alexander Mackenzie, from [[canada]], by land, the twenty-second of July, one thousand seven hundred and ninety-three."*
- Led the 1789 expedition from Fort Chipewyan down the northern river now named the *Mackenzie River*, hoping it would lead to the Pacific but reaching the Arctic Ocean instead.
- Disappointed by his first journey's Arctic destination, he nicknamed the waterway the *"Disappointment River"* because it failed to provide a commercial trade route to Asia.
- Traveled over the Continental Divide and through the Coast Mountains with indispensable guidance and logistics from indigenous *Nuxalk* and *Carrier* peoples.
- Served as a prominent partner in the Montreal-based *North West Company*, the chief commercial rival to the chartered *Hudson's Bay Company*.
- Published his journals in 1801 as *Voyages from Montreal*, an influential account that alerted the [[british-empire]] and spurred the [[united-states]] to launch its own western exploration.
- Born in Stornoway in [[scotland]], he relocated to New York as a child and fled to Montreal during the [[american-revolution]], where he entered the fur trade.

## connections

- [[canada]] — the northern realm where he explored Arctic and Pacific watersheds and established interior trade routes.
- [[scotland]] — his birthplace in the Outer Hebrides, to which he returned later in life after gaining fame and wealth.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial crown that knighted him in 1802 in recognition of his transcontinental expeditions.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the era of global surveying and mapping to which his northern overland journeys belonged.
- [[united-states]] — the young nation whose leaders studied his published accounts, prompting the Lewis and Clark expedition.
- [[colonialism]] — the geopolitical contest for land and resource extraction that drove European rivalry in the Pacific Northwest.
- [[american-revolution]] — the war that displaced his loyalist family from New York, sending him to Montreal to enter the fur trade.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean connecting his Scottish homeland, his commercial base in Montreal, and British global trade networks.
