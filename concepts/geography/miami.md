## summary

**Miami**, affectionately known as the **Magic City**, is a coastal metropolis situated in southeastern Florida in the *United States*, fronting the *Atlantic Ocean* and *Biscayne Bay*. Distinguished as the only major American city planned and founded by a woman, citrus grower *Julia Tuttle*, it developed rapidly following the arrival of *Henry Flagler*'s *Florida East Coast Railway*. It functions as a premier international finance and trade gateway to Latin America and the Caribbean, celebrated for its Art Deco architecture, bilingual culture, and vulnerability to coastal environmental change.

## you gotta know

- Officially founded in 1896 after agriculturalist *Julia Tuttle* convinced rail baron *Henry Flagler* to extend his *Florida East Coast Railway* south by presenting him with frost-free orange blossoms following a devastating freeze.
- Frequently termed the "Gateway to the Americas" due to its immense concentration of bilingual financial institutions, foreign consulates, and maritime shipping lines trading with Latin American hubs like [[mexico-city]].
- Transformed demographically during the [[cold-war]] by the arrival of hundreds of thousands of Cuban exiles fleeing *Fidel Castro*'s regime, commemorated in neighborhoods such as *Little Havana* along *Calle Ocho*.
- Experienced major migration waves including the *Freedom Flights* from 1965 to 1973 and the 1980 *Mariel boatlift*, which brought over 100,000 Cuban refugees through *Biscayne Bay*.
- Boasts the world's largest concentration of resort *Art Deco* and *Streamline Moderne* architecture in the *Miami Beach Architectural District*, preserved through the efforts of activist *Barbara Capitman*.
- Sits on porous oolitic limestone, making its low-lying neighborhoods, such as *Brickell* and *South Beach*, highly susceptible to "sunny day" tidal flooding and sea level rise from the [[atlantic-ocean]].
- Bordered immediately to the west by the freshwater marshes of the *Everglades*, a subtropical wetland ecosystem drained in the 20th century to allow residential development.
- Houses *PortMiami*, widely recognized as the "Cruise Capital of the World" for servicing the highest volume of cruise passenger traffic on the globe.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — nation where the city stands as the cultural and commercial capital of South Florida.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — body of water directly bordering the city's coastline, barrier islands, and shipping lanes.
- [[cold-war]] — geopolitical rivalry that triggered large-scale political migration from Cuba and Central America to the city.
- [[modernism]] — design and architectural movement that influenced the post-war Miami Modern (MiMo) style.
- [[mexico-city]] — major Latin American metropolis linked to the city through multinational corporate headquarters and aviation routes.
- [[michigan]] — Midwestern industrial state whose snowbirds and retirees historically populated Florida's coastal urban centers.
