## summary

**Texas** is the second-largest U.S. state by both area and population, occupying the southwestern portion of the country between the Rocky Mountains and the Gulf of Mexico. Historically independent (the *Texas Republic*, 1836–1845) before annexation by the United States, Texas embodies the mythology of the American frontier: sprawling ranches, oil wealth, and a distinctive cultural identity. Its geography, politics, and economy have made it a shaping force in American history for nearly two centuries.

## you gotta know

- Became an independent nation (the *Texas Republic*) in 1836 after declaring independence from *Mexico*, remaining sovereign for nine years before joining the *United States* in 1845.
- The *Rio Grande* forms its southern and western boundary with *Mexico*, a crucial geographic and political border.
- The *Red River of the South* marks much of its northern boundary and flows into the *Mississippi River*, connecting Texas to the broader continental water system.
- Home to major oil reserves discovered in the early 20th century; petroleum extraction shaped the state's economy, infrastructure, and global influence.
- Became the first state to elect a Black woman to Congress, *Barbara Jordan*, in 1972—a defining moment in post-Reconstruction Southern politics.
- The state encompasses diverse ecosystems: piney woods in the east, deserts in the west, coastal plains along the *Gulf of Mexico*, and prairie grasslands in the north.
- Hosts the Space Center in *Houston*, which coordinated the U.S. moon landings and remains the heart of American space exploration.
- The *Hill Country* region is characterized by limestone formations, spring-fed rivers, and scenic hills; it is a cultural and tourism center distinct from the rest of the state.
