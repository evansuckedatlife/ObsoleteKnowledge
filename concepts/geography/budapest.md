## summary

**Budapest** is the capital and largest city of *Hungary*, situated on the *Danube River* in Central Europe where the river flows through a major geographical transition. Formed by the merger of two cities—Buda on the west bank and Pest on the east—Budapest represents centuries of imperial rule, artistic flourishing, and political turbulence. Today it remains one of Europe's most architecturally distinctive and culturally significant cities.

## you gotta know

- The city developed from Roman settlements (*Aquincum* in Buda, *Contra Aquincum* in Pest) that took advantage of natural hot springs.
- Buda and Pest developed separately for centuries under different rulers; Buda was the royal capital of Hungary under the medieval kingdom, while Pest grew as a commercial center.
- The unification of Buda and Pest (1873) coincided with the formation of the *Austro-Hungarian Empire*, making Budapest a dual capital of a vast multinational realm.
- The city became the cultural and intellectual heart of Hungarian nationalism; the Hungarian language, distinct from German, provided a focus for national identity under imperial rule.
- Belle Époque Budapest (late 19th–early 20th century) was an era of architectural grandeur, art, and Jewish intellectual flourishing; the city became a center of European culture.
- The Danube's thermal springs led to the construction of famous public bathhouses (Hungarian baths), tourist attractions and social centers combining leisure with health.
- The city suffered catastrophically during World War II; Jewish Budapest (once the largest Jewish community in Central Europe) was decimated by the Holocaust.
- Soviet-era Budapest and modern Budapest have experienced economic transformation and integration into the European Union, though political tensions with Brussels persist.
