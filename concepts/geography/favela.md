## summary

A **favela**, often referenced in the plural form **favelas**, is an informal, dense urban settlement or shantytown community built on unregulated municipal land or steep hillsides throughout Brazil. Emerging in the late nineteenth century as impoverished veterans and newly emancipated afro-Brazilians sought shelter near urban labor markets, these settlements proliferated rapidly in major metropolitan centers like Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo. Favelas represent both severe socio-economic disparities and resilient epicenters of Brazilian cultural innovation, giving rise to globally influential musical traditions, community associations, and artistic movements.

## you gotta know

- The name originates from *Morro da Favela* (now *Morro da Providência*) in Rio de Janeiro, settled in 1897 by impoverished veterans of the *War of Canudos* who named the hill after the resilient *favela* shrub (*Cnidoscolus quercifolius*) native to northeastern Bahia.
- Proliferated significantly following the 1888 abolition of slavery under the *Golden Law* (*Lei Áurea*), as destitute freedpeople encountered severe structural discrimination, lack of land access, and displacement from central urban tenements.
- Emerged as the vibrant birthplace of *samba*, where afro-descendant religious traditions, musical rhythms, and neighborhood gatherings coalesced into the world-famous samba schools (*escolas de samba*) that lead Rio's annual Carnival celebrations.
- *Rocinha*, sprawling across a steep mountainside in Rio de Janeiro's South Zone between the affluent neighborhoods of São Conrado and Gávea, is widely cited as the largest single favela in Brazil.
- Gained global cultural visibility through landmark works of international cinema, most notably the 2002 film *City of God* (*Cidade de Deus*), adapted from Paulo Lins's novel and directed by Fernando Meirelles and Kátia Lund.
- Experienced controversial state security interventions, most visibly the implementation of Police Pacifying Units (*Unidades de Polícia Pacificadora* or *UPPs*) aimed at suppressing entrenched drug trafficking cartels ahead of the 2014 World Cup and 2016 Summer Olympics.
- Fostered contemporary urban musical and youth movements including *baile funk* and *funk carioca*, distinctive bass-heavy electronic genres whose lyrics articulate neighborhood pride, romantic life, and everyday social reality.
- Constructed largely through vernacular incremental building techniques known as *autoconstrução*, where residents gradually upgrade makeshift wooden shacks into multi-story brick, cinderblock, and reinforced concrete dwellings.

## connections

- [[south-america]] — the geographic continent where Brazilian demographic transitions and industrialization drove massive informal urbanization.
- [[colonialism]] — the historic system of chattel slavery and racial hierarchy whose enduring socio-economic legacies produced modern urban segregation.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean flanking Brazil's coastal urban centers, where hillside favelas overlook beachfront tourist promenades.
- [[realism]] — the literary and artistic approach committed to depicting working-class hardship, directly influencing gritty portrayals of favela life.
- [[modernism]] — the architectural and political movement whose state-directed urban clearance initiatives often clashed with informal community settlements.
