## summary

**Pennsylvania** is a northeastern U.S. state founded as a Quaker refuge by William Penn in 1681, becoming one of America's original thirteen colonies. Located between the Atlantic seaboard and Appalachian interior, Pennsylvania has been central to American history, industry, and innovation—from Revolutionary War battlegrounds to steel mills and coal mines fueling the Industrial Revolution. Its geography ranges from urban industrial cities to rural farmland and mountain wilderness.

## you gotta know

- Pennsylvania was chartered to William Penn as a haven for Quakers and other religious minorities, becoming uniquely tolerant among British colonies.
- The state borders the Delaware River, which forms its eastern boundary and served as a crucial military line during the American Revolution.
- Philadelphia, Pennsylvania's largest city and colonial capital, was the de facto capital of the United States during the Revolution and early republic (hosting the Continental Congress and Constitutional Convention).
- The Appalachian Mountains run through Pennsylvania's interior, separating the more developed eastern regions from western frontiers.
- Pennsylvania was central to the American Revolution, hosting the Battles of Brandywine and Germantown, and later serving as George Washington's winter headquarters at Valley Forge.
- The state became the nation's industrial heartland in the 19th and 20th centuries, with vast anthracite coal deposits and steel mills driving economic growth and immigration.
- Pennsylvania's landscape has shifted from colonial forests to agricultural regions, industrial centers, and modern service economies.
