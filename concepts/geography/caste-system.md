## summary

The **caste system** is an ancient, institutionalized system of hierarchical social stratification, endogamy, and hereditary occupation native to the Indian subcontinent. Traditionally conceptualized through textual religious divisions and localized kinship units, it structured socio-economic privileges, communal interactions, and ritual purity for millennia. Transformed and codified through European imperial administration and challenged by modern constitutional democracy, it remains a defining concept in South Asian social history and political reform.

## you gotta know

- Divided doctrinally into four broad scriptural orders known as **varna**: *Brahmins* (priests and scholars), *Kshatriyas* (rulers and warriors), *Vaishyas* (merchants and agriculturalists), and *Shudras* (laborers and artisans).
- The *Purusha Sukta*, a famous hymn found in the tenth mandala of the foundational *Rigveda*, provides the primary cosmogenic myth, depicting the four ranks emerging from the dismembered body parts of the cosmic primeval being *Purusha*.
- Beyond theoretical textual quadrants, day-to-day social reality was organized around thousands of localized, occupational, endogamous kinship communities known as **jati**, which governed dietary rules, trade rights, and intermarriage.
- Communities existing entirely outside this fourfold schema were historical outcasts, subjected to severe social exclusion as *Untouchables*, who later embraced the self-chosen sociopolitical dignity term *Dalit* ("the oppressed").
- The administrative apparatus of the [[british-empire]] rigidly formalized, enumerated, and institutionalized these fluid local identities into bureaucratic categories through the colonial decennial censuses, beginning particularly under *Herbert Hope Risley* in 1901.
- Crusading jurist and Dalit icon *B. R. Ambedkar* fiercely contested these systemic oppressions, authoring the seminal treatise *Annihilation of Caste* and leading mass religious conversions to *Buddhism* while drafting the post-independence constitution.
- Independent [[india]] officially banned untouchability in 1950 via Article 17 of its constitution and established an extensive affirmative action regime known as the reservation system for *Scheduled Castes* and *Scheduled Tribes*.

## connections

- [[india]] — the geographical cradle and cultural landscape where this hereditary social order took root and evolved.
- [[british-empire]] — British administrators systematized and codified local hierarchies through imperial census policies.
- [[colonialism]] — imperial ethnography reinforced rigid caste divides to facilitate administrative control across the subcontinent.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — American activists like *Martin Luther King Jr.* drew direct moral comparisons between anti-Black segregation and Dalit struggles.
- [[buddhism]] — arose historically in northern South Asia offering an alternative egalitarian spiritual framework rejecting hereditary Vedic dominance.
- [[hinduism]] — ancient sacred texts like the *Dharmashastras* and *Manusmriti* formulated its classical theological justifications.
- [[silk-road]] — ancient Eurasian trade network along which merchants traveled, spreading South Asian social traditions and goods.
