## summary

**Lake Ontario** is the easternmost and smallest in surface area of the Great Lakes of North America, bounded by the province of Ontario in [[canada]] and the state of New York in the [[united-states]]. Positioned at the lowest surface elevation of the chain, it receives the cumulative discharge of the upper lakes via the Niagara River and flows directly into the St. Lawrence River, draining the entire basin toward the [[atlantic-ocean]]. Bordered by the Golden Horseshoe megalopolis, it serves as a crucial shipping corridor, industrial engine, and freshwater reservoir.

## you gotta know

- Possesses the smallest surface area of any Great Lake at roughly 7,340 square miles (19,000 square kilometers), yet maintains an average depth of 283 feet and maximum depth of 802 feet, making it vastly deeper than Lake Erie.
- Serves as the immediate source of the St. Lawrence River, which carries the freshwater outflow of the entire Great Lakes basin past Montreal and Quebec City into the [[atlantic-ocean]].
- Receives the overwhelming majority of its inflow via the Niagara River and Niagara Falls from Lake Erie, which collects water cascading from [[lake-huron]] and [[lake-michigan]].
- Bypassed for commercial maritime shipping by the *Welland Canal*, an eight-lock canal cutting through the Niagara Escarpment that forms an essential link in the St. Lawrence Seaway.
- Bounded on its northern and western shores by the *Golden Horseshoe*, a densely urbanized Canadian industrial region anchored by Toronto, Mississauga, and Hamilton.
- Contested heavily as a primary naval battleground during the War of 1812, featuring a feverish naval arms race between British shipbuilders at Kingston and American yards at Sackets Harbor.
- Historically connected to the Hudson River and [[new-york-city]] through the *Erie Canal* system, which met the lake via its Oswego branch.
- Contains the *Thousand Islands* archipelago at its northeastern exit into the St. Lawrence River, a scenic boundary region shared between Ontario and New York.

## connections

- [[lake-huron]] — the upstream Great Lake that supplies water to Lake Ontario via the St. Clair, Detroit, and Niagara river systems.
- [[lake-michigan]] — the companion Great Lake contributing hydrological discharge eastward through the lower lakes.
- [[canada]] — the nation bordering the lake's northern, western, and southwestern perimeters within Ontario.
- [[united-states]] — the nation bordering the southern and eastern margins of the lake in New York.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean receiving the lake's freshwater outflow via the St. Lawrence River.
- [[new-york-city]] — the coastal metropolis historically linked to Lake Ontario shipping by the Erie Canal.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the economic transformation that turned the lake's ports into centers of steelmaking, milling, and rail freight.
