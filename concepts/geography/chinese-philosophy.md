## summary

**Chinese philosophy** designates the rich intellectual, ethical, and metaphysical traditions that developed in ancient China, achieving enduring definition during the classical *Hundred Schools of Thought* era. Anchored by the influential doctrines of *Confucianism*, *Daoism*, and *Legalism*, this tradition focuses primarily on moral cultivation, harmonious societal order, statecraft, and the holistic unity between nature and human action. Over two millennia, these philosophical frameworks shaped Chinese imperial governance, scholarship, social structures, and spatial arts.

## you gotta know

- Reached an extraordinary intellectual zenith during the Eastern Zhou dynasty's *Spring and Autumn* and *Warring States* periods, when wandering scholars debated rival methods for rectifying social chaos.
- *Confucianism*, articulated by *Confucius* (*Kongzi*) and developed by *Mencius* and *Xunzi*, centers upon human-hearted benevolence (*ren*), moral righteousness (*yi*), filial piety (*xiao*), and the meticulous observance of ritual propriety (*li*).
- *Daoism*, traditionally traced to the legendary sage *Laozi* and his text the *Daodejing*, alongside the parables of *Zhuangzi*, emphasizes alignment with the natural way (*Dao*), unforced action (*wuwei*), and spontaneous simplicity (*pu*).
- *Legalism*, systematized by the philosopher *Han Feizi* and adopted by the state of Qin, discarded moral cultivation in favor of strict rewards and punishments, institutional mechanics, and supreme imperial authority.
- *Mohism*, founded by *Mozi*, proposed radical doctrines of universal impartial love (*jian'ai*), state utilitarianism, opposition to aggressive warfare, and pioneering inquiries into logic.
- Integrated dualistic cosmological concepts of *Yin and Yang* alongside the *Five Phases* (*Wuxing*), which interpreted all dynamic natural transformations and provided the foundation for traditional sciences, medicine, and spatial layout.
- Underwent significant renewal during the Song and Ming dynasties as *Neo-Confucianism*, spearheaded by figures such as *Zhu Xi* and *Wang Yangming*, who synthesized classical ethics with sophisticated metaphysics.
