## summary

**The North Pole** is the northernmost point of Earth's axis, located in the Arctic Ocean surrounded by pack ice. Unlike the South Pole's solid Antarctic continent, the North Pole sits on a drifting ice sheet with no permanent land, making it one of Earth's most extreme and inaccessible locations. Exploration of the pole has driven some of history's greatest polar expeditions and remains a symbol of human ambition against nature's harshest conditions.

## you gotta know

- The North Pole is situated at 90 degrees north latitude in the Arctic Ocean, roughly 450 miles north of the nearest land (Greenland and the Canadian Arctic).
- Unlike Antarctica, there is no permanent research station at the North Pole; ice thickness varies from 6 to 40 feet and constantly drifts with ocean currents.
- Early polar explorers faced near-impossible odds: Frederick Cook claimed to reach it in 1908, followed by Robert Peary in 1909, though both claims remain disputed.
- The North Pole experiences extreme seasons: continuous daylight in summer and continuous darkness in winter, making navigation extraordinarily challenging.
- The USS Nautilus, the world's first nuclear submarine, made history in 1958 by passing under the Arctic ice, reaching the North Pole submerged.
- The magnetic north pole (where compass needles point) differs from the geographic North Pole and has shifted significantly over geological time and historical records.
- Arctic exploration in the 20th century was driven by Cold War competition between the Soviet Union and the United States, with both nations exploring polar regions.

## connections

- [[arctic-ocean]] — the North Pole lies at the center of the Arctic Ocean, defining its geography.
- [[canada]] — Canada's Arctic territories extend toward the pole and have played a role in polar sovereignty and exploration.
- [[russia]] — the Soviet Union and modern Russia have conducted extensive Arctic research and polar expeditions.
- [[greenland]] — the nearest substantial landmass to the North Pole, serving as a base for many polar expeditions.
