## summary

**19th-Century Art** encompasses the diverse artistic movements and styles that flourished during an era of profound social and technological transformation. Spanning from Romanticism's celebration of emotion and nature through Impressionism's revolutionary approach to light and perception, the century saw European art move decisively away from academic tradition toward experimental freedom and personal expression. The period witnessed industrialization, urbanization, and photography fundamentally challenging what art was and what it should represent to modern society.

## you gotta know

- The century opened with Romanticism, emphasizing emotion, imagination, and nature as reaction against Enlightenment rationalism; artists like *Turner* and *Caspar David Friedrich* pursued transcendent landscape.
- The invention of photography (1839) disrupted painting's monopoly on representation and forced artists to seek new expressive purposes beyond mere documentation.
- Realism emerged mid-century, focusing on contemporary life and ordinary people rather than historical or mythological ideals; *Courbet* and *Millet* scandalized academic tradition with peasant subjects.
- Impressionism (1870s–1880s) revolutionized painting by prioritizing light, color, and perception over narrative and precise detail; *Monet* and *Renoir* faced ridicule from conservative critics.
- Industrialization and urbanization created new subjects: factories, trains, city streets, and modern leisure replaced landscape and historical narrative—a fundamental shift in artistic subject matter.
- The century saw dramatic expansion of international art markets, independent exhibitions, and artist autonomy from state academies and official salons; artists could now exhibit and sell directly.
- Post-Impressionism (1880s–1890s) pushed beyond Impressionism toward abstraction, symbolism, and distorted form in the hands of *Van Gogh*, *Cézanne*, and *Gauguin*.
- The period ended with Art Nouveau and early modernism pointing toward the radical abstractions of the 20th century; color and form had been liberated from representation.

## connections

- [[impressionism]] — the revolutionary movement that redefined painting through light and perception.
- [[romanticism]] — the emotional and imaginative movement opening the century.
- [[realism]] — the movement prioritizing contemporary subjects and ordinary life.
- [[modernism]] — the broader shift toward experimentation and rejection of tradition.
- [[post-impressionism]] — late-century movements pushing beyond Impressionist aesthetics.
- [[photography]] — the technology that displaced painting's representational function.
- [[industrialization]] — the social force that transformed both artistic subjects and society itself.
