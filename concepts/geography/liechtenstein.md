## summary

**Liechtenstein**, officially the **Principality of Liechtenstein** (*Fürstentum Liechtenstein*), is a tiny alpine microstate nestled in Central [[europe]] along the upper Rhine river between Switzerland and Austria. Governed as a constitutional hereditary monarchy under the Princely House of Liechtenstein, it is one of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world alongside Uzbekistan. Despite lacking an active standing military since the nineteenth century, the principality developed into one of the wealthiest sovereign states per capita on Earth through specialized high-tech manufacturing, precision engineering, and financial services.

## you gotta know

- Capital at Vaduz and doubly landlocked geography: Situated in the Upper Rhine Valley of the Alps, the country has its capital at Vaduz, overlooked by the medieval Vaduz Castle (*Schloss Vaduz*), while its largest settlement by population is Schaan; it shares borders solely with landlocked Switzerland to the west and south and landlocked Austria to the east.
- Creation within the Holy Roman Empire: The territory achieved imperial immediacy (*Reichsfreiheit*) when Prince Johann Adam Andreas of Liechtenstein purchased the Lordship of Schellenberg in 1699 and the County of Vaduz in 1712, which Holy Roman Emperor Charles VI united and elevated into an imperial principality in 1719 to allow the princely family a seat in the Imperial Diet (*Reichstag*).
- Disbandment of the army and the legendary 1866 campaign: The principality abolished its armed forces in 1868 due to maintenance costs following the Austro-Prussian War of 1866; popular quiz-bowl folklore frequently notes that its contingent of eighty soldiers returned home without casualties alongside an Italian or Austrian officer friend, effectively returning with eighty-one men.
- Customs and currency union with Switzerland: Following the economic devastation and collapse of the Austro-Hungarian Empire after [[world-war-i]], the nation dissolved its longstanding customs union with Austria, adopting the Swiss franc as its official legal currency in 1920 and establishing a permanent customs union with Switzerland in 1924.
- Industrial specialization and dental exports: Rather than relying solely on finance and corporate headquarters, the principality is a major manufacturing power host to multinational corporations like the construction toolmaker Hilti in Schaan and Ivoclar Vivadent in Schaan, the latter being the world's leading commercial producer of artificial porcelain teeth and dental prosthetics.
- Princely constitutional powers: Unlike most European constitutional monarchs who hold purely ceremonial roles, the Reigning Prince of Liechtenstein retains extensive executive authority, including the prerogative to veto parliamentary legislation, dismiss governments, and propose referendums, powers reaffirmed by national referendum in 2003 under Prince Hans-Adam II.
