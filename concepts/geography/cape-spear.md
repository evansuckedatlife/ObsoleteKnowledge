## summary

**Cape Spear**, historically known in French as **Cap d'Espoir**, is a rugged headland situated on the *Avalon Peninsula* near the provincial capital of *St. John's*, *Newfoundland*. It is celebrated as the easternmost point of land in *Canada* and in North America outside of *Greenland*, projecting prominently into the open waters of the *Atlantic Ocean*. Recognized as a Canadian National Historic Site, the promontory is renowned for its historic 1836 lighthouse, its multi-generational lighthouse keepers, and its strategic wartime service safeguarding maritime shipping lanes.

## you gotta know

- Marks the easternmost geographic point of North America excluding *Greenland*, positioned at 52°37' W longitude on the craggy shoreline of the *Avalon Peninsula*.
- Anchored by the *Cape Spear Lighthouse*, erected in 1836 atop dramatic cliffs, which represents the oldest surviving lighthouse structure in *Newfoundland and Labrador*.
- Fortified heavily during *World War II* as *Fort Cape Spear*, a joint coastal defense installation operated by Canadian and American forces to protect transatlantic convoys and the harbor approaches of *St. John's* against German submarines.
- Features preserved wartime military artifacts overlooking the sea, including concrete observation bunkers, underground magazines, and two 10-inch coastal artillery pieces on disappearing carriages.
- Maintained for over a century and a quarter by the *Cantwell* family, whose successive generations served as resident keepers from the mid-nineteenth century until the facility transitioned to automated operation.
- Originally charted by Portuguese navigators during the *Age of Exploration* in the early sixteenth century and designated *Cabo da Esperança*, meaning *Cape of Hope*, which later became gallicized as *Cap d'Espoir* before entering English usage.
- Provides a premier shore-based observation platform for marine wildlife, notably humpback whales and seabirds, and sits squarely along *Iceberg Alley*, where massive glacial fragments drift south from the Arctic via the *Labrador Current*.
- Stands today as a popular terminus for the *East Coast Trail*, connecting hikers through coastal wilderness to the nearby settlement of *Petty Harbour*.
