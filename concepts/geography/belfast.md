## summary

**Belfast**, known endonymously in Irish as **Béal Feirste**, is the capital and largest city of Northern Ireland, located on the banks of the River Lagan where it opens into Belfast Lough. A preeminent industrial boomtown of the nineteenth-century [[british-empire]], it was celebrated globally for its sprawling textile manufacturing and maritime engineering, including the construction of the RMS *Titanic* at the *Harland and Wolff* shipyard. During the late twentieth century, the city became the main urban battleground of *The Troubles*, a bitter ethno-nationalist conflict whose legacy persists in the towering *peace lines* that still weave through working-class districts.

## you gotta know

- Capital and primary port of Northern Ireland, situated along Belfast Lough and sheltered beneath the basalt promontory of *Cavehill*.
- Nicknamed *Linenopolis* during the [[industrial-revolution]] after becoming the global epicenter of Irish linen production, powered by mechanised wet-spinning mills.
- Home to the iconic *Harland and Wolff* heavy shipyard, marked by the yellow gantry cranes *Samson and Goliath*, which constructed the ill-fated ocean liner RMS *Titanic* between 1909 and 1911.
- Devastated during [[world-war-ii]] by the April–May 1941 *Belfast Blitz*, when German Luftwaffe air raids targeted the shipyards and aircraft factories, killing over nine hundred civilians.
- Served as the epicenter of *The Troubles* (ca. 1968–1998), a thirty-year conflict between predominantly Catholic Irish nationalists/republicans and Protestant British unionists/loyalists.
- Characterized by urban *peace lines* (peace walls), extensive physical barriers of concrete, metal sheeting, and razor wire erected between sectarian communities along the *Falls Road* and *Shankill Road*.
- Governed politically from the neoclassical Parliament Buildings at *Stormont*, the seat of the power-sharing Northern Ireland Assembly established by the 1998 *Good Friday Agreement*.
- Internationally renowned for political street art and large-scale murals commemorating historical events such as the 1690 *Battle of the Boyne* and the 1981 *Irish hunger strike*.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — functioned as an industrial powerhouse and shipbuilding capital supplying naval fleets and mercantile trade routes.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — transformed an Ulster market town into the largest linen producer and premier dry-dock shipyard in the world.
- [[scotland]] — supplied waves of Presbyterian settlers during the seventeenth-century Plantation of Ulster, establishing the regional cultural identity of Ulster-Scots.
- [[england]] — central partner in the United Kingdom whose military deployment and political administration shaped twentieth-century Northern Ireland.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — served as the shipping highway for passenger liners, transatlantic commerce, and emigrant travel departing Belfast Lough.
- [[berlin]] — shares the architectural and social legacy of physical security walls constructed to separate politically divided urban populations.
- [[cold-war]] — coincided historically with the height of *The Troubles*, sharing international diplomatic and intelligence entanglements.
