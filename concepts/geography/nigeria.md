## summary

**Nigeria**, officially the **Federal Republic of Nigeria**, is the most populous nation in Africa and the sixth most populous in the world, located along the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa. Known as the "Giant of Africa" due to its demographic scale and expansive economy, the nation was created through the administrative amalgamation of diverse regional protectorates under the *[[british-empire]]* in 1914. Its contemporary cultural and political landscape is characterized by deep multilingual diversity, profound religious contrasts between north and south, rich petroleum reserves, and influential global contributions in literature, music, and cinema.

## you gotta know

- Lagos, situated on the Atlantic coastline around a network of lagoons, is the economic engine of West Africa and the country's most populous megacity, having served as the national capital until 1991 when the federal government relocated inland to the planned, ethnically neutral capital of Abuja.
- The country's physical geography is defined by the convergence of the Niger River and Benue River at the city of Lokoja, which form a grand Y-shaped confluence flowing south into the vast, ecologically fragile, and oil-abundant Niger Delta emptying into the *[[atlantic-ocean]]*.
- The nation contains over 250 distinct ethnic groups and 500 languages, dominated politically and socially by three major populations: the predominantly Muslim Hausa-Fulani across the northern savannahs, the religiously mixed Yoruba in the southwest, and the largely Christian Igbo concentrated in the southeast.
- Fractured along ethnic and political lines following independence, the country plunged into the devastating Nigerian Civil War (also known as the Biafran War) from 1967 to 1970, when the southeastern region attempted secession as the Republic of Biafra, causing widespread humanitarian catastrophe and famine before federal forces prevailed.
- A global literary heavyweight, the country produced Chinua Achebe, whose celebrated 1958 novel *Things Fall Apart* reshaped modern African literature, and Wole Soyinka, who in 1986 became the first African writer awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature.
- The northeast, bordering Lake *[[chad]]*, has suffered severe instability since 2009 due to the violent extremist insurgency of Boko Haram, an Islamist militant group infamous for opposing Western education and carrying out the 2014 mass kidnapping of schoolgirls in Chibok.
- Member of OPEC and historically Africa's largest crude oil exporter, producing premium light sweet crude known as Bonny Light, while also hosting "Nollywood," one of the largest and most prolific film production industries in the world.

## connections

- [[atlantic-ocean]] — forms the nation's southern maritime boundary along the Bight of Benin and Gulf of Guinea.
- [[colonialism]] — boundaries were established through British colonial amalgamations of distinct pre-existing kingdoms and caliphates.
- [[british-empire]] — ruled the territory as a colony and protectorate until granting formal independence in 1960.
- [[chad]] — northeastern neighbour sharing the shrinking waters and security challenges of the Lake Chad basin.
- [[islam]] — dominant religion throughout the northern states, where several jurisdictions incorporate Sharia penal codes.
- [[christianity]] — prominent faith embraced across the southern states, flourishing through major evangelical and historic churches.
- [[linguistics]] — exceptionally diverse linguistic landscape utilizing English as the official unifying language alongside Nigerian Pidgin.
