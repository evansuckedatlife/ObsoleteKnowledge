## summary

**Batu Khan** was a thirteenth-century Mongol military commander and the grandson of [[genghis-khan]] who founded the *Golden Horde*, ruling the vast steppe lands of western Eurasia. Between 1236 and 1242, he orchestrated the devastating Mongol invasion of medieval Rus' and Eastern [[europe]], permanently transforming the political geography of the Slavic world and the Pontic-Caspian steppe. By establishing his imperial capital at *Sarai* on the lower Volga River, he instituted two centuries of tributary dominance over [[russia]] that reshaped Eurasian trade and territorial boundaries.

## you gotta know

- Founded the *Golden Horde*—also known as the *Ulus of Jochi* or the *Kipchak Khanate*—establishing Mongol suzerainty over the fragmented principalities of [[russia]] through severe tax levies, censuses, and military intimidation.
- Subjugated the northern and southern Rus' principalities between 1237 and 1240, razing major urban centers including Ryazan, Vladimir, and *Kiev*, which shifted the geographic center of East Slavic civilization away from the Dnieper basin.
- Launched a synchronized blitzkrieg into Central [[europe]] alongside general *Subutai*, routing Polish and German chivalric forces at the *Battle of Legnica* while personally crushing King Béla IV of Hungary at the *Battle of Mohi* in 1241.
- Abruptly halted his westward push toward Austria and the [[mediterranean-sea]] in the spring of 1242 after receiving news that Great Khan *Ögedei* had died in Karakorum, pulling his tumens back across the Carpathian Mountains to safeguard his dynastic interests.
- Constructed the sprawling capital of *Sarai Batu* near the mouth of the Volga River, commanding critical northern crossroads of the [[silk-road]] and collecting duties on trade traveling between Central Asia, [[iran]], and the Black Sea.
- Inaugurated the system of granting the *yarlik*, a formal charter of investiture that forced Slavic grand princes such as *Alexander Nevsky* to travel to the Volga to pay homage, laying the geopolitical groundwork for the eventual preeminence of Moscow.
- Acted as supreme kingmaker within the wider Mongol realm by defying his bitter rival *Güyük Khan* and orchestrating the 1251 kurultai that elevated *Möngke Khan* to the imperial throne, securing de facto autonomy for his own western ulus.

## connections

- [[genghis-khan]] — charismatic founder of the Mongol world empire whose death led to the territorial partition assigning the unexplored western steppe appanage to Batu's paternal lineage.
- [[europe]] — continent whose eastern monarchies were dismantled and geographically isolated during his devastating winter campaigns of 1241.
- [[russia]] — civilization whose cultural and administrative trajectory was redirected under the tributary isolation of the *Tatar Yoke* imposed by his descendants.
- [[silk-road]] — overland trans-Eurasian trade network whose northern branch flourished through the security and customs posts maintained across his steppe domain.
- [[middle-east]] — neighboring southwestern theater where his brother *Hulagu Khan* founded the *Ilkhanate*, sparking territorial wars between their two Mongol realms over grazing lands in Azerbaijan.
- [[christianity]] — religious sphere across Eastern and Central Europe that regarded his sudden equestrian invasion as an apocalyptic scourge sent to punish human sin.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — late-medieval imperial power that absorbed the Crimean Tatars, the final direct geopolitical successor state derived from the fragmentation of his khanate.
