## summary

The **Camargue** is a vast natural wetland delta in southern *[[france]]*, situated south of Arles between the two distributaries of the Rhône River and the *[[mediterranean-sea]]*. Forming western Europe's largest river delta, it is an internationally protected biosphere reserve composed of brackish lagoons, salt marshes, and reed beds. Celebrated for its unique pastoral traditions and rich biodiversity, the region is legendary for its wild white horses, fighting black bulls, and colossal breeding colonies of greater flamingos.

## you gotta know

- Formed by the delta of the Rhône River as it splits into the *Grand Rhône* to the east and the *Petit Rhône* to the west before emptying into the *[[mediterranean-sea]]*.
- Comprises over 900 square kilometres of marshes, gravel bars, and shallow saline lagoons, the largest being the *Étang de Vaccarès*.
- Celebrated for its ancient breed of indigenous white Camargue horses, managed by mounted herdsmen called *gardians* who live in distinctive thatched cabins.
- Hosts Camargue black bulls, which are bred primarily for traditional, non-lethal Provencal bullfighting (*course camarguaise*).
- Serves as the premier nesting ground in northern Europe for the greater flamingo, drawing thousands of pairs to its saline banks each year.
- The principal settlement within the marshes is the coastal pilgrimage town of Saintes-Maries-de-la-Mer, central to Romani cultural veneration of Saint Sarah.
- Major human industries in the saline flats include commercial sea-salt harvesting at Salin-de-Giraud and specialty red rice cultivation.

## connections

- [[france]] — the European nation in which the Rhône delta is located.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — the sea bordering the southern edge of the delta's lagoons.
- [[europe]] — the continent where the Camargue forms the largest river delta wetland.
- [[rome]] — the ancient empire whose regional hub at nearby Arles profoundly shaped the surrounding lower Rhône valley.
- [[impressionism]] — the artistic movement closely tied to neighboring Arles and Provence, where painters captured southern light and wetlands.
