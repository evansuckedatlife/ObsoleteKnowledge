## summary

**Chinese architecture** is a classical structural tradition characterized by bilateral symmetry, modular timber post-and-lintel framing, interlocking bracket systems, and cosmological alignment. Evolving continuously across dynasties from antiquity through the Ming and Qing eras, it emphasizes horizontal expansiveness organized around internal courtyards rather than monumental vertical mass. Guided by standard architectural treatises such as the Song dynasty *Yingzao Fashi*, this structural system translates ancestral reverence, imperial hierarchy, and natural harmony into enduring palaces, temples, pavilions, and urban plans.

## you gotta know

- Utilizes flexible, timber-framed structural skeletons without load-bearing masonry walls, which granted ancient structures notable resilience against earthquakes through shock-absorbing joinery.
- Relies heavily on the *dougong*, an intricate modular system of interlocking wooden bracket clusters that cantilever outward from pillars to support the substantial weight of wide overhanging roofs.
- Organizes spatial layout along a rigorous north-south axial symmetry, placing the most elevated and significant halls toward the rear facing south, embodying moral order and political authority.
- Incorporates the modular courtyard residence known as the *siheyuan*, an inward-facing design that shielded occupants from external noise and sandstorms while establishing generational living quarters.
- Integrates the spatial philosophy of *Feng shui* and cosmological planning manuals like the *Kaogongji*, positioning settlements and buildings relative to mountains, rivers, and cardinal alignments.
- Displays distinctive curved hip-and-gable roof profiles covered in glazed ceramic tiles, where bright imperial yellow tiles were reserved strictly for the emperor's palaces, while commoners used unglazed grey tiles.
- Reached a monumental imperial culmination in the *Forbidden City* in Beijing, which features sprawling ceremonial halls, vast stone courtyards, and tiered marble terraces arrayed across a grand imperial axis.

## connections

- [[china]] — geographical and cultural civilization whose dynastic prestige and artistic traditions were immortalized in timber and stone.
- [[chinese-philosophy]] — source of cosmological theories, filial domestic hierarchies, and Daoist natural balance that dictated structural planning.
- [[great-wall-of-china]] — premier military defense work exemplifying classical rammed-earth and kiln-fired brick masonry across northern frontiers.
- [[japan]] — neighboring nation whose classical timber-frame Buddhist temples and capital layouts drew directly from Tang and Song dynasty building models.
- [[imperial-authority]] — state power symbolically demonstrated through restrictive building sumptuary laws, color-coded roofing, and roof-ridge guardian figures.
