## summary

The **Al-Aqsa Mosque**, specifically denoting the silver-domed congregational prayer hall located on the southern end of the *Temple Mount* or *Haram al-Sharif* in the *Old City of Jerusalem*, is the third-holiest site in [[islam]]. Situated in the historic heart of the [[middle-east]] near [[israel]], the site represents the traditional destination of the Prophet Muhammad's miraculous journey known as the *Isra and Mi'raj*. Anchored in complex imperial histories stretching from the *Umayyad Caliphate* through the *Crusades* and the *Ottoman Empire*, the mosque remains a central religious, political, and cultural focal point in contemporary international relations.

## you gotta know

- Replaced the initial direction of Muslim prayer (*qibla*) toward *Jerusalem* before the orientation was permanently redirected toward *Mecca*.
- Venerated in Islamic tradition as the "Farthest Mosque" described in the opening verse of *Surah Al-Isra* in the Quran, marking the embarkation point of the Prophet's celestial ascent.
- Constructed originally in wood and stone under the Umayyad caliph *Abd al-Malik* and completed by his son *al-Walid I* in the early eighth century.
- Converted into a palace and headquarters called the *Palace of Solomon* by the *Knights Templar* during the Crusader kingdom period of [[christianity]], before being restored to Islamic worship by *Saladin* in 1187.
- Occupies the broader platform traditionally identified in [[judaism]] as the *Temple Mount*, the ancient location of the First and Second Temples destroyed during the era of the [[roman-empire]].
- Underwent frequent reconstructions following severe earthquakes along the *Jordan Rift Valley*, resulting in its distinctive architectural blend of Umayyad, Abbasid, Fatimid, and Ottoman elements.
- Maintained under the custodianship of the *Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan* through the Jerusalem *Waqf*, serving as an enduring focal point for political and religious tensions across the region.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the faith for which the site serves as the third-holiest sanctuary after *Mecca* and *Medina*.
- [[middle-east]] — the geographic region whose political and religious history is profoundly shaped by the complex.
- [[israel]] — the modern state exercising security jurisdiction around the *Old City of Jerusalem*.
- [[judaism]] — the religious tradition sharing the sacred elevated topography of the ancient *Temple Mount*.
- [[christianity]] — the faith that occupied and repurposed the complex during the medieval *Crusades*.
- [[roman-empire]] — the imperial power whose siege in 70 CE destroyed the Second Temple preceding later Islamic construction.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — the body of water anchoring the Levant and maritime approaches to *Jerusalem*.
