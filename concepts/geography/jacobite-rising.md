## summary

The **Jacobite Rising** refers to a series of military rebellions (primarily 1645–1746) by Scottish Highlanders and their English Catholic allies attempting to restore the Stuart dynasty to the British throne. The term "Jacobite" derives from *Jacobus*, the Latin form of James, referencing the deposed *James II*. The final and most famous rising in 1745–1746 under Bonnie Prince Charlie resulted in catastrophic defeat at Culloden, ending both Highland military power and the clan system as an independent force.

## you gotta know

- Arose from religious conflict: the Stuart kings were Catholic or Catholic-sympathizers in a newly Protestant Britain following the Reformation.
- The 1688 Glorious Revolution deposed *James II*, replacing him with the Protestant *William III* and his wife *Mary*; Jacobites viewed this as illegitimate usurpation.
- Support concentrated in the Scottish Highlands, where the clan system remained strong and loyalty to the Stuarts ran deep; loyalty to the Crown was personal, not institutional.
- Multiple risings occurred (1689, 1708, 1715, 1719, 1745); each was progressively more desperate and smaller in scale, reflecting declining support and military capacity.
- The 1745 Rising under *Charles Edward Stuart* (Bonnie Prince Charlie) momentarily reached as far south as Derby before retreating; it came closest to succeeding.
- The decisive defeat at *Culloden* (1746) broke Highland military capacity and Scottish independence aspirations for generations; the battle lasted only an hour.
- Government response was merciless: executions, forfeiture of lands, and systematic dismantling of the clan system followed; over 1,200 Jacobites died at Culloden.
- The government banned Highland dress, tartan, and Gaelic language—cultural erasure designed to break Highland identity and prevent future resistance.
- The aftermath enabled the *Highland Clearances*, transforming feudal clan tenure into capitalist sheep farming, displacing thousands and depopulating the Highlands.
