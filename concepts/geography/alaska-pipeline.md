## summary

The **Alaska Pipeline**, officially designated the **Trans-Alaska Pipeline System** (**TAPS**), is an 800-mile crude oil conduit extending north-to-south across the rugged wilderness of [[alaska]] from *Prudhoe Bay* on the [[arctic-ocean]] to the ice-free maritime port of *Valdez* on *Prince William Sound*. Constructed between 1974 and 1977 in the wake of the 1973 global energy crisis, it ranks among the most ambitious, politically contentious, and expensive civil engineering achievements in the history of the [[united-states]]. The system reshaped northern resource geopolitics, created the economic bedrock of modern state governance through the *Alaska Permanent Fund*, and pioneered groundbreaking cold-weather engineering to operate reliably across active seismic faults and fragile subarctic permafrost.

## you gotta know

- Built between 1974 and 1977 to convey crude petroleum from vast fields at *Prudhoe Bay* to the tanker terminal at *Valdez*, spanning eight hundred miles across the entirety of [[alaska]].
- Spurred by the 1968 discovery of oil on the *North Slope* by *ARCO* and *Exxon*, gaining sudden federal urgency during the 1973 Arab oil embargo and passing Congress with a tie-breaking vote by Vice President *Spiro Agnew*.
- Traverses three imposing mountain ranges—the *Brooks Range*, the *Alaska Range*, and the *Chugach Mountains*—reaching its crowning geographical summit of 4,739 feet at *Atigun Pass*.
- Engineered with an elevated, above-ground zigzag configuration mounted on vertical support members equipped with heat pipes and ammonia thermosyphons to prevent hot oil from melting delicate permafrost.
- Crosses the massive subarctic [[yukon-river]] over the wooden-decked *E. L. Patton Bridge*, flanked along its northern half by the *James W. Dalton Highway*, which was constructed as an essential supply artery.
- Accelerated the passage of the *Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act* of 1971, extinguishing aboriginal title across vast territories in exchange for cash and regional corporate land ownership to clear the pipeline right-of-way.
- Terminated at the deepwater port of *Valdez*, which subsequently became the epicenter of a massive environmental disaster in 1989 when the supertanker *Exxon Valdez* grounded on *Bligh Reef*.
- Outfitted with Teflon-coated sliding shoes and flexible joint loops to absorb tectonic shifts, allowing it to survive the severe 7.9-magnitude *Denali Fault earthquake* in 2002 without rupturing.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the federal republic that authorized construction through executive and congressional mandates to lessen dependence on foreign oil imports.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical contest whose strategic resource imperatives and national defense considerations prioritized domestic arctic petroleum security.
- [[canada]] — the adjacent northern nation whose territory was evaluated as an alternative overland route before designers selected an all-Alaskan maritime transit corridor.
- [[arctic-ocean]] — the northern marine basin whose coastal shelf at the *Beaufort Sea* holds the prolific oil reservoirs that feed the pipeline.
- [[yukon-river]] — the monumental northern river spanned by a specialized 2,295-foot bridge carrying both the conduit and commercial vehicular traffic.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the historical era of mechanized industrialization and fossil-fuel reliance that necessitated continental-scale hydrocarbon extraction infrastructure.
- [[mississippi-river]] — peer continental transport corridor in the lower forty-eight states often contrasted in American economic geography with northern transit systems.
