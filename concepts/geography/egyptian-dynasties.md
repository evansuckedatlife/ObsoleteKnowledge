# Egyptian Dynasties

## summary

**Egyptian dynasties** refer to the system of periodization used to organize ancient Egypt's three-millennia history into distinct royal houses and cultural epochs. Scholars divide the span from circa 3100 BCE (unification under the First Dynasty) to 30 BCE (Roman conquest) into major kingdoms—the Old Kingdom (pyramid-building era), Middle Kingdom (classical age), and New Kingdom (imperial peak)—separated by "intermediate periods" of fragmentation. This framework maps the rise and fall of dynasties, shifting power centers, and transformations in art, religion, and statecraft.

## you gotta know

- The **Old Kingdom** (c. 2686–2181 BCE) was the pyramid age; the Great Pyramids of Giza and the Sphinx were built under the Fourth Dynasty; centered in Memphis; collapsed into fragmentation.
- The **Middle Kingdom** (c. 2055–1650 BCE) saw cultural and administrative consolidation; art and literature flourished; Egypt extended control to Nubia; ended with invasion by the Hyksos.
- The **New Kingdom** (c. 1550–1070 BCE) was Egypt's imperial zenith; Thebes became the capital; pharaohs like Thutmose III and Ramesses II commanded vast territories; temples and tombs (including the Valley of the Kings) reached monumental scale.
- The **Ptolemaic dynasty** (305–30 BCE) was founded by Alexander the Great's general after his death; Greek-speaking, it ruled from Alexandria and blended Greek and Egyptian culture; Cleopatra VII was its last ruler.
- Major breaks—the First and Second Intermediate Periods—marked times when central authority fragmented and regional governors (nomarchs) held power.
- The entire chronology depends on overlapping king lists, astronomical alignments, and textual references; exact dates remain debated by Egyptologists.
- Dynasties were matrilineal in some periods; succession sometimes followed the female line, and marriage between relatives was practiced.

## connections

- [[ancient-egypt]] — the civilization whose history the dynasty system periodizes.
- [[pharaoh]] — the divine rulers who constituted each dynasty's lineage.
- [[nile-river]] — the geographic axis that determined where capitals arose and cultural centers shifted.
- [[cleopatra]] — the final Ptolemaic ruler, whose reign ended with Rome's conquest of Egypt.
- [[pyramids]] — monumental tombs built by Old Kingdom pharaohs, especially the Fourth Dynasty.
- [[hieroglyphics]] — the writing system that preserved records of dynasties, kings, and events via inscriptions and papyri.
