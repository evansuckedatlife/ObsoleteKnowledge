## summary

The **Viking Age** was a transformative period of early medieval history from the late eighth century to the mid-eleventh century, defining the era when Scandinavian maritime power peaked globally through extensive raiding, commercial expansion, and continental settlement. Originating in *Scandinavia*, Norse seafarers utilized revolutionary shipbuilding to navigate the open expanses of the [[atlantic-ocean]], dominate the [[baltic-sea]], and penetrate the major waterways of [[europe]]. These wide-ranging journeys linked territories from [[england]] and [[france]] to the [[middle-east]] and [[canada]], fundamentally altering regional politics and commerce before Scandinavia assimilated into medieval European culture and [[christianity]].

## you gotta know

- Began traditionally in 793 with the plundering of the holy monastery on *Lindisfarne* off the northeast coast of [[england]], an infamous seaborne assault that announced centuries of unpredictable raids across the coastal regions of [[europe]].
- Concluded essentially in 1066 at the *Battle of Stamford Bridge*, where English forces under *King Harold Godwinson* decisively defeated the invading Norwegian king *Harald Hardrada*, breaking Scandinavian military ambitions in [[england]] shortly before the Norman Conquest.
- Relied upon technologically sophisticated clinker-built longships featuring symmetrical designs, light oak frames, and shallow drafts, allowing warriors to cross stormy seas and maneuver through shallow rivers for rapid surprise attacks.
- Reached westward across the northern [[atlantic-ocean]] to establish permanent commonwealths on [[iceland]], outposts in Greenland under *Erik the Red*, and the short-lived settlement of *Vinland* in modern-day [[canada]] led by *Leif Erikson*, half a millennium before the [[age-of-exploration]].
- Carved out permanent political footholds across Western Europe, including the expansive *Danelaw* territory across eastern [[england]], the vibrant trading port of Dublin in Ireland, and the Duchy of Normandy granted to the Norse leader *Rollo* in northern [[france]].
- Extended commercial networks eastward through the *Varangian trade route*, navigating the Dnieper and Volga river systems through Slavic lands to reach Constantinople and trade amber, furs, and captives for silver dirhams from [[islam]] in the [[middle-east]].
- Transformed domestic Scandinavian society through royal conversions from indigenous Norse polytheism to [[christianity]], a gradual cultural shift championed by monarchs like Denmark's *Harald Bluetooth* and Norway's *Olaf Tryggvason* to centralize political authority and gain legitimacy among European peers.

## connections

- [[england]] — suffered the initial *Lindisfarne* attack, saw northern territories governed under the *Danelaw*, and hosted the era-ending *Battle of Stamford Bridge*.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — served as the primary nautical corridor for Norse expansion to the Faroes, [[iceland]], Greenland, and North America.
- [[france]] — endured repeated raids along the Seine before King *Charles the Simple* ceded Normandy to *Rollo* to protect Paris.
- [[baltic-sea]] — acted as the core maritime highway connecting Scandinavian emporia like *Birka* and *Hedeby* with continental eastern Europe.
- [[christianity]] — represented both the faith of monastery targets plundered for silver and the religion whose adoption eventually concluded the pagan era.
- [[canada]] — preserves *L'Anse aux Meadows* in Newfoundland, the singular confirmed archaeological site of Norse settlement in North America.
- [[middle-east]] — supplied millions of silver coins through thriving Eurasian river trade connecting Scandinavian merchants with the Islamic world.
