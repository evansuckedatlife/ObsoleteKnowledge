## summary

**New Brunswick and Nova Scotia**, which together with Prince Edward Island constitute the historic Canadian region known as the **Maritime Provinces** or simply **The Maritimes**, are two coastal provinces located along the Atlantic seaboard of eastern [[canada]]. Connected physically to one another by the narrow Isthmus of Chignecto and flanking the world-famous Bay of Fundy, the two provinces share a deep coastal history rooted in Indigenous Mi'kmaq territory, French Acadian settlement, and British imperial administration. As original founding partners of Canadian Confederation in 1867, they remain vital centers of Atlantic commerce, shipbuilding, and maritime culture.

## you gotta know

- Separated by the Bay of Fundy, a dynamic body of water possessing the highest tidal range on Earth, where water levels rise over fifty feet twice daily between the two provinces.
- Joined geographically by the narrow Isthmus of Chignecto, a low-lying marshy neck of land that links the Nova Scotian peninsula to the North American continent through New Brunswick.
- New Brunswick stands as Canada's only officially bilingual province, legally guaranteeing equal status to English and French under provincial statutes and the Canadian Charter.
- Originally inhabited by the Mi'kmaq and Maliseet peoples before becoming the French colony of Acadia, which saw the tragic 1755 Great Upheaval (*Grand Dérangement*) when British authorities forcibly expelled thousands of Acadians.
- Both provinces entered Canadian Confederation as original signatory partners on July 1, 1867, following the landmark Charlottetown and Quebec constitutional conferences.
- Nova Scotia's capital, Halifax, features one of the world's deepest natural ice-free harbors and was the site of the catastrophic 1917 Halifax Explosion during [[world-war-i]].
- Settled heavily following the American Revolutionary War by thousands of United Empire Loyalists who fled the newly independent [[united-states]], prompting the formal partition of New Brunswick from Nova Scotia in 1784.
- Observe Atlantic Standard Time, placing their regional clocks thirty minutes behind the adjacent [[newfoundland-time-zone]] located across the Cabot Strait.

## connections

- [[canada]] — two of the four original founding provinces that united under the British North America Act of 1867.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — dominant marine body shaping regional fisheries, weather systems, naval harbors, and coastal history.
- [[newfoundland-time-zone]] — adjacent eastern time zone operating thirty minutes ahead of Maritime clocks on the island of Newfoundland.
- [[british-empire]] — colonial ruler that expelled the Acadians, resettled Loyalist refugees, and fortified naval installations at Halifax.
- [[france]] — European empire that originally established seventeenth-century Acadia at settlements like Port-Royal.
- [[colonialism]] — historical process of imperial conflict, territorial partitioning, and displacement of indigenous Mi'kmaq communities.
- [[united-states]] — southern neighbor whose revolutionary war prompted massive migration of Loyalist refugees to Maritime shores.
