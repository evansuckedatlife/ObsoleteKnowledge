## summary

The **Forbidden City** is a massive imperial palace complex in central Beijing, China, built during the Ming Dynasty (1406–1420) as the residence of Chinese emperors and the administrative heart of imperial governance. Named "forbidden" because commoners were forbidden entry without imperial permission, it sprawls across 178 acres and contains nearly 1,000 buildings organized into nested courtyards and gardens, representing the apex of classical Chinese architecture and the cosmological order. Its geography within Beijing—positioned at the city's center along the north-south axis—reflected imperial ideology, making it both a physical structure and a spatial embodiment of centralized Chinese authority.

## you gotta know

- Constructed under the Yongle Emperor of the Ming Dynasty (1406–1420), replacing the Yuan Dynasty's capital elsewhere and establishing Beijing as the capital city.
- Contains approximately 980 buildings across 108 acres of walled grounds, organized by function (administrative, residential, ceremonial) within hierarchically nested courtyards.
- The spatial layout embodies Chinese cosmological principles: the main north-south axis aligns with the north celestial pole, and the emperor's residence is positioned at the cosmic center.
- Served as home and workplace for 24 emperors across the Ming (1368–1644) and Qing (1644–1912) dynasties, encompassing nearly 500 years of imperial history.
- Remained physically isolated from Beijing's urban grid due to protective walls, creating a "forbidden" zone separate from ordinary city life.
- Suffered damage during the Boxer Rebellion (1900), foreign occupation, and Chinese Civil War; now a UNESCO World Heritage site and major museum, opened to the public in 1949.
