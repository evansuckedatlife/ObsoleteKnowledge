## summary

**Annapolis** is the capital city of the U.S. state of Maryland, located along the mouth of the *Severn River* where it opens into the *Chesapeake Bay*. Founded in 1649 by Puritan settlers as *Providence* and later renamed to honor the future *Queen Anne* of Great Britain, the historic seaport briefly functioned as the temporary capital of the nation under the *Articles of Confederation*. Today, Annapolis is renowned internationally as the seat of the *United States Naval Academy*, a premier center for American collegiate education and sailing culture, and a hub of early American colonial architecture proudly known by the local moniker **Crabtown**.

## you gotta know

- Served as the temporary capital of the United States from November 1783 to August 1784, when the Congress of the Confederation met within the historic *Maryland State House*.
- Site of *George Washington's* momentous voluntary resignation of his military commission as commander-in-chief of the Continental Army on December 23, 1783, establishing civilian authority over the military.
- Location of the formal congressional ratification of the 1783 *Treaty of Paris*, which officially concluded the American Revolutionary War, signed into law on January 14, 1784.
- Home to the *United States Naval Academy*, founded in 1845 by Secretary of the Navy *George Bancroft* at the site of historic *Fort Severn* to train officers for maritime service.
- Hosted the 1786 *Annapolis Convention*, a commercial interstate conference led by *Alexander Hamilton* and *James Madison* that directly called for the landmark 1787 Philadelphia Constitutional Convention.
- Features the historic *Maryland State House*, completed in 1779, which remains the oldest U.S. state capitol building in continuous legislative use and boasts the largest wooden dome built without nails in the country.
- Contains the crypt and elaborate marble sarcophagus of Revolutionary War naval commander *John Paul Jones*, permanently entombed beneath the dome of the *Naval Academy Chapel*.
- Preserves eighteenth-century colonial estate landscapes such as the *William Paca House and Garden*, reflecting the regional living collection principles of an [[arboretum]] overlooking the waterways of the bay.
