## summary

**Memphis** is the shared name of two internationally renowned riverine cities: the monumental ancient capital of Lower Egypt located at the entrance to the *[[nile-river]]* delta, and the cultural crossroads city situated atop the Fourth Chickasaw Bluff along the *[[mississippi-river]]* in southwestern Tennessee. In antiquity, **Memphis Egypt**—originally known as **Inebu-hedj**—served for millennia as a sacred royal administrative metropolis, the theological sanctuary of the creator god *Ptah*, and the necropolitan gateway to the royal pyramids at Saqqara, Dahshur, and Giza. In American history, **Memphis Tennessee** arose as an indispensable river port, the celebrated cradle of blues, rock and roll, and soul music, and the tragic epicenter of the *[[civil-rights-movement]]*.

## you gotta know

- Founded around 3100 BCE by the unifier of Upper and Lower Egypt, pharaoh *Menes* (frequently identified with *Narmer*), who constructed dikes along the Nile to establish the fortress city of *Inebu-hedj* ("White Walls").
- Preserved the great temple precinct of *Hut-ka-Ptah* ("Enclosure of the Ka of Ptah"), the primary sanctuary of the artisan god *Ptah*, which furnished the Greek name *Aigyptos* that ultimately produced the modern name of *[[egypt]]*.
- Flanked by an immense necropolis stretching over twenty miles, serving as the administrative court for the Old Kingdom kings who erected the Step Pyramid of Djoser at Saqqara and the pyramids of Giza.
- Founded in the *[[united-states]]* in 1819 by future president *Andrew Jackson*, *John Overton*, and *James Winchester*, who named the city after its Egyptian predecessor because of its commanding bluffs over the Mississippi River.
- Site of the tragic assassination of *Martin Luther King Jr.* on April 4, 1968, at the *Lorraine Motel*, where the civil rights leader had arrived to support striking municipal sanitation workers.
- Celebrated worldwide as the birthplace of modern American popular music, home to *Sun Studio* where *Sam Phillips* recorded *Elvis Presley*, *Johnny Cash*, and *Howlin' Wolf*, alongside the classic soul catalog of *Stax Records*.
- Captured by Union gunboats during the naval Battle of Memphis in June 1862 during the *[[american-civil-war]]*, transforming the city into a strategic federal staging base and hospital center.
- Anchored by the historic *Beale Street* entertainment district, designated by Congress as the *"Home of the Blues"* and popularized by pioneering African American composers like *W. C. Handy*.

## connections

- `[[egypt]]` — the ancient civilization whose dynastic kings ruled from the northern capital of Memphis for many centuries.
- `[[nile-river]]` — the life-giving river whose annual floodwaters and commercial transport enabled the ancient capital to flourish.
- `[[mississippi-river]]` — the great North American inland waterway upon whose bluffs the Tennessee city was established to control cotton traffic.
- `[[united-states]]` — the nation within which the Tennessee city evolved into an essential transportation junction, logistics capital, and cultural icon.
- `[[civil-rights-movement]]` — the epic campaign for racial equality that reached a climactic, heartbreaking turning point with Dr. King's assassination in Memphis.
- `[[american-civil-war]]` — the struggle between Union and Confederate forces that resulted in the rapid capture and wartime occupation of the Tennessee riverfront.
- `[[ancient-greece]]` — the Mediterranean culture whose travelers and historians, including *Herodotus*, visited Egyptian Memphis and transcribed its dynastic legends.
