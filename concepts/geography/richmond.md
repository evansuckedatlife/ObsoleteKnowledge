## summary

**Richmond** is the capital of Virginia, situated on the James River in the state's central region. Founded in 1737, Richmond rose to prominence as a colonial port and became deeply entangled in American revolutionary history. Later, it served as the capital of the Confederate States of America during the Civil War, making it a site of profound historical conflict. Today, Richmond is Virginia's economic and cultural center, preserving colonial architecture and serving as a window into American political and social history.

## you gotta know

- Richmond was founded as a trading post by William Byrd II and formally chartered as a city in 1742; its location on the James River made it a vital colonial port.
- Patrick Henry, the revolutionary orator, made his famous "Give me liberty, or give me death!" speech in Richmond in 1775, galvanizing colonial resistance.
- Richmond became Virginia's capital in 1780, moving from Williamsburg, and was briefly the capital of the newly independent United States during the Revolution.
- The city was captured and partially burned by British forces under General Benedict Arnold in 1781, demonstrating the war's reach into American heartland.
- From 1861 to 1865, Richmond served as the capital of the Confederate States of America, making it the political and military center of Southern resistance during the Civil War.
- General Robert E. Lee made Richmond his military headquarters, and the city saw intense fighting and multiple Union attempts to capture it before its fall in 1865.
- Modern Richmond preserves Civil War battlefield sites, colonial-era buildings, and 18th-century homes; the city has emerged as a cultural hub with museums, theaters, and universities.
