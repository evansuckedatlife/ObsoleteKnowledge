## summary

The **Equator** is the imaginary great circle around the Earth situated at zero degrees latitude, equidistant from the North and South Poles and dividing the planet into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. Spanning approximately 40,075 kilometers, this planetary baseline experiences nearly constant twelve-hour days throughout the year and receives the highest annual solar irradiance on Earth. Understanding its unique geography, climate systems, and rotational dynamics is essential in physical geography, astronomy, meteorology, and the history of geodesy.

## you gotta know

- Defined as the line of zero degrees latitude, it spans approximately 40,075 kilometers (24,901 miles) across the globe and serves as the fundamental reference line for the geographic coordinate system.
- Traverses thirteen sovereign nations across three continents: Ecuador, Colombia, and Brazil in [[south-america]]; São Tomé and Príncipe, Gabon, the Republic of the Congo, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, Kenya, and Somalia in Africa; and Indonesia, with its territorial waters also encompassing the Maldives and Kiribati in [[southeast-asia]] and the Pacific.
- Marked by the equatorial bulge resulting from Earth's axial rotation, creating an oblate spheroid where the equatorial radius exceeds the polar radius by approximately twenty-one kilometers, which places the summit of Mount *Chimborazo* in the [[andes]] farther from the center of the Earth than Mount *Everest*.
- Features the highest point on the line on the southern slopes of Mount *Cayambe* in Ecuador at an elevation of 4,690 meters, which represents the only location on the line with permanent snow cover.
- Experiences a theoretical *Coriolis force* of zero, which prevents the formation of tropical cyclones within roughly five degrees latitude of the line and generates the calm equatorial maritime region traditionally known as the *doldrums* or the *Intertropical Convergence Zone*.
- Serves as the most fuel-efficient latitude for space vehicle launches into geostationary orbit because Earth's eastward rotational surface speed peaks here at approximately 1,670 kilometers per hour, conferring maximum initial momentum to orbital payloads.
- Served as the focal destination of the historic 1735 *French Geodesic Mission* sent by the *Académie des Sciences* of [[france]] during the [[enlightenment]] to measure a degree of meridian arc in Spanish colonial [[south-america]], confirming *Isaac Newton*'s theory that Earth flattens at the poles.

## connections

- [[south-america]] — the continent where three nations lie along the line, including Ecuador, which derives its sovereign name directly from the geographic feature.
- [[andes]] — the massive mountain range crossed by the line, giving rise to unique high-altitude equatorial climates and snow-capped peaks like *Cayambe*.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean basin crossed by the line at the Gulf of Guinea and off the northeastern coast of Brazil, hosting major equatorial current systems.
- [[nile-river]] — the great river system whose equatorial headwaters and lakes, including Lake Victoria, lie immediately adjacent to and across the line.
- [[southeast-asia]] — the maritime and island region where the line bisects the archipelago of Indonesia across Sumatra, Borneo, Sulawesi, and Halmahera.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the historical era when European navigators crossed the line into the Southern Hemisphere, giving rise to maritime traditions such as line-crossing ceremonies.
- [[enlightenment]] — the intellectual period during which scientific expeditions measured the equatorial meridian to resolve major debates over planetary geometry.
