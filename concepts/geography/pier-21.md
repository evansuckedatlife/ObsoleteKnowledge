## summary

**Pier 21** is a historic ocean liner terminal and immigration shed situated on the harbor waterfront of Halifax, Nova Scotia, in [[canada]]. Operating continuously from 1928 until its closure in 1971, the facility served as the premier maritime port of entry for more than one million immigrants, refugees, and displaced persons arriving across the [[atlantic-ocean]]. Often celebrated as the "Gateway to Canada" and regarded as the northern counterpart to Ellis Island in [[new-york-city]], it was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1997 and now houses the Canadian Museum of Immigration.

## you gotta know

- Handled more than 1.5 million transatlantic passenger arrivals between 1928 and 1971, processing roughly one-third of all immigrants who settled in [[canada]] during the twentieth century.
- Served as the principal staging and embarkation port for approximately 500,000 Canadian military personnel sailing overseas to [[europe]] during [[world-war-ii]], as well as the arrival depot for returning wounded veterans.
- Processed nearly 48,000 British and European "war brides" and their roughly 22,000 children who journeyed across the [[atlantic-ocean]] following [[world-war-ii]] to establish new domestic lives with Canadian servicemen.
- Welcomed tens of thousands of displaced persons and political refugees fleeing Soviet-dominated Central and Eastern Europe during the early [[cold-war]], including massive influxes of exiles from [[poland]], Czechoslovakia, and survivors of the 1956 Hungarian Revolution.
- Integrated directly with the transcontinental railway network, routing newly landed passengers through medical inspections and customs halls directly into Canadian National Railway colonist passenger railcars bound for the Canadian Prairies and western provinces.
- Handled the postwar Italian migration wave during the 1950s and 1960s, which comprised the largest single ethnic cohort to pass through the terminal's processing wickets in the modern era.
- Ceased active immigration operations in March 1971 due to the rise of commercial jet aviation, which rendered transatlantic maritime passenger liners obsolete for transoceanic travel.
- Reopened as an interpretive museum and research archive in 1999, later elevated in 2011 to become the Canadian Museum of Immigration at Pier 21, the only national museum situated in Atlantic Canada outside the National Capital Region.

## connections

- [[canada]] — the dominion nation whose cultural mosaic and demographic growth were fundamentally shaped by arrivals at this Halifax terminal.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the northern ocean passage crossed by transatlantic liners docking at Halifax Harbour's Ocean Terminals.
- [[poland]] — Central European state from which thousands of wartime refugees and anti-communist exiles fled to settle in Canadian communities.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict during which the shed served as a high-security military embarkation depot for Canadian troops and returning war brides.
- [[cold-war]] — the postwar geopolitical struggle that drove waves of Eastern European defectors and displaced persons through the port.
- [[new-york-city]] — the American metropolis housing Ellis Island, the landmark federal processing depot with which this site is constantly compared.
- [[point-pelee-national-park]] — fellow Canadian historic and geographical landmark managed under the federal conservation and national parks system.
- [[plymouth]] — English Channel naval port from which many British emigrants, troop convoys, and ocean transports departed for Canadian waters.
