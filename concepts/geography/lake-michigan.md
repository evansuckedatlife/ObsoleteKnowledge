## summary

**Lake Michigan** is the third-largest of the Great Lakes by surface area and the second-largest by volume, notable as the only Great Lake located entirely within the [[united-states]]. Hydrologically joined to [[lake-huron]] through the wide, deep Straits of Mackinac, it is bordered by the states of Michigan, Indiana, Illinois, and Wisconsin. As the maritime gateway for Chicago and Milwaukee, it anchors vital shipping networks that connect Great Lakes commerce to the drainage basin of the [[mississippi-river]].

## you gotta know

- Stands as the only Great Lake situated completely within the borders of the [[united-states]], possessing no shoreline in [[canada]].
- Forms a single hydrologic body of water with [[lake-huron]] across the four-mile-wide *Straits of Mackinac*, sharing identical surface water levels at 577 feet above sea level.
- Bordered by the metropolitan area of *Chicago*, serving as the city's primary source of industrial cooling, shipping access, and municipal drinking water.
- Connected artificially to the watershed of the [[mississippi-river]] through the *Chicago Sanitary and Ship Canal*, which completed the civil engineering feat of reversing the Chicago River in 1900.
- Features the largest system of freshwater coastal sand dunes on Earth, highlighted by protected preserves at *Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore* and *Indiana Dunes National Park*.
- Supplies water that moves through the lower Great Lakes chain toward [[lake-ontario]] and out through the St. Lawrence Seaway to the [[atlantic-ocean]].
- Hosted significant heavy industry along its southern shoreline, notably in the steel manufacturing centers of *Gary, Indiana*, and South Chicago.
- Heavily impacted by aquatic invasive species introduced via transoceanic ballast water, most prominently the sea lamprey, alewife, zebra mussel, and quagga mussel.

## connections

- [[lake-huron]] — the hydrologic twin lake connected directly across the Straits of Mackinac.
- [[lake-ontario]] — the downstream Great Lake that eventually receives outflow originating in Lake Michigan.
- [[united-states]] — the country within which the entirety of Lake Michigan's waters and shoreline are contained.
- [[mississippi-river]] — the major continental river system linked to Lake Michigan via the engineered waterways at Chicago.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the era of rapid development that made the lake's southern shores a hub of steel mills, grain elevators, and freight railways.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean terminal reached by commercial freighters departing Lake Michigan via the St. Lawrence Seaway.
