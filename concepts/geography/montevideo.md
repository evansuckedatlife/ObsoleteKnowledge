## summary

**Montevideo**, officially founded as **San Felipe y Santiago de Montevideo**, is the capital, chief seaport, and largest metropolis of Uruguay, positioned on the northeastern shore of the Río de la Plata estuary. Established in 1724 as a Spanish defensive stronghold against Portuguese expansion, the city grew into a prosperous maritime commercial hub that now concentrates roughly one-third of the nation's population. It is internationally celebrated for hosting the inaugural FIFA World Cup, its vital role in early twentieth-century naval history, and as the headquarters of South American trade governance.

## you gotta know

- Established in 1724 by Spanish military governor *Bruno Mauricio de Zabala* to counter Portuguese encroachments from neighbouring Colonia del Sacramento.
- Traces its name to maritime tradition, traditionally attributed to a lookout aboard *Ferdinand Magellan's* voyage exclaiming "Monte vide eu" ("I see a hill") upon sighting the conical *Cerro de Montevideo*.
- Hosted every match of the inaugural 1930 FIFA World Cup, anchored by the newly constructed *Estadio Centenario*, where the Uruguayan national football team defeated Argentina in the final.
- Stood as the dramatic backdrop to the 1939 Battle of the River Plate, the first naval engagement of [[world-war-ii]], culminating in the scuttling of the German cruiser *Admiral Graf Spee* just outside the harbor.
- Preserves its historical core in the *Ciudad Vieja*, which opens through the remaining gate of the colonial citadel onto the *Plaza Independencia*, site of the *Palacio Salvo* and the mausoleum of *José Gervasio Artigas*.
- Features the *Rambla*, an uninterrupted twenty-two-kilometre coastal promenade along the estuary that anchors local civic culture, beach life, and the ubiquitous custom of sharing yerba mate.
- Functions as the diplomatic capital of regional economic integration, hosting the administrative secretariats of both Mercosur and the Latin American Integration Association.
