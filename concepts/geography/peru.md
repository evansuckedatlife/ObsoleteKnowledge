## summary

**Peru** is a South American nation straddling the Andes mountains, the Amazon rainforest, and the Pacific coast, making it one of the continent's most geographically diverse regions. It is the heartland of the Inca civilization and retains profound indigenous cultural roots. Peru's rich archaeological heritage, biodiversity, and modern society make it central to understanding both pre-Columbian Americas and contemporary South America.

## you gotta know

- Home to Machu Picchu, the 15th-century Inca citadel hidden in the Andes and now one of the world's most iconic archaeological sites, perched at 2,430 meters elevation.
- The Inca Empire, centered in Peru with its capital at Cusco, was the largest pre-Columbian state in the Americas and controlled vast territories across South America through sophisticated administration.
- The Quechua people, descendants of the Inca, still inhabit the Andes and speak Quechua as their primary language, preserving indigenous traditions, knowledge systems, and cultural practices.
- Peru contains both the highest peaks of South America (in the Andes) and vast tropical rainforest (the Amazon), creating extreme ecological contrasts within its borders.
- The Spanish conquest in the 16th century under Francisco Pizarro devastated Inca civilization and established colonial rule that lasted until independence in 1821.
- Peru's economy historically depended on precious metals and mining; the legendary Potosí mines, though in neighboring Bolivia, were connected to Peruvian trade networks and commerce.
- Modern Peru struggles with political instability and inequality but remains a major global source of copper, other minerals, and agricultural products like quinoa and avocado.
- The Moche and Nazca civilizations preceded the Inca, leaving mysterious geoglyphs and sophisticated pottery that reveal advanced pre-Inca cultures and artistic traditions.
- Peru's geography and resources have made it a crossroads of indigenous empires and colonial powers, shaping its diverse mestizo culture and economic development patterns.
- The Peruvian current, also known as the Humboldt Current, flows north along Peru's Pacific coast, making it one of the world's richest fishing grounds.
