## summary

**Federico García Lorca** was a celebrated Spanish poet, dramatist, and theatre director who stood as one of the preeminent literary figures of the 20th century. A central member of the avant-garde *Generation of '27*, he combined traditional Andalusian folklore, Romani song, and flamenco traditions with striking modernist surrealism. His tragic execution by Nationalist forces at the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War silenced a brilliant voice and turned him into an enduring international symbol of artistic martyrdom.

## you gotta know

- Achieved explosive fame across the Spanish-speaking world with his poetry collection *Romancero gitano* (*Gypsy Ballads*), which celebrated Andalusian folk culture and mythic themes.
- Formulated the famous aesthetic theory of *duende*, a mysterious, visceral artistic power rooted in the earth, suffering, and an acute awareness of mortality.
- Wrote a haunting, surrealist critique of industrial capitalism and alienation titled *Poeta en Nueva York* (*Poet in New York*), composed during his stay in [[new-york-city]].
- Penned a world-famous rural dramatic trilogy exploring repression, passion, and fate, consisting of *Bodas de sangre* (*Blood Wedding*), *Yerma*, and *La casa de Bernarda Alba* (*The House of Bernarda Alba*).
- Directed *La Barraca*, a government-sponsored touring university theatre company that brought classical Spanish drama to rural villages across [[spain]].
- Mourned the death of a close friend in the monumental elegy *Llanto por Ignacio Sánchez Mejías*, famous for the repetitive refrain *"a las cinco de la tarde"*.
- Maintained intimate friendships and creative collaborations with leading Spanish modernists, including painter *Salvador Dalí* and filmmaker *Luis Buñuel*.
- Arrested and summarily executed by Francoist militias near *Granada* in August 1936 due to his outspoken left-wing sympathies, artistic rebellion, and homosexuality.

## connections

- [[spain]] — the Iberian nation whose cultural landscapes, folk traditions, and civil conflict profoundly shaped his literary production and destiny.
- [[poetry]] — the primary creative medium through which he explored grief, passion, surrealist metaphors, and Andalusian music.
- [[tragedy]] — the classical dramatic genre he resurrected in rural Spanish settings, depicting honor killings, maternal tyranny, and inescapable destiny.
- [[modernism]] — the international 20th-century artistic movement that influenced his experimental imagery and his participation in the *Generation of '27*.
- [[new-york-city]] — the towering American metropolis where he witnessed the 1929 stock market crash, inspiring his bitter critique of modern urban mechanization.
- [[romanticism]] — the earlier expressive movement whose fascination with ballads, folklore, and rebel outcasts served as a historical precursor to his work.
