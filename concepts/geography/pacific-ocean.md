## summary

The **Pacific Ocean** is Earth's largest and deepest ocean, spanning nearly a third of the planet's surface and separating Asia from the Americas. Named by Ferdinand Magellan for its apparent tranquility, it is studded with thousands of islands, rimmed by volcanic peaks, and traversed by trade routes of immense strategic importance. Its scale and remoteness shaped European exploration, Pacific island cultures, and the geopolitics of global commerce and naval power.

## you gotta know

- Covers approximately 165 million square kilometers, larger than all other oceans combined and accounting for roughly 46% of world ocean surface; nearly half of Earth's water.
- Bordered by the "Ring of Fire"—a volcanic arc encircling the basin—responsible for the majority of Earth's active volcanoes and frequent earthquakes worldwide.
- Contains thousands of islands, from continental landmasses like Australia and Japan to tiny atolls and remote archipelagos with distinct cultures and navigational expertise.
- Named by Ferdinand Magellan in the 16th century during his circumnavigation voyage; he called it the "Mar Pacifico" for its apparent calm relative to Atlantic storms.
- Became increasingly important to European and American imperial ambitions in the 19th and 20th centuries, especially after the opening of Asian trade routes and markets.
- Borders North America's western coast (Canada and the United States) and provides crucial trade access to Asian markets and economic partners.
- Home to unique island civilizations including Polynesia, Melanesia, and Micronesia, with distinct languages, cultures, and sophisticated navigational traditions developed over centuries.
