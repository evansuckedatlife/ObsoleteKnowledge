## summary

**Georgia** names two distinct geographical entities of historical and contemporary significance. *Georgia the U.S. state* (part of the Deep South, with Atlanta as its major metropolis) is known for its agricultural wealth, Appalachian foothills, and pivotal role in American Civil War history and civil rights movements. *Georgia the Caucasus nation* (in the South Caucasus between Europe and Asia, straddling the Russia-Turkey-Iran borderlands) is an ancient crossroads of empires, religion, and trade. Both occupy culturally and geopolitically contested zones where powerful neighbors have shaped their trajectories.

## you gotta know

- **Georgia (U.S. state):** One of the thirteen original colonies and ratifier of the Constitution; lies in the American Southeast with the Appalachian foothills in the north and Atlantic coastal plain in the southeast.
- **Georgia (Caucasus):** Located between the Black Sea and the Caspian, bordered by Russia (north), Turkey (southwest), and Azerbaijan (southeast); its capital, Tbilisi, sits on the Mtkvari River in a strategic east-west passage.
- *Atlanta*, Georgia's (U.S.) capital, emerged as a major railroad hub and industrial city; served as General *Sherman*'s invasion point during his 1864 march through the Deep South toward Savannah.
- Mount Elbrus, the highest peak in the Caucasus (18,510 feet) and Europe's tallest mountain by some measures, stands on Georgia's (Caucasus) northern border with Russia.
- The U.S. state was central to both slavery-era plantation economy and Reconstruction-era political transformation; *John Lewis*, the civil rights leader, represented Georgia in Congress.
- Georgia (Caucasus) was historically a kingdom and later absorbed into the Russian and Soviet empires; reasserted independence in 1991 and remains contested between Russian and Western spheres of influence.
