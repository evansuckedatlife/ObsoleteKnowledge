## summary

**Canton** (the historic Western exonym for **Guangzhou**, also romanized as **Kwangchow**) is a massive port metropolis in southern [[china]], situated along the Pearl River (Zhu Jiang) near the South China Sea. Serving for centuries as the southern maritime terminus of the Silk Road, it held a legendary commercial monopoly under the Qing dynasty's Canton System, making it the sole Chinese port legally open to Western trade prior to the Opium Wars. As the capital of Guangdong province and the epicenter of Cantonese language and culture, Canton has repeatedly functioned as a revolutionary crucible that reshaped modern East Asian history.

## you gotta know

- Governed Western commerce between 1757 and 1842 through the *Canton System*, an imperial Qing trade monopoly that confined European merchants to riverside merchant compounds known as the *Thirteen Factories* outside the city walls.
- Handled all foreign trade through the *Cohong*, an exclusive guild of Chinese merchant houses (headed by wealthy figures like *Howqua*) that regulated transactions and collected duties on behalf of the imperial court.
- Served as the focal point for the outbreak of the First Opium War in 1839, when Imperial Commissioner *Lin Zexu* confiscated and destroyed over twenty thousand chests of British-smuggled opium at nearby Humen (the Bogue).
- Opened as one of five initial treaty ports under the 1842 *Treaty of Nanking*, which dismantled the Cohong monopoly, ceded neighbouring Hong Kong to the [[british-empire]], and ended Canton's exclusive status as the trade capital of China.
- Sparked the Second Opium War (Arrow War) in 1856 following the seizure of the British-flagged merchant ship *Arrow* in the Pearl River harbour, leading to Anglo-French military occupation of the city.
- Acted as the cradle of Chinese republicanism in the early twentieth century, serving as the political revolutionary headquarters of *Sun Yat-sen*, who established the Kuomintang military government here.
- Founded the prestigious *Whampoa Military Academy* on an island in the Pearl River in 1924, training both Nationalist and Chinese Communist military leaders under superintendent *Chiang Kai-shek* and political director *Zhou Enlai*.
- Renowned globally for Cantonese cuisine (*Yue* cuisine), celebrated for dim sum, fresh seafood, roast meats, and the traditional teahouse practice of *yum cha*.

## connections

- [[china]] — chief commercial and cultural metropolis of the Lingnan region and Guangdong province.
- [[british-empire]] — primary Western mercantile power that clashed with Qing officials over the tea, silk, and opium trade at the port.
- [[colonialism]] — subjected to Western gunboat diplomacy, unequal treaties, and foreign concessions on Shamian Island.
- [[cape-of-good-hope]] — rounded by European East Indiamen on the multi-month ocean voyage delivering silver to Canton and returning with tea.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — fueled British manufactured exports and driven by consumer demand for Canton tea, textiles, and porcelain.
- [[world-war-ii]] — suffered devastating Japanese aerial bombardment and occupation between 1938 and 1945.
- [[linguistics]] — epicentre of the Yue branch of the Chinese language family, commonly known worldwide as Cantonese.
