## summary

**Hispaniola**, named *La Española* by European navigators and known originally as *Quisqueya* or *Bohio* to the indigenous Taíno, is the second-largest island in the West Indies, situated in the Caribbean Sea within the [[atlantic-ocean]]. Divided politically between the Francophone nation of Haiti in the west and the Hispanophone Dominican Republic in the east, it is the most populous island in the Americas. As the site of the earliest permanent European settlements in the New World during the [[age-of-exploration]], the island was central to the catastrophic disruption of indigenous life, the transatlantic slave trade, and landmark anti-colonial revolutions.

## you gotta know

- Encountered by *Christopher Columbus* in December 1492 during his first voyage across the [[atlantic-ocean]], who named it *La Isla Española* after his flagship *Santa María* ran aground along its northern coast.
- Inhabited prior to European arrival by hundreds of thousands of indigenous *Taíno* people, who were organized into five distinct chiefdoms (*cacicazgos*) and largely decimated by disease and forced labor.
- Home to *Santo Domingo*, established by *Bartholomew Columbus* in 1496 on the southern coast, which remains the oldest continuously inhabited European-founded settlement in the Americas.
- Divided in 1697 by the Treaty of Ryswick, in which [[spain]] formally ceded the western third of the island to [[france]], creating the lucrative plantation colony of *Saint-Domingue*.
- Catalyst for the historic Haitian Revolution beginning in 1791, an unprecedented successful uprising of enslaved people inspired partly by the [[french-revolution]] and led by *Toussaint Louverture*.
- Marked by extreme ecological and geographical contrast, containing the Caribbean's highest peak, *Pico Duarte*, as well as its lowest point and largest saltwater lake, *Lake Enriquillo*.
- Endured repeated interventions and prolonged military occupations by the [[united-states]] throughout the early twentieth century to protect financial interests and secure naval routes.

## connections

- [[age-of-exploration]] — the historical era inaugurated in the Americas when European voyages established their initial footholds on the island.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean that surrounds the West Indies and carried early exploratory fleets, trading vessels, and enslaved peoples to the island.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian power whose royal crown funded early voyages to the island and founded its historic colonial capital at Santo Domingo.
- [[france]] — the European kingdom that colonized the western third of the island, creating the brutally productive sugar colony of Saint-Domingue.
- [[colonialism]] — the system of foreign imperial domination that transformed the island through exploitation, plantations, and enslavement.
- [[french-revolution]] — the European political upheaval that ignited debates over emancipation and accelerated the Haitian Revolution on the island.
- [[united-states]] — the continental power that occupied both nations of the island during the early twentieth century under the Roosevelt Corollary.
