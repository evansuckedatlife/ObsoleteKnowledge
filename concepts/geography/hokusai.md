## summary

**Katsushika Hokusai**, widely recognized across the globe simply as **Hokusai**, was a prolific Japanese painter, draftsperson, and ukiyo-e woodblock printmaker of the late *Edo period*. Active throughout eight decades of artistic creation in the city of *Edo*, he revolutionized Japanese landscape art by portraying natural scenery and everyday labor with unprecedented vitality and compositional daring. His immortal masterwork, the print series *Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji*, established Japan's sacred volcano as a global visual icon and exerted an immense, transformative impact on the rise of Western [[impressionism]].

## you gotta know

- Created *The Great Wave off Kanagawa*, the most famous woodblock print in history, capturing towering crested waves that frame *Mount Fuji* in low perspective.
- Designed the celebrated multi-print landscape series *Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji*, published in the 1830s using newly imported synthetic *Prussian blue* pigment.
- Painted *Fine Wind, Clear Morning*, often referred to as *Red Fuji*, depicting the volcano's slopes glowing with rust-red autumn illumination under scattered clouds.
- Compiled the fifteen-volume *Hokusai Manga*, an encyclopedic compendium of thousands of lively sketches depicting acrobatics, folk deities, animals, and architectural designs.
- Adopted more than thirty artistic pseudonyms over his long career, famously signing his later works *Gakyo Rojin Manji*, meaning *The Old Man Mad About Painting*.
- Spearheaded the global phenomenon of *Japonisme* in nineteenth-century [[europe]], directly inspiring masters such as *Vincent van Gogh*, *Claude Monet*, and *Edgar Degas*.
- Practiced *Nichiren Buddhism*, which deeply informed his spiritual reverence for mountain landscapes and his obsession with attaining artistic immortality past age one hundred.
- Produced notable ghost illustrations, including *The Ghost of Oiwa*, as well as erotic shunga masterpieces such as *The Dream of the Fisherman's Wife*.

## connections

- [[japan]] — island nation where he lived and worked throughout the Tokugawa shogunate.
- [[impressionism]] — European art movement whose leading figures adopted his daring asymmetrical compositions and flat color planes.
- [[modernism]] — broad aesthetic movement whose pioneers drew heavily upon his graphic styling and expressive linear freedom.
- [[realism]] — nineteenth-century Western artistic tendency that admired his honest depictions of working-class artisans and common tradespeople.
- [[europe]] — continent where his exported woodcuts set off widespread artistic revolutions following the opening of Japanese treaty ports.
- [[china]] — traditional cultural source whose classical paintings, folklore, and mythological narratives influenced his formative technical training.
