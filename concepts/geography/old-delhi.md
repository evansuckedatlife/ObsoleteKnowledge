## summary

**Old Delhi**, originally founded as **Shahjahanabad** in 1639 by the Mughal emperor *Shah Jahan*, is a historic walled city within the National Capital Territory of Delhi, [[india]]. Renowned for its monumental red sandstone architecture, sprawling bazaars, and labyrinthine alleys, it served as the political seat of the Mughal Empire until the fall of the dynasty following the 1857 rebellion. Today, colloquially known as **Purani Dilli**, it remains a vital spiritual, cultural, and commercial heart of northern India, standing in sharp contrast to the planned imperial avenues of New Delhi designed during the era of the [[british-empire]].

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1639 when *Shah Jahan* decided to shift the Mughal capital from Agra, naming the new fortress city *Shahjahanabad*.
- Dominated by the colossal *Red Fort* (*Lal Qila*), constructed from red sandstone along the banks of the Yamuna River, where the Prime Minister of [[india]] hoists the national flag every Independence Day.
- Contains the *Jama Masjid*, one of the largest and most venerated mosques of [[islam]] in South Asia, commissioned by *Shah Jahan* and completed under imperial supervision in 1656.
- Bisected by *Chandni Chowk* ("Moonlight Square"), a historic thoroughfare historically designed by the emperor's daughter *Jahanara Begum*, originally centered on a reflective canal that mirrored the moon.
- Retains several surviving historic gates of its original defensive wall, including the *Kashmiri Gate*, *Ajmeri Gate*, *Delhi Gate*, and *Turkman Gate*.
- Suffered immense destruction, looting, and exile of its Mughal nobility after the British crushed the 1857 Indian Rebellion, which brought the formal end of Mughal authority and inaugurated direct rule by the [[british-empire]].
- Celebrated for its distinct culinary heritage, especially the street foods of *Paranthe Wali Gali* and traditional Mughlai feasts prepared around the historic quarters of the walled city.

## connections

- [[india]] — the nation in which this historic Mughal capital forms the cultural core of modern Delhi.
- [[islam]] — the faith whose architecture and community life define the city's grand monuments, notably the *Jama Masjid*.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power that besieged the city in 1857 and later constructed New Delhi to project imperial supremacy.
- [[colonialism]] — the geopolitical dynamic that drastically altered the urban fabric and demography of the historic metropolis.
- [[palace-of-westminster]] — the legislative seat of government in London that oversaw direct Crown governance of Delhi after 1858.
