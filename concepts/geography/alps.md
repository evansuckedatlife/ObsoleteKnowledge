## summary

The **Alps** are the highest mountain range in Europe, stretching roughly 750 miles across eight countries and forming a natural barrier between northern and southern Europe. Rising to 15,771 feet at *Mont Blanc*, they have shaped European history, culture, and trade routes for millennia. The Alps remain central to European identity—a symbol of natural grandeur, a crucible of mountaineering, and a defining feature of the continent's geography.

## you gotta know

- The highest mountains in Europe, with *Mont Blanc* as the highest peak; home to some forty peaks above 13,000 feet.
- Span eight countries: France, Italy, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Austria, Germany, Slovenia, and Croatia; Switzerland occupies the largest portion.
- Formed by the collision of the African and European tectonic plates beginning roughly 80 million years ago; uplift continues.
- Heavily glaciated; contain the largest glaciers in Europe outside of Scandinavia, though many are retreating rapidly due to climate change.
- Critical watershed: the Alps feed major European rivers including the Rhône, Rhine, Danube, and Po.
- Historically a formidable barrier that shaped political boundaries, trade routes, and military strategy; mountain passes like the *Great St. Bernard Pass* enabled cultural exchange.
- Center of modern mountaineering; *Mont Blanc* and *the Matterhorn* attracted climbers from the 18th century onward and defined the sport.
- Home to distinctive Alpine cultures, flora, and fauna; the term "Alpine" now describes the characteristic landscape and ecology.
- Known for dangerous climbing conditions; avalanches, crevasses, and extreme weather make the Alps a testing ground for mountaineering technique and equipment innovation.
