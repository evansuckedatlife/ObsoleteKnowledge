## summary

**Banff National Park** is the oldest national park in [[canada]], established in 1885 within the Canadian Rocky Mountains of southwestern Alberta. Covering over 6,600 square kilometres of mountainous terrain, the park encompasses dramatic alpine glaciated valleys, dense coniferous forests, and iconic turquoise glacial waters. Originally founded around commercialized natural thermal hot springs along transcontinental rail lines, the reserve evolved into a cornerstone of North American wilderness conservation and alpine tourism.

## you gotta know

- Established originally in 1885 as *Rocky Mountains Park* under federal legislation following the discovery of geothermal hot springs at Sulphur Mountain's Cave and Basin.
- Renowned for glacial lakes that display vivid turquoise hues caused by suspended glacial flour, most celebrated at *Lake Louise* and *Lake Moraine* in the Valley of the Ten Peaks.
- Linked northward to *Jasper National Park* via the scenic *Icefields Parkway*, traversing rugged alpine passes beside the expansive *Columbia Icefield*.
- Heavily commercialized by the *Canadian Pacific Railway*, which erected opulent chateau-style grand hotels including the *Banff Springs Hotel* and *Chateau Lake Louise* to attract luxury travelers.
- Occupies the traditional and ancestral territories of Indigenous peoples, including the Stoney Nakoda, Blackfoot Confederacy, and Ktunaxa nations, who used the mineral waters and valleys for millennia.
- Became an international model for ecological mitigation by installing extensive vegetated wildlife overpasses and underpasses across the *Trans-Canada Highway*.
- Forms a key component of the collective UNESCO Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks World Heritage Site alongside neighboring parks *Jasper*, *Yoho*, and *Kootenay*.

## connections

- [[canada]] — North American nation that initiated its federal protected parks system and environmental heritage policy through Banff's creation.
- [[british-empire]] — imperial dominion framework under which nineteenth-century Canadian nation-building and resource management took shape.
- [[united-states]] — neighbor whose 1872 creation of Yellowstone National Park provided the direct institutional precedent for Canadian park preservation.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of railway expansion and steam transportation that physically opened the remote mountain wilderness to international travel.
- [[romanticism]] — aesthetic and philosophical current that elevated sublime alpine vistas and therapeutic wilderness retreats in public culture.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — terminus of the transcontinental railway network that ferried European tourists across Canada to the Rocky Mountains.
