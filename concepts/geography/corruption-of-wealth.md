## summary

**Corruption of Wealth** describes the ethical degeneration, moral carelessness, and spiritual bankruptcy that accompany extreme material privilege. A pervasive thematic preoccupation across global literature and social thought, it analyzes how financial insulation dismantles human empathy and creates systemic injustice. In the *[[united-states]]*, the motif critiques the hollow promises of unbridled capitalism and the myth of meritocratic virtue.

## you gotta know

- Forms the central thematic conflict of *F. Scott Fitzgerald*'s *The Great Gatsby*, where *Tom* and *Daisy Buchanan* embody the reckless cruelty of people who smash up things and retreat into their money.
- Serves as a primary critique during the *Gilded Age* and the *[[industrial-revolution]]*, inspiring naturalist works by *Mark Twain*, *Theodore Dreiser*, and *Edith Wharton*.
- Explored in *[[american-literature]]* as the dark underbelly of the American Dream, exposing how the relentless pursuit of social status breeds deception, exploitation, and spiritual desolation.
- Parallels Southern gothic representations in the *[[deep-south]]*, where decaying plantation dynasties cling to illusory aristocratic prestige built upon brutality and economic exploitation.
- Features prominently in Victorian social realism across *[[england]]* through novelists like *Charles Dickens*, who contrasted industrial magnates with the destitute working class.
- Rooted in ancient philosophical and religious critiques, echoing biblical warnings that the love of money is the root of evil and classical descriptions of oligarchy in *[[ancient-greece]]*.
- Intersects with critiques of imperialism and *[[colonialism]]*, demonstrating how extractive wealth dehumanizes both the colonizer and the colonized.

## connections

- [[american-literature]] — provides the major canvas for analyzing modern capitalist greed and aristocratic decay.
- [[deep-south]] — regional setting where antebellum wealth built on human bondage produced profound cultural rot.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — historical transformation that generated unprecedented financial disparities and urban vice.
- [[modernism]] — literary movement that portrayed post-war disillusionment with bourgeois prosperity and empty consumerism.
- [[new-york-city]] — geographic epicenter of American high finance, stock speculation, and conspicuous consumption.
- [[realism]] — nineteenth-century artistic style dedicated to exposing the harsh socio-economic truths behind elite polish.
- [[colonialism]] — systemic exploitation where vast riches accumulated abroad poisoned metropolitan domestic morality.
