## summary

**Algiers**, known in Arabic as **El Djazaïr** meaning "The Islands", is the capital and largest metropolis of Algeria, situated along a crescent bay on the southern shores of the [[mediterranean-sea]]. Often nicknamed *Alger la Blanche* ("Algiers the White") due to its shimmering white buildings rising from the waterfront up steep coastal hills, it was historically an influential Barbary corsair base and later the administrative nucleus of French [[colonialism]] in North Africa. The city was also the primary battleground of the Algerian War of Independence, immortalized in political philosophy and cinematic history.

## you gotta know

- Its historic center is the UNESCO-listed *Casbah of Algiers*, an ancient, densely populated walled citadel filled with Ottoman palaces, winding alleys, and steep public stairways.
- Flourished from the sixteenth to early nineteenth centuries as a primary stronghold of the Barbary corsairs, ruled by naval commanders such as the Ottoman admiral *Hayreddin Barbarossa*.
- Spanish author *Miguel de Cervantes* was captured by Barbary corsairs in 1575 and held prisoner as a slave in Algiers for five years before being ransomed, an experience that influenced *Don Quixote*.
- Invaded and colonized by [[france]] in 1830 following the infamous "Fly Whisk Incident" between the French consul and the Dey of Algiers, initiating over 130 years of French colonial dominance.
- Served as the temporary capital of Free France under General *Charles de Gaulle* during [[world-war-ii]] following Allied amphibious landings in *Operation Torch* in November 1942.
- The urban theater of the brutal *Battle of Algiers* (1956–1957), where the Algerian National Liberation Front (FLN) waged an urban guerrilla campaign against French paratroopers.
- Overlooked by the imposing *Monument of the Martyrs* (*Maqam Echahid*), an iconic concrete architectural landmark erected in 1982 to commemorate those who died in the Algerian War.
- Features the landmark basilica *Notre-Dame d'Afrique*, perched on a cliff overlooking the bay with the syncretic inscription praying for both Christians and Muslims.

## connections

- [[france]] — European state that invaded the city in 1830 and incorporated it as an integral administrative department until 1962.
- [[colonialism]] — historical global movement that shaped the modern urban geography and painful twentieth-century conflicts of the city.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — major body of water onto whose southwestern bay the city opens.
- [[islam]] — primary faith of the city's inhabitants since the early medieval period, visible in its historic mosques and shrines.
- [[middle-east]] — cultural and geopolitical region closely connected with the Arab-Berber heritage and history of the city.
- [[cold-war]] — postwar era during which the city became a diplomatic capital for the Non-Aligned Movement and global anti-colonial movements.
- [[alhambra]] — architectural gem across the western Mediterranean tied culturally to the Andalusian refugees who settled in the city's Casbah.
