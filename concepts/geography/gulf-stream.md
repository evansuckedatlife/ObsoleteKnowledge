## summary

The **Gulf Stream** is a powerful, warm western boundary current in the North Atlantic that originates in the Florida Straits and flows northeastward along the eastern coast of North America before transitioning into the North Atlantic Drift. Driven by wind stress and the global thermohaline circulation, it transports vast amounts of thermal energy from the tropics to northwestern Europe. It serves as a vital component of Earth's climate system, profoundly moderating regional temperatures and shaping historical maritime navigation routes across the globe.

## you gotta know

- Originates as warm water exiting the Gulf of Mexico through the Straits of Florida between Cuba and the Florida Keys, fed in part by currents sweeping past [[havana]].
- Flows north-northeast along the coastline of the [[united-states]], hugging the continental shelf before detaching near Cape Hatteras, North Carolina, into deep open waters.
- Continues eastward as the North Atlantic Current, delivering warm water that dramatically moderates winter climates across western and northern [[europe]], keeping ports ice-free in nations like [[england]] and Norway.
- First mapped scientifically for postal and shipping navigation by *Benjamin Franklin* and his cousin *Timothy Folger* in 1769 to explain why British mail packets sailed more slowly than American merchant ships.
- Forms the western and northern boundary of the North Atlantic Subtropical Gyre, trapping floating Sargassum seaweed in the calm interior waters known as the Sargasso Sea.
- Transports nearly four billion cubic feet of water per second near Cape Hatteras, a volume measured in sverdrups that vastly exceeds the combined outflow of all the world's freshwater rivers.
- Plays a central role in the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (*AMOC*), where warm, saline surface waters cool and sink in the subpolar North Atlantic to drive deep global ocean conveyor currents.

## connections

- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the primary ocean basin whose surface gyres and overturning circulation are anchored by this current.
- [[europe]] — the continent whose mild oceanic climate is heavily sustained by the northward transport of tropical warmth.
- [[united-states]] — the nation whose eastern seaboard directly borders and shapes the initial path of the current.
- [[canada]] — the coastal region where the current encounters cold subpolar waters near Newfoundland and the Grand Banks.
- [[england]] — an island nation experiencing milder winters than its northern latitude would typically dictate due to Atlantic drift waters.
- [[havana]] — the historic Cuban port located along the Florida Straits where early Spanish treasure fleets caught the current outbound for Europe.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the historical era during which navigator *Juan Ponce de León* first documented the current's immense force against sailing ships.
