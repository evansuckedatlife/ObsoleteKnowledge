# Central Europe

## summary

**Central Europe** is a cultural and geographic region encompassing the lands between Western Europe and the *Soviet Union*, historically centered on the *Danube* basin and the *Austro-Hungarian Empire*. Its peoples (Poles, Czechs, Hungarians, Slovaks, Romanians) speak Indo-European languages and predominantly practice Christianity. The region has been perpetually contested: claimed by empires to the west and east, and carved up by the world wars, Cold War, and post-Cold War reconfiguration.

## you gotta know

- Geographically bounded by the Alps to the west and the *Carpathian Mountains* to the east; the *Danube* River flows through its heart, historically linking it economically to the *Balkans* and *Black Sea*.
- The *Austro-Hungarian Empire* (1867–1918) was the dominant regional power, uniting Czechs, Hungarians, Poles, Romanians, and South Slavs under a dual monarchy; its collapse after WWI created modern nation-states.
- Divided after WWII: Western zones became independent or neutral, while Soviet-controlled zones became satellite states of the *Soviet Union* until 1989–1991.
- The *Prague Spring* (1968) symbolized the region's desire for autonomy; Soviet tanks crushed it, reinforcing Soviet dominance and the *Cold War* divide.
- Post-1989, Central European nations (Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary, Slovakia) joined *NATO* and the *European Union*, reorienting westward and integrating into Western institutions.
- Culturally and linguistically distinct from both Western Europe (Romance/Germanic languages) and Eastern Europe/Russia (Slavic); this "in-between" status has shaped its historical trajectory.
- Modern Central Europe is relatively prosperous and stable compared to neighbors, though nationalist movements and illiberal governance have emerged in recent years.
