## summary

The **Partition of India** in 1947 was the geopolitical and humanitarian catastrophe that split the British Indian subcontinent into independent India and Pakistan along religious lines. Conceived as a solution to majority-Muslim and majority-Hindu regions, the partition instead triggered mass migration, communal violence, and between 200,000 and 2 million deaths. The event redefined South Asian geopolitics and remains a source of tension and trauma across the region.

## you gotta know

- The partition was announced on August 15, 1947, as British rule ended; India and Pakistan became independent nations on that date, with Mountbatten's Plan proposing the division.
- Muhammad Ali Jinnah led the Indian National Muslim League's demand for Pakistan, arguing that Muslims needed their own nation-state separate from Hindu-majority India.
- Mahatma Gandhi opposed partition, advocating instead for a unified independent India with safeguards for religious minorities; his assassination just months later was by a Hindu extremist opposed to his pluralism.
- The partition created Pakistan in two geographically separated regions: West Pakistan and East Pakistan (which later became Bangladesh in 1971 after civil war and Indian military intervention).
- Approximately 15 million people were displaced as Hindus and Sikhs fled Pakistan and Muslims fled India; entire communities were uprooted within weeks, creating refugee camps and humanitarian crises.
- Communal violence and massacres accompanied the partition; the Rawalpindi Massacre and violence in Delhi and Punjab were among the deadliest, with communities turned against neighbors.
- The partition left the disputed territory of Kashmir unresolved, leading to three wars between India and Pakistan and ongoing tensions that persist into the 21st century.
- The borders drawn by British administrator Radcliffe, who had never visited India, separated families, severed economic ties, and created lasting resentments and geopolitical instability.
- Partition fundamentally reshaped South Asia: it created the world's largest Muslim-majority nation (Pakistan) and left India as a Hindu-plurality state, reshaping regional politics permanently.
