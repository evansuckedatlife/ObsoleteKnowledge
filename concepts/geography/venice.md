## summary

**Venice** is a historic city-state built on lagoon islands in northeastern Italy, founded in the 5th century and rising to become a Mediterranean superpower during the Middle Ages and Renaissance. Once the heart of a maritime empire controlling crucial trade routes between Europe and the Orient, Venice developed a unique republican government and a cosmopolitan culture that shaped Renaissance art and thought. Today it stands as one of the world's most iconic and architecturally distinct cities—a living museum of Renaissance splendor built entirely on water.

## you gotta know

- Built on 118 small islands connected by approximately 150 canals and 400 bridges, with no roads—a unique geographic adaptation that became architectural identity.
- The *Venetian Republic* (c. 700–1797) was one of history's most successful and longest-lasting republics, rivaling great powers and maintaining its independence for over 1,000 years.
- Dominated Mediterranean trade, especially spice routes from the *Middle East* and *Asia*, accumulating immense wealth and becoming the hub of European-Oriental commerce.
- Produced masterpieces of art and architecture during the *Renaissance*, including works by *Titian*, *Tintoretto*, and *Paolo Veronese*; the *Basilica di San Marco* is an architectural crown jewel.
- The *Doge* (Venetian chief executive) wielded power constrained by law and councils, making Venice a model of checks and balances that influenced later democratic theory.
- Connected to mainland Italy via the *Po Valley*, whose territories fed and enriched the city; control of Po-valley cities was critical to Venetian survival.
- Declined as a superpower after the opening of Atlantic trade routes and European colonial expansion shifted economic power away from the Mediterranean in the 16th–17th centuries.
