## summary

**Calgary** (popularly known as **Cowtown**) is the largest city in the Canadian province of Alberta, situated at the confluence of the *Bow River* and the *Elbow River* in the transition zone between the Canadian Rocky Mountain Foothills and the Canadian Prairies. Established in the late nineteenth century as a frontier post of the North-West Mounted Police, the settlement expanded rapidly following the arrival of the transcontinental railway, which transformed the surrounding grassland into an agricultural and ranching hub. During the twentieth century, major petroleum discoveries reshaped the municipal economy, turning the city into the administrative and commercial capital of the Canadian oil and gas industry. Calgary gained global prominence as the first Canadian host of the Olympic Winter Games in 1988 and continues to attract international visitors each July for the world-famous *Calgary Stampede*.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the host of the *Calgary Stampede*, a world-renowned ten-day rodeo, exhibition, and western festival held every July that honors the region's agricultural roots and cattle heritage.
- Hosted the 1988 Winter Olympic Games, the first Winter Olympics staged in [[canada]], which featured memorable competition at *Canada Olympic Park* and the debut of the Jamaican national bobsled team.
- Built around the confluence of the *Bow River* and the *Elbow River*, providing a picturesque urban valley setting adjacent to the Front Ranges of the Canadian Rockies.
- Rose to become the financial, corporate, and engineering headquarters of the Canadian petroleum sector after the historic *Turner Valley* and *Leduc* oil discoveries.
- Founded in 1875 as *Fort Brisebois* by the North-West Mounted Police, soon renamed *Fort Calgary* by Colonel *James Macleod* after *Calgary Bay* on the Isle of Mull in [[scotland]].
- Connected to eastern commerce in 1883 by the transcontinental tracks of the Canadian Pacific Railway, triggering a dramatic influx of ranchers, homesteaders, and European settlers.
- Frequently experiences the *Chinook*, a warm and dry foehn wind descending from the Rocky Mountains that can raise local winter temperatures by over twenty degrees Celsius within hours.
- Connected downtown by the *Plus 15* Skywalk, one of the world's most extensive climate-controlled pedestrian skybridge networks, designed to shelter pedestrians from harsh winter weather across eighty bridges.

## connections

- [[canada]] — largest municipal population in the province of Alberta and the chief corporate engine of the nation's energy sector.
- [[denver]] — fellow high-altitude energy capital and historic railway terminus situated directly along the eastern flank of the Rocky Mountains.
- [[halifax]] — fellow major Canadian metropolitan center, offering a maritime Atlantic contrast to Calgary's western prairie topography and heritage.
- [[scotland]] — namesake location of the city, which was christened after *Calgary Bay* and *Calgary Castle* on the Isle of Mull.
- [[british-empire]] — established as a British colonial outpost through the paramilitary policing of the North-West Mounted Police.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — spurred into rapid industrial growth by the expansion of continental steam railways and mechanical oil drilling apparatus.
