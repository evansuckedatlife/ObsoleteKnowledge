# Great Plains

## summary

The **Great Plains** is a broad expanse of grassland and prairie stretching from Canada to Texas, between the Rocky Mountains and the Mississippi River. Spanning roughly 500,000 square miles across the central United States and southern Canada, the region is characterized by flat or gently rolling terrain, sparse precipitation, and historically vast herds of buffalo and nomadic Native American peoples. European settlement transformed the plains into an agricultural heartland through ranching and grain cultivation, though the region's harsh climate and boom-and-bust cycles have defined its history.

## you gotta know

- Formed by sediment deposits from ancient glaciers and the Rocky Mountain erosion; the elevation gradually decreases eastward from the Rockies to the Mississippi.
- Precipitation decreases westward; the eastern portions receive enough rain for corn and wheat, while the western High Plains are semi-arid and suited to ranching.
- Home for millennia to Native American peoples—the Comanche, Cheyenne, Sioux, Blackfoot, and others—who hunted buffalo in nomadic bands adapted to grassland life.
- The buffalo (American bison) numbered perhaps 30–60 million before European contact; systematic hunting reduced them to near extinction by 1890, destroying the ecological and cultural foundation of Plains tribes.
- Rapid settlement after the Civil War (Homestead Act of 1862) brought farmers, ranchers, and railroads; the Transcontinental Railroad (completed 1869) accelerated development.
- The Dust Bowl of the 1930s—caused by drought and soil depletion from decades of intensive farming—created an ecological and economic catastrophe, forcing mass migration westward.
- The plains remain the agricultural core of North America; the region produces corn, wheat, and cattle on an industrial scale.
- Climate variability and isolation characterize the region; winters are harsh, summers hot, and economic cycles tied to commodity prices create boom-and-bust dynamics.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the nation that settled, transformed, and economically depend on the plains.
- [[kansas]] — a state that epitomizes the Great Plains experience of agriculture, weather extremes, and cultural isolation.
