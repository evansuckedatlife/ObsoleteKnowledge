## summary

**Birmingham** is the second-largest city in the United Kingdom and the major urban center of the West Midlands region in [[england]]. Rising from a modest medieval market settlement, it emerged during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries as the preeminent manufacturing powerhouse of the Western world. Celebrated as a primary cradle of the [[industrial-revolution]], the city fundamentally transformed global commerce, municipal engineering, and scientific collaboration.

## you gotta know

- Widely acclaimed as the *Workshop of the World* and the *City of a Thousand Trades*, it earned international fame for its dense concentration of small metalworking shops and skilled craftsmen.
- Industrial entrepreneur *Matthew Boulton* founded the famous *Soho Manufactory* here, partnering with *James Watt* to mass-manufacture the commercial steam engines that propelled the [[industrial-revolution]].
- The city hosted the celebrated *Lunar Society*, an extraordinary circle of [[enlightenment]] natural philosophers, inventors, and manufacturers that included *Erasmus Darwin*, *Joseph Priestley*, and *Josiah Wedgwood*.
- Banker *Thomas Attwood* organized the *Birmingham Political Union* in 1829, leading nationwide civic [[activism]] that proved crucial in securing the passage of the *Great Reform Act* of 1832.
- Radical mayor *Joseph Chamberlain* introduced pioneering policies of *municipal socialism* in the 1870s, transferring ownership of gas and water utilities to the city while clearing inner-city slums.
- Industrial districts across the city suffered devastating aerial bombardment by the German Luftwaffe during the *Birmingham Blitz* of [[world-war-ii]] due to its vital armaments and vehicle plants.
- Radical chemist *Joseph Priestley* saw his house, laboratory, and library burned to the ground during the violent *Priestley Riots* of 1791, incited by reactionary mobs opposing the [[french-revolution]].
- Positioned high on the Birmingham Plateau, the municipality constructed an intricate industrial waterway system containing more total canal mileage than Venice.

## connections

- [[england]] — sovereign realm whose industrial and demographic core expanded rapidly around the West Midlands conurbation.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — epochal economic transformation for which the city served as the supreme manufacturing and mechanical center.
- [[enlightenment]] — intellectual era reflected locally in the scientific inquiries and technological innovations of the Lunar Society.
- [[activism]] — history of assertive political organizing demonstrated by the Birmingham Political Union and early trade unionists.
- [[british-empire]] — global maritime empire that absorbed Birmingham's massive output of firearms, hardware, brassware, and trade coinage.
- [[world-war-ii]] — total war during which the city's factories manufactured Spitfire aircraft and rifles, drawing heavy German air raids.
- [[french-revolution]] — political upheaval abroad whose local ideological sympathizers were targeted in the reactionary Priestley Riots.
