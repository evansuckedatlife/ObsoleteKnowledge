## summary

**Cairo** is the capital and largest city of *Egypt*, situated on the *Nile River* in northeastern Africa. Founded in 969 CE as the capital of a Fatimid caliphate, Cairo grew into the Arab world's most populous metropolis and a center of Islamic learning, architecture, and culture. The city's location as gateway to both Mediterranean and Indian Ocean trade, combined with its role as Egypt's political and cultural heart, makes it one of the world's most historically significant urban centers.

## you gotta know

- Founded by Fatimid caliph al-Muizz li-Din Allah as *al-Qahirah* (the Victorious); developed as the capital of a vast Islamic empire.
- Medieval Cairo became famous for its bazaars, mosques, and madrasahs (Islamic schools); the historic Islamic Quarter (Old Cairo) remains one of the world's finest preserved medieval cities.
- The city's strategic location made it a crossroads of trade routes connecting Asia, Africa, and Europe; wealth accumulated through this commerce funded architectural and cultural achievements.
- Served as the center of Islamic scholarship and cultural prestige for centuries; the Al-Azhar Mosque (970 CE) remains one of the world's oldest continuously operating universities.
- The Mamluk era (13th–16th centuries) transformed Cairo into a cosmopolitan metropolis with stunning monuments; Mamluk architecture defines much of medieval Cairo's surviving grandeur.
- Suffered conquest and decline under Ottoman rule (16th–19th centuries), though it remained Egypt's cultural and administrative center.
- Modern Cairo emerged in the 19th century under Muhammad Ali's reforms and Khedival modernization; colonial-era Cairo developed European-style quarters alongside Islamic neighborhoods.
- Today Cairo is the Arab world's largest city (population ~21 million metropolitan area); it faces challenges of congestion, pollution, and rapid urban growth.
