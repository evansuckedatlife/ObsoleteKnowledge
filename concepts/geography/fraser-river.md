## summary

The **Fraser River**, often referred to simply as the **Fraser**, is the longest river in the Canadian province of British Columbia, traveling approximately 1,375 kilometers from the Rocky Mountains to the Pacific Ocean. Draining a vast and topographically dramatic basin of over 220,000 square kilometers, the river flows through massive gorges before forming a rich agricultural delta south of *Vancouver*. The river holds legendary status in Canadian history as the focal point of the 1858 gold rush, an essential transport corridor, and one of the world's most productive habitats for spawning Pacific salmon.

## you gotta know

- Originates near *Fraser Pass* close to *Mount Robson* in the Canadian Rocky Mountains and empties into the *Strait of Georgia* in the Pacific Ocean.
- Named for Scottish-Canadian explorer and fur trader *Simon Fraser*, who led a perilous 1808 expedition down the river for the *North West Company*, mistaking it for the Columbia River.
- Sparked the *Fraser Canyon Gold Rush* of 1858, drawing more than thirty thousand prospectors and prompting the British government to officially establish the mainland *Colony of British Columbia*.
- Contains the dramatic chasm known as *Hell's Gate*, where steep granite cliffs compress the river into a ferocious torrent flowing through a gap only thirty-five meters wide.
- Suffered an ecological disaster at Hell's Gate in 1914 when rock blasting during construction of the *Canadian Northern Railway* triggered a massive rockslide that severely obstructed upstream salmon migration.
- Sustains an immense commercial and cultural salmon fishery, serving as the primary nursery and migration route for millions of *sockeye*, *chinook*, *coho*, *pink*, and *chum* salmon.
- Harbors colossal prehistoric *white sturgeon* (*Acipenser transmontanus*), which can exceed three meters in length and represent the largest freshwater fish species in North America.
- Serves as the economic lifeline and drainage basin for British Columbia's populous Lower Mainland, flowing past communities such as *Prince George*, *Hope*, *Chilliwack*, *Abbotsford*, and *Richmond*.

- [[british-empire]] — prompted British colonial authorities to claim and administer mainland territory to preempt American annexation during the 1858 gold rush.

- [[colonialism]] — transformed indigenous territories through sudden mining migrations, railway construction, and colonial treaties.
- [[united-states]] — drew massive waves of American miners from the California Gold Rush, spurring diplomatic boundary settlements in the Pacific Northwest.
- [[mississippi-river]] — stands alongside other monumental North American river systems that defined continental exploration and commerce.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — fueled British Columbia's logging, mining, railway construction, and industrial salmon canning enterprises throughout the late nineteenth century.
