## summary

The **Indian Ocean** is the world's third-largest ocean, bordered by Africa to the west, Asia to the north and east, and extending south toward the Antarctic. It has been a crucible of maritime trade, cultural exchange, and imperial ambition for millennia, connecting the Mediterranean world to the Far East along ancient spice routes and monsoon currents. Its geopolitical and economic significance shaped the Age of Exploration and remains central to global commerce today.

## you gotta know

- Spans approximately 70 million square kilometers, bounded by Africa, the Arabian Peninsula, India, Southeast Asia, and Australia; the world's third-largest ocean by area.
- Home to crucial trade straits including the Strait of Hormuz and the Strait of Malacca, controlling passage for oil and container shipping; strategic chokepoints of global commerce.
- Historically dominated by monsoon winds, which shaped maritime navigation and determined sailing seasons for traders and explorers; predictable patterns enabled regular trade routes.
- Zheng He's 15th-century Ming treasure fleets dominated this ocean with unprecedented naval power, establishing Chinese prestige across multiple continents before European arrival.
- Major African rivers including the Zambezi and Limpopo discharge into the Indian Ocean, making it the outlet for interior African trade and resource networks.
- Receives warm currents and tropical systems that make it a zone of intense weather and rich marine biodiversity; supports fisheries feeding millions.
- Gateway to Asia for European powers during the Age of Exploration; Portuguese, Dutch, and British rivalries played out on its waters, reshaping global geopolitics.
