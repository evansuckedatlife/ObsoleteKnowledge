# South Pole

## summary

The **South Pole** is the southernmost point on Earth, located at the axis of rotation in *Antarctica*. It is the geographic and symbolic center of Antarctica, one of the most extreme and remote places on the planet, and has been the focus of exploration, scientific research, and international cooperation since the early 20th century.

## you gotta know

- The South Pole is distinguished from the *Magnetic South Pole*, which shifts over time due to Earth's magnetic field; the geographic pole is fixed at the intersection of all meridians.
- *Roald Amundsen*, a Norwegian explorer, reached the South Pole first on December 14, 1911, in a fierce race against the British expedition led by *Robert Falcon Scott*, who arrived 33 days later.
- Scott's expedition ended in tragedy: the team perished in the Antarctic cold while attempting to return, their final camp discovered by a search party the following year, becoming a symbol of polar exploration's deadly risks.
- Antarctica was claimed by multiple nations in the 19th and early 20th centuries, but the *Antarctic Treaty System* (1961) established Antarctica as an international zone dedicated to peaceful scientific research, prohibiting military activity.
- The South Pole is the site of the *Amundsen-Scott South Pole Station*, a major research facility operated jointly by the U.S. and other nations, conducting climate science, astronomy, and atmospheric research.
- The South Pole experiences extreme conditions: temperatures drop to -70°C (-94°F), the region is in total darkness for half the year, and high altitude (nearly 3,000 meters) makes human settlement challenging.
- The polar regions are crucial for understanding climate change; Antarctica's ice sheet contains records of Earth's climate spanning hundreds of thousands of years, making it central to climate science.
