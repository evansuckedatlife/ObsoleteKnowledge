# Canada

## summary

**Canada** is the world's second-largest country by area, occupying the northern portion of the North American continent from the Atlantic to the Pacific and northward to the Arctic Ocean. Originally inhabited by Indigenous peoples for millennia, Canada was colonized by French and British powers, eventually achieving independence within the British Commonwealth and developing into a prosperous, stable democracy. Its vast natural resources, multicultural society, and role as a bridge between Atlantic and Pacific economies make it a significant global player despite its relatively small population concentrated in southern regions near the U.S. border.

## you gotta know

- Canada spans six time zones and encompasses an enormous variety of geography: the *Rocky Mountains* in the west, vast prairies in the center, the *Canadian Shield* (ancient, mineral-rich bedrock) in the north and east, and fertile lowlands in the south.
- *Hudson Bay*, the world's second-largest bay, dominates Canada's north-central geography; it was the center of the fur trade and remains ecologically significant as a subarctic marine ecosystem.
- Canada's southern border with the *United States* is the world's longest international boundary (approximately 8,900 kilometers); the two nations share the *Great Lakes* and major river systems.
- Indigenous peoples inhabited Canada for at least 15,000 years before European contact; hundreds of distinct nations, cultures, and languages existed; colonization by French and British powers devastated Indigenous populations through disease, warfare, and displacement.
- *New France*, established by French explorers in the 16th–17th centuries, centered on the *St. Lawrence River* valley; it was conquered by Britain in the *French and Indian War* (1754–1763), and France ceded all North American territories to Britain.
- *L'Anse aux Meadows* in Newfoundland is archaeological evidence of Norse (Viking) settlement around 1000 CE—the earliest confirmed pre-Columbian European presence in North America.
- Canada achieved Confederation in 1867 as a self-governing dominion within the British Empire; it gradually acquired full sovereignty (completed in 1982 with the *patriation* of its constitution).
- Modern Canada is a multicultural, bilingual nation (English and French official languages) with strong traditions of democracy, rule of law, and social welfare; it remains a Commonwealth realm with the British monarch as head of state.
