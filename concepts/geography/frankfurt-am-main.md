## summary

**Frankfurt am Main**, commonly referred to simply as **Frankfurt**, is a major global financial center situated along the Main River in the western German state of Hesse. Long celebrated as a Free Imperial City and the historic location where emperors of the *Holy Roman Empire* were elected and crowned, the city later hosted the revolutionary 1848 *Frankfurt Parliament*. Today, it serves as the financial engine of continental Europe and a vital transportation crossroads, anchoring the *Frankfurt Stock Exchange* and the *European Central Bank*.

## you gotta know

- Serves as the primary financial hub of continental Europe, housing both the *European Central Bank* (ECB) and Germany's central bank, the *Deutsche Bundesbank*.
- Hosts the *Frankfurt Stock Exchange* (*Börse Frankfurt*), one of the oldest and largest security exchanges in the world, operated by Deutsche Börse and home to the benchmark DAX index.
- Hosted the historic *Frankfurt Parliament* (*Frankfurter Nationalversammlung*) of 1848 in *St. Paul's Church* (*Paulskirche*), which drafted the first democratic constitution for a united German state.
- Served for centuries as the mandatory election site and later the coronation city for emperors of the *Holy Roman Empire* following the Golden Bull of 1356, ceremonies held at *St. Bartholomew's Cathedral* (*Kaiserdom*).
- Celebrated as the birthplace of polymath and writer *Johann Wolfgang von Goethe* in 1749, whose restored family residence is preserved today as the *Frankfurter Goethe-Haus*.
- Hosts the annual *Frankfurt Book Fair* (*Frankfurter Buchmesse*), the largest and most prestigious international publishing trade event in the world, boasting a legacy stretching back to the fifteenth century.
- Features a skyline nicknamed "Mainhattan," distinguished as one of the few European city centers dominated by modern high-rise office towers, including the Norman Foster-designed *Commerzbank Tower*.
- Originated the influential mid-twentieth-century sociological movement known as the *Frankfurt School*, founded by scholars such as *Max Horkheimer*, *Theodor Adorno*, and *Jürgen Habermas* at the Institute for Social Research.
