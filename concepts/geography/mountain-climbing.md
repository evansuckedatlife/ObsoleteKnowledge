## summary

**Mountain climbing**, or mountaineering, is the sport and practice of ascending mountains for sport, exploration, or conquest. What began as extreme mountaineers testing themselves against Alpine peaks evolved into a global pursuit encompassing diverse techniques and environments. The history of mountaineering reflects changing cultural attitudes toward nature, risk, and human capability.

## you gotta know

- Modern mountaineering emerged in the Alps during the 18th and 19th centuries; climbers like Horace-Bénédict de Saussure pioneered Alpine ascents for scientific and sporting purposes.
- The Matterhorn, a spectacular 4,478-meter peak in the Alps, became an iconic symbol of mountaineering when first successfully climbed in 1865; the ascent sparked the modern mountaineering movement.
- Mount Everest, the world's highest mountain at 8,849 meters, was first summited in 1953 by Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay; reaching Everest has become a goal for wealthy climbers worldwide.
- K2, the world's second-highest mountain, remains far more dangerous than Everest; approximately 1 in 4 climbers attempting K2 die, making it the mountaineer's ultimate test.
- The Seven Summits challenge—climbing the highest peak on each continent—has become a major goal in modern mountaineering since the 1980s, popularized by mountaineer Dick Bass.
- Modern mountaineering employs sophisticated equipment including ropes, pitons, carabiners, oxygen systems, and protective gear; altitude sickness and avalanches remain the greatest dangers.
- Women have progressively broken mountaineering's gender barriers; Junko Tabei was the first woman to summit Everest in 1975, opening the sport to broader participation.
- High-altitude mountaineering faces extreme environmental hazards: the "death zone" above 8,000 meters has insufficient oxygen for human acclimatization; climbers risk pulmonary edema and cerebral edema.
- The commercialization of Everest climbing has created ethical tensions between accessibility, environmental preservation, and the physical and psychological demands of mountaineering.
