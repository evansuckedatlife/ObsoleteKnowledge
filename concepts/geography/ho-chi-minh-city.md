## summary

**Ho Chi Minh City**, historically and still colloquially known as *Saigon*, is the most populous city and the primary commercial engine of Vietnam in [[southeast-asia]]. Situated in the south along the Saigon River near the fertile Mekong Delta, the settlement grew from a small Khmer fishing village named *Prey Nokor* into the capital of French Cochinchina and later the capital of the Republic of Vietnam. Following the dramatic conclusion of the Vietnam War in 1975, the city was renamed in honour of the revolutionary communist leader *Ho Chi Minh*, evolving today into a booming manufacturing, financial, and cultural hub.

## you gotta know

- Originally inhabited as a swampy Khmer fishing outpost known as *Prey Nokor* before Vietnamese settlers and military commanders annexed and organized it as *Gia Dinh* in 1698.
- Transformed during nineteenth-century European [[colonialism]] into the capital of French Cochinchina and French Indochina, acquiring broad tree-lined boulevards and the nickname the *Paris of the Orient*.
- Retains distinctive European colonial architecture, notably the red-brick *Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon* and the *Saigon Central Post Office*, which was designed with contributions from *Gustave Eiffel*.
- Served as the national capital of the anti-communist Republic of Vietnam, commonly known as South Vietnam, from 1955 until the unification of the country in 1975.
- Formed the central focal point of the decisive *Fall of Saigon* on April 30, 1975, marked by chaotic American helicopter evacuations during *Operation Frequent Wind* and North Vietnamese tanks crashing through the gates of the Independence Palace.
- Features prominent historical wartime sites such as the *Cu Chi Tunnels*, an immense subterranean network used by the Viet Cong during fighting against the [[united-states]], and the War Remnants Museum.
- Functions as Vietnam's economic engine, driving national development through dynamic private commerce, international shipping, and rapid industrialization along the Saigon River.
