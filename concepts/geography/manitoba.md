## summary

**Manitoba** is a Canadian province situated at the longitudinal heart of [[canada]], bordered by Ontario to the east, Saskatchewan to the west, Nunavut to the north, and the United States to the south. Serving as the easternmost of Canada's three Prairie Provinces, it encompasses expansive southern agricultural plains, ancient Canadian Shield granite, northern boreal forests, and an Arctic coastline along Hudson Bay. In quiz bowl, Manitoba is renowned for the nineteenth-century *Red River Rebellion* led by Métis leader *Louis Riel*, the transformative *Winnipeg General Strike* of 1919, its massive freshwater lakes left behind by glacial *Lake Agassiz*, and the northern port of *Churchill*, celebrated worldwide as the "Polar Bear Capital of the World."

## you gotta know

- The provincial capital and largest metropolitan center is *Winnipeg*, positioned at the confluence of the *Red River* and the *Assiniboine River* at the historic indigenous and trade crossroads known as *The Forks*.
- Entered Canadian Confederation in 1870 as the fifth province via the *Manitoba Act*, which was negotiated following the *Red River Rebellion* led by the Métis statesman *Louis Riel*, who formed a provisional government to protect French Catholic and Métis land rights.
- The subarctic northern town of *Churchill* on the coast of *Hudson Bay* is known internationally as the "Polar Bear Capital of the World," serving as a premier ecotourism and scientific hub for observing polar bear migrations, beluga whales, and the aurora borealis.
- Dominated hydrographically by immense freshwater bodies, including *Lake Winnipeg*, *Lake Manitoba*, and *Lake Winnipegosis*, which represent remnants of the vast prehistoric glacial *Lake Agassiz*.
- Witnessed the historic *Winnipeg General Strike* of May–June 1919, one of the most significant labor actions in North American history, which shut down the city for six weeks before being violently suppressed on "Bloody Saturday" by the Royal Northwest Mounted Police.
- Sparked a profound federal political crisis through the *Manitoba Schools Question* in the 1890s, when the provincial government eliminated public funding for French-language Catholic schools, paving the way for the national electoral victory of *Wilfrid Laurier*.
- Contested during the early fur trade era between the English *Hudson's Bay Company*, which claimed the territory under *Rupert's Land*, and French-Canadian voyageur traders from [[montreal]] operating on behalf of the *North West Company*, culminating in the 1816 *Battle of Seven Oaks*.

## connections

- [[canada]] — the confederation Manitoba entered in 1870, later becoming the agricultural and railway nexus linking eastern Canada to the western prairies.
- [[british-empire]] — the royal authority that granted the 1670 charter for Rupert's Land to the Hudson's Bay Company, establishing British hegemony over the region.
- [[montreal]] — the eastern commercial hub whose North West Company dispatched voyageur fur traders across Manitoba's river systems in rivalry with the Hudson's Bay Company.
- [[colonialism]] — the imperial expansion and settlement policies that displaced First Nations and sparked armed resistance from the Métis population of the Red River Valley.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean connected to Manitoba's northern frontier via Hudson Bay and the Hudson Strait, providing a historic maritime route for Arctic fur-trade shipping.
