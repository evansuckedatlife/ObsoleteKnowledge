## summary

A **speakeasy**, also historically known as a **blind pig** or **blind tiger**, was an illicit establishment operating covertly to sell alcoholic beverages during the era of nationwide Prohibition in the [[united-states]]. Flourishing primarily between 1920 and 1933 following the ratification of the *Eighteenth Amendment* to the [[united-states-constitution]], these clandestine subterranean drinking dens, basements, and backroom saloons spanned urban landscapes from [[new-york-city]] to Chicago. They served as vital social incubators of the [[roaring-twenties]], catalyzing the vibrant rise of the [[jazz-age]], fostering unprecedented interracial and cross-class mingling, and accelerating newly won social freedoms for women before their eventual decline upon the repeal of Prohibition.

## you gotta know

- Proliferated across urban centers in the [[united-states]] to circumvent the restrictions of the *Volstead Act*, relying on secret passwords, brass peepholes, engraved membership cards, and heavily disguised storefronts—such as tearooms, basements, tailor shops, and soda fountains—to screen thirsty patrons at the door.
- Financed and supplied by powerful [[organized-crime]] syndicates led by figures like *Al Capone*, who established intricate bootlegging operations that smuggled illicit spirits across the northern border from [[canada]] and imported European rum and scotch across maritime routes in the [[atlantic-ocean]].
- Served as the primary commercial performance venue for the [[jazz-age]], providing stages where *Duke Ellington*, *Louis Armstrong*, *Bix Beiderbecke*, and *Fats Waller* popularized early [[jazz]] in legendary nightspots such as the *Cotton Club* and *Connie's Inn* in Harlem.
- Dismantled the strictly male preserve of the pre-Prohibition saloon by enthusiastically welcoming female patrons, which directly spurred the rise of flapper fashions, modern cocktail culture, cigarette smoking, and unchaperoned mixed-gender socializing in American nightlife.
- Spawned renowned high-society retreats in [[new-york-city]] such as the *21 Club* on 52nd Street, which famously featured a hidden brick-lined cellar behind a massive two-and-a-half-ton camouflaged door operated by electronic levers, and the Greenwich Village literary haunt *Chumley's*.
- Targeted relentlessly by federal Prohibition Bureau agents, most colorfully the disguise-wearing duo *Izzy Einstein* and *Moe Smith*, who utilized elaborate costumes and theatrical undercover personas to execute thousands of club raids across the nation with an astonishing conviction rate.
- Derived its popular moniker from Pennsylvania saloon keeper *Kate Hester* or nineteenth-century British and American slang advising patrons to 'speak easy' to avoid attracting law enforcement, while terms like *blind pig* originated from saloon owners who charged admission to view exotic animals while providing 'complimentary' alcoholic drinks to bypass licensing.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — the nation where speakeasies flourished nationwide under federal Prohibition between 1920 and 1933.
- [[new-york-city]] — the urban epicenter harboring tens of thousands of illicit clubs, from Greenwich Village bohemian haunts to Harlem dance halls.
- [[jazz]] — the revolutionary musical genre that developed rapidly and reached diverse public audiences on speakeasy bandstands.
- [[united-states-constitution]] — the legal framework whose *Eighteenth Amendment* banned intoxicating liquors and whose *Twenty-First Amendment* ended the speakeasy era.
- [[organized-crime]] — the underworld syndicates that funded, protected, and supplied illicit clubs through lucrative bootlegging operations.
- [[canada]] — the northern neighbor that served as a primary distillery source and transit corridor for illicit liquor smuggled into American venues.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the maritime expanse traversed by 'Rum Row' cargo ships smuggling foreign liquor from Europe and the Caribbean into coastal cities.
