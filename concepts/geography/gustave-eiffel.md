## summary

**Gustave Eiffel** was a French civil engineer renowned for his pioneering work with iron structures and his iconic architectural achievements of the 19th century. Most famously, he designed the Eiffel Tower for the 1889 Paris Exposition and engineered the iron framework of the Statue of Liberty. His innovations in structural engineering transformed how cities and monuments were built, making him one of the defining figures of the Belle Époque.

## you gotta know

- Born in 1832 in Dijon; studied at the École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures, a leading French engineering school; trained in both theoretical and practical engineering disciplines.
- Designed the iron lattice framework for the Statue of Liberty, collaborating with sculptor Frédéric Bartholdi; his engineering made the monument's unprecedented scale and durability possible.
- Created the Eiffel Tower as the entrance arch for the 1889 Paris World's Fair; it rose 1,000 feet and stunned viewers with its daring iron-lattice design, revolutionary for its time.
- Pioneered the use of iron in large-scale public monuments, proving that metal structures could be both functional and aesthetically striking; challenged prevailing architectural conventions of stone.
- Built many bridges, viaducts, and railway structures across Europe; his techniques became the standard for large-span construction and influenced generations of engineers worldwide.
- Remained active in engineering projects late into life; the tower bearing his name became one of the world's most recognizable landmarks and symbols of Paris.
- His work transformed industrial engineering from a purely technical discipline into an art form; influenced modern architecture and the aesthetics of structural design.
