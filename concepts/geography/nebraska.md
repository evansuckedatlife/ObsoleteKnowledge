## summary

**Nebraska** is a state in the central Great Plains of the United States, characterized by vast grasslands, agricultural abundance, and a frontier history shaped by Native American dispossession and European settlement. Admitted to the Union in 1867, Nebraska developed as a major center of agriculture and livestock, earning the nickname **Cornhusker State** and becoming synonymous with the American agricultural heartland. The state gained wider political prominence through the populist and progressive politics of *William Jennings Bryan*, who launched the Democratic Party toward antimonopoly reform from Nebraska's base of power and left an indelible mark on American political thought.

## you gotta know

- Nebraska was acquired by the United States through the Louisiana Purchase (1803) and opened to European settlement through Indian removal policies that displaced Native Americans, particularly the Pawnee and Sioux peoples who had inhabited the Great Plains for centuries.
- The state was traversed by the Oregon Trail and other frontier routes, attracting settlers seeking free land under the Homestead Act (1862), which granted 160 acres to settlers who improved the land for five years; millions of acres passed to European American hands.
- Nebraska's economy developed around agriculture (corn, wheat) and ranching; the state became a breadbasket of the nation and a center of meatpacking and grain processing industries, making it an economic powerhouse in American agriculture.
- William Jennings Bryan, a three-time Democratic presidential candidate from Nebraska, represented agrarian and populist interests against industrial monopolies; his political career and oratory defined late-19th-century American politics and influenced the Democratic Party for generations.
- The Union Pacific Railroad ran through Nebraska, connecting California to the East and making the state a crucial node in transcontinental commerce; railroad politics and land grants shaped Nebraska's infrastructure and political development.
- The 1980s farm crisis devastated Nebraska's agricultural economy as commodity prices collapsed and farm debt accumulated; the state experienced significant economic and social disruption, forcing many rural communities to confront mechanization and consolidation.
- Today Nebraska remains a major agricultural and livestock production center, though the state has diversified into biotechnology and ethanol production, reflecting both agricultural tradition and modernization pressures.
