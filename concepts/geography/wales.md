## summary

**Wales** is a constituent country of the United Kingdom occupying the western peninsula of Great Britain, known for its rugged mountains, castles, and distinctive Celtic culture. Conquered and formally annexed by *England* in 1536, Wales nevertheless retained a strong Welsh-language tradition and cultural identity that survived centuries of assimilation pressure. Today Wales represents a unique blend of British governance and Celtic pride, with a devolved government and a living native language.

## you gotta know

- Mountainous terrain dominated by the *Snowdonian* Alps in the north; geographically isolated from England, which fostered separate cultural development.
- The Welsh language (Cymraeg) is a Celtic language closely related to Cornish and Breton, quite distinct from English, and actively revived in recent decades.
- *Castles* are ubiquitous: Edward I of England built a ring of conquest castles (*Caernarfon*, *Conwy*, *Harlech*) after subjugating Wales in 1282.
- Formally united with England under Henry VIII's Laws in Wales Acts (1536, 1543), ending its independence but not its cultural distinctiveness.
- Industrial revolution: South Wales became a coal-mining and steel-producing powerhouse in the 19th century, driving urbanization and global influence.
- Strong connection to Arthurian legend: many Welsh place names and myths associate Wales with *King Arthur* and the Celts' mythological past.
- The *Welsh National Eisteddfod* is an annual cultural festival celebrating Welsh language, music, and literature—a living continuation of medieval bardic traditions.
- Devolution (1999): Wales gained its own Assembly (later Senedd), allowing home rule in education, health, and culture while remaining part of the UK.
