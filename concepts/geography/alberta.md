## summary

**Alberta**, often nicknamed **Wildrose Country** or abbreviated **AB**, is a western Canadian prairie province bordered by [[british-columbia]] to the west, Saskatchewan to the east, the Northwest Territories to the north, and Montana in the [[united-states]] to the south. Named after Princess Louise Caroline Alberta, fourth daughter of Queen Victoria, it joined [[canada]] as a province in 1905 under the *Alberta Act* alongside Saskatchewan. Known for its vast fossil-fuel reserves in the Athabasca oil sands, fertile agricultural plains, and dramatic Canadian Rocky Mountain landscapes, Alberta is a powerhouse of energy production and a frequent subject in quiz-bowl geography competitions.

## you gotta know

- Edmonton is the provincial capital, celebrated as the "Festival City" and home to the massive West Edmonton Mall, while Calgary is the largest metropolis, famed for the Calgary Stampede rodeo and hosting the 1988 Winter Olympics.
- The Athabasca oil sands around Fort McMurray contain one of the largest proven petroleum reserves on Earth, driving vast bitumen extraction that anchors the provincial economy while generating intense environmental debate over pipeline infrastructure.
- Banff National Park, founded in 1885 around the Cave and Basin hot springs as Canada's first national park, showcases celebrated Rocky Mountain glacial waters including Lake Louise and Moraine Lake in the Valley of the Ten Peaks.
- Jasper National Park, connected to Banff via the high-altitude Icefields Parkway, protects the Columbia Icefield, the Athabasca Glacier, and Mount Columbia, the highest peak in the province.
- Dinosaur Provincial Park in the Red Deer River valley badlands is a UNESCO World Heritage Site renowned for exceptional Cretaceous dinosaur discoveries, supported by the world-leading Royal Tyrrell Museum of Palaeontology in Drumheller.
- The Frank Slide of 1903 remains Canada's deadliest rockslide, occurring when ninety million tons of limestone sheared off Turtle Mountain and smothered the sleeping mining community of Frank in the Crowsnest Pass in ninety seconds.
- Wood Buffalo National Park, straddling the northern border with the Northwest Territories, is Canada's largest national park, sheltering the largest wild herd of wood bison and the only known natural nesting site of the whooping crane.
- Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump in the southern foothills preserves thousands of years of Blackfoot communal hunting practices, where plains bison were driven over a cliff face above the Oldman River basin.

## connections

- [[british-columbia]] — western neighbor across the Continental Divide, sharing the Rocky Mountain crest and major railway transit routes.
- [[canada]] — country to which Alberta was admitted as a province in 1905 by prime minister Sir Wilfrid Laurier.
- [[united-states]] — adjacent to Montana along the 49th parallel, participating in the Waterton-Glacier International Peace Park.
- [[british-empire]] — named for Queen Victoria's fourth daughter, Princess Louise Caroline Alberta, Duchess of Argyll.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — driven by transcontinental railroad construction, coal mining in the passes, and modern petroleum extraction.
- [[world-war-ii]] — site of extensive flight training facilities under the British Commonwealth Air Training Plan and internment camps.
