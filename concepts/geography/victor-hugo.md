## summary

**Victor Hugo** (1802–1885) was the towering figure of French Romanticism and one of Europe's greatest novelists, whose works defined French literature for generations. Through novels like *Les Misérables* and *Notre-Dame de Paris*, he championed the poor and outcast while crafting epic narratives of human suffering and redemption. His political activism and moral vision made him a national conscience—celebrated, exiled, and eventually canonized as France's literary giant.

## you gotta know

- Born into nobility but became an advocate for the poor and oppressed, a journey that shaped all his work.
- First published poetry as a young man and was celebrated by King Louis XVIII before his political views shifted left.
- *Notre-Dame de Paris* (1831, also titled *The Hunchback of Notre-Dame*) was a sensation across Europe, blending Gothic atmosphere with social criticism.
- *Les Misérables* (1862) is his masterwork: an epic novel of Jean Valjean's redemption, poverty, justice, and revolution set against Napoleonic France.
- Exiled from France for 19 years (1851–1870) due to his opposition to Napoleon III; spent years in Belgium and the Channel Islands.
- Also a significant poet and dramatist; his plays broke classical conventions and helped define Romantic drama.
- Used serialization and mass publication to reach working-class readers—making him one of the first truly "popular" literary giants.
- Wrote with moral urgency about injustice: capital punishment, poverty, slavery, women's rights—themes that resonated globally.
