## summary

**Environmental science** is an interdisciplinary academic field that integrates physical, biological, and information sciences to study the environment and solve ecological crises. Rapidly coalescing in the mid-twentieth century—principally across the [[united-states]] and [[europe]] following the [[industrial-revolution]]—the discipline bridges natural sciences like geology, meteorology, and chemistry with conservation policy and resource management. It is essential for measuring planetary boundaries, assessing ecological degradation, and formulating global responses to pollution, biodiversity loss, and anthropogenic climate change.

## you gotta know

- *Rachel Carson*'s 1962 book *Silent Spring* catalyzed the modern environmental movement by exposing how synthetic pesticides such as DDT bioaccumulate through food chains, causing reproductive collapse in raptors and widespread ecological harm across the [[united-states]].
- The discipline gained sweeping institutional authority following the inaugural *Earth Day* in April 1970, which prompted the creation of the Environmental Protection Agency to enforce federal air and water quality standards.
- Research on chlorofluorocarbons (*CFCs*) by atmospheric chemists *Mario Molina* and *F. Sherwood Rowland* revealed the catalytic destruction of the ozone layer, culminating in the 1987 *Montreal Protocol*, widely considered the most effective international environmental treaty.
- Climate change science expanded from nineteenth-century calculations by *Svante Arrhenius*, who demonstrated that carbon dioxide emissions from fossil fuels burned since the [[industrial-revolution]] would enhance the natural greenhouse effect within the [[atmosphere]].
- The *Anthropocene* was popularized by atmospheric chemist *Paul Crutzen* and biologist *Eugene Stoermer* to designate an informal geological epoch in which human activity has become the dominant driver of global environmental change.
- Conservation scientists quantify modern [[biodiversity]] loss as a sixth mass extinction event, documenting contemporary vertebrate extinction rates that exceed natural background rates by hundreds of times.
- The *tragedy of the commons*, formalized by ecologist *Garrett Hardin* in 1968, models how self-interested individuals deplete shared, unowned environmental resources to the long-term detriment of the entire community.
- *Hubbert peak theory*, introduced by geophysicist *M. King Hubbert* in 1956, utilized logistic curves to predict when petroleum extraction rates in an oil-producing region would reach an irreversible physical apex before declining.

## connections

- [[industrial-revolution]] — unleashed mass fossil fuel combustion, urban effluents, and toxic emissions that prompted the development of systematic pollution monitoring.
- [[united-states]] — established foundational environmental statutes, including the Clean Air Act and Wilderness Act, in response to high-profile industrial disasters.
- [[cold-war]] — financed pioneering research into radioactive fallout and global climate simulation that laid the groundwork for modern ecological monitoring systems.
- [[atmosphere]] — the planetary gaseous layer where solar radiation, ozone chemistry, and greenhouse gas accumulation determine the global climate system.
- [[dna]] — provides the molecular architecture for conservation genetics, enabling researchers to track genetic erosion and population bottlenecks in threatened wildlife.
- [[biodiversity]] — serves as the primary metric for evaluating ecosystem resilience, species richness, and biological integrity under human pressure.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — acts as a vital marine heat and carbon sink whose deep overturning circulation regulates global climatic patterns.
