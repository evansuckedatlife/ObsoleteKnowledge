## summary

**New Orleans** is a major port city at the mouth of the *Mississippi River* in southeastern *Louisiana*, founded by the French in 1718. Its unique cultural identity emerged from the collision of French, Spanish, African, and American influences, creating a distinctly polyglot urban society unlike anywhere else in North America. The city became the birthplace of jazz and remains central to American music, literature, and cultural memory as a symbol of cultural hybridity.

## you gotta know

- Founded as *La Nouvelle-Orléans* by French colonists in 1718; transferred to Spanish rule (1763–1803), then purchased by the United States in the *Louisiana Purchase* (1803).
- Developed as a major Atlantic port despite its swampy, hurricane-prone location; geography forced architectural and social innovation including raised architecture and complex drainage systems.
- Enslaved African labor built the city's economy; the slave trade dominated commerce, and slavery remained embedded in daily life longer than elsewhere in the United States.
- The city's unique demographic makeup included free people of color, Creoles (mixed-race descendants of French/Spanish and African ancestry), and immigrants from throughout the Atlantic world.
- Jazz emerged from New Orleans' African, Caribbean, and European musical traditions, nurtured in dance halls, churches, and street parades beginning in the early 20th century.
- *Louis Armstrong* and other jazz pioneers made New Orleans synonymous with the music that would define American culture globally; jazz was born here and nowhere else.
- The city's French Quarter (Vieux Carré) preserves colonial architecture and street culture unlike any other American city; it remains the cultural and tourist heart.
- Devastated by *Hurricane Katrina* (2005), exposing deep racial and economic inequalities; recovery remains ongoing and incomplete, with continuing debates over gentrification.
