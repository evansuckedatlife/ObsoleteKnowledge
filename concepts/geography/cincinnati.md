## summary

**Cincinnati**, historically celebrated as the **Queen City** and colloquially dubbed **Porkopolis**, is a major cultural, commercial, and industrial center in southwestern Ohio along the northern bank of the *Ohio River*. Established in 1788 and named in commemoration of the *Society of the Cincinnati*, the settlement expanded explosively during the nineteenth century as an essential nexus of steamboat commerce, manufacturing, and agricultural transport. Situated directly across from Northern Kentucky on the border between free and slave territories, the city functioned as a vital gateway for the *Underground Railroad* and a northern staging ground during national expansion.

## you gotta know

- Acquired the enduring moniker "Queen of the West" during its antebellum boom years, an epithet famously memorialized by poet *Henry Wadsworth Longfellow* in his tribute to regional viticulture titled *Catawba Wine*.
- Gained the nickname "Porkopolis" during the 1830s as the foremost pork-packing hub in the world, pioneering early disassembly-line processing systems that influenced modern industrial slaughterhouse design.
- Joined to the northern Kentucky cities of Covington and Newport across the river by *John A. Roebling's* landmark *Suspension Bridge*, which served as the physical and structural precursor to his *Brooklyn Bridge* in [[new-york-city]].
- Represented a critical crossing point for enslaved fugitives escaping toward freedom, providing the backdrop and real-world encounters that inspired *Harriet Beecher Stowe* to write *Uncle Tom's Cabin*.
- Houses the densely preserved historic district of *Over-the-Rhine*, settled primarily by waves of nineteenth-century German immigrants who established expansive brewing facilities, underground lagering cellars, and ornate Italianate streetscapes.
- Developed the *Miami and Erie Canal* to link the river basin northward to Lake Erie, facilitating seamless freight transfers between Midwest farm belts and Atlantic maritime shipping networks.
- Formed the home of the *Cincinnati Red Stockings*, founded in 1869 as America's first openly all-professional baseball team, and developed a distinct local culinary tradition featuring Mediterranean-spiced meat chili served over spaghetti.
