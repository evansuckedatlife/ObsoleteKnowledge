## summary

**Lake Placid** is a renowned village and glacier-carved freshwater lake situated in the Adirondack High Peaks of Essex County in northern New York. Revered globally as a premier winter sports destination, it holds the rare distinction of being one of only three locations on Earth to have hosted the Winter Olympic Games twice, in 1932 and 1980. The community achieved immortality in twentieth-century sporting lore as the stage for the dramatic "Miracle on Ice," where an underdog American collegiate squad defeated the powerhouse national hockey team of the [[soviet-union]].

## you gotta know

- Hosted the Winter Olympic Games on two historic occasions, first during the global economic crisis of 1932 and later in 1980, joining St. Moritz and Innsbruck as the only two-time Winter Olympic village hosts.
- Served as the venue for the legendary 1980 *Miracle on Ice*, in which head coach *Herb Brooks* guided a team of amateur American college hockey players to an electric 4–3 upset victory over the four-time defending champion [[soviet-union]].
- Bordered directly by *Mirror Lake*, the scenic downtown body of water around which the commercial center of the village actually sits, while the larger lake named **Lake Placid** extends to the north.
- Preserves the historic farm and burial vault of the radical abolitionist martyr *John Brown*, who was laid to rest on his homestead here following his fateful 1859 raid on the federal armory at Harpers Ferry.
- Developed into a world-class winter retreat by decimal-system cataloger and spelling reformer *Melvil Dewey*, whose exclusive *Lake Placid Club* pioneered winter tourism and organized the 1932 Olympic bid.
- Witnessed the historic sweep of American speed skater *Eric Heiden*, who captured a record-shattering five individual Olympic gold medals across every distance from 500 to 10,000 meters at the 1980 Games.
- Operates ongoing elite Olympic training complexes, including the *Mount Van Hoevenberg* combined bobsled, skeleton, and luge track alongside the towering ski jumping jumps overlooking the Adirondack wilderness.
