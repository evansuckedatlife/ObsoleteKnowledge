## summary

**Miguel de Cervantes** (1547–1616) was a Spanish writer and poet whose masterwork *Don Quixote* stands as one of literature's greatest novels and a foundational work of the modern novel form. Living during Spain's Golden Age, Cervantes experienced military service, captivity, poverty, and finally literary triumph late in life. His innovative narrative technique, psychological depth, and satirical exploration of chivalry and reality established him as a defining figure of Spanish cultural achievement.

## you gotta know

- Born in Alcalá de Henares, central Spain, in 1547; lived through Spain's imperial peak under Philip II and Philip III.
- Participated in the Battle of Lepanto (1571) as a galley soldier; lost the use of his left hand to a gunshot wound; this experience informed his later writing and military knowledge.
- Captured by Barbary corsairs (1575–1580) and held for ransom in Algiers; ransomed by his family; these years of captivity and suffering shaped his later works.
- Published *La Galatea* (1585), a pastoral romance that gained moderate recognition; worked in various administrative positions; experienced financial instability throughout his life.
- Published *Don Quixote* in two volumes (1605 and 1615); the novel became an immediate sensation across Europe, translated into English and French within years, making Cervantes famous but not wealthy.
- *Don Quixote* pioneered the modern novel: psychological realism, narrative complexity, embedded stories, and metafictional awareness of the novel as a form distinguished it from earlier prose narratives.
- The novel satirizes chivalric romance traditions while exploring themes of reality vs. illusion, idealism vs. pragmatism, and the power of storytelling and imagination.
- Continued writing plays, poetry, and shorter works (*Novelas ejemplares*, 1613) until his death in 1616, just one day before William Shakespeare; considered the greatest Spanish writer of all time.
