## summary

The **Amazon Rainforest** is the world's largest tropical rainforest, spanning roughly 5.5 million square kilometers across nine countries with Brazil containing approximately 60% of its territory. Often called "the lungs of the Earth," it generates approximately 6–9% of the world's oxygen and regulates global climate patterns through its vast carbon stores and water cycling. Home to an estimated 10% of all species on Earth and to dozens of indigenous nations, the Amazon remains one of humanity's most biodiverse and ecologically significant ecosystems.

## you gotta know

- Covers an area roughly equivalent to the continental United States; divided among Brazil, Peru, Colombia, and six other nations.
- Named after the *Amazon River*, which flows through it and discharges more water than the next seven largest rivers combined.
- Contains an estimated 10% of all species known to science; roughly half are found nowhere else; thousands of species remain undocumented.
- Home to indigenous peoples including the *Yanomami*, Kayapó, and dozens of other nations with continuous cultural presence spanning millennia.
- Produces roughly 20% of the world's oxygen; absorbs roughly 2 billion tons of carbon dioxide annually, making it critical for climate regulation.
- Experiences "flying rivers"—water vapor released by trees forms rainfall that moves westward and sustains other ecosystems across South America.
- Facing rapid deforestation for cattle ranching, agriculture, and logging; deforestation rates have accelerated dramatically since the 1970s.
- Contains vast medicinal plant diversity; indigenous knowledge systems have identified thousands of pharmaceutical compounds; Western science has explored only a fraction.
