## summary

**Baghdad** (also historically rendered **Bagdad**) is the capital of modern Iraq and the second-largest city in the [[middle-east]]. Founded in the eighth century along the fertile banks of the *Tigris River*, it served as the political, commercial, and intellectual capital of the *Abbasid Caliphate* throughout the golden age of [[islam]]. The metropolis became an unparalleled global center of scholarship, medicine, and philosophy before suffering catastrophic devastation during the Mongol sack of 1258, yet it endured to become a vital administrative center in the modern era.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 762 CE by the second *Abbasid* caliph, *Al-Mansur*, who designed it as a fortified concentric circle officially named *Madinat al-Salam* ("City of Peace"), earning it the famous epithet of the *Round City*.
- Reached its cultural and scientific zenith under caliphs *Harun al-Rashid* and *Al-Ma'mun*, who sponsored the *House of Wisdom* (*Bayt al-Hikma*) to translate classic philosophical and scientific texts from [[ancient-greece]], Persia, and [[india]] into Arabic.
- Provided the rich cosmopolitan backdrop for many legendary tales and framed narratives in the medieval anthology *One Thousand and One Nights*, including stories surrounding the caliph's court and the merchant *Sindbad*.
- Suffered catastrophic destruction in 1258 when the Mongol commander *Hulagu Khan* besieged and sacked the capital, executing caliph *Al-Musta'sim* and reportedly turning the waters of the *Tigris River* black with ink from discarded library books.
- Annexed into the [[ottoman-empire]] in 1534 after being captured by sultan *Suleiman the Magnificent*, enduring centuries of intermittent conflict with neighboring Safavid Persia for regional dominance.
- Seized by armed forces of the [[british-empire]] in March 1917 during the Mesopotamian campaign of [[world-war-i]], after which British authorities established a League of Nations mandate and created the Kingdom of Iraq.
- Features renowned architectural monuments including the thirteenth-century *Mustansiriya Madrasah*, the *Al-Kadhimiya Mosque*, and the prominent modern bronze relief sculpture *Freedom Monument* designed by *Jawad Saleem* on *Tahrir Square*.
- Underwent severe modern devastation and political transformation during the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, particularly through the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War, and the 2003 coalition invasion led by the [[united-states]].

## connections

- [[middle-east]] — major political, economic, and cultural capital situated at the heart of the region.
- [[islam]] — imperial seat of the *Abbasid Caliphate* and historic center of classical theological and legal scholarship.
- [[ancient-greece]] — intellectual origin of philosophical and scientific texts translated into Arabic at the *House of Wisdom*.
- [[ottoman-empire]] — imperial state that conquered the city in 1534 and governed Mesopotamia for nearly four centuries.
- [[world-war-i]] — global conflict that ended Ottoman rule following the British capture of the city in 1917.
- [[british-empire]] — foreign power that held the post-war mandate and delineated the borders of modern Iraq.
- [[ancient-iraq]] — historic cradle of civilization encompassing ancient capitals like Babylon and Ctesiphon located nearby.
- [[united-states]] — nation that spearheaded the 2003 invasion and subsequent military occupation of the Iraqi capital.
