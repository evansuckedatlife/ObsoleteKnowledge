## summary

**Las Vegas**, colloquially known simply as **Vegas**, is the most populous city in the state of Nevada and the primary economic hub of the American Southwest's Mojave Desert. Founded in 1905 and incorporated in 1911, the desert oasis grew rapidly following the construction of the nearby Hoover Dam and the statewide legalization of gambling in 1931. World-renowned as an international capital of entertainment, hospitality, and nightlife, the metropolitan area is globally famous for its lavish resort casinos and mega-hotels.

## you gotta know

- Located within Clark County in southern Nevada, settled originally around natural artesian springs that supplied travelers across the arid Mojave Desert with lush green meadows (hence the Spanish name *Las Vegas*, meaning "the meadows").
- Propelled toward major economic growth in the 1930s by thousands of workers who constructed the nearby *Hoover Dam* on the Colorado River, prompting Nevada to legalize commercial casino gambling in 1931.
- Associated worldwide with the legendary *Las Vegas Strip*, a four-mile corridor of towering casino resorts that technically lies just south of the city limits in the unincorporated townships of *Paradise* and *Winchester*.
- Shaped profoundly during the mid-twentieth century by organized crime figures such as *Bugsy Siegel*, who opened the landmark *Flamingo Hotel* in 1946, before modern corporate ownership reshaped the resort landscape.
- Hosted regular above-ground atomic bomb detonations at the nearby *Nevada Test Site* during the [[cold-war]], turning mushroom clouds into an unexpected tourist spectacle viewed from downtown casino balconies in the 1950s.
- Underwent a transformation in 1989 with the opening of *The Mirage* by Steve Wynn, sparking an era of themed mega-resorts that redefined international casino hospitality and entertainment.
- Faces severe ongoing environmental and resource pressures centered on water conservation and the allocation of water rights from *Lake Mead* along the drought-stricken Colorado River.
