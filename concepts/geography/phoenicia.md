## summary

**Phoenicia** was an ancient region on the eastern Mediterranean coast (modern-day Lebanon) whose maritime civilization dominated Mediterranean trade for over a millennium. The Phoenicians were the ancient world's greatest seafarers and traders, spreading their influence and alphabet throughout the Mediterranean. Though their independent civilization fell to successive empires, their descendants founded Carthage, and their alphabet became the ancestor of all modern Western writing systems.

## you gotta know

- Phoenicia's heartland was on the Levantine coast, home to major city-states including Tyre, Sidon, and Byblos, each an independent trading power with its own governance and resources.
- The Phoenicians were master mariners and merchants who established trading colonies and posts throughout the Mediterranean, from the coasts of North Africa to Spain and Greece.
- They invented and refined the alphabet, which they spread throughout the Mediterranean; their consonantal system was the ancestor of Greek, Latin, and all modern alphabets used worldwide.
- The Phoenician city of Tyre produced Tyrian purple dye, a rare and luxurious color extracted from murex shells; it became a sign of imperial or royal status and made Tyre immensely wealthy.
- The legendary Queen Dido, according to tradition, was a Phoenician princess from Tyre who founded the city of Carthage in North Africa, establishing a rival superpower.
- Carthage, Phoenicia's greatest colony, became a superpower rivaling Rome; it was destroyed by Rome in the Punic Wars but represented Phoenician maritime dominance at its height.
- Phoenicia was conquered and absorbed by successive empires—the Assyrian, Persian, and finally Greek and Roman empires—but Phoenician culture and maritime dominance persisted through their colonies.
- Phoenician traders were instrumental in spreading knowledge, goods, and cultural practices across the ancient Mediterranean, acting as economic and cultural intermediaries between civilizations.
- The Phoenicians developed sophisticated seafaring technology, navigation techniques, and trade networks that were the envy of the ancient world and enabled their commercial hegemony.
