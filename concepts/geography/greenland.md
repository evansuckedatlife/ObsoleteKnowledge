## summary

**Greenland** is the world's largest island, a subarctic and arctic territory in the *North Atlantic* and *Arctic Ocean* between *Canada* and *Iceland*. Once a Viking colony, then largely abandoned, then recolonized by *Denmark*, Greenland remains Danish territory yet increasingly asserts autonomy as climate change reshapes its ice sheet and geopolitical importance. Its harsh climate, sparse population, and strategic Arctic position make it central to contemporary climate science and great-power competition.

## you gotta know

- Greenland's vast interior is covered by the Greenland ice sheet, the second-largest ice mass on Earth after Antarctica; it contains roughly 7% of the world's fresh water and significantly influences global sea levels.
- *Norse* settlers from *Iceland* established farming colonies around 985 CE under *Erik the Red*; these settlements mysteriously died out around 1500, possibly due to cooling climate and Inuit pressure.
- The *Kalaallit* (Inuit people) are indigenous to Greenland; after Norse disappearance, Greenlandic culture crystallized around hunting, fishing, and dog sledding in isolation from the broader Arctic.
- *Denmark* recolonized Greenland in the 18th century; it remained a remote colonial outpost until World War II, when strategic Arctic importance increased sharply.
- Greenland's capital, *Nuuk*, sits on the west coast; the island's geography—rugged fjords, glaciers, and small isolated communities—makes internal travel difficult and flight-dependent.
- Modern Greenlandic identity emphasizes indigenous Kalaallit heritage and Danish political links; the territory gained home rule in 1979 and self-government in 2009, with *Denmark* retaining foreign policy control.
- The ice sheet has accelerated melting in the 21st century, raising global sea levels and transforming Greenland's economy (tourism, fishing) and geopolitics (*Arctic passage* shipping routes, mineral resources).
- Geopolitically, Greenland is contested: the *United States* has shown interest in acquisition (historically and recently), while *Russia* and *China* eye Arctic resources and the emerging *Northwest Passage*.
