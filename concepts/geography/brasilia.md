## summary

**Brasília**, frequently spelled in English without the diacritic as **Brasilia**, is the federal capital of Brazil, located on the high plateau of the *Planalto Central* within the country's Federal District. Inaugurated in 1960 under the ambitious presidency of *Juscelino Kubitschek*, the city was planned and erected in fewer than five years to shift the nation's political gravity away from coastal Rio de Janeiro into the undeveloped interior. It remains one of the preeminent urban masterpieces of twentieth-century [[modernism]], recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site for its radical layout and sculptural public monuments.

## you gotta know

- Structured according to the celebrated *Plano Piloto* designed by urban planner *Lúcio Costa*, a bird-shaped or airplane-shaped master plan organized along the intersecting *Eixo Monumental* and *Eixo Rodoviário*.
- Architecturally defined by the sculptural, cast-concrete governmental buildings designed by Pritzker Prize laureate *Oscar Niemeyer*, who favored fluid, expressive curves over rigid structural right angles.
- Commissioned by President *Juscelino Kubitschek* under the campaign pledge of achieving "fifty years of progress in five," fulfilling a constitutional goal dating to 1891 to colonize and populate the central scrublands.
- Centers on the *Praça dos Três Poderes* (Square of the Three Powers), an austere civic plaza flanked by the executive *Palácio do Planalto*, the supreme court building, and the iconic twin towers of the *National Congress*.
- Houses the *Cathedral of Brasília*, a circular crown-like sanctuary formed by sixteen curved concrete columns enclosing stained glass panels beneath an underground entrance.
- Bordered by *Lake Paranoá*, an expansive artificial reservoir created by damming the local river network to alleviate severe dry-season aridity and provide recreation for the planned capital.
- Constructed primarily by hundreds of thousands of internal migrant workers from the northeast known as *candangos*, who were ultimately excluded from the elite planned core and settled in peripheral satellite communities.

## connections

- [[south-america]] — the continent whose largest country established this master-planned inland capital city.
- [[modernism]] — the twentieth-century architectural movement that dictated the functionalist zoning, sweeping highways, and reinforced concrete of the city.
- [[bolivia]] — the neighboring Andean nation lying directly west of the Brazilian interior lowlands and agricultural savannas.
- [[colonialism]] — the Portuguese imperial era during which earlier administrative centers Salvador and Rio de Janeiro were founded along the Atlantic coast.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the coastal maritime domain from which the national government deliberately withdrew to settle the inland frontier.
- [[cold-war]] — the geopolitical period that formed the backdrop of the city's construction and its subsequent seizure by a 1964 military dictatorship.
