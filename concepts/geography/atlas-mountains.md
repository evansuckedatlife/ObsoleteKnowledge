## summary

The **Atlas Mountains** form a major mountain system stretching approximately 2,500 kilometres across Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia in northwestern Africa. Rising between the Atlantic Ocean and the [[mediterranean-sea]], the system acts as a profound physical barrier that divides the fertile Mediterranean coastal basin from the hyper-arid expanse of the Sahara Desert. Inhabited primarily by indigenous Berber populations for millennia, the range features rugged alpine peaks, deep gorges, and significant mineral wealth that have shaped North African history and commerce since antiquity.

## you gotta know

- Toubkal (*Jbel Toubkal*), situated in southwestern Morocco within the High Atlas sub-range, is the highest peak in the entire system and all of North Africa at 4,167 metres (13,671 feet).
- The system is physiographically divided into distinct sub-ranges: the Anti-Atlas, High Atlas, and Middle Atlas in Morocco, alongside the Tell Atlas and Saharan Atlas running parallel eastward across Algeria and Tunisia.
- Derives its European name from classical Greek mythology, associated with the Titan *Atlas*, who was condemned to hold up the celestial spheres and turned into stony mountain peaks by *Perseus*.
- Formed through several distinct geological episodes, primarily during the tectonic collision of the African and Eurasian continental plates during the Paleogene and Neogene periods.
- Serves as the ancestral homeland of the Amazigh (Berber) people, whose historic mountain architecture of fortified earthen villages (*ksour*) and granaries (*agdars*) adapted to steep, arid slopes.
- Hosted now-extinct mammalian fauna, including the Atlas bear (Africa's only native bear species) and the Barbary lion, while remaining the primary natural habitat of the endangered Barbary macaque.
- Creates an indispensable rain shadow and watershed, trapping moisture carried inland by westerly oceanic systems to supply essential rivers such as the *Oum Er-Rbia* and *Moulouya*.

## connections

- [[mediterranean-sea]] — northern body of water whose maritime air masses interact with coastal mountain slopes to produce fertile agricultural belts.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — primary oceanic source of precipitation and winter snowfall for the higher western elevations of the range.
- [[roman-empire]] — classical Mediterranean power that established fortified frontier zones (*limes*) along the northern slopes of the mountains in *Mauretania*.
- [[islam]] — major world religion embraced across the region following Arab conquests, blending with local Amazigh traditions throughout mountain settlements.
- [[france]] — European imperial power that penetrated the ranges during nineteenth- and twentieth-century colonial campaigns in Algeria and Morocco.
- [[spain]] — European nation directly across the Strait of Gibraltar sharing geological affinity through the Betic-Rif mountain arc.
- [[andes]] — fellow major continental fold mountain chain produced by prolonged tectonic collision and crustal uplift.
