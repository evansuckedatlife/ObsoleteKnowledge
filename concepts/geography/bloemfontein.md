## summary

**Bloemfontein**, also designated by its Sesotho name **Mangaung** and widely popularized as the **City of Roses**, is the judicial capital of South Africa and the administrative center of the Free State province. Founded as a military outpost in 1846 by British major *Henry Douglas Warden*, it served for half a century as the capital of the Boer republic known as the Orange Free State. The city holds monumental significance in modern African history as both the crucible of the Second Boer War and the founding birthplace of the African National Congress.

## you gotta know

- Houses the Supreme Court of Appeal of South Africa, acting as the judicial pillar of the national government alongside administrative Pretoria and legislative Cape Town.
- Hosted the critical *Bloemfontein Conference* in May and June 1899, where high-stakes negotiations between Transvaal president *Paul Kruger* and British High Commissioner *Alfred Milner* collapsed, precipitating the Second Boer War.
- Fell to British imperial forces under Field Marshal *Lord Roberts* in March 1900, after which imperial authorities converted surrounding grounds into a notorious concentration camp where thousands of Boer women and children died from disease.
- Commemorates Boer civilian wartime casualties through the sandstone obelisk of the *National Women's Monument* (*Vrouemonument*), where prominent humanitarian *Emily Hobhouse* is interred.
- Served as the founding location of the *South African Native National Congress* (later the African National Congress) in January 1912 under the leadership of *John Langalibalele Dube* and *Sol Plaatje*.
- Celebrates an extensive municipal heritage of floriculture that gave rise to its popular moniker, featuring the sprawling rose gardens of *King's Park* and an annual spring rose festival.
- Stands as the unexpected birthplace of celebrated fantasy author *J. R. R. Tolkien*, who was born in the town in 1892 while his father managed a local bank branch.

- [[british-empire]] — the global imperial dominion that fought to subordinate the Free State republic, occupied the town in 1900, and later incorporated it into the 1910 Union of South Africa.
- [[colonialism]] — the broader institutional framework that dispossessed indigenous populations, concentrated rural civilians into wartime camps, and catalyzed black political mobilization.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the technological wave that brought heavy railway connectivity and telegraph networks linking this inland highveld city to coastal ports.
- [[world-war-i]] — the international conflict during which disgruntled Afrikaner veterans in the surrounding Free State mounted the anti-British *Maritz Rebellion* of 1914.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean linking South African ports to European metropoles, through which military reinforcements, civil servants, and emigrants traveled.
