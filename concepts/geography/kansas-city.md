## summary

**Kansas City** is a major metropolitan center in the central *United States*, straddling the state line at the confluence of the *Missouri River* and the *Kansas River*. Established originally as a river landing and frontier trading depot, it evolved rapidly during the nineteenth century into the premier rail terminus, stockyard center, and grain exchange of the American Midwest. Renowned for its distinctive tradition of jazz music, dry-rub barbecue culture, and sweeping boulevard architecture, the city played a pivotal historical role in the westward expansion and political development of the nation.

## you gotta know

- Comprises two legally distinct incorporated municipal entities: *Kansas City, Missouri* (the primary core city in Jackson County) and *Kansas City, Kansas* (in Wyandotte County), divided by State Line Road.
- Founded at *Kaw Point*, the scenic promontory where the *Kansas River* empties into the eastward-bending *Missouri River*, an essential navigation landmark documented by *Lewis and Clark* in 1804.
- Served as the jumping-off point for major westward overland pioneer routes in the nineteenth century, including the *Santa Fe Trail*, the *Oregon Trail*, and the *California Trail*.
- The bloody border warfare of "Bleeding Kansas" and Civil War violence culminated nearby in the October 1864 Battle of *Westport*, frequently dubbed the "Gettysburg of the West."
- Flourished as an agricultural and meatpacking powerhouse after the 1869 completion of the *Hannibal Bridge*, the first permanent railroad span over the Missouri River.
- Controlled during the 1920s and 1930s by political boss *Tom Pendergast*, whose machine allowed alcohol, gambling, and nightlife to flourish openly during Prohibition.
- Fostered the legendary Kansas City jazz sound centered around the historic *18th and Vine* district, nurturing jazz innovators such as *Count Basie*, *Bennie Moten*, and *Charlie Parker*.
- Known colloquially as the "City of Fountains," claiming second place globally behind Rome in total working fountains, alongside an extensive network of city boulevards planned by *George Kessler*.
