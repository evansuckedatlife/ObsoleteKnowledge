## summary

**Lake Huron** is the second-largest of the Great Lakes by surface area and the fifth-largest freshwater lake on Earth, shared between the province of Ontario in [[canada]] and the state of Michigan in the [[united-states]]. Connected to [[lake-michigan]] at the Straits of Mackinac, it receives the discharge of Lake Superior and directs its water southward toward Lake Erie and [[lake-ontario]]. Famous for its intricate coastlines, dangerous seasonal storms, and thousands of islands, it contains Manitoulin Island and encompasses the broad expanse of Georgian Bay.

## you gotta know

- Second-largest Great Lake by surface area at approximately 23,000 square miles (59,600 square kilometers), possessing the longest shoreline of any Great Lake when counting its island perimeters.
- Contains *Manitoulin Island*, recognized as the largest freshwater island in the world, which itself holds more than one hundred inland lakes.
- Forms an undivided hydrologic body of water with [[lake-michigan]] across the *Straits of Mackinac*, crossed overhead by the landmark *Mackinac Bridge*.
- Encompasses *Georgian Bay*, a massive northeastern arm within [[canada]] largely shielded by the Bruce Peninsula, often referred to as the sixth Great Lake.
- Recorded as the first Great Lake seen by European explorers, visited in 1615 by French explorers *Étienne Brûlé* and *Samuel de Champlain*.
- Discharges at its southern tip past Port Huron and Sarnia into the *St. Clair River*, which flows through Lake St. Clair and the Detroit River toward [[lake-ontario]].
- Feared for catastrophic autumn tempests that have claimed hundreds of vessels, most famously the *Great Lakes Storm of 1913*, which sank eight large ore carriers on Lake Huron alone.
- Indented on the Michigan shore by *Saginaw Bay*, historically celebrated for timber transport, Saginaw Valley lumber mills, and commercial fisheries.

## connections

- [[lake-michigan]] — the hydrologic partner lake joined across the Straits of Mackinac at an identical surface elevation.
- [[lake-ontario]] — the downstream Great Lake that receives drainage from Lake Huron via Lake Erie and Niagara Falls.
- [[canada]] — the nation bordering the eastern half of the lake, encompassing Georgian Bay and Manitoulin Island.
- [[united-states]] — the nation bordering the western shoreline along Michigan's Lower and Upper Peninsulas.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean basin reached by Lake Huron's discharge through the St. Lawrence River.
- [[colonialism]] — the era of New France exploration, Jesuit missions, and fur-trade networks along the Great Lakes.
