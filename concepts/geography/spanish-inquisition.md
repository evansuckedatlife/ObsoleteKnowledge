## summary

The **Spanish Inquisition**, formally known as the **Tribunal of the Holy Office of the Inquisition**, was an ecclesiastical court established in 1478 by the *Catholic Monarchs* of [[spain]] to enforce religious orthodoxy throughout the [[iberian-peninsula]]. Operating under royal rather than purely papal jurisdiction, the tribunal initially targeted Jewish converts suspected of secretly maintaining their ancestral faith before expanding its scope to include Muslim converts, Protestant reformers, and political dissidents across the growing [[spanish-empire]]. It endured for more than three centuries across [[europe]] and the Americas, profoundly shaping Spanish society, culture, and external reputation until its final abolition in 1834.

## you gotta know

- Established in 1478 by *Ferdinand II of Aragon* and *Isabella I of Castile* under authority granted by Pope *Sixtus IV*, placing religious policing directly under the authority of the Spanish crown rather than [[rome]].
- *Tomás de Torquemada*, a Dominican prior and confessor to the queen, served as the first Grand Inquisitor, organizing regional tribunals, standardizing interrogation procedures, and aggressively prosecuting suspected backsliders.
- Initially concentrated on prosecuting *conversos*—Jewish converts suspected of secretly observing [[judaism]]—a relentless campaign that directly motivated the 1492 *Alhambra Decree* mandating the total expulsion of practicing Jews from [[spain]].
- Following the fall of Granada and the completion of the *Reconquista*, inquisitors turned their apparatus against *moriscos*, Muslim converts suspected of privately maintaining [[islam]] and Arabic traditions in [[moorish-spain]] and [[al-andalus]].
- Trials culminated in dramatic public ceremonies called *autos-da-fé* ("acts of faith"), where penitents wore conical paper hats and yellow penitential sacks called *sanbenitos* before unrepentant heretics were surrendered to secular magistrates for burning at the stake.
- Operated an extensive system of ideological censorship, promulgating an *Index of Prohibited Books* that banned vernacular translations of the Bible and subjected major literary figures, including [[cervantes]] and mystic *Teresa of Ávila*, to formal investigations.
- Exported its jurisdiction across the Atlantic during the [[age-of-exploration]], establishing permanent colonial tribunals in *Mexico City*, *Lima*, and *Cartagena* to police European settlers, indigenous populations, and enslaved peoples within the [[spanish-empire]].
- Rival Protestant powers in [[england]] and the Netherlands seized upon lurid accounts of subterranean dungeons, iron collars, and sadistic torture to circulate sensationalist anti-Spanish propaganda that forged the historiographical *Black Legend* (*La Leyenda Negra*).

## connections

- [[spain]] — the Iberian kingdom whose unifying monarchs utilized the tribunal to enforce religious and political homogeneity across regional territories.
- [[christianity]] — the Catholic faith whose theological orthodoxy the tribunal defended against heresy, crypto-religious practices, and Protestant doctrine.
- [[judaism]] — the targeted faith of *conversos*, whose suspected clandestine rituals triggered the founding of the tribunal and subsequent expulsion orders.
- [[islam]] — the historic religion of [[moorish-spain]] whose forced converts (*moriscos*) faced rigorous inquisitorial surveillance until their eventual expulsion in 1609.
- [[spanish-empire]] — the global imperial realm across which colonial branches of the Holy Office operated to police orthodoxy in overseas viceroyalties.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the period of global maritime expansion that carried inquisitorial authority to Spanish outposts in the Americas and Asia.
- [[renaissance]] — the intellectual era whose humanist scholarship and vernacular writings were tightly monitored and censored by inquisitorial authorities.
- [[rome]] — the papal seat that issued the authorizing bull but periodically resisted the Spanish crown's complete political control over the courts.
