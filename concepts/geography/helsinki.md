## summary

**Helsinki** is the capital, largest city, and cultural heart of Finland, situated on the rocky northern coast of the Gulf of Finland in northern [[europe]]. Founded in the sixteenth century by King *Gustav I* of Sweden, it became the country's preeminent metropolis after *Russia* incorporated Finland and moved the grand duchy's capital from Turku to be closer to [[st-petersburg]]. Today, it is globally celebrated for its neoclassical and functionalist architecture, the diplomatic breakthroughs of the [[cold-war]], and its vibrant maritime character.

## you gotta know

- Founded in 1550 by King *Gustav Vasa* of Sweden under the Swedish name *Helsingfors* to rival the Hanseatic port city of Reval, modern-day Tallinn.
- Rebuilt extensively in the neoclassical style by architect *Carl Ludvig Engel* after Czar *Alexander I* of [[soviet-union|Russia]] transferred the capital there in 1812, creating the monumental Senate Square and Helsinki Cathedral.
- Hosted the landmark 1975 Conference on Security and Co-operation in Europe, which produced the influential *Helsinki Accords* that reduced tensions during the [[cold-war]] and promoted human rights across the Eastern Bloc.
- Guarded by the maritime sea fortress of *Suomenlinna*, also known as *Sveaborg*, an eighteenth-century bastion fortification spread across six interconnected islands and designated a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- Served as host of the 1952 Summer Olympic Games, originally scheduled for 1940 before being postponed due to the outbreak of [[world-war-ii]].
- Famous for innovative twentieth-century modernist architecture, including the copper-domed *Temppeliaukio Church*, which was quarried directly into bedrock, and the functionalist public projects of architect *Alvar Aalto*.
- Anchored by the central railway station designed by *Eliel Saarinen*, renowned for its granite facade and granite lantern-bearer statues flanking the main portal.

## connections

- [[europe]] — the northern continental realm where the city serves as a major Scandinavian and Nordic capital.
- [[cold-war]] — the global ideological conflict during which the city hosted the momentous 1975 accords between Western and Eastern powers.
- [[soviet-union]] — the bordering superpower whose predecessor empire moved the Finnish capital to the city and fought wars against Finland.
- [[st-petersburg]] — the imperial Russian capital whose proximity prompted the Russian Empire to relocate Finland's administrative centre to the city.
- [[world-war-ii]] — the global conflict during which the city suffered devastating aerial bombings while defending Finnish sovereignty.
- [[world-war-i]] — the war whose turbulent aftermath enabled Finland to declare its national independence in December 1917.
