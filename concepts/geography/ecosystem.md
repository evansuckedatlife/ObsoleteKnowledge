## summary

An **ecosystem**, or **ecological system**, is a dynamic community of living organisms interacting with one another and with the non-living chemical and physical components of their environment. First coined by British botanist *Arthur Tansley* in 1935, the concept integrates biological communities with abiotic cycles of energy, nutrients, and water across terrestrial and aquatic biomes worldwide, establishing that organisms cannot be understood in isolation from their physical setting. Understanding these systems is fundamental to physical geography and ecology, revealing how biological productivity, trophic structures, and material cycling sustain global biodiversity and buffer Earth against anthropogenic disruption and climate change.

## you gotta know

- Primary producers capture solar radiation through photosynthesis or inorganic compounds through chemosynthesis, fueling the base of food webs that channel organic matter and metabolic energy through ascending *trophic levels*.
- Energy transfers between trophic tiers are inherently inefficient, with approximately ninety percent of usable energy dissipated as metabolic heat at each step, limiting the length of food chains.
- Terrestrial life is organized into major *biomes*—such as the tropical rainforests of [[south-america]] and [[southeast-asia]]—whose global distributions and canopy structures are determined primarily by regional temperature and precipitation regimes.
- *Robert Paine* formulated the *keystone species* concept after showing that removing the predatory ochre sea star *Pisaster ochraceus* allowed common mussels to monopolize space and drastically reduce community diversity.
- *Arthur Tansley* introduced the term in 1935 to refute the idea that vegetation communities functioned as holistic *superorganisms*, arguing instead for a physical system encompassing both organisms and their inorganic habitat.
- *Ecological succession* describes the orderly, directional replacement of community assemblages over time following environmental change, progressing from *primary succession* on barren, soil-free substrates like lava flows to *secondary succession* where residual soil and seed banks remain intact.
- Biogeochemical cycles continuously circulate essential elements like carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus between living biomass and abiotic reservoirs via microbial decomposition, atmospheric exchange, and geological weathering.
- *Raymond Lindeman* established quantitative trophic dynamics in a landmark 1942 study of *Cedar Bog Lake*, introducing caloric energy accounting that inspired the systems ecology of *Eugene* and *Howard Odum*.

## connections

- [[south-america]] — contains the vast Amazon basin, housing the planet's largest contiguous tropical rainforest and terrestrial freshwater drainage system.
- [[australia]] — characterized by deep evolutionary isolation that yielded unique sclerophyll biomes and an extraordinary endemic radiation of marsupials.
- [[southeast-asia]] — encompasses tropical archipelagos exhibiting immense marine and terrestrial richness divided by the faunal boundary of the *Wallace Line*.
- [[andes]] — exhibits steep altitudinal ecological zonation, creating compressed climatic bands that range from cloud forests to high-elevation alpine puna.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — hosts critical pelagic and benthic marine zones interconnected by global thermohaline circulation and coastal upwelling systems.
- [[nile-river]] — forms a vital desert riparian lifeline whose seasonal hydrological pulses historically fertilized floodplains and supported thriving biotas.
- [[dna]] — encodes the hereditary information that underpins genetic diversity, driving speciation and adaptive fitness within ecological communities.
