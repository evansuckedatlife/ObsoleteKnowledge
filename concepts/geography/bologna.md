## summary

**Bologna** is the historic capital and economic center of the Emilia-Romagna region in northern [[italy]], positioned at the foot of the Apennine Mountains between the Reno and Savena rivers. Established at a vital transit junction along the ancient *Via Aemilia*, the city grew into an intellectual, commercial, and artistic powerhouse during the Middle Ages and the Italian [[renaissance]]. It holds enduring worldwide acclaim as the home of the oldest university in the Western world, its extensive UNESCO-protected networks of covered porticoes, and its foundational contributions to global culinary culture.

## you gotta know

- Houses the *University of Bologna* (*Alma Mater Studiorum*), founded in 1088 as a guild of scholars, making it universally recognized as the oldest continuously operating university in the world.
- Known by three famous traditional Italian monikers: *La Dotta* ("the learned", for its university), *La Grassa* ("the fat", for its rich gastronomy), and *La Rossa* ("the red", for its terracotta rooftops and its historic leftist politics).
- Dominated visually by its leaning medieval *Two Towers* (*Due Torri*), the taller *Asinelli* and the steeply inclined *Garisenda*, which survive from scores of stone defense towers built by feuding noble families.
- Features more than thirty-eight kilometers of UNESCO-designated covered pedestrian walkways known as porticoes, culminating in the world's longest continuous arcade leading to the *Sanctuary of the Madonna di San Luca*.
- Regarded as the culinary heart of northern Italy, serving as the birthplace of *ragù alla bolognese*, cured *mortadella*, and traditional filled *tortellini* served in capon broth.
- Anchored at its historic center by *Piazza Maggiore*, surrounded by the civic *Palazzo d'Accursio*, the bronze *Fontana del Nettuno* designed by Giambologna, and the massive unfinished facade of the *Basilica of San Petronio*.
- Founded originally by the Etruscans under the name *Felsina* before falling to the Boii Gauls and being re-established as the strategic colony of *Bononia* by the [[roman-empire]] in 189 BCE.

## connections

- [[italy]] — the southern European nation in which this regional capital and primary railway crossroads is situated.
- [[renaissance]] — the era of intellectual and cultural rebirth during which the city thrived as a center of humanism, anatomy, and painting.
- [[rome]] — the historic Italian capital and seat of the Papal States, which directly governed the city from the sixteenth century until Italian unification.
- [[roman-empire]] — the classical empire that laid down the foundational urban grid and constructed the *Via Aemilia* highway through *Bononia*.
- [[europe]] — the continent whose unified higher education framework was established by the landmark 1999 *Bologna Declaration*.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — the geographic basin that fostered the trade routes, agricultural traditions, and maritime networks of the Italian peninsula.
- [[ancient-greece]] — the classical civilization whose philosophical and legal traditions were revived by medieval scholars at the city's law school.
