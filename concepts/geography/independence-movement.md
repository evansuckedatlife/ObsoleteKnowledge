# Independence Movement

## summary

**Independence movements** are organized efforts by colonized or subordinated peoples to gain political sovereignty and end foreign domination. The most prominent was the Indian independence movement (1857–1947), which mobilized millions against British colonial rule through nonviolent resistance and political organization, culminating in Indian independence and partition. The broader phenomenon of decolonization—the end of European colonial empires in Asia and Africa (1945–1980)—represented a fundamental geopolitical shift that reshaped the global order and inaugurated the postcolonial world.

## you gotta know

- The Indian National Congress (founded 1885) evolved from a debating society into a mass political movement challenging British rule; early leaders petitioned for reforms within the empire.
- Mohandas *Gandhi* transformed the movement through *satyagraha* (nonviolent resistance), reframing independence as a moral imperative; his campaigns of civil disobedience (salt march, boycotts) mobilized peasants and workers.
- World War II accelerated decolonization; Britain's military exhaustion and India's strategic importance in the Pacific War strengthened independence negotiators; the "Quit India" movement (1942) forced Britain to commit to withdrawal.
- Partition (1947) split British India into the independent nations of India and Pakistan, with communal violence claiming at least 1 million lives and displacing 15 million; a foundational trauma in South Asian history.
- India achieved independence under Jawaharlal Nehru as prime minister; Gandhi was assassinated by a Hindu nationalist in 1948, within months of independence.
- The success of nonviolent resistance inspired decolonization movements worldwide; Gandhi's methods influenced civil rights activists from Martin Luther King Jr. to Nelson Mandela.
- Colonial independence movements in Asia (Indonesia, Vietnam, Burma) and Africa (Kenya, Algeria, Ghana) followed in the 1950s–1960s, often violently; Cold War rivalry accelerated imperial withdrawal.
- Independence movements redrew the global map; dozens of new nations emerged; the United Nations membership nearly tripled; former colonies asserted sovereignty though often struggling with poverty, authoritarian governance, and neocolonial economic dependence.

## connections

- [[india]] — the geographic center of the most consequential independence movement; decolonization reshaped South Asian politics and society.
- [[british-raj]] — the colonial system whose exploitation and governance mobilized Indian resistance and nationalism.
- [[gandhi]] — the iconic leader who synthesized Hindu philosophy and Western democratic ideals into a mass movement.
- [[partition-of-india]] — the traumatic outcome of independence; communal violence and forced migration that continues to define South Asian conflict.
- [[civil-disobedience]] — the method of nonviolent resistance central to the Indian movement and later decolonization struggles.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power whose withdrawal from India signaled the beginning of the empire's collapse globally.
