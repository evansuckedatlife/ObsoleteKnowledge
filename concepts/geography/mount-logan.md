## summary

**Mount Logan** is the highest peak in [[canada]] and the second-highest summit in North America, rising to an elevation of 5,959 metres in southwestern Yukon. Anchoring the Saint Elias Mountains within Kluane National Park and Reserve, it forms a colossally broad glacial massif with the largest base circumference of any non-volcanic mountain on Earth. Its proximity to the subarctic Pacific generates severe storms and colossal ice fields that make it one of the most formidable alpine environments in the world.

## you gotta know

- Rises to 5,959 metres (19,551 feet) in Yukon's Saint Elias Range, surpassed in North American elevation only by Denali in the neighbouring [[united-states]] territory of Alaska.
- Named in 1892 by geologist *Israel Cook Russell* in honour of Sir *William Edmond Logan*, founder and first director of the Geological Survey of [[canada]].
- Features a massive summit plateau spanning over twenty kilometres in length, supporting eleven distinct sub-peaks that each rise above 5,000 metres.
- First successfully summited on June 23, 1925, by an international expedition led by *Albert H. MacCarthy*, whose party endured an arduous sixty-five-day overland approach through uncharted wilderness.
- Situated within Kluane National Park and Reserve, which joins Wrangell-St. Elias and Glacier Bay to form a contiguous UNESCO World Heritage wilderness preserve.
- Feeds vast river systems of ice including the *Hubbard Glacier* and *Logan Glacier*, accumulating ancient ice layers studied by paleoclimatologists to reconstruct global atmospheric history.
- Continues to experience active tectonic uplift as the Pacific plate collides with the North American plate, causing the mountain to steadily grow taller over geological time.
