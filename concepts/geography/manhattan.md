## summary

**Manhattan**, coextensive with **New York County** and centered primarily on **Manhattan Island**, is the historical, financial, and cultural nucleus of *New York City*. Bounded by the *Hudson River*, the *East River*, and the *Harlem River*, the island was originally inhabited by the Munsee-speaking *Lenape* before developing into the seventeenth-century Dutch fur-trading outpost of *New Amsterdam*. In quiz bowl, Manhattan is celebrated as the crucible of modern international commerce, renowned for the rigid urban street grid established by the *Commissioners' Plan of 1811*, the iconic architecture of *Wall Street* and *Midtown*, and world-renowned cultural institutions spanning from *Broadway* to *Central Park*.

## you gotta know

- Central island borough and economic powerhouse of *New York City*, separated from the Bronx by the *Harlem River* and *Spuyten Duyvil Creek*, from Brooklyn and Queens by the *East River*, and from New Jersey by the *Hudson River*.
- Derives its name from the indigenous Munsee Delaware word *Mannahatta* (*island of many hills*), first recorded in 1609 by Robert Juet, an officer aboard *Henry Hudson*'s vessel the *Half Moon*.
- Reputedly purchased in 1626 from local Native Americans by Dutch West India Company director *Peter Minuit* for trade merchandise traditionally valued at sixty Dutch guilders.
- Urban morphology north of Houston Street was largely dictated by the influential *Commissioners' Plan of 1811*, an ambitious master plan that imposed a regular rectilinear grid of numbered streets and avenues.
- Contains the 843-acre landscape masterpiece of *Central Park*, designed by *Frederick Law Olmsted* and *Calvert Vaux*, whose construction in the 1850s involved the demolition and displacement of predominantly African American and immigrant settlements like *Seneca Village*.
- Anchors global capital markets around *Wall Street* in Lower Manhattan, housing the *New York Stock Exchange* and *Federal Hall*, where *George Washington* was inaugurated as the first president of the United States.
- Served as the fertile breeding ground for twentieth-century cultural movements, including the flowering of African American literature and music during the *Harlem Renaissance* and the rise of the commercial theater district around *Times Square*.
- Linked to surrounding regional landmasses through pioneering engineering triumphs, including the *Brooklyn Bridge*, the *Holland Tunnel*, and the *George Washington Bridge*.

## connections

- [[new-york-city]] — municipal federation of five boroughs for which Manhattan serves as the administrative, cultural, and symbolic center.
- [[united-states]] — served as the fledgling republic's first national capital under the modern Constitution from 1789 through 1790.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — maritime gateway providing the sheltered deep-water anchorages that fueled the island's ascension as an international trading metropolis.
- [[colonialism]] — contested seventeenth-century outpost founded as Dutch *New Amsterdam* before being captured by England in 1664 and renamed for the Duke of York.
- [[american-revolution]] — critical theater of the 1776 New York Campaign, after which the island remained under British military occupation until Evacuation Day in 1783.
- [[modernism]] — architectural and aesthetic capital of twentieth-century skyscraper construction, international finance, abstract expressionism, and jazz.
- [[manitoulin-island]] — contrasting North American insular geographic study, opposing Manhattan's hyper-dense verticality to Manitoulin's pristine freshwater wilderness.
- [[martinique]] — historical transatlantic mercantile partner linked to the port of New York through colonial rum, sugar, and modern diaspora connections.
- [[managua]] — Latin American capital whose twentieth-century development was directly shaped by financial and geopolitical decisions emanating from Wall Street.
