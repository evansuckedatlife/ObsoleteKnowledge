# Iraq

## summary

**Iraq** is a country in western Asia occupying the territories of ancient *Mesopotamia*, the region between the *Tigris* and *Euphrates* rivers where some of humanity's earliest civilizations emerged. Dominated by Arab and Kurdish populations, predominantly Islamic in faith, Iraq has endured colonial partition, authoritarian rule, and continuous conflict, making it a pivotal but turbulent player in Middle Eastern geopolitics. Its vast oil reserves and historical significance give it strategic importance far beyond its borders.

## you gotta know

- Iraq is bounded by *Turkey* to the north, *Iran* to the east, *Saudi Arabia* and *Kuwait* to the south, and *Syria* and *Jordan* to the west; the Persian Gulf provides its southeastern coastline.
- The *Tigris* and *Euphrates* rivers flow through Iraq, defining its geography and historically supporting agriculture in their floodplains; control of these waterways has long been contested with upstream neighbors.
- *Ancient Mesopotamia* ("the land between rivers"), centered in present-day Iraq, saw the rise of the *Sumer*, *Akkad*, *Babylon*, and *Assyrian* empires—some of humanity's first urban civilizations, inventors of writing and law codes.
- The *Ottoman Empire* ruled Iraq (then known as the *Mesopotamian provinces*) for four centuries; after Ottoman collapse in World War I, Britain received a League of Nations mandate and established the Kingdom of Iraq in 1921.
- Iraq's modern borders were drawn by European powers without regard for ethnic and sectarian divisions: the population is split between *Arab* majorities (predominantly *Sunni* and *Shia* Muslims) and *Kurdish* minorities concentrated in the north.
- The discovery and exploitation of vast *oil reserves* in the early 20th century made Iraq strategically vital to Western powers; oil revenues have funded both infrastructure and conflict.
- *Baghdad*, founded in 762 CE, became a center of Islamic learning and culture in the medieval *Abbasid Caliphate* and remains the capital; it was devastated by Mongol invasion in 1258 and later by modern warfare.
- The 1953 *Iraqi coup* (backed by the CIA and British intelligence), the 1979 *Iranian Revolution*, the 1980–88 *Iran-Iraq War*, the 1991 and 2003 U.S. invasions, and subsequent sectarian conflict have defined modern Iraq's instability.
