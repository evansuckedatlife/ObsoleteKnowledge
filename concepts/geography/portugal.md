# Portugal

## summary

**Portugal** is a small but historically significant nation occupying the western portion of the *Iberian Peninsula*, bordered by *Spain* to the east and the *Atlantic Ocean* to the west and south. Though modest in size and population, Portugal played an outsized role in the Age of Exploration (15th–17th centuries), establishing a vast maritime empire and pioneering transoceanic trade routes to Africa, Asia, and the Americas. Today it is a stable European democracy and *European Union* member, though its colonial past and economic subordination within Europe continue to shape its identity.

## you gotta know

- Portugal occupies the westernmost Iberian territory, with a coastline stretching over 1,200 kilometers along the Atlantic; the *Tagus River* (or *Tejo*) flows through central Portugal and its estuary defines *Lisbon*'s geography.
- The kingdom of Portugal emerged in 1139 from Christian reconquest of the peninsula from Muslim rule; it became an independent nation-state in the 12th century, making it one of Europe's oldest nation-states with stable borders.
- The *Portuguese Age of Exploration* (c. 1400–1600), sponsored by *Prince Henry the Navigator* and later monarchs, pioneered maritime routes around Africa to India and established trading posts and colonies across four continents.
- Portuguese navigators, including *Vasco da Gama* (who reached India in 1498) and *Ferdinand Magellan* (who initiated the first circumnavigation, 1519–1522), opened global trade routes and established Portugal as a maritime superpower.
- The *Portuguese Empire* at its height controlled territories in Africa (Angola, Mozambique), Asia (India's Goa, Macau, East Timor), South America (Brazil), and elsewhere; it was Europe's longest-lasting colonial empire, persisting until the 1970s.
- *Lisbon*, Portugal's capital, is built on hills overlooking the Tagus estuary; it was largely destroyed by an earthquake in 1755 but was rebuilt in a rational grid pattern and remains a historic Atlantic port.
- Portugal was ruled by a fascist dictatorship (*Estado Novo*) from 1932 to 1974, during which it brutally resisted decolonization; the *Carnation Revolution* (1974) ended dictatorship and eventually led to independence for Angola, Mozambique, and other colonies.
- Modern Portugal is a stable, affluent member of the European Union and *Eurozone*, though it has faced economic crises (notably the 2008 financial crisis) and continues to grapple with regional inequality and emigration.
