## summary

**Baku** is the capital and largest city of Azerbaijan, situated on the southern shore of the Absheron Peninsula along the western coastline of the Caspian Sea. Located 28 metres below global sea level, it is both the lowest-lying national capital on Earth and the largest city situated on the landlocked Caspian basin. Famed historically as a pioneer of the industrial petroleum sector, the city juxtaposes ancient Islamic and medieval fortifications with striking modern architecture and dynamic energy commerce.

## you gotta know

- Lies 28 metres below sea level on the landlocked *Caspian Sea*, making it the lowest-elevation national capital and the lowest-lying metropolis worldwide.
- Catalyzed the late-nineteenth-century global petroleum industry, generating over half of the world's extracted crude oil by 1900 through operations directed by the *Nobel brothers* and the *Rothschild family*.
- Stood as the prime strategic objective of Nazi Germany's 1942 summer offensive (*Operation Blue* or *Fall Blau*) during *World War II*, aiming to cut off Soviet fuel supplies.
- Preserves the medieval walled core of *Icherisheher*, containing the enigmatic twelfth-century *Maiden Tower* (*Giz Galasi*) and the fifteenth-century sandstone *Palace of the Shirvanshahs*.
- Nicknamed the "City of Winds" (*Küləklər şəhəri*) due to fierce coastal gales, driven alternately by the frigid northern *Khazri* and warm southern *Gilavar* winds.
- Influenced by subterranean hydrocarbon reserves that produce perpetual natural gas blazes, celebrated at the ancient *Ateshgah* fire temple and the burning hillside of *Yanar Dag*.
- Showcases cutting-edge contemporary architectural landmarks, including the flame-shaped LED-illuminated *Flame Towers* and Zaha Hadid's flowing *Heydar Aliyev Center*.

## connections

- [[soviet-union]] — socialist state that relied upon Baku as its premier strategic oil producer, refining center, and maritime industrial hub.
- [[world-war-ii]] — worldwide military conflict in which Allied and Soviet mechanization depended directly upon Baku's petroleum yields to resist Axis advances.
- [[middle-east]] — adjacent cultural and geopolitical realm whose historical Persian empires repeatedly governed and culturally shaped the Absheron region.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — global technological epoch that spurred commercial drilling, oil tanker innovation, and pipeline construction in the city's oilfields.
- [[islam]] — primary faith of Azerbaijan, reflected in the city's historic minarets, shrines, and Islamic legal traditions.
- [[cold-war]] — postwar standoff during which the Caspian basin formed an intensely guarded perimeter between Soviet territory and the southern alliance perimeter.
- [[roman-empire]] — ancient empire whose eastward military presence reached the outskirts of Baku, evidenced by Latin rock inscriptions at Gobustan.
