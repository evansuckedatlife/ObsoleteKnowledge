## summary

The **Rocky Mountains**, often called the **Rockies**, form the largest mountain system in North America, stretching more than 3,000 miles from northern [[canada]] through the western [[united-states]] to *New Mexico*. Rising dramatically above the [[great-plains]] to the east, the range forms the rugged spine of the continent and hosts the hydrological crest known as the *Continental Divide*. Its vast alpine wilderness, rich mineral belts, and historic role as a formidable barrier to westward expansion make it one of the most physically and culturally significant mountain systems in the world.

## you gotta know

- *Mount Elbert* in *Colorado* is the highest peak in the entire mountain system at 14,440 feet, anchoring the *Sawatch Range* in the Southern Rockies.
- Encompasses the *Continental Divide* of North America, directing precipitation westward toward the *Pacific Ocean* and eastward toward the *Atlantic Ocean* and the *Arctic Ocean*.
- Contains world-famous national parks including *Yellowstone National Park*, *Grand Teton National Park*, *Glacier National Park*, and *Rocky Mountain National Park*, which harbor iconic megafauna like grizzly bears, wolves, and bighorn sheep.
- Provides the headwaters for several of the continent's most critical river systems, notably feeding the [[mississippi-river]] via the *Missouri River*, as well as the *Colorado River*, the *Columbia River*, and the *Rio Grande*.
- Traversed by the [[lewis-and-clark-expedition]] in 1805 through *Lemhi Pass* and *Lolo Pass*, and later bypassed by hundreds of thousands of westward emigrants crossing *South Pass* in [[wyoming]] along the *Oregon Trail*.
- Formed primarily during the *Laramide orogeny* between 80 and 55 million years ago, when the low-angle subduction of the *Farallon Plate* drove compressional crustal deformation unusually far inland.
- Catalyzed widespread nineteenth-century boomtown settlement following major mineral strikes, including the *Pikes Peak Gold Rush* of 1859 and massive silver and copper booms in mining districts such as *Leadville* and *Butte*.
- Defined geologically and geographically by its distinct northern terminus at the *Liard River* in *British Columbia* and southern terminus in the *Sangre de Cristo Mountains* of northern *New Mexico*, comprising major subranges like the *Wind River Range*, *Bitterroot Range*, and *Wasatch Range*.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — contains the Southern, Middle, and Northern subranges of the chain, spanning from Idaho and Montana down to New Mexico.
- [[canada]] — holds the Canadian Rockies across Alberta and British Columbia, renowned for extensive glaciation and dramatic valley landscapes.
- [[mississippi-river]] — relies on eastern Rocky Mountain snowpack to supply major tributaries such as the Missouri, Platte, and Arkansas rivers.
- [[great-plains]] — borders the mountains immediately to the east along the steep structural boundary known as the Colorado Front Range.
- [[lewis-and-clark-expedition]] — navigated the perilous mountain passes of the Bitterroot Range while charting a path to the Pacific coast.
- [[andes]] — forms the South American segment of the broader American Cordillera, sharing a continuous spine of subduction-related mountain building.
- [[wyoming]] — provides the natural opening of South Pass, the pivotal low-elevation corridor used by pioneers to cross the Continental Divide.
