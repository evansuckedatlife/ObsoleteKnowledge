# Statue of Liberty

## summary

The **Statue of Liberty** is a colossal neoclassical copper statue on *Bedloe Island* (now *Liberty Island*) in *New York Harbor*, a gift from *France* to the *United States* dedicated in 1886. Designed by French sculptor *Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi* with an iron framework by *Gustave Eiffel*, it has become the world's most recognizable symbol of freedom, democracy, and American ideals, and a gateway monument greeting millions of immigrants arriving by ship.

## you gotta know

- The statue stands 93 meters tall (151 meters including the pedestal), holding a torch aloft in her right hand and a tablet inscribed with the date of *American* independence (July 4, 1776) in her left.
- *France* commissioned the statue as a gift to celebrate American independence and democracy; it was funded by French public donations and arrived disassembled in 1885, requiring U.S. donations to construct the pedestal.
- The statue depicts *Libertas*, the Roman goddess of liberty, rendered in *Bartholdi*'s neoclassical style; it was controversial at its dedication for its radical association with republicanism and freedom in an era of monarchies.
- Designed by *Bartholdi* with structural ironwork by *Gustave Eiffel* (who later designed the *Eiffel Tower*), it represented a feat of engineering and artistic ambition in the 19th century.
- The statue became synonymous with immigration to America, particularly for the millions who arrived through *Ellis Island* (opened 1892), though the statue itself predates modern mass immigration and was not initially linked to immigrants.
- A inscription added later — *Emma Lazarus*'s poem "The New Colossus" — crystallized the statue's association with immigration: "Give me your tired, your poor, your huddled masses yearning to breathe free."
- The statue has been vandalized, restored, and closed multiple times for security; it remains one of the most visited monuments in the world and is a *UNESCO* World Heritage Site.
