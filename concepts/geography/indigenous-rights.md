## summary

**Indigenous rights** encompass the collective and individual legal, territorial, and civil entitlements asserted by First Nations peoples against settler-colonial nation-states. In [[australia]], the struggle for **Aboriginal land rights** and legal recognition of **Native title** directly challenged the foundational colonial fiction of *terra nullius*—the doctrine that the continent belonged to no one prior to British settlement. Grounded in unceded **Aboriginal sovereignty**, this political and legal movement has reshaped constitutional law, environmental co-management, and human rights frameworks globally.

## you gotta know

- Overturned two centuries of legal dispossession in [[australia]] through the landmark High Court decision *Mabo v Queensland (No 2)* in 1992, led by Meriam activist *Eddie Mabo*, which legally extinguished the doctrine of *terra nullius*.
- Catalyzed nationwide legislative reform through the *Native Title Act 1993*, which established a legal process for Indigenous communities to claim traditional land rights where continuous cultural connection has been maintained.
- Further expanded by the 1996 *Wik Peoples v Queensland* High Court decision, which determined that statutory pastoral leases did not automatically extinguish native title rights, establishing the principle of coexistence.
- Galvanized by political activism such as the 1966 *Wave Hill Walk-Off* led by *Vincent Lingiari*, which transformed a Gurindji strike for equal pay into a historic return of traditional homelands by Prime Minister *Gough Whitlam*.
- Achieved constitutional reform in the *1967 Australian Referendum*, when over ninety percent of the public voted to include Indigenous Australians in the national census and empower the federal parliament to make laws on their behalf.
- Established the *Aboriginal Tent Embassy* in Canberra in 1972 on the lawns of Old Parliament House, creating an enduring permanent vigil demanding self-determination, treaty rights, and sovereign title.
- Advanced major international norms, culminating in the 2007 *United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples* (UNDRIP), affirming rights to self-determination, cultural preservation, and free, prior, and informed consent.

## connections

- [[australia]] — the nation-state whose legal foundations were transformed by the historic Mabo decision and land rights statutes.
- [[indigenous-culture]] — the ancient customary laws, connection to country, and ceremonial practices that form the basis for native title.
- [[colonialism]] — the imperial ideology of expropriation, forced child removal, and legal erasure that rights advocates fought to dismantle.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — the global human rights upsurge whose tactics inspired Australian Freedom Rides led by activist *Charles Perkins*.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power whose original settlement proclamations falsely declared colonized territories empty land.
- [[canada]] — peer Commonwealth nation where First Nations land claims and reconciliation efforts followed similar legal battles.
