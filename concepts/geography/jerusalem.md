# Jerusalem

## summary

**Jerusalem** is a city of extraordinary historical and religious significance located in the Levantine highlands of the Eastern Mediterranean, sacred simultaneously to Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. Claimed as capital or held sacred by multiple peoples over millennia, Jerusalem has been repeatedly conquered, destroyed, and rebuilt; today it remains divided between Israeli control and Palestinian areas, with its political status one of the world's most intractable disputes. Few places on Earth hold such profound meaning for such divergent communities.

## you gotta know

- Jerusalem is located in the Judean Hills in the East Mediterranean region, approximately 40 kilometers east of the Mediterranean coast and 50 kilometers north of the *Dead Sea*.
- *Judaism* reveres Jerusalem as its holiest city: it was the site of the *First Temple* (destroyed 586 BCE) and *Second Temple* (destroyed 70 CE); the *Western Wall*, a remaining section of the Second Temple's retaining wall, is Judaism's holiest prayer site.
- *Christianity* venerates Jerusalem as the site of *Jesus*'s crucifixion, resurrection, and ascension; the *Church of the Holy Sepulchre* marks the traditional location of Golgotha (crucifixion) and Jesus's tomb, making it Christianity's holiest site.
- *Islam* holds Jerusalem as its third-holiest city after *Mecca* and *Medina*; the *Dome of the Rock* (built 688–691 CE) marks the site where Muslims believe *Muhammad* ascended to heaven, and the *Al-Aqsa Mosque* is among Islam's holiest shrines.
- Jerusalem was the capital of the ancient Israelite kingdoms (roughly 1000–586 BCE) under *King David* and his successors; it was conquered and rebuilt by successive empires: Babylonian, Persian, Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Ottoman, and British.
- The *Crusades* (11th–13th centuries) centered on Jerusalem and the Holy Land; European Christian armies repeatedly conquered and lost the city, massacring Muslim and Jewish populations in the process.
- The *Ottoman Empire* ruled Jerusalem from 1517 to 1917 as part of the Levantine provinces; the city's population remained Muslim-majority, with minority Jewish and Christian communities.
- Since 1948, Jerusalem has been divided between Israeli and Palestinian control; Israel captured the entire city in 1967 and declared it its "eternal capital," while Palestinians claim East Jerusalem as their future capital—a core dispute in Israeli-Palestinian negotiations.
