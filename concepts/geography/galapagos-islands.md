## summary

The **Galápagos Islands**, officially designated in Ecuador as the **Archipiélago de Colón**, form an isolated volcanic archipelago straddling the equator in the eastern Pacific Ocean roughly six hundred miles west of continental [[south-america]]. Renowned globally for their extraordinary density of endemic wildlife and pristine marine ecosystems, the islands provided the crucial natural laboratory that inspired *Charles Darwin* during the second voyage of *HMS Beagle*. Today administered as a national park and marine reserve, the archipelago remains an indispensable touchstone for evolutionary biology, geology, and global conservation science.

## you gotta know

- Visited in 1835 by naturalist *Charles Darwin*, whose observations of morphological variations among mockingbirds, marine iguanas, and particularly ground finches laid the empirical groundwork for natural selection and modern [[dna]] studies.
- Governed as a province of Ecuador, which annexed the territory in 1832 under General *José de Villamil* and designated it as the country's first national park in 1959.
- Composed of eighteen major islands, three smaller islands, and over one hundred islets and rocks formed by a geological volcanic hotspot on the *Nazca Plate*.
- Famous for remarkable endemic fauna, including the giant Galápagos tortoise (*Chelonoidis niger*), the marine iguana (*Amblyrhynchus cristatus*), the flightless cormorant, and the Galápagos penguin, the only penguin species living north of the equator.
- Formed around the active shield volcanoes of the western islands, such as *Wolf Volcano* and *Sierra Negra* on *Isabela Island*, the largest landmass in the chain.
- Oceanographically sustained by the collision of major marine currents, notably the cold *Humboldt Current*, the *Cromwell Undercurrent*, and the warm *Panama Current*, creating upwellings of exceptional biodiversity.
- Inhabited by a famously long-lived tortoise named *Lonesome George*, the last surviving individual of the *Pinta Island* subspecies (*Chelonoidis niger abingdonii*), who died in 2012.
- First recorded in European history when *Fray Tomás de Berlanga*, the Bishop of Panama, drifted off course while sailing to Peru in 1535 during early maritime excursions under [[spain]].

## connections

- [[south-america]] — the continental landmass situated directly east, to which the archipelago belongs politically under Ecuadorian jurisdiction.
- [[dna]] — the fundamental genetic blueprint whose evolutionary diversification through adaptive radiation was demonstrated by Galápagos finch morphologies.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — the broad maritime era that brought early European navigators, whalers, and buccaneers to chart the remote Pacific islands.
- [[colonialism]] — the framework of imperial expansion and territorial claim that led [[spain]] to control the region before South American independence movements.
- [[spain]] — the Iberian crown whose explorers, led by the Bishop of Panama, first documented the chain as the enchanted islands or *Islas Encantadas*.
- [[gros-morne-national-park]] — fellow world-renowned wilderness and UNESCO World Heritage locality recognized internationally for revealing fundamental planetary processes.
