## summary

The **Dead Sea** is the lowest point on Earth's surface at approximately 1,410 feet (430 meters) below sea level, straddling the border between Israel and Palestine-Jordan in the Levantine region. A hypersaline terminal lake fed by the *Jordan River*, it is so mineral-rich that the human body floats effortlessly, making it both geographically unique and historically symbolically charged. Its stark landscape of salt formations, mineral deposits, and barren shores has been inhabited and contested since antiquity, connecting biblical history, economic geology, and contemporary geopolitics.

## you gotta know

- At 1,410 feet below sea level, it is the lowest elevation on any terrestrial landmass and one of Earth's most extreme environments.
- The *Jordan River* feeds it from the north, but the Dead Sea has no outflow, leading to evaporation and accumulation of dissolved minerals at roughly nine times the salinity of the Mediterranean.
- Historical names include *Salt Sea* (biblical) and *Sea of Lot* (Talmudic), reflecting ancient Near Eastern geography and mythology.
- Mineral extraction has been conducted on industrial scales; salt, potash, and bromine compounds are extracted commercially and form a major economic resource for the surrounding regions.
- The sea's surface has dropped dramatically in recent decades due to reduced inflow from the Jordan River and intensive upstream irrigation, creating environmental concern.
- Ancient Judean sites like Masada overlook its shores, making it a focal point in Jewish historical geography and memory.
- Its extreme salinity prevents most aquatic life, hence the biblical name; only halophilic (salt-loving) bacteria and algae survive, creating a unique ecosystem adapted to extremes.
- Geologically, the Dead Sea occupies the southern portion of the *Jordan Rift Valley*, a tectonic depression marking the boundary between the Arabian and African plates, part of a vast geological fault system extending from Turkey to Mozambique.
- The mineral-rich waters and therapeutic mud have made it a destination for health tourism and dermatological treatment, adding economic value beyond industrial extraction.
