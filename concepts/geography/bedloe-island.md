## summary

**Bedloe's Island**, officially redesignated as **Liberty Island** by an act of the federal government in 1956, is a prominent island in Upper New York Bay within the bustling maritime harbor of [[new-york-city]]. Famous worldwide as the permanent home of the colossal neoclassical sculpture *Liberty Enlightening the World*, the island serves as an enduring symbol of constitutional freedom, democratic government, and welcome to generations of immigrants arriving in the [[united-states]] from across the [[atlantic-ocean]]. Presented as a monumental gift by the people of [[france]] to commemorate the centennial of American independence and the historic revolutionary alliance between the two republics, the island remains among the most recognizable geographical and cultural landmarks in North America.

## you gotta know

- Dedicated as the site of the *Statue of Liberty* in October 1886 by President *Grover Cleveland*, honoring the diplomatic friendship forged during the [[american-revolution]] between [[france]] and the [[united-states]].
- Renamed by an Act of Congress in 1956 from its historic moniker, which originated with seventeenth-century Dutch merchant *Isaac Bedloo*, who acquired the property under English colonial authority.
- Anchors the colossal statue atop *Fort Wood*, an eleven-pointed star-shaped masonry redoubt completed in 1811 as part of the Second System of American coastal fortifications for harbor defense.
- Utilizes an ingenious internal iron pylon and flexible skeletal truss framework designed by civil engineer *Gustave Eiffel*, which allows the exterior skin of hammered repoussé copper sheets to expand and flex against oceanic winds.
- Served diverse utilitarian functions across the colonial and early national periods, including operating as a municipal quarantine station or pest house during smallpox epidemics and an ordnance storage depot for the military.
- Remains an exclave belonging to the borough of Manhattan in the state of New York, despite being situated entirely within the surrounding waters of New Jersey, and is administered as a National Monument by the National Park Service.
- Stood as the scene of the 1860 hanging of notorious maritime criminal *Albert Hicks*, which was the final public execution carried out under federal civil jurisdiction in the harbor of [[new-york-city]].
- Known historically to the indigenous Lenape people as *Minnissais*, meaning small island, prior to European settlement and Dutch colonization in the seventeenth century.

## connections

- [[new-york-city]] — the metropolitan harbor in which the island is situated as a federal enclave of Manhattan.
- [[france]] — the European republic that designed, funded, and built the colossal copper statue standing on the island.
- [[united-states]] — the sovereign nation whose harbor it guards and whose federal government designated it a national monument.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean body connecting European immigrant voyages with the primary shipping channels of New York Harbor.
- [[american-revolution]] — the foundational conflict whose Franco-American military alliance and centennial inspired the creation of the memorial.
- [[french-revolution]] — the historical era that advanced shared republican philosophy and political symbolism commemorated by the monument.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the technological age of advanced metallurgy and structural engineering that made the colossal statue feasible.
