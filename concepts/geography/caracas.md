## summary

**Caracas**, officially founded as **Santiago de León de Caracas**, is the capital, largest metropolis, and political heart of *Venezuela*, located in northern *South America*. Nestled within a mountain valley of the Venezuelan coastal range, the city sits only a short distance from the Caribbean Sea, shielded by the imposing peak of *Cerro El Ávila*. Renowned historically as the cradle of South American emancipation and the birthplace of *Simón Bolívar*, the metropolis expanded into an oil-fueled modern architectural showcase in the mid-twentieth century before becoming the focal point of modern political and economic transformations.

## you gotta know

- Revered across Latin America as the birthplace of *Simón Bolívar*, the towering leader known as *El Libertador*, as well as his intellectual revolutionary precursor *Francisco de Miranda*.
- Established in 1567 by Spanish conqueror *Diego de Losada* under the formal name **Santiago de León de Caracas**, following violent campaigns against local indigenous groups led by the chieftain *Guaicaipuro*.
- Separated from its Caribbean coastal outlet and primary seaport at *La Guaira* by the steep mountain wall of *El Ávila National Park* (*Waraira Repano*), which rises above 2,100 meters.
- Modernized dramatically during the mid-twentieth century using national petroleum revenues, culminating in the construction of the *Ciudad Universitaria de Caracas*, an architectural masterpiece designed by *Carlos Raúl Villanueva* and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- Rocked in February 1989 by the *Caracazo*, a widespread outbreak of civil unrest, looting, and severe military crackdowns triggered by structural economic adjustments and transit price increases.
- Devastated throughout its history by seismic activity, most famously during the disastrous 1812 earthquake that killed thousands of inhabitants and was exploited by royalist clergy to undermine the newly declared republic.
- Holds the national remains of founding heroes inside the *National Pantheon of Venezuela*, a former sacred sanctuary reconstructed in the nineteenth century to honor *Simón Bolívar*.
- Characterized geographically by profound urban contrast, where affluent modernist sectors stand adjacent to dense informal communities known as *barrios*, prominently exemplified by the vast expanse of *Petare*.

## connections

- [[south-america]] — the continent for which the city functioned as an epicenter of revolutionary political movements.
- [[spain]] — the imperial monarchy that founded the colonial capital and lost control of it following the South American wars of independence.
- [[andes]] — the dominant mountain chain whose northeastern branches terminate in the coastal ranges that cup the city's valley.
- [[colonialism]] — the system of Spanish imperial administration that organized the territory under the Captaincy General of Venezuela.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the broader maritime system accessible to the capital through the Caribbean ports situated immediately over the coastal ridge.
- [[cold-war]] — the global period during which Venezuelan oil extraction drove the modernization of urban infrastructure and influenced hemispheric energy politics.
