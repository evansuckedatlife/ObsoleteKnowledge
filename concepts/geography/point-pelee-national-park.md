## summary

**Point Pelee National Park** is a national park located in Essex County in southwestern Ontario, [[canada]], jutting into the shallow waters of western Lake Erie. Established in 1918, it preserves the southernmost point of the Canadian mainland, sitting at a latitude of 41°58'N—the same geographical parallel shared with northern California, Rome, and the Mediterranean basin. Renowned internationally among birdwatchers and naturalists, the triangular peninsula encompasses a fragile combination of deciduous Carolinian forests, barrier sand spits, and expansive freshwater marshes that provide an essential staging ground for continental avian and insect migrations.

## you gotta know

- Forms the southernmost extremity of mainland [[canada]], terminating in a narrow, dynamically shifting sand spit that changes shape and orientation according to Lake Erie's wind-driven currents and lake levels.
- Created in 1918 through the energetic lobbying of ornithologists and conservationists like *Percy Taverner*, becoming the first national park in [[canada]] established explicitly for ecological preservation rather than wilderness recreation.
- Anchors the Carolinian Life Zone, a rare southern deciduous forest ecosystem found nowhere else in the country, supporting hardwood species such as the tulip tree, sassafras, red mulberry, and hackberry.
- Designated a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance in 1987 due to its 1,100-hectare freshwater cattail marsh, which covers roughly seventy percent of the park's total land area and shelters rare reptiles and amphibians.
- Serves as a globally recognized migration funnel where hundreds of species of neotropical songbirds, including more than forty species of wood-warblers, concentrate each May during their northbound journey across the Great Lakes.
- Acts as a critical autumn gathering terminal for monarch butterflies (*Danaus plexippus*), which roost by the thousands in hackberry and cedar trees at the tip of the peninsula before embarking on their journey to central Mexico.
- Harbors numerous Canadian species at risk within its borders, including the endangered prothonotary warbler, the eastern foxsnake, the five-lined skink, the spotted turtle, and the prickly pear cactus (*Opuntia humifusa*).
- Located directly across Lake Erie from the [[united-states]], sharing identical climatic conditions and seasonal flyway dynamics with northern Ohio and Pennsylvania.

- [[united-states]] — neighbouring republic across Lake Erie whose borders and Great Lakes flyways align directly with the park's migratory corridor.
