## summary

**Cambodia** is a Southeast Asian nation centered on the Mekong River Delta, renowned as the home of the Khmer civilization and the sprawling temple complex of Angkor Wat. Devastated by war and genocide in the 20th century, Cambodia has since rebuilt its society while struggling with the legacies of conflict. Its profound historical achievements and tragic modern history make it essential to understanding both classical Southeast Asia and contemporary global conflicts.

## you gotta know

- Home to Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world, built by the Khmer Empire in the 12th century as a Hindu temple later converted to Buddhist use.
- The Khmer Empire at its height controlled much of Southeast Asia and developed sophisticated hydraulic engineering, agriculture, and art that shaped the region's culture.
- The Mekong River runs through Cambodia and provides its lifeblood, supporting fisheries, agriculture, and sustaining the fertile Tonlé Sap lake ecosystem.
- Cambodia fell under French colonial rule in the 19th century as part of French Indochina, gaining independence in 1953 under King Norodom Sihanouk.
- The Cambodian Civil War (1970-1975) followed by the Khmer Rouge regime (1975-1979) saw up to 2 million deaths from genocide and starvation; the trauma defines modern Cambodia.
- Vietnam invaded Cambodia in 1979 to overthrow the Khmer Rouge, but its subsequent occupation lasted a decade and was controversial internationally and domestically.
- Modern Cambodia has a constitutional monarchy and has gradually developed tourism and economy around its archaeological and natural heritage, particularly Angkor.
- The Khmer people comprise the majority of the population and speak Khmer, which uses its own unique script distinct from surrounding Southeast Asian languages.
- Cambodia's recovery from decades of conflict has been gradual, marked by challenges in democracy, press freedom, and human rights despite economic growth and tourism development.
