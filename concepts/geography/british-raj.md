## summary

The **British Raj** refers to *British* colonial rule over the *Indian* subcontinent from 1757 to 1947, a period of nearly two centuries that fundamentally transformed Indian geography, administration, economy, and society. Beginning with the *East India Company's* territorial acquisitions and formalized under direct Crown governance from 1858 to 1947, the Raj established unprecedented centralized administration, resource extraction, and cultural imposition. It ended with Indian independence under *Gandhi* and *Jawaharlal Nehru*, leaving legacies—administrative systems, railways, English language, and deep social tensions—that persist across the Indian subcontinent today.

## you gotta know

- The *East India Company*, a private trading enterprise, initially established footholds along the Indian coast; after military victories (especially the Battle of Plassey, 1757), it gradually conquered territory.
- Direct Crown rule began in 1858 following the Indian Rebellion of 1857; *India* formally became a Crown colony under a British viceroy appointed from London.
- Span nearly two centuries: roughly 1757–1947 under various formal arrangements; the most formalized period (the Raj proper) lasted 1858–1947.
- Established a unified administrative system across India's fragmented political landscape; this centralization was unprecedented and created the geographic and bureaucratic foundation of the modern nation-state.
- Extracted vast wealth: India's share of global GDP fell from roughly 23% (1700) to roughly 4% (1947); famines killed millions (particularly the Bengal Famine of 1943); resources flowed to Britain.
- Built extensive railways (40,000 miles of track by 1900s) ostensibly for commerce and governance but also serving military and extraction purposes; railways became a defining symbol of the Raj.
- Introduced English as the language of elite administration and education; created an English-educated Indian middle class that became the vanguard of independence movements.
- Enabled the Indian independence movement itself: the railways, education systems, and administrative structures the Raj created provided the infrastructure and consciousness for nationalist organizing; *Gandhi*, *Nehru*, and countless other independence leaders were educated under Raj institutions.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — the Raj was the jewel in the imperial crown; India was Britain's most valuable colonial possession.
- [[india]] — the subcontinent was the geographic and economic center of Raj power and extraction.
- [[gandhi]] — led the independence movement and civil resistance against the Raj; India's first independent prime minister.
- [[ceylon-sri-lanka]] — neighboring British colony governed under similar imperial frameworks; gained independence around the same time.
- [[partition-of-india]] — the geopolitical consequence of independence; the Raj's administrative unification was shattered along religious/ethnic lines in 1947.
- [[colonialism]] — the Raj exemplifies the economic, cultural, and political mechanisms of European colonialism.
- [[imperialism]] — a defining expression of 19th-century European imperial expansion.
- [[independence-movement]] — Indian nationalism emerged directly from Raj exploitation and governance.
