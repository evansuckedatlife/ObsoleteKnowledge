# South America

## summary

**South America** is the fourth-largest continent, occupying the southern and central portions of the Western Hemisphere and bordered by the Caribbean Sea, Atlantic Ocean, and Pacific Ocean. Home to over 430 million people across twelve independent nations and three territories, it encompasses extraordinary biodiversity, vast natural resources, complex colonial histories, and profound cultural diversity spanning indigenous civilizations to European settlement.

## you gotta know

- *Amazon Rainforest* covers roughly 40% of the continent, spanning nine countries with Brazil containing about 60%; it is the world's largest tropical rainforest and a critical carbon sink and biodiversity reservoir.
- The *Andes Mountains* form the world's longest continental mountain range (over 7,000 km), running the spine of the continent and forming borders between nations; they are geologically active and culturally significant to indigenous populations.
- *Brazil* dominates by area and population, comprising nearly half the continent's territory and nearly half its people, and is a major economic and political power in Latin America and globally.
- Indigenous peoples—*Aztecs*, *Inca*, *Mapuche*, *Guaraní*, and hundreds of smaller groups—inhabited South America for millennia before European contact; many survive today in remote regions and cities.
- European colonization (primarily *Spanish* and *Portuguese*) began in the 15th century, creating a colonial caste system and exploitative economies based on precious metals, agriculture, and enslaved labor; independence movements in the early 19th century established the modern nations.
- The continent contains the *Amazon River* (world's largest by discharge), the *Atacama Desert* (driest place on Earth), and *Lake Titicaca* (highest navigable lake); it straddles the equator, offering tropical, temperate, and polar climates.
- Major economic sectors include agriculture (sugar, coffee, soybeans), mining (copper, lithium, gold), hydroelectric power, and manufacturing; oil reserves in Venezuela and elsewhere have shaped geopolitics and conflict.
