## summary

**Social mobility** is the movement of individuals, families, or social groups through a system of socioeconomic stratification or class hierarchy over time. While contemporary human geography, spatial sociology, and economics investigate how neighborhood environments, regional infrastructure, and urbanization dictate these life trajectories, the pursuit of **upward mobility** forms a cornerstone of powerful cultural narratives such as the *American Dream*. Understanding the systemic barriers and historical pathways to class ascension is fundamental for analyzing wealth inequality, migratory patterns, and the recurring artistic critique of social striving across literature and philosophy.

## you gotta know

- *The American Dream* popularized the powerful ethos that any individual, regardless of their birth or inherited status, can achieve prosperity and elevated class through diligence, forming a defining ethos across the [[united-states]].
- *The Great Gatsby Curve*, popularized by economist *Alan Krueger* using data from *Miles Corak*, demonstrates an empirical relationship showing that nations with higher income inequality consistently suffer from lower intergenerational mobility.
- Human and spatial geographers examine the *geography of opportunity*, discovering that childhood neighborhoods, regional segregation, and commuting zones significantly determine adult earnings, as thoroughly documented by the *Opportunity Insights* research project.
- In nineteenth-century [[england]], the unprecedented commercial wealth generated by the [[industrial-revolution]] challenged entrenched aristocratic titles, prompting [[charles-dickens]] in *Great Expectations* to critique the shallow obsession with gentility and demonstrate that true nobility lies in character rather than class.
- Twentieth-century demographic shifts, most notably the *Great Migration* across the [[united-states]], represented widespread geographic efforts to attain economic progress, although discriminatory policies such as redlining created lasting barriers to housing equity.
- Classical sociology established the structural taxonomy of class movement through *Pitirim Sorokin*'s groundbreaking 1927 treatise *Social and Cultural Mobility*, which defined the crucial distinction between horizontal occupational shifts and vertical movements between socioeconomic strata.
- Economic historians differentiate between *absolute mobility*, which measures whether children earn more real income than their parents, and *relative mobility*, which evaluates an individual's rank fluidness across the income distribution.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — provided the primary geographic and ideological landscape for modern mobility myths, despite marked geographic variations in actual economic fluidness.
- [[charles-dickens]] — captured Victorian anxieties regarding class ascent, demonstrating through characters like Pip that social elevation often corrupts personal integrity.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — shattered feudal agrarian structures across [[europe]], fostering an urban factory working class while simultaneously creating a prosperous commercial bourgeoisie.
- [[enlightenment]] — articulated the philosophical groundwork for meritocracy, universal rights, and rational governance that challenged divine-right and hereditary caste privileges.
- [[french-revolution]] — dismantled the hereditary privileges of the nobility and clergy in [[france]], establishing legal equality as a foundational civic norm.
- [[civil-rights-movement]] — challenged Jim Crow segregation, labor exclusion, and disenfranchisement to secure civic equity and economic opportunity for Black Americans.
- [[new-york-city]] — served as a central global gateway for [[immigration]] and capital accumulation, dramatizing the immense spatial divide between tenement life and elite society.
- [[capitalism]] — created market-based mechanisms for wealth generation and capital accumulation, simultaneously enabling upward ascent and deepening class divides.
