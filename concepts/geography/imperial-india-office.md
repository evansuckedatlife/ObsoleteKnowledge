## summary

The **India Office**, also known historically as the **Imperial India Office**, was the British government department established in London in 1858 to oversee the governance of the Indian Empire. Created in the aftermath of the Indian Rebellion of 1857, it liquidated the administrative authority of the British East India Company and transferred executive sovereignty directly to the British Crown. Headed by the **Secretary of State for India**, a cabinet minister advised by the **Council of India**, the office exercised supreme administrative and financial authority over the Viceroy and provincial administrations until Indian independence and partition in 1947.

## you gotta know

- Established by the *Government of India Act 1858*, which abolished the dual control of the East India Company's Court of Directors and the government's Board of Control, formalizing direct imperial Crown rule under the British Raj.
- Headquartered in a monumental Whitehall building designed by *George Gilbert Scott* and *Matthew Digby Wyatt*, which featured the sumptuously decorated Foreign and Commonwealth Office quadrangle and Council Chamber.
- The political head was the *Secretary of State for India*, a senior member of the British cabinet who held ultimate accountability to the British Parliament in [[england]] for Indian revenues, military affairs, and public works.
- Assisted by the *Council of India*, a statutory advisory body of retired colonial administrators, judges, and military officers charged with vetting financial expenditures and administrative policies.
- Oversaw major imperial administrative reorganizations, including the *Morley-Minto Reforms* of 1909 and the *Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms* of 1919, which introduced limited legislative representation while retaining ultimate executive veto power in Whitehall.
- Administered external territories and transit routes crucial to the security of British [[india]], including Aden, the Persian Gulf residencies, [[ceylon-sri-lanka]], and Burma, which was detached as a separate colonial entity under the Burma Office in 1937.
- Dismantled in August 1947 following the *Indian Independence Act*, when its diplomatic and consular obligations were transferred to the Commonwealth Relations Office, leaving behind an extensive archive now preserved within the British Library.
