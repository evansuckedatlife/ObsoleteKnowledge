## summary

**Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi** (1869–1948), known as **Mahatma** ("Great Soul"), was the spiritual and political leader of India's independence movement against British colonial rule. Through nonviolent civil disobedience and mass mobilization across the Indian subcontinent, he transformed the geography of political resistance and helped secure India's sovereignty in 1947. His assassination by a Hindu nationalist in 1948 marked the transition from freedom fighter to iconic martyr whose philosophy of *satyagraha* (truth-force) influenced global movements for justice and decolonization.

## you gotta know

- Born in Gujarat, India; studied law in England and practiced as a barrister in South Africa, where he developed his philosophy of nonviolent resistance against racist colonial rule.
- Led the *Salt March* (1930), a 240-mile journey to the Arabian Sea to protest the British salt monopoly, which sparked mass civil disobedience and imprisoned thousands, including Gandhi himself.
- Negotiated India's independence during the twilight of the British Raj, most notably at the Round Table Conferences in London and through correspondence with British leaders.
- Championed the spinning wheel (*charkha*) and homespun cloth (*khadi*) as symbols of Indian self-sufficiency, economic independence, and rejection of British industrial goods.
- Advocated for Hindu-Muslim unity and opposed the Partition of India (1947), viewing the division into India and Pakistan as a tragic fracture of the subcontinent's political geography.
- Assassinated in Delhi in January 1948 by *Nathuram Godse*, a Hindu extremist opposed to his pluralism and acceptance of Pakistan's creation.
