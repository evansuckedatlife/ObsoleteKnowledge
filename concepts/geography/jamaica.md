## summary

**Jamaica** is an island nation of the Greater Antilles in the Caribbean Sea, situated south of Cuba and west of the island of Hispaniola. Inhabited originally by indigenous *Taíno* peoples before Spanish colonization, it was seized in the seventeenth century by the [[british-empire]] and developed into one of the world's most lucrative, brutal sugar-producing plantation economies. Today, the nation exerts an outsized global cultural influence through music, athletics, and religious movements, with its national capital centered at *Kingston*.

## you gotta know

- Seized from the Spanish in 1655 during the Anglo-Spanish War by an English expedition under *William Penn* and *Robert Venables* as part of *Oliver Cromwell's* Western Design.
- The notorious privateer haven of *Port Royal*, often described as the wickedest city in the world, served as a pirate base for figures like *Henry Morgan* until a catastrophic earthquake submerged two-thirds of it in 1692.
- The mountainous interior provided refuge for self-emancipated communities known as *Maroons*, who fought the colonial power in two major *Maroon Wars* under leaders such as *Granny Nanny* and *Cudjoe*.
- Major enslaved rebellions repeatedly rocked the island, notably *Tacky's War* in 1760 and the 1831 *Baptist War* led by *Samuel Sharpe*, which accelerated the abolition of slavery across the empire.
- The rugged karst landscape of *Cockpit Country* features dramatic conical limestone hills, sinkholes, and underground caves that historically sheltered anti-colonial resistance forces.
- The misty *Blue Mountains*, reaching the island's highest elevation at *Blue Mountain Peak*, produce the highly prized and globally traded *Jamaica Blue Mountain Coffee*.
- *Kingston* gave rise in the twentieth century to global musical genres including *ska*, *rocksteady*, and *reggae*, popularized internationally by singer *Bob Marley* and the *Rastafari* movement.
- The 1865 *Morant Bay Rebellion*, led by Baptist deacon *Paul Bogle*, was violently suppressed by Governor *Edward Eyre*, triggering intense political scandal and direct crown rule.

## connections

- [[british-empire]] — ruled the island as a prize sugar colony from the mid-seventeenth century until granting independence in 1962.
- [[colonialism]] — shaped the island's society, economy, and demographic makeup through the transatlantic slave trade and extractive chattel slavery.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — encompasses the maritime trade routes that connected the Caribbean to Europe, Africa, and North America.
- [[age-of-exploration]] — initiated European contact with the island when *Christopher Columbus* landed in 1494 during his second transatlantic voyage.
- [[united-states]] — destination for extensive twentieth-century Jamaican migration and commercial exchange, heavily influencing Caribbean diplomacy.
- [[world-war-ii]] — hosted allied naval and air installations under the destroyers-for-bases agreement between Britain and the American government.
