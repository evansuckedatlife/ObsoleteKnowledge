## summary

**Lake Titicaca** is a massive alpine body of water situated on the high *Altiplano* plateau straddling the border between Peru and Bolivia in western [[south-america]]. Sitting at an elevation of 3,812 meters, it is celebrated as the highest navigable body of water in the world and the largest freshwater lake by volume on the continent. Revered in Andean mythology as the ancestral cradle of the Inca civilization, it remains a vital ecological haven and cultural landmark sustained by indigenous Aymara and Quechua communities.

## you gotta know

- Sits at an altitude of approximately 3,812 meters (12,507 feet) above sea level high in the [[andes]] mountain range, earning the distinction of the world's highest navigable lake for commercial vessels.
- Split geographically between Peru to the west and Bolivia to the east, with major shoreline settlements including Puno on the Peruvian side and Copacabana on the Bolivian side.
- Fed by glacial melt and precipitation through more than twenty rivers, while draining almost all of its outflow into the *Desaguadero River*, which carries water south to Lake Poopó.
- Features the famous artificial floating islands constructed from dried totora reeds by the indigenous *Uros* people, who initially built them as mobile defense refuges against Inca expansion.
- Regarded in Inca cosmology as the sacred birthplace of humanity and the sun god *Inti*, who sent the mythical first Inca rulers, *Manco Cápac* and *Mama Ocllo*, from *Isla del Sol* (Island of the Sun).
- Divided into two distinct sub-basins connected by the narrow *Strait of Tiquina*: the larger and deeper *Lago Mayor* (or *Lago Chucuito*) and the smaller *Lago Menor* (or *Wiñaymarka*).
- Home to unique endemic and critically endangered wildlife adapted to high altitude, including the giant *Titicaca water frog* (*Telmatobius culeus*) and the flightless *Titicaca grebe*.
