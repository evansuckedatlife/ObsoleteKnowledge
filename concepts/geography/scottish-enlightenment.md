## summary

The **Scottish Enlightenment** was a period of intellectual ferment in 18th-century Scotland that produced foundational ideas in philosophy, economics, political thought, and natural science. Centered in Edinburgh and Glasgow, luminaries like David Hume and Adam Smith developed empiricist epistemology and early capitalist economic theory that would reshape Western thought. Though geographically remote from continental Enlightenment centers, Scotland punched far above its weight in intellectual influence during this transformative era.

## you gotta know

- Flourished roughly between 1730 and 1790, peaking in the latter half of the 18th century as Scotland recovered economically after the failed Jacobite Rising of 1745.
- Centered in Edinburgh, which became a hub of coffee-house debate, literary societies, and university intellectual life.
- David Hume developed radical empiricism and skepticism, questioning causation and innatism, influencing Kant and later philosophy.
- Adam Smith wrote *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) and *The Wealth of Nations* (1776), establishing foundational economic and social thought.
- James Watt, Joseph Black, and other natural philosophers made breakthroughs in engineering, chemistry, and physics that enabled the Industrial Revolution.
- Characteristic themes included skepticism toward received authority, emphasis on observation and experience, and practical application of ideas to improve society.
- Often called the "Scottish Age of Reason"; contrasted with Continental Enlightenment in being more pragmatic, skeptical, and focused on natural philosophy.
