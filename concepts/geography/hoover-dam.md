## summary

**Hoover Dam**, originally authorized as **Boulder Dam**, is a colossal concrete arch-gravity structure spanning the Black Canyon of the Colorado River along the border of Nevada and Arizona in the [[united-states]]. Constructed between 1931 and 1936 during the depths of the Great Depression, the structure tamed the volatile seasonal flooding of the lower river basin while generating massive hydroelectric output and impounding *Lake Mead*. It stands as a pinnacle achievement of twentieth-century civil engineering that catalyzed the urban and agricultural transformation of the American Southwest.

## you gotta know

- Built in Black Canyon to impound the Colorado River, creating *Lake Mead*, which remains the largest artificial reservoir by total water capacity in the [[united-states]].
- Authorized under the 1928 *Boulder Canyon Project Act* and dedicated in September 1935 by President *Franklin D. Roosevelt*, finished more than two years ahead of its projected schedule.
- Constructed by a specialized consortium of six major engineering contractors known as *Six Companies, Inc.*, under the legendary direction of construction superintendent *Frank Crowe*.
- Named originally for Secretary of Commerce and later President *Herbert Hoover*, who mediated the foundational 1922 *Colorado River Compact*, the dam was officially renamed *Boulder Dam* by Interior Secretary *Harold Ickes* in 1933 before Congress permanently restored the Hoover name in 1947.
- Engineers solved the problem of extreme chemical heat generated by curing concrete by pouring the structure in individual interlocking vertical blocks cooled by an internal grid of refrigerated pipes.
- Features striking *Art Deco* architectural appointments designed by *Gordon B. Kaufmann*, highlighted by terrazzo floor mosaics, elegant intake towers, and *Oskar J.W. Hansen*'s monumental bronze statues, the *Winged Figures of the Republic*.
- Provided the critical electrical power and water security that fostered the explosive wartime expansion of defense industries in Southern California during [[world-war-ii]].
- Permanently halted the natural seasonal sediment and water flush downstream, fundamentally altering the estuarine ecology of the [[gulf-of-california]].

## connections

- [[grand-canyon]] — upstream geological landmark through which the Colorado River rushes before settling into the waters of *Lake Mead*.
- [[gulf-of-california]] — marine terminal basin in Mexico that suffered ecological desiccation following the dam's impoundment of river waters.
- [[grand-coulee-dam]] — New Deal contemporary dam on the Columbia River that exceeded it in total physical volume and peak power capacity.
- [[united-states]] — nation whose federal Bureau of Reclamation financed, engineered, and oversees the facility.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — era of mechanized manufacturing, structural metallurgy, and bulk concrete chemistry that made such mega-engineering projects feasible.
- [[world-war-ii]] — global conflict whose Pacific theater aircraft manufacturing and naval shipyards relied heavily on the dam's electrical generation.
