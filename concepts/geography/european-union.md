## summary

The **European Union** is a supranational political and economic union of 27 member states, established through successive treaties beginning with the European Economic Community in 1957. Designed to prevent future European wars through economic integration and shared governance, it has created a single market, a shared currency (the *euro*), and a continental parliament. The EU represents the most ambitious political experiment in international cooperation, though it faces persistent tensions over sovereignty, immigration, and economic inequality.

## you gotta know

- Originated as the European Coal and Steel Community (1951), a six-nation agreement to pool coal and steel production under shared authority; *Belgium*, *France*, West *Germany*, *Italy*, and the Netherlands were founders.
- The European Economic Community (1957) expanded integration to all economic sectors; by the 1990s, it was renamed the European Union and added political governance structures.
- The *Maastricht Treaty* (1992) introduced the euro, a shared currency adopted by 19 EU member states; it symbolized continental unity but also created asymmetries between strong and weak economies.
- The EU operates through complex institutions: the European Commission (executive), the Council of Ministers (legislative), and the European Parliament (directly elected by citizens since 1979).
- *Cold War* membership divisions were profound: Western Europe joined the EU while Eastern Europe remained in the Soviet sphere until 1989; post-Cold War expansion eastward after 2004 reshaped EU politics.
- The 2008 financial crisis exposed structural vulnerabilities: southern European nations (Greece, *Spain*, *Italy*) could not devalue currency to restore competitiveness, triggering austerity and social upheaval.
- *Brexit* (2020) marked the first member departure, reflecting rising populism and doubt that EU membership served all citizens equally, particularly in economically depressed regions.
- Contemporary EU debates center on climate policy, digital regulation, immigration quotas, and the balance between Brussels bureaucracy and national democracy.
