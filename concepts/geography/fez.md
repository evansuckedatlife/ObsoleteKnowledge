## summary

**Fez**, alternatively transliterated as **Fes**, is an ancient inland metropolis in northern Morocco situated along the Saïss plain between the Rif and Middle Atlas mountains. Founded at the close of the eighth century by *Idris I* and expanded under his successor *Idris II*, the city long served as the spiritual, intellectual, and traditional dynastic capital of the Moroccan sultanates. It remains renowned throughout the Islamic world for its historic medina, traditional craft guilds, vibrant leather tanneries, and prestigious center of classical scholarship.

## you gotta know

- Houses the venerable *University of al-Qarawiyyin*, founded in 859 CE by the wealthy refugee *Fatima al-Fihri*, recognized by UNESCO as the oldest continuously operating higher learning institution in the world.
- The ancient core, *Fes el-Bali*, stands as one of the largest preserved medieval walled cities on Earth, functioning as a massive car-free urban maze navigated entirely by foot and pack animals.
- World-famous for its historic outdoor leather tanneries, particularly the medieval *Chouara Tannery*, where artisans treat and dye animal skins using circular stone vats containing natural pigments, pigeon guano, and limestone.
- Lends its name to the iconic brimless, truncated conical red felt headwear known as the *fez* or *tarboosh*, which was historically colored with crimson berry dye produced in the city before gaining widespread adoption across the Ottoman Empire.
- Structurally divided into distinct historical sectors, encompassing the original eighth-century *Fes el-Bali*, the thirteenth-century royal quarter *Fes el-Jdid*, the Jewish quarter known as the *Mellah*, and the twentieth-century French-built *Ville Nouvelle*.
- Served as the setting for the 1912 *Treaty of Fez*, an agreement imposed on Sultan Abd al-Hafid that formally created the French Protectorate in Morocco and sparked severe local uprisings.
- Blossomed into a renowned refuge for intellectual exiles following the Reconquista, absorbing waves of Andalusian Muslims and Sephardic Jews who enriched the city's architectural styles and religious scholarship.
- Showcases magnificent examples of Marinid architecture decorated with intricate cedar woodwork, carved plaster, and geometric *zellij* mosaic tilework, notably preserved in the *Bou Inania Madrasa* and *al-Attarine Madrasa*.

## connections

- [[islam]] — the faith that inspired the city's founding by the Idrisid dynasty and centers its intellectual life on al-Qarawiyyin.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — the northern maritime theater connected through Moroccan trading corridors to southern Europe and the Levant.
- [[middle-east]] — the broader cultural and religious sphere with which Fez maintained continual religious and academic communion.
- [[colonialism]] — the European imperial project that led to the French military administration established by the 1912 Treaty of Fez.
- [[spain]] — the neighboring Iberian power from which thousands of Andalusian refugees migrated to Fez after the fall of Islamic Granada.
- [[france]] — the Western imperial nation that administered Morocco during the protectorate period and constructed the modern administrative quarters.
