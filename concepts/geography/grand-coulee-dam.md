## summary

**Grand Coulee Dam** is a gargantuan concrete gravity dam spanning the Columbia River in the high desert of central Washington state in the [[united-states]]. Constructed between 1933 and 1942 as the crown jewel of the New Deal's Public Works Administration, it remains the largest concrete structure by volume and the greatest individual producer of hydroelectric power in North America. By impounding the expansive waters of *Franklin D. Roosevelt Lake*, the multi-purpose complex provides crucial regional flood control, generates over twenty billion kilowatt-hours annually, and supplies irrigation water to hundreds of thousands of agricultural acres across the Columbia Basin Project.

## you gotta know

- Contains nearly twelve million cubic yards of concrete, making it the most massive concrete structure ever erected in North America, with a crest spanning over four-fifths of a mile.
- Conceived during the Great Depression under President *Franklin D. Roosevelt* to provide industrial employment, cheap public power, and systematic reclamation of arid farmland in central Washington.
- Backed up the flow of the Columbia River into *Franklin D. Roosevelt Lake*, a reservoir extending over 150 miles northward almost to the border with [[canada]].
- Supplied the essential, uninterrupted electrical output that energized regional aluminum smelters and the secretive plutonium production facilities at the *Hanford Site* during [[world-war-ii]].
- Immortalized in American folk music when the Bonneville Power Administration commissioned singer *Woody Guthrie* in 1941 to write promotional ballads, producing anthems such as *Roll On, Columbia* and *Grand Coulee Dam*.
- Completely lacked fish ladders for its 550-foot height, permanently barring migratory salmon and steelhead from reaching more than 1,100 miles of native upstream spawning grounds and devastating the subsistence culture of the *Colville* and *Spokane* tribes.
- Expanded in the 1970s through the construction of the Third Powerplant, which raised the dam's total generating capacity to more than 6,800 megawatts.
