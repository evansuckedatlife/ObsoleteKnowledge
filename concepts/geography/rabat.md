## summary

**Rabat** is the political and administrative capital of the Kingdom of *Morocco*, situated on the northwestern coastline of Africa where the *Bou Regreg* river empties into the [[atlantic-ocean]]. Established in the twelfth century by the *Almohad Caliphate* as a fortified military stronghold known as *Ribat al-Fath*, the city was designed to serve as a launching ground for military expeditions into Islamic *Iberia*. Ranked alongside *Fes*, *Marrakech*, and *Meknes* as one of the four historic imperial cities of Morocco, it combines medieval Almohad monuments with the early twentieth-century urban architecture of [[france]].

## you gotta know

- Forms an expansive metropolitan region on the Atlantic seaboard directly opposite its historical twin city, the corsair port of *Salé*, across the estuary of the *Bou Regreg*.
- Founded in 1146 by the Almohad caliph *Abd al-Mu'min*, who constructed a fortified monastery (*ribat*) to assemble armies for campaigns across the *Strait of Gibraltar*.
- Dominated by the monumental red sandstone *Hassan Tower*, the unfinished minaret of an immense twelfth-century mosque commissioned by the Almohad ruler *Abu Yaqub Yusuf al-Mansur*.
- Preserves the fortified *Kasbah of the Udayas*, an ancient clifftop fortress overlooking the ocean that features whitewashed and indigo-painted buildings and classic Andalusian gardens.
- Served in the seventeenth century as the capital of the pirate-dominated *Republic of Bou Regreg*, which flourished when expelled Moriscos arrived after their eviction from [[spain]].
- Designated as the official capital of modern Morocco in 1912 by French resident-general *Louis-Hubert Lyautey*, who transferred the seat of administration from *Fes* during the era of [[colonialism]].
- Contains the *Mausoleum of Mohammed V*, an architectural masterpiece of modern Alaouite design housing the white onyx tombs of the king of independence and his son *Hassan II*.
- Designated a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2012, recognized specifically for the harmonious coexistence between historic Islamic monuments and modern urban planning influenced by [[france]].

## connections

- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean basin bordering the city, which historic maritime expeditions and corsair vessels navigated for centuries.
- [[islam]] — the religious and civilizational tradition that inspired the grand mosques, madrasas, and caliphal architecture of the capital.
- [[france]] — the colonial power that established a protectorate over Morocco in 1912 and laid out the modern *Ville Nouvelle*.
- [[colonialism]] — the period of European imperial administration that shifted the center of Moroccan political authority to coastal ports.
- [[spain]] — the neighboring Iberian nation from which thousands of expelled Muslims and Moriscos migrated, revitalizing local seafaring and commerce.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — the nearby aquatic realm accessed via Gibraltar that connected North African ports to European and Ottoman trade routes.
- [[middle-east]] — the broader cultural and geopolitical region with which Morocco shares enduring linguistic, legal, and spiritual ties.
