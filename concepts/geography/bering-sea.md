# Bering Sea

## summary

The **Bering Sea** is a shallow marginal sea of the North Pacific, separating *Alaska* from eastern *Russia* by roughly 55 miles at its narrowest point (the *Bering Strait*). Extraordinarily rich in marine life and mineral resources, it has been contested territory between indigenous peoples, Russians, and Americans. The sea is a critical fishery, particularly for salmon and crab, and has become a strategic point in climate change and geopolitical competition for Arctic resources.

## you gotta know

- Named for Danish explorer *Vitus Bering*, who explored it in the 1730s under Russian sponsorship; the strait at its northern end is also named for him.
- The *Bering Strait* (56 miles wide) is the only water barrier between Asia and North America; during ice ages when sea levels dropped, land bridges (Beringia) connected the continents, allowing human migration.
- One of the world's most productive fisheries: salmon, crab, pollock, and halibut support major commercial and subsistence fisheries, particularly for *Alaska*.
- Indigenous peoples—*Yupik*, *Inupiat*, *Chukchi*, *Siberian Yup'ik*—have inhabited its coasts for thousands of years and maintain traditional subsistence practices.
- The *Yukon River* flows into it, providing freshwater input and connecting interior North America to the sea.
- Climate change is rapidly warming the Bering Sea, shifting fish stocks, threatening Arctic marine mammals, and opening new shipping routes and resource exploration opportunities.
- Geopolitical significance: Russian-American boundary disputes, subsistence rights for indigenous peoples, and emerging Arctic competition for natural gas and minerals make it strategically important.
