## summary

**Georgian architecture** refers to the predominant classical design style across Great Britain, Ireland, and colonial territories between 1714 and 1830, spanning the reigns of the first four Hanoverian monarchs named *George*. Inspired by Renaissance Palladianism and the ruins of classical antiquity, the style emphasized proportion, symmetry, and restrained ornament. It transformed urban centers across the British Isles and the Atlantic seaboard, leaving behind recognizable uniform brick terraces, elegant squares, and aristocratic country houses.

## you gotta know

- Defined by strict adherence to classical Roman and Greek orders, popularized widely by *Andrea Palladio* and disseminated through architectural pattern books by authors like *Colen Campbell* in *Vitruvius Britannicus*.
- Façades are universally recognizable by rigorous bilateral symmetry, evenly spaced multi-pane sash windows (commonly six-over-six), and balanced entranceways flanked by classical pilasters.
- Townhouses frequently feature pedimented porticos and distinctive semi-circular decorative fanlights positioned directly above panelled wooden entry doors.
- Famous urban planning masterpieces include the *Circus* and *Royal Crescent* in *Bath*, designed by *John Wood, the Elder* and *John Wood, the Younger*.
- *Dublin* underwent massive expansion under the *Wide Streets Commission*, creating iconic brick townhouses and garden squares that contrasted with the rural traditions of the native [[gaelic]] population.
- The New Town of *Edinburgh* in [[scotland]], planned by *James Craig* in 1766, stands as one of the finest unified examples of neoclassical Georgian civic design in [[europe]].
- In the American colonies, the movement evolved into colonial Georgian and later *Federal style* buildings, exemplified by historic brick civic structures and Southern plantations.
- The later period saw the emergence of the *Regency style*, championed by *John Nash*, which incorporated stucco façades, bows, and more eclectic classical ornamentation.

## connections

- [[england]] — the imperial heartland where the style was developed, codified, and patronized by the Hanoverian royal court and aristocracy.
- [[scotland]] — the home of pioneering Georgian architects such as *Robert Adam* and the location of the acclaimed *Edinburgh New Town*.
- [[british-empire]] — the global imperial network that exported Georgian civic planning, forts, and government houses across worldwide colonies.
- [[enlightenment]] — the rational philosophical movement whose admiration for classical antiquity and order directly informed Georgian architectural theory.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the economic expansion that financed extensive Georgian urban housing developments, brickworks, and infrastructure canals.
- [[gaelic]] — the linguistic and cultural heritage of the rural Irish and Scottish populations who often lived in stark socio-economic contrast to the Anglo-Irish Ascendancy dwelling in Georgian urban terraces.
