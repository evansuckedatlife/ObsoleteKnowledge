## summary

**The Arctic Ocean** is the smallest and shallowest of Earth's five oceans, surrounding the North Pole and largely covered by ice, with coastlines touching Russia, Canada, Greenland, and Arctic island territories. For millennia, Arctic peoples adapted to one of Earth's harshest environments, but the region remained largely inaccessible to European exploration until relatively recent times. Today, the Arctic Ocean is becoming strategically and economically significant due to climate change, resource exploration, geopolitical competition, and indigenous sovereignty questions.

## you gotta know

- The Arctic Ocean covers approximately 14.5 million square kilometers and is ringed by the northernmost landmasses including the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, Greenland, northern Russia, and Arctic island groups; the North Pole lies within its ice-covered central basin.
- Arctic sea ice forms and melts seasonally; thick multi-year ice once dominated, but recent decades have seen dramatic ice loss due to climate change, causing concern about ecological disruption and creating new navigational possibilities.
- The ocean is fed by major river systems including the *Mackenzie River* (draining western Canada), the *Yukon River*, and vast Russian rivers, which discharge enormous quantities of freshwater into the Arctic and influence its unique oceanographic conditions.
- Indigenous peoples—the *Inuit*, *Yupik*, *Sámi*, *Chukchi*, and other Arctic groups—have inhabited Arctic regions for thousands of years, developing sophisticated adaptations to extreme cold, seasonal darkness, and ice-dependent ecology through hunting, fishing, and herding traditions.
- The Arctic was historically isolated from European contact, but exploration accelerated in the 16th–19th centuries as powers sought the "Northwest Passage" (a navigable sea route through Arctic Canada to Asia); these quests were driven by both scientific curiosity and commercial ambition.
- The Arctic is geologically rich in petroleum, natural gas, and mineral resources; as ice retreat makes exploration and extraction possible, resource competition intensifies among Arctic nations and between national governments and indigenous peoples asserting land rights.
- The Arctic Ocean and surrounding regions have become a focal point of Cold War rivalry and contemporary geopolitical competition; Russia, Canada, the United States, and other nations dispute sovereignty over ice-covered territories and shipping routes.
- Climate change is transforming the Arctic faster than almost any other region on Earth; melting ice threatens traditional Arctic ecosystems, indigenous ways of life, and existing infrastructure while creating new economic opportunities and geopolitical vulnerabilities.
