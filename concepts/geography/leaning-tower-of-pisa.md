## summary

The **Leaning Tower of Pisa**, known locally as the **Torre pendente di Pisa** or simply the **Tower of Pisa**, is the freestanding bell tower (*campanile*) of the cathedral in the Tuscan city of Pisa in central [[italy]]. Constructed in stages over nearly two centuries beginning in 1173, the Romanesque marble structure began tilting almost immediately due to inadequate foundation depth set in soft, unstable alluvial soil. It stands as one of the most recognizable architectural wonders in [[europe]], famous both for its precarious angle and its role in early scientific folklore.

## you gotta know

- Functions primarily as the freestanding bell tower for the neighboring Pisa Cathedral (*Duomo di Santa Maria Assunta*) within the historic *Piazza dei Miracoli* (Square of Miracles).
- Began tilting visibly during the construction of its third story in 1178 because of an unstable foundation resting on soft mud, sand, and clay deposited by the Arno and Serchio rivers.
- Took nearly two centuries to finish, with work halting repeatedly due to wars between Pisa and rival city-states like Genoa and Florence, which inadvertently allowed the underlying soil to settle and saved the tower from early collapse.
- Built in an ornate Romanesque design featuring bands of white and grey marble arches, credited in its early phase to architects *Bonanno Pisano* and *Gherardo din Gherardo*, and completed by *Tommaso di Andrea Pisano* around 1372.
- Served as the legendary setting where physicist and astronomer *Galileo Galilei* reportedly dropped two cannonballs of different masses from the top to demonstrate that acceleration under gravity is independent of mass.
- Subjected to major modern geotechnical stabilization efforts between 1990 and 2001, which carefully removed subsoil beneath the raised northern side and reduced the lean to ensure structural safety.
- Features seven large bells tuned to musical notes, though they were silenced during preservation work to prevent vibrations from aggravating the tilt.
