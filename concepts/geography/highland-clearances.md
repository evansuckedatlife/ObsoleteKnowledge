# Highland Clearances

## summary

The **Highland Clearances** were the systematic removal of tenant farmers and crofters from the Scottish Highlands between roughly 1760 and 1850, as landlords transformed communal clan lands into privately owned estates used for sheep ranching. Driven by economic rationalization, agricultural improvement ideology, and the dismantling of the clan system following the Jacobite defeat of 1746, the Clearances displaced tens of thousands and fundamentally reshaped the geography, demography, and social structure of the Highlands. Mass emigration to North America, Australia, and other colonies resulted, making the Clearances a crucial episode in the history of Scotland's internal colonization and global diaspora.

## you gotta know

- Followed the suppression of the Jacobite clans in 1746, which had already destabilized the Highlands' traditional feudal social order and made landlords more interested in profit than obligation to tenants.
- Landlords enclosed common lands and removed tenant farmers to create unified sheep-ranching estates, applying "improvement" ideology that prioritized capitalist efficiency over community continuity.
- Affected primarily the far north (Sutherland, Ross and Cromarty) and the Hebridean islands, where entire glens were emptied of human population within a generation.
- Timing overlapped with the Industrial Revolution, when Lowland Scotland industrialized; the Highlands were economized into a rural periphery serving urban industrial needs.
- Sparked mass emigration: an estimated 46,000 Scots emigrated to North America and Australia during the Clearances, establishing diaspora communities and exporting Highland grievance internationally.
- Led to depopulation: the Highlands' share of Scotland's population fell from roughly 40 percent (1700) to less than 10 percent (1900), an absolute decline with lasting cultural consequences.
