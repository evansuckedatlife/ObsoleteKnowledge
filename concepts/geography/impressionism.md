## summary

**Impressionism** was a revolutionary art movement that emerged in 1870s Paris, fundamentally transforming how artists approached light, color, and perception. Born from the scientific study of light and optical mixing, the movement rejected academic tradition in favor of capturing fleeting moments and atmospheric effects directly from nature. The movement, centered in France, became a defining characteristic of late 19th-century visual culture and paved the way for modern art.

## you gotta know

- Named derisively from Claude Monet's painting *Impression, Sunrise* (1872); critics used "impressionism" mockingly, but the name stuck.
- Emerged directly from French intellectual and artistic culture; Paris was the undisputed center, with plein-air painting experiments conducted in the French countryside.
- Key figures include Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Edgar Degas, Berthe Morisot, and Camille Pissarro, who exhibited together independently rather than in official Salon exhibitions.
- Emphasized visible brushstrokes, broken color (optical mixing), and the capture of light and shadow rather than precise detail; artists often worked rapidly on small canvases.
- Combined scientific study of light with Romantic sensitivity to nature; influenced by Japanese prints and photography's fresh perspectives on composition.
- Catalyzed modernism by proving that art could be experimental, subjective, and market-driven outside traditional institutions; this freedom inspired Post-Impressionism, Fauvism, and beyond.
- The movement rejected academic tradition and the Salon system, organizing independent exhibitions (eight shows between 1874–1886) where artists could display work without official gatekeepers.
- Impressionist subjects ranged from water lilies and haystacks (Monet's serial paintings exploring light's effects) to ballet dancers, cafés, and urban landscapes capturing modernity.
- Economic barriers to entry were low; Impressionists worked on smaller, portable canvases rather than monumental history paintings, democratizing art production.
- By the 1890s, Impressionism had achieved market success and influenced a generation of artists to seek experimental freedom; major museums now recognize it as foundational to modern art.

## connections

- [[france]] — the birthplace of Impressionism, and particularly Paris as the artistic capital.
- [[paris]] — the city where Impressionist painters gathered, exhibited, and developed their revolutionary technique.
- [[modernism]] — the broader artistic shift toward experimentation and rejection of tradition that Impressionism initiated.
- [[claude-monet]] — the movement's quintessential figure; his water lilies and haystacks exemplify Impressionist principles.
- [[visual-art-movements]] — Impressionism's position within the larger history of European art.
- [[belle-epoque]] — the cultural period of Belle Époque Paris coincided with Impressionism's heyday.
