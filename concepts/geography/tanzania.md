## summary

**Tanzania** is a large East African nation formed in 1964 from the merger of Tanganyika and Zanzibar, encompassing diverse landscapes from *Mount Kilimanjaro* to the *Serengeti* and serving as home to over 120 ethnic groups and languages. As a major center of human prehistory—particularly the Olduvai Gorge—and as the site of significant post-colonial nation-building under Julius Nyerere, Tanzania represents both ancient human heritage and modern African independence. Its ecological, cultural, and geopolitical significance makes it a key player in East African affairs.

## you gotta know

- *Mount Kilimanjaro* (5,895 m), Africa's highest mountain, is located in northeastern Tanzania; it is a dormant volcano and iconic symbol of the continent, attracting thousands of climbers annually.
- The *Serengeti*, a vast savanna ecosystem in northern Tanzania, is renowned for its annual migration of wildebeest, zebras, and gazelles—one of the world's most spectacular natural phenomena—and for its diverse megafauna including lions, leopards, and African wild dogs.
- *Lake Victoria*, the world's second-largest freshwater lake, is shared by Tanzania, Uganda, and Kenya; it is the source of the *Nile River* and supports millions of people through fisheries and agriculture.
- The *Olduvai Gorge*, located in northern Tanzania, is one of the most important paleontological sites in the world; fossil discoveries there by Louis and Mary Leakey provided crucial evidence of human evolution and early hominid species.
- Tanzania was colonized by Germany in the late 19th century, then partitioned between Britain and Belgium after World War I; the British mandate territory of Tanganyika gained independence in 1961, while Zanzibar (an Arab sultanate and British protectorate) gained independence in 1964 and shortly after merged with Tanganyika to form Tanzania.
- *Julius Nyerere*, Tanzania's first president (1964–1985), pioneered the ideology of *Ujamaa* (African socialism) and attempted to build a unified, non-aligned nation free from Cold War pressures; he remains a significant figure in post-colonial African political thought.
- Dar es Salaam, Tanzania's largest city, is a major port on the Indian Ocean and historical hub of East African trade; the capital was relocated to Dodoma in the 1970s as part of Nyerere's nation-building efforts.
- Tanzania is ethnically and linguistically diverse; *Swahili* is the national language, and over 120 ethnic groups including the *Masai*, *Arusha*, and *Sukuma* maintain distinct cultures and traditions.
