## summary

An **ice sheet**, also known as a **continental glacier** or **inland ice**, is a colossal mass of continuous glacial ice spanning more than fifty thousand square kilometers that completely buries the underlying regional topography. In modern geography, Earth features only two active examples: the massive Antarctic ice sheet and the Greenland ice sheet, which collectively contain the vast majority of the planet's freshwater reserves. These dynamic bodies regulate global sea levels, drive oceanic thermohaline circulation, and serve as invaluable archives of Earth's paleoclimate through deep stratigraphic ice cores.

## you gotta know

- Defined by glaciologists as covering an area exceeding 50,000 square kilometers, which distinguishes them categorically from smaller alpine ice fields, ice caps, and valley glaciers.
- Contains approximately 99 percent of the world's freshwater ice, with the Antarctic mass alone sequestering sufficient water volume to elevate global sea levels by roughly 58 meters if melted.
- Covered large expanses of North America during the Pleistocene epoch under the massive Laurentide ice sheet, whose advance and retreat carved the Great Lakes and scoured the terrain of modern *Canada*.
- Discharges ice outward toward the sea via fast-moving ice streams and outlet glaciers, feeding vast floating ice shelves like the Ross Shelf that act as structural buttresses against interior collapse.
- Preserves high-resolution paleoclimatic archives within deep interior domes, where projects such as *EPICA Dome C* and *Vostok* have extracted ice cores recording atmospheric composition across 800,000 years.
- Experiences structural vulnerability through marine ice sheet instability, particularly along the West Antarctic sheet where bedrock slopes downward below sea level, exposing grounding lines to warm oceanic currents.
- Captivated nineteenth-century landscape painters and scientific naturalists, whose expeditions into polar latitudes captured the sublime scale of calving glaciers and towering sea ice for public exhibitions.

## connections

- [[canada]] — the northern nation whose bedrock, boreal topography, and extensive river networks were sculpted directly by the melting of the Laurentide glacial sheet.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean basin receiving cold meltwater and calving icebergs from Greenland, driving the critical Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation.
- [[hudson-river-school]] — the American romantic painting movement whose artists, especially *Frederic Edwin Church*, voyaged north to paint monumental Arctic icebergs and glacial frontiers.
- [[andes]] — the high-altitude continental mountain system whose alpine valley glaciers and high ice fields offer contrast to polar continental sheets.
- [[polar-regions]] — the high-latitude geographic zones where persistent freezing temperatures allow enormous thicknesses of perennial ice to accumulate.
- [[south-pole]] — the southernmost point of the globe, positioned at an altitude of over 2,800 meters upon the high, frozen plateau of East Antarctica.
- [[penguin]] — flightless marine birds whose breeding cycles and foraging behaviors depend heavily on coastal sea ice and perimeter shelves ringing Antarctica.
