## summary

**Emigration** is the act of leaving one's native country or region to settle permanently in another, distinct from immigration, which describes that same relocation from the perspective of the destination state. Across modern history, massive waves of human departure have been impelled by economic dislocation, state persecution, famine, political upheaval, and coercive land clearances. From the Highland Clearances to global industrial urbanization, organized out-migration redistributed populations globally, fostering enduring diaspora communities and radically reshaping global demographics.

## you gotta know

- Defined conceptually as the outward flow of human populations from a sending community, driven by a combination of negative "push" factors at home and attractive "pull" factors abroad.
- Driven acutely in eighteenth- and nineteenth-century [[scotland]] by the Highland Clearances, wherein landlords evicted traditional tenant farmers to consolidate pastoral sheep walks, forcing thousands into overseas transit.
- Accelerated enormously by the [[industrial-revolution]], as agrarian enclosure, urban overcrowding, and technological disruption spurred millions of European laborers to seek homesteads or industrial wages overseas.
- Traversed primarily across the [[atlantic-ocean]] during the "Century of Immigration" (roughly 1820 to 1924), channeling over fifty million Europeans to destination harbors in North and South America.
- Stimulated systematically by the imperial framework of the [[british-empire]], which actively organized assisted passages, land grants, and settler colonization in Canada, Australia, and New Zealand.
- Facilitated the worldwide propagation of the [[english-language]], as departing British, Scottish, and Irish emigrants carried their mother tongue into overseas frontier settlements and port cities.
- Resulted in durable transnational remittance economies, mutual aid organizations, and vibrant expatriate institutions that maintained political, cultural, and linguistic links with ancestral homelands.

## connections

- [[scotland]] — the country whose Highland and Lowland populations dispersed globally under the pressures of agrarian clearance, industrialization, and poverty.
- [[british-empire]] — the sprawling maritime domain whose trade routes, settler programs, and naval dominion channeled massive outflows of metropolitan subjects.
- [[industrial-revolution]] — the structural economic transformation that shattered traditional peasant economies and generated colossal migratory pressures toward new centers of labor.
- [[colonialism]] — the geopolitical matrix through which European powers encouraged outward migration to populate, administer, and economically exploit foreign territories.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the primary maritime highway across which nineteenth- and twentieth-century European emigrants traveled to American shores.
- [[united-states]] — the premier transatlantic destination that absorbed tens of millions of outward migrants fleeing starvation, conflict, and economic stagnation.
- [[english-language]] — the vehicle of communication spread globally by waves of outward migrants founding settlements across multiple oceans and continents.
