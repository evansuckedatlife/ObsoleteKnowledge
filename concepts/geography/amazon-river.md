# Amazon River

## summary

The **Amazon River** is the world's largest by discharge volume, flowing approximately 4,000 miles through the heart of South America before emptying into the Atlantic. Originating in the *Andes* and draining an immense basin spanning multiple countries (primarily *Brazil*), it is the lifeblood of the *Amazon rainforest*, sustaining extraordinary biodiversity and indigenous cultures. The Amazon system is a critical regulator of global climate and weather patterns.

## you gotta know

- Discharges roughly 209,000 cubic meters of water per second into the Atlantic—about 10% of all river water entering the world's oceans.
- The basin covers approximately 2.1 million square miles, spanning nine countries but primarily *Brazil*; it represents roughly 10% of all tropical rainforest on Earth.
- The river is navigable for most of its length, serving as the primary highway for communities in the interior; steamboats and canoes are lifelines for remote settlements.
- Home to over 1,100 fish species and countless undiscovered organisms; the rainforest canopy harbors biodiversity surpassing most terrestrial ecosystems.
- Indigenous peoples, including the *Yanomami*, have inhabited the basin for millennia, developing sophisticated ecological knowledge and sustainable harvesting practices.
- The Amazon produces roughly 20% of the world's oxygen (often exaggerated in pop culture, but significant); deforestation threatens this carbon sink and accelerates climate change.
- Deforestation, cattle ranching, dam construction, and illegal gold mining have destroyed roughly 20% of the original forest; tipping points toward savannization are a major climate concern.
