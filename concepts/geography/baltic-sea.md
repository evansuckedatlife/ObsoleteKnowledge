# Baltic Sea

## summary

The **Baltic Sea** is a shallow, semi-enclosed sea in Northern Europe, bordered by Scandinavia, *Germany*, Poland, and Russia. Rich in amber and strategic for trade, it has been a contested and economically vital region since the medieval period. The *Hanseatic League* emerged as a mercantile power dominating its waters, and in modern times, control over the Baltic has been a flash point between Russia and the West.

## you gotta know

- Bordered by nine countries: *Sweden*, *Finland*, *Russia*, *Estonia*, *Latvia*, *Lithuania*, Poland, *Germany*, and *Denmark*; enclosed by the Danish straits.
- Amber trade was historically crucial: the Baltic's deposits were Europe's primary source, traded with the *Mediterranean* and Near East since antiquity.
- The *Hanseatic League* (13th–17th centuries) monopolized Baltic commerce, establishing trading posts, standardizing practices, and controlling routes to Russia and the interior.
- The *Elbe River* and other major rivers (Oder, Vistula, Dnieper) feed the Baltic, making it central to Northern European hydrology and commerce.
- Post-WWII, the Baltic was divided by the *Cold War*: Soviet-dominated eastern shores vs. independent/Western western shores; reunification symbolized the Cold War's end.
- Modern geopolitical tension: Russia views the Baltic as its sphere; NATO expansion into the Baltic states (*Lithuania*, *Latvia*, *Estonia*) has intensified tensions.
- Environmental challenges: eutrophication from agricultural runoff, overfishing, and pollution threaten its ecology despite its relatively cold, stable climate.
