# Florence

## summary

**Florence** is a city in central Italy on the *Arno River*, the birthplace and epicenter of the *Renaissance* and one of humanity's greatest centers of art, learning, and culture. Home to the Medici banking family and an independent city-state during the Middle Ages and Renaissance, Florence concentrated unprecedented wealth and patronage into art and architecture, producing legendary artists and thinkers who transformed European civilization. Though no longer politically dominant, Florence remains synonymous with Renaissance genius and remains a pilgrimage site for lovers of art and history worldwide.

## you gotta know

- Florence is located in Tuscany in central Italy, situated on the *Arno River* flowing northwestward toward the Mediterranean; its strategic position on a crucial river crossing and within the Italian peninsula gave it commercial and cultural advantages.
- The *Medici family*, merchant-bankers who rose to prominence in the 14th century, became Florence's de facto rulers and became patrons of artists, architects, and thinkers; their wealth and influence made Florence a beacon of the emerging *Renaissance*.
- The *Renaissance* (roughly 14th–17th centuries)—a cultural and intellectual rebirth centered on humanism, the rediscovery of classical texts, and revolutionary artistic techniques—exploded in Florence and radiated outward to transform all of Europe.
- *Leonardo da Vinci* (1452–1519), painter, sculptor, architect, scientist, and polymath, epitomized Renaissance genius; he created masterpieces including the *Mona Lisa* and *The Last Supper* and filled thousands of notebook pages with scientific investigations.
- *Michelangelo Buonarroti* (1475–1564) revolutionized sculpture and painting; his marble *David* (1504) and his frescoes on the *Sistine Chapel* ceiling in Rome remain among humanity's supreme artistic achievements.
- *Florence Cathedral*, begun in 1296, was crowned by *Filippo Brunelleschi*'s revolutionary dome (1420s), a masterpiece of engineering and design that defined Renaissance architecture; the cathedral's dome remains the city's iconic symbol.
- Florence was an independent *city-state* and republic (with intervals of Medici rule) until the 16th century, when it was absorbed into the Grand Duchy of Tuscany and eventually the unified Italian state in 1861.
- Florentine artists, architects, and scholars—including *Dante Alighieri* (poet), *Giotto* (painter), *Donatello* (sculptor), and *Machiavelli* (political theorist)—fundamentally shaped European intellectual and artistic traditions.
