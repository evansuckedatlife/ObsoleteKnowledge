## summary

**Spanish Literature** is the body of written works produced in Spain and Spanish-speaking regions, encompassing some of Europe's most influential and distinctive voices. The tradition reaches back to the medieval *Reconquista* and flowered in the Renaissance and Golden Age (16th–17th centuries), when *Cervantes*, *Lope de Vega*, and *Calderón* defined modern drama and the novel. Spanish letters have remained central to Western literature through Romanticism, modernism, and contemporary fiction.

## you gotta know

- Founded on a unique blend of Christian, Jewish, and Islamic traditions due to Spain's medieval history of religious coexistence and conflict.
- The *Spanish Golden Age* (Siglo de Oro, 16th–17th centuries) was a peak of literary, artistic, and cultural achievement rivaling Italy's Renaissance.
- *Miguel de Cervantes* and his novel *Don Quixote* (1605) is often called the first modern novel and revolutionized narrative form across Europe.
- *Lope de Vega* was the most prolific dramatist of the Golden Age, inventing Spanish theatrical conventions that influenced European drama.
- The *Generation of '98* responded to Spain's loss of empire, producing philosophers and writers like *Miguel de Unamuno* grappling with national identity.
- *Federico García Lorca* brought modern Spanish poetry to international acclaim in the 20th century before his execution in the Spanish Civil War.
- Spanish literature uniquely bridges European intellectual traditions and the literatures of Latin America, making it a global cultural hub.
