# Hieroglyphics

## summary

**Hieroglyphics** (from the Greek for "sacred carvings") was the writing system of ancient Egypt, comprising pictorial signs representing sounds, words, and concepts. Used from circa 3200 BCE until the 4th century CE, hieroglyphics combined phonetic and ideographic elements into a sophisticated script carved into stone monuments, painted on tomb walls, and written on papyrus. The decipherment of hieroglyphics by scholars in the 19th century—triggered by the Rosetta Stone—opened the entire written record of Egyptian civilization to modern understanding.

## you gotta know

- Primarily phonetic, not purely pictorial: while signs depicted objects (eye, bird, lotus), most functioned as sound-signs (rebus principle); a single sign could represent a sound, a word, or both.
- Written right-to-left, top-to-bottom, or in boustrophedon (alternating direction); punctuation and word-spacing were not standardized; determinatives (clarifying signs) followed words to indicate meaning.
- Formal hieroglyphic script (monumental) was used on temple walls and royal tombs; simplified cursive scripts (Hieratic and later Demotic) were used on papyrus for administrative and literary texts.
- Numerals were expressed logographically; fractions using symbols; colors encoded meanings (black for fertility, red for chaos).
- Survived unchanged for over 3,000 years—an extraordinary continuity; conservative scribal training and the sacred association preserved the system even as spoken language evolved.
- Gradually replaced by Demotic script in daily use; Coptic (the written form of Christian Egyptian, using Greek letters) finally replaced it.
- Knowledge of hieroglyphics was lost after the late antique period; the script was unreadable for over 1,500 years until Champollion's decipherment (1822) using the Rosetta Stone.
- The Rosetta Stone (196 BCE) provided parallel texts in hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Greek, allowing scholars to reconstruct the phonetic values and unlock the entire Egyptian textual tradition.
