## summary

**Namibia** is a southwestern African nation, bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the west and characterized by vast desert landscapes. Once a German colony and later a South African mandate, Namibia became independent in 1990 and is defined by its sparse population, mineral wealth, and striking natural geography. The Namib Desert, one of Earth's oldest and driest deserts, runs along its entire western coast.

## you gotta know

- Namibia is sparsely populated with only around 2.5 million people across an area larger than France, making it one of the least densely populated African nations.
- The Namib Desert dominates the landscape, stretching over 1,300 miles along the Atlantic coast and featuring some of the world's highest sand dunes that can exceed 800 feet.
- Originally settled by San and Khoi peoples, later Bantu groups migrated south, and then German colonizers arrived in the 1880s establishing German South-West Africa as a colony.
- Germany's colonial administration established Windhoek as the capital; after World War I, South Africa took control as a League of Nations mandate territory.
- Under South African rule during apartheid, Namibia remained under strict military control until a costly independence war (1966–1990) led to liberation and UN-supervised independence.
- Namibia's economy is heavily dependent on diamonds, uranium, and other mineral exports, plus fishing along its rich Atlantic coast and offshore waters.
- The country is named for the Namib Desert, with "namib" meaning "place of nothingness" in the Khoi language, reflecting the desert's stark, barren character.
- Windhoek, the capital, sits in Namibia's interior and has developed into a modern city serving as the political and commercial hub of the nation.
