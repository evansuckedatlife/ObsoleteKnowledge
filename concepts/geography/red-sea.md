## summary

**The Red Sea** is a narrow body of water connecting Africa and Asia, bounded by Egypt and Sudan to the west and the Arabian Peninsula to the east. Formed by a geological rift valley, it connects northward to the Mediterranean via the Suez Canal and southward to the Indian Ocean through the Strait of Bab el-Mandeb. For millennia, the Red Sea has served as a crucial trade route linking Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and Asia, while its coral reefs and warm waters support unique marine ecosystems.

## you gotta know

- The Red Sea is approximately 1,200 miles long and 190 miles wide at its broadest, with depths reaching over 7,000 feet in the central trough.
- Its name's origin is debated: possibly from red algae blooms, red-brown mountains along its coasts, or ancient geographical naming conventions.
- The Suez Canal, completed in 1869, connects the Red Sea to the Mediterranean, creating the world's most important shipping chokepoint linking Europe to Asia and reducing voyage times by weeks.
- For millennia, the Red Sea facilitated trade between the Mediterranean world, Arabian Peninsula, Indian Ocean, and African interior, supporting major commercial networks.
- Historically, ancient Egypt and Rome controlled Red Sea trade; later, Arab and Indian merchants dominated; modern shipping carries roughly 12% of global trade through the Red Sea.
- The Red Sea's coral reefs and biodiversity make it a center for marine research and a popular diving destination, though recent conflicts have disrupted tourism and shipping.
- Modern geopolitical tensions in the region (including the Suez Crisis of 1956) have made control of Red Sea passages strategically vital to global commerce.

## connections

- [[egypt]] — Egypt's Red Sea coast is home to the Suez Canal and ancient trade networks; the western shore defines Egypt's eastern boundary.
- [[saudi-arabia]] — Saudi Arabia's western coast borders the Red Sea, providing access to crucial seaports and pilgrimage routes to Mecca.
- [[suez-canal]] — connects the Red Sea to the Mediterranean, making it one of the world's most strategically important waterways.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — connected to the Red Sea via the Suez Canal, linking two major maritime trade systems.
- [[sinai-peninsula]] — juts into the Red Sea between Egypt and the Arabian Peninsula, historically separating African and Asian trade.
