## summary

The **African empires**, notably the medieval **Sahelian kingdoms** of West Africa such as *Ghana*, *Mali*, and *Songhai*, were powerful sovereign states that dominated the savannah corridor between the Sahara Desert and the southern forests from the eighth through the late sixteenth century. Flourishing through lucrative trans-Saharan trade networks exchanging sub-Saharan gold and ivory for North African salt and manufactured goods, these civilizations built legendary urban centers of Islamic learning and commerce such as *Timbuktu* and *Gao*. Their diplomatic clout, immense wealth, and strategic command of vital trade routes deeply integrated West Africa with [[islam]], [[egypt]], and the broader [[middle-east]] long before European maritime expansion into the [[atlantic-ocean]].

## you gotta know

- *Mansa Musa*, ruler of the *Mali Empire*, undertook an extravagant 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca via [[egypt]], distributing and spending so much gold that he caused severe inflation in Cairo and earned Mali depiction on the 1375 *Catalan Atlas*.
- The *Mali Empire* was established in the early thirteenth century by *Sundiata Keita*, the legendary "Lion King" commemorated in the *Epic of Sundiata*, following his victory over the Sosso ruler *Soumaoro Kanté* at the 1235 Battle of Kirina.
- The *Songhai Empire* superseded Mali in the late fifteenth century under the military leader *Sonni Ali*, who deployed a fleet of war canoes along the Niger River to capture *Timbuktu* and *Djenné*, before *Askia Muhammad* (*Askia the Great*) standardized laws and fostered intellectual life.
- The *Ghana Empire*, known internally as Wagadou and praised by Arab chroniclers as the "Land of Gold," derived immense revenue at its capital of *Koumbi Saleh* by taxing gold dust and rock salt passing across the desert until its eleventh-century fragmentation.
- The empire of *Kanem-Bornu*, centered in the Lake Chad basin, sustained one of the longest continuous regimes in human history under the *Sayfawa dynasty*, dominating vital northeastern caravan trails through the Fezzan to the [[mediterranean-sea]].
- Imperial Songhai collapsed following the 1591 Battle of Tondibi, where an expeditionary force sent by the Moroccan Saadi Sultanate under *Judar Pasha* utilized gunpowder weapons and arquebuses to rout the larger cavalry of *Askia Ishaq II*.
- Beyond the Sahel, regional powers included the *Kingdom of Aksum* in the Ethiopian highlands, the commercial *Swahili city-states* trading across the Indian Ocean, *Great Zimbabwe* with its monumental dry-stone architecture, and the *Kingdom of Benin* famous for sophisticated bronze relief plaques.

## connections

- [[islam]] — provided a unifying religion, legal administration, and Arabic literacy across urban courts, epitomized by the *Sankore Madrasah* in *Timbuktu*.
- [[egypt]] — served as a key diplomatic, scholarly, and economic destination during *Mansa Musa*'s historic 1324 pilgrimage through Cairo.
- [[trans-saharan-trade]] — the network exchanging sub-Saharan gold and ivory for North African rock salt that generated the primary revenue of the Sahel.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean whose emerging coastal European trade routes in the sixteenth century gradually marginalized inland desert commerce.
- [[middle-east]] — the destination of West African pilgrim routes and source of merchant networks linking the Sahara to Arabian markets.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — the northern terminus for trans-Saharan commodities, connecting North African port emporia with European markets.
- [[nile-river]] — the vital fluvial corridor facilitating commercial and religious communication between northeastern African kingdoms and Mediterranean civilizations.
- [[colonialism]] — the late-nineteenth-century European Scramble for Africa that dismantled the sovereign territories of historic kingdoms into foreign protectorates.
