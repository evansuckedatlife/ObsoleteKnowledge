## summary

**Avignon** is a historic city in southeastern [[france]], situated along the eastern bank of the *Rhône* River within the Provence region. It is most famous historically as the fourteenth-century capital of the Catholic Church during the Avignon Papacy, when seven successive pontiffs governed Western Christendom from outside [[rome]]. Renowned for its extraordinary concentration of medieval architecture, including the massive fortress-palace *Palais des Papes* and its celebrated ruined bridge, the city served as a vital nexus of European diplomacy, religious authority, and early humanist thought.

## you gotta know

- Served as the enclave of the Catholic papacy between 1309 and 1377 during the so-called "Babylonian Captivity," initiated when Pope *Clement V* relocated the Holy See under the heavy influence of the French crown.
- Houses the colossal *Palais des Papes* (*Palace of the Popes*), one of the largest and most significant medieval Gothic buildings in [[europe]], which served as both an unbreachable fortress and opulent papal residence.
- Celebrated for the surviving arches of the twelfth-century *Pont Saint-Bénézet* stretching into the *Rhône*, famously commemorated worldwide in the traditional French children's dance song *"Sur le pont d'Avignon"*.
- Remained an autonomous papal possession alongside the surrounding *Comtat Venaissin* enclave for nearly five centuries, until French troops and local revolutionary assemblies officially annexed it to [[france]] in 1791 during the [[french-revolution]].
- Became the epicenter of the Great Western Schism from 1378 to 1417, hosting the antipopes *Clement VII* and *Benedict XIII* as rival factions fractured European political and religious loyalties.
- Served as a cradle of early [[renaissance]] humanism, notably acting as the residence of the Italian scholar and poet *Petrarch*, who famously first set eyes on his poetic muse *Laura* in the city's church of *Sainte-Claire* in 1327.
- Endured the catastrophic arrival of the Black Death in 1348, during which Pope *Clement VI* was forced to consecrate the waters of the *Rhône* River so corpses could receive a sacred burial without delay.
- Hosts the world-renowned *Festival d'Avignon*, one of modern theatre's most prestigious annual international showcases, established in 1947 by actor and director *Jean Vilar* within the grand Honor Courtyard of the papal palace.

## connections

- [[france]] — modern sovereign nation containing the city, whose medieval monarchs heavily influenced the relocation of the papacy to Provence.
- [[christianity]] — world religion whose administrative, theological, and financial structures were overhauled during the fourteenth-century papal residence in the city.
- [[rome]] — ancient Italian capital of the papacy whose political instability provoked the prolonged papal abandonment in favor of the Provencal seat.
- [[renaissance]] — transformative intellectual movement heralded by scholars like *Petrarch* who frequented the cosmopolitan and wealthy papal court.
- [[french-revolution]] — defining geopolitical event whose national assembly formally stripped the papacy of its sovereignty over the city in 1791.
- [[europe]] — continent whose emerging nation-states formed rival factions and diplomatic alliances around the competing courts of the Great Schism.
- [[mediterranean-sea]] — major body of water connected via the strategic *Rhône* commercial corridor, fostering regional trade and maritime wealth.
