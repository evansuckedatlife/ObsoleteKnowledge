## summary

The **Adirondack Mountains**, colloquially called the **Adirondacks**, form a vast circular dome massif in northeastern New York within the [[united-states]]. Geologically distinct from the neighboring Appalachian chain, the range is an ongoing uplift of Precambrian crystalline rock structurally continuous with the Canadian Shield across the border in [[canada]]. Containing thousands of glacial lakes, forty-six traditional High Peaks, and the highest summits in New York, the range is renowned as the headwaters of the *Hudson River* and as a historic crucible for American wilderness preservation.

## you gotta know

- The range is crowned by *Mount Marcy*, the highest natural elevation in New York at 5,344 feet, whose southwestern slope harbors *Lake Tear of the Clouds*, the officially recognized source pond of the *Hudson River*.
- Protected within *Adirondack Park*, established by the New York State Legislature in 1892 and fortified by the constitutional *Forever Wild* clause of 1894, which strictly forbids the sale, lease, or destruction of state forest lands.
- The village of *Lake Placid*, situated in the northern part of the range, hosted two iterations of the Winter Olympic Games in 1932 and 1980, the latter witnessing the celebrated *Miracle on Ice* hockey upset between the [[united-states]] and the [[soviet-union]].
- Covering more than six million acres, *Adirondack Park* is the largest state-level protected reserve in the contiguous [[united-states]], featuring a distinctive checkerboard patchwork of publicly preserved wilderness and privately owned working lands, hamlets, and timber tracts.
- Unlike the folded sedimentary belts of the nearby Appalachians, the Adirondacks form a distinct radial dome of anorthosite and metamorphic bedrock, constituting an isolated southern extension of the Laurentian Highlands in [[canada]].
- Outdoor recreation centers on the traditional forty-six *High Peaks*, summits originally surveyed by *Verplanck Colvin* as exceeding four thousand feet, conferring the title of *Adirondack 46ers* to dedicated hikers who summit each peak.
- During the late nineteenth century Gilded Age, prominent financiers constructed elaborate wilderness retreats known as *Great Camps*, pioneering a rustic architectural idiom of unpeeled logs and indigenous granite that heavily shaped early national park lodge design.
- The name originates as a Mohawk derogatory exonym, *ha-de-ron-dah*, meaning "bark eaters," an epithet historically hurled at neighboring Algonquian peoples who consumed tree bark during severe winter famines.

## connections

- [[united-states]] — federal republic containing the mountain range, state park lands, and the Olympic site of Lake Placid.
- [[canada]] — neighboring northern nation whose ancient Precambrian Canadian Shield is geologically continuous with the uplifted Adirondack dome.
- [[new-york-city]] — downstream coastal metropolis linked hydrographically to the mountains through the Hudson River and historically dependent on northern watershed conservation.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — principal marine basin that ultimately receives runoff draining from the High Peaks via the Hudson, Champlain, and Saint Lawrence watersheds.
- [[soviet-union]] — superpower hockey powerhouse defeated at Lake Placid by the underdog American team during the 1980 Winter Olympic Games.
- [[romanticism]] — philosophical and aesthetic movement that inspired nineteenth-century Hudson River School painters to depict the wild, sublime nature of the Adirondack backcountry.
- [[american-revolution]] — historical conflict in which strategic water routes flanking the eastern Adirondack slopes, notably Lake Champlain and Lake George, served as pivotal invasion corridors.
