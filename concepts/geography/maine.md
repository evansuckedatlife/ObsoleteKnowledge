## summary

**Maine**, colloquially celebrated as the **Pine Tree State**, is the northeasternmost state in the United States and the largest in New England. Bordered by New Hampshire, the Canadian provinces of Quebec and New Brunswick, and the Atlantic Ocean, it is celebrated for its jagged rocky coastline, maritime heritage, and vast pine forests. Admitted to the Union under a pivotal antebellum compromise on slavery, the state has played an influential role in American naval history, conservation, and literature.

## you gotta know

- The Missouri Compromise of 1820: Originally governed as an exclave of Massachusetts, the territory achieved statehood as the twenty-third state under the Missouri Compromise, admitted as a free state alongside slaveholding Missouri to preserve the sectional balance in the United States Senate.
- The Aroostook War and Webster-Ashburton Treaty: Border friction over fertile timberland between local lumberjacks and British New Brunswick sparked the bloodless Aroostook War of 1838–1839, settled definitively when Daniel Webster negotiated the Webster-Ashburton Treaty of 1842.
- Joshua Chamberlain and the 20th Maine: At the Battle of Gettysburg during the American Civil War, Bowdoin professor Colonel Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain directed the 20th Maine Infantry in a decisive bayonet charge on Little Round Top, saving the Union flank.
- The Maine Law of 1851: Spearheaded by Portland mayor Neal Dow, the "Father of Prohibition," the state passed the pioneering Maine Law of 1851, the first statewide ban on the manufacture and sale of alcohol, inspiring temperance laws nationwide.
- Wilderness geography and national parks: The state contains Mount Katahdin, the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail situated in Baxter State Park, and Acadia National Park on Mount Desert Island, the first national park designated east of the Mississippi River.
- Literary legacy from Jewett to King: The state's coastal villages and deep woodlands provided the backdrop for Sarah Orne Jewett's *The Country of the Pointed Firs*, Henry David Thoreau's *The Maine Woods*, and Stephen King's fictional towns such as Derry and Castle Rock.
- Sinking of the USS Maine: A pre-dreadnought battleship named after the state exploded in Havana Harbor in 1898, killing 266 crewmen and rallying public support behind the Spanish-American War under the slogan "Remember the *Maine*."
