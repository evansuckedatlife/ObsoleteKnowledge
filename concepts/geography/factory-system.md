## summary

The **factory system** is the organizational and technological framework that consolidated mass production under one roof, replacing artisanal and cottage industries with centralized, machine-driven manufacturing. Emerging in late 18th-century *England* powered by the *steam engine*, it enabled unprecedented output at lower cost but demanded new forms of labor discipline, capital concentration, and social hierarchy. The factory system became the economic foundation of modern capitalism and transformed human work, settlement patterns, and daily life.

## you gotta know

- Factories centralized production because *steam engines* required fixed installations; multiple machines powered from one engine were more efficient than scattered cottage operations.
- *James Watt*'s improved steam engine (1769) and *Matthew Boulton*'s manufacturing enterprise pioneered the factory model; the *textile industry* (spinning and weaving) was the first to fully mechanize.
- Factory production relied on division of labor: workers performed repetitive, specialized tasks rather than mastering a complete craft; this efficiency multiplied output but deskilled labor.
- Raw materials flowed into factories in unprecedented volume; standardized products flowed out; logistics and transportation networks (canals, railways) were reorganized accordingly.
- The factory required massive capital investment in machinery; ownership concentrated in merchant-manufacturers and early industrialists, creating a capitalist class distinct from merchants and landowners.
- Workers abandoned rural life for factory towns; crowded urban tenements, long hours (12–16 daily), dangerous conditions, and low wages characterized early factory labor.
- Child labor was rampant: children's small fingers were ideal for textile machinery, and they were cheaper than adults; reformers did not successfully restrict child labor until the mid-1800s.
- The factory system generated new ideologies: socialist critique of exploitation and alienation, classical economic theory justifying competitive markets, and nationalist pride in productive capacity.
- Factory discipline introduced the time-clock, supervision hierarchies, and efficiency metrics that defined modern work; resistance to this discipline took forms from Luddism to labor unions.
