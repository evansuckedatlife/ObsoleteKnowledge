## summary

**Montreal**, officially **Montréal**, is the most populous metropolis in the Canadian province of Quebec and the second-largest urban center in [[canada]]. Situated on the Island of Montreal at the confluence of the St. Lawrence River and the Ottawa River, the city takes its name from *Mount Royal*, the prominent volcanic hill rising at its geographical core. In quiz bowl, Montreal is celebrated as the historic commercial capital of Canada, the cultural epicenter of French-speaking North America, and the host city for landmark twentieth-century international spectacles, most notably the *Expo 67* World's Fair and the *1976 Summer Olympics*.

## you gotta know

- Occupies the Island of Montreal at the strategic junction of the *St. Lawrence River* and the *Ottawa River*, centered on *Mount Royal* (*Mont Royal*), whose summit park was designed by *Frederick Law Olmsted*.
- Functions as the primary cultural and economic capital of Francophone [[canada]], representing one of the largest predominantly French-speaking cities in the Western world alongside Paris.
- Founded in 1642 as *Ville-Marie* by French colonists led by *Paul Chomedey de Maisonneuve* and the nurse *Jeanne Mance*, operating initially as a Roman Catholic religious mission and frontier fur-trading post.
- Captured by British forces during the French and Indian War and formally ceded by [[france]] under the 1763 *Treaty of Paris*, subsequently growing under the [[british-empire]] into the premier shipping and financial hub of British North America.
- Hosted the celebrated *Expo 67* universal exposition during Canada's centennial, which featured architectural masterpieces like *Moshe Safdie*'s modular residential complex *Habitat 67* and *Buckminster Fuller*'s geodesic dome biosphere.
- Hosted the *1976 Summer Olympic Games*, commemorated for Romanian gymnast *Nadia Comăneci*'s historic perfect scores, as well as the exorbitant cost overruns of *Roger Taillibert*'s *Olympic Stadium*, popularly dubbed "The Big Owe."
- Houses a renowned climate-controlled network of subterranean pedestrian passages known as the *Underground City* (*RÉSO*), connecting metro stations, shopping complexes, and universities to shelter pedestrians during harsh Canadian winters.

## connections

- [[canada]] — the nation within which Montreal served as the historic commercial and rail gateway, driving national industrial expansion.
- [[france]] — the colonial power that established the settlement of Ville-Marie and bestowed the French language and civil legal traditions that shape modern Montreal.
- [[british-empire]] — the imperial power that took control of the city in 1760 and chartered English-speaking merchant enterprises such as the Bank of Montreal.
- [[manitoba]] — fellow Canadian territory linked historically to Montreal through the transcontinental fur trade networks of the North West Company and the Canadian Pacific Railway.
- [[colonialism]] — the transatlantic expansion that established European trading posts and settlements along the Indigenous-inhabited St. Lawrence River.
- [[atlantic-ocean]] — the ocean accessible from Montreal via the St. Lawrence River, establishing the city as an inland deepwater seaport.
